BENDIGO ART GALLERY: Bendigo, Australia
ID: bendigo-art-gallery-bendigo
Website: https://www.bendigoregion.com.au/bendigo-art-gallery/sponsors-and-partners (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Regional public gallery at 42 View Street, Bendigo, Victoria, owned and managed by the City of Greater Bendigo (Wikipedia; Art Collector).
- Founded in 1887; the collection was first housed in the former Bendigo Volunteer Rifles room, which William Charles Vahland converted in 1890 into Bolton Court. William Beebe designed Drury Court in 1897 (Wikipedia).
- Fender Katsalidis Architects designed a new sculpture gallery in a refurbishment and expansion from 1998 to 2001 (Wikipedia).
- Collection: 19th-century British and European painting, Australian art from the 19th century onward, and contemporary Australian art; the biennial Arthur Guy Memorial Painting Prize (A$50,000) and Paul Guest Prize for drawing (A$15,000) are held here (Wikipedia; Art Collector).
- Exhibitions: Frida Kahlo, "In her own image", developed with Museo Frida Kahlo in Mexico, drew 105,135 visitors, over 90 percent from outside Bendigo; the Victorian Government states that major Bendigo Art Gallery exhibitions since 2012 have drawn 688,000 visitors (Premier of Victoria, 15 July 2025).
- Visiting: the gallery closed on 1 December 2025 for redevelopment, with exhibitions held off-site until early 2028 (Wikipedia; Bendigo Advertiser).
- Redevelopment designed by Jackson Clements Burrows Architects and Clare Design, appointed in September 2022: retained heritage courts, a free ground-floor space for contemporary Australian and First Nations collections, about 1,200 m² of upstairs exhibition space, a learning centre, a "Place of Keeping" developed with the Dja Dja Wurrung people, and a new internal street linking View Street and Rosalind Park (ArchitectureAU).

FUNDING
Bendigo Art Gallery: mainly public: City of Greater Bendigo and Victorian Government, with philanthropic foundations
- The gallery is owned and managed by the City of Greater Bendigo (Wikipedia).
- The stage one redevelopment cost is A$45 million: A$21 million from the Victorian Government (agreed in 2023), A$9 million from the City of Greater Bendigo, A$4 million from the Gallery Board and A$9.35 million from the gallery's philanthropic campaign (Bendigo Advertiser, 22 May 2025; ArchitectureAU, 23 May 2025).
- Philanthropy: Sidney Myer Fund A$4 million and Ian Potter Foundation A$3 million, plus private donors (Bendigo Advertiser). The Ian Potter Foundation lists A$3 million over five years for the Bendigo Art Gallery Redevelopment, awarded in March 2025: "This grant supports the redevelopment of the Gallery's current premises" (Ian Potter Foundation).
- Federal funding: the City's bid for A$15 million from the federal Growing Regions fund toward the redevelopment was rejected in January 2025. The local federal member Lisa Chesters said the application did not get past the first assessment stage and was ineligible because of a mistake; the council's chief executive Andrew Cooney said it was an "unfortunate timing issue" because not all philanthropic funding could yet be confirmed (Bendigo Advertiser, 25 January 2025).
- Stage two still depends on A$15 million in federal funding (Bendigo Advertiser, May 2025).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public and charitable: the City of Greater Bendigo owns it and puts A$9 million into the redevelopment, the Victorian Government A$21 million, and the Sidney Myer Fund and Ian Potter Foundation A$7 million between them. The point worth watching is the federal A$15 million for stage two.
