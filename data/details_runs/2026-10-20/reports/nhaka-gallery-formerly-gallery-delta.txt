NHAKA GALLERY (FORMERLY GALLERY DELTA): Harare, Zimbabwe
ID: nhaka-gallery-formerly-gallery-delta

OVERVIEW
- Nhaka Gallery & Creative Space, at 110 Livingstone Road, Greenwood Park, Harare; it is run by the Women's Gallery Arts and Research Trust and, in the article's words, aims to build "a creative, safe, dynamic space for nurturing Zimbabwe's next generation of artists" (Greedy South, Tafadzwa Madzika, 11 March 2023).
- The same article says the address was Gallery Delta until 2022 and that Delta's founders "helped young artists learn their craft, get international exposure, and launch their careers".
- Gallery Delta was founded in 1975 by Derek Huggins, a former detective inspector with Rhodesia's British South Africa Police, and his wife, the artist Helen Lieros; it began in Strachan's building on Manica Road and moved in 1991 to 110 Livingstone Avenue, a house dating to 1894 offered by Colette Wiles, daughter of the painter Robert Paul (Wits University, Tinashe Mushakavanhu, 26 July 2021).
- Gallery Delta restored the house between 1991 and 1993 and added an amphitheatre; over four decades the couple organised or promoted roughly 500 exhibitions, and artists who passed through include Berry Bickle, Portia Zvavahera and Richard Mudariki (Wits University).
- Lieros and Huggins ran the gallery for 46 years and died in Harare within a week of each other (Wits University, 26 July 2021; Africa.com, 28 July 2021).
- Nhaka's March 2023 exhibition "Women, Power & Mobility", opened on 10 March 2023 to mark International Women's Day, honoured rural women and rethought transport as a matter of freedom; artists included Lovemore Kambudzi, Lillian Magodi, Richard Witikani, Yandani Mlilo, Webster Mubayireni, Nothando Chiwanga and Keith Zenda, and artworks could be bought through an auction on 28 April (Greedy South).

FUNDING
Nhaka Gallery: trust-run, with embassy and UN Women support for one 2023 show
- Nhaka operates under the Women's Gallery Arts and Research Trust (Greedy South).
- "Women, Power & Mobility" was a collaboration with Mobility for Africa and was supported by the Swiss and Australian embassies and UN Women (Greedy South).
- Artworks from that show were offered through an auction on 28 April (Greedy South).
- Gallery Delta, the earlier gallery, was privately owned and ran in its early years as a voluntary, part-time effort; in 2008 it was donated into trust as the Gallery Delta Foundation for Art and the Humanities, governed by an independent board of trustees (Wits University).

BOTTOM LINE
Nhaka is a small trust-run gallery whose documented backing is two embassies and UN Women for one 2023 show, plus an auction of works. Its address was previously Gallery Delta, a privately owned gallery that was placed in trust in 2008 and whose founders died in 2021.
