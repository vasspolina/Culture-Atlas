SEOUL MUSEUM OF ART: Seoul, South Korea
ID: seoul-museum-of-art-seoul
Website: https://sema.seoul.go.kr/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum at Misulgwan-gil 30 (Seosomun-dong 37), Jung-gu, central Seoul, known as SeMA (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 19 August 1988 in the Gyeonghuigung Palace area; a larger main branch opened in 2002 behind Deoksugung Palace, replacing it as the main branch (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia describes the main building as the old Korean Supreme Court building, remodelled, and lists Samoo Architects & Engineers as the architecture firm, with three storeys above ground, two basements and 13,434 square metres of floor area.
- Branches: Nam-Seoul Museum of Art (2004), Nanji Residency (2006), Buk-Seoul Museum of Art (2013), Art Archives (2023), Photo SeMA (2025) and the SeMA Nam June Paik Memorial House (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia states that the museum is operated by Seoul City Council.

FUNDING
Seoul Museum of Art: mainly public: Seoul Metropolitan Government, with corporate co-sponsorship of prizes and a private patrons' association
- Seoul Metropolitan Government: Kyunghyang Shinmun describes the museum as an agency under the Seoul city government (24 June 2025). On 4 September 2026 the Seoul Metropolitan Council's Culture, Sports and Tourism Committee reviewed a supplementary budget for the museum whose largest item, exhibition planning, was 1.957 billion won (Seoul Shinmun, Ryu Jeong-im, title translated).
- Hana Financial Group co-sponsors the SeMA-Hana Art Prize, which supports promising critics and media artists; the prize is awarded together with Hana (SeMA support page, translated).
- Patrons' association SeMA人[in]: founded in March 2014 as an incorporated association; its page says donations go to exhibitions, research, education, artist support, access for disadvantaged groups and collection gifts. The chair is Ko Kyung-mo, chief executive of Eugene Investment & Securities. Directors listed include Kim Jung-tae (former chairman of Hana Financial Group), Yun Jong-ha (vice chairman of MBK Partners), Seo Jin-woo (vice chairman, SK China), Yun Won-ki (vice president of IMM Investment), Park Jung-min (former chief executive of SK Stoa), Seo Jung-ho (chairman of Ambassador Hotel Group) and Kim Tae-sung, the secretary general, an executive director at Hana Securities.

Red flags: State censorship or punishment
- The critic Nam Woong, second winner of the SeMA-Hana Art Criticism Award, which the museum runs, submitted an essay in January 2025 for the catalogue of "We Constantly Seep into Other Rivers" (title translated), which opened on 6 March 2025 at the Seoul Museum of Art Archive. The museum left the essay out of the catalogue (Kyunghyang Shinmun, Jung Won-sik, 24 June 2025).
- Nam says the museum refused the essay as "not neutral" because it criticised the 3 December martial law declaration; he made this public in April 2025 in the webzine of Queer Solidarity Action and called it censorship. The museum says it discussed with him whether the essay fit the exhibition's intent (Kyunghyang Shinmun).
- The museum stayed silent for two months. On 19 June 2025 it posted a statement saying it had no intention of excluding a work over a political event or viewpoint and apologising for not handling the matter carefully enough (translation); it said it would publish an archival catalogue in December with the essay, the protest statements and press coverage (Kyunghyang Shinmun).
- Critics including SeMA-Hana award winners issued a joint protest on 2 June 2025 and artists in the exhibition issued a statement on 18 June. As of 23 June, more than 700 artists, curators and critics had signed the Artists' Solidarity Against Censorship statement (Kyunghyang Shinmun).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Seoul Museum of Art is the Seoul Metropolitan Government, with Hana Financial Group co-sponsoring a prize and a patrons' association whose directors come from Korean finance and industry, including Hana, MBK Partners and SK executives. The documented problem is the 2025 catalogue row, in which the museum itself left out an essay critical of the martial law declaration and, on 19 June 2025, apologised for how it handled the matter.
