TANDANYA NATIONAL ABORIGINAL CULTURAL INSTITUTE: Adelaide, Australia
ID: tandanya-national-aboriginal-cultural-institute
Website: https://www.tandanya.com.au

OVERVIEW
- Aboriginal-owned and managed multi-arts cultural centre at 253 Grenfell Street, Adelaide, on Kaurna Country; the space describes itself as Australia's first national Aboriginal cultural institute (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1989; in 1989 the South Australian Government gifted the building to Tandanya "as a symbol of reconciliation and support for First Nations peoples" (stated by the space).
- The name comes from Tarndanya, the Kaurna name for the Adelaide city centre and parklands (Wikipedia).
- Housed in the former Grenfell Street power station, built as an electrical lighting station for the Adelaide Electric Supply Company and later used as a TAFE campus (stated by the space). Wikipedia gives 1901 as the date, a rebuild in 1912 to 1913 with a Palladian-style facade, and a heritage listing in November 1984.
- The Aboriginal Lands Trust owns the building; Arts Minister Andrea Michaels had said it was "not a government-owned site" (InDaily, David Simmons, 12 July 2024).
- Programmes have included exhibitions, performances, workshops, NAIDOC Week events and the Tarnanthi art fair since 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: InDaily reported on 12 July 2024 that the institute had been closed since April 2023. The space states it is preparing for its relaunch in 2027 and is not currently open for public programming or daily access; selected events, venue hire and community activities continue during the renewal period.

FUNDING
Tandanya: South Australian government funding and repair money, with the building owned by the Aboriginal Lands Trust
- State repairs: in July 2024 the South Australian government committed $780,000 to building repairs (air-conditioning, sprinklers, walls, electrical work, painting, carpets), and had spent $90,000 on fire panel, fire door and emergency light work in 2023 (InDaily, David Simmons, 12 July 2024).
- Operating support: InDaily (David Washington, 12 March 2024) says Tandanya "isn't technically a government-run institution, but it has always relied on state government funding". The site's footer carries the Government of South Australia and CreateSA logos.
- Federal funding: Wikipedia states that Tandanya was told in May 2020 it would no longer receive Australia Council funding.
- Other income: Wikipedia lists Fringe venue hire, grants and two streams of paid membership. Its 2021/22 annual report showed a loss of $284,000, attributed to ageing infrastructure, COVID-19 restrictions and workforce gaps (Wikipedia).
- Governance: Wikipedia states, from the 2015-16 annual report, that the centre was governed by a 10-member board of Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander descent residing in South Australia.

BOTTOM LINE
Tandanya's documented money is South Australian government funding: $780,000 committed to building repairs in July 2024 and $90,000 spent on fire safety work in 2023 (InDaily). InDaily also reports that it has always relied on state government funding. The Aboriginal Lands Trust owns the building.
