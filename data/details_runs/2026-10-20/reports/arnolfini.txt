ARNOLFINI: Bristol, United Kingdom
ID: arnolfini
Website: https://arnolfini.org.uk (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre at 16 Narrow Quay, on Bristol's harbourside (Arnolfini website).
- Founded in 1961 in Clifton by Jeremy Rees, with his wife Annabel and the painter John Orsborn; named for Jan van Eyck's "The Arnolfini Portrait". It moved from Queen Square to W Shed and reached its present home in 1975 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Bush House, a Grade II* listed former tea warehouse built in 1831 and extended in 1835 to 1836 for an iron foundry, designed by Richard Shackleton Pope, in Pennant Sandstone; a 2005 redevelopment added an attic storey (Wikipedia).
- Ownership: in July 2015 the building was sold to the University of the West of England (UWE Bristol), and Arnolfini leases back its artistic space; the sale proceeds replenished its endowment fund (Wikipedia).
- Run by an independent charity (CIO, registered charity no. 311504) (Arnolfini website).
- On site: galleries and a cafe bar open daily from 9am (Arnolfini website).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday, 11:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; the website describes Bush House as an accessible building and links a separate access page.

FUNDING
Arnolfini: public and institutional: Arts Council England, Bristol City Council and UWE Bristol, plus a charitable trust
- The website says Arnolfini is supported by Arts Council England and the Ashley Clinton Barker-Mill Trust, and names UWE Bristol.
- Wikipedia states it is funded by Arts Council England and Bristol City Council. In 2017 it lost its £750,000 annual Arts Council England grant after an assessment that it was "not viable"; the funding was reinstated in 2019 under executive director Gary Topp (Wikipedia).
- UWE Bristol owns the building and leases the gallery space back (Wikipedia).

Red flags: cancelled Palestine Film Festival events and artist boycott
- November 2023: Arnolfini cancelled two events in the Bristol Palestine Film Festival, a screening of "Farha" and a poetry evening with Lowkey. Both were later held at other venues (The Bristol Cable, Adam Quarshie, 6 Feb 2024).
- Stated reason: the gallery "could not be confident that the event would not stray into political activity" (The Bristol Cable).
- Protest: activists held two sit-ins in the foyer, in late November and in December 2023. A call from Bristol Artists for Palestine for artists to disengage from the gallery had over 1,200 signatories by 6 February 2024, including Alice Oswald, Nikesh Shukla and Robert Del Naja (The Bristol Cable). Bristol Artists for Palestine puts the number of boycotting artists at more than 1,400 (Artists for Palestine UK).
- Apology: in January 2024 Arnolfini apologised on its website, saying the original communication "was not clear enough" and expressing "deep regret for the distress caused to those we consider allies" (The Bristol Cable). On 3 May 2024 its Board of Trustees and Senior Leadership Team published a further apology saying the cancellation "was wrong" and that a new policy on programming decisions was in development (Arnolfini website).
- Outcome: Bristol Artists for Palestine said the May 2024 statement "provided a resolution to the artists' demands" and ended the boycott (Artists for Palestine UK, an advocacy source).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism came from artists and local press; the one art-press mention, in 2025, is favourable.
1. The Bristol Cable (local press, not art media): Adam Quarshie, "'Speaking is a political act': Bristol artists push Arnolfini for action over Palestine controversy" (6 February 2024)
URL: https://thebristolcable.org/2024/02/speaking-is-a-political-act-bristol-artists-push-arnolfini-for-action-over-palestine-controversy/
- The sound artist Nik Rawlings said the gallery failed to uphold freedom of expression on Palestine, that the situation "could set a dangerous precedent across the arts and charity sector", and that the January statement "says nothing about the decision-making".
- Rawlings described a "wilful misreading of the Charity Commission guidelines" on political activity.
- The Cable reports that Arnolfini did not reply to its request for comment.
2. Hyperallergic: Anna Souter, "Dana Awartani's Gentle Reparation of Gaza" (13 August 2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1032270/dana-awartani-gentle-reparation-of-gaza-arnolfini-bristol/
- Not criticism of the institution: a review of Awartani's show "Standing by the Ruins" at Arnolfini.
- Says Arnolfini cancelled two Bristol Palestine Film Festival events, which led to controversy and a boycott, and that after mediation with local groups, including Bristol Artists for Palestine, the gallery apologised and committed to upholding Palestinian freedom of expression.

BOTTOM LINE
The public money behind Arnolfini (Arts Council England, Bristol City Council, UWE Bristol) does not come from a flagged funder. The weak point is the 2023 cancellation of Palestine Film Festival events by the gallery itself, which drew an artist boycott with over 1,200 signatories at the Cable's count, and two apologies, in January and May 2024. Worth watching is the policy on programming decisions that the gallery said it is developing.
