DES MOINES ART CENTER – Des Moines, USA
Website: https://desmoinesartcenter.org/ (news and annual-report pages loaded 2026-10-20; /support not retrieved)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum that grew out of the Des Moines Association of Fine Arts. It was founded with a bequest from James D. Edmundson, a Council Bluffs lawyer and banker who died in 1933: $600,000 at his death, more than $1.5M by the time it was built (dsm magazine, 2025).
- Opened in 1948. Three buildings: Eliel Saarinen (1948), I. M. Pei (1968) and Richard Meier (1985). Also runs the Pappajohn Sculpture Park, opened in 2009 (Wikipedia; own site).
- General admission is "always free for all" (own site). Director and CEO is Kelly Baum; the 2024 board president was Jason Gross (2024 annual report).

FUNDING
Des Moines Art Center: endowment and private gifts, plus local public arts grants.
- Endowments and trusts dating back to the Edmundson bequest (own annual report; dsm magazine).
- Bravo Greater Des Moines: Cultural Leadership grants and capital grants (Meier and Saarinen renovations, Pei lighting). Bravo's grant money comes from 17 local governments, including Des Moines and Polk County (stated by the space).
- Polk County Board of Supervisors, Prairie Meadows and the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation are named as grantors in the 2024 annual report.
- Named donors include the Meredith and Macomber families, the Edmundson Art Foundation, the Kyle and Sharon Krause Family Art Acquisition Fund and the Estate of Dale Jansen (65 works). John and Mary Pappajohn funded the sculpture park and gave 31 sculptures (stated by the space).
- No gift amounts or financial totals appear in the 2024 annual report.

Red flags: Artist lawsuit over Greenwood Pond: Double Site
- Mary Miss sued in April 2024 to stop the removal of her 1996 work, which sits in city-owned Greenwood Park. The museum estimated restoration at $2.6M or more and said it had spent nearly $1M maintaining the work (The Art Newspaper).
- Settled 14 Jan 2025: the Art Center pays Miss $900,000, and she drops her claim. She plans to give the money to a public-art fund set up by The Cultural Landscape Foundation (KCRG/AP; ArtReview).
- The Art Center said "no taxpayer or public funds will be used" for the removal, which was scheduled to start 24 Feb 2025 (own release).
- This is an artist-rights dispute. No reporting found that ties it to a funder.

BOTTOM LINE
Funding is open and local: an old endowment, private and family donors, and regional public arts grants paid through Bravo. No contested funder was found. The thing to watch is the Mary Miss settlement and the removal of her work, which concern how the museum looks after its collection, not who funds it.
