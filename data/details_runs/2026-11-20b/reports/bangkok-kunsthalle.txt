BANGKOK KUNSTHALLE: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: bangkok-kunsthalle
Website: https://khaoyaiart.org/bk/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art centre at 599 Pantachit Alley (Soi Phantha Chit), Pom Prap Sattru Phai, on the edge of Bangkok's Chinatown, about a 6 to 7 minute walk from Hua Lamphong station (Bangkok Kunsthalle).
- Founded by the art patron Marisa Chearavanont through her organisation Khao Yai Art, launched in 2023; it opened in January 2024 with Michel Auder's "Nine Plus Five" (12 January to 11 February 2024) (ArtAsiaPacific; Domus).
- Occupies the former Thai Watana Panich printing house, founded in 1935 as a textbook publisher, whose building burned in 2001 and stood empty for 23 years (Bangkok Kunsthalle).
- The brutalist complex of three connected buildings covers nearly 65,000 square feet (Wikipedia); Tatler Asia gives 8,000 square metres and credits the conversion to the architect Duangrit Bunnag of DBALP (Tatler Asia, October 2026).
- The space opens one floor at a time; in 2024 only the ground floor was in use (Domus; Art Basel).
- Led by director and curator Stefano Rabolli Pansera, formerly director of Hauser & Wirth St. Moritz, planning about four shows a year (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Korakrit Arunanondchai's "nostalgia for unity" (2024) drew about 900 visitors a day (curator Mark Chearavanont in art4d, June 2025).
- Current season: Bas Jan Ader's "Bright at Midnight" (to 10 January 2027) and Renato Leotta's "Gift to Entropy" (to 30 December 2026) (Time Out Bangkok).
- Its rural counterpart is Khao Yai Art Forest, about 150 km east of Bangkok, opened in February 2025 (Art Basel; Domus).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Sunday 2pm to 8pm, closed Monday and Tuesday; admission to all exhibitions is free (Bangkok Kunsthalle; Time Out Bangkok).

FUNDING
Bangkok Kunsthalle: private: the founder's philanthropy, from the Chearavanont family of CP Group, with six named private donors
- Marisa Chearavanont is married to Soopakij Chearavanont, chairman of the Charoen Pokphand (CP) Group, which ArtAsiaPacific calls Thailand's largest private company and ArtReview Thailand's largest agribusiness.
- Operator: donations go to Khaoyai Art Social Enterprise Co., Ltd, a company, through an account at Siam Commercial Bank's CP Tower branch (Bangkok Kunsthalle support page).
- Donors: the Team page lists six "Founder's Circle & Collector's Roundtable Donors": Sangita Jindal, Isabel Liu, Takeo Obayashi, Nunthinee Tanner, Elisa Yu and Lisa Zhang, with no amounts or dates (Bangkok Kunsthalle).
- No budget or accounts are published.

Red flags: 2024 protest by artn't inside Korakrit Arunanondchai's show
- During "nostalgia for unity" in 2024, two members of the Chiang Mai collective artn't appeared in the show in T-shirts reading "WORLD CLASS ARTIST, WORLD CLASS GALLERY, WORLD CLASS CORPORATION, WORLD CLASS EXPLOITATION" and "THIS COUNTRY IS YOUR GALLERY FOR EXHIBITING THE ART OF MONOPOLY" (ArtReview).
- A member told ArtReview "the origins of art funding deserve critical examination."

Not related: the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC), whose main funder is the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration and which altered a 2025 exhibition under Chinese embassy pressure. It is a different institution.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical piece; the rest of the coverage is admiring features on the founder, director and building.
1. ArtReview: Pearamon Tulavardhana, "Will the New Bangkok Kunsthalle Fire up the Art Scene?" (2024)
URL: https://artreview.com/will-the-new-bangkok-kunsthalle-fire-up-the-art-scene/
- "Philanthropy is crucial in a country where government funding for the arts is limited," but "concerns must be raised when an act of philanthropy may end up doing something else entirely."
- Asks whether the space can start "critical conversations about the issues of exploitation and environment", and notes its disconnect from Chinatown businesses facing rising rents.
- Links the show's haze to burning by contract feed-corn farmers whose crop is sold to multinational agriculture companies, without naming CP Group.
2. Art Basel: Nick Hackworth, "Can a kunsthalle turn Bangkok into Southeast Asia's premier art hub?" (2024)
URL: https://www.artbasel.com/stories/kunsthalle-art-forest-turn-thailand-into-southeast-asia-premier-art-hub
- Not criticism: an admiring feature that does not mention CP Group.

WIDER CONTEXT
- A 2015 US class action alleged that Costco sold prawns from CP Foods whose feed came from fishmeal suppliers using boats crewed by slaves, following a 2014 Guardian investigation (Taipei Times, from The Guardian); a US court dismissed the case (Wikipedia, citing Reuters of January 2017), and CP Foods said it owns no fishing vessels.
- In September 2026 Thailand's Court of Appeal let a class action by Samut Songkhram fish farmers against CP Foods over the spread of blackchin tilapia proceed; the plaintiffs seek more than 2.486 billion baht, and the court has not ruled on liability (The Nation).
- CP Foods belongs to CP Group, chaired by the founder's husband (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
Private money from the founder's family fortune and a small donor circle, with no published accounts. The open question is the CP Group connection, which artn't targeted inside the space in 2024.
