WHANKI MUSEUM: Seoul, South Korea
ID: whanki-museum-seoul
Website: http://whankimuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; could not be opened: "self signed certificate" error)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum at 210-8 Buam-dong, Jongno-gu, in central Seoul, near Seongbuk-dong (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Kim Hyang-an (1916 to 2004), the widow of the painter Kim Whanki, who established the Whanki Foundation after his death and built the museum (Munhwa Ilbo, 5 September 2016). Construction began in 1990 and the museum opened in November 1992 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Kim Whanki (1913 to 1974), a first-generation Korean abstract painter.
- Designed by the architect Kyu Sung Woo (Wikipedia).
- On site: a main building designed to show Kim's art, Annex 1 for special exhibitions with a café and art shop, and Annex 2 for lectures (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Time Out lists Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm, closed Mondays, with admission varying by exhibition. No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Whanki Museum: private foundation: the Whanki Foundation; no funder list or accounts published
- The Whanki Foundation established the museum (Wikipedia). Korea JoongAng Daily (25 June 2026) names the museum director as Park Mee-jung and the foundation president as Jang Jae-ryong.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, donor, state grant or accounts for the museum or the foundation. Neither the Korea Herald nor the Korea JoongAng Daily coverage addresses its funding.
- No reporting found of how the museum came to hold Kim Whanki's works. Munhwa Ilbo does not explain this.

Red flags: protest over the poisoned ginkgo tree
- Event: security camera footage showed two men drilling holes into the base of a ginkgo tree beside the museum and injecting a chemical on 22 April 2026; the tree is estimated at 100 to 200 years old (Korea Herald, TWIG24). The museum said workers it hired applied herbicide (Korea Herald).
- Protest: Buam-dong residents held an event beside the museum on 23 May 2026 to pray for the tree and demand an apology (Hankyoreh, 25 May 2026). On 26 May residents and the Seoul Environmental Alliance held a press conference in front of the museum demanding a public apology and restoration (Kyunghyang Shinmun, 10 June 2026).
- Museum's account: the roots were damaging the wall beside the tree, and it could not get agreement from the tree's many co-owners (Kyunghyang Shinmun, TWIG24, Korea JoongAng Daily). It posted an apology on 31 May (Kyunghyang Shinmun). Some residents and groups said it did not mention the herbicide (TWIG24). It has not disclosed the chemical or the amount (Korea JoongAng Daily, 25 June 2026).
- Complaint: on 29 June 2026, 60 residents, environmental groups and individuals filed a criminal complaint against museum director Park Mee-jung and the Whanki Foundation for property damage and violating the Soil Environment Conservation Act (Korea Herald).
- Status in July 2026: the tree was in critical condition. Jongno-gu wanted a "Beautiful Tree" designation, but only 16 of the 46 landowners had replied and the required majority consent was not met (Herald Economy, 12 July 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Nothing is published about who pays for the museum, so its money cannot be judged either way. The documented weak point is the April 2026 herbicide injection and the residents' protest and criminal complaint that followed. Worth watching: the outcome of the complaint and the tree, and whether the foundation publishes accounts or a donor list.
