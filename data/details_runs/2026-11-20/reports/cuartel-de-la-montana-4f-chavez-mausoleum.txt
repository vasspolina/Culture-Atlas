CUARTEL DE LA MONTAÑA 4F (CHÁVEZ MAUSOLEUM): Caracas, Venezuela
ID: cuartel-de-la-montana-4f-chavez-mausoleum
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Former barracks in the La Planicie sector of the 23 de Enero parish, western Caracas, with a view over the Miraflores presidential palace (Aletheia; NTN24).
- Built in 1906 on the orders of President Cipriano Castro; it housed the Military Academy and later the Ministry of Defence, and in the late 1970s a presidential decree made it a national monument and the Military History Museum (Aletheia).
- On 4 February 1992 Lieutenant Colonel Hugo Chávez used it as his command post in the coup attempt against President Carlos Andrés Pérez. In 2002, as president, he made it the museum of the Bolivarian Revolution and the seat of the Bolivarian Militia, and it took the name "Cuartel de la Montaña 4F" (Aletheia).
- Chávez died on 5 March 2013 and his body was brought here that month. It lies in a sealed marble sepulchre on a base shaped as "the flower of the four elements", surrounded by water; visitors walk a 300 metre route through two photo rooms to the lit central hall (Cooperativa, EFE, 2013).
- The sarcophagus is guarded by the Presidential Honour Guard in the uniform of Bolívar's Hussars, the same guard that stands at the National Pantheon and the Carabobo arch (Aletheia).
- Closed temporarily in July 2026 for structural damage from the earthquakes of 24 June 2026 (NTN24).

FUNDING
Cuartel de la Montaña 4F: public: the Venezuelan state and its armed forces
- Owner and user: the state and its armed forces. The Bolivarian Militia based here is a reserve body of the National Bolivarian Armed Forces made up of civilians and retired soldiers (Aletheia).
- Visits: in 2013 the government organised the visits in groups of about 15, and visitors had free transport from the city centre (Cooperativa, EFE).
- The July 2026 closure was announced through the Fundación Hugo Chávez (NTN24).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is academic and concerns how the state stages Chávez's memory.
1. Aletheia (Universidad Nacional de La Plata): Jhonatan Taguaruco, "Articulaciones y disputas simbólicas en las representaciones del Mausoleo Presidencial Cuartel de la Montaña 4-F" (2016)
URL: https://aletheia.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/article/download/ATHv6n12a04/11054/26959
- Argues that after 2013 the government used Chávez as the axis for framing memory in public spaces, and that civilian and military currents of chavismo compete over that memory at the mausoleum.
- Reads the Hussar honour guard as a staging that projects Chávez's continuity as president and sets him beside the founding figures of the nation, and the plaque naming him "Comandante Supremo" as a rank that exists outside the traditional chain of command.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 24 August 2025 the barracks hosted the second day of militia registration called by President Nicolás Maduro amid tension with the United States; visitors were shown a video of Maduro urging people to join the Bolivarian Militia (El Heraldo, EFE).
- After explosions in Caracas in the early hours of 3 January 2026, Maldita.es found that a video said to show the mausoleum being bombed showed the Bolivarian Militia's General Command at the former Cagigal Observatory (Maldita.es, 5 January 2026).
- In January 2026, after being sworn in as acting president following the capture of Maduro by the United States, Delcy Rodríguez visited Chávez's tomb and reaffirmed her loyalty before the sarcophagus, according to a Vice Presidency press note (Vanguardia, 6 January 2026).
- Connection: Vanguardia describes the mausoleum as a political reference point where officials go in moments of transition, as a signal of continuity with Chávez's project.

BOTTOM LINE
State money only: no funding concern. Its weight is political, as the shrine of the governing movement, and its future turns on who holds power in Caracas.
