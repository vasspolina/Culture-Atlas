WHITECHAPEL GALLERY: London, UK
ID: whitechapel-gallery
Website: https://www.whitechapelgallery.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery at 77-82 Whitechapel High Street, East London; the nearest stations are Aldgate East and Liverpool Street (stated by the gallery).
- Founded in 1901 to serve East London; Charles Aitken was the first director, 1901 to 1911 (Wikipedia, gallery).
- The original building was designed by Charles Harrison Townsend in the British Modern Style (Art Nouveau). The 2009 expansion took in the adjacent former Passmore Edwards library, roughly doubled the gallery's size, cost about £13.5 million, was partly paid for by the Heritage Lottery Fund and reopened in April 2009 (Wikipedia).
- On site: nine exhibition spaces, an auditorium, study and studio spaces, a bookshop and a café (stated by the gallery).
- Picasso's "Guernica" was shown there in 1938 and Mark Rothko's first UK exhibition in 1961 (Wikipedia). Iwona Blazwick directed it from 2002 to 2022; Gilane Tawadros has been director since 2022.
- Visiting: open 11 am to 6 pm Tuesday to Sunday, until 9 pm on Thursday, closed Monday. Free displays; one ticketed show per season at £16.50, £9.50 concession; under-16s and members free; Thursday evenings pay what you can. Blue Badge parking and Easy Read guides are offered.

FUNDING
Whitechapel Gallery: mixed: Arts Council England plus private patrons, trusts and corporate sponsors
- Arts Council England: annual funding fell from £1,510,168 (2018 to 2022) to £1,437,955 (2023 to 2026) (The Art Newspaper, via USA Art News, 2023). The gallery's footer says it is supported with public funding by Arts Council England.
- The 2021-22 annual report showed a deficit of £73,016; the gallery restructured in 2023 and made six full-time staff redundant, three of them curators (USA Art News, citing The Art Newspaper, March 2023).
- Bloomberg sponsored the Goshka Macuga work that opened the reopened gallery in 2009 (Museums Journal, 2009).
- Phillips was auction partner of the March 2025 Art Icon gala, for which Doris Salcedo received the award; Pommery supplied the drinks (stated by the gallery).
- The expansion was financed partly by the Heritage Lottery Fund and a charity auction of donated works that also created an endowment (Wikipedia, Museums Journal).
- The gallery's support page names no individual donors, corporate patrons or trusts. Its donor list and annual reports were not readable here, so claims of Bloomberg Philanthropies, Max Mara, Swarovski Foundation and City Bridge Trust as major donors are not confirmed by an opened page.
- No reporting found of a campaign against Bloomberg aimed at the Whitechapel.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the gallery or its funders found. The one documented dispute is a 2006 exhibition cut, and a 2023 restructure drew news coverage.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lucian Harris, "Whitechapel Gallery accused of 'self-censorship'" (2006)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2006/12/01/whitechapel-gallery-accused-of-self-censorship
- Lender Marcel Fleiss found five of his 12 works removed from the Hans Bellmer show on the eve of opening and called it self-censorship; he said he would never lend to the gallery again.
- Curator Agnes de la Beaumelle said Blazwick told her the works could offend the local Muslim population and anti-paedophile groups. The Whitechapel and the Pompidou both said the show was cut because the London spaces were smaller.
2. USA Art News: Romario, "Six key jobs culled at London's Whitechapel Gallery, including three curatorial posts" (2023), reporting The Art Newspaper
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/six-key-jobs-culled-at-londons-whitechapel-galleryincluding-three-curatorial-posts
- Reports the redundancies, the Arts Council cut and the deficit; Tawadros called it a "small re-structure". Not criticism of the funders.
3. Artforum: "Whitechapel Slashes Jobs, Eliminates Curatorial Posts"
URL: https://www.artforum.com/news/whitechapel-slashes-jobs-eliminates-curatorial-posts-90267
- Paywalled; only a search excerpt was read.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public funding from Arts Council England plus patrons, but no donor list was readable, so the names on it cannot be confirmed. The weak point is the 2006 Bellmer removals, which lenders called self-censorship and the gallery attributed to space. Worth watching: the gallery's published donor list and accounts, which would show whether Bloomberg Philanthropies funds it today.
