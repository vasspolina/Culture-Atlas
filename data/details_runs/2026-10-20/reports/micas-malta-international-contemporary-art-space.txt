MICAS MALTA INTERNATIONAL CONTEMPORARY ART SPACE: Floriana, Malta
ID: micas-malta-international-contemporary-art-space
Website: https://micas.art (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the restored Floriana fortifications and the Old Ospizio complex at the edge of Valletta; it bills itself as the country's first national contemporary arts museum (Apollo, MICAS).
- Phyllis Muscat is executive chairperson; the museum opened on 25 October 2024, about three years late according to the National Audit Office (Apollo; The Shift, 20 December 2025).
- The site holds 17th-century fortifications and the Old Ospizio, a Knights-era poorhouse that later served as a gunpowder store; Carlo Terpolilli of the Florentine firm Ipostudio led the contemporary intervention, and a steel roof joins the historic parts (MICAS).
- The opening exhibition was by the Portuguese artist Joana Vasconcelos; works by Ugo Rondinone and Conrad Shawcross are also on display (MICAS).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; adults EUR 10, seniors and students EUR 7, children 6 to 11 EUR 4, under 5 free; step-free, wheelchair and pushchair friendly, with accessible toilets and Blue Badge parking (MICAS visit page).

FUNDING
MICAS: mainly public: the Maltese state and the European Regional Development Fund
- The project cost EUR 30 million, funded by the Maltese state and the European Regional Development Fund (Apollo). The National Audit Office put the cost at around EUR 26 million (The Shift, reporting the audit).
- The gallery works were co-financed 80% EU funds and 20% national funds under Operational Programme I, European Structural and Investment Funds 2014 to 2020 (Ministry press release, 3 July 2020).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor list or named private donor. The site invites sponsors and donors to support the museum.
- The board is Phyllis Muscat, Georgina Portelli, Claire Cassar and Joyce Dimech (MICAS about page).

Red flags: Rondinone commission through a board member's trade
- The sculpture "The Radiant" by Ugo Rondinone was bought through Galerie Eva Presenhuber, Zurich, for USD 440,000 (EUR 401,000) after a USD 110,000 discount, plus EUR 41,000 for freight, insurance and a concrete base (The Shift, 13 January 2024).
- No public call was published; the artist was selected directly. Board member Francis Sultana said the board chose Rondinone unanimously (The Shift).
- Sultana is CEO of the art dealer David Gill Gallery. Unverified: The Shift, citing unnamed sources, reported that Sultana sells Rondinone's work through his private London business; he did not respond when asked.
- Culture minister Owen Bonnici denies any conflict of interest and says MICAS has oversight from the National Audit Office and MPs (Apollo).
- Sultana left the board in early 2025. MICAS's own page says he served seven years and had intended to step down after the October 2024 opening; The Shift reported on 28 February 2025 that Bonnici removed him and replaced him with Joyce Dimech, and that Bonnici and Sultana did not answer its questions.

Red flags: transparency and governance
- Freedom of information: Muscat refused a request for the works acquired, their cost and suppliers, saying the board's handling of public funds is not covered by the FOI law (The Shift, 13 March 2024).
- National Audit Office, reported by The Shift on 20 December 2025: missing performance guarantees and insurance on works, approvals issued days after works began, retrospective payments, only five board meetings in the last year, and inadequate information on board honoraria.
- The same report: Muscat spent EUR 225,000 on the launch, a third of it going to TEC Ltd, which The Shift calls one of the Labour Party's key logistics partners. The article carries no reply from MICAS or the ministry.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. The one art-press piece found reports the accusations as background to a mostly descriptive opening-day feature. The detailed criticism comes from the Maltese investigative site The Shift, which is news media rather than art press.
1. Apollo: Elise Morton, "Contemporary art gets a permanent home in Malta" (2024)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/micas-malta-contemporary-art-joana-vasconcelos/
- Reports accusations of misuse of public funds and conflicts of interest over the EUR 500,000 Rondinone statue commissioned in 2019, including the accusation that the sculptor had close commercial ties to a MICAS board member.
- Records the minister's denial.
- Mostly a descriptive feature on the opening exhibition, not a full critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public: Maltese state and EU funds, with no private donor found. The weak points are the direct purchase of a EUR 500,000 sculpture without a public call, from an artist whose work The Shift's unnamed sources say the board member sells, the refusal of a freedom of information request, and the National Audit Office's findings on documentation and board practice. Watch the audit follow-up and whether MICAS publishes accounts and acquisitions.
