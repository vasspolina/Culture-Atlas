YELTSIN CENTER: Yekaterinburg, Russia
ID: yeltsin-center-yekaterinburg
Website: https://yeltsin.ru/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural centre at ulitsa Borisa Yeltsina 3, Yekaterinburg (stated by the Center). The nine-floor building holds the Yeltsin museum on two floors and shares the rest with the Center's own spaces and outside tenants (Meduza, 2026).
- Founded under the 2008 federal law on heritage centres of former presidents as a non-commercial foundation, the Presidential Center of Boris Yeltsin; it opened on 25 November 2015, with Vladimir Putin and Dmitry Medvedev attending (Yeltsin Center, Wikipedia, Meduza).
- Named for Boris Yeltsin, the first president of Russia, born in the Urals.
- Architect Boris Bernaskoni; the exhibition was designed by the American firm Ralph Appelbaum Associates (Wikipedia, Meduza).
- On site: the museum, an archive, a lecture hall, a library, a gallery, a science museum, a bookshop, cafes, a fitness club, a hotel and a Sberbank branch (Meduza).
- No reporting found of opening days, admission prices or accessibility on the pages opened; the site lists discounts only for the museum and gallery.

FUNDING
Yeltsin Center: mainly public with corporate and personal donors: federal budget plus Russian companies and businessmen
- Revenue from opening to the start of the war averaged 1.6 billion roubles a year in budget funds, 1.4 billion in donations and 114 million in the Center's own income (Meduza/Bereg, March 2026).
- The state share of income rose from 56% (2015 to 2021) to 77% as average yearly donations in 2023 and 2024 fell almost 14-fold; spending fell from over 2 billion roubles in 2023 to 457 million in 2024 (Meduza/Bereg).
- Companies thanked for help creating the exhibition: EVRAZ, Gazprom, Magnit, Rosneft, Rusagro and Sber. The Center also thanked Putin, Medvedev, Roman Abramovich, Aras Agalarov, Mikhail Gutseriev, Oleg Deripaska, Mikhail Prokhorov and Alisher Usmanov (Meduza/Bereg). Rosneft and Prokhorov were sponsors at the 2015 opening.
- Its board of trustees is chaired by Anton Vaino, head of the Presidential Administration; members include Alexander Voloshin, Naina Yeltsina, presidential envoy Artyom Zhoga and Sverdlovsk governor Denis Pasler (Yeltsin Center). The Center posts accounting reports on its site.
- No reporting found of what each named donor gave.

Red flags: state pressure on programming and staff
- Statement: on 25 February 2022 the Center published a call to stop the war; it vanished from the site in April 2022, which the press service attributed to "requirements of law enforcement" (my translation) (Meduza/Bereg).
- Fine: on 20 August 2025 the Verkh-Isetsky court fined first deputy executive director Lyudmila Telen 45,000 roubles for "discrediting the army" over a repost of "No to war!" from Tatyana Yumasheva's page (Novaya Gazeta Europe).
- Cancellations: Nina Khrushcheva's 26 November 2024 book presentation was cancelled for "technical reasons" after pro-government channels and the movement Zov Naroda filed complaints (Mediazona). Meduza lists further cancelled events, including a Mirzoev play in spring 2025.
- Regional oversight: two sources told Meduza/Bereg the Sverdlovsk region's internal policy department requests the Center's lecturer lists. The Center did not answer Bereg's questions.
- The Center's funders at opening included Rosneft and Gazprom, oil and gas companies.

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the money is Russian state budget and donations from named Russian companies and businessmen, with no foreign funder found. The weak points are the censorship pressure documented since 2022, the dependence on the Presidential Administration, and oil and gas sponsors at the opening. Worth watching: the state's share of income and any move to label the Center a foreign agent, which the Justice Ministry declined to examine in 2022, saying the presidential-centres law puts such centres outside routine checks (Meduza).
