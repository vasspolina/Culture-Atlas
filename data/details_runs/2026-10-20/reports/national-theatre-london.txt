NATIONAL THEATRE: London, UK
ID: national-theatre-london
Website: https://www.nationaltheatre.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24; returned HTTP 403 to automated requests, so its pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Three-auditorium theatre complex on London's South Bank, in a listed concrete building by Denys Lasdun.
- Founded by Laurence Olivier, who became its first artistic director; the company opened on 22 October 1963 with Hamlet at the Old Vic, as the National Theatre Company, and stayed there until 1976 (Wikipedia).
- The Royal prefix was approved in 1988 (Wikipedia).
- Architect Denys Lasdun, with Peter Softley; built by Sir Robert McAlpine. The main material is pale concrete cast in rough wooden formwork, so the wood grain shows inside and out. Grade II* listed since 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Three stages opened separately between 1976 and 1977: the Olivier (1,160 seats), the Lyttelton (890) and the Cottesloe, renamed the Dorfman (400) in 2014 after Lloyd Dorfman, chairman of Travelex Group (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of current opening hours, admission or accessibility terms: the theatre's own site blocked access.

FUNDING
National Theatre: mixed: ticket sales, Arts Council England and donations from companies, individuals, trusts and foundations
- In 2015-16 turnover was about £105 million: 75% earned (58% ticket sales, 5% NT Live and digital, 12% commercial), 17% Arts Council England, 1% Learning and Participation, 9% companies, individuals, trusts and foundations (Wikipedia). No reporting found of a later breakdown that could be opened.
- Lloyd Dorfman, chairman of Travelex Group, is the namesake of the Dorfman Theatre (Wikipedia).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies is a partner: see Red flags. No reporting found of the amount.
- No reporting found of a full list of current corporate, trust and individual funders that could be opened.

Red flags: Shell corporate membership
- Shell was a corporate member of the National Theatre. Al Jazeera reported it gave £15,000 to £30,000 a year.
- On 7 October 2019 the theatre declared a climate emergency and said the membership would end in June 2020 (Al Jazeera, News Agencies). The theatre called Shell a valued and longstanding supporter.
- The decision came after the Royal Shakespeare Company dropped BP days earlier (Al Jazeera).
Red flags: open letter on Bloomberg Philanthropies
- On 28 May 2026 Artists for Palestine UK published an open letter signed by 261 artists, including Caryl Churchill and Miriam Margolyes. It asked the National Theatre, Royal Shakespeare Company, Royal Court, Young Vic and Almeida to end their partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies (Morning Star, Berny Torre; The Stage, Katie Chambers).
- The letter says the partnerships undermine the theatres' own ethical commitments. It points to Bloomberg Philanthropies funding for the Bloomberg-Sagol Center for City Leadership at Tel Aviv University, which the letter says trains officials who govern illegal Israeli settlements in the West Bank (Morning Star).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies and the theatres were contacted for comment; the Morning Star reported no response.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the National Theatre's funders was found in art media. The Shell and Bloomberg objections were reported in news and theatre trade outlets, and the one critical piece found concerns the building, in design press.

- Criticises the architecture, not the institution or its funders.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Artists for Palestine UK says Bloomberg Philanthropies is complicit through its funding of the Tel Aviv University centre; this is the letter's claim, not a finding by a court or regulator (Morning Star, The New Arab).
- The National Theatre is one of five theatres named in the letter.

BOTTOM LINE
The theatre's main income is ticket sales and public money, and the one fossil fuel tie found, Shell, ended in June 2020. The weak points are its Bloomberg Philanthropies partnership, which is the target of a May 2026 open letter, and the lack of a funder list that could be read. Watch for any response from the theatre and for its current annual accounts.
