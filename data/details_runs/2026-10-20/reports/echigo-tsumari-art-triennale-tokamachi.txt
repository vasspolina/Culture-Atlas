ECHIGO-TSUMARI ART TRIENNALE: Tokamachi, Japan
ID: echigo-tsumari-art-triennale-tokamachi
Website: https://www.echigo-tsumari.jp/en/

OVERVIEW
- Outdoor art triennale spread across the Echigo-Tsumari region of Niigata Prefecture, in the towns of Tokamachi and Tsunan, an area of 760 square kilometres (2024 summary report).
- Created by the Tokyo commercial gallery Art Front Gallery and directed by Fram Kitagawa. The first edition ran from 20 July to 10 September 2000, with 153 works. The Niigata prefectural government had developed the Art Necklace Plan for rural revitalisation in 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Artworks occupy vacant traditional houses, abandoned schools, storefronts and terraced rice fields, in response to rural depopulation and an ageing population (Kyoto Journal, Amiko Matsuo, 18 March 2015).
- The 2024 edition (the ninth) ran from 13 July to 10 November, 89 days, with 312 artworks by 278 artists and groups from 42 countries and regions (2024 summary report). Total visits were 545,931.
- Organisers of the 2024 edition: the Echigo-Tsumari Art Triennale executive committee, the NPO Echigo-Tsumari Satoyama Collaborative Organisation, the Japan Arts Council and the Agency for Cultural Affairs. The mayor of Tokamachi chairs the committee and the general producer is Soichiro Fukutake (honorary adviser of Benesse Holdings and honorary chairman of the Fukutake Foundation).
- Visiting in 2024: open 10:00 to 17:00 (to 16:00 in October and November), closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays. The general passport cost 4,500 yen on the day and 3,500 yen in advance (2024 summary report).

FUNDING
Echigo-Tsumari Art Triennale: public and local money: Tokamachi and Tsunan, the Agency for Cultural Affairs and the Japan Arts Council, plus tickets and donations
- The committee's 2024 income was JPY 359.9 million (359,886 thousand yen) and JPY 623.2 million across fiscal 2022 to 2024 (2024 summary report).
- Tokamachi City paid JPY 39.0 million and Tsunan Town JPY 5.1 million in 2024; the Agency for Cultural Affairs and the Japan Arts Council paid JPY 70.0 million.
- Donations and sponsorship came to JPY 91.6 million in 2024, JPY 41.0 million was drawn from the Echigo-Tsumari fund, passports and tickets brought JPY 105.3 million, and official tours JPY 5.0 million.
- Donation detail as of 15 January 2025, with the previous edition in brackets: 30 companies, corporations and individuals gave JPY 21.0 million (49 gave JPY 57.4 million); 4 passport purchase sponsors gave JPY 4.0 million; 17,913 individual furusato nozei (hometown tax) donors gave JPY 263.4 million to Tokamachi in fiscal 2023 and 2024, after return gifts and portal fees; 15 companies gave JPY 25.9 million through the corporate furusato nozei scheme in fiscal 2022 to 2024; the total across all categories was JPY 319.3 million.
- Corporate supporters named for 2024: East Japan Railway Company (JR East), Tokyo Electric Power Company (TEPCO) Niigata head office, and four Niigata prefectural regional offices. Their support was artwork reception, festival information and passport sales during the festival.
- TEPCO Niigata head office staff handled artwork reception from 13 July to 31 August 2024, 72 people in total, and TEPCO's Shinano River power station connecting water tank hosted Nikita Kadan's work "Things from Another Place" (title translated). JR East staff handled reception from 13 July to 10 November 2024, 159 people in total (2024 summary report appendix).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the triennale is public and local: in 2024 Tokamachi and Tsunan paid JPY 44.1 million and the Agency for Cultural Affairs with the Japan Arts Council JPY 70.0 million, against JPY 105.3 million from passports and tickets and JPY 91.6 million in donations and sponsorship, with hometown-tax gifts of JPY 263.4 million routed through Tokamachi. TEPCO and JR East take part as corporate supporters with staff time and, for TEPCO, a venue.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critical points come from academic studies summarised on Wikipedia, which question how the festival affects rural life.
1. Wikipedia: summary of the academic critics Susanne Klien and Thekla Boven
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echigo-Tsumari_Art_Triennial
- Wikipedia says Klien argues that the artist and community collaboration could infringe upon the lifestyles and smooth operation of rural communities, and suggests it leads to "the nostalgic romanticization of rural life expected by urban audiences" rather than genuine cooperation.
- Wikipedia says Boven studied the reuse of 11 closed elementary schools in Tokamachi as exhibition spaces and concluded that it "may not be the most effective use of these edifices in their local context".
2. Hyperallergic: Ysabelle Cheung, "Revitalizing a Dying Region of Rural Japan with Art" (4 September 2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/revitalizing-a-dying-region-of-rural-japan-with-art
- Feature on the festival. It reports that younger people have left for Japan's cities, leaving mostly elderly farmers, and that Kitagawa says the population is still falling.
- Kitagawa says villagers were initially hesitant and organisers had to visit many homes to win them over.
