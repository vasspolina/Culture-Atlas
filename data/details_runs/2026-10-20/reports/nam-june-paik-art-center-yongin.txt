NAM JUNE PAIK ART CENTER: Yongin, South Korea
ID: nam-june-paik-art-center-yongin
Website: https://njp.ggcf.kr/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads in Korean, with an English site at njpart.ggcf.kr)

OVERVIEW
- Public art centre in Giheung-gu, Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, at 10 Paiknamjune-ro, reached from Giheung station (Wikipedia; centre site).
- The basic plan was set with the artist Nam June Paik in November 2001; the building opened with a Nam June Paik Festival on 8 October 2008 (Gyeonggi Province English site). Paik died before it opened (JoongAng Daily, 2010).
- Named for Nam June Paik, the Korean American video artist; its permanent collection is of his work, with temporary exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Kirsten Schemel and Marina Stankovic of KSMS Schemel Stankovic Architects, Berlin, who won a 2003 international competition of 430 entries (centre site). Three storeys above ground and two below, about 5,600 square metres on a 30,983 square metre site, with a screen facade of layered reflective and partly printed glass.
- Holds galleries, a video archive and multi-purpose spaces (centre site). It awards the Nam June Paik Art Center Prize, first given in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 6pm (until 7pm in July and August), closed Mondays, 1 January and Thanksgiving. Adults 4,000 won; students and soldiers 2,000 won; under 6, over 66 and people with disabilities free (Gyeonggi Province English site, undated). The centre's own site lists sign language interpretation for exhibition guides. No reporting found of further accessibility details.

FUNDING
Nam June Paik Art Center: public: Gyeonggi Province through the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation, with Hyundai Motor as sponsor of its exhibition series
- The centre is run by the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation (centre site footer). The site states that individual donations and corporate and organisational sponsorship support its programmes.
- Hyundai Motor: sponsor of the Hyundai Translocal Series, launched in 2025, in which the centre and the Pinacoteca de Sao Paulo co-organise an exhibition opening in November 2026 (Hyundai Motor Group, 23 June 2026; Herald).
- Gyeonggi Ilbo (23 June 2025) says Hyundai Motor's sponsorship raised the number of 2025 exhibitions to four and gives a 2025 exhibition budget of about 510 million won. It states no Hyundai amount or duration.
- No reporting found of the amount or term of Hyundai's sponsorship in any source opened. Hyundai's announcements do not disclose funding terms.
- No reporting found of the centre's own accounts; the foundation's PDF documents could not be read.

Red flags: Hyundai and child labour at a subsidiary
- July 2022: Reuters reported that SMART Alabama, a Hyundai subsidiary in Luverne, Alabama, hired minors as young as 12; a former employee estimated about 50 (Wikipedia, summarising Reuters). SMART supplies parts to Hyundai's Montgomery plant.
- Hyundai said it does not tolerate illegal employment; the US Department of Labor and Alabama fined other suppliers, and neither state labour department accused Hyundai or Kia of wrongdoing (Wikipedia).
- 24 February 2023: Hyundai CEO Jae Hoon Chang announced Hyundai would divest its controlling stake in SMART (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly Gyeonggi Province's public budget, with Hyundai Motor as the sponsor of its exhibition series. The weak points are the undisclosed size and term of the Hyundai sponsorship and Hyundai's 2022 to 2023 child-labour case at its Alabama subsidiary, which Hyundai exited in 2023. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes sponsor amounts for the Translocal Series.
