THAPONG VISUAL ARTS CENTRE: Gaborone, Botswana
ID: thapong-visual-arts-centre-gaborone
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Membership-driven visual arts organisation in Gaborone with a gallery, exhibitions, studios and workshops; since 2002 it has hosted international artists on residencies of a few months (Contemporary And).
- Contemporary And gives 1998 as its founding year. Mmegi (2016) dates the origin earlier: the first Thapong International Artists' Workshop was held in Kanye in November 1989, and in 1999 the workshop set up a centre in an old building in Gaborone Village donated by the National Museum and Art Gallery.
- SOAS Gallery lists the Thapong workshops among the Triangle Arts Trust's International Artists Workshops, a network directed by Robert Loder (SOAS, "Transitions" exhibition page).
- The gallery is a colonial-era house that once housed colonial officials; a coffee shop was built in front of it and six large studios were added (Mmegi, 24 July 2014). The refurbishment of the gallery, studios and café was conceived in 2009, carried out from September 2013 and finished in April 2014 (Daily News).
- It runs the Thapong Artist of the Year Awards (TAYA), introduced in 2004 and open to members in good standing (Mmegi, 2016 and 2021).
- Reginald Bakwena is coordinator, called director by Mmegi in 2024, and sat on the board of the newly created Arts Council in 2021 (Mmegi). Mesh Moeti was acting chairman in October 2024 (Fantastic Moments).
- Recent programme: "Resilience: The Unbreakable Human Spirit", ten artists curated by Thabo Kgatlwane with the Embassy of Ukraine in Botswana, 1 to 13 September 2026 (Mmegi, 4 September 2026).

FUNDING
Thapong Visual Arts Centre: mixed: Botswana's culture ministry and corporate foundations, with diamond-trade sponsors for its buildings and events
- 2013 to 2014 refurbishment: the acting culture minister Vincent Seretse gave the funders as the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Culture P2.2 million, Diamond Trading Company (DTC) Botswana P700,000, the FNB Foundation P250,000, De Beers P75,000 and Collegium Book Publishers P10,000, with Plascon donating paint worth P10,000 (Daily News, August 2014). Mmegi reported earlier figures of P1.5 million from the ministry for studios and P654,908 from DTC for the gallery, with the FNB Foundation paying for the coffee shop (Mmegi, 30 October 2013), and later put the cost of renovating the old gallery at P250,000 (Mmegi, 24 July 2014).
- The ministry funds art associations to help run the President's Day Competitions (Daily News, 2014); in 2021 it did not hold them, which Thapong said cut into its own programme (Mmegi, 17 October 2021).
- BTC Foundation: a three-year partnership worth P1.7 million, signed in 2018, covering the BTC Phonebook cover competition, TAYA, training for art teachers, and administration and curator fees; BTC had sponsored the phonebook competition before and added TAYA in 2016 (Daily News, 23 September 2018). Winners of each competition usually receive P50,000 including an exchange programme (Mmegi, 2021).
- FNBB Foundation: sponsorship valued at P2.5 million, launched in October 2024 with the online store Botswana Arts Online and nationwide workshops for artists; the First Lady, Neo Masisi, gave the keynote (Fantastic Moments, 30 October 2024).
- HB Botswana: a three-year partnership from 2023 with a rotating exhibition of members' work at HB Botswana's Gaborone premises; HB pays artists a fee to rent the works on display, and artists keep sales proceeds (Mmegi, 12 April 2024; Daily News, April 2024).
- British Council Southern Africa and British Council Botswana paid three emerging artists P30,000 each for new commissioned works shown at Thapong in 2019 (Mmegi, 22 July 2019).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: state money and corporate foundation grants, none of them from a flagged industry.
