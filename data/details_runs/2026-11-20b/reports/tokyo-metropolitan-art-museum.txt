TOKYO METROPOLITAN ART MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: tokyo-metropolitan-art-museum
Website: https://www.tobikan.jp/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public art museum at 8-36 Ueno Park, Taito-ku, Tokyo, seven minutes' walk from the Park Gate of JR Ueno station (museum website).
- It opened on 1 May 1926 as the Tokyo Prefectural Art Museum, Japan's first public art museum, after the coal industrialist Sato Keitaro (1868 to 1940), known as the "god of coal" (石炭の神様, own translation), moved by a 1922 newspaper article calling for a permanent museum, gave 1 million yen to build it (museum website, centenary exhibition page).
- Buildings: the first, with columns and a grand staircase, was by Okada Shin'ichiro; Maekawa Kunio designed the 1975 replacement, with the main floor below ground and several blocks around a central plaza (museum website).
- Role: mainly a venue for art societies' exhibitions and touring shows; its collection and art library went to the new Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo in 1995, and it resumed its own programme after the 2012 renewal, under the mission "entrance to art" (museum website).
- Centenary in 2026: "What a Wonderful World: Masterworks from the Musée d'Orsay" (14 November 2026 to 28 March 2027), "Ways of Reading" and an exhibition on the museum's own history (19 November 2026 to 11 January 2027) (museum website).
- Visiting: museum entry is free and exhibition prices vary; open 9:30 to 17:30, Fridays to 20:00 during special and thematic exhibitions; closed on the first and third Monday, and on Mondays for special exhibitions (museum website).

FUNDING
Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum: public: the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, with newspaper and corporate partners for big shows
- Founding gift: Sato Keitaro's 1 million yen from his fortune in the coal industry paid for the original building (museum website).
- Operator: the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture runs the museum (museum website); the Tokyo Metropolitan Government calls it one of its policy partner bodies (Tokyo Metropolitan Government, 11 September 2026).
- In September 2026 the government chose the foundation without open competition for 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2037, citing its ties to the city's culture policy and its Arts Council Tokyo; the appointment needs the assembly's vote (Tokyo Metropolitan Government).
- Big exhibitions are co-organised with media companies: the Orsay show with Nikkei and TV Tokyo, sponsored by SMBC Nikko Securities, Obayashi, Dai-ichi Life, Daikin Industries, Dai Nippon Printing, Daiwa House, Takasago Thermal Engineering and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, with Japan Airlines and Nippon Cargo Airlines in cooperation (museum website).
- Newspapers have backed exhibitions here since the 1927 Asahi show of Meiji and Taisho masterpieces (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: a city museum run by the city's own culture foundation, with Japanese companies sponsoring the big loan shows. The coal fortune behind the 1926 building is history, not current money.
