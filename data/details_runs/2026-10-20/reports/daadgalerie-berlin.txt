DAADGALERIE: Berlin, Germany
ID: daadgalerie-berlin
Website: https://www.berliner-kuenstlerprogramm.de

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition space of the Berliner Künstlerprogramm (BKP) of the DAAD, the German Academic Exchange Service, a residency programme for international artists and cultural workers. Projects from the programme are shown in the daadgalerie and at other venues in Berlin, across Germany and abroad (stated by the programme).
- The programme began in 1963 as a Ford Foundation initiative; the DAAD took it over in 1965 and renamed it the Artists-in-Berlin Program (Wikipedia). The gallery opened in 1978 in Henny Porten's former villa on Kurfürstenstraße in West Berlin and moved to Zimmerstraße near Checkpoint Charlie in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- In January 2017 the gallery moved to Oranienstraße 161 in Kreuzberg, a former shoe department store built in 1910/12 by the architect Oskar Kaufmann. It has about 500 square metres over two floors: a 250 m² ground-floor gallery and a first-floor studio for about 100 visitors (taz, Linda Gerner, 17 January 2017).
- The programme says it leaves Oranienstraße 161 by 30 September 2026. From 3 October its new season runs at the Grüner Salon of the Volksbühne, Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz, 10178 Berlin, and at other Berlin venues.
- The programme shows exhibitions, readings, concerts, film screenings and performances by current and former guests of the programme.
- Visiting: the Grüner Salon is currently not wheelchair accessible, for structural reasons (stated by the programme). Events there have been listed with free admission.

FUNDING
daadgalerie: public programme: run by the DAAD with the German Federal Foreign Office and the Berlin Senate
- The DAAD runs the programme with help from the German Federal Foreign Office and the Senate of Berlin, an arrangement in place since 1965 (Wikipedia).
- The Ford Foundation created the programme in 1963 with an initial donation of three million US dollars (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The daadgalerie belongs to the Berliner Künstlerprogramm, which the DAAD runs with the assistance of the German Federal Foreign Office and the Senate of Berlin (Wikipedia). The Ford Foundation began the programme in 1963 with an initial donation of three million US dollars. The programme leaves Oranienstraße 161 on 30 September 2026.
