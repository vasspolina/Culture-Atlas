ID: dalhousie-art-gallery-halifax

WIDER CONTEXT
- Spring 1992: the gallery showed No Laughing Matter, an Independent Curators International exhibition brought in by its curator Sue Gibson Garvey, including photo-text works from Carrie Mae Weems's series Ain't Jokin'. Black Dalhousie students, among them the Black Canadian Students Association of Dalhousie, and then a growing part of the public called for the works to be removed (Canadian Art, Sarah Hollenberg, "Dirty Words: Sensitive", 9 May 2018).
- The students' association wrote that the work "perpetuates hatred and restrictive stereotypes"; the university's employment equity officer and Black student advisor wrote that they found it "offensive and degrading to black people". Students held a sit-in (Canadian Art).
- The gallery, directed by Mern O'Brien, kept the works up; before the opening, "Removing these works from the exhibition was not considered." Staff posted the students' letter beside the works, offered benches for the sit-in, refused to let students block the view and increased security (Canadian Art).
- University president Howard Clark said he upheld freedom of expression and, in an editorial in the Dalhousie News, distanced himself from both the gallery and Weems's work. "The works stayed up, despite mounting negative attention from local politicians" (Canadian Art).
