SCIENCE MUSEUM: London, UK
ID: science-museum-london
Website: https://www.sciencemuseum.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- National science museum on Exhibition Road, South Kensington, London SW7 2DD, part of the Science Museum Group of five museums.
- Founded in 1857 from the Royal Society of Arts collection and surplus items from the Great Exhibition; it became independent in 1909 (Wikipedia).
- Sir Richard Allison designed the present building, which opened in stages from 1919 to 1928; the Wellcome Wing was added in 2000 (Wikipedia).
- Galleries include the Energy Hall, Exploring Space, Making the Modern World, Medicine: Wellcome Galleries, Wonderlab, Flight and an IMAX cinema (Wikipedia).
- It had 2,817,852 visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 10.00 to 18.00, closed 24 to 26 December; free entry with a free ticket booked online; Wonderlab, Power Up and IMAX are paid extras (museum website).

FUNDING
Science Museum: mainly public with corporate sponsors: DCMS grant, donations and sponsors
- Science Museum Group, year ending March 2023: total income 128 million pounds; 63% was grant in aid from the Department for Culture, Media and Sport; donations, grants and sponsors gave 24.4 million pounds (19%), including 13.6 million in donations and legacies; trading income was 20.1 million (Civil Society, 16 August 2023).
- Adani Green Energy: sponsors the Energy Revolution gallery, announced in 2021 and opened in March 2024; no amount is published (Museums Association).
- BP: sponsored the museum for about 25 years, including the former Energy Gallery, the 2015 Cosmonauts exhibition and the museum's training academy. The partnership ended on 31 August 2026 "at the end of its agreed term"; the Science Museum Group says terms and amounts are "commercially sensitive" (The Art Newspaper).
- Equinor: sponsorship ended in 2024 (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of a list of sponsors for 2023-24; the report was published 22 November 2024 but its sponsor pages were not read.

Red flags: BP sponsorship, fossil fuels
- BP's partnership ran about 25 years and ended on 31 August 2026 (The Art Newspaper).
- Culture Unstained, a campaign group, says a teacher-backed boycott supported by the National Education Union and a staff union survey preceded the decision (Culture Unstained, 28 August 2026).
- The museum gave no reason beyond the agreed term and said reports of a ten-year deal to 2028 were inaccurate (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Adani Green Energy, fossil fuels
- Adani Green is part of the Adani Group, described as the world's largest private developer of coal mines (The Art Newspaper).
- 23 March 2024: more than 150 protesters hung a 12-metre banner in the Energy Hall at the gallery opening (Museums Association).
- 2021: two trustees and adviser Chris Rapley resigned over the deal; campaigners say a due diligence report on Adani's coal mining, environmental and human rights impacts was buried (Museums Association).
- Unverified: that Adani Group companies manipulated their share prices, an allegation by Hindenburg Research, reported by USA Art News (2023); Adani-linked companies lost more than 100 billion dollars in share value.
- The museum's CEO Ian Blatchford said trustees disagree with calls for wholesale disengagement from entire sectors (Museums Association).

Red flags: campaigns
- Unverified: that BP's Cosmonauts sponsorship promoted its stake in Rosneft and that Blatchford received Russia's Pushkin Medal from Putin for hosting the exhibition, from Culture Unstained, an openly anti-oil-sponsorship group.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is mainly funded by the state, with sponsors providing about a fifth of income in 2022-23. The weak points are Adani Green's continuing sponsorship, whose group is described as the largest private coal-mine developer, and BP's 25-year record, which ended on 31 August 2026. Watch whether the Adani deal ends and whether sponsor terms are ever published.
