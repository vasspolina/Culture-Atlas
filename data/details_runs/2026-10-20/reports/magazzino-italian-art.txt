MAGAZZINO ITALIAN ART: Cold Spring, USA
ID: magazzino-italian-art
Website: https://www.magazzino.art (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and research center for postwar and contemporary Italian art at 2700 Route 9, Cold Spring, New York, in the Hudson Valley (museum website), about 80 km north of New York (Artribune).
- Founded by the married collectors Nancy Olnick and Giorgio Spanu; inaugurated in June 2017 with the exhibition "Rebel with a cause" (museum website). Wikipedia gives the public opening as 28 June 2017.
- "Magazzino" is Italian for "warehouse." Wikipedia says the building was a computer chip factory; The Art Newspaper (2017) calls it a former dairy distribution centre.
- Spanish architect Miguel Quismondo converted and extended the building to more than 18,000 sq ft of exhibition space with a courtyard and library (The Art Newspaper). The Robert Olnick Pavilion, designed by Alberto Campo Baeza and Quismondo, opened in September 2023 and is dedicated to Nancy Olnick's father (museum website; La Voce di New York).
- On site: galleries with a permanent Arte Povera display, a Research Center, an Education Center, Spazio Aperto, a store and Café Silvia (museum website).
- Visiting: the pages read gave no opening hours. The museum was free at opening; Wikipedia states that admission fees began in 2023 and gives no price. Accessibility: no reporting found.

FUNDING
Magazzino Italian Art: self-funded: the founders' own money and gifts to a foundation they control
- At opening the space was privately funded by Olnick and Spanu (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- It became a foundation in 2018, and its executive director, Vittorio Calabrese, said the aim was to grow support through memberships and corporate partnerships (La Voce di New York, 2023).
- The Magazzino Italian Art Foundation (EIN 83-1666946) is a tax-exempt organisation with its exemption issued in July 2019 (ProPublica). ProPublica shows contributions received of $12.6M in 2019, $8.5M in 2020, $3.3M in 2021, $7.0M in 2022, $9.1M in 2023 and $3.6M in 2024, with net assets of $25.4M at the end of 2024; the range is $3.3M (2021) to $12.6M (2019). Only the original 2018 Form 990-PF is listed, with no extracted figures.
- ProPublica returns a 404 (checked 2026-10-25) for EIN 81-4144498, so no figures here come from that number.
- Trustees named on the 2024 filing: Nancy Olnick, Giorgio Spanu and Stella Spanu; Diana Schulte is secretary and treasurer (ProPublica).
- Nancy Olnick is the daughter of New York construction magnate Robert Olnick (La Voce di New York).
- No reporting found of the individual donors behind the contributions; the pages read name none.
- No reporting found of government funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the founders' own, passed through a foundation they sit on, and no flagged funder appears in any source read. The weak points are that the foundation's donors are not named in any source read and that no source read states where the founders' wealth comes from. Worth watching: the donor list as corporate partnerships grow.
