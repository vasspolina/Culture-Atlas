THE ALDRICH CONTEMPORARY ART MUSEUM: Ridgefield, USA
ID: aldrich-contemporary-art-museum-ridgefield
Website: https://thealdrich.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting contemporary art museum at 258 Main Street, Ridgefield, Connecticut; it describes itself as the only museum in Connecticut dedicated solely to contemporary art (stated by the museum).
- Founded in 1964 by the art collector and fashion designer Larry Aldrich (1906 to 2001) as the Larry Aldrich Museum; renamed the Aldrich Museum of Contemporary Art in 1967 and The Aldrich Contemporary Art Museum in 2004 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder. Aldrich sold his own art collection to fund the museum (Wikipedia).
- Building: the original 1783 "Old Hundred" building housed the museum from 1964 to 2003 and now holds the offices; since 2004 the museum has occupied a 17,000 square foot building designed by Tappé Associates of Boston, with architect Charles Mark Hay (the museum; Wikipedia).
- On site: rotating exhibitions, a sculpture garden, a studio space, a shop, and school, teen, family and summer camp programmes.
- Visiting: open Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday 12 to 5 pm and Saturday 10 am to 5 pm; closed Tuesday. Adults $20, seniors (60+) $15, students $9; free for members, educators, children under 13, military families, SNAP or EBT cardholders (up to four visitors) and on the third Saturday of each month. Step-free entrance, elevators to both floors, wheelchairs on request, free parking.

FUNDING
The Aldrich: mainly private giving: individual donors, family foundations and banks, with a state arts grant
- Annual finances (US tax filings via ProPublica): 2024 revenue $5.54M, expenses $3.47M, contributions $3.46M (62.5% of revenue), net assets $47.4M. 2023 revenue $4.60M, expenses $3.31M, contributions $3.17M. 2022 revenue $2.29M, expenses $3.11M.
- The museum's supporters page lists gifts of $1,000 or more received from 1 January to 31 December 2025, without amounts: Bank of America, Fairfield County Bank, the Connecticut Department of Economic and Community Development Office of the Arts, Connecticut Humanities, the Steven & Alexandra Cohen Foundation, the Anne S. Richardson Fund, the Cornelia T. Bailey Foundation, the David T. Langrock Foundation, the Denise and Michael Kellen Foundation, the Gage Fund, the Gary and Susie Singer Arts & Culture Foundation, the Goldstone Family Foundation, The Goodnow Fund, the Milton and Sally Avery Arts Foundation, the Pamela F. and Robert J. Morganti Charitable Foundation, the Ridgefield Rotary Club, the Ruth Krauss Foundation and the Wadsworth Russell Lewis Trust Fund, plus individuals. The supporters page lists 164 individual entries.
- Further foundations on the supporters page, listed without a year: the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, the Cowles Charitable Trust, the Robert Lehman Foundation, the Perlmutter Family Foundation and Phileas (The Austrian Office for Contemporary Art).
- Corporate sponsors named without a year: Freixenet Mionetto USA, Litchfield Distillery, Ethan Allen, Strada Education Network, WSHU Public Radio Group, and the galleries Hauser & Wirth, Matthew Marks, Paula Cooper, David Kordansky and Andrew Edlin.
- The founder's fortune came from the dress trade: Aldrich entered the fashion trade in 1924 (Wikipedia).
- Board officers: chair Diana Bowes, vice-chair Andrew J. Pitts, treasurer Linda M. Dugan, secretary Julie Phillips (the museum).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Hyperallergic (Aaron Short, "Connecticut's Artists Have Been Hiding in Plain Sight", 14 July 2026) covers the museum's inaugural decennial survey of 40 Connecticut artists in a favourable feature.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Link to the museum: the Steven & Alexandra Cohen Foundation is credited on the museum's supporters page for "generous support for The Aldrich's exhibitions and education programs", among gifts of $1,000 or more received from 1 January to 31 December 2025.
- Wikipedia (Steve Cohen) says S.A.C. Capital Advisors, Steven A. Cohen's firm, pleaded guilty to insider trading charges in 2013 and paid $1.8 billion in penalties. Cohen avoided criminal indictment himself; the SEC sued him over failure to supervise Mathew Martoma and Michael Steinberg, and he settled in January 2016. Wikipedia says he founded Point72 Asset Management in 2014 as a family office.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Aldrich is ordinary American museum funding: individual donors, family foundations, two banks, a state arts office and gallery sponsors, with a founder's fortune from fashion that was spent on the museum's start. The one name to know is the Cohen Foundation, whose namesake's firm pleaded guilty to insider trading charges in 2013 (Wikipedia).
