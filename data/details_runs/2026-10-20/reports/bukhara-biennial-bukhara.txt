BUKHARA BIENNIAL: Bukhara, Uzbekistan
ID: bukhara-biennial-bukhara
Website: https://bukharabiennial.uz/en/bb2025 (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art biennial held in newly restored historic buildings across Bukhara, a UNESCO Creative City of Craft and Folk Art (stated by the space).
- First edition, "Recipes for Broken Hearts", ran from 5 September to 23 November 2025, with artistic director Diana Campbell (stated by the space).
- Produced by the Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation (ACDF); commissioner Gayane Umerova chairs the ACDF and the Uzbekistan National Commission for UNESCO (stated by the space; New Eastern Europe).
- Uzbek, Central Asian and international artists showed site-specific works, with events, performances and food programmes (stated by the space).
- Between 1.6 and 1.8 million people attended (New Eastern Europe, 2026).
- Visiting: admission was free (Hyperallergic).

FUNDING
Bukhara Biennial: state-run: the ACDF with state bodies and corporate partners, led by Bukhara Oil
- The ACDF is state-funded (Hyperallergic) and state-formed (Artforum); President Shavkat Mirziyoyev signed the authorising documents (New Eastern Europe).
- 2025 Lead Partners: the Tourism Committee of Uzbekistan, the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Uzbekistan and Bukhara Oil (stated by the space).
- 2025 Partners: Acwa Power, Agrobank, Apex Insurance, Media City, Ocard, Uzcard, Uzum, Vision Invest and Yandex Uzbekistan (stated by the space, no amounts).
- New Eastern Europe called the funds "exorbitant by Uzbek standards" but gave no figure. No reporting found of the budget or any partner's amount.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Bukhara Oil)
- "Bukhara Oil" is one of three Lead Partners of the first edition (stated by the space).
- The Bukhara Oil Refinery, in operation since 1997 with about 50,000 barrels a day of capacity, is run by Bukhara Refinery, a subsidiary of the state oil and gas company Uzbekneftegaz (NS Energy).
- The partners page does not give the company's legal name, so the match to the refinery rests on the name alone.

Red flags: an autocratic state's flagship
- The ACDF is led by Umerova, a close associate of Saida Mirziyoyeva, the president's daughter and number two in his administration (Artforum; New Eastern Europe).
- Uzbekistan ranked 148th in the 2025 Reporters Without Borders index, and blogger Otabek Sattori was sentenced to six years (New Eastern Europe).
- The organisers did not answer Hyperallergic's questions about government involvement (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Reviews praise the art and criticise the state behind it as artwashing; Hyperallergic and New Eastern Europe make the fullest case.
1. Hyperallergic: Nevdon Jamgochian, "The Bukhara Biennial Will Break Your Heart" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1052634/the-bukhara-biennial-will-break-your-heart
- The art is strong but the biennial is part of an "artwashing" system: "the autocratic government that funded it lurks in the shadows."
- Cites Amnesty International on censorship and the violent suppression of the 2022 Karakalpakstan protests, and UNESCO's worry that state development could destroy the old city; Alerte Héritage called the plan "catastrophic in every respect".
- Calls the event "an oasis in a desert of repression".
2. New Eastern Europe: Anna Gzubicka, "Bukhara delights with art, while the authorities count political dividends" (2026)
URL: https://neweasterneurope.eu/2026/02/20/bukhara-delights-with-art-while-the-authorities-count-political-dividends/
- Curator Timur Karpov called it a project of "international PR and artwashing", weak as art and strong as politics.
- Foreign coverage was mostly praise, as with Qatar's World Cup and Saudi Arabia's biennial.
3. Artforum: Rachel Wetzler, column on the biennial (2025)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/columns/bukhara-biennial-on-site-rachel-wetzler-1234736624/
- Places the biennial inside state-formed ACDF programming and ties Umerova to Saida Mirziyoyeva; reports a delayed Tashkent centre opening and an exhibition quietly dropped from a preview tour.
- Paywalled; only excerpts were read.

BOTTOM LINE
The biennial is a state project of the Mirziyoyev government, run by a foundation close to the president's daughter, with a state oil refiner's name among its lead partners. State money alone is not the issue; the weak points are the fossil-fuel lead partner and the artwashing case made in the art press. Worth watching: the budget, the legal identity of "Bukhara Oil", and the second edition's partners.
