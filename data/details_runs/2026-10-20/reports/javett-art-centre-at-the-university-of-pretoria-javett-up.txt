JAVETT ART CENTRE AT THE UNIVERSITY OF PRETORIA (JAVETT-UP): Pretoria, South Africa
ID: javett-art-centre-at-the-university-of-pretoria-javett-up
Website: https://javettup.com/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads, with a banner saying the centre is open during construction)

OVERVIEW
- Art centre at 23 Lynnwood Road, Pretoria, on the University of Pretoria campus; a bridge gallery links it to the main campus across Lynnwood Road (Javett-UP; ART AFRICA).
- A partnership between the University of Pretoria and the Javett Foundation; it opened in September 2019 with the inaugural exhibition "All in a Day's Eye: The Politics of Innocence" (Hyperallergic, 9 October 2019).
- Michael Javett, who founded the Javett Foundation, wanted to make the Javett family's art collection more accessible to the public and to academics (ART AFRICA, 13 February 2019). No source opened states the origin of the name.
- Building: designed by Pieter Matthews & Associates, with AECOM as constructing engineers and Anton Coetzee as project manager (Javett-UP); cast in concrete, its form draws on rock formations at the Mapungubwe site (ART AFRICA).
- On site: nine exhibition spaces including the Mapungubwe gallery, which displays the Golden Rhino (Javett-UP), and a student gallery beneath the bridge (ART AFRICA).
- Visiting: the website's visit page shows an "Entry Fees" heading without figures. No reporting found of opening days, admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
Javett-UP: university and private foundation: the University of Pretoria and the Javett Foundation
- ART AFRICA (13 February 2019) calls the centre a "joint venture" and says the University of Pretoria would provide land and funding, with the Javett Foundation also providing funding.
- The Javett Foundation helped fund the building and has committed to further annual donations over the next decade; the page gives no amounts (Javett-UP).
- The Javett Foundation is a South African philanthropic trust established in 2013 by Michael Javett, consolidating his and his family's earlier giving; he died in 2022. It lists Javett-UP as a beneficiary without a sum and does not accept unsolicited funding applications (Javett Foundation). Trustees: Catherine Kennedy, Leigh Bregman, Pfunzo Sidogi and Stewart Shaw Taylor.
- No reporting found of amounts, of other donors, of accounts or of a board for the centre itself.

Red flags: protest over a convicted murderer's artwork in the opening exhibition
- The opening exhibition, curated by Gabi Ngcobo, included Zwelethu Mthethwa's 1996 pastel "The Wedding Party". Mthethwa is serving an 18-year sentence for killing Nokuphila Kumalo, a 23-year-old sex worker, and maintains his innocence (Hyperallergic, 9 October 2019).
- 26 September 2019: the curators phoned Lesego Tlhwale of SWEAT (Sex Worker Education and Advocacy Taskforce). 28 September: SWEAT sent an open letter calling for the work's removal. 30 September: the curators invited SWEAT to a public dialogue. 1 October: SWEAT responded publicly, endorsed by women's rights organisations (Ngcobo and research team, Magazine C&, 18 October 2019).
- SWEAT's petition for removal had over 900 signatures by 7 October 2019; its start date is not given in the sources opened (Hyperallergic).
- Artist Candice Breitz asked that her video installation "Profile" be switched off; the centre unplugged it but declined to display a #SayHerName poster in its place (Hyperallergic).
- The centre said it would not remove the painting and revised and expanded the placard. Ngcobo said the work was shown as "evidence" of misogyny in Mthethwa's art (Hyperallergic).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is a university and a family philanthropic trust, with no flagged industry or state funder found. The weak points are that no amounts, other donors or accounts are published, and that keeping Mthethwa's work on view in 2019 drew a documented protest. Worth watching: the size of the Javett Foundation's ten-year pledge.
