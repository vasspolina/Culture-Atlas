ARETE FOUNDATION FOR ARTS AND CULTURE: Tripoli, Libya
ID: arete-foundation-for-arts-and-culture-tripoli

OVERVIEW
- Libyan non-profit arts organisation in Tripoli, founded in 2012, a few months after the 2011 revolution (Arab Fund for Arts and Culture, AFAC); Newsweek calls it an NGO.
- Founded by the poet Khaled Mattawa and his wife, the artist Reem Gibriel (MERIP interview, 17 Nov 2021); they moved to Tripoli in January 2012 (Hisham Matar in Newsweek).
- Named for Arete of Cyrene, a Greek-Libyan philosopher (University of Michigan LSA, 17 Sep 2014); Newsweek gives the name's meaning as knowledge, excellence in work, or living up to one's highest human potential.
- Programmes: a cinema club, gallery exhibits, a reading series, a staged production of Ibsen's "An Enemy of the People", a professionalism workshop for young artists, and an annual video art show described as the first of its kind in the country (University of Michigan LSA); Mimeta (15 Dec 2014) says the cinema club had held 60 screenings.
- Helped set up the first Tripoli International Poetry Festival in April 2012 (Newsweek). "First Glance", described as Libya's first video art exhibit, ran on 6 and 7 November 2012 at Old City Works in Tripoli's Old City, organised by Arete with Gibriel as executive director (Jadaliyya).
- Still active: on 2 March 2023 it held a symposium on the first issue of the book "Room 211" at Art House (Dar Al Funoon), Tripoli, with the Libyan Association for Literature and Arts and Dar Al-Ferjani (Libyan Events listing).

FUNDING
Arete Foundation for Arts and Culture: grant-funded: Arab and European arts funds, local businesses and the Libyan Ministry of Culture, as named by its founder
- AFAC lists three Arete projects: "Cultural Program 2012" (AFAC Express, 2012), "Poems for Life" (2016) and the North Africa Cultural Program National Fund (2019).
- Mattawa names Arete's funding sources as AFAC and Al-Mawred Al-Thaqafi, "later the British Council and Prince Claus Fund", local businesses and the Libyan Ministry of Culture (MERIP, interview with Atef Said, 17 Nov 2021). He also says the Libyan state "was and remains the only funder (controller and censor) of artistic endeavors."
- The University of Michigan article (2014) says Arete struggled to get funding and attention in a country where the arts were neglected.

BOTTOM LINE
None of the funders Mattawa names belongs to a flagged industry or is a sanctioned or contested donor. They are Arab and European arts funds, local businesses and the Libyan Ministry of Culture, and AFAC's grantee page lists three grants between 2012 and 2019. Worth watching is the Ministry of Culture's funding, since Mattawa describes the state as controller and censor of artistic endeavors.
