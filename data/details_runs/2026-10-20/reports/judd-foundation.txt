JUDD FOUNDATION: Marfa, USA
ID: judd-foundation
Website: https://juddfoundation.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit that maintains and preserves Donald Judd's permanently installed living and working spaces, libraries and archives at 101 Spring Street in New York and in Marfa, Texas (stated by the space).
- Set up by Judd's estate in 1996, two years after his death; his will asked that the New York and Marfa properties be kept as he had installed them (Roberta Smith, New York Times; ARTnews). Rainer Judd is President and Flavin Judd Artistic Director; Fairfax Dorn and Ellen Salpeter co-chair the board.
- 101 Spring Street is a five-storey cast-iron building of 1870, bought by Judd in 1968; its restoration began in 2010 and it opened to the public in June 2013 (stated by the space). ARTnews put the cost of the overhaul at an estimated $23 million.
- In Marfa it keeps La Mansana de Chinati/The Block, Judd's residence and studio with a library of over 13,000 volumes, plus the Architecture Office, Art Studio, Cobb House, Whyte Building and the ranch houses (stated by the space).
- The Architecture Office reopened in September 2025 after a seven-year restoration (stated by the space).
- Booked guided visits only: Marfa Tuesday to Sunday, $30 ($17 students and seniors); New York Tuesday to Saturday, $32.50 ($17.50). Visitors must be 12 or older; the New York visit climbs five flights, with an elevator for visitors who use a mobility aid.

FUNDING
Judd Foundation: private: an endowment built from sales of Judd's work, donors and ticket income, with small public grants
- In 2006 the foundation consigned 36 Judd works to Christie's to create an endowment; 25 sold in one evening auction (ARTnews, 2016). Roberta Smith wrote that the money was to pay off debts, fund an endowment and maintain the 16 buildings the foundation owns; she gave the Christie's guarantee of about $20 million as a report, not a confirmed figure.
- Year to June 2023: revenue $6,057,875, of which contributions $2,092,799, programme revenue $295,957 and investment income $402,143; expenses $6,844,195; net assets $110,155,491 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- Supporters from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, government and foundations: Bloomberg Philanthropies, Texas Commission on the Arts, The Henry Moore Foundation, The Japan Foundation New York, Villa Albertine, The New York Community Trust, and five family foundations and funds (supporters list).
- Corporate supporters on the same list: Bristol Myers Squibb (spelled "Bristol Meyers Squibb"), Gagosian Gallery, Neuberger Berman Group LLC, Paula Cooper Gallery, Sony Music Entertainment, J. Crew Group and four others.
- No reporting found of the amount any named supporter gave.

Not related: the Chinati Foundation, the museum Judd set up on a former Army base in Marfa with Dia's help. It runs that 400-acre site separately (WGBH) and is a different organisation from the Judd Foundation.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the foundation itself. What exists concerns the 2006 Christie's sale of Judd's works to build the endowment.
1. New York Times (republished by the Taipei Times): Roberta Smith, "Christie's takes minimalist art to the max" (2006)
URL: https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2006/04/27/2003304881
- Smith asked whether the foundation had "exhausted all fundraising possibilities" before selling the works.
- She reported arguments, from unnamed critics, that the sale could deflate the Judd market and that slower private sales could have placed more works in public collections.
- Smith disclosed that she once worked briefly for Judd.
2. ARTnews: Zoë Lescaze, "Specific Objectives: The Complex Task of Preserving Donald Judd's Legacy" (2016)
URL: https://davidzwirner.com/news/2016/specific-objectives-the-complex-task-of-preserving-donald-judd-s-legacy
- Marianne Stockebrand, then a board member, resigned when the board decided on the sale.
- Arne Glimcher, founder of Pace Gallery, criticised the sale and blamed Richard Schlagman, then a board member, for selling works at bargain prices.
- Read as a reprint on the site of David Zwirner, a dealer that works with the foundation.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hallie Ayres in e-flux Education, "Life on two planes: the schoolhouses of Marfa" (2025), criticised Judd's view of the Southwest as a blank canvas and described art-world newcomers, tourism investment and the town's commercialisation; the essay was published with the Chinati Foundation.
- It names the Judd Foundation only in a footnote, about its lawsuit against Kim Kardashian over furniture.
- The Judd Foundation owns and runs Judd's buildings in that town, so it sits inside this critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Judd Foundation is clean: an endowment from the sale of Judd's own work, private patrons and small public grants, with no flagged funder. The weak point is the 2006 Christie's sale, which cost it a board member. Worth watching: corporate partners such as Bristol Myers Squibb and Neuberger Berman, whose amounts are not published.
