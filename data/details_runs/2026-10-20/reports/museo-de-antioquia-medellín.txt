MUSEO DE ANTIOQUIA: Medellín, Colombia
ID: museo-de-antioquia-medellín
Website: https://www.museodeantioquia.co/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Carrera 52 No. 52-43 in Medellín (museum website).
- Began in 1872 as the Museo de Zea, founded by Manuel Uribe Ángel, Antonio José Restrepo and Martín Gómez. In 1946 a group including Teresa Santamaría and Joaquín Jaramillo Sierra reopened it as a private non-profit entity; it received legal status from the Gobernación in 1953 (Spanish Wikipedia). The English page and Minuto30 give 1881 as the founding year.
- Since 2000 it occupies the former Palacio Municipal, an Art Deco building won in a 1931 council competition by H. M. Rodríguez e Hijos and built from 1932 to 1937. It housed the city government until 1988, when it passed to Empresas Públicas de Medellín; it was declared a national monument in 1995 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Fernando Botero first offered to donate works in 1978; Plaza Botero opened in 2002 with 23 monumental sculptures (Spanish Wikipedia).
- María del Rosario Escobar has directed the museum since 2016 (Spanish Wikipedia). It also holds a specialised library in visual and plastic arts (museum website).
- Visiting: open Monday to Saturday 9:40 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. and Sundays and holidays 9:40 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.; closed on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. Entry is by wristbands sold in the online store (museum website).
- SeñaLab, a mediation laboratory built with the ASANSO collective and the Deaf community, explored the collection in Colombian Sign Language; the museum says sign-language tours and activities are planned to continue (museum website).

FUNDING
Museo de Antioquia: mixed: a private non-profit financed by public programmes and corporate allies
- Budget: 7,600 million Colombian pesos in 2022, with an aim of almost 2,000 million more in 2023, and a debt of 360 million pesos at the end of 2022. Director María del Rosario Escobar said pre-pandemic figures had not yet been reached (El Colombiano, Kirvin Larios, "La cultura en Medellín tiene agenda 2023, pero pide más respaldo", 9 January 2023).
- Part of its income depends on the state programme Formación de Públicos, which brings residents of strata 1, 2 and 3 to museums and theatres; its budget fell by more than 400 million pesos against 2019 (El Colombiano, 9 January 2023).
- Public programmes named on the museum site: SeñaLab is funded by the Medellín Mayor's Office 2026 arts and culture grants, and the project "Habitar la herida" is supported by the Área Metropolitana del Valle de Aburrá and the Ministerio de las Culturas' national cultural concertation programme.
- Promotora Cultural de Antioquia, a private fund announced in 2020 by the Proantioquia and Sofía Pérez de Soto foundations with public and private allies, made a first donation of 1,120 million pesos to four institutions, 280 million pesos each, including the museum. El Colombiano reported that the initial advisory committee was formed by Grupo Sura, Grupo Argos, Bancolombia and Comfama, together with the Sofía Pérez de Soto, Proantioquia and Fraternidad foundations and Universidad Eafit (El Colombiano, Ronal Castañeda, "Nace un fondo privado para apoyar a la cultura", 2 July 2020).
- Allies page of the museum website, logos with alt text: Alcaldía de Medellín, Cámara de Comercio de Medellín, Coltejer, EPM, Mineros S.A., Fundación MUV, Metro de Medellín, Ministerio de las Culturas and Sura.
- Minuto30 (Sonia López, 1 August 2025) describes EPM as a key ally of Medellín's cultural institutions, names the Museo de Antioquia among institutions that found in EPM a fundamental ally, and says museums run free admission for families in strata 1, 2 and 3 with EPM's support.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a mix of public programme funds from the national ministry, the city and the metropolitan area, plus corporate allies from Medellín's business sector, with Promotora Cultural's 2020 donation of 280 million pesos and EPM named by press.
