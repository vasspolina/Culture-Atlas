FOREIGN LANGUAGES PUBLISHING HOUSE: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: foreign-languages-publishing-house
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- North Korea's state publisher of foreign-language material, which KCNA called in 1999 "the nation's comprehensive publishing center for overseas propaganda" (KCNA, 10 December 1999).
- Location: KCNA placed the press group in Sosong district, Pyongyang (1999), and Wikipedia's Korea Today page gives Sochong-dong, Sosong District; Wikipedia's page on the house says Potonggang district, citing a dead link.
- Founding: KCNA reported that the group marked the 50th anniversary of its establishment on 4 December 1999, which puts its founding in 1949, and that it began by publishing a magazine called New Korea. Wikipedia gives 10 December 1949, citing the same item, whose dateline is 10 December.
- KCNA's 1999 item lists its units: publishing houses for the pictorial Korea, the Pyongyang Times, foreign-language magazines, foreign trade, Korea Today and information services, a foreign-language printing plant and a school for translators. It said Kim Jong Il had guided its work on the spot seven times.
- Output: KCNA claimed it had translated and published works of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il in at least tens of millions of volumes of some 700 titles, and that its periodicals reached at least 160 countries and international organisations (1999). It publishes the Pyongyang Times, Korea Today, Korea and Foreign Trade of the DPRK and runs the Naenara and Publications of the DPRK web portals (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia says it employs a small group of foreigners to revise translations.

FUNDING
Foreign Languages Publishing House: party-controlled: the Workers' Party of Korea's Propaganda and Agitation Department, plus the state
- The house is under the control of the Workers' Party's Propaganda and Agitation Department, which also decides on its staff (Wikipedia, citing North Korea Leadership Watch).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with the cultural sector's funds at 103.1 percent of the year before and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence (KCNA, copied by GlobalSecurity).

Red flags: a publisher controlled by the sanctioned Workers' Party (US order of 2016)
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and of the Workers' Party of Korea in the United States or in the control of US persons (Federal Register). The order does not name the house.
- The house reports to the party's Propaganda and Agitation Department (Wikipedia).
- The US North Korea Sanctions Regulations exempt the import and export of information and informational materials, such as publications, except where persons blocked under the UN Participation Act are involved (31 CFR 510.213).

Not related: the Foreign Languages Publishing House in Moscow, Foreign Languages Press in Beijing and the Foreign Languages Publishing House in Hanoi, now Thế Giới Publishers, which Wikipedia lists as similar publishers in other countries.

WIDER CONTEXT
- OFAC sanctioned senior officials of the Propaganda and Agitation Department over human rights abuses and censorship: its director Kim Ki Nam and first vice director Ri Jae Il on 6 July 2016, and first vice director Choe Hwi and vice director Kim Yo Jong on 11 January 2017 (OFAC).
- The department these officials led controls the house.

BOTTOM LINE
The house is a party propaganda body paid for by the North Korean party and state, both blocked by the US. Its books themselves fall under the US exemption for informational materials.
