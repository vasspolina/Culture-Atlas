21_21 DESIGN SIGHT: Tokyo, Japan
ID: 21-21-design-sight-tokyo
Website: https://www.2121designsight.jp/designsight/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Design exhibition venue in the garden of Tokyo Midtown at 9-7-6 Akasaka, Minato-ku, near Roppongi station (Hibiya and Oedo lines).
- Opened in March 2007, alongside Tokyo Midtown, after Issey Miyake called for a design museum in a 2003 open letter in the Asahi Shimbun.
- The space names Miyake as founder; its directors are Miyake, Taku Satoh (now museum director) and Naoto Fukasawa, with Noriko Kawakami as associate director (stated by the space).
- Tadao Ando designed two buildings of reinforced concrete and glass under tent-like folded steel roofs; about 70% of the building is below ground (Architectural Record, Art Space Tokyo).
- The site is part of Tokyo Midtown, built by Mitsui Fudosan on the former Japan Defense Agency grounds, which closed in May 2000.
- Galleries 1 and 2 hold the main shows; Gallery 3 opened in March 2017 for the tenth anniversary; there are also events and workshops.
- Open 10:00 to 19:00, last entry 18:30, closed Tuesdays and 27 December to 3 January; prices change per show, for example 1,400 yen general, 800 yen university, 500 yen high school, free for junior high and younger in 2024, with free entry for disability certificate holders and one companion.

FUNDING
21_21 DESIGN SIGHT: corporate-sponsored, run by the Miyake Issey Design Culture Foundation with Mitsui Fudosan, Miyake Design Studio, Issey Miyake Inc and Shiseido as main partners
- The Miyake Issey Design Culture Foundation (Kōeki Zaidan Hōjin) co-organises the programme with the venue and is linked from the site footer.
- Main partners as of October 2026: Mitsui Fudosan, Miyake Design Studio, Issey Miyake Inc and Shiseido (stated by the space).
- Partner I tier: Taisei Corporation, Komatsu Matere, Takeo, Toray and Y's; Partner G tier: 15 firms including Kokuyo, Sangetsu, Seiko Watch, Parmigiani Fleurier Distribution Japan and YKK AP (stated by the space).
- Individual partners pay for a membership valid for 365 days (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of amounts paid by any partner, of state funding, or of published accounts for the foundation.

Red flags: main partner Mitsui Fudosan and the Jingu Gaien redevelopment
- Mitsui Fudosan is one of the developers of the Jingu Gaien redevelopment in Tokyo, with Meiji Jingu, the Japan Sport Council and Itochu (FCCJ Number 1 Shimbun, AFP).
- On 5 September 2023 ICOMOS issued a Heritage Alert over the project, citing the loss of about 3,000 trees (FCCJ, AFP).
- In September 2023 campaigners submitted a petition with almost 225,000 signatures against the felling; developers put the trees to be removed at 743, down from 892 (AFP).
- Ryuichi Sakamoto called on Governor Yuriko Koike not to sacrifice the trees for economic profit, and Haruki Murakami also opposed the plan (FCCJ).
- The protest targets Mitsui Fudosan's project, not 21_21 DESIGN SIGHT; no reporting found of a protest aimed at the venue.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism. Coverage is mostly descriptive or admiring of the building and shows; one columnist questioned the gap between the stated mission and the practice.
1. artscape International: Alan Gleason, "Alphabet Soup at 21_21 DESIGN SIGHT" (year not stated on the page)
URL: https://artscape.jp/artscape/eng/ht/0809.html
- The venue presents itself as "not so much a museum as a research center for design", but in practice it runs "rather straightforwardly as a design museum" with regular shows, workshops and lectures.
- Places it within Roppongi's "relentless image upgrade" through new museums.
2. Architectural Record: Naomi Pollock, "21_21 Design Sight" (2007)
URL: https://architecturalrecord.com/articles/8056-21_21-design-sight
- Not criticism: a favourable review of Ando's building and its link to Miyake's work.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is corporate: its main partners are Miyake's own companies, Shiseido and Mitsui Fudosan, the developer of the Tokyo Midtown complex it sits in. The weak point is that Mitsui Fudosan is one of the developers of the Jingu Gaien project, which drew an ICOMOS Heritage Alert and a large petition. No partner amounts or foundation accounts are published.
