LINCOLN CENTER FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS: New York, USA
ID: lincoln-center-for-the-performing-arts
Website: https://www.lincolncenter.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Performing arts complex of 16.3 acres in the Lincoln Square neighbourhood of Manhattan's Upper West Side, with 30 indoor and outdoor facilities (Wikipedia).
- Built from 1959 as part of the Lincoln Square Renewal Project in Robert Moses's urban renewal programme; John D. Rockefeller III led the effort as first president and later chairman. The first building, Philharmonic Hall, opened in 1962 (Wikipedia).
- The David H. Koch Theater opened on 23 April 1964 as the New York State Theater, designed by Philip Johnson and John Burgee; the city owns it and New York City Ballet is its main tenant (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of opening days or admission, which vary by venue.

FUNDING
Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts: private philanthropy and corporate sponsorship, with a billionaire's naming gift for the Koch Theater
- The Campus Fund supporter list (footnote date 1 June 2026) names seven donors in the top Leader tier: Aman New York, American Express, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Kenneth C. Griffin with Citadel and Citadel Securities, Hearst, JPMorganChase and The New York Times. No amounts are posted (stated by the space).
- In July 2008 David H. Koch, co-owner of Koch Industries, pledged $100 million, announced as the largest private capital donation on the campus. It supported a $200 million capital campaign by New York City Ballet and New York City Opera for upgrades to the New York State Theater; the theater took the name David H. Koch Theater in November 2008 (BizBash, Wikipedia). The pledge ran over ten years and also covered operating and maintenance costs (American Prospect, Wikipedia).
- The name is set for at least fifty years, and the Koch family holds first refusal on any new name (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the share of public money in the current budget.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, the Koch name on the theater
- Koch Industries runs petroleum refining, pipeline and oil and gas operations; David Koch was a majority owner until his death in 2019. His $100 million pledge of July 2008 gave the theater his name for at least fifty years, with the Koch family holding first refusal on any new name (Wikipedia, BizBash).
- Protest: on 21 November 2025 three people disrupted the Met Opera's Carmen at Lincoln Center, pausing the show for about 20 minutes (AOL, citing the New York Post). Nate Smith, who led the action, and two others were arrested and charged with trespassing. Smith wrote an open letter demanding that Lincoln Center remove donor names tied to Koch Industries, Chevron and Texaco, citing the removal of the Sackler name from museums (OperaWire, 2 December 2025).
- Earlier objection: Harold Meyerson argued in 2013 that the city should remove Koch's name because of his political funding, and repay the gift (American Prospect).
- No reporting found of Lincoln Center removing or reviewing the name.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Jane Mayer's New Yorker article of 2010, read on air on Democracy Now (25 August 2010), pairs Koch's gifts to New York arts institutions with the Kochs' funding of the Tea Party. Charles Lewis of the Center for Public Integrity describes the Kochs' political spending as aimed at the furtherance of Koch Industries.
- The land was cleared for the complex: more than 7,000 residents and 800 businesses of the San Juan Hill area were displaced (Wikipedia).
- Lincoln Center was built on that cleared site as part of the same renewal project.

BOTTOM LINE
Funding comes from private donors, and the weak point is the Koch name on the theater, which was the target of a documented protest in November 2025 and a 2013 call for removal. Leader-tier donors include Kenneth Griffin, Citadel and Bloomberg Philanthropies; no reporting found of a campaign against them at Lincoln Center. Watch whether the name or the donor list is reviewed.
