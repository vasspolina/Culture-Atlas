MILITARY TROPHY PARK: Baku, Azerbaijan
ID: military-trophy-park
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Open-air park of about 5 hectares in Baku's Khatai district showing military equipment captured from Armenian forces in the 2020 war over Nagorno-Karabakh (Report.az, 13 April 2021).
- It stands on a lot next to Primorsky Park on Nobel Avenue in the "White City" area, beside a planned Victory Museum (Turan, 9 April 2021; paywalled, only the opening paragraph was read).
- Sources differ on when work began: January 2021 according to Report.az, February 2021 according to Turan. President Ilham Aliyev opened the park on 12 April 2021 at a ceremony where he also handed out new military awards (president.az); it opened to the public on 14 April (RFE/RL).
- More than 300 exhibits, up to 150 of them heavy: tanks, combat vehicles, artillery, air-defence missile systems, small arms and military vehicles. A mock-up of a ten-line Armenian defence system with shelters and barracks, a panel of more than 2,000 captured Armenian number plates reading "Karabakh is Azerbaijan!", displays on Armenian war crimes and remains of an Iskander-M missile complete the show (Report.az).
- At the opening Aliyev called the captured hardware "further evidence of our strength and the enemy's defeat" (president.az).
- Until October 2021 it also showed wax mannequins of Armenian soldiers and a corridor of helmets of Armenian soldiers. The park's director wrote that the mannequins were removed on 1 October and the helmets on 8 October 2021 (International Court of Justice order, 7 December 2021).

FUNDING
Military Trophy Park: state project of the Azerbaijani presidency, stocked by the armed forces
- Aliyev ordered its construction in January 2021 (OC Media, 15 October 2021). Its exhibits are hardware the Azerbaijani Armed Forces took as trophies (president.az).

Red flags: 2021 objections to the mannequins and helmets
- RFE/RL reported that the mannequins had exaggerated features such as hooked noses and that their sculptors said they had "tried to create the most freakish depictions" of Armenians.
- In a letter to Aliyev made public on 27 April 2021, Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights Dunja Mijatović called the scenes of dead and dying Armenian soldiers "highly disturbing and humiliating" and said such displays strengthen hostile sentiment and hate speech (Public Radio of Armenia).
- On 20 May 2021 the European Parliament, in a resolution on prisoners of war, deplored the opening of the "so-called Trophy Park in Baku", said it "may be perceived as a glorification of violence" and urged that it be closed without delay (resolution P9_TA(2021)0251, as reproduced by the Armenian National Institute).
- Azerbaijan's foreign ministry answered that it had "every right to perpetuate its victory through parades, parks, etc" and that "this practice exists in military museums in many countries of the world" (ministry commentary no. 131/21, quoted by Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2021 case at the International Court of Justice
- Armenia asked the court in 2021 to order the park closed or suspended, citing the mannequins as incitement to racial hatred (ICJ order).
- Armenia's representative Yegishe Kirakosyan said the removals came after Armenia filed its suit and that Armenia still sought full closure (OC Media, 15 October 2021).
- On 7 December 2021 the court ordered no closure. It took note of Azerbaijan's assurance that the mannequins and helmets had been removed permanently and would not return, and unanimously ordered Azerbaijan to prevent the incitement and promotion of racial hatred against people of Armenian origin, including by its officials and public institutions (ICJ order).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from political and human rights reporting, not the art press.
1. RFE/RL: Neil Hauer, "Azerbaijan's 'Ethnic Hatred' Theme Park Draws Ire, Imperils Reconciliation" (2021)
URL: https://www.rferl.org/a/azerbaijan-karabakh-theme-park-armenia-ethnic-hatred-aliyev/31217971.html
- Calls it unusual for such a museum to open months after a war, with cartoonish mannequins of enemy soldiers; an Armenian interviewee called it the most hateful, racist thing he had seen.
- Laurence Broers of Conciliation Resources sees it as the product of years of rhetoric against Armenians, which he says became more salient in the late 2000s, before the return of Ramil Safarov, whom Aliyev pardoned in 2012; journalist Arzu Geybulla found it disrespectful even to Azerbaijan's own dead.
- Names Aliyev; criticises the government's display, not an art institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is purely state money; the problem is the message the state chose to display, not who paid. Worth watching: whether Azerbaijan keeps its assurance to the court.
