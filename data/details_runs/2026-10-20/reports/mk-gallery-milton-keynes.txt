MK GALLERY: Milton Keynes, UK
ID: mk-gallery-milton-keynes
Website: https://mkgallery.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art gallery at 900 Midsummer Boulevard, Milton Keynes, with five galleries, a cinema, a cafe, a shop and an artist-designed play area (MK Gallery website).
- Opened in 1999 as Milton Keynes Gallery, run by the Milton Keynes Theatre and Gallery Company; it has no permanent collection (Wikipedia, MK Gallery website).
- The original building was by Blonski-Heard; 6a architects, with artists Gareth Jones and Nils Norman, extended and remodelled it and the gallery reopened in March 2019 (Wikipedia, MK Gallery website).
- The 2018-19 works added five double-height galleries, the Sky Room auditorium for up to 300 people and a new entrance reviving the 1999 sandstone and terracotta colour scheme (Business MK, 21 November 2018).
- Director is Anthony Spira; the gallery is a registered charity, number 1059678, a member of the Arts Council's National Portfolio and of the Plus Tate network (MK Gallery website).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; membership includes free entry to exhibitions, and the site lists no general admission price. The site offers an access-requirements form covering wheelchair use, BSL, audio description, captions and hearing loop.

FUNDING
MK Gallery: mixed public and private: Arts Council England, Milton Keynes Council, charitable trusts and individual donors
- Arts Council England funds the gallery as a National Portfolio organisation, and Milton Keynes Council is a funder (Business MK, 2018). For the expansion, a 2015 town council notice gave a total of GBP 10 million, with Arts Council England providing GBP 2.9 million (Central Milton Keynes Town Council, 26 February 2015).
- The support page names programme supporters: Arts Council England, Children in Need, the Elephant Trust, the Eranda Rothschild Foundation, the Ernest Cook Trust, Freelands Foundation, MK Community Foundation and Paul Hamlyn Foundation.
- Expansion donors named: the Anson Charitable Trust, Bernard Sunley Charitable Foundation, Charles Wolfson Charitable Trust, the Finnis Scott Foundation, the Foyle Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, the Headley Trust, J Paul Getty Jnr Charitable Trust and Ann Limb.
- Make Your Mark supporters named: Milton Keynes Council, MK Community Foundation, Reuben Foundation, the Sackler Trust, Sir Siegmund Warburg's Voluntary Settlement and South East Midlands Local Enterprise Partnership; the Sylvia Waddilove Foundation UK and the Wolfson Foundation are named as Stepping Up and Take Your Seat supporters.
- Benefactors are Niall Curran and Susan Reid and Marjorie Mackintosh; 16 patrons are also named (MK Gallery support page).
- No reporting found of amounts or dates for any gift except the Arts Council figure above.

Red flags: Getty family oil money
- The support page lists the J Paul Getty Jnr Charitable Trust among the donors to the gallery's expansion; it gives no date or amount (MK Gallery support page, stated by the gallery).
- J. Paul Getty Jr. was the son of J. Paul Getty, who built Getty Oil into one of the biggest petroleum companies in the US (obituary, Health Club Management, 2003). Wikipedia says the family's wealth came from the oil business, and that Getty Jr. lived on income from a family trust: about USD 100,000 a year in the late 1960s and later over USD 20 million a year.
- No page read states where the charitable trust's own endowment came from; the Charity Commission register pages refused automated access. No reporting found of a protest aimed at MK Gallery over this gift.

Red flags: Sackler Trust named as a supporter
- The support page, which is undated, lists the Sackler Trust under "Make Your Mark Supporters" and gives no amount (MK Gallery support page, stated by the gallery).
- Context: Members of the Sackler family and Purdue Pharma face US lawsuits alleging they fuelled the opioid crisis by marketing OxyContin; the company denies this. Theresa Sackler, who chairs the Sackler Trust, is the widow of Mortimer Sackler, a former Purdue chief executive who died in 2010. In March 2019 she announced that the Trust and the Dr Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation were pausing all new UK charitable giving (Gareth Harris, The Art Newspaper, 16 November 2019). No page read says whether the pause ended.
- No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at MK Gallery over the Sackler Trust.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mixed: Arts Council England and the local council plus named trusts and individuals, with one company, David Lock Associates, among its patrons. The weak points are two trusts tied to flagged industries, the Sackler Trust (opioids) and the J Paul Getty Jnr Charitable Trust (Getty oil family), both named among supporters with no date or amount. Worth watching: whether the gallery dates its donors.
