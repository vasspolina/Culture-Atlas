INSTITUTE OF CONTEMPORARY ARTS (ICA): London, United Kingdom
ID: ica-london
Website: https://www.ica.art

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre with galleries, a cinema, a bookshop, a cafe and a bar in Nash House on The Mall, near Buckingham Palace, 10 Downing Street and Piccadilly Circus (ICA website).
- Founded in 1946 by Roland Penrose, Peter Watson, Herbert Read, Peter Gregory, Geoffrey Grigson and E. L. T. Mesens as a space for artists, writers and scientists to debate ideas outside the Royal Academy (Wikipedia).
- It moved to Nash House, part of Carlton House Terrace, in 1968 with Arts Council support; David Kohn Architects refurbished the building from 2012 to 2020 (Wikipedia).
- It is a private limited company and registered charity (no. 236848) governed by a board of trustees chaired by Professor Dame Henrietta Moore. Bengi Ünsal is director (ICA website).
- Visiting: closed Monday, open Tuesday to Sunday, 12pm to 11pm. Exhibitions close at 8pm (ICA website).

FUNDING
Institute of Contemporary Arts: Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation, with donations, trading and charitable activities
- The ICA states that it is an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation and lists Arts Council England funding, donations and legacies, charitable activities and trading as its income sources (ICA governance page).
- Arts Council England reduced the ICA's funding by £200,000 per year. A spokesperson said the ICA's deficit for fiscal year 2023/24 was expected to be around £800,000, and director Bengi Ünsal said "the pandemic, inflation and changing patterns in donations and grants" had led to an operating deficit for the last few years (The Art Newspaper, Kabir Jhala, 5 July 2024).
- January 2025: Bloomberg Philanthropies sponsored the New Contemporaries exhibition at the ICA. The ICA said it has not received Bloomberg funding since 2020 and that the funding went directly to New Contemporaries, which confirmed it received an undisclosed sum (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 16 January 2025).

Red flags: protests and staff dispute over Palestine
- 14 January 2025: more than 20 artists in the New Contemporaries show walked out of its preview and joined pro-Palestine demonstrators at the ICA. The protest, led by the UK wing of the Gaza Biennale and supported by the Palestinian Youth Movement, demanded that the ICA cut ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies. An ICA spokesperson dated the protest to 13 January, called it "misguided" and called the information it relied on "factually untrue" (The Art Newspaper, 16 January 2025).
- 13 March: an all-staff meeting announced that 14 ICA roles were at risk of redundancy, and the union saved three. Former workers say at least six employees resigned in solidarity with the fired workers and against the ICA's financial mismanagement (Hyperallergic, Maya Pontone, 19 July 2024).
- Unverified: former workers and the group Cultural Workers Against Genocide say the cuts included staff who had received informal warnings in October over an open letter calling for a boycott of Israel. The ICA denies this and attributes the cuts to financial pressures, calling the claims "categorically untrue" (The Art Newspaper, 5 July 2024 and 16 January 2025; Hyperallergic, 19 July 2024).
- July 2024: the artist Rheim Alkadhi said she would withdraw her solo show "Templates for Liberation" unless the ICA responded with "disclosure and accountability" (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Three pieces from 2024 and 2025 report staff and artist protests against the ICA's management and sponsor ties, with the ICA's responses. None reviews the programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Kabir Jhala, "Workers allege redundancies at London's Institute of Contemporary Arts are related to Palestine solidarity actions" (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/07/05/institute-contemporary-arts-london-workers-redundancies-palestine-protest
- Former workers allege the redundancies are linked to their Palestine organising; an anonymous former worker called the financial explanation a "smokescreen".
- Director Bengi Ünsal said the ICA was "left with no option other than to restructure the organisation."
2. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "London Art Institute Workers Say They Were Fired for Supporting Palestine" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/ica-london-workers-say-they-were-fired-for-supporting-palestine
- Former workers say they were dismissed for joining the 20 October Global Strike for Palestine and posting the open letter; the ICA denied this and cited financial pressures.
3. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Artists at London's ICA stage mass walkout in support of pro-Palestine protesters" (2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/01/16/artists-at-ica-london-stage-mass-walkout-in-support-of-pro-palestine-protesters
- Protesters demanded that the ICA cut ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies and cited 14 former staff who linked their redundancies to the Israel boycott letter.
- The ICA said it has not received Bloomberg funding since 2020; the funding went directly to New Contemporaries.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the ICA is Arts Council England funding, which was cut by £200,000 a year, plus donations, trading and charitable activities. The documented disputes concern the 2024 redundancies and the Bloomberg sponsorship of New Contemporaries.
