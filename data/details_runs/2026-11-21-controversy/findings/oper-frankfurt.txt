Red flags: 2026 dispute over the demolition of the theatre building
- The City of Frankfurt plans to demolish the existing building at Willy-Brandt-Platz, put a new opera house on the site and build a separate Schauspielhaus at Neue Mainzer Straße 49 (Entwicklungsstadt, Marie Neise, 6 February 2026).
- The Bund Deutscher Architektinnen und Architekten argues that full demolition would lose the building's architectural qualities and calls for partial renovation (Entwicklungsstadt, 6 February 2026).
- The Aktionsgemeinschaft Schauspielhaus Frankfurt wants the remaining facade fragments of the 1902 Schauspielhaus preserved, and "wirft der Stadt vor, die Besichtigungen zu behindern" ("accuses the city of obstructing the viewings", translated); culture commissioner Ina Hartwig rejected the accusation (Entwicklungsstadt, 6 February 2026).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In January 2020 the city council voted "mit großer Mehrheit" ("by a large majority", translated) to demolish the theatre complex that houses the opera and the drama stage; a new building on the site had been estimated at 874 million euros and a renovation at 918 million euros (epd via evangelisch.de, 31 January 2020).
- On 16 November 2020 the Deutsche Stiftung Denkmalschutz, ICOMOS Deutschland and four other heritage and art history bodies signed an open letter to the mayor and three city department heads regretting the decision to demolish the building, which is in part a listed monument, and saying that the culture commissioner's remarks on the State Monuments Office report of May 2020 contradicted principles of monument protection (Deutsche Stiftung Denkmalschutz, open letter, 16 November 2020).
