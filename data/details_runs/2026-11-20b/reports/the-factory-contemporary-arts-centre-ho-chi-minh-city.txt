THE FACTORY CONTEMPORARY ARTS CENTRE: Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
ID: the-factory-contemporary-arts-centre-ho-chi-minh-city
Website: https://factoryartscentre.com (checked 2026-11-20; serves spam, a Turkish football-streaming page)

OVERVIEW
- Independent contemporary art centre at 15 Nguyen U Di, Thao Dien ward, District 2, Ho Chi Minh City (e-flux directory).
- Founded in 2016 by the artist Ti-A (Viet Nam News; Artribune); ArtAsiaPacific names the founder as Ti-a Thuy Nguyen and reported in 2016 that the centre had opened to the public in March.
- It describes itself as the first purpose-built independent space for contemporary art in Vietnam (e-flux directory).
- Building: HTAP Architects, with Hoanh Tran and Archie Pizzini in charge, designed the 880 square metre centre, completed in 2017 (ArchDaily); its street front is a block of decorated shipping containers (Viet Nam News).
- On site in 2017: exhibition spaces, an arts library, workshops, a co-working space, the F Kitchen and Bar and the Red Goat Café (Viet Nam News).
- Zoe Butt, executive director and curator of Sàn Art from 2009, became artistic director in February 2017 (ArtAsiaPacific; Artribune).
- Programmes named in 2018: The Glass Box, a private exhibition programme for its VIP community; the Reading Room, an open-access library; and Pollination, a residency for young Southeast Asian curators and artists with one partner institution each year (Art & Market).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 7 pm (e-flux directory); in 2017 entry cost VND10,000 to VND100,000 depending on the activity (Viet Nam News).

FUNDING
The Factory Contemporary Arts Centre: self-funded: a social enterprise paying for programmes from art sales and on-site businesses
- All profit from art sales and from the businesses on the property pays the running costs of the art programmes, stated by the centre (Viet Nam News, 2017; Art & Market, 2018).
- The Glass Box aims to cultivate interest in collecting and in supporting the production and presentation of contemporary art in Vietnam (Art & Market).
- Its curatorial assistant said in 2019 that The Factory is not a commercial gallery: it had sold works, but not actively, and planned a sales team only to help artists become independent (Artribune; translated from the Italian).
- Butt said in 2018 that Pollination aims at "an independent network of private interest" across Southeast Asia (Art & Market).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is descriptive and admiring; the one critical passage is about Vietnam's exhibition licensing, not the centre.
1. Artribune: Redazione, "Ho Chi Minh City. The Factory Contemporary Arts Centre" (27 March 2019)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/dal-mondo/2019/03/ho-chi-minh-city-factory-contemporary-arts-centre/
- First-person account by curatorial assistant Lê Thiên Bảo: official education centres on socialist realism, every exhibition needs a licence, and the Minister of Culture approves works by aesthetic and political criteria (translated from the Italian).
- Criticises the national system, not the institution; it reports no show of the centre refused.
2. Art & Market: Ian Tee, "Social Enterprise Meets Art Infrastructure at The Factory" (2018)
URL: https://www.artandmarket.net/profile/2018/10/28/social-enterprise-meets-art-infrastructure-at-the-factory
- Names the lack of arts infrastructure and of financial support for artistic production in Vietnam as the centre's challenges.
- Not criticism of the centre.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In Vietnam a work judged unsuitable cannot be exhibited, though it can appear in a catalogue, because the licence to publish and the licence to exhibit are separate (Artribune, translated from the Italian).
- The centre works inside that licensing system.

BOTTOM LINE
The stated money is the founder's own enterprise, with no named donor, state body or sponsor. Worth watching is whether the centre still operates.
