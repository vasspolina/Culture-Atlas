CITY GALLERY WELLINGTON: Wellington, New Zealand
ID: city-gallery-wellington-wellington
Website: https://citygallery.org.nz/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art gallery, Te Whare Toi, at 67 Victoria Street on Te Ngākau Civic Square in central Wellington (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Opened in 1980 as the Wellington City Art Gallery; it moved to Civic Square in 1993 and took its current name (Wikipedia).
- The building is the former Wellington Central Library, an Art Deco concrete building by Gummer and Ford completed in 1940; a 2008 to 2009 addition brought galleries and an auditorium (Wikipedia).
- Run since 1995 for Wellington City Council by the Wellington Museums Trust, now trading as Wheako Pōneke Experience Wellington, which also runs Wellington Museum, Capital E and Space Place (Wikipedia, 2025/26 annual report).
- Closed on 1 July 2024 for planned maintenance; the building was designated earthquake prone in 2024, and the council spent $12.3 million on strengthening, and it reopened on 10 October 2026 with a Cornelia Parker survey (1News, 2025/26 annual report).
- Visiting: open daily 10am to 5pm, with Thursdays until 9pm; the Cornelia Parker show is ticketed through Ticketmaster (stated by the space). No reporting found of current access details.

FUNDING
City Gallery Wellington: mainly public: Wellington City Council, through Experience Wellington
- Wellington City Council gave Experience Wellington a $10,244,594 operating grant and a $2,096,574 rental grant in the year to 30 June 2026, out of total revenue of $15,743,056; the rental grant goes back to the council as rent on the gallery and other council buildings (2025/26 annual report).
- Non-council revenue was $3,210,133, including $258,633 in sponsorship and partnerships and $325,644 from the Ministry of Education (2025/26 annual report).
- The council appoints the trust board, which includes councillors (2025/26 annual report).
- Named supporters include Simpson Grierson (the law firm sponsoring the Cornelia Parker show), Singapore Airlines, the Spark Foundation, Transpower Community Fund, the Lottery Community Fund, the Michael and Suzanne Borrin Foundation, Pelorus Trust, Stout Trust and individual donors (2025/26 annual report, Simpson Grierson).
- The City Gallery Wellington Foundation, a separate charity, had revenue of $217,900 and assets of $1.6 million in the year to June 2025; it pays for exhibitions and non-building capital costs (Charities Register data via CharityData).

Red flags: 2019 protest over the Theo Schoon exhibition
- In August 2019 students led by Anna McAllister protested at "Split Level View Finder: Theo Schoon and New Zealand Art", wearing shirts reading "Theo Schoon is a racist" and urging people to stay away (RNZ via NZ Herald).
- They said Schoon appropriated Māori motifs and called Māori "degenerates", and that the gallery should hire a Māori cultural adviser (RNZ via NZ Herald).
- The protest targeted the show, not its sponsor, the Fletcher Trust (stated by the space).

Red flags: 2021 restructure and open letters
- In 2021 Experience Wellington proposed cutting the director and chief curator posts; artists ran a #SaveCityGallery campaign with two open letters, and patrons wrote that the change might breach the Local Government Act (ArtAsiaPacific).
- The Chartwell Trust put its art loans to the gallery on hold (ArtAsiaPacific).
- The director role was disestablished and senior curator Robert Leonard was removed; Wellington's mayor questioned the restructure (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two clusters: the 2019 Schoon show, criticised for its handling of Māori art, and the 2021 restructure, criticised by the local art community and an international museum body.
1. The Spinoff: Martin Patrick, "The past is a fucked up place: Theo Schoon, reviewed and reconsidered" (2019)
URL: https://thespinoff.co.nz/art/24-08-2019/the-past-is-a-fucked-up-place-theo-schoon-reviewed-and-reconsidered
- Schoon took imagery without credit and recorded Ngāi Tahu rock drawing sites without consultation.
- The Māori modernism section could have been much larger, to the show's benefit; the curators did not treat the material as unproblematic.
- Respects the protesters but is wary of their call to scrap the show.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: Gabrielle Tse, "Wellington's City Gallery Faces Controversial Restructuring" (2021)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/wellington-s-city-gallery-faces-controversial-restructuring
- Reports the open letters calling the changes "structural, substantial, and far-reaching" and the lack of public consultation.
- Notes CIMAM issued and then withdrew a statement at the request of Mayor Andy Foster; CIMAM's 9 June 2021 statement said losing the specialist roles would weaken the gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind City Gallery is clean and overwhelmingly municipal: Wellington City Council pays most of the operating budget and owns the building. The weak points are governance, not funders: the 2021 restructure drew open letters and an international statement, and the 2019 Schoon show drew a protest. Worth watching: whether the reopened gallery regains a dedicated curatorial leadership.
