MUSEO NACIONAL DE ARTE DE BOLIVIA: La Paz, Bolivia
ID: museo-nacional-de-arte-de-bolivia-la-paz
Website: https://www.fundacionculturalbcb.gob.bo/notas-de-prensa-mna/2022/np-mna-023-2022.php (checked 2026-10-24; the press-note page loads, but the foundation's home page returned a 522 error)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in the Palacio Diez de Medina, across from Plaza Murillo, La Paz (Wikipedia).
- Part of the Fundación Cultural del Banco Central de Bolivia (FC-BCB), which describes it as the state's most important and extensive repository of plastic and visual arts. The museum marks 3 August as its anniversary and turned 56 in 2022 (FC-BCB press note); Wikipedia gives 1964 for its establishment.
- The mansion was owned by a mayor, then by noble families, and was a hotel in the late 19th century. In 1964 it was adapted as the museum, keeping its two courtyards and three floors (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the architect of the adaptation.
- The permanent collection includes colonial paintings by Melchor Pérez de Holguín and Gregorio Gamarra, plus contemporary art (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Arte de Bolivia: public: Bolivian state, through the Banco Central de Bolivia's cultural foundation
- Supreme Decree 4243 of 19 May 2020 (Article 4) required the Banco Central de Bolivia to give the Fundación Cultural the resources to carry out the decree, under Article 82 of Law 1670; Decree 4462 of 3 February 2021 abrogated it (Lexivox). No reporting found of an annual budget figure for the museum.
- The foundation oversees nine Bolivian cultural institutions and has a board replaced in March 2020; Guillermo Mariaca became president in April 2020 (CIMAM, 2020).
- Alicia Medrano of Mexico donated works by Ricardo Pérez Alcalá that were shown in the museum's 2022 anniversary exhibition (FC-BCB press note).
- No reporting found of a private sponsor, donor list or published accounts for the museum.

Red flags: Protest over the forced exit of the museum's director
- Dismissal: on 1 June 2020 the foundation asked director Max Jorge Hinderer Cruz, in post since March 2019, to step down (CIMAM). He and Elvira Espejo Ayca of the National Museum of Ethnography and Folklore were the only two directors of nine institutions asked to resign, and both were dismissed (Apollo, Valeria Costa-Kostritsky, 29 July 2020). Lucía Querejazu Escobari replaced Hinderer (Apollo).
- Reasons: the foundation's president told La Razón the request was "natural" with a change of presidency; CIMAM says no transparent process assessed the directors' performance (CIMAM). Apollo reports the removals by the interim government after Evo Morales left office in November 2019; Hinderer called them "symbolic violence" and Espejo said the "old guard" had returned.
- Protest: CIMAM wrote to the foundation's president, Guillermo Mariaca, on 23 June 2020, and had received no reply by 29 June; it asked the board to withdraw the requests (CIMAM).
- Aftermath: in February 2021 the foundation reinstated Espejo and Máximo Pacheco as an "acto de desagravio" for dismissals by the "gobierno de facto". Hinderer was not included, which Correo del Sur says "causó extrañeza" (my translation: caused surprise). Iván Castellón is named museum director (Correo del Sur, 18 February 2021).

Red flags: Foundation management
- Unverified: Jorge Aliaga, a former director of the foundation's Santa Cruz cultural centre, denounced an irregular attempt to buy a La Paz property valued at 10 million bolivianos with money from the Santa Cruz centre's budget. In his complaint, the centre under Silvana Vázquez spent only 6 of the 20 million bolivianos allocated. Source: eju.tv, Emilio Martínez, 19 June 2015, which states the purchase was suspended after his complaints. No reporting found of a response.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2020 dismissal of Hinderer; art-press coverage is one magazine feature and one museum-association statement. No criticism of the collection or programme found.
1. Apollo: Valeria Costa-Kostritsky, "Status anxiety: the battle over culture in Bolivia" (2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/bolivia-museum-directors-sacked/
- Reports the removal of Hinderer and Espejo by the interim government, the only two of nine museum directors asked to step down, and Hinderer's reading of it as "symbolic violence".
2. CIMAM Museum Watch Committee: statement on the Museo Nacional de Arte (29 June 2020)
URL: https://cimam.org/museum-watch/museum-watch-actions/la-paz-international-museum-best-cases-danger/
- Says the requests came without a transparent process for assessing the directors' performance, describes both as internationally respected, and asks the board to withdraw the requests and to decide leadership through open discussion.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and flows through a Banco Central foundation, with no private sponsor named. The weak points are the board's 2020 removal of its director, which the director links to politics, Hinderer's exclusion from the 2021 reinstatements, and the lack of published museum accounts. Worth watching: who runs the museum now and whether the foundation publishes its budget.
