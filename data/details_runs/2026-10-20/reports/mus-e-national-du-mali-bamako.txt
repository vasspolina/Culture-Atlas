MUSÉE NATIONAL DU MALI: Bamako, Mali
ID: mus-e-national-du-mali-bamako

OVERVIEW
- National museum in Bamako, on Avenue de la Liberté (Artes Sere).
- Opened on 14 February 1953 as the Musée soudanais, attached to the Institut français d'Afrique noire; it became the Musée national du Mali at independence in 1960 (Wikipedia, French).
- On 8 March 1982 it moved into new premises in stabilised earth ("banco stabilisé"); architect Jean-Loup Pivin drew on traditional architecture (Wikipedia, French).
- Holds about 10,000 objects, mainly ethnographic, archaeological and fine-art pieces (Wikipedia, French). Its contemporary art collection rests on a donation from the association ADEIAO, and it regularly presents parts of the Bamako Biennale for Photography (Artes Sere).
- In August 2026 it showed "TƐGƐNƆ", a reinterpretation of its collections by visual artist Abdou Ouologuem, under Mali's declared 2026 to 2027 "Année de la Culture et de l'Éducation" (Afrikinfos-Mali, "Mali : vers la revitalisation du Musée national", 5 August 2026).
- Director General: Daouda Keïta (Maliexpress, 21 April 2026).

FUNDING
Musée National du Mali: public: the Malian state, with foreign project grants
- The culture minister, Mamou Daffé, presided over the launch of the Spanish-funded project at the museum and said the State backs its success (Maliexpress, 21 April 2026).
- Spain: the project "Repenser le Musée national du Mali", launched on 21 April 2026, is financed by AECID, the Spanish international development cooperation agency. It covers staff training, conservation and security of the collections, and cultural mediation for young people. Spain's ambassador Antonio Guillén Hidalgo called it a way to bring the two nations closer (Maliexpress, 21 April 2026; Bamada.net, "Culture : Des perspectives pour rénover le Musée National", 22 April 2026). The minister presented it as a response to the crisis, the lack of conservation resources and falling attendance (Maliexpress).
- European Union: financed the rehabilitation and extension of the museum in 2002, under President Alpha Oumar Konaré (Wikipedia, French).
- Aga Khan Trust for Culture: museum director Samuel Sidibé signed a collaboration agreement in June 2006 providing a new information technology system and better conservation facilities, with a plan to reorganise the archaeology and textile reserve collections (Wikipedia, English).
- Wikipedia (French) states that weak financial means and a lack of qualified staff after independence led to deterioration of the collections.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Malian state, with project grants from Spain's AECID (2026), the European Union (2002 extension) and the Aga Khan Trust for Culture (2006 agreement).
