MASS MOCA: North Adams, USA
ID: mass-moca
Website: https://massmoca.org (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum and performing arts centre in a former factory complex at 1040 MASS MoCA Way, North Adams, Massachusetts. The galleries cover about 300,000 square feet, and the campus is home to over 40 creative businesses (museum website).
- The site was the Arnold Print Works textile mill from 1860 to 1942, then the Sprague Electric Company, which made capacitors there until it closed the factory in 1985 (Wikipedia).
- Staff of the Williams College Museum of Art began the project in 1986. The Commonwealth of Massachusetts granted $18.6 million and local residents and businesses gave $8 million; the museum opened in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Building 6 opened in May 2017, adding roughly 130,000 square feet (Wikipedia). Directors: Joe Thompson from 1999 to 2021, then Kristy Edmunds.
- Programmes: more than 75 performances and artist residencies a year, including the Bang on a Can Summer Institute and the Solid Sound, FreshGrass festivals; 50% of programme resources go to the performing arts (museum website).
- Visiting: open daily 10 am to 5 pm except Tuesday. Adults $25, seniors and veterans $22, students $15, ages 6 to 16 $10, under 6 free; $2 for holders of Massachusetts EBT, WIC and ConnectorCare cards. Wheelchairs, gallery stools and listening devices are available on request.

FUNDING
MASS MoCA: mixed: private foundations and individual giving, ticket income, and public capital money
- Form 990 data (ProPublica): in the year to September 2025 revenue was $20,153,078, of which contributions were $12,474,577; expenses $21,578,422; net assets $83,199,152. In FY2024 revenue was $18,328,954 against expenses of $20,678,157.
- The museum says a ticket covers only one third of the cost of each visitor experience, and the rest comes from gifts, grants, sponsors and endowment (museum website).
- Barr Foundation: $1 million to build the museum's capacity to work with the City of North Adams, including staff and infrastructure consultants (museum release, about February 2024). The Barr Foundation logo also appears on the museum's support page.
- Andrew W. Mellon Foundation: $500,000 over 24 months from September 2023 for technical and production residencies (Mellon Foundation).
- National Endowment for the Arts: a $50,000 grant awarded in 2024 for Jeffrey Gibson's installation was terminated in May 2025; the Institute of Museum and Library Services also cut funding it had awarded (ArtReview).
- State: $18.6 million from the Commonwealth of Massachusetts for the original project (Wikipedia). The museum's support page also shows the Massachusetts Office of Travel & Tourism logo, without saying what it provides.
- No reporting found of a Massachusetts Cultural Council grant, or of named corporate sponsors with amounts.

Red flags: wage strikes by museum staff
- 6 to 26 March 2024: about 120 members of UAW Local 2110 struck over wages. The union said 58% of the unit earned $16.25 an hour, about $43,600 a year full-time (Hyperallergic, WSWS).
- The union asked for an $18.25 minimum and 4.5% raises; the museum had offered $17.25 and 3.5% (Hyperallergic). The settlement set an $18 minimum within 30 days, 3.5% raises each year, and an average rise of 12.1% by the second year (Hyperallergic, The Art Newspaper).
- It was the second strike in two years: a one-day wage strike took place in 2022 (The Art Newspaper).
- The museum stayed open during the 2024 strike, which WSWS, an openly socialist outlet, calls strikebreaking; WSWS also calls the settlement a sellout and says $18 is below a living wage in western Massachusetts.
- Edmunds called the deal a "watershed moment"; the museum said it would close Wednesdays in April "while managing financial risks" (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum in art media beyond the 2024 labour dispute, which is reported rather than argued against the museum.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "After 19-Day Strike, MASS MoCA Workers Secure Higher Wages" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/880592/after-19-day-strike-mass-moca-workers-secure-higher-wage/
- Argues prior pay was too low: a few thousand dollars below an estimated single-adult living cost in Berkshire County.
- Names director Kristy Edmunds and UAW Local 2110. The union had not responded to a request for comment.
2. The Art Newspaper: Anni Irish, "Mass Moca employees end three-week strike following dispute over wages" (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/27/mass-moca-employees-end-three-week-strike-following-dispute-over-wages
- Not criticism: reports both sides' statements neutrally, including Edmunds saying wage increases were "never a matter of if, but a matter of how fast".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind MASS MoCA is largely foundation and individual giving plus a state capital grant, with no flagged funders found. The weak point is staff pay: two strikes in two years, the second over pay of $16.25 an hour for 58% of the unit. Worth watching: finances, since expenses exceeded revenue in FY2024 and FY2025, and the loss of federal grants.
