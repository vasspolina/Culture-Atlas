PARRISH ART MUSEUM: Water Mill, USA
ID: parrish-art-museum-water-mill
Website: https://parrishart.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 279 Montauk Highway in Water Mill, on Long Island's East End, on 14 acres of meadow.
- Founded in 1898 by Quaker attorney Samuel Longstreth Parrish as the Art Museum of Southampton; it opened its Water Mill building on 10 November 2012 (Wikipedia).
- The museum carries the surname of its founder, Samuel Longstreth Parrish, who founded it to house his Italian Renaissance paintings and copies of classical and Renaissance sculpture (Wikipedia).
- The Water Mill building is a single-storey concrete building of about 34,000 square feet designed by Herzog & de Meuron; the museum bought the site in 2005 for $3.8 million (Wikipedia).
- The collection runs from the 19th century to the present, with the largest public collection of William Merritt Chase's paintings and a major Fairfield Porter holding (Wikipedia). Seven permanent-collection galleries and three temporary exhibition galleries (Wikipedia).
- Open Thursday to Monday, 11 am to 5 pm, with the grounds open daily; adults pay $20, and members, children 18 and under, students with ID, SNAP recipients, veterans and active-duty military, and Shinnecock Nation members enter free.
- Free parking is behind the building, wheelchairs are available on request, and service animals are permitted inside.

FUNDING
Parrish Art Museum (Water Mill): donor-funded: gifts from individuals, foundations and companies, with county, state and federal grants
- Revenue was $14.7 million in fiscal 2024, of which $12.9 million was contributions; expenses were $7.3 million and net assets $44.0 million (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer). In fiscal 2023 revenue was $4.7 million and expenses $6.1 million (ProPublica).
- The 2024 donor list (museum website) names Bank of America in the $80,000 to $100,000+ tier, with residents of the Southampton School District in the same tier.
- $50,000 to $79,999: Wells Group of New York, the Gerton / Arts and Letters Foundation and The Rosenkranz Foundation.
- $20,000 to $49,999: the Institute of Museum and Library Services, the New York State Council on the Arts, Suffolk County, J.P. Morgan Private Bank, Related Companies, Arias New York, Wölffer Estate Vineyard, Bloomberg Philanthropies, the Neuwirth Foundation, the Derfner Foundation, the CAL Foundation, the Movado Group Foundation, the Penthesilea Fund, the Robert Lehman Foundation and the Harold & Arlene Schnitzer CARE Foundation.
- The museum's own About page says programmes are supported in part by the New York State Council on the Arts and by property taxpayers in the Southampton and Tuckahoe Common school districts.
- Gifts in kind in 2024 came from Campari Group, Casa Dragones, Christie's, Clare V., Dry Farm Wines and Wölffer Estate Vineyard.
- The museum's About page links its annual reports at parrishart.org/about/financials/.

BOTTOM LINE
The Parrish is a private non-profit museum paid for mainly by contributions: its 2024 donor list names banks, local companies, family foundations and Bloomberg Philanthropies, plus county, state and federal grants in the $20,000 to $49,999 tier. The 2024 donor list and ProPublica filings document the money.
