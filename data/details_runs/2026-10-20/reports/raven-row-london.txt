RAVEN ROW: London, United Kingdom
ID: raven-row-london
Website: https://www.ravenrow.org (checked 2026-10-25; loads, but the page text could not be read, so no content was confirmed from the site itself)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art exhibition centre at 56 and 58 Artillery Lane, Spitalfields, near Liverpool Street station (Time Out; Wikipedia for the street address).
- Founded by Alex Sainsbury, who bought the two houses in 2004 and opened the gallery in 2009 with a show by the New York artist Ray Johnson. Sainsbury set up a charity to run it (Wikipedia, Another Magazine).
- Takes its name from the stretch of Artillery Lane that was called Raven Row until 1895 (Wikipedia).
- Building: two houses built about 1690 and rebuilt in the 1750s for Huguenot silk weavers and traders (Wikipedia). 6a Architects converted them and added two contemporary galleries at the back (The Art Newspaper). The houses stood empty for ten years before the launch (The Art Newspaper).
- Programme: exhibitions, with Sainsbury running the programme and sometimes curating, alongside guest curators (Galleries Now). It closed its exhibition programme in 2017 and reopened in January 2023 with "People Make Television", a show of BBC community programming from the 1970s and 1980s (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper). Sainsbury said there was always going to be an intermission, and that the building lease runs for at least 20 years (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm (Time Out, Galleries Now); free (Time Out, Wikipedia). No reporting found of its accessibility arrangements.

FUNDING
Raven Row: self-funded: a private trust settled by its founder Alex Sainsbury
- The gallery is funded by the Glass-House Trust, of which Sainsbury is settlor (Galleries Now).
- Sainsbury is described as a collector, curator and supermarket heir, and says he funds the gallery himself, calling it a "streamlined operation" (The Art Newspaper, 2022).
- Trustees of the charitable company: Alex Sainsbury, Alex Farquharson (Director, Tate Britain), Melanie Keen and Jenni Lomax (Galleries Now).
- No reporting found of any corporate sponsor, public grant or other named donor.
- No reporting found of the gallery's accounts from the pages read; the Charity Commission register pages could not be opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the institution. The pieces found are exhibition reviews in a general listings magazine, and they criticise individual shows, not the space or its funding. No review in the art press was found that questions the space's money.
1. Time Out: Eddy Frankel, "Chronoplasticity" (2024)
URL: https://timeout.com/london/art/chronoplasticity
- Gives the show 3 out of 5. Argues the show works as a loose visual mixtape without a cohesive theme, saying it "should've been called" "Some stuff Lars Bang Larsen likes".
- Praises the tapestries by the Black Panthers and the Zapatistas and calls the upstairs mini-exhibition "great".
- Criticises the curation of one show, not the institution, its founder or its funding.
2. Time Out: Katie McCabe, "56 Artillery Lane" (2017)
URL: https://www.timeout.com/london/art/56-artillery-lane
- Gives the show 4 out of 5 and mostly praises it. Says it "doesn't always come off", citing a Stanley Spencer painting set beside copper-painted furniture oxidised with urine.
- Criticises the hang of one show, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Raven Row is a single private trust funded by its founder, a supermarket heir, with no corporate or public funder reported. The weak points are that the funding is one person's choice and that the pages read give no accounts or donor detail. Worth watching: the trust's accounts on the Charity Commission register, which this research could not open.
