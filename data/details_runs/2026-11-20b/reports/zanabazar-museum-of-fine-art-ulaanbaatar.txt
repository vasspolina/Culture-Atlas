ZANABAZAR MUSEUM OF FINE ART: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia
ID: zanabazar-museum-of-fine-art-ulaanbaatar
Website: http://www.zanabazarmuseum.mn/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Mongolian fine art in Chingeltei District, Ulaanbaatar (iKon.mn); the National Centre for Cultural Heritage (NCCH) lists it among Mongolia's museums.
- A Council of Ministers resolution (No. 87, 31 March 1965) set up the Fine Arts Museum; it opened on 23 July 1966 with 350 works, and its modern art became the separate Painting Gallery in 1989 (museum site, in Mongolian; own translation). Google Arts & Culture gives over 300 works at the opening.
- Named in 1995, on his 360th anniversary, for Gombodorjiin Zanabazar (1635 to 1724), the first Jebtsundamba Khutuktu (museum site), whose works it holds, among them the Sita Tara statues, the Five Dhyani Buddhas and the Bodhi Stupa (Google Arts & Culture).
- Building: a Russian merchant put up the two-storey trading house in 1913 to 1914; a bank issued paper money there, it held a Soviet Red Army commandant's office from July 1921, the State Department Store from 1930 to 1961 and the Mongolian Craftsmen's Union committee from 1961 to 1965. It has been on the capital's heritage list since 2002 and the state-protected list since 2020 (museum site; own translation).
- Collection: the museum gives nearly 20,000 objects (over 18,000 in its own timeline) in more than 10 halls, about a tenth of them on show, including 68 of the more than 200 works the government listed as unique between 1996 and 2007 (museum site). NCCH gives more than 16,000 objects in 10 halls; Google Arts & Culture gives nearly 16,600 in 12 galleries, including thangkas, coral masks and B. Sharav's paintings "A Day in Mongolia" and "Airag Feast".
- Director since 2020: Pürevsükh Baigalmaa, PhD (museum site).
- Recent programme: a solo show of more than 70 silk paintings by the master artisan Ukhaanzaya Dorjnamjin, 15 to 21 October 2025 (Qazinform, citing MONTSAME).
- Neighbouring tower: the museum sued the capital city's planning office over its approval of a 28-storey office and banking building for Trade and Development Bank beside the museum, arguing that groundwater settlement could crack its protected building, that the tower would stand three metres from its protected zone and that no protective measures were planned. The first-instance court dismissed the claim, the appeal court upheld that, and on 2 December 2025 the Supreme Court upheld both rulings (G. Mörön, iKon.mn, 4 December 2025, "Г.Занабазарын нэрэмжит музейн дэргэд 28 давхар барилга барихыг эсэргүүцсэн нэхэмжлэлийг гурван шатны шүүх хэрэгсэхгүй болгов", translated: three levels of court dismiss the claim against a 28-storey building beside the museum).

FUNDING
Zanabazar Museum of Fine Art: public: a state museum, with foreign project grants
- Founded under the Ministry of Culture by the 1965 government resolution (museum site); NCCH carries its page on the government domain ncch.gov.mn (NCCH).
- Since 2011, by decision of the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, the museum spends the income it earns itself (museum site; own translation).
- UNESCO: a 2004 to 2007 project, "Development of the Zanabazar Museum of Fine Arts and protection of nomadic cultural heritage", under UNESCO's programme for heritage protection and museum development, covered registration, conservation and staff training (museum site; own translation of the title).
- US Ambassador's Fund for Cultural Preservation: from August 2010, under a project the museum's staff wrote, restorers of the cultural heritage centre cleaned, consolidated and restored the finds from the Shoroon Bumbagar tomb in Zaamar sum, Töv province, which then joined the collection (museum site).
- Prince Claus Fund (Netherlands): an audio-guide project under an agreement of 3 July 2012 (museum site).

BOTTOM LINE
The money on record is Mongolian state money and foreign cultural grants, none of them from a flagged source. Worth watching: the protected building beside the planned bank tower.
