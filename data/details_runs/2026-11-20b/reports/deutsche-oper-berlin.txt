DEUTSCHE OPER BERLIN: Berlin, Germany
ID: deutsche-oper-berlin
Website: https://deutscheoperberlin.de/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Opera house at Bismarckstraße 34 to 37 in Charlottenburg, Berlin, and the largest of the city's three opera houses (German Wikipedia).
- The City of Charlottenburg built the first house, the Deutsches Opernhaus by Heinrich Seeling, which opened on 7 November 1912; it was destroyed on 23 November 1943 (German Wikipedia).
- Building: Fritz Bornemann's post-war house, built from 1957 to 1961 behind a concrete and pebble facade, opened on 24 September 1961 with "Don Giovanni"; it has 1,859 seats (German Wikipedia).
- Since 1 January 2004 it has been one of the houses of the Stiftung Oper in Berlin, the public foundation that also runs the Staatsoper Unter den Linden, the Komische Oper, the Staatsballett and the Bühnenservice (Jüdische Allgemeine; Berlin budget plan).
- Leadership: Aviel Cahn, formerly head of the Grand Théâtre de Genève, became Intendant on 1 August 2026, succeeding Dietmar Schwarz, who led the house from 2012 until his contract ended in summer 2025 (Berliner Zeitung, 11 April 2024; Jüdische Allgemeine). Thomas Fehrle is managing director (opera imprint).
- Conductors: principal guest conductors Maxime Pascal and Michele Spotti and conductor in residence Titus Engel; the Icelandic composer Bára Gísladóttir is composer in residence and is writing a new opera for the house (opera website).
- Audience: occupancy was close to 90 percent in early 2024, according to Fehrle (Berliner Zeitung, 11 April 2024).

FUNDING
Deutsche Oper Berlin: public: the State of Berlin through the Stiftung Oper in Berlin
- State of Berlin: the 2026/2027 budget gives the Stiftung Oper in Berlin 174,107,000 euros for 2026 and 180,348,000 euros for 2027, after the governing CDU and SPD groups cut the draft figures of 174,357,000 and 180,798,000 euros by 250,000 and 450,000 euros on 14 November 2025 (Berlin budget plan, Einzelplan 08; Berlin parliament main committee amendment).
- The Deutsche Oper's share of that grant is 56,159,100 euros in 2026 and 58,039,000 euros in 2027, up from 52,505,750 euros actually paid in 2024 (Berlin budget plan, Einzelplan 08).
- Its 2026 business plan expects 75,717,700 euros of income: 56,895,700 euros from Berlin, 3,582,000 euros from the federal government and other states, and 12,281,200 euros of sales such as tickets (same budget plan).
- Deficits: the plan shows an annual loss of 1,810,836 euros in 2024, a planned loss of 3,950,100 euros in 2025 and 900,100 euros in 2026 (same budget plan).
- Förderkreis der Deutschen Oper Berlin e.V.: the friends' association, chaired by Heike Maria von Joest, pays towards productions, with 16 shows in 2026/27 credited "mit Unterstützung des Förderkreises", and funds young singers through its Talent Circle, with scholarships named for the families von Falkenhausen/van Aubel, Sandvoss and Landt and for Opera Foundation Australia and Opera Foundation New York (opera website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution itself; the one critical piece on record concerns the new Intendant's programming.
1. Berliner Zeitung: Peter Uehling, "Die Spielzeit an der Deutschen Oper: Heißer Scheiß im Glanz des Gestrigen" (2026)
URL: https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/kultur-vergnuegen/die-spielzeit-an-der-deutschen-oper-heisser-scheiss-im-glanz-des-gestrigen-li.10030351
- Argues that Cahn's first season for 2026/27 is pulled between box-office success and artistic ambition, close to "schizophrenic" (own translation).
- The repertoire leans on revivals of productions about 60 years old ("Tosca", "Lucia di Lammermoor") and three 30-year-old Götz Friedrich stagings, which must bring in the audiences the new productions will drive away.
- Criticises the programme, not the funding. Paywalled; only excerpts were read.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Berlin state money with a modest friends' association behind it. Worth watching are the recurring operating losses and the house's share of a tight Berlin culture budget.
