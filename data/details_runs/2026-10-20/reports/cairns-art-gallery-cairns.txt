CAIRNS ART GALLERY: Cairns, Australia
ID: cairns-art-gallery-cairns
Website: https://www.cairnsartgallery.com.au (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Regional public art gallery in central Cairns, Far North Queensland, run by the company Cairns Art Gallery Limited.
- Opened on 15 July 1995 as the Cairns Regional Gallery; the idea of a public gallery for Cairns dates from 1979 (stated by the space).
- Housed in the Public Curators Building, designed by government architect R.C. Nowland and built from 1934 to 1936; government departments used it until its conversion to a gallery began in 1994.
- The building is part of the Cairns Court House Complex on the Queensland Heritage Register; the conversion kept the terrazzo floor, timber wall panelling and stained glass windows.
- Since 2026 the gallery also runs the former Court House and the Mulgrave Gallery, in the former Mulgrave Shire Council offices, as one precinct; it holds a permanent collection and a First Nations research archive.
- The Public Curators Building opens Monday to Friday 9am to 5pm, Saturday 10am to 5pm and Sunday 10am to 2pm; the Mulgrave and Court House buildings close on Sunday. Entry is free. No accessibility information was found on the site.

FUNDING
Cairns Art Gallery: mainly public: Cairns Regional Council, with foundation and club sponsorship
- Cairns Regional Council pays about 55 to 60 percent of annual operating costs; the rest comes from commercial activity, sponsorship and donations (stated by the space).
- Mayors, councillors, state ministers, artists and residents raised more than $2 million for the 1994 to 1995 conversion (stated by the space).
- The Mulgrave Gallery cost $24.3 million: $12.1 million from the Australian Government and $12.2 million from Cairns Regional Council, with the Queensland Government as a partner (Cairns Local News, September 2024).
- The Cairns Art Gallery Foundation, set up in 2000 with its own volunteer board chaired by Sarah Mort, has given more than $840,000 towards 79 works by 28 artists (stated by the space). Cairns Airport chief executive Richard Barker is one of its patrons (Cairns Local News, June 2025).
- Cairns RSL Club supported the Artists Fellowship Program, three fellowships of $7,500 each in 2021 and again in 2022.
- No reporting found of the Double R Arts Foundation or of who stands behind it.
- No reporting found of fellowship rounds after 2022.

Red flags: gambling club sponsorship
- Cairns RSL Club runs a sports bar with gaming machines, Keno and TAB betting (Wanderlog venue listing).
- The club sponsored the gallery's Artists Fellowship Program in the 2021 and 2022 rounds, $22,500 a year (gallery exhibition pages).
- No reporting found of a protest or objection to the sponsorship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery runs mostly on Cairns Regional Council money, with federal money for the Mulgrave building and acquisitions paid for by its own foundation. The one weak point is sponsorship of the 2021 and 2022 fellowships by a club that earns from gaming machines and betting. Worth watching: whether the RSL sponsorship continues, and who is behind the Double R Arts Foundation.
