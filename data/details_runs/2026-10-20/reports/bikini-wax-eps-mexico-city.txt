BIKINI WAX EPS: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: bikini-wax-eps-mexico-city

OVERVIEW
- Independent art project space run by a collective of artists, writers, poets and filmmakers in Mexico City; sources spell the name "Biquini Wax EPS". The city's tourism site gives the address as Pedro de Alba 232, Iztaccihuatl, Benito Juárez; My Art Guides gives Lago Ayarza 22, Torre Blanca, Miguel Hidalgo.
- Elephant (2019) names Daniel Aguilar Ruvalcaba as founder: he started it in 2011 at his grandmother's home in Guanajuato and moved it to Mexico City in 2013 when he went to study art. My Art Guides instead says artist Israel Urmeer founded it in 2008 in Heroica Zitácuaro, Michoacán, and that it has run in Mexico City since 2011.
- Elephant describes a "contemporary art sect" with twelve members and no leader; members do not show their own work in the space.
- From March 2017 to October 2018 the group occupied a squatted building in the Doctores neighbourhood (Elephant). My Art Guides lists it as housed in an apartment where some members live.
- Programme: exhibitions, performances, reading groups and lectures, up to two or three events a month (My Art Guides). It describes itself as a "self-organised study centre/experimental exhibition-making (para)site" and is a member of the de Appel Curatorial Programme 2024 to 2026 (de Appel).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday, 1pm to 8pm (My Art Guides). The city tourism site recommends checking the venue's Instagram for current events.

FUNDING
Bikini Wax EPS: collectively run, with named backers
- My Art Guides lists its supporters as Tacos Güero, Roy's and Moncho's parents, Tezcatlipoca, and PAC (Patronato de Arte Contemporáneo).
- The Mexico City tourism site describes the space as "entirely collective".
- Elephant quotes Ruvalcaba: "We don't want to make it a platform for us. It's for everyone." Its 2017 to 2018 programme of exhibitions, performances, workshops and seminars was free.

BOTTOM LINE
The documented support is a list of named backers, including the Patronato de Arte Contemporáneo, and the city tourism site calls the space entirely collective. Sources differ on its founding: Elephant names Daniel Aguilar Ruvalcaba (2011), My Art Guides names Israel Urmeer (2008).
