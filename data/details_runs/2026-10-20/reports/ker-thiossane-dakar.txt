KËR THIOSSANE: Dakar, Senegal
ID: ker-thiossane-dakar
Website: https://ker-thiossane.org (checked 2026-10-25; down, the connection was refused; an earlier attempt on 2026-10-24 timed out)

OVERVIEW
- Villa for art and multimedia in the Sicap neighbourhood of Dakar, run as a Senegalese non-governmental association (Pixelache, 2010; Agenda 21 for Culture). A 2024 listing gives the address as Villa N 1695, Sicap Liberté 2 (au-senegal.com).
- Activities began in 2002. Pixelache names Marion Louisgrand-Sylla and François Sylla as founders; the Daniel Langlois Foundation says it was initiated by Momar François Sylla, an artist and musician, and that it officially opened in September 2003.
- Sylla grew up in the villa and wanted a space rooted in the Sicap neighbourhood; Louisgrand-Sylla had worked in Paris at Mains d'Oeuvres (Pixelache).
- Programmes: multimedia workshops and production residencies, the Afropixel festival (first held in 2008, then 2010, 2012, 2014 and 2016 according to the Moleskine Foundation), and the School of the Commons, launched around Afropixel in 2014 (Agenda 21 for Culture).
- In November 2024 it showed an installation by Mour Fall and Cléophée Moser, made in a Strats4Africa residency held there, as part of the Biennale de Dakar 2024 (au-senegal.com).
- Visiting: no reporting found of regular opening days, admission or accessibility. The 2024 exhibition ran 8 to 28 November, 10:30 to 18:30 daily (au-senegal.com).

FUNDING
Kër Thiossane: project-funded: small association living on grants from the Daniel Langlois Foundation and the EU's ACP Cultures programme
- Daniel Langlois Foundation (Montreal): a 2001 grant under its Program for Organizations from Emerging Regions paid for a pilot workshop and performance for the Dak'Art 2002 biennale, computer equipment, renovation of the villa and the project website. The foundation states no amount.
- European Union, ACP Cultures programme (European Development Fund): about 262 million CFA francs (EUR 399,409) for the "Rose des vents numérique" project, reported on 28 October 2009 citing the APS news agency (Osiris). Pixelache calls it the first EU funding the villa received and describes a two-year project linking independent multimedia centres and artists across Africa and the Caribbean.
- Pixelache wrote in 2010 that the villa had been open only intermittently because of inconsistent resources.
- The School of the Commons factsheet names the City of Dakar as a partner and gives no funders or budget (Agenda 21 for Culture).
- No reporting found of UNESCO International Fund for Cultural Diversity or Francophonie money on any page opened.
- No reporting found of recent annual reports, accounts or a board list; the association's own site could not be opened.

BOTTOM LINE
The money found behind Kër Thiossane is small foreign grant money: the Langlois Foundation in 2001 and an EU ACP Cultures grant announced in 2009, with nothing in the pages opened tying it to a flagged industry or a government. The weak points are thin, old reporting on funders (nothing after 2009 with an amount) and a website that is down. Worth watching: who funds its current Afropixel and School of the Commons work.
