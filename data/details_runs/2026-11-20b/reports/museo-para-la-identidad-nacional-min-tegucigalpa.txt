MUSEO PARA LA IDENTIDAD NACIONAL (MIN): Tegucigalpa, Honduras
ID: museo-para-la-identidad-nacional-min-tegucigalpa
Website: https://www.min.hn (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- History and culture museum in the former Palacio de los Ministerios in Barrio Abajo, in the historic centre of Tegucigalpa (museum website).
- Opened on 19 January 2006 under President Ricardo Maduro (El Heraldo, 2019); the museum says Maduro conceived the project after visiting the Panama Canal Museum (museum website).
- Building: Marco Aurelio Soto laid the first stone in 1882; it first housed the city's General Hospital, which moved out in May 1926, then several ministries; it was restored from late 2004 and is listed as a national monument by the Honduran Institute of Anthropology and History (museum website). Spanish Wikipedia adds a second storey in 1933, under Tiburcio Carías Andino.
- Permanent halls cover Honduras's geological origins, geography, historical formation and present, plus a Copán Virtual hall and a hall of busts of national heroes (Spanish Wikipedia); temporary shows have included Goya, Picasso, Miró and Rembrandt (El Heraldo).
- Programmes: the EDUMIN school programme, Zotz Tours walks through the historic centre, Mini Artistas workshops for ages 6 to 12 at a suggested L. 100, a Saturday chess space, a volunteer programme and an Amigos del MIN membership (museum website).
- Executive director: Eva Carolina Gómez (museum website). About 930,000 people visited between 2006 and 2019 (Spanish Wikipedia); El Heraldo gave an annual average of 70,000 in 2019.
- The museum marked its 20th anniversary on 30 October 2026 (TuNota).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday 9:00 to 17:00, Sunday 10:00 to 16:00 (Spanish Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Museo para la Identidad Nacional: private non-profit foundation with an endowment and corporate patrons, set up under a state project
- Operator: the Fundación Hondureña para la Identidad Nacional (FHIN), a non-profit, runs the museum (museum website).
- Board: named on 25 November 2022 under president Thierry de Pierrefeu, with "the challenge of backing the museum's sustainability in the time of the greatest financial crisis lived through by the museum" (museum website, own translation). Members: Aline Flores Pavón (vice president), Arturo Zacapa (secretary), Ana Carolina Galeano Burgos, Lorena Isabel Maduro Andreu (treasurer), Ruy Axel Merriam Rubiano, Leonel Zacarias Bendeck Nasser (fiscal), Lizzeth Guadalupe Villatoro Flores, José Alfredo Castillo Callejas, Hilda Mercedes Castañeda Moncada and Karla Sofía Simón Saide.
- Endowment: De Pierrefeu said the museum won credibility with local donors, the international community and multilateral bodies from its start, and that an independent endowment fund separated it from changing government budgets (TuNota, 30 October 2026).
- Corporate patrons: Televicentro and Emisoras Unidas are a "socio pilar y fundador" (pillar and founding partner, own translation) of the museum; Grupo Flores, Grupo ILP, Grupo Ficohsa and the agency La Pulpe were strategic allies of the anniversary launch in May 2026 (TuNota, 22 May 2026). De Pierrefeu said patrons pay for free entry for more than 400 public-school pupils a day (TuNota).
- Earned income: venue hire, a shop and memberships (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One essay criticises the national story the museum tells; none addresses its funding.
1. Hyperallergic: Annette Lin, "How a Museum Wields Its Power in Crafting a National Identity" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/519157/how-a-museum-wields-its-power-in-crafting-a-national-identity/
- The permanent galleries treat the Maya as if they were the only pre-colonial culture, though they lived only in the western fringes of modern Honduras.
- Quotes the historian Darío A. Euraque, who calls this the "Mayanization" of Honduras, a policy dating to Tiburcio Carías Andino's dictatorship of the 1940s; nearly 20 per cent of Hondurans identify as indigenous or minority, including Lenca, Miskito, Garífuna and Maya Ch'orti'.
- The interactive screens and VR paint a "sunny picture" beside emigration of over 250,000 Hondurans in a year, more than 120,000 dengue cases and nationwide protests.
- The current page asks readers to register; the full text was read in the Internet Archive copy of November 2020.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on a private foundation, an endowment and Honduran corporate patrons led by a television group, with no flagged funder on record. It publishes no accounts, so the size of the endowment, each patron's gift and the 2022 financial crisis remain unexplained.
