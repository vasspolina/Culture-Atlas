NATIONAL GALLERIES OF SCOTLAND: Edinburgh, UK
ID: national-galleries-of-scotland
Website: https://www.nationalgalleries.org (checked 2026-10-24; returned HTTP 403 to automated requests, so its pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Executive non-departmental public body that runs the Scottish National Gallery, the Scottish National Portrait Gallery and the Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art in Edinburgh, and two partner galleries, Duff House and Paxton House (Wikipedia).
- The National Gallery opened on The Mound in 1859 and the Portrait Gallery in 1889; the modern art gallery was established in Inverleith House in 1959, moved to the former John Watson's Institution in 1984, and the Dean Gallery (now Modern Two) opened in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Governed under the National Galleries of Scotland Act 1906, as amended in 1985, by trustees appointed by Scottish Government ministers; registered charity, about 327 staff, Director-General Anne Lyden (Wikipedia).
- New Scottish galleries opened at the National in September 2023 (The Art Newspaper, Rachel Ashcroft, 12 January 2024). No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility details.

FUNDING
National Galleries of Scotland: mainly public: Scottish Government grant-in-aid, plus donations, membership and sponsors including Baillie Gifford
- Scottish Government grant-in-aid; for 2024/25 about 95% goes on salaries, leaving 5% for running costs. A dossier by senior executives expects a deficit, and energy costs rose 72% in two years (The Art Newspaper, Rachel Ashcroft, 12 January 2024).
- The galleries hope to cover running costs through visitor donations and membership (The Art Newspaper). No income totals could be confirmed: the 2023-24 accounts PDF could not be read.
- Baillie Gifford, an Edinburgh asset manager: sponsor of the galleries; Baillie Gifford declined to discuss the value or length of the sponsorship (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 2 July 2024).
- BP: the galleries hosted the BP Portrait Award, shown in London first and then touring to Edinburgh; they ended it "in its present form" after the 2019 show (Hyperallergic, 11 November 2019). No reporting found of a full donor list.

Red flags: protest against Baillie Gifford sponsorship
- 2 July 2024: the galleries said they would keep Baillie Gifford as a sponsor after protests over its ties to fossil fuels and Israel. Director-General Anne Lyden said the galleries are "confident that the funding we receive from Baillie Gifford meets our strong ethical standards"; an ethics committee reviewed the relationship and leaders consulted the Scottish Government (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw).
- Campaign: the Fossil Free Books campaign demanded that Baillie Gifford divest from fossil fuels and from companies involved in Israeli occupation; its statement had more than 800 signatories. In May 2024, after protests, Baillie Gifford ended its sponsorship of nine book festivals (The Art Newspaper). Baillie Gifford says 2% of client money is in fossil fuel companies against a market average of 11% (its own statement).
- 4 August 2026: Snack (Zoë White), interviewing Art Workers for Palestine Scotland, an openly partisan advocacy group whose campaign targets the Edinburgh International Festival, reports in its own words that "many of the largest institutions including the National Galleries of Scotland still receive backing from the firm".

Red flags: BP sponsorship dropped under pressure
- 2016 and 2018: the Scottish branch of the activist group BP or Not BP? protested the BP Portrait Award at the Edinburgh gallery, in 2016 dressed as BP executives, in 2018 at the main entrance. 11 November 2019: the galleries announced they would no longer host the exhibition "in its present form" after the 2019 prize; trustees cited the "climate emergency" (Hyperallergic, Naomi Polonsky).

Overall: Criticism concerns sponsors, not the collection or buildings; art press coverage is mostly news reports of the campaigns and the galleries' replies.

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the money is Scottish Government grant, of which 95% goes on salaries in 2024/25, so private income matters to the galleries. The weak points are the Baillie Gifford sponsorship, kept in 2024 after protests, and its undisclosed value. Worth watching: whether Baillie Gifford stays and whether the accounts disclose its gift.
