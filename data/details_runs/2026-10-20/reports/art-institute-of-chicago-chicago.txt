ART INSTITUTE OF CHICAGO: Chicago, USA
ID: art-institute-of-chicago-chicago
Website: https://www.artic.edu/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum and art school at 111 South Michigan Avenue in Grant Park, on city land administered by the Chicago Park District (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1879 as the Chicago Academy of Fine Arts, on the assets of the bankrupt Chicago Academy of Design (1866); renamed the Art Institute of Chicago in 1882, with banker Charles L. Hutchinson as first president (Wikipedia).
- The Beaux-Arts building by Shepley, Rutan and Coolidge was built for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition; Renzo Piano's Modern Wing, 264,000 square feet costing $294 million, opened on 16 May 2009 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds nearly 300,000 works in 11 curatorial departments; the School of the Art Institute of Chicago (SAIC) is part of the same institution; James Rondeau has led it since 2016.
- Open Monday and Wednesday 11:00 to 17:00, Thursday to 20:00, Friday to Sunday to 17:00, closed Tuesday; adults $32, Chicago residents $20, Illinois residents $27; free for children under 14, Chicago teens, Link and WIC cardholders and Illinois educators.

FUNDING
Art Institute of Chicago: privately funded: earned income, endowment and family fortunes, on city-owned land
- Fiscal year to June 2025: revenue $464.4 million, of which $250.4 million (53.9%) programme revenue, $80.5 million contributions, $21.6 million investment income; net assets $1.95 billion; Rondeau was paid $1,024,393 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- Kenneth and Anne Griffin gave $19 million toward the Modern Wing, one of the largest private gifts to the museum; its central court is named for them, and Kenneth Griffin sat on the board (The Art Newspaper, 2008).
- Gifts of $70 million (2018) and $75 million (2024, for a new modern art building) are recorded (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the size of Crown family or Pritzker gifts to the museum.

Red flags: Crown family and General Dynamics
- Steven Crown, a principal of Henry Crown and Company, is a trustee; the museum has a Henry Crown Gallery (Chicago Journal blog, 2023).
- ArtReview reports a 10 percent stake in weapons maker General Dynamics tied to Crown Family Philanthropies (2025).
- On 10 November 2023 about 75 protesters, mostly SAIC students, demanded the trustees refuse Crown donations, remove Steven Crown and rename the Henry Crown Gallery (Chicago Journal blog).

Red flags: Gaza encampment and arrests, 2024
- On 4 May 2024 SAIC students and local groups camped in the North Garden, demanding the museum disclose its funding and divest from backers of Israel, naming the Crown Foundation and General Dynamics (Hyperallergic).
- Police arrested 68 people; the museum said a security officer was shoved and exits were blocked, and later dropped trespassing charges (Hyperallergic, ABC7).
- On 24 October 2024 about 200 students, faculty and staff walked out demanding SAIC cut ties with the Crown family, from the World Socialist Web Site, an openly partisan socialist outlet.

Red flags: Thomas Pritzker and Jeffrey Epstein
- Pritzker is a past chair of the museum's board (Wikipedia, Hyperallergic).
- In unsealed 2024 court papers Virginia Giuffre said "I believe I was with Tom once"; Pritzker "continues to vehemently deny" it (Hyperallergic).
- Released emails show contact with Epstein from at least 2010 to early 2019 (WBEZ); Pritzker quit as Hyatt executive chairman on 16 February 2026, admitting "terrible judgment" (PBS).
- No reporting found of a museum response or of Pritzker's current board status.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In September 2025, 21 participants in the Chicago Architecture Biennial signed a letter over sponsor Crown Family Philanthropies' General Dynamics stake, and some withdrew (ArtReview).
- The same family has a trustee and a named gallery at the Art Institute.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on earned income and a large endowment. The weak points are the Crown family's arms fortune on its board, a former chair tied to Epstein, and the 2024 arrests of its own students. Worth watching: whether it answers the disclosure demands.
