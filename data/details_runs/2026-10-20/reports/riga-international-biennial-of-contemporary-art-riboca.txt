RIGA INTERNATIONAL BIENNIAL OF CONTEMPORARY ART (RIBOCA): Riga, Latvia
ID: riga-international-biennial-of-contemporary-art-riboca
Website: https://riboca.com (checked 2026-10-24; parked, redirects to a GoDaddy for-sale page)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennial in Riga, focused on the Baltic region; it has no permanent building and used sites around Riga and in Jūrmala.
- Founded and directed by Agniya Mirgorodskaya through the Riga Biennial Foundation, announced in 2017; the first edition ran 2 June to 28 October 2018 (Wikipedia).
- RIBOCA1, "Everything Was Forever, Until It Was No More", was curated by Katerina Gregos and used eight sites, among them the former biology faculty and the Andrejsala docks (The Art Newspaper, Wikipedia).
- RIBOCA3, curated by René Block, was postponed in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine; the 2023 programme, due on 10 August, was suspended in July 2023 (ArtReview).
- Visiting: the last Riga edition, RIBOCA2, ran from 20 August to 13 September 2020 at Andrejsala (Wikipedia); the 2023 Riga programme never opened and the website is parked.

FUNDING
RIBOCA: private: the founder's family money; the organisers said funding moved to a US charity after 2022
- 2018: the first edition "is funded by the St Petersburg-based fishing entrepreneur Gennady Mirgorodsky", father of the founder (The Art Newspaper).
- After the war began, an undated RIBOCA statement said financing would come through Just A Moment, a US charity registered by Mirgorodskaya and her husband Robert William Pokora, an American real estate investor (USA Art News, 12 July 2023).
- Pokora agreed to give a fixed percentage of his earnings to a new endowment fund, a temporary solution (USA Art News, 7 July 2023).
- Art Focus Now (21 July 2023) states that only 150,000 euros came directly from Pokora's business and that the largest donation came from Mirgorodskaya herself, printing that amount as "around seven hundred Euros".
- Art Focus Now also states that applications for state funding went to the Latvian Ministry of Culture and the State Fund for Cultural Capital.
- No reporting found of an annual report, accounts, donor list or board.

Red flags: boycott and campaign against the biennial
- 30 June 2023: four Latvian artists withdrew from the third edition: Ēriks Božis, Maija Kurševa, Armands Zelčs and Amanda Ziemele (Art Focus Now, 21 July 2023).
- Kurševa told Collecteurs.com it was "impossible to accept money from a Russian business to produce commissioned artwork" (quoted by Art Focus Now).
- Curator Žanete Liekīte campaigned against the biennial and said the issue was ethical funding, not ethnicity (USA Art News, 12 July 2023).
- Early July 2023: the organisers suspended RIBOCA3, citing the heritage of their executive members as "too significant to overcome" (ArtReview, 7 July 2023; Biennial Foundation, 11 July 2023).
- Unverified: that Common Ground, the refugee centre RIBOCA opened in 2022, was set up to shield the biennial from questions about funding, a suspicion Art Focus Now attributes to some locals.

Red flags: Pyotr Aven, EU-sanctioned supporter and former board member
- Art Focus Now (21 July 2023) states that Pyotr Aven, co-owner of Alfa Bank, supported RIBOCA; it gives no amount or year.
- USA Art News (7 July 2023) writes that Liekīte questioned the unannounced disappearance of Aven from the RIBOCA board; no date for his departure is given.
- The EU listed Aven on 28 February 2022; an EU court annulled the sanctions in April 2024, citing a lack of evidence (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is about the founders and the money, not the art, and comes from Latvian curators and artists quoted in news coverage; no signed art-press essay was found.
1. USA Art News: Romario, "'What We Are Providing May Simply Be Unwanted'" (2023)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/what-we-are-providing-may-simply-be-unwanted-after-backlash-from-the-latvian-art-scene-for-its-links-to-russia-the-riga-biennial-has-called-off-its-third-edition
- Liekīte, in a Latvian opinion piece, argued that helping Ukrainian refugees without naming the aggressor is contradictory: "one hand reaching out to Ukraine, while the other is held on Russia's knee".
- Kurševa called the organisers' explanation about artist fees "entirely unpersuasive". Names Mirgorodskaya, Pokora and Aven.
2. USA Art News: Romario, "Riga biennial cancelled after outcry over organisers' Russian links" (2023)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/riga-biennial-cancelled-after-outcry-over-organisers-russian-links
- Liekīte compared the funding to the Sackler family's and to Warren Kanders's while he ran a tear gas business.
3. Art Focus Now: Grigory Nekhoroshev, "RIBOCA: Postponement or End?" (2023)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/riboca-postponement-or-end/
- Reports critics who saw Russian wealth as soft power and the state-fund applications as unfair competition, while admitting the biennial raised Riga's profile.

BOTTOM LINE
The biennial's money came from a Russian fishing businessman's family and, after 2022, from a US charity the founder and her husband registered; no accounts or donor list are published. The weak points are the family money, Aven's undated link as supporter and former board member, and the lack of disclosure. Watch whether it returns and who pays.
