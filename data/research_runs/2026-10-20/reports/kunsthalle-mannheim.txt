KUNSTHALLE MANNHEIM – Mannheim, Germany
Website: https://www.kuma.art (homepage loaded 2026-10-20 and showed opening hours 10–18; the /en/museum/organisation page returned a fetch error)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum housing the City of Mannheim's art collections. The Jugendstil building by Hermann Billing was built in 1907 for the city's 300th-anniversary International Art Exhibition; the museum opened in 1909 under Fritz Wichert (Wikipedia).
- A new building by gmp (Gerkan, Marg and Partners) opened on 1 June 2018: seven exhibition halls, a 22m glass-roofed atrium, 13,000 sq m, linked to the Billing building (The Art Newspaper, 31 May 2018).
- Director Johan Holten since 2019; Luisa Heese deputy director from 1 June 2025 (Wikipedia). The Stifterkreis page lists Luisa Heese as chair of its Vorstand.
- Admission prices, discounts and accessibility details were not found in the sources retrieved.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Mannheim: mainly municipal, with a large private building gift
- City of Mannheim: owns and runs the museum (Wikipedia; the city's own pages). No reporting found of its annual operating subsidy.
- New building: €68.3M total. Hector Stiftung II Kunst gGmbH €50M; City of Mannheim €10M; Baden-Württemberg state urban development funding €2.62M (cut from a planned €4.5M); private donations and grants just under €4.9M, with a €2.85M gap at the time (Rhein-Neckar-Blog, independent local outlet). The Art Newspaper gives the same €50M of €68M and €10M city figures.
- Hans-Werner Hector, a co-founder of SAP, is the source of the Hector Foundation II money (The Art Newspaper). The H. W. & J. Hector Foundation has also funded exhibition space since 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Kunsthallen Ausstellungs GmbH provides an endowment for exhibition budgets (Wikipedia).
- Stifterkreis: 57 "persons, foundations and firms", none named. Its Stiftungsrat is chaired by Dr Susanne Fuchs and includes Lord Mayor Christian Specht, Culture Mayor Thorsten Riehle, two city councillors and two Förderkreis representatives (stated by the space).
- Long-term loan of 38 Anselm Kiefer works from businessman Hans Grothe (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors by name, or of annual accounts.

Red flags: Nazi-era provenance
- In 2018 the museum found Nazi-looted art in its collection, including Wilhelm Leibl's The Drinker (1874), acquired from a Nazi agency, and listed 25 further works suspected of being looted or sold under duress (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
A city-run museum whose 2018 building was mostly paid for by SAP co-founder Hans-Werner Hector's foundation, with smaller city and state shares. No flagged funder was found. Worth watching: the provenance research on works acquired 1933–45, and the unnamed Stifterkreis members.
