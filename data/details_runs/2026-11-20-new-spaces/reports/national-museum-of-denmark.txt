NATIONAL MUSEUM OF DENMARK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: national-museum-of-denmark
Website: https://nationalmuseet.dk/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Denmark's main museum of cultural history, at Ny Vestergade 10 in central Copenhagen, in the building known as the Prince's Palace.
- The museum counts 22 May 1807, the day the royal commission for the keeping of antiquities was set up, as its birthday (stated by the space). Wikipedia names Christian Jürgensen Thomsen as founder.
- The museum is a state institution and a research institution under the Ministry of Culture, with 15 museums and castles across the country (stated by the space).
- Rane Willerslev has been director since 2017 (Wikipedia).
- In 2024 the museum returned a 17th-century feathered cloak of the Tupinambá people to Brazil (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday except in week 7 and at Easter. Adults pay DKK 150, DKK 135 online, and under 18s enter free.

FUNDING
National Museum of Denmark: half public: a state appropriation, with ticket and shop sales and foundation grants for the other half
- Ordinary operating income in 2025 was DKK 533.3 million, of which the state appropriation was DKK 265.4 million (annual report 2025).
- Sales of goods and services, mainly admissions and shops, brought DKK 184.4 million and grants for its own operations DKK 83.4 million.
- The ministry added an extraordinary DKK 7.0 million in 2025 to cover overspending. The report calls the year's result "not entirely satisfactory" (translation) and cites falling admission income, higher pay and underestimated IT costs.
- The report says the museum depends on grants from foundations for new exhibitions and research.
- All sites together had 1,350,845 visitors in 2025, against 1,383,800 in 2024 on the same count.
- On 3 November 2026 the museum said it must find DKK 11 to 13 million by cutting posts because visitor numbers kept falling, after cutting 21 posts in January 2026. Director Rane Willerslev said "I am sorry that we must again say goodbye to employees" (translation, museum press release).

BOTTOM LINE
The state pays about half and visitors most of the rest, and the accounts are published. The weak point is dependence on ticket income, which has now led to two rounds of job cuts in one year. Worth watching: which foundations fill the DKK 83 million grant line.
