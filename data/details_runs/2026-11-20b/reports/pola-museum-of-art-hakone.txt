POLA MUSEUM OF ART: Hakone, Japan
ID: pola-museum-of-art-hakone
Website: https://www.polamuseum.or.jp/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1285 Sengokuhara, Hakone, Kanagawa Prefecture, in a forest with beeches more than 300 years old (museum website; e-flux directory).
- It opened in September 2002 to show the collection of Suzuki Tsuneshi (鈴木常司, 1930 to 2000; e-flux spelling), son of Pola's founder and president of the cosmetics company for about 40 years, who died before it opened (museum website).
- Collection: about 10,000 items gathered over some 40 years: Western painting, Japanese Western-style and Nihonga painting, prints, East Asian ceramics, glass and cosmetic tools (e-flux directory).
- Building: it rises no more than 8 metres above ground so as not to top the trees; most of it sits in a circular pit 74 metres across on seismic isolation rubber, under a glass atrium with a 20-metre "light wall" (museum website; Artscape).
- A forest walking trail opened for the tenth anniversary; the Atrium Gallery for contemporary artists followed in 2017 (museum website).
- Director: Noguchi Hiroko (野口弘子, own romanisation), a former hotel general manager, since 1 July 2023, after Kijima Shunsuke (木島俊介), director from July 2014 (Bijutsu Techo, 30 June 2023).
- Current shows, to 7 April 2027: "A New Eye: Monet and 21st Century Art" for the centenary of Monet's death and the museum's 25th year, with the collection shows "Collection Cinema" and "Cézanne Legend" (museum website; titles translated).
- Visiting: 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:30, open every day except for changeovers (closed 1 December 2026 and 8 to 26 April 2027); adults 2,200 yen, high school and university students 1,700 yen, free for junior high age and under since 10 August 2019, 1,100 yen for disabled visitors and one companion (museum website).

FUNDING
Pola Museum of Art: private foundation: the Pola Art Foundation, built on the Pola founding family's fortune
- The Pola Art Foundation was set up in May 1996 with the personal assets of Suzuki Tsuneshi, and became a public interest foundation in July 2010 (museum website).
- Its chairman is 鈴木郷史 (Suzuki Satoshi, own romanisation) (museum website, legal notice).
- Besides running the museum, the foundation gives grants for young artists' training abroad, curators' research and international art exchange (museum website; Mecenat Association).
- The overseas training grant has run since 1996, choosing about 18 artists a year; more than 430 have taken part, among them Miwa Yanagi, Koki Tanaka and Hiraki Sawa (museum website).
- Since 2017 the HIRAKU Project shows artists from that grant in the Atrium Gallery (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: a cosmetics family's foundation carries the museum. The weak point is dependence on that one family fortune.
