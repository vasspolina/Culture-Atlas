REYKJAVÍK ART MUSEUM: Reykjavík, Iceland
ID: reykjavík-art-museum-reykjavík
Website: https://listasafnreykjavikur.is/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum (Listasafn Reykjavíkur) on three sites in central Reykjavík: Hafnarhús at Tryggvagata 17, Kjarvalsstaðir at Flókagata 24 and Ásmundarsafn at Sigtún 105; it also looks after public art in the city (museum website).
- Founded in 1973; Kjarvalsstaðir, opened that year beside the Klambratún garden and named for the painter Jóhannes S. Kjarval, was the first building in Iceland designed for visual art exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Hafnarhús, a harbour warehouse of the 1930s, was renovated by Studio Grandi architects in 1998 to 2000 (Visit Reykjavík) and reopened in April 2000 (Wikipedia).
- Ásmundarsafn opened in 1983 in the home and studio of the sculptor Ásmundur Sveinsson, which he built largely himself between 1942 and 1950 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 17,000 items, mostly by Icelandic artists of the 20th and 21st centuries, with collections of Erró, Kjarval and Sveinsson; about fifteen exhibitions open each year, with around 200 public events and more than 10,000 students a year (museum website).
- Current season: "Leaky Bodies, Shared Worlds", works by thirty Polish artists from the Bunkier Sztuki collection in Kraków; "Kjarval and the 20th Century: When Modernity Anchored" at Kjarvalsstaðir; and "Listvinnzlan: Resonance", work by disabled artists, at Ásmundarsafn after the 2026 Reykjavík Arts Festival (museum website).
- Director: Markús Þór Andrésson since 1 September 2025, appointed for five years; he succeeded Ólöf Kristín Sigurðardóttir after her ten years in the post (City of Reykjavík). The museum had 24 full-time posts and about 40 staff including hourly workers in 2024 (city committee presentation, 9 February 2024).
- The museum won the Icelandic Museum Award in May 2024 for its outreach work (City of Reykjavík).
- Visiting: Hafnarhús is open daily 10:00 to 17:00, Thursdays to 22:00; one ticket covers all three sites for 24 hours; the entrance is step-free, with a lift and accessible toilets (museum website).

FUNDING
Reykjavík Art Museum: municipal: financed and managed by the City of Reykjavík
- Wikipedia states that Reykjavík city is responsible for the museum's management and financing, and that the museum is in charge of the city's art collection.
- Its director and staff received the 2024 award together with the head of the city's culture and sports division, and its contact address is on the city's reykjavik.is domain (City of Reykjavík; museum website).
- Earned income: tickets, the museum shop in Hafnarhús, and group tours by appointment (museum website).
- Festival partners: Iceland Airwaves, Reykjavík Museum Night, Reykjavík Arts Festival, DesignMarch and Reykjavík Cultural Night (museum website).

Not related: the National Gallery of Iceland (Listasafn Íslands), whose website is listasafn.is. It is a separate national museum, which also runs the Einar Jónsson Museum, and is not part of the city museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city department paid for from the municipal budget, with ticket and shop income on top; no private or flagged funder is on record.
