WERELDMUSEUM AMSTERDAM (FORMER TROPENMUSEUM): Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: wereldmuseum-amsterdam-former-tropenmuseum
Website: https://amsterdam.wereldmuseum.nl (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A museum of world cultures on Linnaeusstraat in East Amsterdam, in a building opened in 1926 as part of the Colonial Institute on the site of the Oosterbegraafplaats cemetery, which was moved outside the city boundaries (Wereldmuseum).
- Around 1910 the Colonial Institute engaged the Van Nieukerken family of architects, a father and his sons, who drew on Dutch Renaissance castles of the 16th and 17th centuries (KIT, Witness in Stone).
- Its collection grew from two older ones: the Colonial Museum in Haarlem, which opened in 1871 from objects Frederik Willem van Eeden began gathering in 1864, and the ethnographic collection of the Artis zoo (Wereldmuseum).
- After the Second World War it became the Indies Institute, then the Tropenmuseum of the Royal Tropical Institute under the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; in 2014 it merged with Museum Volkenkunde in Leiden and the Afrika Museum in Berg en Dal into the National Museum of World Cultures, and the museums took the name Wereldmuseum in 2023 (Wereldmuseum).
- The semi-permanent exhibition Our Colonial Inheritance opened in 2022; 2025 exhibitions included Poetry of the People, Onvoltooid Verleden on restitution, Made in China and Ekow Nimako's Building Black Civilizations (annual accounts 2025).
- The National Museum of World Cultures counted 239,509 visitors in 2025 across Amsterdam, Leiden and the Wereldlab in Nijmegen (annual accounts 2025).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10 to 17, and on Mondays during school holidays (Wereldmuseum).

FUNDING
Wereldmuseum Amsterdam: mainly public: the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, with lottery money
- It is run by the foundation Stichting Nationaal Museum van Wereldculturen, which in 2025 had income of 25.9 million euros for its Amsterdam, Leiden and Nijmegen sites: 20.3 million euros in structural ministry subsidy (14.2 million for public activities, 1.6 million for housing, 4.6 million for collection care), 5.0 million euros of own income and 0.6 million euros in one-off public grants; it closed the year with a surplus of 647,870 euros (annual accounts 2025).
- Private money in 2025 came to 1,041,876 euros, of which 781,251 euros from charity lotteries, 219,000 euros from private funds and 34,000 euros from companies (annual accounts 2025); in its 2025 fundraising account the museum gives its "greatest thanks" to the VriendenLoterij, for 800,000 euros (annual accounts 2025, translated by the atlas).
- The Amsterdam building is rented from a third party; the museum lists uncertainty over the lease and structural maintenance, "as a result of the organisational and financial situation of the landlord", among its risks for 2026 (annual accounts 2025, translated by the atlas).
- In October 2011 DutchNews reported that the museum then received 20 million euros from the foreign ministry's development budget, and that the ministry would stop this subsidy from 2013 because of aid cuts (DutchNews, 13 October 2011).

Red flags: Founded in 1926 to promote Dutch colonial interests
- Sponsors including the Ministry of Colonies commissioned the Colonial Institute; its articles of association gave the museum two aims: collecting and spreading knowledge about the overseas territories, which the museum itself labels "colonial propaganda", and promoting all interests stemming from the colonial possessions (Wereldmuseum).
- After 1945 the renamed Indies Institute was involved in preparing soldiers to fight against Indonesian independence (Wereldmuseum).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two recent pieces credit the museum's decolonial work and fault how far it goes: one for leaving out the voices of descendant communities, one for keeping power at the top of the organisation.
1. De Groene Amsterdammer: Niels Mathijssen, "Vuile handen maken" (2025)
URL: https://www.groene.nl/artikel/vuile-handen-maken
- Reviews the restitution exhibition Onvoltooid verleden: its questions are challenging and its information interesting, but the story of descendants from the communities of origin is barely made tangible (translated by the atlas).
- Argues that the return of human dignity called for at the opening by Ciraj Rassool is unfortunately not seen in practice, and that the colonial gaze remains deeply anchored in the museum and its collection (translated by the atlas).
2. Museums Journal (Museums Association): Carol Ann Dixon, "A whole new world" (2025)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/features/2025/11/a-whole-new-world/
- Praises Our Colonial Inheritance and the commissions from artists with diasporic links to the former Dutch empire.
- From her talks with staff, argues that more could be done to share interpretative power and that hierarchical staffing and governance structures need disrupting.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money today, plus lottery income, with no flagged funder. The weak point is historical: an institution built to serve Dutch colonialism, still working through that origin in public.
