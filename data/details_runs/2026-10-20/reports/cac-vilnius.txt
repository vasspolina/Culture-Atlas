CONTEMPORARY ART CENTRE (CAC / ŠMC): Vilnius, Lithuania
ID: cac-vilnius
Website: https://cac.lt

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art institution in Vilnius at Vokiečių st. 2, with a Sapieha Palace branch at L. Sapiegos st. 13 focused on contemporaneity and heritage; the centre has operated across the two venues since July 2024 (CAC).
- Founded in 1992 by the Lithuanian Ministry of Culture as successor to the Arts Exhibition Palace, whose Vilnius building it took over (Wikipedia, CAC). Open House Vilnius calls it "the largest institution of this kind in the Baltic States."
- The Art Exhibition Palace building was designed by V. E. Čekanauskas, with R. Jakas as constructor (1967) and V. Ozarinskas as reconstruction architect (2006) (Open House Vilnius).
- Valentinas Klimašauskas took over the centre from its founding director, Kęstutis Kuizinas, in early 2025 (e-flux, 31 July 2025).
- The main building reopened in mid-2024 after a renovation that began in 2021 (CAC).
- Organises the Baltic Triennial every three years; Klimašauskas co-curated the 14th edition, "The Endless Frontier", in 2021 (CAC).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 19:00, closed Mondays. Tickets 12 euros, concessions from 6 euros (all visitors on Wednesdays), family ticket from 18 euros; free entry for visitors with disabilities and a companion, children before school age, visitors aged 80 and over, and several other groups. A quieter visit with reduced light and sound runs on Tuesdays 15:00 to 17:00.

FUNDING
Contemporary Art Centre (CAC / ŠMC): public: founded by the Lithuanian Ministry of Culture, with European Regional Development Fund money for two building projects
- Set up and founded by the Lithuanian Ministry of Culture in 1992 (CAC).
- Modernisation of the main building: 4,175,534 euros allocated, financed by the European Regional Development Fund under two measures of the 2014 to 2020 EU funds operational programme, "Modernisation of Cultural Infrastructure" and "Renovation of State-Owned Buildings (II)"; planned completion 31 December 2023 (CAC).
- Revitalisation of Sapieha Palace: 11,872,255.69 euros allocated, financed by the European Regional Development Fund under the measure "Revitalisation of Cultural Heritage Objects"; the page calls it a jointly financed project; planned completion 31 December 2023 (CAC).

BOTTOM LINE
The centre was founded by the Lithuanian Ministry of Culture in 1992. Its two building projects are financed by the European Regional Development Fund: 4,175,534 euros for the main building and 11,872,255.69 euros for Sapieha Palace.
