FOTOGALLERIET: Oslo, Norway
ID: fotogalleriet-oslo
Website: https://fotogalleriet.no

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit kunsthalle for photography and camera-based art at Møllergata 34, 0179 Oslo (Store norske leksikon; Oslo Art Guide).
- Opened on 5 September 1977 in a converted potato cellar at Oscars gate 50A, started by a group of photographers including Tom Sandberg, Dag Alveng, Bjørn Høyum, Per Berntsen, Jamie Parslow and Jim Bengston; the first show featured the British photographers Paul Hill and Raymond Moor (Store norske leksikon).
- Store norske leksikon calls it the first exhibition venue in the Nordic countries to show only photographic and camera-based art; the foundation's own site calls it the longest-operating institution of its kind in the Nordic countries and the only kunsthalle in Norway focusing on art photography.
- It is an "ideell stiftelse" (non-profit foundation); its longest-running partner is the artists' association Forbundet Frie Fotografer, with which it shares governance, and the annual Spring Exhibition (Vårutstillingen), established by that association in 1976, is held there (Store norske leksikon; the foundation's website).
- Programme: exhibitions, public events, research and education, and early solo shows for artists including Ida Ekeblad and Matias Faldbakken; it is a member of the FUTURES photography network since 2022 and co-founded Creative Neighbourhood in 2013 (the foundation's website).
- Its library holds about 1,100 books and 1,400 journals (Store norske leksikon).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday 12:00 to 17:00, closed Monday and Tuesday; step-free entrance, an accessible restroom, guide dogs welcome (the foundation's website; Oslo Art Guide).

FUNDING
Fotogalleriet: mainly public: Norwegian state culture funding, Oslo municipality and the Norwegian Photographic Fund
- Operational funding comes from the Ministry of Culture and Equality; the Norwegian Photographic Fund (Nofofo) and the City of Oslo also provide support (the foundation's website).
- Store norske leksikon lists Norsk kulturråd, Nofofo and Oslo kommune as the main funders and says Forbundet Frie Fotografer pushed for the gallery to be placed on the state budget, which happened in 1986.
- Past project grantors listed are the European Union, the EEA, the Goethe Institute, the Institut français, the Fritt Ord Foundation and Arts Council Norway (the foundation's website).
- Board: Terje Abusdal (chair), Hedevig Anker, Hanan Benammar and Jan Christensen, with annual reports for 2020 to 2025 (the foundation's website).

BOTTOM LINE
Fotogalleriet is funded by Norwegian public money, the Ministry of Culture and Equality and the City of Oslo, with the Norwegian Photographic Fund and project grants from named cultural and EU funds.
