STROOM DEN HAAG: The Hague, Netherlands
ID: stroom-den-haag
Website: https://www.stroom.nl (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for contemporary art in the public domain at Hogewal 1 in central The Hague. It describes itself as an expertise centre for art, society and the public domain (stated by the space).
- Dutch Wikipedia dates its founding to 1989; Stroom's own site says it has operated since 1990. It handles non-museum visual arts policy in The Hague (Wikipedia).
- The name is not explained in the pages read.
- Building: a former 19th-century shop building, renovated in 2004 to plans by the architect Wim Cuyvers. Stroom has been based there since 2005 (Wikipedia).
- What it does: advises on and facilitates art in public space, supports The Hague's artists and initiatives, and presents programmes. It runs a library, bookshop and archive and the city's Ouborg Prize for visual art, awarded to a Hague artist in cooperation with the Kunstmuseum. Public works it has overseen include James Turrell's "Hemels Gewelf" (1996), Vito Acconci's "Park in het Water" (1997) and the Peter Struycken sculpture gallery in the centre (Wikipedia).
- Run by a board of four and a directorate of an artistic director and a business director; Alexandra Landré is listed as artistic director (stroom.nl).
- Visiting: the pages read give no opening hours or admission. No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
Stroom Den Haag: mainly public: the Municipality of The Hague
- The Municipality of The Hague provides financial support for Stroom's public mission (stated by the space).
- Stroom is a foundation (Stichting Stroom Den Haag) with ANBI public-benefit tax status (stroom.nl). It publishes annual reports for 2005 to 2025 and a 2025 to 2028 multi-year plan, but the PDFs did not open as text, so no budget figures were verified.
- The Hague funds arts organisations through a multi-year plan for 2025 to 2028 (denhaag.nl, 13 September 2024), which does not name Stroom or give amounts for institutions. Stroom's own multi-year plan covers the same years.
- No reporting found of Mondriaan Fund support for Stroom itself, or of any corporate sponsor.
- Board (stroom.nl): Els van der Plas (chair, director of the Allard Pierson in Amsterdam), Joost Vrieler (secretary, senior policy advisor at the Province of Groningen), Peter Verbaas (treasurer, partner at Charistar BV) and Bircan Bozbey (director-manager of Trias, Centrum voor de Kunsten).
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Stroom is public money from the Municipality of The Hague, and nothing found points to a flagged funder. The weak point is that its budget figures sit in annual-report PDFs that did not open as text, so the amounts, and any Mondriaan Fund grant, remain unconfirmed. Worth watching is its financial mix under the 2025 to 2028 plan.
