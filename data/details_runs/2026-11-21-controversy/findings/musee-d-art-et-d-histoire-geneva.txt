WIDER CONTEXT
- In August 2021 a letter to Geneva's mayor, Frédérique Perler, signed by about a hundred people from the cultural sector, questioned the choice of Marc-Olivier Wahler as director and called for his removal. Named signatories included the historian Bernard Lescaze, the former Conseiller d'État Laurent Moutinot and Erica Deuber Ziegler (Radio Lac, 17 August 2021).
- The letter accused Wahler of not understanding the essence of the museum's collections, spending too much on communication and showing contempt for staff. SIT union secretary Valérie Buchs confirmed the criticism, and the unions said management was too top-down and staff were never consulted, a way of working they said was found in all City institutions (Radio Lac, 17 August 2021).
- The petitioners denounced "historical nonsense liable to mislead the public who visit" and accused Wahler of planning to replace the graphics cabinet with a café (The Art Newspaper, 27 September 2021). Le Temps counted 117 signatures (Le Temps, 19 November 2021).
- Wahler replied that he wanted to move the graphics cabinet to give it more space, that accusations of overspending on exhibitions were false and several official audits of the accounts had found no grounds for complaint, and that the protesters "want the museum to remain the domain of an elite" (The Art Newspaper, 27 September 2021).
- Sami Kanaan, Geneva's top culture official, said in September 2021 that he stood behind Wahler's vision and had no hesitation about appointing him permanently from November (The Art Newspaper). Wahler's two years in post ended on 1 November 2021 and he was confirmed in the job (Le Temps, 19 November 2021).
