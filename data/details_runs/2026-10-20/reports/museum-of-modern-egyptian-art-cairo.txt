MUSEUM OF MODERN EGYPTIAN ART: Cairo, Egypt
ID: museum-of-modern-egyptian-art-cairo
Website: https://www.fineart.gov.eg/

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in Zamalek, Cairo, part of the National Cultural Centre on the former fair grounds of Zamalek (Wikipedia); Asharq Al-Awsat places it on Opera House Square.
- Wikipedia gives its inauguration as 1927. Asharq Al-Awsat (Mohamed El-Kafrawy, 22 January 2025) reports that the museum has been in its current building since 1983, after its collection moved among several venues, mostly heritage palaces, and that it opened to the public in 1991 as one of the art venues of the Ministry of Culture, represented by the Fine Arts Sector.
- Collection: more than 13,000 paintings and sculptures tracing Egyptian art from early 20th century pioneers to contemporary artists, including Mahmoud Said, Ragheb Ayad, Gazbia Sirry and Abdel Hadi Al-Gazzar (Wikipedia). Asharq Al-Awsat puts about 1,000 works on display and 16,000 to 18,000 in storage.
- Closures: Asharq Al-Awsat reports that the museum was reopened in 2005, closed at times afterwards, reopened in 2020 and was then partly closed after the COVID-19 crisis and for development work. The works it lists include a permanent fix for ceiling leaks, central air conditioning, urgent maintenance including lighting and paint, updated security systems and an updated display layout, with a reopening show, "Khabiat Bikar", honouring the artist Hussein Bikar.
- Visiting: from February 2025 the museum was set to open 9 am to 9 pm every day except Monday, and from 1 pm on Fridays (Asharq Al-Awsat).

FUNDING
Museum of Modern Egyptian Art: mainly public: the Egyptian Ministry of Culture
- The Ministry of Culture owns the museum (Wikipedia) and runs it through the Fine Arts Sector (Asharq Al-Awsat).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Egyptian state: the Ministry of Culture owns it and the Fine Arts Sector runs it.
