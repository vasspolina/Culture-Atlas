MUSEO DE ARTE Y DISEÑO CONTEMPORÁNEO (MADC): San José, Costa Rica
ID: museo-de-arte-y-diseño-contemporáneo-san-josé
Website: https://madc.cr/

OVERVIEW
- State contemporary art and design museum on Avenida 3, calle 15, San José, inside the Centro Nacional de la Cultura (Cenac), which belongs to the Ministerio de Cultura y Juventud (La Nación; Wikipedia).
- Opened on 21 February 1994 in the former Fábrica Nacional de Licores (Fanal), and the collection began with 196 works under the direction of Virginia Pérez-Ratton (La Nación, Fiorella Resenterra and Daniel Soto Morúa, 27 July 2019).
- Law 7758 of 1998 created the museum as a body of "maximum deconcentration" of the Ministry of Culture, with its seat at the Cenac (text of the law on the museum's site).
- The Fanal factory was a state liquor monopoly, opened on 24 August 1856; the Ministry of Culture moved in in 1993, and the building is protected as of historic-architectural interest (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Spanish Wikipedia describes four temporary exhibition halls, the Pila de la Melaza outdoor molasses tank and El Tanque, a former fuel tank used as an exhibition space; La Nación dates Sala 1.1 to 2011 and El Tanque to 2016. The collection held 1,166 items in 2019 (La Nación).
- It closed in November 2018 for renovation and reopened on 14 February 2019 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Visiting: exhibition rooms Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 16:50; the video library and documentation centre by appointment, Monday to Friday (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte y Diseño Contemporáneo: mainly public: Costa Rica's Ministry of Culture through the national budget
- Article 7 of Law 7758 sets the museum's income: allocations, transfers and subsidies in the annual national budget law; donations, transfers and subsidies in cash or services; cash donations, works or services from public and private bodies, received directly or through the Fundación; public or private funds the Fundación receives and allocates to the museum's upkeep and conservation; and fees for its services.
- A Fundación seeks private-sector support for the museum; the museum and the Fundación are authorised to hold fundraising activities, and both submit their budgets to the Contraloría General de la República for approval (Law 7758, Articles 3, 8, 9).
- The museum's Junta Administrativa has five members: two from the Ministry, two named by the Fundación and one from the Junta Nacional de Curadores (Law 7758, Article 4).
- Directors are chosen jointly by the Ministry and the board of the Fundación ProMADC; Verónica Zúñiga Salas started for five years on 18 June 2019 (Semanario Universidad, Ana Beatriz Fernández González, 18 June 2019). Spanish Wikipedia lists Paz Monge Cortés as director since 2020 and Daniel Soto Morúa as chief curator since 2016.
- The museum posts budgets for 2013 to 2020 on its site.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Semanario Universidad (Natalia Díaz Zeledón, "Artistas y trabajadores de cultura marchan por San José contra recortes presupuestarios", 24 October 2020) reported that artists and arts workers marched in San José against budget cuts negotiated in the Legislative Assembly: about 4,100 million colones approved by the Comisión de Hacendarias, affecting several institutions under the Ministry of Culture and Youth. Photographer and visual artist Sara Mata said her collective began organising over news of a merger of the Museo de Arte Costarricense and the MADC, and that the merger was part of the government's proposal to the International Monetary Fund.
- The MADC is one of the two museums named in the proposed merger, it is an agency of that ministry, and the article's photo caption places a protest near the MADC.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Costa Rican state: Law 7758 makes it a body of the Ministry of Culture funded by the national budget, with a private foundation raising extra donations.
