BARBICAN CENTRE: London, United Kingdom
ID: barbican-centre-london
Website: https://www.barbican.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre inside the Barbican Estate in the City of London, with a concert hall, theatre, art gallery, cinemas, library, conservatory, restaurants and conference halls (Wikipedia).
- Built as the City of London's gift to the nation at a cost of £161 million; Queen Elizabeth II opened it on 3 March 1982 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia says the City of London Corporation owns, funds and manages it.
- Building: designed by Chamberlin, Powell and Bon in the Brutalist style, listed Grade II in September 2001 (Wikipedia).
- Venues: Barbican Hall (1,943 seats, home to the London Symphony Orchestra), Barbican Theatre (1,156 seats), The Pit (200 seats), Barbican Art Gallery, The Curve and three cinema screens (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Barbican site has a "Free things to do" section and an Access Scheme.

FUNDING
Barbican Centre: mainly public: the City of London Corporation
- The Barbican site calls the City of London Corporation "the founder and principal funder of the Barbican Centre".
- In December 2024 the Corporation's Court of Common Council approved a £191 million package for repairs and upgrades, about 80% of the amount needed for the first phase of a five-year renewal programme, with a fundraising campaign launching in 2025 for the rest (International Arts Manager).

Red flags: protest and withdrawals over a cancelled Gaza lecture, 2024
- February 2024: the Barbican decided not to host the London Review of Books lecture "The Shoah after Gaza" by Pankaj Mishra; the Guardian reported the cancellation on 6 February (Censorship at the Barbican campaign page). The Barbican said the lecture was publicised "prematurely" (Hyperallergic). The lecture took place at St James's Church, Clerkenwell, on 28 February (campaign page).
- The collectors Lorenzo Legarda Leviste and Fahad Mayet withdrew two Loretta Pettway quilts from the "Unravel: The Power and Politics of Textiles in Art" exhibition, and the Barbican removed them on 29 February. Yto Barrada (7 March), Cian Dayrit and Diedrick Brackens (8 March), Mounira al Solh (9 March) and Art Jameel, which rescinded its loan of a Pacita Abad painting (11 March), followed; nine works in total (campaign page, run by Leviste and Mayet, an advocacy source).
- The Barbican wrote on 22 February that "there was no intention to censor the speaker involved" (campaign page). It pointed to its 2023 Equity, Diversity & Inclusion Strategy and its London Palestine Film Festival, and put labels in the show stating that works were withdrawn "as an act of solidarity with Palestine" (Hyperallergic, campaign page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Three pieces cover the 2024 lecture cancellation and lender withdrawals, a 2021 book of staff accounts of racism, and a 2026 open letter over a director's departure.
1. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Lenders Withdraw Works From Barbican Show, Alleging 'Censorship'" (5 March 2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/875873/lenders-withdraw-works-from-barbican-show-alleging-censorship/
- Reports that Leviste and Mayet withdrew two Pettway quilts on 29 February after the Barbican decided not to host the Mishra lecture.
- Quotes Leviste calling the decision a "blatant act of repression" and the precedent "chilling".
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "London's Barbican shakes up staff following racism allegations published in tell-all book" (10 August 2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/08/10/londons-barbican-shakes-up-staff-following-racism-allegations-published-in-tell-all-book
- Reports that "Barbican Stories", published in June 2021 by the Barbican Stories Collective, collects more than 100 accounts from current and former staff of discrimination dating back to 2014, describing the work culture as "subtle and insidiously" racist.
- Reports that managing director Nicholas Kenyon was stepping down and that the City of London Corporation commissioned an external review of staff experiences of racism by the law firm Lewis Silkin LLP. Kenyon welcomed the review.
3. ArtReview: "Cultural figures sign open letter over departure of Barbican arts director" (20 February 2026, no author named)
URL: https://artreview.com/cultural-figures-sign-open-letter-over-departure-of-barbican-arts-director/
- Over 170 signatories, among them John Akomfrah, Isaac Julien, Salman Rushdie and Pankaj Mishra, asked the Barbican Board and the City of London Corporation to explain the departure of Devyani Saltzman, director for arts and participation, whose time in post the article calls "comparatively short".
- The letter argues the departure raises questions about the institution's commitment to "global majority leadership at the highest levels" and asks the Barbican to publish current diversity data on senior leadership and commission an independent review of how senior appointments and exits are decided.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Barbican is public: the City of London Corporation funds and runs it, with a £191 million renewal package approved in December 2024. In 2024 lenders and artists withdrew nine works in protest at the Barbican's decision not to host a Pankaj Mishra lecture, and press has covered a 2021 book of staff accounts of racism and a 2026 open letter over a director's departure.
