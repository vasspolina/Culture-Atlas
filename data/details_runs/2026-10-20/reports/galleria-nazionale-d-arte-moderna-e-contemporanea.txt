GALLERIA NAZIONALE D'ARTE MODERNA E CONTEMPORANEA: Rome, Italy
ID: galleria-nazionale-d-arte-moderna-e-contemporanea
Website: https://lagallerianazionale.com (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art at Viale delle Belle Arti 131, Rome, about 600 metres from Flaminio metro station (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1883 on the initiative of minister Guido Baccelli (Wikipedia).
- Cesare Bazzani designed the building, built from 1911 to 1915, and doubled it in 1934; a wing by Luigi Cosenza opened in 1988 and closed ten years later over safety concerns (Wikipedia).
- Shows about 1,100 19th and 20th century paintings and sculptures, and runs a library, archives, a restoration lab and the Caffè delle Arti (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Renata Cristina Mazzantini is director (Finestre sull'Arte).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 9am to 7pm; €15 full, €2 for EU citizens aged 18 to 25, free for under-18s, disabled visitors and a companion, and on the first Sunday of the month; fully accessible by lifts and platforms, with step-free entry at Via Gramsci 71 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Galleria Nazionale: public: Italy's Ministry of Culture, with corporate exhibition sponsors
- A Ministry of Culture museum; its email and staff sit on the ministry's cultura.gov.it domain (stated by the space).
- The ministry paid €1.5 million for "Il tempo del Futurismo" (3 December 2024 to 28 February 2025); main sponsors were Autostrade per l'Italia and Enel, with Ferrovie dello Stato Italiane and Unipol as sponsors and ACI Storico as technical partner (Finestre sull'Arte).
- It publishes annual reports from 2015 to 2023 (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of a standing sponsor list.

Red flags: Enel, gas and coal power
- Enel's thermal plants generated 33.11 TWh in 2025, from combined-cycle gas, coal and oil and gas, and it sold 6.3 billion cubic metres of natural gas; zero-emission sources were 82.2% of consolidated output (Enel 2025 results).

Red flags: Autostrade per l'Italia and the Morandi bridge
- The Ponte Morandi in Genoa collapsed on 14 August 2018, killing 43 people (Il Post).
- On 16 July 2026 the Genoa court sentenced former Autostrade CEO Giovanni Castellucci to 12 years; 32 of 57 defendants were convicted. Autostrade and Spea Engineering settled for about €30 million in total and were not tried (Il Post).
- Autostrade is now 88.06% owned by Holding Reti Autostradali, whose shareholders are Cassa Depositi e Prestiti, Blackstone and Macquarie (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sharp criticism in the Italian art press of the Futurism show as a minister's project, built without a scientific committee and at the cost of the permanent collection.
1. Finestre sull'Arte: Leonardo Bison, "The other side of the futurism exhibition: GNAM emptied, library and archives closed" (2025)
URL: https://www.finestresullarte.info/en/opinions/the-other-side-of-the-futurism-exhibition-gnam-emptied-library-and-archives-closed
- Argues the show is political: it began as a minister's idea and had no scientific committee, and the organising committee lacked Futurism specialists.
- Reports that the library and archive closed from October without a press release, that masterpieces went to Turin and the galleries were half-empty, and that Boccioni's "Antigrazioso" was damaged during installation.
- Traces the politicisation back to a 2023 Tolkien show the government wanted.
2. Artribune: Alberto Dambruoso, "La recensione della mostra del Futurismo di Alberto Dambruoso, il curatore defenestrato" (2024)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/arti-visive/2024/12/dambruoso-polemiche-futurismo-mostra/
- The excluded co-curator calls the show poorly educational, not international and badly hung, with no wall texts, translated audio guide or catalogue at opening.
- Calls his removal and that of the scientific committee an "epurazione" (purge, our translation); names minister Gennaro Sangiuliano, curator Gabriele Simongini and Mazzantini but does not say who decided.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for by the Ministry of Culture; corporate money comes in for exhibitions. The weak points are the Futurism show's sponsors, Enel with its gas and coal plants and Autostrade with the Morandi collapse, and the ministry's direct hand in programming. Worth watching: whether the same sponsors return for the rehang after 2025.
