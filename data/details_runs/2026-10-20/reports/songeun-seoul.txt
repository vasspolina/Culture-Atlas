SONGEUN: Seoul, South Korea
ID: songeun-seoul
Website: https://songeun.or.kr/ (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art space at 441 Dosan-daero, Cheongdam-dong, Gangnam-gu, Seoul, run by the SongEun Art and Cultural Foundation (SongEun website; Metalocus).
- The foundation was established in 1989 and created the SongEun Art Award in 2001; the current space opened in September 2021 (SongEun website). An earlier SongEun Art Space in Cheongdam-dong already hosted the award exhibition in 2011 (Energy Economy Newspaper).
- The name means "hidden pine" (SongEun website).
- Building: designed by Herzog & de Meuron, its first project in Korea. Eleven floors above ground and five below, 8,167 m2 gross, with 2,981 m2 of exhibition space; the concrete facade is textured with larch plywood boards (SongEun website; Metalocus, 17 October 2021).
- The building is shared with the headquarters offices of ST International; its lobby and garden are open to the public at all times (Metalocus).
- Programme: the SongEun Art Award, for artists aged 45 and under, with 20 million won and a solo show for the winner; the 24th edition drew 598 applicants and 20 finalists (The Korea Times, 19 December 2024).
- Visiting: free exhibitions, open 11:00 to 19:00 with hours varying by day (Visit Gangnam); no reporting found of closed days or accessibility in the pages opened.

FUNDING
SongEun: corporate-family foundation: the Yoo family's energy group (Samtan, ST International)
- Samtan was founded by Yoo Sung-yeon; the foundation was set up in 1989 to return his business wealth to society, and Yoo Sang-deok, then Samtan chairman, chaired the foundation (Energy Economy Newspaper, 2011). The JoongAng Daily (2011) calls the foundation part of the energy business group Samtan.
- BizHankook (2025) says Yoo Sang-duk, eldest son of Yoo Sung-yeon, leads ST International, which owns the Samtan Building in Daechi-dong; most of its major affiliates are headquartered in the ST SongEun Building.
- Metalocus lists the foundation and ST International as clients of the building.
- No reporting found of the foundation's budget, annual spending or donor list in the pages opened.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Coal: Samtan's core business is bituminous coal. In 2011 the Energy Economy Newspaper said its Paser mine in Indonesia handled about 65 percent of South Korea's bituminous coal business.
- In August 2017 Samtan's Indonesian subsidiary KIDECO signed a three-year contract to supply 2.5 million tons a year to a Chinese buyer, expected to bring in more than 370 billion won (Energy Economy Newspaper).
- The foundation was founded by Yoo Sung-yeon, the Samtan founder, and chaired in 2011 by his son. No reporting found of Samtan's coal holdings after 2017 in the pages opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation is funded by the founding family's energy group, and no foundation accounts or donor list were found. The weak point is that the group's core business is coal mining and trading in Indonesia, and ST International shares the building. Watch for the foundation's donation disclosures and for the group's current coal holdings.
