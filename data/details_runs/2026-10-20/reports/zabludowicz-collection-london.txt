ZABLUDOWICZ COLLECTION: London, UK
ID: zabludowicz-collection-london
Website: https://www.zabludowiczcollection.com/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art collection, founded in 1994 (stated by the collection); Poju Zabludowicz has been its founder and trustee since 1994 (Wikipedia). Its London project space at 176 Prince of Wales Road, Chalk Farm, closed in December 2023 (stated by the collection).
- The London space opened in September 2007 in a former Methodist chapel (stated by the collection; The Art Newspaper calls it a former Methodist church).
- It hosted more than 100 free exhibitions and over 640 free events and drew over 250,000 visitors (The Art Newspaper, 2023).
- The collection now works through artwork loans to institutions, a digital presence, commissions, residencies in Sarvisalo, Finland, and the Master Class and Curatorial Testing Ground programmes for emerging artists and curators.
- The London building has new owners and reopened in 2025 as Camden Arts Projects; the Zabludowicz Collection says this on its visit page, and Time Out reports that Camden Arts Projects is a not-for-profit space. Time Out does not say who runs it.
- Visiting: the London collection is viewable by email request. The New York viewing space (24th Floor, 1500 Broadway) is free, by appointment only. Groups of up to 60 can visit Sarvisalo by appointment. The collection says all its activities are free; no accessibility information is published.

FUNDING
Zabludowicz Collection: privately funded by the founders' family fortune through the Zabludowicz Art Trust
- The collection says all its activities are privately funded and that it is principally owned by the Zabludowicz Art Trust. Zabludowicz Art Projects UK is a registered charity, number 1120067 (stated by the collection).
- The charity's own report for 2019 says it has "no regular source of income other than the donations it receives from the Zabludowicz Art Trust" (quoted by The White Pube, citing its Companies House filing). Its income was £1,181,521 in 2019 and £1,114,769 in 2018 (The White Pube).
- Poju Zabludowicz is director and trustee of the Zabludowicz Art Trust (The White Pube, citing the collection's 2019 financial report).
- Where the family money comes from: Poju Zabludowicz heads the holding company Tamares, which invests in companies and real estate (Wikipedia). Tamares sold its British Israel Property operation for $700m in 2011, and the couple's 2022 net worth was £1.495bn (The Art Newspaper, citing The Sunday Times).
- Poju Zabludowicz's father, Shlomo Zabludowicz, built the family business around the Israeli defence contractor Soltam (Wikipedia, Hyperallergic).
- No reporting found of any public funder, corporate sponsor or outside donor of the collection.

Red flags: boycott and protest over the founders' ties to Israel
- Since 2014 the group Boycott Divest Zabludowicz (BDZ) has urged artists and institutions to boycott the collection (Wikipedia).
- In May 2021 BDZ's call was signed by more than 600 artists and organisations (Hyperallergic, 18 May 2021). The collection's spokesperson said it is not a political organisation and called the petition misinformed.
- In July 2021, 25 artists and cultural workers sent signed letters to Zabludowicz Art Projects and the Zabludowicz Art Trust withdrawing ("deauthoring") their past work (Hyperallergic, 26 July 2021). The article names 24 of them.
- The collection answered through a statement by the founders dated 21 May 2021 that welcomed the ceasefire and backed a two-state solution (Hyperallergic).
- In 2023 the collection said the London closure was unrelated to the boycott or the war in Gaza (Time Out, 2025).

Red flags: arms and defence business in the founders' family money
- Poju Zabludowicz chairs and is CEO of the Tamares Group, which holds a stake in Knafaim. Knafaim is the ultimate beneficiary of Maintenance Wings Limited Partnership, which provides military aircraft maintenance to the Israeli Air Force (Hyperallergic, July 2021).
- BICOM, a pro-Israel lobby group founded by Poju Zabludowicz, was financed through the fortune of his father Shlomo Zabludowicz, an Israeli arms dealer (Hyperallergic, July 2021).
- A 2009 Channel 4 Dispatches documentary reported business interests of Poju Zabludowicz in a shopping centre in Ma'ale Adumim, a West Bank settlement (Hyperallergic, May 2021).
- Poju Zabludowicz has donated a total of £380,000 to the UK Conservative Party (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism exists, almost all of it tied to the 2014 to 2021 boycott campaign. It targets the founders' business and political ties, not the exhibitions.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "600+ Artists and Organizations Urge Boycott of Zabludowicz Art Trust Over Israel Ties" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/646541/artists-boycott-the-zabludowicz-art-trust-over-israel-ties/
- Reports BDZ's call to withdraw work from the Trust and subsidiaries including Daata Art Fair and Times Square Space.
- Lists the reasons: Poju Zabludowicz's ties to BICOM, Tamares's stake in Knafaim, the West Bank shopping centre and his father's firm Soltam.
- Names Eyal Weizman, Claire Bishop and Lawrence Abu Hamdan among signatories, and Rachel Pimm and Jasmine Johnson as artists who withdrew work. Includes the collection's reply.
2. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Citing Pro-Israel Ties, 25 Artists Disaffiliate From London Collection" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/665123/citing-pro-israel-ties-artists-disaffiliate-from-zabludowicz-collection/
- Reports 25 artists and cultural workers withdrawing their work, modelled on the BDS and PACBI calls, and wanting the Trust to divest from and sever ties with the Israeli state.
- Names Michelle Williams Gamaker, Harold Offeh, Amalia Pica and Jack Strange among others.
- Gives the collection's reply through the founders' 21 May statement.
3. The White Pube: "ZM" (Zarina Muhammad per the site's credits), "BDZ" (2021)
URL: https://thewhitepube.co.uk/art-thoughts/bdz/
- Argues the collection "artwashes" money from Soltam Systems, a military aircraft maintenance contract, a West Bank shopping centre and Poju Zabludowicz's political donations.
- Argues the collection targets young, precarious artists with exposure and purchases, and urges artists to sign the BDZ pledge and deauthor past work.
- Names Poju, Anita and Tiffany Zabludowicz and the collection's director Elizabeth Neilson. This is an openly partisan critics' essay, run by two independent critics.
4. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Zabludowicz collecting couple to close London gallery space after 17 years" (2023)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/11/30/zabludowicz-collecting-couple-to-close-london-gallery-space-after-17-years
- Not criticism: a news report on the closure that records the 2021 disaffiliation of 25 artists.
- Gives no financial reason for the closure.

BOTTOM LINE
The collection runs on the founding family's private fortune, with no outside funder found. The weak points are that fortune's links to arms and defence companies and a pro-Israel lobby, as reported by Hyperallergic, and the boycott campaign running since 2014, with 25 artists withdrawing work in 2021. The collection's published accounts and board were not readable in the sources opened; watch the Zabludowicz Art Trust's donations to the charity.
