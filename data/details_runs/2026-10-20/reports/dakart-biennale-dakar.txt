DAK'ART BIENNALE: Dakar, Senegal
ID: dakart-biennale-dakar
Website: https://biennaledakar.org

OVERVIEW
- Biennale of Contemporary African Art, held in Dakar every two years; the 16th edition runs from 19 November to 19 December 2026 (stated by the biennale).
- Conceived in 1989 as a biennale alternating between literature and art; the first edition in 1990 focused on literature and 1992 on visual art. From 1996 it was devoted to contemporary African art (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia lists the Senegalese government as founder and the general secretary of the Dakar Biennale as organiser. From 2000, participating artists had to hold African citizenship; in 2014 the event opened to non-African nationals for the first time (Wikipedia).
- Organiser of the 2026 edition: the Government of Senegal, through the Ministry of Culture, Crafts and Tourism (biennale press release, 26 January 2026). The general secretariat is at 19 Avenue Hassan II, Dakar.
- 2026 edition: theme "(Anti)fragilité : Arts de la réparation et stratégies du contrecoup"; artistic director Morad Montazami; the Democratic Republic of the Congo is guest country of honour; 54 artists in the International Exhibition, 75 artists and designers in all including the Design section. It was launched on 24 September 2026 at the Musée des Civilisations noires (Au Sénégal).
- The 15th edition, scheduled for 16 May to 16 June 2024 (Dakaractu), was postponed to 7 November 2024, fewer than three weeks before the opening, by the biennale's general secretariat after election-related turbulence (Contemporary And, 31 July 2024).
- Minister of Culture Amadou Ba said in December 2025 during the 2026 budget debate in the National Assembly that "le modèle doit être changé" and that State support remains necessary in the current format. He noted the biennale has no site of its own: the former Palais de la Justice that housed the official exhibitions does not belong to it (Senego, 9 December 2025; quotations and summary are translations).

FUNDING
Dak'Art Biennale: public: the Senegalese State through the Ministry of Culture
- The Senegalese government organises the event and the Minister of Culture says State support is necessary in the current format (biennale press release; Senego).
- Wikipedia says the Senegalese government supported the event and confirmed that support after Abdoulaye Wade became president in 2000. The European Commission was a major financial partner but did not support the 2010 edition. The 2008 edition ran on a smaller budget and was organised at the last minute.
- Unverified: the Collectif des artistes indépendants du Sénégal, coordinated by Amadou Diop, said in April 2024 that the 2022 edition had a budget of one billion ("un milliard", currency not given in the article) and was never genuinely evaluated with stakeholders, and demanded a postponement and an organisational, technical and financial audit of the 15th Dak'Art (Dakaractu, 21 April 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns the 2024 postponement, its announcement and the State's cultural policy, not the art shown.
1. Contemporary And (C&): Black Pages, "In the Wake of Dak'Art Biennale 2024: A Momentum for Reflection" (2024)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/magazines/in-the-wake-of-dakart-biennale-2024-a-momentum-for-reflection/
- Aderemi Adegbite notes the cancellation surprised artists, gallerists and the biennale team, since it was announced through the biennale's social media pages. He argues that redirecting funds from international projects to Dak'Art could have prevented the postponement.
- Mohamed Amine Cissé says Senegal's cultural policy has been "stagnant at the same level for about twenty years" and calls a postponement "disastrous for the country's image".
- Jennifer Houdrouge says a sector built around a biennial must learn to become autonomous beyond that event; Jareh Das says transparency is essential in times of uncertainty and crisis.
- Names the postponement, announced through the biennale's social media pages, and the State's cultural policy.

BOTTOM LINE
Dak'Art is organised by the Government of Senegal through the Ministry of Culture, Crafts and Tourism, and the Minister of Culture said in December 2025 that State support remains necessary. Wikipedia names the European Commission as a major financial partner that did not support the 2010 edition. Criticism in 2024 concerned the postponement and its announcement, and an artists' collective called for an audit.
