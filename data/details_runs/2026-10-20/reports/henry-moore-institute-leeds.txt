HENRY MOORE INSTITUTE: Leeds, UK
ID: henry-moore-institute-leeds
Website: https://henry-moore.org/what-we-do/about-the-henry-moore-institute/

OVERVIEW
- Sculpture gallery, research library and archive of sculptors' papers at 74 The Headrow in Leeds, beside Leeds Art Gallery (henry-moore.org, Wikipedia).
- Part of the Henry Moore Foundation, set up by the sculptor Henry Moore; the Institute opened to the public on 22 April 1993 and grew out of a 1982 partnership between the Foundation and Leeds Art Gallery, the Henry Moore Centre for the Study of Sculpture (Wikipedia).
- Named for Henry Moore.
- Jeremy Dixon and Edward Jones converted three former wool merchants' offices into a Grade II listed building, linked to Leeds Art Gallery by a bridge (Wikipedia).
- The Institute curates the sculpture displays at Leeds Art Gallery and administers the Leeds collection for Leeds Museums and Galleries (henry-moore.org), and runs a programme of visiting research fellowships (Foundation accounts 2023-24).
- Admission is free. The Institute closed for refurbishment from 26 November 2023 and reopened on 12 July 2024. The Foundation's trustees report that disabled access has improved at the Institute (Foundation accounts 2023-24).

FUNDING
Henry Moore Institute: private endowment: the Henry Moore Foundation's investment portfolio, with a council contribution
- The Foundation's accounts say its income is mainly derived from investments provided from the assets left by its founder. It held £95.4m in investments managed as a quasi-endowment at 31 March 2024. Total investment income that year was £2.8m (£2.2m the year before). Total incoming resources were £8.9m, which included £4.3m from selling three duplicate casts of works in its collection (Foundation accounts 2023-24).
- Leeds City Council gave £145,743 in 2023-24 (and in 2022-23) as a contribution towards the running costs of the Institute and upkeep of the Council's sculpture collections and archive of artists' papers (Foundation accounts 2023-24).
- Arts Council England gave restricted grants of £77,500 (£77,000 the year before) on behalf of the Yorkshire Sculpture International partnership, in which the Institute takes part (Foundation accounts 2023-24).
- Museum and Gallery Tax Relief of £157,063 was received for the Foundation in 2023-24 (Foundation accounts 2023-24).
- Bloomberg Connects: the Foundation joined the Bloomberg Connects app in December 2022 with a £50,000 grant from Bloomberg Philanthropies for content for the app. Its restricted fund for the app stood at £44,881 at 31 March 2024 (£47,535 a year earlier, £2,654 spent). The accounts also record an application to Bloomberg Philanthropies' Digital Accelerator Program (Foundation accounts 2023-24). Henry Moore Studios & Gardens, the Foundation's site in Hertfordshire, is on the app (henry-moore.org).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 more than 260 UK artists published an open letter, reported by The New Arab, asking the National Theatre, the Royal Shakespeare Company, the Young Vic, the Royal Court and the Almeida to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies, which the letter calls "complicit in grave war crimes" through its involvement with Israeli settlements. The letter was aimed at those theatres and does not name the Foundation or the Institute.
- The connection: Bloomberg Philanthropies gave the Foundation, which runs the Institute, £50,000 for its Bloomberg Connects app content (Foundation accounts 2023-24).

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute is paid for chiefly from the Henry Moore Foundation's endowment of about £95m, with £145,743 a year from Leeds City Council and Arts Council England money for the Yorkshire Sculpture International partnership. The Foundation's 2023-24 accounts record a £50,000 grant from Bloomberg Philanthropies for its Connects app content.
