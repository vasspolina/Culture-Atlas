MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS BUDAPEST / HUNGARIAN NATIONAL GALLERY: Budapest, Hungary
ID: museum-of-fine-arts-budapest-hungarian-national-gallery
Website: https://www.szepmuveszeti.hu (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- The Museum of Fine Arts (Szépművészeti Múzeum) stands on Heroes' Square in Budapest and opened in 1906; Albert Schickedanz and Fülöp Herzog designed it in an eclectic, neoclassical style and it was built between 1900 and 1906 (Wikipedia).
- It holds over 100,000 non-Hungarian works in six departments: Egyptian, antique, old sculpture, old master paintings, modern art and graphics (Wikipedia).
- László Baán has led the museum since 2004 (Wikipedia). In 2026 USA Art News calls him director general of both the National Gallery and the Museum of Fine Arts.
- The museum was extensively restored as part of the Liget Budapest project, a park-renewal plan that also produced the House of Hungarian Music and a new Museum of Ethnography building (USA Art News, 8 September 2026).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays; last entry 17:00. The pages read give no ticket prices, free days or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museum of Fine Arts Budapest: mainly public: Hungarian state, with corporate sponsors for exhibitions
- The museum's sponsor page states that the Ministry for Social Relations and Culture is its maintainer (fenntartó), not a sponsor.
- 2026 sponsors named on the page, with what each supports: OTP Bank (main sponsor: the graphic exhibition space and two graphic shows); E.ON Energiamegoldások (main sponsor: the Egyptian exhibition); MBH Bank (main sponsor: the Gauguin exhibition); PPG Trilak (key supporter: "Vasarely 120"); MVM Group (key supporter: "Vasarely 120"); Szerencsejáték Zrt. (key supporter: the Egyptian exhibition); Veolia Energia Magyarország (key supporter: the Gauguin exhibition).
- Partners: KLM and K&H Bank (Dutch and Flemish rooms of the renovated Old Picture Gallery); Laterex Építő funds additions to the European collection; the Dutch embassy has supported restoration of Dutch and Flemish works for nearly ten years. Cooperation partners include Air France-KLM, Danubius Hotels and Porsche Hungária.
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (MVM Group, key supporter)
- MVM Group is a Hungarian state company with 100% state ownership (Wikipedia). Its member companies include electricity and gas network operators, a gas storage company and electricity and gas retailers; it also includes the Paks nuclear power company (MVM website).
- No reporting found of a campaign aimed at MVM's sponsorship of the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself. Most criticism found concerns the Liget Budapest project and its director Baán, and a 2026 decision to drop its final building.
1. USA Art News: unsigned, on the scrapped New National Gallery (8 September 2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/hungary-scraps-new-national-gallery-plans-in-city-park/
- Reports that Hungary's new government under Prime Minister Péter Magyar scrapped the planned 50,000 sq. m New National Gallery in City Park (about 100 billion forints, funded from state resources under the previous government); a decree halting the remaining construction was issued on 18 June.
- Baán hopes the SANAA design can be built elsewhere. Tamás Don, president of AICA Hungary, called for public professional discussions and asked whether relocation is the only solution.
2. hvg.hu: Sághy Erna, "Kétségek a múzeumi negyed ötletpályázata körül" (undated)
URL: https://hvg.hu/kultura/201345_ketsegek_a_muzeumi_negyed_otletpalyazata_ko
- Reports architects were divided over the museum quarter plan; competition co-winner István Berkeczi voiced unease, and critics argued building on City Park reduces green space.
- Describes the project as largely Baán's one-person vision with government backing and no impact studies. Total budget 150 billion forints.
- Hungarian news weekly, not art press.

WIDER CONTEXT
- National Cultural Fund (NKA): in late April 2026 Baán resigned from the NKA committee, writing to the minister that many grants, some very large, did not match the fund's basic aims. Outgoing culture minister Balázs Hankó said the tenders were open and followed the rules; Péter Magyar, the incoming prime minister, said an investigation was needed (Economx, 5 May 2026).
- Liget Budapest protests: activists occupied part of City Park from 17 March 2016 when workers began cutting trees for the museum quarter; a 2016 Ipsos survey cited by Waging Nonviolence found 75 percent of Budapest respondents opposed new facilities there. A letter from 13 Hungarian architects, planners and historians to UNESCO and ICOMOS argued the project would cause "irreversible damage" to the Andrássy Avenue World Heritage buffer zone; it does not name the Museum of Fine Arts.
- Connection: Baán was ministerial commissioner for the museum quarter (hvg.hu), and the museum was restored under Liget Budapest.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-maintained and its 2026 sponsors are named by exhibition, but no amounts are published, and one key supporter is the state-owned MVM Group. The weak points are MVM's role as a gas and electricity group and the director's close tie to the Liget Budapest project, which drew protests. Worth watching: what follows the scrapping of the New National Gallery.
