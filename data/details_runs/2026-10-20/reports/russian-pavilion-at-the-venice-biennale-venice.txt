RUSSIAN PAVILION AT THE VENICE BIENNALE: Venice, Italy
ID: russian-pavilion-at-the-venice-biennale-venice
Website: none confirmed (pavilionrus.com returned a server error when checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National pavilion of the Russian Federation in the Giardini, the Venice Biennale's main gardens.
- Designed by Alexey Shchusev and built between 1913 and 1914 (Wikipedia).
- Russia owns the building and had only to notify the Biennale of its request to take part in 2026 (AP).
- Russia last showed in 2019. In February 2022 artists Kirill Savchenkov and Alexandra Sukhareva and curator Raimundas Malašauskas withdrew after the invasion of Ukraine, and the pavilion stayed shut (Designboom). It was lent to Bolivia in 2024 (Meduza).
- 2026: "The Tree Is Rooted in the Sky", a series of sound performances by 38 artists, commissioner Anastasiia Karneeva (Meduza).
- Visiting: open to the public only on preview days, 5 to 8 May 2026, then closed from the 9 May opening to 22 November; recordings of the performances are shown on screens over the windows (Artlyst).

FUNDING
Russian Pavilion: state-owned building; exhibition funding unpublished, one outlet names Novatek
- Countries that own their pavilions do not pay to use them (ArtReview, 11 March 2026). The pavilion publishes no accounts, funder list or board.
- Novatek, the gas producer owned by Leonid Mikhelson, paid for the renovation before the 2021 Architecture Biennale (Filonenko, IWM).
- Unverified: that Novatek finances the 2026 project, from Euromaidan Press, a Ukrainian outlet strongly opposed to Russia's participation.
- No reporting found of Russian state budget lines for the pavilion.

Red flags: boycott and protest
- February 2022: Savchenkov, Sukhareva and Malašauskas quit; Savchenkov wrote that there is "no place for art when civilians are dying" (Designboom, ArtReview).
- 2025: Michał Murawski, co-curator of the Ukrainian pavilion, protested at the Russian pavilion, accusing the Biennale and architect Thomas Heatherwick, whose project it hosted, of collaborating with Russia (Meduza, Internazionale; translation of the Italian).
- March 2026: the European Commission said Russia's return was "not compatible with the EU's collective response" to the invasion; culture ministers of 22 European countries signed a protest letter (ArtReview, Meduza).
- 30 April 2026: the Biennale's five-member jury resigned (AP). 6 May 2026: about 50 members of Pussy Riot and FEMEN blockaded the pavilion (AP).

Red flags: EU funding withdrawn
- On 21 July 2026 the EU's education and culture agency ended a 2 million euro grant to the Biennale Foundation over the Russian pavilion; the Biennale said it would contest the decision (ArtReview, European Pravda).

Red flags: sanctioned circle around the commissioner
- Karneeva is the daughter of a retired FSB general who was a Rostec deputy director; she co-runs the company Smart Art with Ekaterina Vinokurova, daughter of Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, who is under US, UK, Canadian, Australian and Japanese sanctions (ArtReview, Finestre sull'Arte).
- Ukraine sanctioned Karneeva in April 2026 (Euromaidan Press).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Heavy and recent. Critics attack the Biennale's decision to readmit Russia and the people behind the pavilion, not the art.

- Argues the national-pavilion format makes the Biennale a soft power platform by design. Not an art outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Filonenko links Russia's 1993 handover and the 2024 Bolivia loan to Russian gas and lithium deals, arguing culture is tied to resource trade (IWM, 2024).
- The Russian pavilion is one of the Biennale's national pavilions, so these critiques of the forum apply to it.

BOTTOM LINE
The building belongs to the Russian state and no funder list is published; Novatek's money is documented for the 2021 renovation and unverified for 2026. The weak points are the commissioner's family and business circle, the lack of any disclosed budget and the EU grant lost over the 2026 reopening. Watch whether the Biennale's challenge to the EU decision succeeds and who funds the next Russian project.
