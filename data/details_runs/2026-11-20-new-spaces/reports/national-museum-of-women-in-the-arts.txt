NATIONAL MUSEUM OF WOMEN IN THE ARTS: Washington, USA
ID: national-museum-of-women-in-the-arts
Website: https://nmwa.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private, nonprofit museum of art by women at 1250 New York Ave. NW in downtown Washington, DC.
- Founded by Wilhelmina Cole Holladay and Wallace F. Holladay and incorporated in November 1981; it opened in its building in April 1987.
- The Holladay Collection, begun in the 1970s, is the core of the holdings.
- The building is a 1908 Masonic Temple of 78,810 square feet, bought in 1983.
- A renovation by Sandra Vicchio & Associates of Baltimore began in 2021 and the museum reopened on 21 October 2023.
- On site: galleries, a library and research centre with 25,000 books and print resources, a shop and a café.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10 am to 5 pm; adults USD 16; visitors 21 and under free; free Community Days on the first Sunday and second Wednesday of each month.

FUNDING
National Museum of Women in the Arts: privately funded: members, individual donors, endowment income and earned income
- Fiscal 2025 support and revenue was $14,183,270: grants and contributions $4,969,868, membership $1,376,171, earned income $2,770,415 and net investment income $5,066,816 (Year in Review 2025).
- Expenses were $18,344,892, so net assets fell by $4,161,621 to $195,460,854.
- More than 9,000 supporters gave in the year, and 58,702 people visited.
- The Space to Soar capital campaign paid for the $67.5 million renovation.
- Largest campaign gifts: Wilhelmina C. and Wallace F. Holladay, Sr. ($15 million or more); Gloria and Dan Logan with the Revada Foundation, and Jacqueline Badger Mars ($5 million to $14.9 million).
- Public money in the campaign: Events DC ($2 million to $4.9 million), the DC Commission on the Arts and Humanities ($500,000 to $999,999) and an Institute of Museum and Library Services grant.
- The museum reported "a major funding loss" in fiscal 2025 from cuts to the National Endowment for the Humanities; members and donors filled the gap (stated by the museum).
- Form 990 data for the year to June 2025 give contributions of $7,322,699, 55.7% of revenue (ProPublica).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private philanthropy led by the founding family, with small city and federal grants, and none of the named donors is objected to. The weak point is the operating gap in fiscal 2025, when spending ran $4.2 million ahead of revenue. Worth watching: federal arts and humanities grants.
