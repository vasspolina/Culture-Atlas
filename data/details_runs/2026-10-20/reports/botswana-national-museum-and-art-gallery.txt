BOTSWANA NATIONAL MUSEUM AND ART GALLERY: Gaborone, Botswana

OVERVIEW
- National museum and art gallery in central Gaborone, near the Main Mall (Music in Africa).
- Created in 1967 by an Act of Parliament and opened to the public in 1968; also called the National Museum and Art Gallery (Wikipedia).
- It includes the National Art Gallery and the Octagon Gallery, and since November 2007 has overseen the National Botanical Garden (Wikipedia).
- It helps preserve Tsodilo, the country's first World Heritage Site, and cares for Tsholofelo Park, where the remains of "El Negro" were reburied after returning from Spain (Wikipedia).
- Collections: wildlife exhibits, music instruments, crafts and paintings by local artists from the 1960s onward, and a courtyard of transport exhibits including a steam locomotive; every August the gallery hosts the national basket show (Music in Africa).
- Run by the Department of National Museum and Monuments, which led the refurbishment project (Mmegi, 2020).
- Visiting: Music in Africa lists the museum as open Tuesday to Sunday with free admission; that profile, added in October 2015, describes the exhibit hall as poorly lit and in need of maintenance. No current hours or accessibility details were found.

FUNDING
Botswana National Museum and Art Gallery: public: Government of Botswana
- Statutory museum set up by Act of Parliament in 1967 (Wikipedia).
- Refurbishment: a groundbreaking ceremony in August 2020 started a project costing about P33 million to redesign the permanent exhibition, with a second phase adding gallery modifications and an extra gallery floor. Contractors named are HL&L Architects and Motaki (Pty) Ltd; the design phase began in April 2019 and completion was expected by mid-2021 (Your Botswana, Mmegi). The minister described it as a government undertaking (Your Botswana).
- Mmegi reported on 25 December 2022 that the refurbishment "has experienced delays and is yet to complete" and gave no new completion date.
- A report undated but tied to the museum's 50th anniversary (Your Botswana) said the Vice President announced P40 million in the National Development Plan 11 to refurbish the museum headquarters, plus P30 million for an ivory museum.
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts, a board or any private or foreign funder.
- No reporting found of whether the refurbished museum has reopened.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Government of Botswana's, with no private, corporate or flagged funder found. The weak points are that no accounts, board or reports could be found, the refurbishment that was due in mid-2021 was still unfinished in December 2022, and no working official website was located. Worth watching is whether the refurbishment completes and what the museum then publishes about its funding.
