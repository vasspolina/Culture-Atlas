KUNSTMUSEUM LIECHTENSTEIN: Vaduz, Liechtenstein
ID: kunstmuseum-liechtenstein
Website: https://www.kunstmuseum.li (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Liechtenstein's state museum of modern and contemporary art, at Städtle 32 in the pedestrian zone of Vaduz.
- A gift of ten paintings to the state in 1967 led to the founding of the state art collection the following year, with Georg Malin as its first curator.
- A group of private donors, the government and the municipality of Vaduz made the building possible. It was handed to the state in August 2000 and opened on 11 November 2000.
- The black cube by Meinrad Morger and Heinrich Degelo with Christian Kerez measures 60 by 25 by 12 metres and has 1,750 square metres of exhibition space.
- Since 2015 the Hilti Art Foundation's building, by Morger + Dettli, has stood beside it and the two houses share an entrance and a ticket.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday to 20:00, closed Monday. Admission is CHF 15, reduced CHF 10, free up to age 16 and for everyone on Wednesdays.

FUNDING
Kunstmuseum Liechtenstein: public: the State of Liechtenstein pays four fifths of the budget
- The state set up the public-law foundation Stiftung Kunstmuseum Liechtenstein to run the museum, and the government appoints the seven members of its board for four years.
- Income in 2025 was CHF 5,454,326, of which the state contribution was CHF 4,385,000 (annual report 2025).
- The Hilti Art Foundation paid CHF 300,000 for guards and CHF 100,000 for education, communication and technical support.
- Donations brought CHF 250,036, sponsorship CHF 11,976 and admissions and tours CHF 143,218.
- The Stiftung Freunde des Kunstmuseum Liechtenstein, founded in 2006, paid for four works by Ana Lupas in 2025, and Ars Rhenia supported a wall painting by Hanna Roeckle.
- The museum and the Hilti Art Foundation together had 28,210 visitors in 2025, up from 27,270.
- Christiane Meyer-Stoll became director in January 2026, after Letizia Ragaglia.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the state's own and the accounts are published each year. The private partner next door, the Hilti Art Foundation, pays a fixed share of the running costs.
