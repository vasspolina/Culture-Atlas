BLACK FAMILY VISUAL ARTS CENTER (DARTMOUTH): Hanover, USA
ID: black-family-visual-arts-center-dartmouth-hanover
Website: none of its own found; visiting details are on https://hop.dartmouth.edu (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Dartmouth College's visual arts building at 22 Lebanon Street, Hanover, New Hampshire, on an Arts Plaza shared with the Hopkins Center and the Hood Museum of Art (The Dartmouth, 2012; Hopkins Center).
- Built as the Visual Arts Center; construction began in spring 2010, and it was renamed for the Black family after a $48 million gift announced in March 2012 (The Dartmouth).
- Named for Leon Black, Dartmouth class of 1973, founder of Apollo Global Management and a college trustee from 2002 to 2011, and his wife Debra (The Dartmouth).
- Designed by Machado and Silvetti Associates of Boston: 105,000 square feet on three storeys around a three-storey atrium, the Arts Forum (The Dartmouth).
- Houses the studio art and film and media studies departments and the digital humanities programme, with Loew Auditorium, a 50-seat screening room, a gallery and a media laboratory (The Dartmouth).
- Visiting: events listed by the Hopkins Center are free and open to the public; the box office is open Tuesday to Friday 12:00 to 17:00 and Saturday 14:00 to 17:00. No accessibility details found.

FUNDING
Black Family Visual Arts Center: private gift: Leon and Debra Black's $48 million through their family foundation
- 2012: the Debra and Leon Black Family Foundation gave $48 million for construction (The Dartmouth, 2026).
- The gift also paid for an Ellsworth Kelly work on the Hopkins Center's east facade (The Dartmouth, 2012); a 27 May 2014 letter from Dartmouth's Robert W. Lasher in the Epstein files puts the Kelly commission at $3 million and lists $500,000 to renovate the president's house (Valley News, 2026).
- The Blacks also endowed professorships in Shakespearean studies and Jewish studies and gave to the Dartmouth College Fund, Kemeny Hall and the Roth Center (The Dartmouth, 2012).
- Dartmouth says it "currently has no financial relationship" with Black (spokesperson Jana Barnello, Valley News, 2026).

Red flags: Epstein-linked donor
- Jeffrey Epstein directed the Black Family Foundation, through which the Dartmouth gifts passed, for ten years until 2007 (Hyperallergic, 2021; Valley News).
- Epstein's name appears on the foundation's 2012 tax forms; the Black family calls this an error (Valley News).
- Black paid Epstein $170 million from 2012 to 2017, which Black says was for tax and investment advice (Valley News); a Dechert LLP review for Apollo put the fees at about $158 million and found no evidence Black took part in crimes (Hyperallergic).
- Senator Ron Wyden wrote to Black on 20 March 2026 that he paid Epstein 30 times more for tax advice than he paid several law firms (Valley News).
- Black denies wrongdoing; three lawsuits accusing him of sexual harassment, abuse or rape have been dismissed (The Dartmouth, 2026).

Red flags: campaign to remove the name
- 2021: alumni of the Dartmouth Community Against Gender Harassment and Sexual Violence called for renaming; the college said it had no plans to change the name (Hyperallergic).
- April 2026: Women of Dartmouth, an alumni group of over 30,000 members, the Student Government Association and The Dartmouth's editorial board called for removing the name (Valley News; The Dartmouth).
- 29 May 2026: about 20 students, faculty and residents rallied outside the building (The Dartmouth).
- June 2026: trustees set up a Naming Policy Committee under chair Gregg Lemkau and discussed community feedback about the centre; no decision on the name was announced (Dartmouth trustees).

BOTTOM LINE
The building's money is one family gift, and the college says the financial tie has ended. The weak point is the name: the gift passed through a foundation Epstein had directed until 2007, and Black paid Epstein $170 million from 2012, the year the building opened, to 2017. Worth watching: the Naming Policy Committee's recommendation.
