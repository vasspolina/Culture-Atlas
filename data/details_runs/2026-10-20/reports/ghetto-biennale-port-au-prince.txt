GHETTO BIENNALE: Port-au-Prince, Haiti
ID: ghetto-biennale-port-au-prince
Website: https://ghettobiennale.org

OVERVIEW
- Cross-cultural arts festival in Port-au-Prince held in the neighbouring areas of Lakou Cheri and Ghetto Leanne on the Grand Rue, hosted by the artists' collective Atis Rezistans, every two years since December 2009 (stated by the festival).
- Founded in 2009 by the Haitian artist André Eugène, a member of Atis Rezistans, and the British artist and curator Leah Gordon (Wikipedia, quoting The Observer).
- Hyperallergic reports the founders conceived it as a "Trojan Horse": Haitian artists had difficulty obtaining travel visas, so outside artists would come to them (Myriam Vanneschi, 27 December 2013).
- The festival lists editions in 2009, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2019, 2022 and 2024 (stated by the festival). The 2013 edition was framed as "Decentering the Market and Other Tales of Progress" (Hyperallergic). The 7th, "Swen Moun" (Heal People), ran from 6 to 31 January 2022 (Biennial Foundation call).
- Atis Rezistans | Ghetto Biennale was shown at St Kunigundis Church in Kassel during documenta fifteen in 2022 (stated by the festival).
- In August 2024 at least three former participants had been killed in crossfire between gangs and police, and most Grand Rue artists had relocated to other neighbourhoods (John Cussans, Invisible Mirrors blog, 7 August 2024).

FUNDING
Ghetto Biennale: minimal budget: a few named private donors and foundations, with visiting artists paying their own costs
- For the 7th edition the organisers wrote "There is NO funding for this event and you will be expected to cover the cost of your flight, accommodation and materials" (Biennial Foundation call, 2021).
- Hyperallergic reported in 2013 that funding was very limited, that visiting artists paid for their own projects, and that the money raised covered local logistics and fees for Haitian guides, drivers and translators.
- Sponsors named on the festival's edition pages: 2015: Owsley Brown III, Fokal, Mathieu Carrière, The Hand/Eye Foundation and The Oloffson Hotel. 2017: Owsley Brown III, Fokal, The Oloffson Hotel, and Ville de Lausanne / Fonds des Arts Plastiques, which supported the artists. 2019: Owsley Brown III, FOKAL, The Oloffson Hotel, Fondation MWÈM and Pioneer Works.
- The Haitian Resource Development Foundation gave logistical support in 2015 and 2017 (festival pages).
- Leah Gordon organised a one-night sale in Hackney, London, on 31 August 2024 to raise money for the relocation and survival costs of Grand Rue artists, with works by André Eugène, Jean Claude Saintilus, Wesner Bazile and Gétho Jean Baptiste (Invisible Mirrors blog).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical essay, from 2013, questions the festival's premises.
1. Hyperallergic: Myriam Vanneschi, "The Many Contradictions of a 'Ghetto Biennale'" (27 December 2013)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/the-many-contradictions-of-a-ghetto-biennale/
- Asks whether the biennial was an exit strategy through networking and markets or "poverty tourism".
- Says visiting artists tended to judge their work by how much it helped Haitians, which the founders did not intend, and that Haitian artists sometimes preferred working for someone over collaborating as peers.
- Notes Haitian artists want to sell work while visiting artists make noncommercial, dematerialised work.
- Names the Ghetto Biennale and covers its 2013 edition.

BOTTOM LINE
The Ghetto Biennale runs on very little money: small private and foundation sponsors named on its edition pages, fundraising by its organisers and visiting artists who pay their own way.
