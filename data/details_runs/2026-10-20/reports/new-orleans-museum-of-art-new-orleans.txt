OVERVIEW
- Art museum in City Park at One Collins C. Diboll Circle, near Carrollton and Esplanade avenues (Wikipedia; museum website).
- Founded in 1911 with funding from the philanthropist and collector Isaac Delgado; it opened on 11 December 1911, and Delgado died in January 1912. Earlier name: Delgado Museum of Art (Wikipedia).
- The building was partly designed by former city chief engineer Benjamin Morgan Harrod; a $23 million expansion and renovation opened in 1993 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 40,000 objects spanning about 5,000 years, including over 12,000 photographs. Behind the main building is the 11.5 acre Besthoff Sculpture Garden with over 90 sculptures (Wikipedia). On site: Café NOMA by Ralph Brennan, a shop and an auditorium (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: museum open Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 5pm, Wednesdays noon to 7pm; the Sculpture Garden is free and open seven days (museum website). A 2023 museum press release gave adults $15, children 12 and under free and Louisiana residents free on Wednesdays; current prices were not available. No reporting found of accessibility details on the pages read.

FUNDING
New Orleans Museum of Art: privately funded: trustees, foundations and donors
- The Helis Foundation, "established and funded by the William Helis Family in 1955" (foundation website), sponsors NOMA's free days through its Art for All programme, which the foundation says began in 2007; Wednesdays became the free day on 3 March 2021 (foundation post, 8 March 2021). A NOMA release of 1 June 2023 credits the foundation for free Wednesday admission for Louisiana residents and free admission for ages 13 to 19. No reporting found of the amounts.
- The Edward Wisner Foundation funded the Wisner Education Wing in 1970 to 1971 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a current donor list or trustee roster that could be opened.

Red flags: Helis Foundation, oil family money
- The foundation's post of 8 March 2021 calls William George Helis, whose family set up the foundation in 1955, five years after his death, an oil magnate. Its leader David Kerstein is described on the foundation's site as "a soft-spoken lawyer, oil-company executive and philanthropist". The foundation's about and Art for All pages do not mention oil.
- The open letter of 12 October 2020 supporting #DismantleNOMA says the museum is sustained by "oil money and other harmful private funders" and cites the Helis Foundation, which it says funds museum events and subsidises free admission for teenagers (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara). Unverified: the letter's description of the foundation as the philanthropic arm of the Helis Oil & Gas Energy Project; no document in the piece shows it.

Red flags: open letters alleging racism, 2020
- In June 2020 five former NOMA workers published an open letter saying 30 employees resigned in two years because of a "toxic work environment and institutional racism". It alleged slurs, wage discrimination against Black workers, surveillance, non-disclosure agreements, and a permanent installation of a plantation parlor (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 25 June 2020).
- NOMA declined to comment on specific allegations, citing "privacy restrictions" (Hyperallergic).
- A second letter on 12 October 2020, signed by more than 150 artists and arts workers including the Guerrilla Girls and Michael Rakowitz, demanded the removal of director Susan M. Taylor and an independent investigation, and urged artists to withhold labour and support. NOMA said it was "committed to being an anti-racist institution" and pointed to an "Agenda for Change" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara).
- On 29 June 2023 NOMA announced Amanda Maples, a white woman, as curator of African art. More than 850 Instagram comments criticised the hire. NOMA announced a town hall, and on 30 June a spokesperson told Gambit her record led to her selection (The Art Newspaper, Torey Akers, 3 July 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is privately funded and little donor detail is published. The clearest funding weak point is the Helis Foundation, whose family founded it and whose leader is an oil-company executive, as the 2020 letter noted. The larger public objections concern working conditions and hiring, which NOMA answered with an anti-racism agenda and a town hall. Watch for a current donor and trustee list.
