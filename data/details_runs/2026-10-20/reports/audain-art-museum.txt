AUDAIN ART MUSEUM: Whistler, Canada
ID: audain-art-museum
Website: https://audainartmuseum.com (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of British Columbia art at 4350 Blackcomb Way in Whistler, on the "unceded, shared territory" of the Squamish and Lil'wat Nations (stated by the museum).
- Founded by Michael Audain and Yoshiko Karasawa to house British Columbia works from their private collection. Audain chose the site in September 2012 and agreed with the Resort Municipality of Whistler in December 2012 to build; construction began in August 2013 and the museum opened in March 2016 (stated by the museum).
- Named for Michael Audain, who is chairman and major shareholder of the home builder Polygon Homes (Wikipedia).
- Building: 56,000 square feet designed by Patkau Architects, raised on pillars because of the floodplain site and steep-roofed for heavy snowfall. The first plan in December 2012 was for 25,000 square feet; the expansion was announced in April 2013 (stated by the museum).
- On site: a permanent collection of British Columbia art including carving, painting and photography, an Indigenous mask collection and a large display of Emily Carr paintings, plus rotating special exhibitions. It received Category A status from the Department of Canadian Heritage in July 2019.
- Visiting: open Thursday to Monday 11:00 to 18:00, closed Tuesday and Wednesday. Adults $22 (before GST), seniors $19, ages 19 to 25 $15; free for under-19s, members and Indigenous peoples. Wheelchair accessible, with an elevator to the upper galleries.

FUNDING
Audain Art Museum: self-funded: the founders' collection and foundation, with an endowment and donations
- The museum was founded to house works from the founders' private collection; the museum's page says memberships and donations support its programmes, and credits the Audain Foundation with funding at least one artwork (stated by the museum).
- Land: leased for 199 years (Wikipedia, Michael Audain page); the museum says it agreed in December 2012 with the Resort Municipality of Whistler to build.
- Construction cost: BCBusiness gave about $35 million in 2015, projected before opening. The museum does not publish a figure.
- Audain Foundation (founded 1997, chaired by Audain) funds British Columbia visual arts and sponsors the $100,000 Audain Prize for the Visual Arts (Wikipedia). Audain is chairman and major shareholder of Polygon Homes, which he joined in 1980 and which with its affiliate Morningstar Homes has completed over 34,000 homes in Metro Vancouver (Wikipedia, Michael Audain page).
- Endowment: the museum's endowment fund goal rose from $25 million to $50 million, and Wikipedia says it was met by mid-2024 (Michael Audain page). The pages opened name no endowment donors.
- 2026 gala raised $1.5 million for the 10-year anniversary (stated by the museum). Membership circles run from $1,000 to $10,000.
- Not confirmed in the pages opened: payments from Polygon Homes, Onni Group, or the Whistler Blackcomb Foundation. No reporting found of any fossil fuel, arms or other flagged funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum found in art media. Architecture and business coverage is admiring.
1. The Globe and Mail: Marsha Lederman, "How a $100-million private donation put the Vancouver Art Gallery's transformation back on track" (2022)
URL: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/art-and-architecture/article-how-a-100-million-private-donation-put-the-vancouver-art-gallerys/
- Not criticism of the museum. Reports that Audain's 2012 announcement of his own museum in Whistler was seen as a blow to the Vancouver Art Gallery's plans.
- Audain's $100 million gift to that gallery: the article says he did not mandate or require anything but suggested several design changes, and that an underground storage floor was removed, saving about $12 million.
2. Azure: "2017 AZ Awards Winner: Architecture Over 1,000 Square Metres" (2017)
URL: https://www.azuremagazine.com/?p=88983
- Praises the building for blending into the forest. Contains no criticism and does not discuss funding.

BOTTOM LINE
No flagged money was found behind the museum: a private collection, a foundation and an endowment, with the founder a home-building chairman, on land leased from the municipality. The weak points are that the endowment donors are not named in the pages opened and no financial statements were found. Worth watching: who the endowment donors are, and whether Polygon, Onni or Whistler Blackcomb Foundation money is current.
