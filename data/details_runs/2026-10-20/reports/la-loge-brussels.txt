LA LOGE: Brussels, Belgium
ID: la-loge-brussels
Website: https://www.la-loge.be (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit space for contemporary art, architecture and theory at Kluisstraat / rue de l'Ermitage 86, in Ixelles, Brussels.
- Founded by the architect Philippe Rotthier, now Honorary President; activities began in 2012 under founding director Anne-Claire Schmitz (2012 to 2019). Wim Waelput is the current director and curator.
- Named for the Masonic lodge that built the house: the Freemason order Le Droit Humain used it until 1976.
- The former temple was built in 1934 to 1935 by the Modernist architects Fernand Bodson and Louis Van Hooveld, and breaks with the Egyptian style usual for Masonic temples. It forms part of a modernist group of four buildings on the site (Heritage Days listing). From 1984 to 2011 it housed the Museum of the Archives d'Architecture Moderne (AAM).
- Programme: exhibitions, plus symposiums, screenings, lectures and performances. The exhibition on show in October 2026 is Michael Lin's "Tea Towels and Serviettes" (10 September to 6 December 2026).
- Open Thursday to Sunday, 13:00 to 18:00; closed 25 December to 1 January and behind closed doors in July and August. Exhibitions are free, with donations at the entrance. The main space is reachable for visitors with reduced mobility but has three steps and no lift; the toilets are in the basement and are not accessible.

FUNDING
La Loge: mainly public: Belgian regional and community bodies, the National Lottery, and a members' group
- Core public support from the Flemish Government, the French Community (FWB) and the Vlaamse Gemeenschapscommissie (VGC). The page gives no amounts.
- Further support from the Municipality of Ixelles-Elsene, COCOF, the Brussels-Capital Region and the National Lottery of Belgium (stated by the space). visit.brussels and the Brussels-Capital Region supported the website.
- First Sight, its supporter community: individual membership €1,050, organisations and companies €2,050 (stated by the space). Named members include Almine Rech Gallery and individual members, among them Pierre Iserbyt, whom the about page lists as chair of the board.
- The space says it relies mainly on public funding and a very small team. It was founded as a privately initiated association by Rotthier, who owned the building according to La Libre (2012); no reporting found of how he financed the conversion or of any ongoing payment by him.
- No reporting found of annual accounts, grant amounts or a corporate sponsor.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind La Loge is public grants, the National Lottery and a small members' group, and nothing found ties it to a flagged funder. The weak points are the lack of published accounts or grant amounts and the unstated terms on which the founder's building is used. Worth watching: whether the space publishes accounts and how First Sight member money, including that of a commercial gallery, relates to the programme.
