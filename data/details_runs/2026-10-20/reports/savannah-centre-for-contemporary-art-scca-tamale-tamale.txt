SAVANNAH CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART (SCCA TAMALE): Tamale, Ghana
ID: savannah-centre-for-contemporary-art-scca-tamale-tamale
Website: https://www.sccatamale.org

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run project space, exhibition and research hub, cultural repository and artists' residency in Tamale, northern Ghana (stated by the centre). Address given on its site: QK 37 Montana Street, Kpambegu, Tamale.
- Founded by the Ghanaian artist Ibrahim Mahama. Work on the building began in February 2014; Mahama says the idea grew out of conversations with students and colleagues, who then saw institutionalising it as important (Will Furtado, "Savannah Center for Contemporary Art", Magazine C&, 10 August 2021). It opened on 15 March 2019 with a retrospective of the Ghanaian modernist Kofi Dawson (White Cube announcement, 27 February 2019).
- Its sister institution is Red Clay, where Mahama has a studio; the two spaces share residencies and exhibitions (Apollo, 28 September 2020).
- Affiliated with blaxTARLINES KUMASI (Magazine C&).
- Programmes: exhibitions, residencies and research. Mahama says children are a major focus of the institutional programme, and that the centre is based on the motto that an ignorant person can teach another ignorant person what that person doesn't know (Magazine C&). A photo caption in Magazine C& refers to the 2020 exhibition "Akutia: Blindfolding the Sun and the Poetics of Peace". The centre's site lists "The Writings on the Wall", 13 September 2025 to 14 March 2026.
- In 2024 the centre received a special recognition from the Art Space Grant, a collaboration between KADIST and AFIELD (AFIELD).
- Visiting: Monday to Thursday 11:00 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, closed Fridays (the centre's website).

FUNDING
Savannah Centre for Contemporary Art (Tamale): self-funded: the founder's own earnings from selling his art (stated by Mahama)
- Asked about the funding structure, Mahama said: "There is no funding structure. I fund everything from the work that I sell as an artist" (Will Furtado, Magazine C&, 10 August 2021).
- Mahama is represented by Apalazzo Gallery and White Cube (Apollo, 24 December 2025).
- The centre's site lists individuals who collaborated with and supported it.

Not related: Ibrahim Mahama, a Ghanaian businessman born in 1971, is listed on Wikipedia's disambiguation page as a different person from the artist (born 1987) who founded the centre.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is paid for by its founder: Ibrahim Mahama states that he funds everything from his own sales as an artist, and Apollo says he is represented by Apalazzo Gallery and White Cube.
