OKAYAMA ART SUMMIT: Okayama, Japan
ID: okayama-art-summit-okayama
Website: https://www.okayamaartsummit.jp

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art festival in Okayama City, created to revive the old castle town area around Okayama Castle and Korakuen Garden. The first edition ran from 9 October to 27 November 2016 on the theme "Development", with Liam Gillick as artistic director (festival website).
- Organised by the Okayama Art Summit Executive Committee, chaired by Okayama's mayor Masao Omori (festival website).
- Rirkrit Tiravanija was artistic director of the 2022 edition (30 September to 27 November 2022); Philippe Parreno, appointed on 24 October 2023, directed the 2025 edition, The Parks of Aomame, from 26 September to 24 November 2025 with thirty individuals and groups from twelve countries and regions (e-flux).
- Admission was free in 2025; the festival site announces the next edition for 2028 (news item of 30 March 2026).
- Yasuharu Ishikawa, president of the Ishikawa Foundation, is executive producer of the 2025 edition and Taro Nasu of the gallery TARO NASU is executive director (festival website).

FUNDING
Okayama Art Summit: public and corporate: Okayama City and Prefecture, the Ishikawa Foundation, and named sponsors
- The executive committee consists mainly of Okayama City, Okayama Prefecture and the Ishikawa Foundation (Tokyo Art Beat, Natsuko Fukushima, 19 October 2022).
- The 2025 about page lists Merco Group as co-organizer; Special Sponsors Ishikawa Holdings, Sanyo Shimbun, Chugin Financial Group and JR West Group; and Sponsors Okayama Toyota Group, Sapix, Tomiya Corporation, Nakashima Holdings, JT, Ryobi Group, RSK, OHK, TSC, ProdCo, Okayama University of Science and Maruzen-Yushodo. Granting Agencies are the Social Creation Arts Fund and Institut Français.

Red flags: Tobacco
- The 2025 edition's about page lists "JT" among the Sponsors, the tier below Special Sponsors. The page gives the name only as "JT"; the logo links to jti.co.jp, which Wikipedia gives as the website of Japan Tobacco, a tobacco company.

Red flags: citizen petitions over executive producer Yasuharu Ishikawa
- Petitioners: the Okayama Citizens' Association for Okayama Art Summit, formed in August 2021. It asked that Ishikawa be replaced as general producer by an expert with ties to Okayama and that the festival concept be reviewed (Tokyo Art Beat, 24 November 2021 and 19 October 2022).
- Background: multiple harassment accusations against Ishikawa, then head of the fashion company Stripe International, were reported for August 2015 to May 2018. He received a "stern warning" in December 2018 with no disciplinary action, did not admit harassment and resigned as company president in March 2020 (Tokyo Art Beat, 19 October 2022).
- Dates: petition to Okayama City Council on 19 November 2021, withdrawn 24 November; to the Prefectural Assembly on 22 February 2022 and the City Council on 25 February. The City Council's Citizens and Industry Committee rejected it on 9 March 2022 and the Prefectural Assembly's Environment, Culture, Health and Welfare Committee on 15 March (Tokyo Art Beat, 24 November 2021, updated 18 August 2022).
- On 8 July 2022 the group submitted a request with 400 signatures; on 15 July the city replied that it would "do its best to find an alternative venue" and did not address the harassment allegations (Tokyo Art Beat, 19 October 2022).
- On 27 September 2022 the executive committee said an extraordinary investigation committee at Stripe did not find harassment by Ishikawa, that it saw no need to explain to citizens or share information with participating artists, and answered "not applicable" when asked for Tiravanija's view (Tokyo Art Beat, title translated).
- The Ishikawa Foundation, of which Ishikawa is president, sits on the executive committee (Tokyo Art Beat, 19 October 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Tokyo Art Beat covered the petitions and the committee's answers; one essay criticises the organisers' lack of explanation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the festival is local public and corporate: Okayama City and Prefecture, the Ishikawa Foundation, and regional sponsors. Documented points are the 2025 Sponsors listing of "JT" and the 2021 and 2022 citizen petitions against executive producer Yasuharu Ishikawa, which committees of the city council and prefectural assembly rejected in March 2022.
