SECESSION (WIENER SECESSION): Vienna, Austria
ID: secession-vienna
Website: https://secession.at (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run exhibition house for contemporary art at Friedrichstrasse 12, Vienna (secession.at, "artist-run since 1897").
- Founded on 3 April 1897 by Gustav Klimt, Koloman Moser, Josef Hoffmann, Joseph Maria Olbrich and others, in opposition to the Vienna Künstlerhaus; Klimt was the first president (Wikipedia). The association's official name is Vereinigung bildender Künstler*innen Wiener Secession.
- Motto on the facade: "Der Zeit ihre Kunst. Der Kunst ihre Freiheit" (To every age its art, to art its freedom), removed in 1908 (secession.at).
- Building: designed by Olbrich; cornerstone laid on 28 April 1898 and completed on 29 October 1898. The City of Vienna provided the site, and Karl Wittgenstein and other patrons paid part of the cost, with the rest from the proceeds of the Secession's first exhibition. Bomb damage and a fire in 1945; renovations in 1963, 1984 to 1985 (Adolf Krischanitz) and 2017 to 2018 (secession.at).
- What is there: contemporary exhibitions whose programme the artists' board decides in a "democratic and collective" process (our translation), and Klimt's Beethoven Frieze (secession.at).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00. Admission EUR 13; over-65s EUR 10; students under 26 and visitors with disabilities (plus one assistant) EUR 7.50; children under 12 free; free on the first Wednesday of each month. The Beethoven Frieze and parts of the exhibitions are barrier-free, with a lift on the left side of the building (secession.at).

FUNDING
Secession: mixed: public support, a bank as main sponsor, and corporate partners
- Main sponsor: Erste Bank. Erste Bank customers pay a reduced ticket of EUR 11 (secession.at).
- The Secession's partner page states that only roughly a third of its budget is covered by grants from the Austrian federal government and the City of Vienna, and calls the house largely self-supporting (secession.at partner page, statement by the space).
- Public funders, named as long-standing supporters of the annual programme: Stadt Wien Kultur (City of Vienna) and the federal ministry for housing, arts, culture, media and sport (secession.at partner page).
- Annual partners: Neuroth, Dorotheum and LG. Exhibition partners 2026: Arbeiterkammer and UNIQA.
- Cooperation partners 2026: Bösendorfer, Holmes Place, Legero United, Wittmann, Bildrecht and KFH Legal; hotel, media (Ö1, W24, Kurier, Der Standard), cultural and in-kind partners are also listed on the partner page. The page gives no start or end years or roles for any partner.
- Other income: Friends membership, donations, ticket and shop sales; annual pass EUR 25 (secession.at).
- Board (artists): Ramesch Daha (president), Jun Yang and Barbara Kapusta (vice presidents), Michael Part (treasurer), Sophie Thun (secretary) and eight other members (secession.at mission page).
- No reporting found of the amounts given by any funder, or of published accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Secession's funding or sponsors found in art media. The only coverage of conflict is of the association's internal governance, from two Austrian outlets whose pages were cut off, and a 2021 report on the new president's plans.
1. Der Standard: "Secession: Holub bietet Rücktritt an" (no date or author visible; the article names Barbara Holub as president since May 2006)
URL: https://www.derstandard.at/story/2992559/secession-holub-bietet-ruecktritt-an
- Reports that eight of the fourteen board members resigned, so the board could no longer make decisions; the subtitle says the president faces the general assembly, and the lead says the resignations had so far not moved her to step down.
- Gives no reasons for the resignations in the part that could be read; the page ends after the lead paragraph.
2. Falter: Matthias Dusini, "Secessionskrieg beendet" (2007)
URL: https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20071212/secessionskrieg-beendet
- Teaser: after months of quarrels, the artists' association has a new leadership. Only the teaser could be read; the page is cut off.
3. Tiroler Tageszeitung: report on the election of Ramesch Daha (5 October 2021)
URL: https://www.tt.com/artikel/18393715/wiener-secession-hat-mit-ramesch-daha-neue-praesidentin
- Not criticism: Daha said the new board would engage critically with its own role, its privileges and the Secession's history (our translation). Names Herwig Kempinger as the previous president, who did not run again.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Secession is a mix of City of Vienna and federal support, Erste Bank as main sponsor and a list of Austrian corporate partners, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no amounts or accounts were found, and that the two governance reports could be read only in part. Worth watching: published accounts and any dated report on the board resignations.
