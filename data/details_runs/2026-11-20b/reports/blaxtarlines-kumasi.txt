BLAXTARLINES KUMASI: Kumasi, Ghana
ID: blaxtarlines-kumasi
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Artist collective, organisational network and art incubator at the Department of Painting and Sculpture, Faculty of Art, Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) in Kumasi (KNUST); it "works in association with, yet independent from, KNUST" (e-flux, Doreen Mende, 2026).
- Formally instituted in 2015 to reform the KNUST College of Art's curriculum (The State of the Arts); Brooklyn Rail describes a group of teachers and students "dissatisfied with the colonial legacy" of that curriculum.
- It grew from kąrî'kạchä seid'ou's Emancipatory Art Teaching project, begun at KNUST in 2003 (The State of the Arts); e-flux names seid'ou and Edwin Bodjawah as cofounders.
- Its name draws on Kwame Nkrumah's use of Marcus Garvey's idea of the Black Star (Brooklyn Rail, Jesse Weaver Shipley, 2026).
- Members include Ibrahim Mahama and Bernard Akoi-Jackson (Brooklyn Rail); e-flux also names Tracy Naa Koshie Thompson, Adjoa Daiki Apodey Kisser, Kwaku Boafo Kissiedu and Kwasi Ohene-Ayeh.
- Programme: end-of-year exhibitions in 2015 (two), 2016 and 2017, and "Spectacles. Speculations…" in 2018, curated by Ohene-Ayeh (The State of the Arts).
- Since 2020 it has run Kelas Bareng, an online joint class, with Gudskul (Indonesia), the Städelschule (Germany) and Nordland Kunst og filmhøgskole (Norway) (KNUST Department of Painting); it also runs Wintanworks (Winter plus Harmattan), a residency exchange planned for February, with Art Hub Copenhagen (Art Hub Copenhagen).
- Ranked No. 69 in ArtReview's 2025 Power 100, up from No. 81 (KNUST). In the 2026 Venice Biennale, "In Minor Keys", it presented "SLOW DOWN: CATTLE BATTLE AHEAD" at the Arsenale, with a video archive, a library, a Kente-weaving loom and a large map; programming changed twice a month (e-flux; Brooklyn Rail).
- Visiting: its exhibitions are free (The State of the Arts).

FUNDING
blaxTARLINES KUMASI: artist-run network based in a university art department, working through joint programmes with partner institutions
- Base: the collective is housed at KNUST's Department of Painting and Sculpture (KNUST), and seid'ou and Bodjawah live in the campus atelier houses (e-flux).
- Economy: e-flux describes value passed on rather than accumulated, "transforming art from commodity to gift"; Art Hub Copenhagen describes its work as a response to "the general lack of public funding for contemporary art practice in the region".
- Wintanworks partner: Art Hub Copenhagen, a non-profit initiated and founded by the Bikuben Foundation in 2018, with Novo Nordisk Foundation support for its research hosting (Art Hub Copenhagen).
- Kelas Bareng is shared between the four partner schools, each hosting sessions (KNUST Department of Painting).

BOTTOM LINE
The collective runs on a university base and partner institutions' programmes, with no sponsor or flagged funder on record. It publishes no accounts, so its own budget and the cost of its Venice presentation are open questions.
