DEPO ISTANBUL: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: depo-istanbul
Website: https://www.depoistanbul.net

OVERVIEW
- Non-commercial space for culture, arts and critical debate in a former tobacco warehouse ("Tütün Deposu") in Tophane, Istanbul, at Hacımimi Mah. Koltukçular Çıkmazı Sok. No:1, Beyoğlu (stated by the space).
- An initiative of Anadolu Kültür, a culture organisation founded in 2002 by people from the art world, the business world and civil society; Anadolu Kültür also runs the Diyarbakır Arts Center (stated by Anadolu Kültür).
- The building was used as a warehouse until the 1950s; from the 9th International Istanbul Biennial in 2005 it was used occasionally as an exhibition and project space (stated by the space).
- After a renovation that kept the original features, the first exhibition opened in January 2009; the annex was renovated in 2009 and has housed the independent radio station Açık Radyo since February 2012 (stated by the space).
- On site: exhibitions, screenings, discussions and workshops, including the SaturDox documentary screenings every other Saturday since 2009; Depo also publishes the online journal Red Thread.
- ArtReview (Kaya Genç, 16 January 2024) describes Depo as a refuge for critical voices in Istanbul since 2008.
- Visiting: open 11:00 to 19:00, closed Sundays and Mondays (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Depo Istanbul: grant-funded through its parent Anadolu Kültür: Civil Rights Defenders, Foundation for Arts Initiatives, Sigrid Rausing Trust and Stiftung Mercator
- Anadolu Kültür, which runs Depo, lists four funders and supporters on its site: Civil Rights Defenders, the Foundation for Arts Initiatives, the Sigrid Rausing Trust and Stiftung Mercator.
- The Sigrid Rausing Trust lists Anadolu Kültür under its Open Societies programme with a grant period of 2022 to 2026 and total previous grants of 250,000 pounds.
- In December 2018 Anadolu Kültür said its operations were transparent and published its 2013 budget and a list of all events it organised that year (Turkish Minute, 28 December 2018).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Kaya Genç (ArtReview, 16 January 2024) reports that Osman Kavala, who the Sigrid Rausing Trust says co-founded Anadolu Kültür in 2002 and remains its chair, was convicted in 2022 of trying to overthrow the government by financing the 2013 Gezi protests and received a whole life sentence. Bianet (Hikmet Adal and Melin Durmaz, 25 April 2022) reports that an Istanbul heavy penal court sentenced Kavala on 25 April 2022 to aggravated life imprisonment under Article 312 of the Turkish Penal Code, "attempting to overthrow the government", and acquitted him of espionage.
- On 16 November 2018 Istanbul police detained 13 people in dawn raids in Istanbul and three other provinces, in an investigation by the Istanbul prosecutor into Kavala over the 2013 Gezi Park protests (Human Rights Watch, 16 November 2018). Bianet (BIA News Desk, 16 November 2018) names Anadolu Kültür's general coordinator Asena Günal and its executive board deputy chair Yiğit Ekmekçi among those detained. One academic was arrested and the others were released (Turkish Minute, 28 December 2018).
- Unverified: President Erdoğan accused Kavala of being the domestic instigator of the Gezi protests, and pro-government media scrutinised the Open Society Foundation, from Turkish Minute, 28 December 2018; the foundation called the idea that organisations orchestrated and financed the protests "inconceivable".
- Depo is an initiative of Anadolu Kültür, so these actions by the Turkish state concern the organisation that runs it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Depo is grants to its parent Anadolu Kültür from four named foundations and rights groups, including the Sigrid Rausing Trust. The 2022 conviction of Anadolu Kültür's chair Osman Kavala and the 2018 police detentions of its staff and others are the context around the space.
