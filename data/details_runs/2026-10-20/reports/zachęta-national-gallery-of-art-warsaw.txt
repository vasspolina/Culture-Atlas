ZACHĘTA NATIONAL GALLERY OF ART: Warsaw, Poland
ID: zachęta-national-gallery-of-art-warsaw
Website: https://zacheta.art.pl/en (checked 2026-10-24; the gallery's own site loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- State contemporary art gallery at pl. Małachowskiego 3 in central Warsaw, showing Polish and foreign art of the 20th and 21st centuries, with a Project Room for young artists at Gałczyńskiego 3.
- The name comes from the Society for the Encouragement of Fine Arts (Towarzystwo Zachęty do Sztuk Pięknych), whose first meeting was on 13 December 1860. After the war the building housed the Central Bureau for Art Exhibitions from 1949; it became the Zachęta State Gallery and was renamed National Gallery of Art in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Stefan Szyller's neo-Renaissance design won an 1894 competition; the building opened in December 1900, was damaged in the 1944 Warsaw Uprising and was reconstructed in 1991 to 1993 (Wikipedia).
- On site: a collection of almost 3,700 works, exhibitions, the Project Room, education programmes, a library and archive, an art bookshop, and the organisation of Poland's Venice Biennale pavilion for over 70 years (stated by the gallery).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 12 to 8pm. Adults pay 30 zł, reduced 20 zł, students up to 26 pay 5 zł, ages 7 to 18 pay 2 zł, under-7s are free. Entry to the main gallery is free on Thursdays, and the Project Room is always free. Guided tours in Polish Sign Language are offered.

FUNDING
Zachęta National Gallery of Art: mainly public: Poland's Ministry of Culture and National Heritage, with ORLEN as named patron
- The gallery's partners page lists the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage as the body that financed the gallery. The subsidy's size was not found.
- ORLEN: listed as patron of the gallery on the gallery's partners page and on ORLEN's own sponsorship page ("The Gallery, whose patron is ORLEN, is a fully accessible and inclusive space"). Neither page gives a start year or an amount.
- Other partners named on the partners page: the Śródmieście District of the City of Warsaw (Project Room), Ingart (collection), IMM, BenQ (Project Room sponsor), Tubadzin and Grohe (bathroom renovation), Kultura Dostępna (co-financed the Art Encyclopedia in Polish Sign Language), the Museum of History of Photography in Cracow and the Gessel Foundation (scholarship programme). The page gives no dates or amounts.
- Annual reports are published, but the 2024 PDF could not be read as text, so revenue was not verified.

Red flags: ORLEN, a fossil-fuel patron
- ORLEN is a multinational oil refiner, petrol retailer and natural gas trader based in Płock; the Polish State Treasury holds 49.9% of its shares (Wikipedia, as of October 2026).
- ORLEN is named as the gallery's patron on the gallery's partners page and on ORLEN's own page (checked 24 October 2026). Neither page gives a start year or an amount, so how long it has paid and how much are not known.
- No reporting found of a protest or campaign aimed at ORLEN's patronage of Zachęta.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one piece found concerns the gallery's leadership and programme under director Janusz Janowski, ending with his dismissal on 22 December 2023. No criticism of Zachęta's funding or of ORLEN's patronage was found.
1. OKO.press: Roman Pawłowski-Felberg, article on the dismissal of Janusz Janowski (22 December 2023)
URL: https://oko.press/zacheta-dyrektor-odwolany-janusz-janowski
- Reports that on 22 December 2023 Hanna Wróblewska, a former Zachęta director and now head of the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage's national institutions department, delivered Janowski his dismissal letter; he did not sign it. The ministry's communiqué gave these reasons: he gave up European funds and a planned partnership with the Kunstmuseum in Trondheim, and curators did not take part in building the exhibition programme. In an interview it reports, Wróblewska also said he had not submitted the mandatory 2022 activity report.
- Reports that ten artists, including Wilhelm Sasnal and Leon Tarasewicz, protested the exhibition "Pejzaż malarstwa polskiego," curated by Janowski, and accused him of copyright infringement; per Gazeta Wyborcza, works by Sasnal and Tarasewicz were shown without the artists' consent. The piece gives the opening as November 2023 and, in a caption, 16 October 2023.
- Reports that Janowski's commission chose Ignacy Czwartos for the Polish pavilion at the Venice Biennale and that three jury members distanced themselves from the decision.
- Does not mention sponsors or ORLEN.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is financed by Poland's culture ministry, with ORLEN, an oil and gas company whose largest shareholder is the Polish state, as named patron. The weak points are the fossil-fuel patron with no published start year or amount, and the director's dismissal by the ministry on 22 December 2023. Watch whether the 2024 and 2025 financial statements show how much ORLEN pays.
