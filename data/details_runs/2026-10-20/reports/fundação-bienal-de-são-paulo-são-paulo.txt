FUNDAÇÃO BIENAL DE SÃO PAULO: São Paulo, Brazil
ID: fundação-bienal-de-são-paulo-são-paulo
Website: https://bienal.org.br (checked 2026-10-20; the record's website field points to a Times Brasil column instead)

OVERVIEW
- Private foundation that runs the São Paulo Bienal from the Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion in Ibirapuera Park, Moema (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- The first Bienal opened in 1951 under industrialist Ciccillo Matarazzo; the Museum of Modern Art of São Paulo ran it until the Fundação Bienal was created in 1962 (Wikipedia).
- The pavilion, designed by a team led by Oscar Niemeyer and Hélio Uchôa, has hosted the Bienal since 1957 and offers about 30,000 square metres of exhibition space (Wikipedia).
- Besides the Bienal it runs a library, a historical archive, travelling shows of each edition and Brazil's pavilion at the Venice Biennale (stated by the space).
- The 36th Bienal, curated by Bonaventure Soh Bejeng Ndikung, ran from 6 September 2025 to 11 January 2026 with 125 participants (Times Brasil); Amanda Carneiro and Raphael Fonseca lead the 37th (stated by the space).
- Admission to the Bienal is free (Times Brasil). No reporting found of the pavilion's accessibility provisions.

FUNDING
Fundação Bienal de São Paulo: mainly federal tax-incentive money: corporate sponsors through the Rouanet law
- 2025 income was R$72.35 million, with a surplus of R$4.2 million (audited financial statements).
- R$40.01 million came through PRONAC, the federal Rouanet tax-incentive programme, under a 2024 to 2027 plan approved at R$263.1 million (2025 accounts).
- São Paulo city pays an annual subsidy fixed by Law 11.630/1994 (R$4.78 million agreed in December 2025); state ProAC, city Pro-Mac, Funarte and Lei Paulo Gustavo grants were smaller (2025 accounts).
- Space rental brought R$7.63 million, sponsorship outside the tax incentives R$6.71 million (Bloomberg L.P., OSPC, Klabin and SESC), the Bienal dinner R$5.01 million and donations R$3.05 million, partly from consulates, embassies and the International Advisory Board (2025 accounts).
- 36th Bienal sponsors: strategic partner Itaú; master sponsors Bloomberg, Bradesco, Petrobras, Instituto Cultural Vale, Citi Brasil and Vivo; also Motiva, Open Society Foundations, Alupar, Iguatemi and Rolex; Royal Air Maroc as carrier (Times Brasil, September 2025).
- The 2026 travelling shows are sponsored by Petrobras (Recife, Salvador, Berlin, Vitória), Vale (Belém), Motiva (Salvador) and Lhoist (Belo Horizonte) (stated by the space).
- The board is chaired by Eduardo Saron; Andrea Pinheiro is president of the executive board; Frances Reynolds chairs the International Advisory Board (stated by the space).

Red flags: Petrobras oil money
- Petrobras, a majority state-owned company in the petroleum industry (Wikipedia), was a master sponsor of the 36th Bienal and sponsors four of its 2026 travelling shows (Times Brasil; stated by the space).

Red flags: Vale and the Brumadinho dam collapse
- Instituto Cultural Vale was a master sponsor in 2025 and Vale sponsors the 2026 Belém show.
- Vale's Córrego do Feijão tailings dam at Brumadinho failed on 25 January 2019, killing 270 people; IBAMA fined Vale R$250 million a day later, and in January 2020 prosecutors charged Vale, the auditor TÜV SÜD and 16 individuals with intentional homicide and environmental crimes (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2014 artists' letter over Israeli state funding
- In 2014, 61 participating artists objected to Israeli consulate funding, about $40,000 of a $10.5 million budget according to Agence France-Presse; the curators issued a statement of support, and the foundation agreed to "clearly disassociate" it from the general sponsorship (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is mostly of exhibitions, not of the foundation's funding; the 2014 sponsorship protest is the one case aimed at its money.
1. Hyperallergic: Mostafa Heddaya, "São Paulo Biennial Removes General Israeli Sponsorship" (2014)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/sao-paulo-biennial-removes-general-israeli-sponsorship
- Reports the artists' letter, which refused to "support the normalization of Israel's ongoing occupation", and the foundation's decision to show the consulate's logo only beside the three Israeli artists it funded.
- Names the Fundação Bienal and its sponsorship directly.
2. e-flux Criticism: Chris McCormack, "36th Bienal de São Paulo, 'Not All Travellers Walk Roads: Of Humanity as Practice'" (2025)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782314/36th-bienal-de-s-o-paulo-not-all-travellers-walk-roads-of-humanity-as-practice
- Says the curator's disclaimer that the show is not about identity politics can look "politically cautious, if not reactionary", and calls the lack of wall texts "a curatorial overstep".
- Argues the show "too readily ignored its political moment", addressed Gaza only indirectly, and emphasised curators' interests over those of artists; reports that some South American journalists found it short of Brazilians.
- Criticises the exhibition, not the funding; does not discuss sponsors.

BOTTOM LINE
The Bienal runs mostly on federal tax money routed through sponsors under the Rouanet law, plus a city subsidy and rental income. The weak points are two of its corporate sponsors, Petrobras and Vale, both still current. Worth watching: whether artists contest those sponsors as they contested Israeli funding in 2014.
