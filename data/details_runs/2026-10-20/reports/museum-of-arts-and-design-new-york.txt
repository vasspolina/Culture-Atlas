MUSEUM OF ARTS AND DESIGN: New York, USA
ID: museum-of-arts-and-design-new-york
Website: https://madmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary craft, art and design at 2 Columbus Circle, New York (museum website).
- Opened in 1956 as the Museum of Contemporary Crafts, with the philanthropist Aileen Osborn Webb a key force behind it. It became the American Craft Museum in 1979 and took the name Museum of Arts and Design in 2002 (Wikipedia).
- In 2002 it won a bid to buy the building at 2 Columbus Circle. The architect Brad Cloepfil of Allied Works Architecture redesigned it as the Jerome and Simona Chazen Building with a glazed terra-cotta facade, giving more than 54,000 square feet, over three times the former space. Changes to the Edward Durell Stone building sparked a preservation debate (Wikipedia).
- The museum had eleven directors between 2013 and 2021, six of them interim. Glenn Adamson was appointed in 2013 and Timothy R. Rodgers became director in 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday and closed Mondays: 10 am to 6 pm except Wednesday, noon to 8 pm. General admission is 20 dollars, seniors 16 dollars, students 14 dollars; members and children 12 and under are free, and admission is pay what you wish from 5 to 8 pm on the first Wednesday of each month. Elevators serve all floors, with an automated entrance door and free manual wheelchairs (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum of Arts and Design: private nonprofit: donors, with New York State and City arts agencies
- Scale: in fiscal year 2024 the museum reported revenue of 8,750,723 dollars and expenses of 12,781,781 dollars, a net loss of about 4.03 million dollars, with net assets of 92,195,673 dollars. Contributions were about 59.7 percent of revenue. Revenue was 22.57 million dollars in 2019, when contributions were about 19.1 million dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, from Form 990 data).
- Public money: its exhibition "Haas Brothers: Uncanny Valley" (11 April to 16 August 2026) is made possible by the New York State Council on the Arts, with support from the Office of the Governor and the State Legislature, and supported in part by public funds from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs in partnership with the City Council. The credit also names Sarah Arison and Suzi Cordish as additional support (museum website).
- Pay: Executive Director Timothy Rodgers received 406,915 dollars in compensation in fiscal year 2024 (ProPublica).
- Published documents: Forms 990 for 2017 and 2020, audited statements for 2013 and 2014 and for 2015 through 2019, and annual reports for 2008 and 2009 are posted on the museum's site (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece of art-press reporting, on a lawsuit by the museum's former chief financial officer. It concerns management, not the art or the funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Benjamin Sutton, report on the lawsuit by the former chief financial officer (26 March 2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/26/museum-arts-and-design-lawsuit-director-expenses-whistleblower
- Denise Lewis, the museum's chief financial officer from 2017 until she was fired in January 2024, sued the museum on 22 March 2024 (a lawsuit, not a finding).
- Unverified: Lewis alleges that the director Timothy Rodgers sought museum funds for personal expenses from an autumn 2023 vacation in Mexico, including a 600 dollar rug charged to the museum's American Express card, and for moving costs to a second home in Connecticut, and that the museum paid all of his husband's healthcare costs, which it does not do for other employees.
- Unverified: Lewis alleges that the museum lacked the whistleblower policy that New York law requires for nonprofits of its size, and that Rodgers pressured her to resign and fired her when she refused. She seeks damages including the earnings she would have received.
- The museum's response: a spokesperson said, "These allegations are untrue. We look forward to making our case in court." Rodgers is not a party to the suit.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private donations plus New York State and City arts agency support, with net assets of 92.2 million dollars at the end of fiscal year 2024 (ProPublica, Form 990 data). The former chief financial officer's March 2024 lawsuit over the director's expenses contains allegations the museum denies.
