NATIONAL ARCHAEOLOGICAL MUSEUM: Athens, Greece
ID: national-archaeological-museum-athens
Website: https://www.namuseum.gr/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Greece's national museum of antiquities at 44, 28th of October (Patission) Street in central Athens.
- Founded in 1829 on Aegina as the first museum of the Greek state, and moved after Athens became the capital in 1834 (stated by the space).
- Governor Ioannis Kapodistrias established it and it took its present name in 1881 (Wikipedia).
- Work on the present building began in 1866 and it opened in 1889, to designs by L. Lange with a façade altered by E. Ziller, on land given by E. Tositsa and with money from the Bernardakis family of St Petersburg (stated by the space).
- On site: permanent and temporary exhibition halls, conservation laboratories, a photographic archive, a library and a lecture theatre.
- Open Wednesday to Monday 08:30 to 15:30 and Tuesday 13:00 to 20:00 from 16 November 2026 to 30 April 2027. Admission is EUR 20, free for EU visitors up to 25 and for all on the first and third Sunday from November to March.

FUNDING
National Archaeological Museum: public: the Greek state, with a EUR 40 million private gift for the extension studies
- The museum is a state body: two directors of the Hellenic Ministry of Culture, Konstantinos Frisiras and Irene Komninou, sit on its board (museum administration page).
- Under law 5021/2023, passed in February 2023, the museum keeps receiving state funds and is responsible for its own fundraising (Pagenews, 14 April 2023).
- Spyros and Dorothy Latsis gave a EUR 40 million sponsorship in memory of Yannis and Erietta Latsis. Parliament ratified the agreement in April 2024 and it covers the studies for the extension, not the building cost (To Vima, 16 October 2026).
- David Chipperfield Architects, with Tombazis and Associates, won the 2023 competition for the extension. To Vima reported that no final construction budget had been announced.
- The museum had 665,414 visitors in 2025 and 642,956 in 2024 (Greek Trip Planner, citing Ministry of Culture data).

Red flags: protest closure over the 2023 museums law
- On 13 February 2023 the museum and four other large state museums were shut as Greek archaeologists protested against the law that put them under a ministry-appointed general director and board (The Greek Herald, 14 February 2023).
- The employees of all five museums wrote to Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis against the plan, and in December 2020 the Association of Greek Archaeologists declared its "absolute opposition" to the draft law (The Art Newspaper, 4 March 2021).

Red flags: refinery holdings behind the extension's donor
- Paneuropean Oil and Industrial Holdings holds 40.41 per cent of HELLENiQ ENERGY, which runs three Greek refineries with a combined capacity of 342,000 barrels a day (HELLENiQ ENERGY corporate profile).
- Naftemporiki's headline of 6 July 2023 names the Latsis Group as the seller in a planned sale of part of that stake, and its text calls Paneuropean the largest shareholder of the company.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One art-press report covers the archaeologists' opposition to the restructuring of the museum. It criticises the ministry's law, not the museum's programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: William Summerfield, "Archaeologists appeal to Greek prime minister to halt restructuring of five big antiquities museums" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/03/04/archaeologists-appeal-to-greek-prime-minister-to-halt-restructuring-of-five-big-antiquities-museums
- Reports that 119 former employees of the archaeological service signed an appeal that denounces "archaeocapitalism".
- Says the conversion would reduce state support and make the museums raise more of their own income from donations, sponsorship, ticketing and events.
- Names the National Archaeological Museum as one of the five museums the bill covers.

BOTTOM LINE
The state runs and pays for the museum, and that money is not a flag. The weak points are the 2023 protest closure and the Latsis family, the one private name, whose holding company is the largest shareholder of Greece's main refiner. Worth watching: who pays for the construction of the extension, which local media put at about EUR 300 million.
