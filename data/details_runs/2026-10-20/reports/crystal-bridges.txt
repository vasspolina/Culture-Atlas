CRYSTAL BRIDGES: Bentonville, USA
ID: crystal-bridges
Website: https://crystalbridges.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of American art at 600 Museum Way, Bentonville, Arkansas, on a 134-acre park linked to downtown Bentonville by five miles of sculpture and walking trails (stated by the museum).
- Founded by Alice Walton as a public non-profit and opened on 11 November 2011; its satellite, The Momentary, a contemporary art space in downtown Bentonville, opened on 22 February 2020 (stated by the museum).
- Named for a nearby natural spring and the bridge construction in the building (stated by the museum).
- Building by architect Moshe Safdie: pavilions around two spring-fed ponds; Wikipedia describes eight interconnected pavilions of about 200,000 sq ft in an Ozark ravine.
- On site: a permanent collection spanning five centuries of American art, temporary exhibitions and the trails.
- Visiting: general admission is free and the permanent collection is always free to view. Galleries are open Monday and Wednesday 11 a.m. to 6 p.m., Thursday and Friday 11 a.m. to 8 p.m., Saturday and Sunday 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.; closed Tuesday, Thanksgiving and Christmas. The front entrance is ADA-accessible with a drop-off area.

FUNDING
Crystal Bridges: family-endowed: the Walton Family Foundation, with Walmart and Walmart Foundation grants for free admission
- 2011 endowment: the Walton Family Foundation made three endowments totalling $800 million, announced on 4 May 2011: $350 million for operations, $325 million for acquisitions and $125 million for capital improvements (Talk Business, The City Wire staff).
- Free admission: Walmart Inc. gave $20 million in 2011 and $3 million in 2017 for free admission, and on 31 July 2023 the museum announced a $7 million Walmart Foundation grant that keeps general admission free through at least 2028 and also funds operations at The Momentary (Talk Business, 20 August 2023).
- Accounts for the year ending 31 December 2024: revenue $131,160,398, of which contributions $37,157,098; expenses $96,110,839; total assets $2,054,888,528 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism dates mostly from the 2011 opening and concerns the Walmart money and the way the collection was acquired.
1. Artlyst: News Desk, "Walmart Gallery In Cross Fire Controversy" (2011)
URL: https://artlyst.com/?p=173533
- Critics saw hypocrisy in a $1.4 billion museum opening while Walmart cut workers' benefits; protesters voiced solidarity with Occupy Wall Street.
- Some figures in the art world argued its collecting inflated prices and drew masterpieces from financially struggling institutions.
- Curator David Houston defended the museum, saying it set few market records for the works it bought.
2. American Alliance of Museums, Museum magazine: Michelle Bradford, "Water Under the Bridge: Crystal Bridges Defies Critics, Wins Hearts" (2012)
URL: https://www.aam-us.org/2012/07/01/water-under-the-bridge-crystal-bridges-defies-critics-wins-hearts/
- The New York Times called it "the art museum that Walmart built"; critics argued wealth alone does not qualify Walton to build an art museum.
- Walton's advisers pursued acquisitions aggressively, which angered some when they sought art with ties to the region or targeted cash-strapped institutions. A 2007 bid of $68 million with the National Gallery of Art for Thomas Eakins's "The Gross Clinic" lost to a Philadelphia fundraising drive.
- Buying Asher B. Durand's "Kindred Spirits" from the New York Public Library provoked protests from people who wanted it kept in New York.
- Tennessee's attorney general tried to stop the purchase of a stake in a collection from the financially troubled Fisk University.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Walmart's treatment of workers: on 20 November 2011 Artlyst reported that many saw hypocrisy in Alice Walton's $1.4 billion spend on the museum while Walmart was cutting back workers' benefits. A spokesman for the union-affiliated MakingChange@Walmart said opening "a huge, opulent museum in the middle of nowhere while the company is cutting health insurance for its employees is troubling" and "sends the message Wal-Mart doesn't care about them". Artlyst says protesters voiced solidarity with Occupy Wall Street.
- Wikipedia's Walmart article describes "strong anti-union policies" at the company and says it does not employ unionised labour. It reports that in November 2013 the National Labor Relations Board found that Walmart had pressured workers in 13 states to avoid Black Friday strikes and had illegally disciplined workers who struck.
- Connection to the museum: Wikipedia describes Alice Walton, the museum's founder, as the daughter of Walmart founder Sam Walton, and Walmart Inc. and the Walmart Foundation have given grants for free admission (Talk Business).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Crystal Bridges is the Walton family: an $800 million endowment from the Walton Family Foundation in 2011, plus Walmart and Walmart Foundation grants that pay for free admission through 2028. Criticism in 2011 and 2012 concerned Walmart's treatment of workers set against the museum's cost, and how the collection was assembled.
