GWANGJU BIENNALE: Gwangju, South Korea
ID: gwangju-biennale-gwangju
Website: https://www.gwangjubiennale.org/ (checked 2026-10-20; the record lists an ArtAsiaPacific article as the website)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale held in the Gwangju Biennale Exhibition Hall on Biennale-ro in Buk-gu, Gwangju (e-flux, stated by the space).
- Founded in September 1995; hosted by the Gwangju Biennale Foundation and the city of Gwangju, which also runs the Gwangju Design Biennale, founded in 2004 (Wikipedia).
- The Foundation's president is Youn Bum-mo (stated by the space, The Korea Times).
- The 16th edition, "You Must Change Your Life", directed by Ho Tzu Nyen with Che Kyongfa, Gahee Park and Brian Kuan Wood, shows 43 artists and groups in a single building, alongside national and institutional pavilions (e-flux, ArtReview).
- The 16th edition runs from 5 September to 15 November 2026; no reporting found of ticket prices or accessibility details for this edition.

FUNDING
Gwangju Biennale: mainly public: the city of Gwangju through its foundation, with corporate sponsors
- The Foundation and the city host the event (Wikipedia). The Korea Times reports more than 10 billion won in public funding and related spending committed to each edition, and a 2026 operating budget of about 14.3 billion won, excluding a separate 1 billion won for basic assets.
- Sponsorship tiers: premier from 200 million won, gold from 100 million won, silver under 100 million won; Kia, Samsung and Kolon have run promotional halls inside the exhibition building (stated by the space). No current sponsor list or amounts are published.
- The Foundation publishes monthly business-promotion expense reports and its regulations on its management page (stated by the space).
- The Park Seo-Bo Art Prize, launched in February 2022 through the painter Park Seo-Bo's GIZI Foundation, was to pay USD 100,000 per edition, USD 1 million over ten editions from 2023 to 2042 (ArtAsiaPacific).

Red flags: Park Seo-Bo Art Prize dropped after protest (2023)
- At the 2023 edition, members of the public objected that honouring Park ran against the biennale's "political spirit", calling his formalist work disconnected from socio-political realities (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Protesters handed out leaflets proposing a prize named after Oh Yoon, a printmaker who responded to the 1980 Gwangju Uprising and massacre (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Oum Jeongsoon won the only prize; on 10 May 2023 Park said he had agreed with the Foundation to abolish it (ArtAsiaPacific).

Red flags: China Pavilion boycott over the Taiwan Pavilion (2026)
- The Foundation renamed the National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts' space from "Taiwan Pavilion" to "NTMoFA Pavilion", then restored the original name on 25 August 2026 after Taiwan objected (Korea JoongAng Daily).
- On 3 September 2026 the Chinese Pavilion withdrew; the Chinese embassy in Seoul said the name was "directly damaging the political foundation of China-South Korea relations" (ArtReview).
- Jeonnam-Gwangju and the Foundation reaffirmed respect for the "one China" position and apologised for procedural shortcomings; protesters rallied at the 4 September press opening (Korea JoongAng Daily, The Korea Times).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the Foundation's handling of the Park Seo-Bo prize and the 2026 pavilion dispute rather than on its art.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Tong Tung Yeng, "Protests at Gwangju Biennale End Newly Established Park Seo-Bo Art Prize" (2023)
URL: https://www.artasiapacific.com/news/protests-at-gwangju-biennale-end-newly-established-park-seo-bo-art-prize/
- Reports objections that a prize from a formalist painter clashed with a biennale rooted in political memory.
- Notes the Foundation gave little comment and announced the end of the prize alongside Nicolas Bourriaud's appointment as curator of the 2025 edition.
2. The Korea Times: Lee Hae-rin, "Taiwan-China row at Gwangju Biennale could have been avoided" (2026)
URL: https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/lifestyle/arts-theater/20260912/taiwan-china-row-at-gwangju-biennale-could-have-been-avoided
- Argues the Foundation was poorly prepared to foresee the diplomatic consequences of its decisions.
- Unverified: a foundation employee, quoted by The Korea Times, said core staff number about 30 with the rest on short-term contracts; the paper adds that the pavilion department lost five of its six contract workers last year.
- Links the row to the 2023 cancellation of the Park Seo-Bo prize.
3. ArtReview: "Chinese Pavilion to boycott Gwangju Biennale" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/chinese-pavilion-to-boycott-gwangju-biennale/
- News report; records the Chinese curators' charge that the organisers let politics intrude on art, and Seoul's statement that its Taiwan policy had not changed.

BOTTOM LINE
The biennale is a city-backed public foundation; its sponsors and their amounts are not published. The weak points are governance: a donor prize withdrawn under protest in 2023 and a 2026 pavilion naming dispute that ended in a boycott and an apology. Worth watching: whether the Foundation publishes its budget and overhauls the pavilion system, as its president said it must.
