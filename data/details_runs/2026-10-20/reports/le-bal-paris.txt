LE BAL: Paris, France
ID: le-bal-paris
Website: https://www.le-bal.fr (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Independent exhibition space for documentary photography, video, cinema and new media, at 6 Impasse de la Défense in the 18th arrondissement, off Place de Clichy (Wikipedia).
- Co-founded by photographer Raymond Depardon and Diane Dufour, its director; it opened in September 2010 (Art Newspaper France, Wikipedia).
- Housed in Chez Isis, a dance hall of the 1920s that became France's largest betting office after the Second World War and fell into disrepair in the 1990s; the City of Paris bought it and made it available to the Association des Amis de Magnum (stated by the space).
- Renovation by Agence Search (Caroline Barat and Thomas Dubuisson); about 300 m2 of exhibition space, with an Art Deco ceiling kept in the front exhibition room (stated by the space).
- On site: exhibitions with a cinema programme, a university seminar, a publishing arm, the BAL Books bookshop, a café, and La Fabrique du Regard, an education programme that trains young people in and through images.
- Open Wednesday 12:00 to 20:00, Thursday and Friday 12:00 to 19:00, weekends 11:00 to 19:00. Admission is 8 euros, 6 reduced; free for under-12s, the unemployed, press, Friends of Le Bal, and disabled visitors with a companion. The accessibility page gives no details.

FUNDING
Le Bal: mixed: city, state and region grants plus foundations and a patrons' circle, with a corporate foundation of an energy-distribution group among the backers
- Dufour said in 2021 that the budget was about 2 million euros: 20% own resources, 40% public funds (city, state, region) and 40% private funds (foundations and the Cercle des amis du BAL) (Art Newspaper France). The same interview says the City of Paris gave an exceptional subsidy that year.
- Main partner: the City of Paris, which has supported the venue since its origin. Public partners: the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Culture (DRAC Île-de-France), Région Île-de-France (since 2009), the Préfecture de Paris et d'Île-de-France (since 2010), the CNAP and the mairie of the 18th arrondissement. No amounts or years of grants are posted (stated by the space).
- Patrons listed: ADAGP, Alter Equity, Artworkers Retirement Society, Fondation BIC, Fondation de France, Fondation Hermès, Fondation pour la diffusion des arts visuels, Fondation Linklaters, Rubis Mécénat and Le Cercle des amis mécènes du BAL. No amounts are posted.
- Fondation Linklaters, the corporate foundation of Linklaters, announced a partnership in February 2016 for the "Mon Journal du Monde" workshops of La Fabrique du Regard; no amount was stated (Le Monde du Droit).
- No reporting found of audited accounts or a board list on the space's site.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Rubis Mécénat
- Rubis Mécénat is an endowment fund created by the Rubis group in 2011 that runs artistic and social projects (its own site). The Rubis group's site lists it under "Our Group".
- The Rubis group's Energy Distribution business line sells road, aviation, industrial and marine fuels, liquefied gases and bitumen; it markets fuels in 23 countries, mainly in Africa and the Caribbean (Rubis).
- Le Bal's partners page lists Rubis Mécénat without an amount or a year.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of Le Bal as an institution was found. Coverage is mostly favourable, and the one critical point found concerns the selection in a single show.
1. Foto8: Max Houghton, review of "Anonymes" (2010)
URL: https://www.foto8.com/live/paris-photo-and-beyond/
- Praises the curatorial choices of the opening show, in particular the first floor.
- Questions whether the Detroit: Self Portrait section fits the theme as well as the rest of the show.
- Criticises one part of the programme, not the institution or its funders.
2. Art Newspaper France: Natacha Wolinski, interview with Diane Dufour, "Nous voulons un lieu ouvert à la confrontation et au pas de côté" (2021)
URL: https://artnewspaper.fr/2021/01/15/diane-dufour-nous-voulons-un-lieu-ouvert-a-la-confrontation-et-au-pas-de-cote
- Not criticism: Dufour answers the charge that Le Bal shows few women artists by listing women it has shown, including Sharon Lockhart and Sophie Ristelhueber.
- She says the venue is not an activist space and that political correctness and cancel culture endanger art.
- No question about its funders is put to her.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from the City of Paris, the state and the region carries the space, with foundations and a patrons' circle making up about 40% of a 2 million euro budget in 2021. The weak point is Rubis Mécénat among the patrons: an endowment fund tied to a group selling fuels, with no amount or year posted. Watch the partners page, which carries no dates, and whether accounts are published.
