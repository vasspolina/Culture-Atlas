Z33: Hasselt, Belgium
ID: z33-hasselt
Website: http://z33.be/

OVERVIEW
- House for contemporary art, design and architecture at Bonnefantenstraat 1, Hasselt (Z33).
- Evolved out of its predecessor PCBK in 2002; it became an independent non-profit organisation on 1 January 2018, taking over former provincial tasks and the work of four smaller organisations (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia says Z33 was housed in the former beguinage in Hasselt; it moved to Bonnefantenstraat 1 in January 2020 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the existing exhibition building Vleugel 58, designed by Gustaaf Daniëls, and the new Vleugel 19, designed by the Italian architect Francesca Torzo, form a 4,600 m2 exhibition space; the project was budgeted at 8.5 million euros (Wikipedia). Vleugel 19 has diamond-shaped bricks that echo Vleugel 58 and the neighbouring begijnhof, and sits between the Jenevermuseum and the begijnhof site (Z33).
- On site: exhibitions, a shop and a cafe (Z33). The Paardsdemer chapel is built into the front facade; VRT NWS reported on 31 May 2024 that it would open permanently with a mural by Amber Andrews (VRT NWS, Lieven Roux).
- Programmes: the talent project FORMAT and the public art project Art on the Meuse; Wikipedia says Z33 brought Manifesta 9 to Flanders in 2012, curated the Ljubljana Design Biennale in 2014 and the Istanbul Biennial in 2018, and draws about 30,000 visitors a year.
- Visiting: closed Monday; Tuesday for booked groups; Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00; Nocturne on the second Thursday of each month, 18:00 to 22:00. Adults pay 12 euros; 6 euros for ages 18 to 26; free for under-18s, Friends of Z33, ICOM members and Museumpas or Haspas holders. Exhibitions, restrooms and the cafe are accessible to people with reduced mobility.

FUNDING
Z33: public: the Flemish Community under the Arts decree since 2006 and the Province of Limburg (Wikipedia), with the Nationale Loterij among its partners
- Wikipedia states that the Flemish Community has supported Z33 structurally under the Arts decree since 2006, and that the Province of Limburg also supported it.
- The Partners section of Z33's organisation page links to Vlaanderen, the city of Hasselt, the Nationale Loterij and the newspaper De Standaard.
- The Paardsdemer chapel project, a gift from Z33 to the city of Hasselt costing 200,000 euros, was funded by the Nationale Loterij and Z33 itself (VRT NWS, Lieven Roux, 31 May 2024).
- VRT NWS reported that Z33 was told some years before 2025 that it would receive fewer subsidies, after which several employees were dismissed (VRT NWS, 3 April 2025).
- Late in 2024 Gwendolyn Rutten (Open VLD, Flemish Parliament) raised concerns about Z33's financial situation in parliament; Culture Minister Caroline Gennez (Vooruit) said her department would look into it (VRT NWS, Yente Van der Sande, 4 February 2025).
- On 4 February 2025 the Limburg prosecutor's office confirmed that the Federal Judicial Police had opened an investigation into possible financial irregularities at Z33 after a complaint (VRT NWS, Yente Van der Sande, "Gerechtelijk onderzoek gestart naar mogelijk financieel wanbeleid bij kunstenhuis Z33 in Hasselt").
- On 3 April 2025 the Limburg prosecutor's office said that after a thorough investigation there were no indications of breach of trust and the case was closed. Board chair An Moons said the board had full confidence in director Adinda Van Geystelen and the staff (VRT NWS, Khalid Sellam and Christine Peeters, "Onderzoek naar financiële onregelmatigheden bij Z33 in Hasselt afgesloten: 'Duidelijkheid komt geen dag te vroeg'").

BOTTOM LINE
Z33 receives structural money from the Flemish Community under the Arts decree since 2006 and received support from the Province of Limburg (Wikipedia); its organisation page lists Vlaanderen, the city of Hasselt, the Nationale Loterij and De Standaard as partners. A Federal Judicial Police investigation into its finances, reported on 4 February 2025, was closed on 3 April 2025 with no indications of breach of trust.
