THE PHILLIPS COLLECTION: Washington, USA
ID: the-phillips-collection-washington
Website: https://www.phillipscollection.org (checked 2026-10-24; /visit, /history and /corporate-partnerships opened)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1600 21st Street NW in Washington's Dupont Circle neighbourhood, holding nearly 3,000 works, mainly Impressionist and modern art (Wikipedia). Renoir's "Luncheon of the Boating Party" is its best-known piece.
- Founded by Duncan Phillips and his mother in late 1918 as the Phillips Memorial Art Gallery; it opened to the public in fall 1921 with 237 works in a room added to the family home (the museum). Duncan's wife, Marjorie Acker Phillips, co-founded it (Wikipedia).
- Founded as a memorial to Duncan's father, Major Duncan Clinch Phillips (died 1917), and his brother James (died in the 1918 flu epidemic) (the museum).
- The 1897 Georgian Revival family house, the Goh Annex (1989 renovation of a 1960 wing) and the Sant Building (2006 expansion) (Wikipedia). The family moved out in 1930 and the whole house became museum space.
- On site: galleries including the Rothko Room, Sunday Concerts since 1941, education programmes and a satellite at THEARC in Southeast DC since 2018 (Wikipedia, the museum).
- Open Tuesday to Saturday 10am to 5pm, Sunday 11am to 5pm; closed Monday. Adults $20, seniors 62+ $15, military $12, students and educators $10, under 18 and members free; free 5 to 8pm on Third Thursdays. No accessibility details on the page opened.

FUNDING
The Phillips Collection: mainly private: Phillips family wealth, donors, corporate sponsors and the sale of artworks
- Duncan Phillips's father was a Pittsburgh window glass millionaire; his maternal grandfather, James Laughlin, was a banker who co-founded the Jones and Laughlin Steel Company (Wikipedia).
- Form 990, fiscal year to July 2025 (ProPublica): revenue $26.6M, expenses $17.4M, contributions $10.0M, total assets $131.8M. FY2024 revenue was $13.7M against $17.3M expenses.
- Corporate partners page: Altria Group (presenting sponsor of "Frank Stewart's Nexus", benefactor sponsor of the 2023 gala, and exhibition sponsor for more than 30 years), Bloomberg (2024 gala sponsor, supporter of the gala for nearly 20 years, and the Bloomberg Connects guide), Bank of America (conservation of two Cezanne works), Northern Trust (2025 gala sponsor). Community partners include ArentFox Schiff, Chevy Chase Trust, DLR Group, GRF CPAs, JM Zell Partners, Pathstone, S&P Global and WC Smith. In-kind: Maggio + Kattar and WilmerHale. No amounts are given.
- Sales: in November 2025 the museum sold three works at Sotheby's and planned to sell seven more, to fund new commissions, acquisitions and collection care (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: Altria, tobacco sponsor
- Altria sponsors exhibitions at the museum and has done so for more than 30 years, per the museum's own partners page.
- A 2019 Guardian report on tobacco money in US arts groups named the Newseum, the Kennedy Center and Alvin Ailey as Altria recipients, and does not name the Phillips (PressReader copy; excerpt read).
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or campaign aimed at Altria's sponsorship at the Phillips.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One recent piece of direct criticism, about the 2025 sale of artworks. No art-press criticism of the tobacco or Bloomberg sponsorship found.
1. Hyperallergic: Isa Farfan, "Why Is DC's Phillips Collection Selling Off Its Masterpieces?" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1058320/why-is-dc-phillips-collection-selling-off-its-masterpieces/
- Reports opposition to selling O'Keeffe, Seurat and Dove works at Sotheby's on 20 November (combined estimate up to $14.8M) and seven more works.
- Liza Phillips, granddaughter of the founders, said "We treasure those pieces"; Eric Gibson of the Wall Street Journal called the sale "reckless" and said the museum should have run a fundraising campaign.
- The museum says the trustees chose the works in line with AAM and AAMD standards and an 11-person members group approved; it reported a $3.6M net loss the previous year.
- Does not mention Altria or Bloomberg.

BOTTOM LINE
The base is Phillips family wealth from glass, steel and banking, plus donors and sales of art. The weak points are the long Altria sponsorship, Bloomberg's gala backing, and the 2025 sale of core works to raise money. Watch the second auction of seven works and the sponsor lists.
