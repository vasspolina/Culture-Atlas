OGDEN MUSEUM: New Orleans, USA
ID: ogden-museum
Website: https://ogdenmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Southern art at 925 Camp Street, New Orleans; the Ogden Museum of Southern Art (Wikipedia).
- Founded around the private collection of New Orleans businessman Roger H. Ogden, whose founding donation was more than 600 works; the collection now holds more than 4,000 works (Wikipedia). The museum opened in August 2003 (Christian Science Monitor, Eliza Strickland, "Southern art gains a home", 5 November 2003).
- Two buildings: Stephen Goldring Hall, completed in 2003 and designed by Errol Barron and Michael Toups, and the Patrick F. Taylor Library, built in 1889 and designed by Henry Hobson Richardson (Wikipedia).
- The unaffiliated Confederate Museum sits between the two Ogden buildings after a court battle over its ownership, with a tunnel planned beneath its hall to connect the two wings (Christian Science Monitor, 2003).
- Visiting: open daily, 10:00 to 17:00, last admission 16:45. Thursday admission, 10:00 to 17:00, is free to Louisiana residents (museum website).

FUNDING
Ogden Museum: private: the founder's collection, the Helis Foundation, membership and donors
- Founder Roger Ogden donated more than 600 works to start the collection (Wikipedia). In 2004 museum officials said Ogden had donated more than 1,200 of roughly 2,700 works (ArtsJournal).
- The Helis Foundation pays for free Thursday admission for Louisiana residents through its Art for All programme and presents the Poydras Corridor Sculpture Exhibition (museum website). It presented the exhibition Basquiat and the Bayou at the Ogden from 25 October 2014 to 25 January 2015 (Grantmakers in the Arts, 1 June 2018).
- Pelican Bomb: "In 2015, the Ogden took over $380,000 from Helis, 10% of the museum's total revenue for that year" (Ann Hackett, 18 May 2018).
- The New Orleans Chapter of The Links, Incorporated presents the 13th Annual HBCU Art Showcase (museum website).
- Membership runs from $60 for individuals to $10,000 and up for the Chairman's Circle (museum website).
- In 2018 the museum was selected for a Bloomberg Philanthropies grant under its Arts Innovation and Management Program (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Helis family)
- The Helis Foundation is a Louisiana private foundation "established and funded by the William Helis Family in 1955". Its Art Funds were created from bequests by Diana Helis Henry and Adrienne Helis Malvin and funded from their personal resources (Helis Foundation website).
- Pelican Bomb writes: "The Helis Foundation, for example, is funded by the family who started the Helis Oil & Gas Energy Project", and calls William Helis "an old-school oil baron" who made a fortune drilling Louisiana oil in the 1930s (Ann Hackett, 18 May 2018).
- William G. Helis Sr. was a Greek immigrant who made a fortune in the oil business and owned racetracks and Thoroughbreds (Wikipedia).
- The foundation pays today for free Thursdays and the Poydras Corridor exhibition at the Ogden (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two items: an essay on oil-linked funding of New Orleans cultural institutions, and a 2004 news report on a lawsuit against the founder.
1. Pelican Bomb: Ann Hackett, "In the Slick of It: Art and the Oil Industry in New Orleans" (18 May 2018)
URL: http://pelicanbomb.com/art-review/2018/in-the-slick-of-it-art-and-the-oil-industry-in-new-orleans
- Argues that New Orleans cultural institutions depend heavily on money from oil and gas companies and the foundations they fund, and that this sponsorship builds the industry's public goodwill.
- Names the Ogden: it took over $380,000 from Helis in 2015, 10 percent of its revenue that year, and the Helis Foundation sponsors the Ogden's "Louisiana Contemporary" exhibition.
2. ArtsJournal: Douglas McLennan, "Lawsuit Filed Against New Orleans Museum" (11 May 2004)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=25923
- A $1.9 million suit claimed Ogden used Louisiana taxpayer money to build a private museum on public land, got around the state's conflict-of-interest laws, and named the museum after himself.
- The suit also alleged he uses his relationship with the museum for personal financial gain. Museum officials said Ogden donated more than 1,200 of its roughly 2,700 works.
- Refers to "New Orleans' new museum of Southern art" and to the founder as "Ogden"; it does not give the museum's name.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private: the founder's collection, membership and donors, with named programme funding from the Helis Foundation, which the Helis family established and funded in 1955 from a fortune built in oil. The Helis Foundation pays today for free Thursdays and the Poydras Corridor exhibition, and Pelican Bomb put its 2015 giving at over $380,000, 10 percent of the museum's revenue.
