SERENDIPITY ARTS FESTIVAL: Panaji, India
ID: serendipity-arts-festival
Website: https://www.serendipityartsfestival.com (checked 2026-10-24; live, showing the 2026 edition, 13-20 December, Panjim, Goa)

OVERVIEW
- Annual multi-discipline arts festival held over about a week in late December in Panaji (Panjim), Goa, using heritage buildings and other venues across the city.
- Founded in 2016 by Sunil Kant Munjal, chairman of Hero Enterprise; organised by the non-profit Serendipity Arts Foundation (Wikipedia).
- The 2023 edition ran nine days across 18 locations and subvenues, with more than 150 events and over 1,500 artists (Harper's Bazaar India); the 2025 edition was the tenth, held 12 to 21 December (Homegrown).
- Visiting: a first-person account in The Goan describes the festival as free to enter. Venues are wheelchair accessible and descriptions are provided in braille, and the programme includes sessions for deaf, blind and learning-disabled visitors (Harper's Bazaar India, on the 2023 edition).

FUNDING
Serendipity Arts Festival: founder-led non-profit; Goa government, embassies and corporate partners support it; no budget published
- Sunil Kant Munjal is founder patron; Hero Enterprise and Munjal are listed as patrons (Wikipedia). Munjal said Hero avoids branding the festival so it does not become a corporate programme (Sakshi Post, January 2024).
- 2025 festival partners page: "Supported By" the Government of Goa, the Sports Authority of Goa, the Directorate of Art and Culture, Kala Academy, Panjim Smart City, the Goa Forest Department and GTDC (stated by the festival).
- 2025 sponsors named by the festival: Milton, Campa, Sotheby's, IHCL, Sun Estates, Blue Tokai, AMD Maestro, Dainik Jagran, NDTV, Goa airports, Grover Zampa Vineyards and Sector Gin.
- Institutional partners in 2025 include the Australian Government, the Japan Foundation, the British Council, Pro Helvetia, the Embassy of France and the City of Ghent (stated by the festival); no amounts.
- Munjal described the Goa government partnership as "excellent" (Sakshi Post).
- No reporting found of the festival's budget, of the amounts Hero or Munjal contribute, or of a full trustee list.

Red flags: closure of exhibits and arrest of performers during the 2019 edition
- Exhibits: on Wednesday 18 December 2019, the fourth day, the festival organisers closed two works in the segment curated by Sudarshan Shetty: Miyah poetry and a graffiti wall with visitors' slogans against the CAA and NRC (ETV Bharat). No state order is reported.
- Reason given: Shetty said he was told "technical issues" caused the takedown; organisers did not comment publicly (ETV Bharat). Poet Ashraful Hussain answered: "It is not a technical glitch, it is political suppression" (IANS, via Morung Express, 20 December 2019).
- Arrests: on the same day four musicians of the band Dastaan Live were arrested and released on bail, after a complainant said their set insulted Hindu religious sentiments through references to "Om" (ETV Bharat). Goa police made the arrests and summoned band members to the station first to demand an apology (The Leaflet).
- Court outcome: on 9 April 2021 the Bombay High Court quashed the FIR against nine band members, finding the complaint disclosed no offence, and said police had acted hurriedly and had no justification for the arrests (The Leaflet).
- Goa government: a listed supporter of the 2025 festival (stated by the festival).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the festival as an institution. Coverage is mostly promotional or admiring; the critical pieces found concern programming and display, not funding or the 2019 closures.
1. Garland: Gopika Nath, "Serendipity Arts Festival: Re-viewing and re-thinking crafting methodologies" (2025)
URL: https://garlandmag.com/serendipity-arts-festival/
- The 2024 craft display in the Directorate of Accounts building was dimly lit and crowded, which made the work hard to appreciate.
- Collaborations conceived by Bridge Bharat left artisans as "no more than skilled labour"; a Kasuti piece used printing instead of counted-thread stitching.
- Criticises the programming and the display, not the funders.
2. Homegrown: Drishya, "Serendipity At 10: India's Biggest Arts Festival Was Exhilarating (& Exhausting)" (2026)
URL: https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-creators/serendipity-at-10-indias-biggest-arts-festival-was-exhilarating-exhausting
- Argues the festival's scale pushes visitors to treat it as a checklist, so that "we come, we see, we post, we move on".
- Says the display of Caravaggio's Magdalene in Ecstasy in a blacked-out room "crystallised the festival's contradictions": an access achievement that can become a must-see trophy.
- Does not discuss funding or sponsorship.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival's funding is not documented in any budget or accounts, so the money behind it cannot be checked beyond the founder patron, Hero Enterprise, the Goa government and a long list of brand and embassy partners. The weak points are that undisclosed money and the 2019 closure of the CAA and NRC exhibits by the organisers, with a reason the poets disputed, on the day Goa police arrested the Dastaan Live musicians. Watch for any published accounts or trustee list.
