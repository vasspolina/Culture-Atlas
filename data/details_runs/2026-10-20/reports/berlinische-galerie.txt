BERLINISCHE GALERIE: Berlin, Germany
ID: berlinische-galerie
Website: https://berlinischegalerie.de (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Berlin state museum of modern art, photography and architecture at Alte Jakobstraße 124-128, Kreuzberg (Berlinische Galerie; Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1975 as a society devoted to exhibiting Berlin art, with the art historian Eberhard Roters as founding director; it moved to a former officers' mess on Jebensstraße in 1978 and to the Martin-Gropius-Bau in 1986 (Wikipedia).
- In 1994 the collection became a public-law foundation under Berlin's Museum Foundations Act (Berlinische Galerie; Wikipedia).
- Building: a glass warehouse built in 1965, where the West Berlin Senate stored window glass as part of the city's Cold War emergency reserves. Architect Jörg Fricke converted it, and the museum opened there in 2004; it reopened in 2015 after a 6 million euro refurbishment of security and technical equipment (Wikipedia).
- Collection of art made in Berlin from 1870 to the present: about 5,000 fine art objects, 15,000 prints and drawings, 73,000 photographs and about 300,000 architectural plans, plus artists' archives (Wikipedia).
- Thomas Köhler has been director since September 2010; the Berlin Senate reappointed him in June 2024 until 1 January 2029 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Monday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Tuesday. Admission 14 euros, reduced 7 euros; free up to age 18, for refugees and for Friends; reduced entry for everyone on the first Wednesday of each month. The building, exhibition rooms and Café Dix are wheelchair accessible, with a tactile guidance system, audio guide and German Sign Language tours.

FUNDING
Berlinische Galerie: mainly public: state of Berlin, with a friends' association and a corporate art prize
- The museum is a Landesmuseum and a public-law foundation set up by the state of Berlin (stated by the museum). Its foundation council is chaired by Dr. Alexander Steinhilber, head of the culture division of the Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion; members include Jens-Rainer Jänig, chair of the Förderverein, Hilke Wagner of the Albertinum Dresden, Uli Mayer-Johanssen and Thomas Jebsen of DKB-Immobilien GmbH (stated by the museum).
- Förderverein Berlinische Galerie e.V., the friends' association, supports the museum mainly financially and had about 1,700 members in 2023 (Wikipedia).
- GASAG Art Prize: the prize is awarded every two years and has been presented in partnership with the Berlinische Galerie since 2010; GASAG initiated it (visitBerlin). The winner receives a purse of 10,000 euros, a show with a catalogue, and a new work that joins the collection (Berlinische Galerie). The 2026 winner is Jeewi Lee, shown until 5 April 2027. GASAG supplies natural gas, biomethane and green electricity to about 800,000 customers and is owned by E.ON Beteiligungen (36.85 percent), Engie Beteiligungs (31.575 percent) and Vattenfall (31.575 percent) (German Wikipedia).
- IBB-Videoraum: the video room is named after Investitionsbank Berlin, which Wikipedia calls the central development bank of the state of Berlin (museum site; Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the state of Berlin, through a public-law foundation whose foundation council is chaired by a Senate culture official, supplemented by a friends' association and corporate partners. The corporate line to watch is GASAG, a natural gas supplier that initiated the museum's biennial art prize in 2010.
