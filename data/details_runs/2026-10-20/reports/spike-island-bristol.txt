SPIKE ISLAND: Bristol, United Kingdom
ID: spike-island-bristol
Website: https://www.spikeisland.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre and studio complex on Bristol's harbourside, in an 80,000 square foot former industrial building (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1976 as Artspace Bristol by a group of artists who wanted affordable space; renamed Spike Island in 1998 (Spike Island).
- The artists first moved into the McArthur's Steel and Metal warehouse on Gas Ferry Road; the threat of increased rents and a proposed redevelopment of the site forced a move in 1989, and in 1992 they moved into the former Brooke Bond tea-packing warehouse (Spike Island history page).
- Redeveloped with lottery funding and private donations into a museum-sized gallery, individual studios, a print studio and a cafe. The space's history page names no architect.
- On site: over 70 subsidised studios, Spike Print Studio, Spike Island Workspace for creative businesses, and the gallery spaces.
- Visiting: gallery open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 5pm; major exhibitions are free; the site links an Access Guide.

Spike Island: mixed public and private: Arts Council England and Bristol City Council, trusts, galleries and individual patrons
- Arts Council England and Bristol City Council are shown as funders on the space's pages; the pages give no amounts or years.

Red flags: protest over a founding member's studio notice (2020)
- 1 May 2020: The Guardian reported that Spike Island's new board had told Howard Silverman, a founding member and long-standing studio holder, to leave his studio during the coronavirus lockdown. ArtsJournal reposted the report on 4 May 2020.
- Fellow studio holders and artists from around the world backed his fight to overturn the decision; the excerpt says some suggested the organisation was "intent on squeezing out older, established tenants in favour of younger ones" (The Guardian, via ArtsJournal).
- Bristol 24/7 also covered the dispute. Its page returned HTTP 403 when opened on 24 October 2026, so no petition count and no Spike Island response are given here.
- No reporting found of the board's final decision.
