WHITECHAPEL GALLERY: London, UK
ID: whitechapel-gallery
Website: https://www.whitechapelgallery.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery at 77-82 Whitechapel High Street, East London; Aldgate East station is under a minute's walk and Liverpool Street about 15 minutes (stated by the gallery).
- Opened in 1901 as one of the first publicly funded galleries for temporary exhibitions in London; Charles Aitken was the first director, 1901 to 1911 (Wikipedia).
- The original building was designed by Charles Harrison Townsend (Wikipedia). The 2009 expansion took in the adjacent former Passmore Edwards library, roughly doubled the gallery's size, cost about £13.5 million, was partly paid for by the Heritage Lottery Fund and reopened in April 2009 (Wikipedia).
- On site: nine exhibition spaces, an auditorium, study and studio spaces, a bookshop and a café (stated by the gallery).
- Picasso's "Guernica" was shown there in 1938 and Mark Rothko's first UK exhibition in 1961 (Wikipedia). Iwona Blazwick directed it from 2002 to 2022, followed by Gilane Tawadros (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 11 am to 6 pm Tuesday to Sunday, until 9 pm on Thursday, closed Monday. Free displays; one ticketed show per season at £16.50, £9.50 concession; under-16s and members free; entry to all exhibitions is pay what you can during Thursday Lates, 6 to 9 pm. Free Blue Badge parking and an Easy Read guide are offered.

FUNDING
Whitechapel Gallery: mixed: Arts Council England plus an auction endowment and corporate sponsors
- Arts Council England: annual funding fell from £1,510,168 (2018 to 2022) to £1,437,955 (2023 to 2026) (USA Art News, 16 March 2023). The gallery's footer says it is supported with public funding by Arts Council England.
- The 2021-22 annual report showed a deficit of £73,016; the gallery restructured in 2023 and made six full-time staff redundant, three of them curators (USA Art News, 16 March 2023).
- Bloomberg sponsored the Goshka Macuga work that opened the reopened gallery in 2009 (Museums Journal, 13 November 2009).
- Phillips hosted the online auction for the 3 March 2025 Art Icon gala, at which Doris Salcedo received the award; Pommery Champagne was served at the drinks reception (stated by the gallery).
- The expansion was partly funded by the Heritage Lottery Fund (Wikipedia). Museums Journal reports a charity auction of artists' donated works that paid for the expansion and created an endowment.
- The gallery's support page names no individual donors, corporate patrons or trusts. Its donor list and annual reports were not readable here, so Bloomberg Philanthropies, Max Mara, Swarovski Foundation and City Bridge Trust as donors are not confirmed: none is named on the support page or the 2025 gala page.
- No reporting found of a campaign against Bloomberg aimed at the Whitechapel.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the gallery or its funders found. The one documented dispute is a 2006 exhibition cut, and a 2023 restructure drew news coverage. An Artforum report on the 2023 job cuts was paywalled and not read.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lucian Harris, "Whitechapel Gallery accused of 'self-censorship'" (2006)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2006/12/01/whitechapel-gallery-accused-of-self-censorship
- Lender Marcel Fleiss found five of his 12 works withdrawn from the Hans Bellmer show and wrote a complaint, calling it "self-censorship"; he said "I will never lend works to them again".
- Pompidou curator Agnès de la Beaumelle told Le Monde that Blazwick was removing works that could offend local Muslim residents and anti-paedophile groups. The Whitechapel and the Pompidou both said the London show was smaller because of the gallery's smaller spaces.
- A decision taken by the gallery, not an act by a state or a funder.
2. USA Art News: Romario, "Six key jobs culled at London's Whitechapel Gallery, including three curatorial posts" (16 March 2023), an aggregator citing The Art Newspaper for Blazwick's comments
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/six-key-jobs-culled-at-londons-whitechapel-galleryincluding-three-curatorial-posts
- Reports the redundancies, the Arts Council cut and the deficit; Tawadros called it a "small re-structure". Not criticism of the funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public funding from Arts Council England plus an auction endowment and occasional corporate sponsors, but no donor list was readable, so named donors cannot be confirmed. The weak point is the 2006 Bellmer removals, which a lender called self-censorship and the gallery attributed to space. Worth watching: the gallery's published donor list and accounts, which would show whether Bloomberg Philanthropies funds it today.
