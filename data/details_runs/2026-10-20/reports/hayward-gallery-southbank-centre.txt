HAYWARD GALLERY (SOUTHBANK CENTRE): London, UK
ID: hayward-gallery-southbank-centre
Website: https://www.southbankcentre.co.uk

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition gallery on the South Bank in central London, part of the Southbank Centre and beside the Royal Festival Hall (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 9 July 1968 under the Arts Council of Great Britain, which managed it until 1986, when management passed to the Southbank Centre; Joanna Drew was the founding director (Wikipedia).
- Named for Sir Isaac Hayward, a former leader of the London County Council (Wikipedia).
- Brutalist building of exposed concrete by Norman Engleback with Ron Herron, Warren Chalk and John Attenborough; structural engineer Ove Arup & Partners; glass pyramids on the flat roofs (Wikipedia).
- A gallery without a permanent collection; it is the base of Arts Council England's National Touring Exhibitions programme (Wikipedia).
- The Southbank Centre holds the buildings on a 150-year lease from Arts Council England that began on 1 April 1988, at a peppercorn rent; Arts Council England owns the freehold (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).

FUNDING
Hayward Gallery (Southbank Centre): Arts Council England core grant, commercial income, fundraising and box office income
- The Hayward is funded through the Southbank Centre, a registered charity (298909) and a National Portfolio Organisation funded by Arts Council England. The accounts say the Southbank Centre relies mainly on the Arts Council England revenue grant, commercial income, fundraising and box office income to cover its operating costs (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).
- Year to 31 March 2024: total income £53.9m. The Arts Council grant was £18.1m (£16.8m unrestricted, £1.3m restricted), down from £19.7m the year before. Donations and sponsorships totalled £2.7m. Other trading, mainly concessions and space rentals, was £20.9m and charitable activities, including tickets, £10.6m (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).
- Unrestricted donation and sponsorship income was £1.5m. The accounts attribute the fall in restricted donations to the phasing of grants from Apple, for the Reframe programme, and from Bloomberg, for digital innovation at the Southbank Centre (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).
- Arts Council England gave a capital grant of £16.7m for refurbishing the Festival Wing, which includes the Hayward Gallery, and holds a fixed and floating charge over the charity's assets. The grant is repayable if the Southbank Centre ceases to operate (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).
- Trustee donations were £65,243 in 2023-24, and the American Fund for Southbank Centre passed on USD 151,263 from US donors (Southbank Centre accounts 2023-24).
- Shell held annual corporate memberships of the Southbank Centre and the British Film Institute, together worth around £20,000, and did not renew them when they expired in 2020 (Gareth Harris, The Art Newspaper, 10 March 2020).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Hayward is the public Arts Council England grant of £18.1m in 2023-24, ticket and trading income of about £31.5m, and £2.7m in donations and sponsorship, all set out in the Southbank Centre's signed accounts. Shell's corporate membership, worth about £20,000 with the British Film Institute's, ended in 2020. The Southbank Centre's accounts name Apple and Bloomberg as the funders of restricted grants for the Reframe programme and digital innovation.
