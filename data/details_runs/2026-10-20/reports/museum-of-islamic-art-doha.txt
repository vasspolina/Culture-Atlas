MUSEUM OF ISLAMIC ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: museum-of-islamic-art-doha
Website: https://mia.org.qa (checked 2026-10-24; the about page loads; www.mia.org.qa returned a self-signed certificate error)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Islamic art on an artificial peninsula about 60 metres off the Doha Corniche, surrounded by a park of roughly 290,000 square metres and linked to the mainland by two bridges (Wikipedia).
- One of the first projects started by Qatar Museums (stated by the museum); the then-emir Sheikh Hamad attended the opening on 22 November 2008 and the public opening followed on 1 December 2008 (Bidoun; Wikipedia gives 8 December).
- Collection of Islamic art from the 7th to the 20th centuries, from Spain, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Turkey, India and Central Asia.
- Designed by I. M. Pei, inspired by the fountain of the Ibn Tulun Mosque in Cairo; limestone exterior, five floors, a central tower and dome; the interior galleries were designed by Wilmotte Associates and the building was constructed by the Turkish firm Baytur.
- On site: a library of more than 21,000 books on Islamic art, a cafe, the IDAM restaurant on the top floor, a gift shop and MIA Park.
- Closed for a ten-month renovation from late 2021 and reopened on 5 October 2022 with 18 renovated galleries.
- Visiting: open Saturday to Tuesday 9am to 7pm, Thursday 9am to 9pm, Friday 1:30pm to 7pm, closed Wednesday (museum site). Admission QAR 50 for non-resident adults; free for Qatar residents with ID and children aged 16 and under (Qatar Living, June 2026). No reporting found of wheelchair access details in the sources read.

FUNDING
Museum of Islamic Art: state-owned: funded through Qatar Museums, the Qatari government's culture body
- Qatar Museums owns the museum (Wikipedia) and is chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site).
- The museum's costs are ringfenced; its budget was not cut when the emir cut culture spending in early 2016 (The Art Newspaper, Georgina Adam, 10 March 2016).
- Qatar Museums does not disclose its budget and declined to comment on it in 2016 (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, private donor or published accounts for the museum.
- No official construction cost found. A 2008 blog gives $300 million; no outlet confirms it.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Owner: the museum is owned by Qatar Museums (Wikipedia), the state culture body that also runs Mathaf and the National Museum of Qatar (Qatar Museums site).
- Leadership: Qatar Museums is chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site).
- Staff cuts: citing the Financial Times, The Art Newspaper reported that Qatar Museums let 240 staff go in early 2016, leaving fewer than 800 from 1,200 two years earlier. Qatar Museums declined to confirm the figures.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum as an institution. The critical pieces found concern its displays and the state spending around it; coverage of the opening was mostly descriptive.
1. Bidoun: Antonia Carver, "Museum of Islamic Art" (2009)
URL: https://bidoun.org/articles/museum-of-islamic-art
- The galleries are "almost devoid of information"; labels are minimal and audio guides carry most of the description.
- Objects are displayed for aesthetic impact, for example astrolabes shown like installations, which gives them less historical context.
- Questions how far the Gulf museum boom delivers on its stated aims of branding, education and "building bridges".
2. The Art Newspaper: Georgina Adam, "Football outscores culture as Qatar spending slumps" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/10/football-outscores-culture-as-qatar-spending-slumps
- Reports the 2016 cuts to Qatar Museums and says the Museum of Islamic Art's costs were ringfenced.
- News report on spending, not a critique of the museum; relies on anonymous sources.
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Debate in Qatar has centred on how public money is used and where unannounced acquisitions go.
- Does not criticise the Museum of Islamic Art; it cites the museum as evidence that Qatar Museums cultivated local and regional culture.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013, social media users led by a Qatari journalist accused the Qatar Museums Authority of corruption, mismanagement and nepotism (openDemocracy, Sarah El-Richani).
- The same article says some Qataris objected to Damien Hirst's sculptures and Adel Abdessemed's "Coup de tete", and that Abdessemed's film "Printemps" was not shown.
- The museum is owned by Qatar Museums, the body these accusations name.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Qatari state money channelled through Qatar Museums, with no private or corporate funder named in any source found. The weak points are the lack of published accounts or budget and the museum's place inside a state culture body. Worth watching: whether Qatar Museums ever publishes accounts or a sponsor list.
