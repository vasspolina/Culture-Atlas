MISK ART INSTITUTE: Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
ID: misk-art-institute
Website: https://miskartinstitute.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit arts organisation in Riyadh running residencies, training, a grant, Misk Art Week and exhibitions, mainly at the Prince Faisal bin Fahd Arts Hall (Misk Art Institute website).
- Established in December 2017 by the Misk Foundation, which Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman founded in 2011 (Misk Art Institute website, Wikipedia). Artist Ahmed Mater was its founding director (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- The hall was established in 1985 by royal decree of King Fahd as the Fine Arts Hall, later named for Prince Faisal bin Fahd. It covers 5,600 square metres and reopened under the Institute at the end of 2019 (Arab News, 31 December 2019).
- The Institute has been the commissioner of Saudi Arabia's national pavilion at the Venice Biennale since 2018 (Arab News).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility; the site gives directions to the hall but no visitor details.

FUNDING
Misk Art Institute: state-linked: subsidiary of the Misk Foundation, founded by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman
- The Institute describes itself as affiliated with the non-profit Mohammed bin Salman Foundation "Misk" (Misk Art Institute website).
- Misk Foundation's own funding sources are not stated in any source read.
- No reporting found of a budget, accounts or donor list for the Institute.

Red flags: Gulf state patronage
- 8 December 2017: The Art Newspaper reported that the Crown Prince launched the Institute as the artistic arm of his Misk Foundation, with Mater as director (Anna Somers Cocks).
- January 2018: at the launch of Misk's international programme at MoMA, Mater described the approach as "instructed by the Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud". Rijin Sahakian wrote that the Crown Prince personally appointed Mater (Hyperallergic, 1 November 2018).
- 31 December 2019: Arab News reported that Culture Minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah, who chairs the Institute, patronised the reopening of the Prince Faisal bin Fahd Arts Hall.

Red flags: institutions declined Misk money after the Khashoggi killing
- October 2018: Misk had planned to fully fund the Brooklyn Museum exhibition "Syria, Then and Now", which opened on 13 October, and a Met seminar on collecting the Middle East; both museums paid for them themselves. The Met's cost was under USD 20,000 (Hyperallergic, Jasmine Weber, 18 October 2018).
- Columbia University announced that Mater's lecture of 22 October 2018 would not take place, and the Middle East Institute withdrew from the Arab Art and Education Initiative (Hyperallergic, 18 October 2018). The Art Newspaper reported on 30 October 2018 that Misk had suspended its foreign programme.
- November 2018: the Gates Foundation suspended its planned engagement with the Misk Foundation, calling the killing "extremely troubling" (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is limited to pieces from October and November 2018, after the Khashoggi killing; none after 2018 was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Rijin Sahakian, "A Coalition of The Willing: How Saudi Arabia and US Arts Institutions Partnered on a Cultural Diplomacy Offensive" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/468850/saudi-arabia-and-us-art-institutions/
- Argues Misk is presented as grassroots but was created and chaired by the Crown Prince, who personally appointed Mater; says The Art Newspaper quoted Stephen Stapleton at length without disclosing his Misk role as director of international programmes.
- Argues the Met and Brooklyn Museum acted too late, having long known the kingdom's record, and that positive press from Sotheby's and The Art Newspaper helped rebrand the Crown Prince as a reformer. Names the Institute at length.
2. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Weber, "Several Major New York Art Institutions Step Away from Saudi-Funded Initiatives" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/466508/several-major-new-york-art-institutions-step-away-from-saudi-funded-initiatives/
- News report, not an essay: lists the museums that took over funding Misk had planned, the Columbia lecture that would not take place and the Middle East Institute's withdrawal.
3. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, with a comment from Stephen Stapleton, "Do not penalise Saudi Arabian artists for the murder of Jamal Khashoggi" (30 October 2018)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/comment/do-not-penalise-saudi-arabian-artists-for-the-murder-of-jamal-khashoggi
- Argues Saudi artists should not be punished for the killing and that fear and self-censorship among them have grown.
- Names the Institute and Mater but does not criticise either; it says the killing forced the West to reassess its view of the Crown Prince.
4. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, "Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince adds art and culture to his portfolio" (8 December 2017)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/saudi-arabia-s-crown-prince-adds-art-and-culture-to-his-portfolio
- Not criticism: describes the Institute's plans; no budget or funders stated.

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute is a subsidiary of the Crown Prince's foundation, chaired by the culture minister in 2019, with no budget, accounts or donor list published. The weak points are that opacity and the October 2018 withdrawals of Western partners after the Khashoggi killing. Worth watching: whether it publishes accounts.
