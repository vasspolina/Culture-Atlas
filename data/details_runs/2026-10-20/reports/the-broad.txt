THE BROAD: Los Angeles, USA
ID: the-broad
Website: https://www.thebroad.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 221 South Grand Avenue in downtown Los Angeles, with the Broad family's collection of art from the 1950s to the present (more than 2,000 works by nearly 200 artists, stated by the museum).
- Founded by Eli and Edythe Broad; opened on 20 September 2015 (Wikipedia). Joanne Heyler is founding director and president.
- Named for the Broads, who financed the building.
- Designed by Diller Scofidio + Renfro with Gensler; a porous fibreglass-reinforced concrete "veil" wraps an inner "vault" for storage and offices. The museum states the building is 120,000 square feet. The site was leased by Broad on Grand Avenue (see Funding).
- Also houses the Broad Art Foundation, a lending library of art that has loaned works since 1984; the museum is planning an expansion on Hope Street (stated by the museum).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 11am to 5pm, Thursday 11am to 8pm, Saturday and Sunday 10am to 6pm; closed Monday. General admission is free; special exhibitions are charged (Wikipedia). The museum reports more than 900,000 visitors a year.

FUNDING
The Broad: self-funded: the founders' money, a $200M endowment and the Broad foundation
- The Broads paid for the building, about $140M, and set up a $200M endowment plus funds for acquisitions (The Art Newspaper, 2015). Eli Broad's fortune came from insurance and home construction (The Art Newspaper).
- The museum files as a private foundation (Form 990-PF). For 2024 it reported revenue of $27.8M, expenses of $29.6M and total assets of $538M (ProPublica).
- Land: Broad paid $7.7M for a 99-year lease on the Grand Avenue site. A councilwoman's aide told Reason the $7.7M was money the Broad foundation paid for affordable housing under a 2004 agreement and that the lease rate is $1 a year, the rate for cultural institutions (Reason, 2010). A county official said the block had first been offered at $1 a year.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor or state grant paying for the museum's running costs.

Red flags: board figure with an SEC settlement (Bruce Karatz)
- Bruce Karatz, former chief executive of KB Home, is listed among the board of trustees of The Broad (Wikipedia).
- On 15 September 2008 he settled civil charges brought by the Securities and Exchange Commission over stock option backdating at KB Home.
- On 10 November 2010 a federal judge sentenced him to five years' probation; the court ended it early, on 27 April 2012.
- The museum's own site does not publish a board list.

Red flags: Broad's role in the Grand Avenue project that controlled the site
- Broad led the Grand Avenue Committee from 2003 to 2007. Within weeks of stepping down as co-chair on 30 November he was in talks with the Grand Avenue Authority, which he had helped form, about a museum site (Consumer Watchdog, an advocacy source).
- The developer, Related Companies, received more than $100 million in city, county and state subsidies and tax breaks for Grand Avenue before the 2008 crash, during Broad's chairmanship (Consumer Watchdog).
- Unverified: that Broad's inside knowledge gave him an advantage in the site negotiation, from Consumer Watchdog, an advocacy group.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Mixed reviews at the 2015 opening, aimed at the building, the collection's taste and Broad's control over the city's museums. No art critic found makes a case against the museum's funding.
1. The Art Newspaper: Julia Halperin, "The Broad: as big as its founders' influence" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/10/01/the-broad-as-big-as-its-founders-influence
- Broad has been "criticised for demanding too much control" and clashed repeatedly with museum directors and board members.
- The Broads' decision not to give their collection to LACMA surprised observers.
- The opening show was faulted for "the splashy and the monumental"; some rooms have "the air of an art fair booth".
2. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "The New Broad Museum Brings LA Lots of Blue-Chip Art and a Few Surprises" (2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/the-new-broad-museum-brings-la-lots-of-blue-chip-art-and-a-few-surprises
- The collection reflects the tastes of two collectors; the opening was delayed 15 months with rising costs and lawsuits. Calls $140 million "a steep price tag for intrigue".
- Says Broad's ties to LA museums "eventually soured" in some cases, such as the LACMA loan.
- Credits the free admission and the inclusion of women, queer artists and artists of colour.
3. Reason: Tim Cavanaugh, article on Broad's Grand Avenue lease (2010), not art press
URL: https://reason.com/2010/08/24/why-is-eli-broad-renting-a-ful/printer/
- Argues the Grand Avenue lease is a taxpayer subsidy to a wealthy donor, with Broad's net worth put at $5.2 billion.
- Reason is a libertarian magazine.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Consumer Watchdog and Reason criticised the $3.2 billion Grand Avenue project, which was stalled for nearly five years in 2010, for public subsidies to its developer.
- The museum sits on the Grand Avenue land Broad leased, which ties it to that critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Broads' own, with a $200M endowment, and no reporting found of a corporate or state funder. The weak points are the public-land lease Broad negotiated after chairing the Grand Avenue committee, his documented control over LA institutions, and trustee Bruce Karatz's 2008 SEC settlement and 2010 probation. Watch the Hope Street expansion and the board list.
