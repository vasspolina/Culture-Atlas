BEIRUT ART CENTER (BAC): Beirut, Lebanon
ID: beirut-art-center-bac
Website: https://beirutartcenter.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art space in Jisr el Wati, an industrial area on the banks of the Beirut River, off Corniche an-Nahr (Beirut Art Center).
- The Beirut Art Center Association project was established in 2005, initiated by Sandra Dagher and Lamia Joreige; the founding members were Dagher, Joreige, Rabih Mroué, Bassam Kawahji and Maria Ousseimi. BAC opened in 2009 (Beirut Art Center).
- Wikipedia states that the first building was a factory converted by the architect Raed Abillama into a two-floor "white cube" of about 1,500 m², and that in 2019 BAC moved to a warehouse in the same neighbourhood.
- Programme: exhibitions of Lebanese and international artists, BAC Design for local industrial and product design, and learning initiatives (Wikipedia; Beirut Art Center).
- The board lists Marwan T. Assaf, Kader Attia, Sandra Dagher, Lamia Joreige, Bassam Kahwaji, Nathalie Khoury, Yola Noujaim, Maria Ousseimi, Yasmina Sabrier and Amira El Solh.
- Visiting: exhibitions are open Tuesday to Friday 12 pm to 7 pm and Saturday 11 am to 5 pm.

FUNDING
Beirut Art Center: private patrons, corporate sponsors and foundations, with Gulf and European cultural bodies among the listed patrons
- The donor page names its funders by year, from the 2008-2009 founding list to 2025, in tiers from benefactor to friend; stated by the space.
- Founding benefactor: Marwan T. Assaf, listed as benefactor in 2008-2009 and again from 2019 to 2025, and now a board member. Founding patrons: Association Philippe Jabre, Cherine Al Maghraby Tayeb and the Ousseimi Foundation. Founding sponsors: Banque Libano-Française, Fidus Wealth Management (SGBL Group), Bankers Assurance, Abela Tourism & Development Co. and ABC.
- Peter and Nathalie Hrechdakian are benefactors from 2019 to 2025.
- Foundations and institutions on the donor roll include the Ford Foundation (patron in 2011, 2012, 2013 and 2015 to 2017), the Boghossian Foundation (supporter in 2015 to 2020 and 2022), the Prince Claus Fund (2020, 2022), Mophradat (2021), Arab Fund for Arts and Culture (benefactor 2020, patron 2021, supporter 2025), the Gerda Henkel Stiftung and J.M. Kaplan Fund (2020), the British Council (patron 2025), Institut Français (patron 2019 and 2025), the Goethe Institute and Pro Helvetia (exhibition patrons 2018 and 2019), and the Danish Arts Foundation (2018).
- BERYT CCI, funded by the World Bank and administered by UNESCO, is listed as a patron in 2023.
- Banks: Banque Libano-Française is listed as a sponsor in every year from 2008-2009 to 2019; Audi Bank 2014 to 2018; Banque Bemo as exhibition sponsor in 2019.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Sharjah Art Foundation is listed on the BAC donor page as an Exhibitions and Activities Patron in 2018. The foundation describes itself as "a legally independent public body established by Emiri Decree and supported by government funding" (Sharjah Art Foundation press release, 8 July 2026). Wikipedia lists its parent organisation as the Department of Culture and Information, Government of Sharjah, and says Hoor Al Qasimi, daughter of Sharjah's ruler Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi, founded it in 2009.
- Qatar Foundation is listed on the donor page as a Patron in 2020, in a joint entry with the British Council's Cultural Protection Fund, the Gerda Henkel Stiftung, the J.M. Kaplan Fund and the Prince Claus Fund. Wikipedia calls Qatar Foundation a "state-led non-profit organization" founded in 1995 by then-Emir Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani. MEED (Ananda Shakespeare, 24 February 2014) calls it "an independent non-profit organisation" supported by an endowment funded by Qatar's hydrocarbons revenues, with stakes in government-backed companies such as Nakilat.
- The donor page lists the names by tier and year only.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Carole and Carlos Ghosn are listed on the donor page as supporters in 2017 and 2018 and as patrons in 2018. Wikipedia reports that Tokyo prosecutors arrested Ghosn on 19 November 2018 over allegations of false accounting at Nissan, and that media reported on 30 December 2019 that he had escaped from Japan and arrived in Beirut.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind BAC is a wide base of private patrons, Lebanese banks and corporate sponsors, and European and US foundations and cultural institutes, with Marwan T. Assaf as founding benefactor. Two Gulf bodies, Sharjah Art Foundation (2018) and Qatar Foundation (2020), appear once each as patrons. Those two entries are the points worth watching.
