MUSEO UNIVERSITARIO DEL CHOPO: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-universitario-del-chopo-mexico-city
Website: https://www.chopo.unam.mx/ (checked 2026-11-20; down, its security certificate failed)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum of the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM) at Dr. Enrique González Martínez 10 in Santa María la Ribera, under UNAM's Coordinación de Difusión Cultural (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The building is a prefabricated Jugendstil structure of iron, brick and glass designed by Bruno Möhring as a machine hall for the Gutehoffnungshütte works and shown at a 1902 exhibition in Düsseldorf; the businessman José Landero y Coss brought part of it to Mexico, and the engineer Luis Bacmeister assembled it from 1901 to 1905 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- It housed the Museo Nacional de Historia Natural from 1913 and passed to UNAM in 1929. After that museum closed in 1964 it stood empty; a demolition firm offered 40,000 pesos for its iron in 1970, and its monument status stopped the plan. UNAM reclaimed and restored it from 1973 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Rector Guillermo Soberón opened it as a university museum on 25 November 1975 (Spanish Wikipedia; Gaceta UNAM). It closed in October 2005 for a redesign by the architect Enrique Norten and reopened on 18 May 2010 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, the Foro del Dinosaurio Juan José Gurrola, a documentation centre and mediateca, a fanzine library, a shop and bookshop, a café and low-cost workshops; in 2024 it opened El Gabinete for emerging artists and an outdoor mural space, Efímero/permanente (UNAM 2024 report).
- The Tianguis Cultural del Chopo began inside the museum on 4 October 1980 and now meets on Saturdays on Juan Aldama; it was recognised as intangible cultural heritage of Mexico City in 2023. The Festival Internacional por la Diversidad Sexual grew from a Semana Cultural Lésbica Gay begun there in 1987 (Gaceta UNAM, Rafael Paz, 21 August 2025).
- Sol Henaro Palomino directs it: since January 2024 according to UNAM's 2024 report, since May 2024 according to Gaceta UNAM (2025). She calls it "un centro social travestido de museo" (a social centre disguised as a museum; translation).
- Visitors: in 2024 its 372 activities drew 101,851 people, and its nine exhibitions 87,351, among them Gran Fury from the Museu de Arte de São Paulo and a Fabián Cháirez show (UNAM 2024 report).
- In 2026 the Juan Gabriel exhibition "Un lugar de ambiente. Legado y disidencia" was due to open on 28 May with the 39th Festival Internacional por la Diversidad Sexual (Roberto Frías, Gaceta UNAM). The museum postponed the opening "hasta nuevo aviso" (until further notice) (Eduardo Alavez, Chilango, 29 May 2026), opened to the public hours later and held the official opening on 13 June; the show runs to 28 August. Henaro said the delay was an administrative matter with the artist's representatives; El Universal said the reasons remained unclear (Frida Juárez Bautista, June 2026). Unverified: social-media users said it was a copyright problem, as reported by El Universal.

FUNDING
Museo Universitario del Chopo: mainly public: UNAM, itself funded by the federal government
- The museum belongs to UNAM, a public university. UNAM's 2026 budget is 59,878 million pesos: 53,749 million (89.8%) federal subsidy and 6,129 million (10.2%) its own income (Mirtha Hernández, Gaceta UNAM).
- In 2024 UNAM's Dirección General de Obras y Conservación and Dirección General del Patrimonio Universitario supported maintenance and restoration of the east façade, new rain downpipes and a larger lightning-protection system (UNAM 2024 report).
- Partners named in 2024: the Instituto Francés de América Latina and the Goethe-Institut for a day on sexual diversity, the Festival Ambulante and the Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana for a theatre call, and INAPAM for free workshops for older adults (UNAM 2024 report).
- The 2005 to 2010 rebuild was a UNAM project under rectors Juan Ramón de la Fuente and José Narro (Spanish Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is UNAM's budget, nine-tenths of it federal, with foreign cultural institutes as occasional partners and no flagged funder. Its one recent episode, the brief cancellation of the Juan Gabriel opening, raised questions about rights, not money.
