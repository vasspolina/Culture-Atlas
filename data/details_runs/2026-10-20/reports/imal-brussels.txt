IMAL: Brussels, Belgium
ID: imal-brussels
Website: https://www.imal.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Art centre and laboratory for digital cultures and technology at 30 Quai des Charbonnages, Molenbeek, Brussels (iMAL).
- Yves Bernard began the project at the end of 1998, named it iMAL (interactive Media Art Lab) in 1999 and launched its first project, CONTinENT, within the European Capitals of Culture programme Brussels 2000 and Helsinki 2000; the non-profit was legally created in May 2000 and he ran it until mid-2021 (iMAL).
- Lucía García is the current director (iMAL).
- Building: Central Office and NP2F designed the renovation and extension, which doubled the floor area; work began in 2016 (European Commission, 7 June 2023).
- Reopening year: iMAL's own About page (French) says "Rouvert en 2020" (reopened in 2020). The European Commission page, dated 7 June 2023, says the new infrastructure's inauguration took place on Wednesday 7 June. The two sources differ and neither explains the gap.
- On site: an exhibition space and a Fablab, a shared workshop with digital fabrication machines; in 2012 Bernard created Belgium's first artistic fablab (iMAL).
- Visiting: open during public events only, with the Fablab open several times a week; exhibitions cost 10 euros, reduced 5 euros, free for children under 6; the exhibition space is wheelchair accessible (iMAL).

FUNDING
iMAL: mainly public: Belgian French- and Flemish-community bodies and the EU, with two private partners
- Structural support comes from the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, the Flemish government through the Vlaams Brusselfonds, and the Flemish Community Commission, VGC (iMAL).
- The partners page also shows, as logos without amounts or years: CCF, Innoviris, Visit Brussels, Creative Europe, the Loterie Nationale and the ERDF (iMAL).
- The renovation and extension received 1.4 million euros from the European Union's European Regional Development Fund, 2014 to 2020 programme (European Commission, 2023).
- Private partners: Fondation Orange and Selligent. The page gives no description of what either pays for and no amounts. No reporting found of their sums.
- No reporting found of annual reports or accounts on the site.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind iMAL is mainly public (Belgian community governments, the EU and the National Lottery), with two private partners whose contribution is not stated. No flagged funder was found. The weak points are the absence of published accounts and of amounts for any funder; worth watching is what Fondation Orange and Selligent pay.
