MOCA BANGKOK: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: moca-bangkok
Website: https://www.mocabangkok.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Privately owned five-floor museum of contemporary art at 499 Kamphaeng Phet 6 Road, Lat Yao, Chatuchak, Bangkok. The Sawasdee guide says the Bang Khen station of the SRT Red Line serves it.
- Founded by the telecoms executive Boonchai Bencharongkul; Wikipedia gives the opening as 23 March 2012 and says it was previously called the Art Centre. His son Kanachai runs it (Sawasdee, 2022); Lux (1 December 2023) calls the son a co-owner and says the father has not fully handed over control.
- Not named for a person.
- Building: a granite façade around a central lightwell, with interiors by the Thai design firm PIA (Sawasdee).
- On site: over 1,000 works by Thai and international artists, with floors devoted to Chalermchai Kositpipat, Thawan Duchanee and 19th-century-style academic paintings (Sawasdee). The museum's site also lists a Four Seasons Art Space and a Suvarnabhumi display; their contents were not read. The son says the museum now shows a new temporary exhibition almost every month (Lux).
- Visiting: THB 250 general, THB 100 for students and over-60s; 10am to 6pm, Tuesday to Sunday (Sawasdee, August 2022; not confirmed on the museum's own pages, which do not show prices). No accessibility information found.

FUNDING
MOCA Bangkok: self-funded: the founder's own money
- The museum grew out of Boonchai Bencharongkul's private collection (Sawasdee). A 2012 post on a personal blog, Dateline Bangkok, says Boonchai funded it entirely; it is a weak source and no other source read states it.
- Boonchai founded and chairs DTAC, which Wikipedia calls Thailand's third-largest mobile phone operator; he partnered with Telenor of Norway and in 2005 he and his three siblings sold most of their shares while he stayed chairman (Wikipedia).
- His son Kit Kanachai Bencharongkul told Lux (2023) that managing the financial responsibilities of running a museum can be stressful; he gave no figures.
- Ticket sales: THB 250 general admission (Sawasdee, 2022).
- No reporting found of any corporate sponsor, state funder, annual accounts or board; the museum's site presents a founder's message and its mission only.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Almost no criticism in the art press. The only critical piece found is a personal blog from the opening year.
1. Dateline Bangkok (blog): Matthew Hunt, "Museum of Contemporary Art" (2012)
URL: https://blog.matthewhunt.com/2012/08/museum-of-contemporary-art.html
- Calls the building impressive but the collection traditional and conservative: religious and mythological paintings, "no political art at all," no video, photography or new media.
- Says Boonchai "is the local art market," filled the museum with his own collection and "personally curated each gallery," and calls it "a conservative version of the Saatchi Gallery."
- A personal blog, not art press. Criticises the programme and the single-patron model, not any funder.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Nation, "Foundation chaired by Boonchai's sister implicated in KCUC embezzlement probe" (16 June 2017): Boonchai's younger sister, Wanna Chirakiti, chairs the Khun Yay Maha Ratana Upasika Chandra Khonnokyoong Foundation, which is under the patronage of the monk Phra Dhammachayo, who faced an arrest warrant on money-laundering charges. Thailand's Department of Special Investigation summoned her several times. A special prosecutor said the foundation built temple facilities with money from Supachai Srisupa-aksorn, who was jailed for stealing from the Klongchan cooperative. The article does not say what she was asked or whether she was charged.
- The special prosecutor said in the same article: "to date there has been no evidence that Boonchai was involved in alleged money-laundering." The article does not describe any donations by Boonchai.
- Wikipedia says Boonchai follows the Dhammakaya movement. No reporting found of any link between the museum's money and the foundation, or of the outcome of the inquiry.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the founder's own, from a telecoms business, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are the lack of any published accounts, board or outside funder, and one-patron control of what the museum shows. Worth watching: how Boonchai's family ties to the Dhammakaya inquiry develop, though no source read links them to the museum.
