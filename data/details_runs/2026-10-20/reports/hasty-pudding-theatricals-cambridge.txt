HASTY PUDDING THEATRICALS: Cambridge, USA
ID: hasty-pudding-theatricals-cambridge
Website: https://hastypudding.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Student theatrical society at Harvard University, known for its annual burlesque musicals, often performed in drag, and its Man and Woman of the Year awards.
- Formed in 1795 as a social club for Harvard College students; its first full production was "Bombastes Furioso" in 1844, and students began writing their own shows in 1882.
- Named for hasty pudding, a cornmeal porridge still served at its banquets.
- It performs in Farkas Hall, a Georgian Revival building of 1888 that Harvard renovated and expanded from 2005 to 2007, keeping the façade; Harvard renamed it Farkas Hall in October 2011 (Harvard Gazette).
- Part of the Hasty Pudding Institute of 1770 with the Hasty Pudding Club and the Harvard Krokodiloes; the cast was men-only until 2018.
- The annual show plays at Farkas Hall, whose theatre seats about 270 and is open to the public (Harvard Gazette).

FUNDING
Hasty Pudding Theatricals: ticket sales and private donors through the Hasty Pudding Institute of 1770
- The Institute's Form 990 for the year to June 2025 shows revenue of $1,662,360: $777,563 from programme services and $736,953 in contributions (ProPublica).
- Its 2021 donor list, the only one posted, is led by The Ann and Andrew Tisch Foundation ($50,000 and up), followed by The Related Companies, The Leonard and Judy Lauder Fund, Ed Forst, The Dinan Family Foundation and Mehmet Oz ($25,000 and up) (stated by the space).
- Andrew L. Farkas has chaired the Institute since its founding in 2012 (The Harvard Crimson). Harvard named Farkas Hall for his father, Robin L. Farkas, in October 2011 to recognise Farkas's gift; the Gazette gives no amount.
- No reporting found of public or corporate funding beyond The Related Companies.

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein money and the chair's friendship with him
- The Jeffrey Epstein Virgin Islands Foundation and Gratitude for America gave the Institute at least $375,000: $75,000 in 2013 and $50,000 a year from 2014 to 2019, more than double the $150,000 the Pudding had acknowledged (The Harvard Crimson, 2026).
- The $50,000 a year placed Epstein in the top "Guardian of the Sphinx" tier, with prime gala seating and show tickets; the Pudding listed him as a 2018 "Guardian of the Sphinx" until The Crimson reported the gift in April 2019 (The Harvard Crimson, 2019).
- Farkas solicited the money; Epstein had been banned from philanthropic gifts to Harvard in 2008 and used the Institute to stay connected to the school (New York Times, republished by The Boston Globe, July 2026).
- Department of Justice files released in January 2026 show the two were close (The Boston Globe). A 2014 text from Epstein, who invited Farkas to his private island, called the two men "two guardians of the Sphinx" and Farkas replied "That's us"; scheduled meetings ran as late as 8 April 2019 (The Harvard Crimson).
- Farkas said his connections to Epstein were "professional, not personal" and that he condemns Epstein's crimes (The Harvard Crimson). He has never been accused of or charged with any crime (The Boston Globe).
- The Pudding gave $50,000 to an anti-sex trafficking nonprofit after the 2016 gift became public; for the rest, its graduate board said it had given more than the amount to "worthy organizations" it did not name (The Harvard Crimson).

Red flags: petitions against the chair
- In September 2019 former cast member Jacob W. Roberts started a petition asking the graduate board to give the 2016 Epstein gift to groups fighting sex trafficking (The Harvard Crimson).
- In February 2026 a majority of the current company, backed by its president Daisy M. Nussbaum, petitioned for Farkas to step down; separate petitions asked Harvard to rename Farkas Hall (The Harvard Crimson).
- By July 2026 three petitions had at least 175 signers, including all of the past year's student leaders; the leadership had not addressed them, and Harvard said it had "no role" in the club's governance (The Boston Globe).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly ticket sales and private donors, but at least $375,000 came from Epstein's charities from 2013 to 2019, solicited by the chair, Andrew Farkas, who was still in post in July 2026. Worth watching: whether Farkas steps down, whether Harvard renames Farkas Hall, and where the redirected money went.
