AL RIWAQ ART SPACE: Manama, Bahrain
ID: al-riwaq-art-space-manama
Website: https://www.alriwaqartspace.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art and design hub in the historic Bank House at Al Guful, Manama, that describes itself as a campus for art and design.
- Opened in 1998 as a commercial gallery in a modest villa in the Adliya district, run by director Bayan Al Barrak Kanoo, and later became an art space and then a non-profit (the space's own history; The National, 11 June 2015, calls it "now a not-for-profit organisation"). Monocle says Kanoo moved to Bahrain from Baghdad in the mid-1980s and began by selling work by Iraqi artists.
- Building: since 2022 the Bank House in Al Guful, called "historic" by the space; the pages read name no architect or date. The earlier home was the Adliya villa.
- What is there: gallery, garden, cafe (run with De'Part), art library, studios for rent, co-working and workshop spaces, an alternative art education programme, residencies (the Application 001 pilot) and the Majal 1.0 design incubation programme. Public festivals have included The Market (2011) and The Nest (2012 to 2018, from the Market 338 and Alwan 338 initiatives).
- Visiting: open Saturday to Thursday, 10 am to 8 pm; the gallery and most events are free, except workshops and courses (the space's FAQ). Accessibility is not stated.

FUNDING
Al Riwaq Art Space: no funder named; the site lists earned income lines (education, space rental, shop, cafe)
- The space publishes no sponsor list, annual report, accounts or board; its partnerships page offers venue hire for corporate events, private events and dinners with De'Part cafe, and its menu lists education, consultations, space rental and a shop.
- In a 2014 Wamda interview Kanoo described the work as "our duty," not about making money, and mentioned an extended period of overdraft among the challenges.
- No reporting found of a state, corporate or foundation funder behind the space or the Bank House move. The pages read do not say who owns the Bank House.

BOTTOM LINE
No funder is named anywhere, so no flagged money can be shown, and none was found. The site lists education, consultations, space rental and a shop as activities. The weak point is that nothing is published on who pays for the Bank House or the programmes. Worth watching: whether the space publishes a supporter list, and who owns the Bank House.
