THE MODEL: Sligo, Ireland
ID: the-model
Website: https://www.themodel.ie (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre and home of the Niland Collection at The Mall, Sligo (Model website).
- The company, now The Model Arts Niland Gallery CLG, was formerly called Model Arts and Niland Gallery; Wikipedia gives 2001 as the centre's establishment and says it was renamed in 2009 as "The Model, home of The Niland Collection". Photo Ireland's wiki traces its origins to a public meeting in the early 1990s.
- The Niland Collection was begun in the 1950s by the Sligo County librarian Nora Niland and holds more than 300 works, including a large body by Jack Butler Yeats (Wikipedia).
- Building: James Owen designed the Italianate stone building for the Board of Works as a model school, opened in 1862. Sligo County Council acquired it in the early 1990s; McCullough Mulvin Architects refurbished and extended it in 2000 to 2001, and a second project in 2008 to 2010 added about a third more floor space. It reopened on 1 May 2010 (Wikipedia, marked "citation needed").
- On site: galleries, artist studios, a performance space, a cafe, a gift shop and a bookshop (Model website; Photo Ireland). It runs exhibitions, music, film with Sligo Film Society, education and artists' residencies.
- Visiting: galleries open Tuesday to Saturday 11am to 5pm, Thursday until 8pm, Sunday 12pm to 4pm; gallery entry is free and events are ticketed. The site links to an accessibility page but the page read gives no details.

FUNDING
The Model: mainly public: Arts Council of Ireland funding and Sligo County Council, with project grants
- Arts Council: The Model was awarded EUR 345,000 under Arts Centre Funding 2025, announced in January 2025, part of a EUR 68.5 million national pot (Ocean FM, Catherine Gaffney, 10 January 2025).
- EUR 10,000 was awarded to The Model Arts and Niland Gallery in a July 2024 round of funding for community and voluntary organisations (Ocean FM, 21 July 2024). The article does not say which fund paid the sum.
- Sligo County Council: acquired the building in the early 1990s and borrowed EUR 6.4 million for the Sligo Cultural Quarter up to December 2010 (Wikipedia, whose funding figures carry a "citation needed" flag). Four board members are county councillors: Liam Brennan, Fergal Nealon, Arthur Gibbons and Declan Bree (Model governance page).
- Capital funding for the 2008 to 2010 redevelopment, as given by Wikipedia: a EUR 2.4 million ERDF grant (the same page also gives EUR 2.9 million), a EUR 1.75 million Arts Council grant and EUR 600,000 from Sligo Borough Council in 2009.
- Governance: a 15-member board chaired by Dr Roddy Quinn, with Noel McCrumlish as company secretary and financial officer; 2024 audited accounts, the constitution and a 2024 to 2028 strategic plan are linked on the page (Model governance page). The accounts file itself was not read.
- The Model says it follows the Arts Council's governance requirements. Funder logos appear on the website as an image with no names; no further funder names were found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is Irish public funding, chiefly the Arts Council and Sligo County Council, with a small grant. The weak points are that annual income and the funder split are not stated on the pages read, and four councillors sit on the board of a council-supported centre. Worth watching: the 2024 audited accounts, which would give the amounts.
