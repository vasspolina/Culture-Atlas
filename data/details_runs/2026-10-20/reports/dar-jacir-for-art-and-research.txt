DAR JACIR FOR ART AND RESEARCH: Bethlehem, Palestine
ID: dar-jacir-for-art-and-research
Website: https://www.darjacir.com (checked 2026-10-24; live; the Donors and Partners page could not be opened)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run centre for art, research and agricultural exchange in the Jacir family home in Area A of Bethlehem, on the Hebron to Jerusalem road, near the separation wall and Rachel's Tomb (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 2014 by Emily Jacir, with Annemarie Jacir and Yusuf Nasri Jacir listed as co-founders; its full name is Dar Yusuf Nasri Jacir for Art and Research. Emily Jacir and Aline Khoury co-direct it (Wikipedia).
- Named for Yusuf Nasri Jacir, whom Wikipedia lists as a co-founder; no source opened states how he is related to the family.
- Building: a 19th-century stone house; Art Asia Pacific and Art Basel give 1890, Wikipedia gives the late 1880s, built by al Mukhtar Yusuf Jacir. Renovation covered plumbing, rewiring and the water supply (Art Asia Pacific, 2017).
- On site: a residency programme begun in 2018, exhibitions, an urban farm, and an archive of tens of thousands of newspapers, photographs and documents from 1860 to 1950 (The Art Newspaper, 19 May 2021). Past residents include Coco Fusco, Isabella Hammad, Michael Rakowitz, Nicolas Jaar and Trevor Paglen (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: by appointment only (Dar Jacir website). No reporting found of admission or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Dar Jacir for Art and Research: mixed private and grant-funded: crowdfunding, foundation and Swedish aid grants, a British Council fund
- 2017 Kickstarter: raised nearly $24,000 in 24 hours against a $45,000 goal. Art Asia Pacific reported that Emily Jacir described the project as community-based and artist-run, with no international funding or oversight (25 May 2017).
- 2018: the A. M. Qattan Foundation announced a US$255,691 grant agreement via the Institute for Palestine Studies to establish the centre, under its "Visual Arts: A Flourishing Field" project, funded by the Consulate General of Sweden through Sida (Qattan Foundation, 10 March 2018).
- Arab Fund for Arts and Culture (AFAC) lists three projects: Training and Regional Events (2018), Visual Arts (2021) and Ecologies of Culture / Creative Placemaking (2026). Amounts are not stated on the page.
- British Council Cultural Protection Fund, with the UK culture department (DCMS): £99,855 for "Global Bethlehem: Securing the Archives of a Travelling Merchant," which Dar Jacir leads with the Khalidi Library as partner; start date March 2024 (Culture in Crisis project page).
- The Art Newspaper (19 May 2021) says the centre is supported by Open Society and by individual artists, including Michael Rakowitz. No amount or year is stated.
- After the 2021 raid an online fundraiser led by the Association for Modern and Contemporary Arab Art raised more than $30,000 against a $50,000 goal (Hyperallergic, June 2021).
- No reporting found of annual accounts, a board or a full donor list on the pages opened.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Raid: Israeli soldiers raided the centre on 15 May 2021, damaged the building and took phones, computers, hard drives, cameras and books, according to the centre. Hyperallergic (Hakim Bishara, 18 May 2021) reported the account and that it was unclear if the centre was specifically targeted; no Israeli army statement appears in it or in The Art Newspaper (Hadani Ditmars, 19 May 2021).
- Days earlier fires from Israeli projectiles destroyed the centre's Urban Farm (Hyperallergic).
- Israel does not fund or control the centre, so this is state action against it, not state patronage of it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage consists of sympathetic news reports on the 2017 crowdfunding and the 2021 raid, based on the centre's own statements.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Dar Jacir is clean: crowdfunding, a Qattan Foundation grant funded by Swedish aid, a UK government culture fund and Open Society, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no accounts, board or full donor list were found on the pages opened, and a 2017 statement of no international funding that sits beside the 2018 Sida-funded grant and later grants. Worth watching is the British Council archive project and AFAC's 2026 award.
