MUSEO DEL BARRO: Asunción, Paraguay
ID: museo-del-barro-asunción
Website: https://www.museodelbarro.org/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of popular, indigenous and contemporary art at Grabadores del Cabichuí 2716, between Cañada and Emeterio Miranda, in the Isla de Francia neighbourhood of Asunción. It is part of the Centro de Artes Visuales (CAV), which also holds the Museo de Arte Indígena and the Museo Paraguayo de Arte Contemporáneo (MPAC).
- The museum was created in 1979, the year construction of its first building began, and opened in San Lorenzo in 1980 with about 800 pieces of popular ceramics from Ita and Tobatí and Guaraní archaeological ceramics; Ysanne Gayet, Carlos Colombino and Osvaldo Salerno started the San Lorenzo opening, and it moved to Asunción in 1983 and settled permanently in the CAV in 1988 (museum history page).
- Olga Blinder and Carlos Colombino created the Colección Circulante in 1972, the origin of the MPAC collection. Ticio Escobar gathered and curated the collection behind the Museo de Arte Indígena (museum history page).
- A tornado hit the CAV in 1993 and it closed for about two and a half years for restoration; it reopened in 1995 (museum history page).
- The museum's holdings are more than 4,000 pieces of mestizo production from the 17th century onward; the programme includes guided visits, audio guides, the Seminario Espacio/Crítica and the shop "El almacén de doña Estela" (museum site).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday 14:00 to 20:00; admission G 50.000 Tuesday to Thursday and free on Friday and Saturday (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo del Barro: private foundation museum, with bank sponsors, an embassy and named individual friends
- The Fundación "Carlos Colombino Lailla", a non-profit civil entity constituted on 31 December 1987 and recognised by Decreto Nº 1035 of 9 June 1989, has the mission of providing premises for the Centro de Artes Visuales, which groups the Museo del Barro, the Museo de Arte Indígena and the Museo Paraguayo de Arte Contemporáneo (museum foundation page).
- Sponsors page: Banco Itaú is the sponsor; the supporters are González Acosta & Wood, the Embajada de Francia and Citibank; the Amigos del Museo are De La Sobera Hnos., Ana Barreto Valinotti, Raúl Antola, Enrique Goossen, Line Bareiro, Marisol Pecci, Paolo Pederzani, Daniel Mendonca, and Menchi Barriocanal and Oscar Acosta.
- Past funders named by the museum: the Swedish International Development Authority (ASDI) contributed to equipping the museum in 1988; the Spanish Agency for International Cooperation (AECI) contributed to building the Museo de Arte Indígena from 1992; German cooperation contributed to equipping the Gabinete "Florian Paucke" in 1996; the Cámara de Senadores gave contributions to the "Estudiantes al Museo" programme from 1995, and the programme stopped receiving subsidies in 2005; local and international, public and private contributions went to recovery after the 1993 tornado (museum history page).
- La Nación reported on 25 November 2023 that the museum and the Fundación Itaú invited the public to the opening of "Arte para (imaginar) un país", curated by Ticio Escobar and Osvaldo Salerno.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a private non-profit foundation, supported by Banco Itaú as sponsor, Citibank, the French embassy and González Acosta & Wood as supporters and named friends, and earlier by contributions from Swedish, Spanish and German cooperation agencies.
