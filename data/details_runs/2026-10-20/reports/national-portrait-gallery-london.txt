NATIONAL PORTRAIT GALLERY: London, UK

OVERVIEW
- National collection of portraits at St Martin's Place, London.
- Proposed by Philip Stanhope, 5th Earl Stanhope, in 1846 and opened in 1856 at 29 Great George Street; it moved to St Martin's Place in 1896 (Wikipedia).
- The St Martin's Place building was designed by Ewan Christian, with a 1933 wing funded by Lord Duveen and the 2000 Ondaatje Wing by Edward Jones and Jeremy Dixon (Wikipedia).
- The Inspiring People refurbishment, designed by Jamie Fobert and Purcell, closed the building from 2020 to June 2023; the project was set at 35.5 million pounds and Wikipedia gives its cost as 41 million.
- The Gallery welcomed 1.4 million visitors in 2025-26, 14 percent of them disabled people (annual report 2025-26). No reporting found of current hours or admission on the pages read.

FUNDING
National Portrait Gallery: mixed: government grant-in-aid, private gifts and corporate partners
- In 2025-26 total income was 32,374,000 pounds, including 11,009,000 pounds of grant-in-aid from the Department for Culture, Media and Sport, 9,911,000 pounds of fundraising and 5,012,000 pounds of trading (annual report 2025-26).
- Corporate Supporters: Beko, Bentley Motors and the Bentley Foundation, the CHANEL Culture Fund, Christie's, Gagosian, Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer (Portrait Award), Taylor Wessing (Photo Portrait Prize) and Viking. Corporate Members include Bank of America, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Deutsche Bank, Morgan Stanley, UBS and Rothschild & Co.
- Major gifts: the Blavatnik Family Foundation gave 10 million pounds in 2022, Randy Lerner 5 million in 2008, and the Heritage Lottery Fund 9.4 million toward the refurbishment (Wikipedia).
- The Bern Schwartz Family Foundation, the Huo Family Foundation and the Bukhman Foundation gave major support in 2025-26 (annual report).

Red flags: BP sponsorship of the Portrait Award (ended 2022)
- BP sponsored the Portrait Award from 1990 and sat on its jury for 23 years until 2020; the NPG and BP announced on 22 February 2022 that the deal would end in December 2022 (The Art Newspaper).
- In 2019 activists from BP or not BP? attached themselves to the main gates, and about 80 artists co-signed a letter against the deal (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).
- BP's UK head said the company must find new uses for its resources as it moves toward net zero by 2050; Culture Unstained said BP still invested in new oil and gas (The Art Newspaper, Culture Unstained is a campaign group).

Red flags: Sackler donation refused (2019)
- In March 2019 the Sackler Trust and the NPG "jointly agreed not to proceed" with a 1 million pound gift to the refurbishment, weeks after photographer Nan Goldin threatened to pull her retrospective (Museums Journal). The Trust said the allegations about OxyContin were vigorously denied.

Red flags: Blavatnik Family Foundation gift (oil money)
- Len Blavatnik built his fortune on Soviet-era privatisations; his AAR group bought 40 percent of Tyumen Oil, and Rosneft paid AAR 28 billion dollars in 2013 for its half of TNK-BP. Ukraine's president sanctioned him personally in December 2023 (Wikipedia). Blavatnik denies connections to Vladimir Putin.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The NPG exit followed those of Tate (2017), the Royal Shakespeare Company (2019) and the National Galleries of Scotland (2019) from BP (Hyperallergic).
- The Gallery is one of those institutions; BP still sponsors the British Museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Public grant-in-aid covers about a third of income; the rest is gifts and corporate support, and BP and the Sackler gift were ended or refused after pressure. The remaining weak point is the 10 million pound Blavatnik gift, from a donor with Russian oil money who has been sanctioned by Ukraine. Watch the current corporate list, which includes Viking and several banks.
