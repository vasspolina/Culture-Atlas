V&A: London, UK
ID: va-london
Website: https://www.vam.ac.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of art, design and performance. The main site, V&A South Kensington, is on Cromwell Road, London SW7.
- Traces its origins to the Government School of Design of 1832, with the museum listed as established in 1852. Earlier names: Museum of Manufactures, South Kensington Museum, Museum of Ornamental Art (Wikipedia).
- The Victoria and Albert Museum name was made public at the 1899 foundation-stone ceremony, laid by Queen Victoria (Wikipedia).
- The Aston Webb building, in red brick and Portland stone, was built 1899 to 1909 (Wikipedia).
- Other sites: V&A East Museum and Storehouse, Young V&A, V&A Dundee and the V&A Wedgwood Collection (V&A website). Wikipedia gives 3,332,300 visitors in 2025.
- Visiting: general admission is free; some exhibitions and events charge. South Kensington is open daily 10:00 to 17:45, Fridays until 22:00 (V&A website).

FUNDING
V&A: mainly public with large private giving: government grant from DCMS, donations, exhibition tickets
- The V&A is a non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (Wikipedia).
- Year to March 2023: total income fell from £131.7m to £121.4m. The DCMS grant was £67.5m (down from £78.3m) and donations and legacies £14.8m (up from £14.5m) (Civil Society). The 2024-25 annual report is on gov.uk, but the PDF could not be read as text, so no later split is given here.
- Blavatnik Family Foundation and Warner Music Group jointly gave £10 million in 2023 to V&A East for the David Bowie Centre (Blavatnik Family Foundation). The Blavatnik Family Foundation also gave to the Exhibition Road Quarter (V&A website).
- Named donors on completed projects (V&A website, no amounts): Europe 1600-1815 (2015): Heritage Lottery Fund, The Wolfson Foundation, Würth Group. Exhibition Road Quarter (2017): The Monument Trust, The Headley Trust, Garfield Weston Foundation. Cast Courts (2018): Garfield Weston Foundation, American Express. Photography Centre (2018): Sir Elton John and David Furnish.

Red flags: Sackler name and opioid money
- The Exhibition Road Courtyard and Centre for Arts Education carried the Sackler name. The Dr Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation is listed as a donor to the Exhibition Road Quarter (V&A website). The Sacklers owned Purdue Pharma, which made OxyContin; Purdue filed for bankruptcy in 2019 (The Art Newspaper).
- Dame Theresa Sackler was a V&A trustee from 2011 to 2019 (The Art Newspaper). The V&A and the family agreed the 2022 change together.
- On 16 November 2019 about 30 PAIN Sackler activists led by Nan Goldin held a die-in in the courtyard. In July 2019 director Tristram Hunt had said the V&A received "very generous support from the Sackler family" and "we remain grateful for that" (The Art Newspaper). After the protest he said "We are not going to be taking names down or denying the past" (Hyperallergic).
- On 3 October 2022 the V&A said the courtyard and centre "will no longer carry the Sackler name".

Red flags: Blavatnik family money
- Len Blavatnik's wealth comes from oil and gas, metals and petrochemicals, including a stake in Tyumen Oil (TNK) and 21% of LyondellBasell; Ukraine's president imposed personal sanctions on him in December 2023. Blavatnik denies connections to Putin (Wikipedia).
- His family foundation is listed as a donor to the Exhibition Road Quarter, completed in 2017, and gave £10 million with Warner Music Group in 2023.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism of the V&A is concentrated on the Sackler gift, from 2019 to 2022. No art-press criticism of the Blavatnik money was found.

BOTTOM LINE
The V&A is mainly publicly funded, with private gifts for capital projects. The documented weak points are the Sackler gift, which the museum defended in 2019 and ended in 2022, and Blavatnik family money from oil, gas and petrochemicals, including 2023 funding for V&A East. Worth watching: the donor lists for V&A East, and the 2024-25 accounts, which this research could not read.
