CONTOUR BIENNALE: Mechelen, Belgium
ID: contour-biennale-mechelen
Website: https://www.contourbiennale.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Biennale for moving image in Mechelen, Belgium, with its ticket office at Kunsthal Mechelen, Onder-Den-Toren 12 (stated by the space).
- Founded in 2003; it merged with the Mechelen arts centre nona in 2017, and nona ran it until 2023 (stated by the space, Kinoki).
- In spring 2023 nona ended the biennale; after a sector petition, the non-profit Contouristen, a group of Belgium-based curators, artists and cultural workers, revived it and has run it since 2024 (Sabzian, Kinoki, stated by the space).
- Past curators include Katerina Gregos (2009), Jacob Fabricius (2013), Natasha Ginwala (2017), Nataša Petrešin-Bachelez (2019) and Auguste Orts (2023) (stated by the space).
- The 11th edition, "The Eyes of the Heart and the Soul of the World", curated by Sofia Lemos, runs from 5 September to 1 November 2026 across five sites: Kunsthal Mechelen, dmvA, TACK TICK, De Noker and the Stiltetuin at the Archbishop's Palace (e-flux).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 13:00 to 18:00; tickets cost €14 (support ticket €25), €7 for students and €3 for UiTPAS, Paspartoe and European Disability Card holders; under-12s enter free (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Contour Biennale: mainly public: the Flemish Community and the City of Mechelen, plus foreign cultural institutes and foundations
- The Flemish Community funds the biennale; the City of Mechelen and Kunsthal Mechelen support it (e-flux, stated by the space).
- International partners and funders for 2026: the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation and its Delegation in France, Ágora Porto, the Mondriaan Fund, Acción Cultural Española, Institut Ramon Llull, the Generalitat de Catalunya, the Etxepare Basque Institute, Institut français, SAHA, the République et canton de Genève, and the embassies of Portugal, Spain, the Netherlands and France (stated by the space).
- Technical partners and sponsors include NH Hotels, Duvel and Yugen Kombucha (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of grant amounts from Flanders or Mechelen, or of filed accounts for Contouristen.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation)
- The Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation is a named 2026 international partner and funder (stated by the space).
- It was founded in 1956 under the will of Calouste Gulbenkian, an oil magnate; its endowment was €3.9 billion in 2017 (Wikipedia).
- It owned the oil company Partex until selling it to a Thai company in June 2019 (Wikipedia). The map counts the oil-built endowment as fossil fuel money.

Red flags: 2023 closure and sector petition
- In spring 2023 nona decided to end Contour and offered two exhibitions a year on a much smaller budget instead (Sabzian).
- A petition from artists, filmmakers, curators and producers said the decision was taken without consulting the artistic committee, which was dissolved, or co-producers (Sabzian).
- The petition said nona had won Flemish funding for its ten-year plan partly on the strength of Contour, then failed to invest in it (Sabzian).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. The main critique on record is the sector's 2024 petition against nona's handling of the biennale, reported by a film platform; no critical review of the 2026 edition was found.
1. Sabzian: "Petition for Contour Biennial of Moving Images" (2024)
URL: https://sabzian.be/news/petition-for-contour-biennial-of-moving-images
- After the 2017 merger, nona became disconnected from the biennale: gaps between editions grew to four years, no coordinator was appointed for years, and weak communication left low visitor numbers.
- Names nona, not the biennale's current organisers; it argues for keeping Contour alive.
2. Kinoki: David Djordjevic, "Contour Biennale returns after three-year hiatus" (2026)
URL: https://www.kinoki.co/contour-biennale-returns-after-three-year-hiatus/
- Puts the 2023 closure down to "conflicting visions" and nona's "estrangement from the biennale's original dedication to audiovisual arts".
- News report, not criticism of the new edition.

BOTTOM LINE
Contour runs mostly on Flemish and Mechelen public money and on foreign cultural institutes. The weak point is the Gulbenkian Foundation, whose endowment comes from an oil fortune, and a governance record that saw its former host close it in 2023. Worth watching: whether Contouristen publishes accounts and secures its own multi-year Flemish funding.
