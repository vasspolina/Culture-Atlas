ICA MIAMI: Miami, USA
ID: ica-miami
Website: https://icamiami.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 61 NE 41st Street in the Miami Design District, free every day.
- Began as the Institute of Contemporary Art within the Museum of Contemporary Art, North Miami, which opened it in 1996; the board sued the city in 2014 over zoning to move, and the institute established its current location in 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its type, the Institute of Contemporary Art; it is not named for a person.
- The Spanish firm Aranguren + Gallegos Arquitectos designed the three-storey building as a "Magic Box" (Wikipedia), with about 20,000 square feet of galleries and a 15,000 square foot sculpture garden; it was due to open on 1 December 2017 (Cultured). Miami Design District Associates, tied to the developer Craig Robins, donated the land (Cultured).
- In 2024 the museum bought the former De la Cruz Collection site for $25 million, adding 30,000 square feet of exhibition space (Wikipedia).
- Alex Gartenfeld is the Irma and Norman Braman Executive Director and Chief Curator.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 11 am to 6 pm, closed Monday and Tuesday (stated by the space).

FUNDING
ICA Miami: private: the Braman family, trustees and corporate and foundation partners
- Form 990 for the year to September 2025: revenue $15,857,598, of which $15,306,592 in contributions; expenses $12,383,397; total assets $79,593,146 (ProPublica).
- Norman and Irma Braman privately funded the building and sculpture garden, "singlehandedly" (Cultured). Irma Braman co-chairs the board with Ray Ellen Yarkin (stated by the space).
- Four partner logos on the About page carry the role labels Exhibitions Support, Free Admission Partner, Neighborhood Partner and Leadership Partner; the page text names none, and the logos link to Knight Foundation, City National, Miami Design District and Cartier (stated by the space). No dates or amounts are given.
- Dozens of South Florida philanthropists, including Robins, paid for the 2024 site purchase (Wikipedia).
- The support page names no donors and lists membership levels from $50 to $10,000.

Red flags: executive committee member Alex Witkoff and World Liberty Financial
- Alex Witkoff sits on the executive committee (stated by the space). He is a co-founder, with his brother Zach, of World Liberty Financial, a cryptocurrency firm Wikipedia calls "closely linked to the Trump family".
- Wikipedia says the firm has been criticised for its ability to benefit the president personally, citing NPR (7 May 2025), The New Yorker (26 May and 11 August 2025) and CBS News (11 June 2025); those pieces were not read here.
- Wikipedia says Witkoff was criticised for seeking Middle East government investment while his father negotiated with those governments as envoy, citing a New York Times investigation of 26 September 2025, which Democracy Now read on air on 3 October 2025. The Times piece was not read here.

Red flags: removal of Edward Said portrait
- The ICA removed Charles Gaines's "Faces 1: Identity Politics, #10, Edward Said" (2018) from his survey and later reinstalled it; it did not publicly address the removal or answer Hyperallergic's inquiries (Hyperallergic, 13 March 2024).
- Unverified: the work was removed over Said's Palestinian identity, from the advocacy group Art Against Displacement, via Hyperallergic, which did not confirm a motive.
- Hyperallergic notes Norman Braman's political support for Marco Rubio and his family foundation's funding of West Bank settlements, and does not connect either to the removal.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution; one Hyperallergic piece on the Said portrait. ARTnews and Miami New Times also covered it, but both were unreadable (blocked or paywalled).
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Questions Remain After ICA Miami Removes Portrait of Edward Said" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/876404/questions-remain-after-ica-miami-removes-portrait-of-edward-said/
- Timeline of the removal is unclear: absent in December 2023 per Miami New Times, not seen on 16 February 2024, reinstalled with its wall label missing.
- Reports Art Against Displacement's claim, from an anonymous tip, that the portrait was removed for Said's Palestinian identity; the reason for the removal stays unexplained.
- Names the ICA and the Bramans; names no trustees.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private and concentrated: the Bramans paid for the building and Irma Braman co-chairs the board. The weak points are Witkoff's seat alongside his crypto firm's political scrutiny and the unexplained removal of the Said portrait. Worth watching: the names and amounts behind the four partner logos, which the site does not state.
