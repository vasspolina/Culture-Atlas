BIENAL DE ARTES MEDIALES DE SANTIAGO: Santiago, Chile
ID: bienal-de-artes-mediales-de-santiago
Website: https://0.bienaldeartesmediales.cl (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Biennial of media arts in Santiago, run by the Corporación Chilena de Video y Artes Electrónicas (CChV). The 17th edition, "Hiperrealidades", ran from 9 to 26 October 2025, curated by the artist Nicolás Oyarce Carrión, with its listed events at the Centro Cultural Gabriela Mistral (GAM), Av. Libertador Bernardo O'Higgins 227.
- Created in 1993 by the CChV under the direction of the artist, theorist and teacher Néstor Olhagaray (1946 to 2020), as the "Primera Bienal de Video de Santiago". It continued the Festival Franco-Chileno de Videoarte (1981 to 1992), which the French Embassy in Chile created in 1981.
- Earlier names: Bienal de Video y Artes Electrónicas (1995), Bienal de Video y Nuevos Medios (1999), Bienal de Video y Artes Mediales (2009); it took its current name with the 11th edition in 2013, when Enrique Rivera became director.
- The 1993 edition included the Concurso Internacional Juan Downey, which the CChV still runs.
- Programme in 2025: electronic art in everyday life, with machines, domestic robots and mechanised toys, immersive installations, performances and virtual reality, including Iván Navarro's "Penumbra" (11 to 26 October).
- Visiting: GAM hours for "Penumbra" were Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 21:00 and Sunday 10:00 to 20:00. The page read does not say whether entry is free, gives no prices, and does not cover accessibility.

FUNDING
Bienal de Artes Mediales de Santiago: public: Chile's Ministry of Cultures programmes, with donations
- The CChV's transparency page lists as its funders the Ministry of Cultures, Arts and Heritage, through its Support Program for Collaborating Cultural Organizations and its Audiovisual Promotion Fund. The ministry is the only logo under "Financia" on the CChV home page.
- The CChV also says it raises money through grant applications, co-production alliances and a donation campaign.
- The CChV posts annual reports and balance sheets for 2018 to 2025 and a list of resolutions for 2017 to 2026 (CChV transparency page); the amounts were not opened for this report.
- Directorate: Luz María Sorolla (Secretaria General), Valentina Montero (Vicepresidenta) and Manuela Ossa (Tesorera); Catalina Ossa is Executive Director.
- No reporting found of donor names, corporate partners or amounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the biennial looks clean: it is Chilean state programme funding plus donations, and its organiser publishes annual reports and balances. The weak points are that donor names and amounts are not given on the pages read, and that the ministry is the only logo under "Financia" on the CChV site. Worth watching are the CChV balance sheets for the size of state funding against private donations.
