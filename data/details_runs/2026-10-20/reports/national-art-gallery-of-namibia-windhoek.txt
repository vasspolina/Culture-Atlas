NATIONAL ART GALLERY OF NAMIBIA: Windhoek, Namibia
ID: national-art-gallery-of-namibia-windhoek
Website: https://nagn.org.na/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- State-owned art gallery in central Windhoek, at the corner of Robert Mugabe Avenue and John Meinert Street (NAGN site).
- Founded in 1990 (Wikipedia). Its site calls it "Namibia's premier institution for the collection, preservation, and promotion of visual arts and crafts" (stated by the gallery).
- Permanent exhibition of Namibian, African and European art, plus exhibitions by local artists (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the building's architect or earlier use.
- Hosts the Bank Windhoek Triennial, "Namibia's biggest visual art competition" (NAGN's words); the 2025/2026 theme is "Namibian Identity: The Linework of a Country's Contours and Wealth", with a N$50,000 first prize and a solo exhibition at the gallery, a launch on 6 August 2026 and a submission deadline of 27 May 2026 (NAGN triennial page).
- Leadership: Wikipedia lists Michelle Olga van Wyk as CEO, Snobia Kaputu as CEO from 2017 to 2022 and Hercules Viljoen as director from 2012 to 2017.
- Visiting: Monday 14:00 to 17:00, Tuesday to Friday 08:00 to 17:00, Saturday 09:00 to 14:00, closed Sunday. No admission fee or accessibility information is listed on the pages read.

FUNDING
National Art Gallery of Namibia: public: a state-owned gallery, with Bank Windhoek as partner of its triennial
- State-owned (NAGN site, Wikipedia). No reporting found of the size of the government grant: the 2023/24 and 2024/25 annual report PDFs exceeded the reading tool's size limit.
- Bank Windhoek hosts the 2025/2026 Triennial in partnership with the gallery; the page states prizes of N$50,000, N$35,000 and N$25,000 but not who pays them or the bank's amount (NAGN triennial page).
- No reporting found of other sponsors on the pages read. The annual reports for 2023/24 and 2024/25 could not be read.

Red flags: 2014 exhibition withdrawn over photographs of Himba girls
- March 2014: the gallery objected to photographs of what the excerpts call underage girls in Paolo Solari Bozzi's show based on his book "Namibia Sun Pictures", which was set to open on 3 March 2014. The gallery contended the girls were too young to consent to being photographed and tried to negotiate with him to withdraw those photographs; he withdrew the whole series instead. This account rests on search-result excerpts of ARTICLE 19 and the Censorpedia wiki of the National Coalition Against Censorship; both pages returned 403 when opened, and no news report of the episode was found.
- The excerpts say the gallery denied that this was censorship, saying the decision to withdraw the whole series was the artist's (stated by the gallery), and that critics noted the arbitrary nature of its request. Both statements come from the same excerpts.
- No reporting found of a boycott or protest aimed at the gallery.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The 2014 episode is recorded by free-expression groups, not by art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery's money is Namibian state funding with Bank Windhoek attached to the triennial; no funder with a documented controversy was found. The weak points are the 2014 withdrawal of a photography show, which the gallery says was the artist's choice and which is documented only in excerpts of two free-expression group pages, and the lack of readable income figures. Worth watching: the amount of the state grant and Bank Windhoek's share in the annual reports.
