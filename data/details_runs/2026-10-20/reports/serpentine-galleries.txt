SERPENTINE GALLERIES: London, UK
ID: serpentine-galleries
Website: https://www.serpentinegalleries.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Two contemporary art galleries in Kensington Gardens, Westminster, London, about five minutes' walk apart and linked by a bridge over the Serpentine Lake: Serpentine South and Serpentine North (Wikipedia).
- Serpentine South opened in 1970 in a Grade II listed former tea pavilion built in 1933 and 1934; Serpentine North opened in 2013 as the Serpentine Sackler Gallery and was renamed in 2021. It occupies The Magazine, a Grade II* listed gunpowder store built in 1805, with an extension by Zaha Hadid Architects (Wikipedia).
- Every year since 2000 the Serpentine has commissioned a temporary summer pavilion from an architect (Wikipedia). The 2026 Pavilion is by LANZA atelier of Mexico City and stands at Serpentine South from 6 June to 25 October 2026 (Serpentine).
- Both galleries are free and open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm (Serpentine).
- Bettina Korek is CEO and Hans Ulrich Obrist is artistic director; the galleries draw up to 1.2 million visitors a year (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Serpentine Galleries: mainly private: individual donors, corporate supporters and trusts, with a small public grant
- The Serpentine is a UK registered charity, the Serpentine Trust, number 298809 (OpenCharities). Its site says it receives 5% of its annual income from Arts Council England and the remaining 95% from individual donors, corporate supporters, charitable trusts and earned income (Serpentine).
- Charity Commission figures: income of £14,756,643 and expenditure of £15,553,882 in the year to 31 March 2025 (OpenCharities).
- Michael R. Bloomberg is chair of the trustees, appointed 14 January 2014 (OpenCharities). Thirteen other trustees are listed, including Barry Townsley, Maja Hoffman, Megha Mittal and Robert Rosenkranz (OpenCharities).
- Serpentine Pavilion: Goldman Sachs supports the Pavilion for the 12th consecutive year (Serpentine, 2026 Pavilion press release).

Red flags: Sackler name removed from the second gallery
- The Serpentine Sackler Gallery was named for the Sackler family after a £5.5 million donation in 2013 from a foundation run by Theresa and Mortimer Sackler (The Art Newspaper, 25 March 2021).
- In 2019 the Serpentine announced it had "no future plans" to accept Sackler funding (The Art Newspaper, 25 March 2021).
- By 25 March 2021 the gallery had removed the Sackler name from its website and exhibition archive and called the space Serpentine North, while the name remained on the building's facade. The gallery denied the change was tied to the Sackler family's controversy and called it part of a "rebranding process"; a spokeswoman said it had "recently introduced new way-finding terminology" to help visitors tell the two galleries apart (The Art Newspaper, 25 March 2021). Purdue Pharma had filed for bankruptcy on 15 March 2021.
- The Sackler name stayed above the main entrance until early 2022 (The Art Newspaper, José da Silva, 12 January 2022). A spokesperson said the gallery was "not in a position to remove permanent signage from the building at that time", that the "rebrand was planned in phases" and that the removal was "the result of discussions" with the board; the Sackler foundation was consulted before the removal, and the gallery did not explicitly say the removal was due to the controversies.
- The article adds that the Serpentine had been criticised for not distancing itself from the name sooner, including by exhibiting artist Hito Steyerl (The Art Newspaper, 12 January 2022).

BOTTOM LINE
The Serpentine is a charity funded mainly by private donors, corporate supporters and trusts, with about 5% of income from Arts Council England; Michael Bloomberg chairs the trustees and Goldman Sachs supports the annual Pavilion. The documented weak point is the 2013 Sackler foundation gift of £5.5 million that led to the gallery being named for the Sackler family, whose name the gallery removed from its website in 2021 and from the building in early 2022, after saying in 2019 it had "no future plans" to accept Sackler funding.
