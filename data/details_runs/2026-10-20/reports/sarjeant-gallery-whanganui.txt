SARJEANT GALLERY: Whanganui, New Zealand
ID: sarjeant-gallery-whanganui
Website: https://sarjeant.org.nz/information/

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery, officially Te Whare o Rehua Sarjeant Gallery, at 4 Pukenamu Drive in Pukenamu Queen's Park in the centre of Whanganui.
- Founded through the bequest of Henry Sarjeant, who in 1912 left a sum the gallery puts at the equivalent of over $70 million in today's terms; the gallery first opened to the public on 6 September 1919 (the gallery's website).
- Holds a collection of more than 8,000 items covering about four centuries of European and New Zealand art.
- The 105-year-old heritage-listed building, with a 14-metre-high dome and Oamaru stone exterior, was earthquake-strengthened and restored. A new wing, Te Pātaka o Tā Te Atawhai Archie John Taiaroa, was led by Warren and Mahoney, with visual narratives co-designed with Te Kāhui Toi o Tūpoho, the artist group appointed by Te Rūnanga o Tūpoho. A carved totara waka walk bridge links the two buildings.
- The redevelopment doubled the gallery's size and added a temperature and humidity-controlled storage area, workshops, a photographic studio, a shop and a café. The gallery reopened on 9 November 2024 after four years.
- Cost: the final cost was $79.4 million against an original 2019/2020 budget of $55.06 million, an overspend of $19.67 million. The project ran 21 months longer than planned; two-thirds of the increased costs related to the existing building, ground conditions, COVID-19 and design variations (Moana Ellis, Local Democracy Reporting, via Awa FM, 31 July 2025).
- Visiting: open every day except Christmas Day, Good Friday and after 1pm on ANZAC Day; gallery 10:00 to 17:00, café 10:00 to 16:00, library and reading room Friday and Saturday 13:00 to 16:00. Entry is free and donations are appreciated. The main entrance is level, there is a public lift, and two wheelchairs are available free.

FUNDING
Sarjeant Gallery (Whanganui): public and charitable: Whanganui District Council, central government and trusts
- The redevelopment cost $79.4 million. Whanganui District Council paid $26.08 million, about $26.1 million (Whanganui District Council and Local Democracy Reporting, 31 July 2025). The gallery puts grants and fundraising at $53.3 million and says nearly $11 million was donated by community and private trusts and individuals.
- Funding sources named by the gallery: Whanganui District Council, Ministry for Culture and Heritage, the Significant Projects Fund (Department of Internal Affairs), the New Zealand Lottery Grants Board, the Provincial Growth Fund (via the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment), public and private trusts, and individual donations.
- In an 18 October media release from the Sarjeant Gallery Trust: a $10 million Ministry for Culture and Heritage grant and $6 million of Lottery Board funding approved earlier that year, both conditional on the Trust raising a further $2.2 million by 20 December; and a $600,000 grant from the Stout Trust, managed by Perpetual Guardian. A media release of 15 June 2017 mentions a $10,000 grant from the Whanganui law firm Treadwell Gordon.
- The gallery's partners page lists, under redevelopment partners and supporters: principal funder Kānoa; supporters Whanganui District Council, Te Runanga O Tupoho, Manatū Taonga, Four Regions Trust, Whanganui Community Foundation, Lotto New Zealand and New Zealand Community Trust.
- Other partners listed on the same page: Whanganui District Council, Craigs Investment Partners, Creative NZ, Clearview Estate, Brews on Drews, H&A Print and the Whanganui Chronicle. A gallery blog post of 2 September 2026 says the exhibition "Ināianei | Current", opening in November 2026, is sponsored by Craigs Investment Partners Whanganui.
- Naming rights for up to 10 spaces were offered in the redeveloped gallery, with premier spaces priced up to $1 million (media release, 18 October).

BOTTOM LINE
Whanganui District Council paid $26.1 million of the $79.4 million rebuild; the gallery puts grants and fundraising at about $53.3 million, of which nearly $11 million came from community and private trusts and individuals. The rebuild ran $19.67 million over its original budget, with two-thirds of the increase tied to the existing building, ground conditions, COVID-19 and design variations according to Local Democracy Reporting.
