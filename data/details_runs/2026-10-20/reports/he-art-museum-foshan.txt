HE ART MUSEUM: Foshan, China
ID: he-art-museum-foshan
Website: https://hem.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private non-profit art museum (HEM) at 6 Yixing Road, Beijiao New Town, Shunde district, Foshan, Guangdong, opposite the headquarters of the appliance maker Midea (e-flux, Al Jazeera).
- Founded by He Jianfeng and his father He Xiangjian, founder of Midea Group; it opened on 1 October 2020 after a planned March opening was delayed by Covid-19. Shao Shu, formerly of Shanghai's Long Museum, was founding director (ArtAsiaPacific).
- The name He (和) is the founder's family name and means "harmony, balance and mixture" (Wikipedia).
- Tadao Ando, commissioned in 2015, designed the RMB 220 million (USD 31 million) concrete building of 16,000 m², with 8,000 m² of exhibition space and a double-helix staircase (ArtAsiaPacific, Wikipedia).
- The He family collection of over 500 works covers modern and contemporary Chinese and international art, with an emphasis on Lingnan School ink painting, plus works by Zao Wou-Ki and Yayoi Kusama.
- In 2020 it opened Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm, closed Monday, with online booking and free admission on the 20th of each month (e-flux); admission was RMB 150 (ArtAsiaPacific).

FUNDING
He Art Museum: family-funded: the He family's money, made from Midea Group
- The museum is family-established and privately funded (e-flux, Wikipedia); the family runs it (Al Jazeera).
- The family fortune comes from Midea Group. He Xiangjian set up the He Xiangjian Charity Foundation in December 2013 with RMB 400 million, renamed He Charity Foundation in 2017, and pledged 100 million Midea shares and RMB 2.4 billion in cash to it (stated by Midea).
- No reporting found of the museum's budget, of which family body pays for it, or of any corporate sponsor or state funder.

Red flags: Midea factories in the Uyghur labour-transfer scheme
- The Bureau of Investigative Journalism, with The New York Times and Der Spiegel, reported on 29 May 2025 that factories directly owned by big brands, "like those run by Midea and LG Electronics", took part in the Chinese government programme that moves Uyghur, Kazakh and Kyrgyz workers from Xinjiang to eastern factories (TBIJ, Daniel Murphy).
- A plastics factory filmed by one of the workers in the investigation has supplied Midea (TBIJ).
- Midea did not respond to several requests for comment (TBIJ).
- The museum's founding money is Midea money, so the map counts it as that company's money.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage is admiring and centres on Ando's building and the collection; Al Jazeera reported commentators largely praised the choice of an industrial city.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on the He family's Midea fortune. The weak point is Midea itself: a 2025 investigation reported Midea-run factories took part in the Uyghur labour-transfer programme, and Midea did not answer. Worth watching: any disclosure of which family body pays the museum's costs.
