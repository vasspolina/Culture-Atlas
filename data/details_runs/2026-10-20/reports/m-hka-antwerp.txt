M HKA: Antwerp, Belgium
ID: m-hka-antwerp
Website: https://www.muhka.be/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Contemporary Art Antwerp, Belgium's oldest contemporary art museum, run by the Flemish Community (Wikipedia, ArtReview).
- Founded in 1982 by the Flemish Community; Wikipedia's infobox gives 20 September 1985 as the establishment date. Flor Bex was the first director, until 1992, and Bart de Baere led it from 2002 (Wikipedia).
- Building: architect Michel Grandsard converted an old grain store in 1987 and designed an extension in 1997 (Wikipedia).
- On site: a permanent collection of Belgian and international contemporary art, an arthouse cinema and a library (Wikipedia). ArtReview puts the collection at about 8,000 objects.
- Visiting: from 19 October to 12 November 2026 entry is free while the Lee Bul exhibition is installed, with the second floor closed. The small lift is out of order, so floors 1, 3 and 5 are not accessible by wheelchair or buggy. Lee Bul opens to the public on 13 November (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
M HKA: public: the Flemish Community, with a Russian gas-linked foundation among its partners
- The Flemish government funds the museum. In October 2025 it cancelled the planned new building, put at 130 million euros by The Art Newspaper (Charles Esche) and at 80 million euros by ArtReview, and announced that the museum would lose museum status and move its roughly 8,000 objects to S.M.A.K. in Ghent by 2028 (ArtReview).
- ArtReview reports that on 4 February 2026 the government revoked the plan and M HKA keeps its museum status. The museum says it is working on a transition plan for 2026 to 2028 after the decision to cancel the new building.
- Its partners page lists Allen & Overy, Klara, V-A-C, De Olifant and De Standaard, with no years or amounts and no clear split between categories (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of annual grant figures.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, V-A-C Foundation
- V-A-C, listed on the partners page, was founded in 2009 by Leonid Mikhelson (Wikipedia). He founded Novatek, which produces petroleum and natural gas, and chairs its management board (Wikipedia, Novatek).
- M HKA and V-A-C set up Present Continuous, an annual scheme for new Russian artists; the 2020 winner's work, "Co-op Sunrise", joins the M HKA collection (Ensembles, M HKA).
- Novatek was placed on the US Treasury's Sectoral Sanctions Identification list on 16 July 2014, and Mikhelson was named in a CAATSA report to the US Congress on 29 January 2018 (Wikipedia, Novatek). The UK sanctioned him in 2022 (Wikipedia, Leonid Mikhelson).
- No reporting found of M HKA ending or pausing the V-A-C link, or of a campaign aimed at that link.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Heavy criticism since October 2025, aimed at the Flemish government's plan to dissolve the museum, not at the museum or its funders.
1. ArtReview: unsigned, "Museum professionals protest plans to asset-strip Antwerp's M HKA" (2025, updated 2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/museum-professionals-protest-plans-to-asset-strip-antwerps-m-hka/
- Museum Watch (CIMAM) called the decision "potentially disastrous" for Antwerp; board members Vasıf Kortun and Eugene Tan resigned.
- Argues the plan was imposed without consultation and treats a collection as a pile of objects. M HKA's leaders cited "flagrant illegalities".
- Criticises the government, not the institution.
2. The Art Newspaper: Charles Esche, "The sorry story of Antwerp museum's dissolution serves as a warning to all" (2026)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/01/06/comment-%7C-the-sorry-story-of-antwerp-museums-dissolution-serves-as-a-warning-to-all
- Calls M HKA a "development laboratory" and blames the Flemish government and Culture Minister Caroline Gennez.
- Says the existing building and storage were already inadequate, so protest could only restore a poor situation.
- Names no private funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is publicly funded, and its main risk has been its own government's attempt to dissolve it, withdrawn in February 2026 according to ArtReview. The weak point on money is V-A-C: a foundation founded by the chairman of the gas producer Novatek, who the UK sanctioned in 2022, still listed as a partner. Watch whether V-A-C stays on the partners page and what the 2026 to 2028 transition plan funds.
