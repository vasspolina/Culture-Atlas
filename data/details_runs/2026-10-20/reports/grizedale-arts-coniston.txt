GRIZEDALE ARTS: Coniston, UK
ID: grizedale-arts-coniston
Website: https://www.grizedale.org/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts residency and commissioning agency based at Lawson Park, a hill farm above Coniston Water in the Lake District (Wikipedia, Grizedale Arts website).
- Roots in the Grizedale Society, founded in 1968 by Forestry Commission head forester Bill Grant, which ran a theatre and the Grizedale Sculpture Trail. Adam Sutherland became director in 1999 and merged the Society into the new Grizedale Arts that year (Wikipedia).
- Moved to Lawson Park in 2007; the farm was refurbished by Sutherland Hussey Architects as an artists' residency and office base and opened in 2009 (Wikipedia). The farm was first recorded under the ownership of Furness Abbey in the 13th century, and John Ruskin purchased the farmhouse and land in the late 19th century (Wikipedia, Lawson Park).
- It had an office in the Coniston Institute from 2012 to 2023 and helped redevelop the village hub, with projects such as the Honest Shop and a revived library (Wikipedia).
- In 2020 it bought The Farmer's Arms, a Grade II listed pub in the Lake District National Park, as a mixed-use pub, B&B, workshop and community arts space; Wikipedia says it reopened in 2021 (Architectural Heritage Fund, Wikipedia).
- Former residency artists Jeremy Deller (2004) and Mark Wallinger (2007) won the Turner Prize, and Grizedale produced Laure Prouvost's "Wantee" for the 2013 Turner Prize (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Lawson Park Open Tuesdays, booked through Eventbrite; meadow volunteering days run in summer (Grizedale Arts website).

FUNDING
Grizedale Arts: public support and grants: Arts Council England and South Lakeland District Council, with loan finance and donations
- The website says the organisation is "made possible by the ongoing support of Arts Council England and South Lakeland District Council".
- The Farmer's Arms purchase in 2020: a £400,000 Architectural Heritage Fund loan covering the purchase, working capital and fit-out, and an £8,100 viability grant (Architectural Heritage Fund).
- The Farmer's Arms appeal: Grizedale put up 25% of the cost from its own reserves and sought to raise £300,000 through donations and a three-tier Founding Benefactor scheme (The Art Newspaper, Louisa Buck, 26 November 2020). Wikipedia says the pub was bought with the support of investors and donors.
- Wikipedia also lists the King's Coronation Orchard Fund (a community orchard in 2024), the Royal Countryside Fund (a vending machine in the pub car park) and a membership scheme, the Grizedale Arts Club, launched in 2024.

BOTTOM LINE
Grizedale Arts says its work is made possible by the ongoing support of Arts Council England and South Lakeland District Council. The 2020 Farmer's Arms purchase was financed by a £400,000 Architectural Heritage Fund loan, its own reserves and donor appeals.
