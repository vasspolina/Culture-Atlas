HORTA MUSEUM: Brussels, Belgium
ID: horta-museum
Website: https://hortamuseum.be/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum in Victor Horta's former house (no. 25) and studio (no. 23) on Rue Américaine in Saint-Gilles, Brussels, with its reception, offices, archives, shop and temporary exhibition space in the neighbouring building at no. 27, built by Jules Brunfaut around 1903 to 1904 and renovated by the architects OZON (Cercle Guimard). Brussels Museums gives the address as Rue Américaine 27.
- Horta built the house and studio from 1898 to 1901 and sold both in 1919 after spending most of the First World War in England and the United States (Cercle Guimard; Brussels Museums).
- At the urging of a small group of Horta admirers, the commune of Saint-Gilles bought the house in 1961; house and studio were listed two years later, and the museum opened in 1969, which the Cercle Guimard calls the first museum in the world devoted from cellar to attic to one architect (Cercle Guimard).
- Named for Victor Horta. The house is a UNESCO World Heritage site (VRT); four Horta town houses were listed in 2000 (The Art Newspaper).
- The interiors keep their mosaics, stained glass and wall decoration, and the museum holds Horta's archives and a reference library on Art Nouveau (Brussels Museums). Horta designed the furniture and lamps himself (The Art Newspaper).
- Visitors rose from 2,000 in 1970 to 65,000 in 2015 (Cercle Guimard).
- Staff named by The Art Newspaper: Elisabeth Horth, head of historical heritage, who oversees the restoration, and Tom Packet, art historian in charge of the archive.
- 2026 works: facades on street and garden, window frames, damp and corroded wrought iron, and the dining room and veranda, with the first phase due to end in November 2026; a second phase in 2029 is to open the garden and refit the studio (VRT, 2 February 2026). The house stays open during the works (The Art Newspaper, 2 September 2026).
- Visiting: closed Mondays and 1 January; Tuesday to Friday 14:00 to 17:30, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:30. Free on the first Sunday of the month; the Art Nouveau Pass and museumPASSmusées are accepted. Brussels Museums lists accessible parking and access with assistance for visitors with reduced mobility.

FUNDING
Horta Museum: mainly public: the commune of Saint-Gilles owns and runs it, with Brussels public bodies, the Fonds Baillet Latour and an unnamed patron behind the 2026 works
- Owner: the commune of Saint-Gilles bought the house in 1961, the studio in 1971 and no. 27 in 2007 (Cercle Guimard).
- The 2007 purchase cost 1,250,000 euros plus 2,150,000 euros for studies and restoration, made possible by Beliris, the cooperation body of the federal state and the Brussels-Capital Region (Cercle Guimard).
- 2026 renewal of 800,000 euros: VRT names Urban Brussels, the commune of Saint-Gilles, the Fonds Baillet Latour, the museum's own resources and a new patron who is not named. The Art Newspaper credits the commune alone.
- The Fonds Baillet Latour also paid for the restoration of the draughtsmen's room, reopened in 2020 (La Libre Belgique, 3 July 2020).
- Where the Fonds Baillet Latour money comes from: Fonds Baillet Latour SC held 5,485,415 shares in the brewer AB InBev, 0.28 per cent of the voting rights, on 31 December 2022, and is bound to vote with AB InBev's reference shareholder (AB InBev statutory board report, 2022).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: mainly public money and a foundation that holds AB InBev shares. The weak point is the anonymous private gift to the 2026 works.
