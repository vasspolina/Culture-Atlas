MUSEO TAMAYO: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-tamayo
Website: https://www.museotamayo.org (checked 2026-10-24; a notice says the museum is closed for installation until 9 November)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the Primera Sección of Chapultepec Park, at Paseo de la Reforma 51, Mexico City, on land the federal government ceded from the former Campo Azteca golf course (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Founded by the artist Rufino Tamayo, who bought international contemporary art from the late 1960s and donated it for a museum; it opened on 29 May 1981 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Named for Rufino Tamayo (Museo Rufino Tamayo).
- Designed by Teodoro González de León and Abraham Zabludovsky, with design starting in 1972 and construction in 1979. An expansion closed the museum from August 2011 to August 2012 and grew the building by about 30% (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; entry $95, free for children, students, teachers, seniors, people with disabilities and everyone on Sundays. Ramps and an elevator reach all levels.

FUNDING
Museo Tamayo: mixed: federal state museum (INBAL) with the Tamayo foundation and corporate sponsors
- Public: five years after opening, in 1986, the museum passed to the national patrimony under the Instituto Nacional de Bellas Artes (INBAL) (Spanish Wikipedia). A 2009 expansion was carried out by INBAL, CONACULTA and the Fundación Olga y Rufino Tamayo (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Founding money: it opened in 1981 with sponsorship from Grupo Alfa and Fundación Cultural Televisa (Spanish Wikipedia). Terremoto (2021) says tension with Televisa's owner Emilio Azcárraga ended this early arrangement.
- Foundation: Olga and Rufino Tamayo created the Fundación Olga y Rufino Tamayo, A.C. on 31 October 1989. Its page names a board chaired by Alejandra Servitje Labarrere, with Jimena Franco Méndez as director, an International Council and a benefactor group called the Círculo Contemporáneo (stated by the foundation).
- Corporate sponsors shown as undated logos on the foundation page, which the page does not caption (names read from image filenames): Casa Dragones, Grupo Habita, Bloomberg, Aeroméxico, AXA, FEMSA, Contramar and others. No amounts are published.
- No reporting found of annual accounts or the size of the federal budget line.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's funding. The two pieces found concern its handling of the dog performance Tragedy and the role of private fundraising.
1. The Art Newspaper: María del Carmen Barrios Giordano and Edgar Alejandro Hernández, "The limits of transnational women's solidarity in Mexico City's art scene" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/11/25/mexico-city-museums-censorhip-womens-solidarity
- Describes Nina Beier's Tragedy (2011), five dogs playing dead on Persian rugs, shown in her exhibition Casts (23 May to 29 September). Animal-rights activists protested at the entrance, the mayor commented, and a local zoning and environmental office opened an investigation into the use of live animals as entertainment.
- Calls the museum's response censorship: it cancelled all further performances three days after the exhibition opened, though they had been scheduled for the full run.
- Argues the museum did not foresee how social media would strip the work of context and that its handling should not become a model.
2. Terremoto: no byline shown, "OTRXS MUNDXS frente a la reafirmación institucional" (2021)
URL: https://terremoto.mx/en/?p=44964
- Says the museum was founded with private money (Grupo Alfa and Televisa) and kept close ties to the private sector after joining INBAL in 1986; its 2019 director Magalí Arriola sought budgets from sources beyond government and exhibitions that can be exported, citing the Museo Jumex.
- Praises the inclusion of emerging artists in OTRXS MUNDXS but criticises the show for reinforcing Mexico City's centralism.
- Mainly criticises an exhibition and the institution's direction; does not name any current sponsor.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bloomberg Philanthropies was the target of a London protest in January 2025: more than 20 artists walked out of the New Contemporaries preview at the ICA on 14 January, and protesters led by the UK wing of the Gaza Biennale, supported by the Palestinian Youth Movement, called for the ICA to cut ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies, the sponsor of New Contemporaries. Unverified: the protest group said Bloomberg backs initiatives in Israel and helps build settlement infrastructure in the West Bank. Bloomberg did not respond to a request for comment. The ICA said it had received no Bloomberg funding since 2020 (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 16 January 2025).
- Connection: Bloomberg is shown among Museo Tamayo's corporate sponsor logos. No reporting found of a protest or campaign aimed at Bloomberg's money at Museo Tamayo.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a federal institute plus the founders' foundation and a short list of undated corporate logos, with no published accounts or amounts. The weak point is Bloomberg, the target of a 2025 protest at a London institution but not, in any reporting found, at this museum.
