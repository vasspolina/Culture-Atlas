KUNSTMUSEUM WOLFSBURG: Wolfsburg, Germany
ID: kunstmuseum-wolfsburg-wolfsburg
Website: https://www.kunstmuseum.de/ (checked 2026-10-24; the site redirects to its ticket shop, which says the website is temporarily unavailable)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art in the centre of Wolfsburg, on the Hollerplatz (German Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1994 with a retrospective of Fernand Léger. Directors: Gijs van Tuyl until 2004, Markus Brüderlin from 2006 until his death in 2014, Ralf Beil from 1 February 2015 to December 2018, Andreas Beitin from 1 April 2019 (German Wikipedia; Tagesspiegel).
- Building by the Hamburg firm Schweger & Partner: a glass-roofed "urban loggia" over the open Hollerplatz, a central hall 40 by 40 metres and 3,500 square metres of exhibition space. A Japanese garden was added in 2007 (German Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibition halls, the collection, and educational programmes; a Freundeskreis (friends' association) supports it, including a "Young Generation" programme (German Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the ticket shop lists Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 18:00 and Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00. It lists no admission prices.

FUNDING
Kunstmuseum Wolfsburg: corporate-linked foundation: the Kunststiftung Volkswagen, built on the Holler estate, with a board chaired by the Volkswagen supervisory board chair
- The museum is financed by the non-profit Kunststiftung Volkswagen (English Wikipedia; Tagesspiegel calls it the museum's Träger, or sponsor).
- A large share of the foundation's funds comes from the foundation of Asta and Christian Holler, former owners of the Volkswagen insurance service company VVD. Christian Holler lived 1900 to 1969, Asta Holler 1904 to 1989; the Holler foundation has sent the museum a large share of its payouts since 1991 (English Wikipedia).
- Hans Dieter Pötsch, chairman of the Volkswagen Group supervisory board, is also chairman of the foundation's board of trustees (e-mobility plus, 24 May 2024). Volkswagen was a major founding partner of the museum (same source).
- No reporting found of annual budget figures or amounts from any funder.

Red flags: Volkswagen's role
- On 11 January 2017 Volkswagen agreed to plead guilty to three felony counts over diesel emissions defeat devices in about 590,000 vehicles sold in the US, with a US$2.8 billion criminal penalty and US$1.5 billion in civil resolutions (US Environmental Protection Agency).

Red flags: director dismissed before an oil exhibition
- In December 2018 the board of trustees of the Kunststiftung Volkswagen revoked Ralf Beil's appointment. Beil's contract would have run to 31 January 2020; the museum said he did not agree to a mutual termination, and ended the contract on 30 June 2019 with pay continuing (Monopol; Tagesspiegel, 14 December 2018).
- Beil wrote that the artistic freedom for his work was "apparently no longer given" at the museum (Monopol, in German, my translation).
- He had planned "Oil. Schönheit und Schrecken des Erdölzeitalters" for autumn 2019, a critical look at the car age. Monopol and Tagesspiegel state it is unclear whether this caused the dismissal. The show opened on 4 September 2021 with Beitin among its curators.
- Unverified: that the Volkswagen supervisory board forced the dismissal because of his critical exhibitions, from the World Socialist Web Site (3 January 2019), an openly Trotskyist outlet. It relies mainly on Beil's own account, and a spokesperson for Pötsch said the board had dealt with the matter for some time.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2018 dismissal; coverage reports Beil's statement and the museum's reply, and no art-magazine piece found makes a case against the foundation's funding itself.
1. Monopol: editorial team, "Künstlerische Freiheit nicht mehr gegeben" (2018)
URL: https://www.monopol-magazin.de/ralf-beil-als-direktor-des-kunstmuseum-wolfsburg-gekuendigt
- Reports Beil's email and the museum's statement; says the oil show may have been the cause but this is unclear.
- Names the foundation's board of trustees but not its members.
2. taz: Bettina Maria Brosowsky, "Kulturpolitik in der VW-Stadt Wolfsburg" (2019)
URL: https://taz.de/Kulturpolitik-in-der-VW-Stadt-Wolfsburg/!5563141
- Argues Wolfsburg has no coherent cultural policy and that critical art loses space where the car industry dominates.
- Notes Beil's email and says it is unclear whether the oil show was the cause. A daily newspaper, not an art magazine.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a foundation built on the Holler estate and governed from the top of Volkswagen, so the museum depends on one corporate circle. The weak points are the 2018 dismissal, which no source proves was caused by the oil show, and that no annual report or board list was found. Watch the museum's independence and whether it publishes its funding.
