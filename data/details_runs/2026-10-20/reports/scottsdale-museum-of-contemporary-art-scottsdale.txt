SCOTTSDALE MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Scottsdale, USA
ID: scottsdale-museum-of-contemporary-art-scottsdale
Website: https://scottsdalearts.org/explore-scottsdale-arts/smoca/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art, architecture and design at 7374 East Second Street, Scottsdale, Arizona, known as SMoCA (Scottsdale Arts).
- Conceived in 1988 and opened in February 1999 in a former movie theater that the Phoenix architect Will Bruder fully remodelled into a minimalist building (Wikipedia).
- Five galleries for changing exhibitions and the permanent collection (Scottsdale Arts); James Turrell's Knight Rise skyspace is permanently on view (Wikipedia).
- Scottsdale Arts, a non-profit, manages the museum among the city-owned venues, with the Scottsdale Center for the Performing Arts and the Scottsdale Civic Center (City of Scottsdale, 26 February 2026).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., Thursday until 7 p.m.; closed Monday and Tuesday. Admission is $16, $13 with concessions, free for visitors aged 18 and under; pay what you wish on Thursdays and every second Saturday (Scottsdale Arts).

FUNDING
Scottsdale Museum of Contemporary Art: mainly public and philanthropic: a City of Scottsdale contract through Scottsdale Arts, plus foundation, corporate and individual gifts
- City of Scottsdale: the City Council approved a three-year agreement with Scottsdale Arts running from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2029, with two one-year extensions. Funding for fiscal year 2026/27 is $6,297,114, including general operating support and set-aside funds for arts education, public art conservation, community arts grants and destination events. The council votes on the amount every year, and the General Fund contribution falls by $250,000 a year, reaching a $1 million annual reduction by year four. The city has worked with Scottsdale Arts since 1987. The agreement follows a 2024 city audit that recommended clearer expectations, stronger financial reporting and better oversight (City of Scottsdale, 26 February 2026).
- Scottsdale Arts, fiscal year 2025 (Form 990 data on ProPublica): revenue $13,038,696, expenses $14,303,581; contributions $8.5 million (65.3%).
- 2024-25 season sponsors and partners named by Scottsdale Arts: City of Scottsdale, Nationwide, Panasonic Connect, Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community, SRP, The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Virginia G. Piper Charitable Trust, Arizona Commission on the Arts, Arizona Community Foundation, National Endowment for the Arts and Virginia M. Ullman Foundation, with other foundations and individuals (Arizona Digital Free Press, 20 January 2025). The list covers all of Scottsdale Arts.

Red flags: retitled and cut touring exhibition, 2025
- The touring show called Transfeminisms in London (KJZZ) opened at SMoCA on 22 February 2025 as "There Are Other Skies". SMoCA's October 2024 press release had announced it as "transfeminisms" across all five galleries (Southwest Contemporary).
- The show filled three galleries instead of five, the planned community Forum gallery was used for another exhibition, and works by Cassils, Myriam Omar Awadi and SaVĀge K'lub were left out (Southwest Contemporary). KJZZ reported that planned artists from Israel and China did not take part.
- Daria Khan, founder of Mimosa House and a co-curator, said "The exhibition was violently edited in Scottsdale" (KJZZ, 24 March 2025; Southwest Contemporary, 13 March 2025) and that the curators felt "very unprotected" after co-curator Jennifer McCabe left the museum (Southwest Contemporary).
- Gerd Wuestemann, chief executive of Scottsdale Arts, said the whole SMoCA team decided how to adapt the show and called the changes pragmatic, citing "spaces, timelines, and budgets" (Southwest Contemporary, KJZZ). McCabe, SMoCA's former director and chief curator, who resigned in October 2024, said the original title "was meant to be provocative but it may have been too hard for people to digest" (Southwest Contemporary).
- KJZZ asked whether the Scottsdale City Council's cut to diversity, equity and inclusion funding played a role; Scottsdale Arts said the two were unrelated (KJZZ).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind SMoCA is a multi-year City of Scottsdale contract, administered by Scottsdale Arts, together with foundation, corporate and individual gifts, with a 2026/27 city payment of $6,297,114 and a 2024-25 sponsor list that includes SRP and Nationwide. The documented dispute is the February 2025 editing of Transfeminisms into "There Are Other Skies", which co-curator Daria Khan called violent and Scottsdale Arts chief executive Gerd Wuestemann called pragmatic.
