SHARJAH ART FOUNDATION: Sharjah, United Arab Emirates
ID: sharjah-art-foundation-sharjah
Website: https://sharjahart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation in Sharjah with its Information Centre at Al Mureijah Square (SAF website).
- Founded in 2009 by Hoor Al Qasimi, daughter of Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi, ruler of Sharjah; Wikipedia places it under the Department of Culture and Information, Government of Sharjah.
- It took over the Sharjah Biennial, first held in 1993 under the Department of Culture and Information, in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: the Biennial, the March Meeting, residencies, grants, commissions and publications (SAF website, Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Saturday to Thursday 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., Friday 4 p.m. to 9 p.m. Most exhibitions and events are free; free guided tours (SAF website).

FUNDING
Sharjah Art Foundation: state-backed: the Sharjah ruler and Sharjah's government; no accounts published
- Ghalya Saadawi wrote in e-flux Journal (June 2011) that the Foundation is funded by the ruler and Sharjah's Ministry of Culture.
- Her profile on the Foundation's site calls it "the independent public arts organisation she established in 2009" (SAF website).
- No reporting found of annual budgets, a sponsor list or a board list.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Wikipedia lists the Department of Culture and Information, Government of Sharjah, as parent organisation; Saadawi says the ruler and Sharjah's Ministry of Culture fund it. Hoor Al Qasimi, the ruler's daughter, is President and Director (SAF website).

Red flags: state censorship and dismissal, 2011
- On 6 April 2011 the ruler of Sharjah, Sheikh Sultan bin Mohammad Al Qasimi, dismissed director Jack Persekian without notice, after the 10th Sharjah Biennial opened on 16 March 2011 and public outcry over Mustapha Benfodil's installation "Maportaliche" (Sharjah Call for Action, e-flux; Jadaliyya).
- The work was removed. Saadawi writes that other works were tampered with or placed "under review" (e-flux Journal); an audio piece was adjusted so it did not coincide with the call to prayer (ArtAsiaPacific, 18 April 2011).
- Hoor Al Qasimi said the work paired sexually explicit language with religious references "in an overt and provocative manner" (Jadaliyya); ArtAsiaPacific reports the work was dismantled after "the public took enormous offense".
- The Foundation said the online campaign was "deliberately misinforming the public" and that no staff had resigned (spokeswoman Sara Fitzmaurice, The National, 13 April 2011). The petition, with 1,627 signatures by Jadaliyya's 2 May 2011 report, demanded public acknowledgment of the censorship and threatened a boycott of the Biennial, the Foundation and Sharjah's cultural activities.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Substantial criticism, almost all about the 2011 dismissal and censorship. No later critique of the Foundation's own conduct found.
1. e-flux Journal: Ghalya Saadawi, "Post-Sharjah Biennial 10: Institutional Grease and Institutional Critique" (2011)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/journal/26/67970/post-sharjah-biennial-10-institutional-grease-and-institutional-critique
- Says the ruler decided to remove Persekian and that the Foundation and its PR firm ran a campaign defending the decision.
- Argues that large institutions everywhere depend on state, private or capital interests; calls for a renewed critique of institutions, not only moral condemnation.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: unsigned news item, "Director's Ouster Jeopardizes Sharjah Art Foundation's Future" (2011)
URL: https://www.artasiapacific.com/news/director-s-ouster-jeopardizes-sharjah-art-foundation-s-future/
- Argues the dismissal threatens the Foundation and Biennial as regional platforms and risks self-censorship.
- Quotes curators Rasha Salti and Haig Aivazian: "To shock was never our strategy, and offense never our intention."
3. Jadaliyya: author not named, "Boat Rocking in the Art Islands: Politics, Plots and Dismissals in Sharjah's Tenth Biennial" (2011)
URL: https://www.jadaliyya.com/Details/23938
- Presents the dismissal as the decision of the ruler, Sheikh Sultan, and questions the international art world's ties to the UAE. The Foundation's reply is quoted.
4. Sharjah Call for Action, posted on e-flux: "An unwarranted dismissal in Sharjah" (2011)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/35699/an-unwarranted-dismissal-in-sharjah/
- Open letter, not an essay, published 11 April 2011: names the ruler as the one who dismissed Persekian on 6 April, calls the decision disproportionate, and threatens a boycott if its demands are not met.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 11 May 2015 Gulf Labor member Walid Raad was turned back at Dubai airport on his way to the Foundation's March Meeting; Gulf Labor campaigns for migrant workers on Abu Dhabi's Saadiyat Island. More than 40 artists in Sharjah Biennial 12 backed the banned artists (The Art Newspaper, Javier Pes, 1 June 2015).
- The article names no authority behind the ban and reports no Foundation response. The link to the Foundation is that the trip was to its event.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Foundation is the ruler's and the state's, with no accounts or funder list published. The documented weak point is 2011, when the ruler dismissed the director over an artwork and other works were altered or placed under review; the Foundation disputed the petition. Watch for any published accounts or governance changes.
