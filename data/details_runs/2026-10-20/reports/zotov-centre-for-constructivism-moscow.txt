ZOTOV CENTRE FOR CONSTRUCTIVISM: Moscow, Russia
ID: zotov-centre-for-constructivism-moscow
Website: https://centrezotov.ru (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for the study of constructivism, with exhibitions, lectures, a cinema and children's programmes, at Khodynskaya Street 2, building 1, in the Presnya district of Moscow.
- Opened on 23 November 2022 with "1922. Constructivism. The Beginning", showing more than 300 works from 17 museums, institutions and private collections (The Blueprint).
- The idea came from VTB Bank and the bureau SPEECH led by Sergey Choban (The Blueprint, Vedomosti).
- Housed in Bakery Plant No. 5, named for Vasily Zotov, a constructivist bakery completed in 1931 to a design by Georgy Marsakov; it operated until 2006 and has regional heritage status (centre's about page, Mos.ru).
- On site: exhibition floors, two cinema halls, a lecture hall, a children's club (The Blueprint) and "Forum", a free ground-floor exhibition space.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 10pm. Ticket prices and accessibility details were not on the pages read.

FUNDING
Zotov Centre for Constructivism: corporate-sponsored: VTB Bank, which also owns the building
- VTB Bank is named "General Partner" on the centre's partners page. VTB owns the building and kept the Zotov name (Art Focus Now, 7 November 2022). No amounts are published.
- RBC Style (May 2025): the centre opened "with the support of VTB" and runs a research grant programme whose size and sources are undisclosed.
- Patrons (mecenaty) named on the partners page: Olga and Igor Dergunov, and Natasha Belaya. No reporting found on who they are or what they gave.
- Partners on the same page include the State Tretyakov Gallery and the Pushkin Museum.
- Art Focus Now (4 January 2023) says businesses owned by Alisher Usmanov and Vasily Anisimov bought the land from the Moscow government in 2006, and VTB then reconstructed the site.
- No annual report, accounts or board were found on the pages read.

Red flags: VTB Bank, a sanctioned general partner
- VTB is the centre's general partner (centre's partners page) and owner of the building (Art Focus Now, 7 November 2022).
- Russian state bodies held over 90% of VTB's capital in February 2022: the Deposit Insurance Agency 47.22%, the Ministry of Finance 32.88%, the Federal Agency for State Property Management 12.13% (Wikipedia).
- US: on 24 February 2022 the US Treasury's OFAC imposed full blocking sanctions on VTB under Executive Order 14024 (Treasury press release, 24 February 2022); a Treasury release of 15 December 2022 says its property is blocked.
- UK: a full asset freeze on VTB was announced on 24 February 2022 (K&L Gates, 25 February 2022).
- EU: Regulation (EU) 2022/345, published 2 March 2022, put VTB among seven banks under the SWIFT ban, in force from 12 March 2022 (DLA Piper).
- No reporting found of a protest aimed at the centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found: two Art Focus Now pieces question its claims and private-museum model, and a Novaya Gazeta review questions one exhibition. None attacks VTB's role.
1. Art Focus Now: Dmitri Bavilsky, "The Rise and Fall of Moscow's Private Museums: A Study in Oligarchic Museology" (2023)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/discoveries/the-rise-and-fall-of-moscows-private-museums-a-study-in-oligarchic-museology
- Argues Zotov is "the apotheosis of the trend towards large, private museums in Moscow", which serve the owner's reputation and raise the value of their properties.
- Links new cultural sites to "creeping gentrification" and notes the building is surrounded by three 44-storey residential towers.
- Writes that given sanctions and Russia's economy it is "fair to assume that the Zotov may be the last such project".
2. Art Focus Now: Irina Mak, report on the opening (7 November 2022)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/moscow-constructivist-landmark-turns-into-a-new-arts-institution/
- Describes the press release's claim to be "the first institution in Russia dedicated to Constructivism" as somewhat exaggerated, since other Moscow centres have long studied the movement.
- Notes VTB first promised the site to the Pushkin Museum for a video art centre, then took it back.
3. Novaya Gazeta: Grigory Mirsky, "Русское невероятное и Лихо" (review of the exhibition "Русское невероятное" at Zotov; 2025, date from the page address)
URL: https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2025/02/04/russkoe-neveroiatnoe-i-likho
- Questions whether the curators' thesis, that constructivism is a natural continuation of Russian consciousness, is too broad and overshadows the material (my translation of his argument).
- Notes the show is educational and that constructivist works have a significant but not central place among ancient, Soviet and contemporary pieces.
- Praises the building's restoration. Does not mention VTB; criticises the exhibition, not the funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is a bank's project: VTB, held over 90% by Russian state bodies and under US and UK asset freezes since 24 February 2022, is general partner and owner of the building. The weak points are dependence on one sanctioned sponsor, undisclosed amounts and patrons, and no published accounts or board.
