GROPIUS BAU: Berlin, Germany
ID: gropius-bau
Website: https://www.berlinerfestspiele.de/gropius-bau

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition house for large temporary exhibitions at 7 Niederkirchnerstrasse in Berlin-Kreuzberg (Wikipedia).
- Built from 1877 to 1881 in neo-Renaissance style by the architects Martin Gropius and Heino Schmieden, and named for Gropius; it first housed the Museum of Applied Arts (Wikipedia).
- Heavily damaged in 1945; the German Wikipedia says the Berlin Wall stood directly in front of the main entrance from 1961 to 1989. Declared a historic monument in 1966, rebuilt from 1978, and reopened in 1981 with a Schinkel exhibition (Wikipedia, German Wikipedia).
- Further renovations followed in 1998 to 2000 and 2010 to 2012 (German Wikipedia).
- Since 2001 the Berliner Festspiele have operated the house on behalf of the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (German Wikipedia).
- Directors: Stephanie Rosenthal from 2018 to 2022; Jenny Schlenzka, appointed in 2023, took over in September 2023. A programme advisory board of seven institutional leaders advises on the programme (German Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions have included Christo and Jeanne-Claude (2001), ancient Egyptian treasures (2006, about 450,000 visitors) and Yayoi Kusama (2021) (German Wikipedia). In 2019 the atrium became free of charge and a new cafe and bookshop opened (The Berliner, Anna Larkin, 20 March 2019).

FUNDING
Gropius Bau: mainly public: German federal government through the Berliner Festspiele, with money raised per exhibition
- The Berliner Festspiele, which operate the house, have been financed exclusively by the federal government since 2001; from 1967 the Land Berlin and the federal government shared the costs equally (German Wikipedia on the Berliner Festspiele).
- In 2002 the Berliner Festspiele merged with the Haus der Kulturen der Welt, the Martin-Gropius-Bau and the Berlin International Film Festival into Kulturveranstaltungen des Bundes in Berlin GmbH, funded by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (German Wikipedia on the Berliner Festspiele).
- Exhibitions are funded one by one: Rosenthal said in 2019 that the house "must fundraise for each of our exhibitions individually", and that a standalone sponsor for the atrium was under consideration (The Berliner, 20 March 2019).

BOTTOM LINE
The Gropius Bau is run by the Berliner Festspiele, which the German federal government finances, on behalf of the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media. Its director said in 2019 that each exhibition raises additional money from outside sources.
