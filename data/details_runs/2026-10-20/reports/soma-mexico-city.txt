SOMA: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: soma-mexico-city
Website: https://somamexico.org (checked 2026-10-25; only the navigation menu loaded, no page text could be read)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit, non-commercial and non-governmental art school, residency and public talks platform in Mexico City. Since 2026 it occupies a house in the San Miguel Chapultepec neighbourhood (SOMA Summer 2025 listed the address as Alumnos 47, San Miguel Chapultepec, Miguel Hidalgo); before that it was in San Pedro de los Pinos (e-flux, 2026).
- Founded in 2009 when the artist Yoshua Okón convened a group of 19 artists (Wikipedia); Carla Herrera-Prats started the SOMA Summer course and it took its current form in 2011 (e-flux, 2015). Okón co-founded the earlier artist-run space La Panadería with Miguel Calderón and places SOMA in that tradition (Artillery, 2013).
- Named by vote of its Artists Council, which included Francis Alÿs, Teresa Margolles, Mario García Torres and Julieta Aranda. Okón links the word to Aldous Huxley's use of it and to the Greek word for body (Artillery, 2013).
- Building: a house with an outdoor space on a ten-year lease from the Mexico City collector Moisés Cosío (e-flux, 2026). The earlier San Pedro de los Pinos site had a patio and two small rooms for student presentations and no studio space (e-flux, 2015). The school has no individual studios and no exhibition programme by design (Artillery, 2013).
- Programmes: a two-year Spanish-language academic programme (PES) that is not accredited; Miércoles de SOMA, a public talk series held almost every Wednesday for fifteen years; a residency programme; and SOMA Summer, an English-language course.
- Visiting: the talks are free and held on Wednesday evenings (e-flux, 2015). No reporting found of opening hours or accessibility for the new house.

FUNDING
SOMA: privately funded: donors, an annual benefit auction and a board of patrons, with no government money
- No government funding: the school "receives no government funding" (e-flux, 2026). Okón told Hyperallergic in 2015 that the state supports only its own institutions and has no fund for non-profits, and that SOMA had asked.
- 2015: it relied mainly on private donors, artists and an annual auction. SOMA Summer was subsidised through agreements with foreign universities and foreign governments, and only about a quarter of participants paid the full tuition of about $3,500. SOMA paid 30 percent tax, and only 30 percent of donations were tax deductible (e-flux, 2015).
- Board: e-flux (2026) calls Aimée Labarrere de Servitje SOMA's board president and names Aimée Servitje as president of the Board of Patrons, who also leads PAC; it does not say whether these are one person.
- Tuition: the PES programme once gave 90 percent scholarships to every student and is now tuition-free (e-flux, 2026). In 2015 the scholarship covered 85 percent and the remaining fee was about $1,000 a year (e-flux, 2015).
- No reporting found of a grant from Fundación Jumex or the Prince Claus Fund.
- No reporting found of a published donor list, annual report or accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little direct criticism of SOMA. Coverage is descriptive or written by people connected to the school, and the e-flux 2026 piece records objections only through the replies of SOMA's own people.
1. e-flux Education: Kim Córdova, "Fifteen years of SOMA: Why institutionality can be its own political act" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/education/features/6732756/fifteen-years-of-soma-why-institutionality-can-be-its-own-political-act
- Ricardo Alzati defends SOMA's Mexico-focused, northern-influenced model, arguing that Mexico lacks postgraduate art programmes while "the United States, unlike Mexico, has thousands of master's programs".
- Cuauhtémoc Medina sees a strength in SOMA "not necessarily creating a 'school'".
- Mentions the 4 July 2025 protests in Mexico City against gentrification and digital nomads.
- The author states she was a student at SOMA and "could never write an objective essay about SOMA".
2. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "Independent Art Spaces Thrive in Mexico City" (15 April 2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/independent-art-spaces-thrive-in-mexico-city
- Not criticism of SOMA: describes it as an unaccredited school centred on dialogue rather than studio practice.
- Reports Okón's account that SOMA has had no public money.

BOTTOM LINE
No flagged funder or government money appears in anything published about SOMA, and it states that it takes no state funding. The weak point is transparency: no donor list, accounts or annual report was found, so the claim of private patronage cannot be checked name by name, and the board president and the landlord are the only named backers. Worth watching is whether SOMA publishes its donors now that it has moved to a leased house.
