WITS ART MUSEUM: Johannesburg, South Africa
ID: wits-art-museum-johannesburg
Website: https://www.wits.ac.za/wam/about-us/

OVERVIEW
- University art museum on the corner of Bertha and Jorissen Streets in Braamfontein, near the Nelson Mandela Bridge, within the University of the Witwatersrand's cultural precinct (Wits).
- The collection began as a teaching collection in the early 1950s, started by Professors Heather Martienssen and John Fassler of the Architecture Department. The Gertrude Posel Gallery was established in Senate House in 1977, named in recognition of Posel's contribution to its establishment (Wits); the Mail & Guardian described the museum's new building on 18 May 2012 (Mail & Guardian).
- Named for the university, the University of the Witwatersrand; its core gallery keeps the name Gertrude Posel Gallery (Mail & Guardian).
- Building: parts of three buildings, Lawson's Building, University Corner and Dental House. The architects Nina Cohen and Fiona Garson reused a former Shell garage and Volvo dealership ramp; the design won the 2012 Visi Magazine Architecture Award (Wits, Mail & Guardian).
- On site: exhibition galleries, climate-controlled collection storage, and the Jack Ginsberg Centre for Book Arts, opened in 2019. The collection includes the Standard Bank African Art Collection, the Wits Museum of Ethnology Collection (2001) and archives such as the Robert Hodgins, Sekoto and Walter Battiss collections (Wits).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 16:00; free entry with donations encouraged; ramps, lifts and wheelchair lifts, and accessible toilets in the Forecourt (Wits).

FUNDING
Wits Art Museum: university museum: the University of the Witwatersrand, private donors and Standard Bank as collection partner
- The museum is part of the University of the Witwatersrand. Its 2012 building cost about R40 million; fundraising took about 10 years, and from the end of 2005 the university and private donors committed space and money. The acquisitions budget was cut in 1992 and restored only after 2005, so donors largely sustained the contemporary collection (Mail & Guardian, Matthew Partridge, 18 May 2012).
- Standard Bank: the Standard Bank African Art Collection began in 1979 as a joint venture with Wits and is housed at the museum, with over 6,000 objects (Standard Bank). The Mail & Guardian describes it as co-owned by Wits.
- Donors named by Wits: Norman Herber gave acquisition funds in the late 1960s; Vittorio Meneghelli gave the first classical African artworks in 1978; John Schlesinger gave over 100 works in 1979; Jack Ginsberg gave his artists' book collections in 2018 (Wits). Robert Hodgins gave about 300 editioned prints and Linda Givon supported the contemporary collection (Mail & Guardian).
- September 2026: Wits announced a R341 million Wits Cultural Precinct, which includes the Wits Art Museum and the Jack Ginsberg Centre for Book Arts. Wits says shares from the Seabrooke Family Trust will fund the majority of the capital cost and Wits will co-invest the balance; Chris Seabrooke is chief executive of Sabvest Capital, which he founded in 1988 (Wits). Billionaires.Africa reports the gift as 1.4 million Sabvest Capital shares, worth R204.4 million at the transfer date, given to the Wits Foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum belongs to the University of the Witwatersrand and was built with university and private donor money, and its Standard Bank African Art Collection has been a corporate joint venture since 1979. The newest money is a 2026 Sabvest share gift from the Seabrooke family trust to the Wits Foundation for the cultural precinct.
