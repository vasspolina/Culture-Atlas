MUAC: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: muac-mexico-city
Website: https://muac.unam.mx (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- University contemporary art museum in the Centro Cultural Universitario at Insurgentes Sur 3000, on the main campus of UNAM in Mexico City (Wikipedia, Time Out).
- Opened in November 2008 as a museum of the Universidad Nacional Autonoma de Mexico (UNAM); Wikipedia describes it as the first Mexican public museum devoted to art made in the 21st century (Wikipedia, Time Out).
- Full name Museo Universitario Arte Contemporaneo; Cuauhtemoc Medina was named chief curator in March 2013, succeeding Maria Ines Rodriguez (Arte al Dia, 21 March 2013).
- Designed by Teodoro Gonzalez de Leon: a minimalist building with an inclined glass facade (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds works by more than 300 artists, with a focus on Mexican art after 1968, and the museum has an Experimental Sound Space, workshops, a bookstore and publications (Wikipedia, Time Out).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday 10:00 to 20:00, Saturday 11:00 to 20:00, Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. General admission MXN 60; free for UNAM students, children under 12, people with disabilities and a companion, and everyone on Thursdays 18:00 to 20:00; half price on Sundays (MUAC website).

FUNDING
MUAC: public university with private benefactors and corporate allies
- MUAC belongs to UNAM; the Patronato Universitario UNAM and the Fundacion Universidad Nacional Autonoma de Mexico are among the supporters named on its benefactors page.
- The benefactors page, whose copyright line reads 2009 to 2017 and which gives no amounts or gift dates, lists: Circulo Justo Sierra (Miguel Aleman Velasco, Fundacion Alfredo Harp Helu, Consejo Nacional para la Cultura y las Artes, Gobierno del Distrito Federal, Ingenieros Civiles Asociados); Circulo Jose Vasconcelos (Eugenio Lopez Alonso, Elias M. Sacal, Grupo Radio Centro, ISA Corporativo); Circulo Alfonso Caso (Jose Luis Chong, CEMEX, Consejo Nacional de Ciencia y Tecnologia, Proeza Slai, Zimat Consultores, Imagenes y Muebles Urbanos, Hoteles City).
- Donors: Alejandro Burillo Azcarraga, Juan Carlos del Valle, Fundacion Televisa and FEMSA. Corporate allies: Bloomberg and Fundacion AXA Mexico. Other named supporters include Banamex, Barclays Bank Mexico, Fundacion Coppel, Tenaris Tamsa, the Han Nefkens Foundation and the French embassy.
- At the opening in 2008 the architect received a government grant of an unstated sum (Frontline Club, quoting La Plaza).
- No reporting found of MUAC's annual budget or a dated current donor list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum's funders was found in art media. The one criticism found concerns the building at its 2008 opening and comes from the Mexican magazine Proceso, not art press.
1. Proceso (as reported by the Frontline Club, 2009): Blanca Gonzalez Rosas and Manuel Larrosa, on the building (2008)
URL: https://www.frontlineclub.com/video_an_introduction_to_muac_mexicos_new_modern_art_museum/
- Gonzalez Rosas argued the design too closely resembles the 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art in Kanazawa and suggested rescinding the architect's government grant.
- Larrosa called the museum a work of self-homage that dominates its space.
- Criticises the building, not the museum's programme or funding; read only through the Frontline Club's summary, not the Proceso pieces.

WIDER CONTEXT
- More than 260 British artists, through Artists for Palestine UK, asked five British theatres in May 2026 to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies, saying it is "complicit in grave war crimes" through involvement with Israeli settlements (The New Arab, 29 May 2026).
- The campaign names the National Theatre, Royal Shakespeare Company, Young Vic, Royal Court and Almeida, not MUAC. Bloomberg is listed as a corporate ally on MUAC's benefactors page.

BOTTOM LINE
MUAC is a public university museum with private benefactors named but no amounts or dates. The weak points are the opacity of its donor list, which includes broadcaster and bottler foundations and a Bloomberg corporate ally, and the lack of any published budget. Worth watching: whether MUAC dates its benefactors and whether the Bloomberg campaign reaches Mexico.
