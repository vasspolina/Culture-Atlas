BONNIERS KONSTHALL: Stockholm, Sweden
ID: bonniers-konsthall-stockholm
Website: https://www.bonnierskonsthall.se

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at Torsgatan 19, Stockholm, opened in 2006. It describes itself as having given space to contemporary art "förutsättningslöst", without conditions, since the doors opened (Bonniers Konsthall press release, 2022; translation).
- The konsthall has its roots in the Maria Bonnier Dahlin Foundation, which Jeanette Bonnier (died 2016) founded in 1985 in memory of her daughter; the first exhibition in September 2006 showed all earlier grant recipients (Bonniers Konsthall website). The foundation awards grants to young artists every year (e-flux).
- It is run by the Bonnier family as part of the Bonnier Group, "one of Scandinavia's biggest media groups" (Bonniers Konsthall website).
- Building: an "iron-shaped" transparent building designed by Johan Celsing, with a glass facade facing the city (Bonnier annual review 2019). The architecture firm Marge led a refurbishment before the reopening on 22 May 2016 (e-flux).
- Joanna Nordin became artistic director in April 2023, succeeding Theodor Ringborg, who started in 2019 (press release, 20 October 2022).
- Visiting: galleries open Wednesday 12 to 20 and Thursday to Sunday 12 to 18, closed Monday and Tuesday. Tickets are 130 SEK, 100 SEK for students and seniors, free for members, under-26s and job applicants, and free for everyone on Fridays. An elevator and stairs lead to every floor, and the large exhibition halls have no thresholds.

FUNDING
Bonniers Konsthall: family-funded: a foundation from Jeanette Bonnier's estate, with the konsthall run by the Bonnier family within the Bonnier Group media business
- Bonnier describes the konsthall as "the public expression of one of the Bonnier families' biggest cultural interests". After Jeanette Bonnier died in 2016 she left "a foundation with the resources to ensure the future financing and development of Bonniers Konsthall" (Bonnier annual review 2017).
- Dagens PS (Ola Söderlund, 20 September 2016), citing Sveriges Radio, reported that her estate was worth 3 billion SEK and that the konsthall receives 15 million SEK a year from the estate's returns. A foundation manages the estate and is chaired by her niece, Christel Engelbert. The article says Jeanette Bonnier decided to donate her shares in the Bonnier companies to the konsthall.
- Bonnier Group, the family's media business (daily press, magazines, books, film and television), reported net sales of SEK 23,311 million and an operating profit (EBITA) of SEK 2,402 million for 2025 (Bonnier website).
- Free admission to all exhibitions began in 2015 (e-flux); the konsthall now sells tickets and keeps Fridays free.

BOTTOM LINE
The konsthall is run by the Bonnier family within the Bonnier Group, and Bonnier's 2017 annual review says Jeanette Bonnier left a foundation to secure its future financing. Dagens PS reported in 2016, citing Sveriges Radio, that the konsthall receives 15 million SEK a year from her estate's returns.
