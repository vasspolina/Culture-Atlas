JOHANNESBURG ART GALLERY: Johannesburg, South Africa
ID: johannesburg-art-gallery-johannesburg
Website: https://joburgheritage.org.za/johannesburg-art-gallery-in-crisis-latest-updates/ (checked 2026-10-24; this is the Johannesburg Heritage Foundation's update page, not a gallery site; no site run by the gallery itself was opened)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum in Joubert Park, Johannesburg, at the corner of Klein and King George streets (Wikipedia).
- Sir Hugh Lane assembled the founding collection, shown in London in 1910; Florence, Lady Phillips set up the first gallery collection with funds from her husband Lionel Phillips and secured city funding for a purpose-built museum (Wikipedia).
- Sir Edwin Lutyens designed the building, with Robert Howden as supervising architect. It opened in 1915 without ceremony and was not completed to Lutyens' designs; Meyer Pienaar and Associates added the north facade and galleries in 1986 to 1987 (Wikipedia).
- It holds over 9,000 works, including Dutch, British, European and South African art and a print cabinet; in 1940 it was the first South African gallery to buy a work by a Black artist, Gerard Sekoto's "Yellow Houses" (Wikipedia).
- Khwezi Gule has been chief curator since 2019 (Wikipedia). In 2023 the Oppenheimer family moved its collection from the gallery to the Brenthurst Library (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: no current opening hours or admission found from the gallery or the City; many halls are closed and the collection is being moved (Wikipedia, Daily Maverick).

FUNDING
Johannesburg Art Gallery: mainly public: the City of Johannesburg
- The City owns and runs the gallery. The Johannesburg Heritage Foundation says the City holds it under a deed of donation with a custodial duty (Johannesburg Heritage Foundation).
- In 2017 Councillor Nonhlanhla Sifumba said millions had been allocated for upkeep before the 2015 centenary but nothing was done to upgrade or maintain the facility (Smithsonian Magazine).
- The Johannesburg Development Agency oversees the restoration (Mail & Guardian). The sources opened state no amount and no funder for the City's restoration or relocation.
- The Foundation and Friends of JAG say their own rescue plan includes funding and contractor costings, and that Business Arts South Africa and the Lutyens Trust endorsed it; the page gives no amounts or funders.
- No corporate sponsor is named in the sources opened. No reporting found of annual accounts.

Red flags: Protest or boycott, legal action against the City, 2024 to 2025
- On 28 August 2024 the Johannesburg Heritage Foundation and Friends of JAG sent the City a legal letter drafted by Webber Wentzel demanding it meet its custodial duty under the deed of donation (Foundation update page; the Foundation is a party, so this is its account).
- The Foundation says the City met the groups on 16 October 2024, had no material progress by 14 November 2024, and that on 25 January 2025 the two groups said they were investigating options including an urgent interdict. The page does not say an interdict was filed.
- In November 2025 the groups said the City had approached them about a partnership but no formal agreement existed; they estimated restoration could take up to five years (Daily Maverick).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in the international art press. Criticism of the City's stewardship comes from South African newspapers and the heritage groups.
1. Mail & Guardian: Thembeka Heidi Sincuba, "In the Shadow of the Gallery: Art, power and the fight for Johannesburg's soul" (2025)
URL: https://mg.co.za/friday/2025-04-27-in-the-shadow-of-the-gallery-art-power-and-the-fight-for-johannesburg-s-soul/
- Describes a century-old institution in an existential crisis from theft, neglect and decay tied to ongoing mismanagement, with the restoration timeline "wobbly".
- Says the Development Agency's meeting felt more like public perception management than consultation.
- Quotes curator Khwezi Gule on the contradiction of working in spaces made to exclude.
2. Smithsonian Magazine: Swapna Krishna, "A Leaky Roof Has Forced the Johannesburg Art Gallery to Temporarily Close" (2017)
URL: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/leaky-roof-forced-johannesburg-art-gallery-temporarily-close-180962228/
- Reports the roof had leaked since 1989 and thieves had stolen copper sheeting; the closure followed January 2017 storms.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia reports a roof section collapsed in 2017 and that media reports blame "inaction, corruption and theft" by the City; it records no specific theft incident.
- The City upgraded storage at Museum Africa, the Brixton Multipurpose Centre and the Johannesburg Library for the collection; Mayor Dada Morero said on 19 November 2025 that the gallery would reopen in July 2027 (Daily Maverick).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is municipal and the city shows no corporate or flagged funder. The weak points are neglect of the building and collection by the City, no published accounts, and a legal dispute with the heritage groups over the City's custodial duty. Worth watching: the 2027 reopening date and who pays for it.
