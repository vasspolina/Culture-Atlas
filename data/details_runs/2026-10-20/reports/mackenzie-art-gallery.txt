MACKENZIE ART GALLERY: Regina, Canada
ID: mackenzie-art-gallery
Website: https://mackenzie.art

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery at 3475 Albert Street, Regina, Saskatchewan, at the southwestern edge of the Wascana Centre park (gallery website; Wikipedia).
- It grew from the art collection of the lawyer Norman MacKenzie, who left it to Regina College (now the University of Regina) in 1936; the college opened the Norman MacKenzie Art Gallery in 1953 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Norman MacKenzie. Its building is the T. C. Douglas Building, named for the former Saskatchewan premier Tommy Douglas, which the gallery moved into in 1990 and which also houses provincial government offices (Wikipedia).
- The gallery became independent of the University of Regina in 1990 and keeps partnerships with it, including custody of art the university owns (Wikipedia).
- Eight galleries with 2,200 square metres of exhibition space; the permanent collection has more than 5,000 works, weighted toward Canadian art, especially Indigenous, Saskatchewan and Western Canadian artists (Wikipedia).
- In 1913 MacKenzie took an 18th-century stone statue of the goddess Annapurna from a temple in Varanasi and misidentified it as Vishnu; the gallery returned it to India in a virtual repatriation ceremony after the artist Divya Mehra found it while researching an exhibition (The Art Newspaper, Kabir Jhala, 25 November 2020).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 10:00 to 17:30, Thursday 10:00 to 21:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:30; closed Monday. Admission is $12; free for members, for youth 17 and under, and for support persons. Wheelchairs, walkers and strollers can be borrowed at the welcome desk, service animals are permitted, and quarterly tours are offered for Deaf, Hard of Hearing, blind and partially sighted visitors.

FUNDING
MacKenzie Art Gallery: mixed: public operating and programming grants, with fundraising income as the largest line and corporate sponsors led by TD
- Revenue for the year to 31 March 2025 was $5,827,575: operating grants $1,283,600, programming grants $432,793, fundraising income $2,859,556, earned income $959,343 and other income $292,283 (2024-25 annual report, summary statements audited).
- The gallery's history page says it receives ongoing funding from the MacKenzie Operating Endowment at the South Saskatchewan Community Foundation, Canada Council for the Arts, the Sask Lotteries Trust Fund for Sport, Culture and Recreation, the City of Regina, the University of Regina and SK Arts (gallery website).
- Corporate sponsors in 2024-25, by band (annual report): TD Ready Commitment $200,000+; Rawlco Radio $100,000+; RBC Foundation $50,000 to $74,999; Canada Life and Link Developments $25,000 to $49,999; ISC and SaskTel $15,000 to $24,999; Benson Badger Advisory Group, EQ Bank, Ledcor Construction, McDougall Gauley and Ripplinger Homes $5,000 to $14,999. A further 14 donors gave up to $4,999 each, among them MNP, PCL Construction, Miller Thomson, MLT Aikins, Saskatchewan Government Insurance and EMP Metals Corp.
- The annual report names Joely Big Eagle-Kequahtooway as "TD Curatorial Fellow, Indigenous Relations". It credits RBC with a supported artist residency and the "RBC Emerging Artists" presentation, Canada Life with presenting Weekly We Make, and Rawlco Radio with a Rawlco Radio Free Day on 1 February 2025.
- In August 2018 the gallery received an anonymous donation (gallery website); Wikipedia gives it as C$25 million, the largest in the gallery's history, endowed to the South Saskatchewan Community Foundation.

Red flags: TD Bank guilty plea, 2024
- TD Ready Commitment is the largest corporate sponsor in the 2024-25 annual report, in the highest band at $200,000+, and the report names a "TD Curatorial Fellow" post (2024-25 annual report, summary statements dated 10 June 2025).
- On 10 October 2024 TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering. It agreed to about $3 billion in penalties: $1.8 billion to the Department of Justice, $1.3 billion to FinCEN and more than $124 million to the Federal Reserve Board, with a cap on the total assets of its two US banking subsidiaries set by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (CNBC, Dan Mangan, 10 October 2024, republished by NBC News).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is funded by a mix of public operating and programming grants ($1.7 million in 2024-25) and fundraising income; the annual report lists corporate sponsors by band. Fundraising income of $2.86 million was 49 percent of the $5.83 million revenue. The documented weak point is TD, its largest corporate sponsor at $200,000 or more in 2024-25, which pleaded guilty on 10 October 2024 to money-laundering conspiracy and Bank Secrecy Act failures.
