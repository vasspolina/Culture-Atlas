ZABLUDOWICZ COLLECTION: London, UK
ID: zabludowicz-collection-london
Website: https://www.zabludowiczcollection.com/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art collection, founded in 1994 (stated by the collection); Poju Zabludowicz has been founder and trustee since 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Its London project space at 176 Prince of Wales Road, Chalk Farm, opened in September 2007 in a former Methodist chapel and closed in December 2023 (stated by the collection). It hosted more than 100 free exhibitions and over 640 free events (The Art Newspaper).
- The collection now works through artwork loans, commissions, residencies in Sarvisalo, Finland, and the Master Class and Curatorial Testing Ground programmes.
- The London building reopened in 2025 as Camden Arts Projects, a not-for-profit space (Time Out).
- Visiting: London viewing is by email request; the New York space (1500 Broadway) is free, by appointment; Sarvisalo takes groups of up to 60 by appointment. No accessibility information is published.

FUNDING
Zabludowicz Collection: privately funded by the founders' family fortune through the Zabludowicz Art Trust
- The collection says all its activities are privately funded and that it is principally owned by the Zabludowicz Art Trust; Zabludowicz Art Projects UK is a registered charity, number 1120067 (stated by the collection).
- The charity's 2019 report says it has "no regular source of income other than the donations it receives from the Zabludowicz Art Trust"; its income was £1,181,521 in 2019 and £1,114,769 in 2018 (The White Pube, citing Companies House filings).
- Poju Zabludowicz heads the holding company Tamares (Wikipedia). Tamares sold its British Israel Property operation for $700m in 2011, and the couple's 2022 net worth was £1.495bn (The Art Newspaper, citing The Sunday Times).
- His father, Shlomo Zabludowicz, built the family business around the Israeli defence contractor Soltam (Wikipedia, Hyperallergic).
- No reporting found of any public funder, corporate sponsor or outside donor.

Red flags: boycott and protest over the founders' ties to Israel
- Since 2014 the group Boycott Divest Zabludowicz (BDZ) has urged artists and institutions to boycott the collection (Wikipedia).
- In May 2021 BDZ's call was signed by more than 600 artists and organisations; the collection's spokesperson said it is not a political organisation and called the petition misinformed (Hyperallergic).
- In July 2021, 25 artists and cultural workers sent letters to the Zabludowicz Art Trust and Zabludowicz Art Projects withdrawing ("deauthoring") their past work (Hyperallergic).
- The founders' statement of 21 May 2021 welcomed the ceasefire and backed a two-state solution (Hyperallergic). In 2023 the collection said the London closure was unrelated to the boycott or the war in Gaza (Time Out).

Red flags: arms and defence business in the family money
- Poju Zabludowicz chairs and is CEO of the Tamares Group, which holds a stake in Knafaim. Knafaim is the ultimate beneficiary of Maintenance Wings Limited Partnership, which provides military aircraft maintenance to the Israeli Air Force (Hyperallergic, July 2021).
- BICOM, a pro-Israel lobby group founded by Poju Zabludowicz, was financed through the fortune of his father, an Israeli arms dealer (Hyperallergic, July 2021).
- A 2009 Channel 4 Dispatches documentary reported his business interests in a shopping centre in the West Bank settlement of Ma'ale Adumim (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism exists, almost all of it tied to the 2014 to 2021 boycott campaign. It targets the founders' business and political ties, not the exhibitions.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "600+ Artists and Organizations Urge Boycott of Zabludowicz Art Trust Over Israel Ties" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/646541/artists-boycott-the-zabludowicz-art-trust-over-israel-ties/
- Reports BDZ's call to withdraw work, with reasons: BICOM, the Knafaim stake, the West Bank shopping centre and Soltam.
- Names Eyal Weizman, Claire Bishop and Lawrence Abu Hamdan among signatories.
2. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Citing Pro-Israel Ties, 25 Artists Disaffiliate From London Collection" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/665123/citing-pro-israel-ties-artists-disaffiliate-from-zabludowicz-collection/
- Reports 25 artists and cultural workers withdrawing work and asking the Trust to divest from the Israeli state.
- Names Michelle Williams Gamaker, Harold Offeh, Amalia Pica and Jack Strange.
3. The White Pube: "ZM", "BDZ" (2021)
URL: https://thewhitepube.co.uk/art-thoughts/bdz/
- Argues the collection "artwashes" money from Soltam, a military aircraft maintenance contract, a West Bank shopping centre and political donations.
- Argues it targets young, precarious artists and urges them to sign the BDZ pledge.
- Names Poju, Anita and Tiffany Zabludowicz and director Elizabeth Neilson. An openly partisan essay.

BOTTOM LINE
The collection runs on the founding family's private fortune, with no outside funder found. The weak points are that fortune's links to arms and defence companies and a pro-Israel lobby, as reported by Hyperallergic, and the boycott campaign running since 2014. Its accounts and board were not readable in the sources opened; watch the Art Trust's donations to the charity.
