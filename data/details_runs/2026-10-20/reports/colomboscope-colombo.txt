COLOMBOSCOPE: Colombo, Sri Lanka
ID: colomboscope-colombo
Website: https://www.colomboscope.lk/partners

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts festival and creative platform for interdisciplinary dialogue in Colombo, held in key historic sites of the city and running since 2013 (Colomboscope website).
- Founded in 2013 by EUNIC Sri Lanka, made up of the Goethe-Institut, the British Council and the Alliance Française de Kotte; the first edition was a small event at Park Street Mews, in the slot left open when the Galle Literary Festival was postponed (Shamalee De Silva-Parizeau, "Five years of art, culture and creative wonder", Daily FT, 26 August 2017).
- The 2013 edition was "Identities"; later editions included "Making history" (2014), "Testing Grounds" at the General Post Office (2016, curated by Susanne Jaschko) and "Re/evolution" (2017), the first organised by a local team (Daily FT, 2017).
- Fold Media Collective, established in 2019, is the organising entity; its members include designers, illustrators, filmmakers and audio-visual artists (Colomboscope website).
- Natasha Ginwala has been artistic director since 2019 (Gujral Foundation).
- The ninth edition, "Rhythm Alliances", ran 21 to 31 January 2026 (Colomboscope website, Gujral Foundation) with around 50 artists and collectives at Port City Colombo, Colpetty Town House, Barefoot Gallery and Radicle Cafe and Gallery in Pettah; admission was free (Berlin Art Link).
- In October 2025 the festival held "Colomboscope Alliances", its first engagement in India, at the Gujral Foundation's Gujral House in New Delhi (Gujral Foundation).

FUNDING
Colomboscope: mixed: European cultural institutes, past corporate title sponsors and individual Friends
- The Partners page names EUNIC Sri Lanka (Goethe-Institut, British Council, Alliance Française de Kotte) as founding partner and lists eleven individual Friends: Koenraad Pringiers, Maliha and Munir Bhimjee, Art and Charlie, Marie Waarlo, Kavan Balasuriya, C.E.H. (Garry) de Saram, Appapillai Navaratnam, Felix Rumprecht, Papaya Books, Ruwangi Amarasinghe and Pallavi Paul (stated by the festival).
- EUNIC has supported the festival through its Cluster Fund since 2015; Colomboscope was one of five projects chosen from over forty applicants for that grant (stated by the festival).
- Goethe-Institut Sri Lanka supported the research visit of guest curator Hajra Haider Karrar for the ninth edition (Colomboscope website).
- The Gujral Foundation is a long-term partner and hosted the 2025 Delhi presentation (Gujral Foundation).
- Standard Chartered supported the 2014 edition (Daily FT, 2017). Cinnamon Hotels and Resorts became title sponsor in 2015, when the festival was renamed Cinnamon Colomboscope (MoMA post, 2018).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Four items: a 2015 review that questions corporate sponsorship, gentrification and the audience; a 2018 essay on sponsorship and the festival's direction; a 2013 criticism of one session and of the British Council's funding; and a 2026 review of the ninth edition.
1. Daily FT: Shiran Illanperuma, "Growing pains: A review of Cinnamon Colomboscope 2015" (28 August 2015)
URL: https://www.ft.lk/article/463577/Growing-pains--A-review-of-Cinnamon-Colomboscope-2015
- Calls the festival its "third and perhaps most ambitious iteration", then asks "whether the festival is a commentary on gentrification or a symptom of it", noting that title sponsor Cinnamon Hotels is involved in Slave Island's development through its Cinnamon Life project.
- Questions how the "refreshingly progressive" discussions fit with corporate and international backing, and describes the audience as predominantly middle and upper-middle class.
- Names the British Council and Goethe Institute as backers alongside Cinnamon.
2. MoMA post: Jyoti Dhar, "Colombo Art Biennale and Colomboscope: Islands in an Unmappable Sea" (18 April 2018)
URL: https://post.moma.org/colombo-art-biennale-and-colomboscope-islands-in-an-unmappable-sea/
- Argues that "time and sponsorship issues resulted in a hasty assortment of works" at Colomboscope 2017, and that "somewhere in their drive to be bigger and better, these festivals lost their charge and, to a certain extent, their way."
- Reports that both Colomboscope and the Colombo Art Biennale "have been accused of catering primarily to the English-speaking elite of Colombo."
3. Asian Correspondent, reprinted by Tamil Guardian: F. Harrison, "Arts festival becomes a govt mouthpiece" (3 April 2013)
URL: https://www.tamilguardian.com/node/25393
- Criticises the 2013 session "Counting the Bodies": three of the four panellists were government spokesmen, two of them military in uniform, and the moderator did not challenge them.
- Objects to British Council funding of the festival; the Council stressed that the festival was "independently curated".
- Tamil Guardian reports from a Tamil perspective (partisan).
4. Berlin Art Link: Sumugan Sivanesan, "Post-National Flows: Colomboscope's 'Rhythm Alliances'" (13 February 2026)
URL: https://www.berlinartlink.com/2026/02/13/colomboscopes-rhythm-alliances-festival-review/
- Review of the ninth edition; calls the Port City dock "neither an ecologically sound nor convenient location" for the Arka Kinari project, citing its securitised setting and reliance on fuel generators.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Colomboscope is a mix of European cultural institutes (Goethe-Institut, British Council and Alliance Française through EUNIC, which has supported it through its Cluster Fund since 2015), eleven individual Friends and past corporate sponsors (Standard Chartered in 2014, Cinnamon Hotels from 2015).
