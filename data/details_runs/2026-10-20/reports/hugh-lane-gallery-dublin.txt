HUGH LANE GALLERY: Dublin, Ireland
ID: hugh-lane-gallery-dublin
Website: https://www.dublincity.ie/culture-art-and-film/arts-culture-public-spaces/hugh-lane-gallery

OVERVIEW
- Municipal gallery of modern and contemporary art at Charlemont House, Parnell Square North, Dublin 1, operated by Dublin City Council and its wholly owned company, the Hugh Lane Gallery Trust (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Sir Hugh Lane, who opened it on Harcourt Street on 20 January 1908 and paid its running costs (Wikipedia). It has been in Charlemont House since 1933 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Lane, who died in 1915 when the RMS Lusitania sank. His will left his collection to London, but an unwitnessed codicil gave 39 paintings to Dublin; a 1959 compromise split the bequest between the two cities, and a 1993 change kept 31 paintings in Ireland (Wikipedia). Under a 2021 partnership, 39 bequest paintings are shared with the National Gallery, London (Dublin City Council).
- Building: Charlemont House, a mansion designed by William Chambers in 1763 for James Caulfeild, 1st Earl of Charlemont. The gallery closed for reconstruction in 2004 and reopened in 2006 with an extension by Gilroy McMahon Architects (Wikipedia).
- Collection: Harry Clarke stained glass, works by Sean Scully, and Impressionist paintings by Degas, Monet, Manet and Morisot. Francis Bacon's London studio, dismantled from 1998 and rebuilt in the gallery in 2001, is part of the permanent collection (Dublin City Council; Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission was free (Wikipedia). The gallery closed on 28 September 2025 for refurbishment (Wikipedia). Dublin City Council and Dublin People (30 July 2025) said the work covers the 1930s wing and a new link to the planned city library, that the gallery was expected to stay closed for at least three years, and that the Bacon studio stays on site and is protected.

FUNDING
Hugh Lane Gallery: public: Dublin City Council
- Dublin City Council owns the gallery. In 2017 the gallery had received €11.6 million over the previous five years, and the council had set aside €3.5 million for a refurbishment (Dublin Inquirer, Cónal Thomas, 15 November 2017).
- The same article reports that the gallery said it needed €2.4 million of extra funding over five years: €1.2 million through council increases rising to €350,000 extra a year by 2022 and €1.2 million through its own income from fundraising, acquisitions, exhibitions and community engagement.
- Sir Hugh Lane met the gallery's running costs when he founded it in 1908, and his bequest of paintings forms part of its collection (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public: Dublin City Council funds it and owns the Trust that runs it. Private giving takes the form of the Lane bequest of paintings.
