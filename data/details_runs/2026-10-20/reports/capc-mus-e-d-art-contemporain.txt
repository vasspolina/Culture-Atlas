CAPC MUSÉE D'ART CONTEMPORAIN: Bordeaux, France
ID: capc-mus-e-d-art-contemporain
Website: https://www.capc-bordeaux.fr

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 7 rue Ferrère in the Chartrons district of Bordeaux, served by tram line B (French Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1973 as a centre for contemporary visual arts; it became the Musée d'art contemporain de la Ville de Bordeaux in 1984 and received the Musée de France label in 2002 (CAPC). French Wikipedia states that Jean-Louis Froment created it and directed it until 1996.
- Building: the Entrepôt Lainé, built in 1824 as a warehouse for colonial produce and designed by the engineer Claude Deschamps. The City bought it in 1973 after it was listed on the supplementary inventory of historic monuments. Architects Denis Valode and Jean Pistre led the renovations of 1984 to 1990 and Andrée Putman was the interior designer, with nearly 3,500 square metres of exhibition space (CAPC).
- French Wikipedia states the collection holds about a thousand works by 140 artists, including Richard Long's "White Rock Line" (1990).
- On site: a café, a library, a shop and residencies (CAPC).
- Visiting: open 11:00 to 18:00, Tuesday to Sunday, closed on public holidays. The site declares its accessibility partially compliant.

FUNDING
CAPC: municipal museum of the City of Bordeaux, with DRAC Nouvelle-Aquitaine as institutional partner and named corporate patrons
- The museum has been the Musée d'art contemporain de la Ville de Bordeaux since 1984 (CAPC). French Wikipedia states that the Ministry of Culture and the City gave it museum status that year.
- DRAC Nouvelle-Aquitaine is listed as institutional partner, with the Musée de France label (CAPC partners page, October 2026).
- Les Amis du Capc, an association, has supported the museum for more than twenty years (CAPC).
- CAPC partners page, October 2026: honorary patron Château Haut-Bailly; main patrons Les Amis du Capc, Palatine, CIC and Lacoste; other patrons Unikalo, Cushman & Wakefield, Château Camensac, Château Haut Selve, Hôtel de Normandie and Seko'o Hotel.
- Unverified: logo file names on the same page identify AXA and Fondation Aleksandr Savchuk as patrons of mediation projects, and Fluxus, EVL, Layher and RAJA ART as patrons of current exhibitions.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One documented dispute in the art press: the City's 2018 dismissal of the director, which criticises the city's handling, not the programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anna Sansom, "French art world shock at sacking of Bordeaux museum director" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/03/13/french-art-world-shock-at-sacking-of-bordeaux-museum-director
- The City of Bordeaux, which controls the museum, dismissed the director María Inés Rodríguez; the city said she faced "managerial difficulties."
- An open letter published in Libération and signed by 50 art professionals, including Christian Boltanski, Hans-Ulrich Obrist and Hou Hanru, called Rodríguez "a consummate professional" and called on the culture minister, Françoise Nyssen, to respond.
- The collector and CAPC Friends member Louis Nègre argued that because the museum answers directly to the mayor, it is hard to run.
- Le Monde cited falling visitor numbers and reports of staff depression and resignations; Rue89Bordeaux reported that employees were "upset" (as reported by The Art Newspaper).

WIDER CONTEXT
- French Wikipedia states that Karfa Diallo, director of the association Mémoires & Partages, criticised in a Mediapart op-ed of 31 August 2023 what he called "a semantic minimisation" of the Entrepôt Lainé's link to the crimes of slavery (translation). After a meeting on 21 September, the museum committed to revising its presentation text, and a memorial plaque or artwork was planned for 2024.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the CAPC is the City of Bordeaux, which runs it as its municipal museum, with DRAC Nouvelle-Aquitaine as institutional partner and named corporate patrons including Palatine, CIC and Lacoste. In 2018 the city dismissed the director María Inés Rodríguez, and 50 art professionals signed an open letter in Libération.
