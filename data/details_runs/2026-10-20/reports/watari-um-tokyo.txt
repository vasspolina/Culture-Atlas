WATARI-UM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: watari-um-tokyo
Website: http://www.watarium.co.jp/

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum in Jingumae, Shibuya, near Gaienmae Station on the Ginza Line (Time Out, Art Space Tokyo).
- Founded by Shizuko Watari and opened in September 1990; it grew out of the commercial Galerie Watari, which she directed from 1972 to 1989 (Wikipedia, Art iT). In 2012 she co-wrote a book on its activities with her children Koichi Watari and Etsuko Watari (Art iT); Koichi Watari is the museum's representative (Bijutsu Techo).
- The name "Watari-um" joins the family name Watari with "museum" (Wikipedia).
- Building by the Swiss architect Mario Botta: a six-storey building on a triangular plot shaped by the road widening for the 1964 Olympics (Wikipedia, Art Space Tokyo). The family approached Botta in 1985 and construction took about two years (Art Space Tokyo interview). The top floors hold offices and the owner's residence (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries on several floors, a museum shop on the first floor, and in the basement a cafe and the On Sundays bookshop (Art Space Tokyo). It holds four exhibitions a year (Time Out). Past shows include Joseph Beuys, Zhang Huan's "Chinese Contemporary Art 1997", Larry Clark, Henry Darger, Mike Kelley and Barry McGee (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 11 am to 7 pm, to 9 pm on Wednesdays; closed Mondays except national holidays and from 31 December to 4 January. Adults 1,000 yen, students under 25 800 yen, members free (Art Space Tokyo).

FUNDING
Watari-um: privately run: admission income, a 2020 crowdfunding campaign and corporate support
- Income: Bijutsu Techo describes the museum as privately run and dependent on ticket sales (26 August 2020), and as depending heavily on admission fees (15 November 2020).
- 2020 crowdfunding: in August 2020, after attendance fell to less than half of normal levels, the museum opened a CAMPFIRE campaign with a target of 5 million yen for operating costs, two exhibitions and digitising its video archive. Pledges reached 5,165,000 yen on the first day (Bijutsu Techo).
- In the November 2020 article Koichi Watari asked for wider tax deductions for people and companies that donate to museums (Bijutsu Techo).
- Etsuko Watari said the museum receives only a small amount of government support and gets more money from corporations (Art Space Tokyo interview).
- Collection: the contemporary art collection began around 1960 with Shizuko Watari's collecting (Art Space Tokyo interview).

BOTTOM LINE
Watari-um is a privately run museum founded by Shizuko Watari that depends heavily on ticket sales (Bijutsu Techo). Its 2020 crowdfunding round raised 5,165,000 yen on the first day, and Etsuko Watari says corporations give it more than the government does.
