VILLA DES ARTS DE CASABLANCA: Casablanca, Morocco
ID: villa-des-arts-de-casablanca-casablanca
Website: https://villadesarts.ma (checked 2026-10-24; returned HTTP 403 and could not be read; the Fondation Al Mada site refused the connection)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art space in an Art Deco villa in Casablanca, run as part of the Fondation Al Mada, which also runs a sister Villa des Arts in Rabat (La Vie eco, Aujourd'hui Le Maroc).
- Opening date: La Vie eco (2024) says the institution was founded in 2009. A search listing of Qantara, which could not be opened, gives June 1999. The sources conflict.
- Building: La Vie eco dates the villa to 1930 and calls it Art Deco and restored with care. Cultural Heritage Online says it dates from the 1930s in the Gauthier district. The architect and the restoration date are not given in the pages read.
- Programme: exhibitions of contemporary Moroccan and African art, workshops, and events. In March 2026 a family workshop series on the esplanade, "Couleurs du Maroc", ran with the Fondation Al Mada (Le360).
- Visiting: regular opening days, hours and admission were not found. A family workshop in March 2026 cost 50 dirhams per family (Le360).

FUNDING
Villa des Arts de Casablanca: corporate-foundation funded: Fondation Al Mada, the foundation of the holding company Al Mada
- La Vie eco names the Fondation Al Mada as the villa's parent foundation. Cultural Heritage Online says the foundation was formerly Fondation ONA.
- Al Mada, formerly Societe Nationale d'Investissement (SNI), is a private holding company founded in 1966, mainly owned by the Moroccan royal family. It merged with ONA Group in 2010 and took the name Al Mada in March 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Its holdings include Attijariwafa Bank, Inwi, Managem, Nareva, Marjane and Lafarge Ciments (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of accounts, a budget or other donors for the villa itself. Le Matin and the foundation's own pages could not be read.

Red flags: Al Mada's coal power
- Nareva is fully owned by SNI (Al Mada) and focuses on renewable and coal energy (Wikipedia).
- Safi power station: two 693 MW ultra-supercritical coal units plus a 4.6 MW biomass unit, in operation since December 2018. Nareva Holding held 50.7%, Mitsui 30.0% and ENGIE 19.3% (Global Energy Monitor).
- In January 2025 ENGIE sold 15.66% to Nareva. In October 2025 African Intelligence reported that Al Mada would take full control of the plant; Global Energy Monitor presents this as a report, not a completed sale.
- The Al Mada connection is as the villa's parent foundation. No reporting found of a protest or campaign aimed at the villa or the foundation.

Red flags: Al Mada transparency
- Leaked 2010 WikiLeaks cables raised doubts about the holding's transparency, and it has been accused of monopolistic practices and corruption (Wikipedia).
- Unverified: in July 2014 the magazine Le Point alleged that public tenders were shaped to benefit the king's companies, naming Nareva Energy Group. The palace's right of reply called this "completely untrue" and said tenders were run "in total transparency" (Morocco World News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The villa is funded through the foundation of Al Mada, a holding mainly owned by the royal family whose subsidiary Nareva co-owns the Safi coal power station. No funding figures for the villa exist in the pages read, and its own site and the foundation's site could not be opened. Worth watching: the founding date and whether the foundation publishes accounts.
