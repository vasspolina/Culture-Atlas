OVERVIEW
- Smithsonian museum of US history, science and technology on Constitution Avenue by the National Mall, Washington.
- Opened in 1964 as the Museum of History and Technology; renamed the National Museum of American History in 1980 (Wikipedia).
- From 2000 its official name carried the donor's name, "Behring Center" (Commonplace).
- The building was one of the last designed by McKim, Mead & White and stands on the site of two temporary war buildings from 1942. An 85 million dollar renovation from 2006 to 2008 added an atrium and a climate-controlled room for the Star-Spangled Banner (Wikipedia).
- A 37 million dollar west wing renovation began in 2012, with floors reopening in stages from 2015 to 2017.
- No reporting found of current opening hours or admission on the pages read.

FUNDING
National Museum of American History: mainly public: US Congress through the Smithsonian, with private donors
- The Smithsonian's budget is about 1.25 billion dollars a year, about two-thirds from federal appropriations; the rest comes from an endowment of about 2.86 billion dollars, private and corporate gifts, memberships and earned revenue (Wikipedia).
- The Smithsonian is governed by a 17-member Board of Regents that includes the Chief Justice and the Vice President (Wikipedia).
- Kenneth Behring gave 80 million dollars in 2000 and received an advisory role on two exhibits; see Red flags for the 2000 to 2002 donor dispute (Commonplace, 2002).
- No reporting found of a current donor list for this museum on the pages read.

Red flags: White House pressure on exhibits, 2025
- March 2025: President Trump signed the executive order "Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History", which alleged the Smithsonian was beholden to "a divisive, race-centered ideology" and targets "improper ideology" at its museums (PBS, Al Jazeera).
- The Washington Post reported in July 2025, and the Smithsonian confirmed on 1 August 2025, that the museum had removed a label saying Trump was impeached twice. The label was added in September 2021 as a temporary measure. The museum said the section was restored to its 2008 appearance and that "We were not asked by any Administration or other government officials to remove content from the exhibit" (PBS, Al Jazeera).
- Unverified: the Washington Post, relying on an unnamed person familiar with the plans, said the display came down after a content review that followed White House pressure to remove an art museum director (Al Jazeera).
- 12 August 2025: White House officials Lindsey Halligan, Vince Haley and Russell Vought wrote to Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch about a review of eight museums, this one included. Within 120 days museums were to begin replacing "divisive or ideologically driven language" (NBC News).

Red flags: donors and exhibit content, 2000 to 2002
- Kenneth Behring gave 80 million dollars in 2000, and the museum took the "Behring Center" name. In 2001 Catherine Reynolds pledged 38 million dollars for an exhibit on American achievers and, after a public backlash, withdrew all but 1.5 million. General Motors received naming rights to a transportation hall for 10 million dollars (Commonplace, October 2002).
- Critics, including a museum scholars' group, charged that Secretary Lawrence Small's donor relationships breached "established standards of museum practice and professional ethics"; museum curator Barbara Clark Smith argued Small wanted a "privatized public sphere" (Commonplace).
