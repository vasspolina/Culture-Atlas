SALT GALATA & SALT BEYOĞLU: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: salt-galata-istanbul
Website: https://saltonline.org

OVERVIEW
- Cultural institution with two Istanbul buildings: Salt Beyoğlu at İstiklal Caddesi 136 and Salt Galata at Bankalar Caddesi 11, Karaköy. Programmes are exhibitions, publications, digital projects, talks, conferences, film screenings, performances and workshops, covering art, architecture, design and social and economic history (Salt).
- Founded by Garanti BBVA in 2011. Garanti BBVA brought together Platform Garanti Contemporary Art Center, the Ottoman Bank Archives and Research Centre and Garanti Gallery, whose resources were transferred to Salt (Salt, History page).
- Founding director Vasıf Kortun served from 2011 to 2017, followed by Meriç Öner as Director of Research and Programs from 2017 to 2021 (Salt, History page).
- Salt Galata is the former headquarters of the Imperial Ottoman Bank (1892 to 1999), designed by the French-Levantine architect Alexandre Vallauri (spelling of Salt's page); it opened as Salt Galata in November 2011. It houses Salt Research (a library and archive), Mastercard Exhibition Hall and an auditorium, and the Ottoman Bank Museum.
- Salt Beyoğlu is a six-floor building on İstiklal Street from the second half of the 19th century, first called the Siniossoglou Apartment; it later hosted the Ottoman Bank Archives and Research Centre (1997 to 2000) and Platform Garanti Contemporary Art Center (2001 to 2010), and reopened as Salt Beyoğlu in 2011. Mimarlar Tasarım, founded by Han Tümertekin, refunctioned both buildings.
- Salt Beyoğlu closed in January 2016 citing "technical reasons" and reopened in April 2018 (Hyperallergic).
- Garanti BBVA's release of 22 May 2026 says Salt has had about 5 million visitors and has opened over 2 million archive documents and sources to public access (translated from Turkish).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday 11:00 to 19:00, Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, at both buildings. Admission and programmes are free.

FUNDING
SALT (Galata and Beyoğlu): corporate-founded: Garanti BBVA, with named corporate and individual supporters
- Garanti BBVA founded Salt in 2011; Hyperallergic calls Garanti "one of the biggest financial institutions in Turkey" and Salt's primary funder. Salt general manager Deniz Ova thanked the founder Garanti BBVA for its support, and Garanti BBVA deputy general manager Ebru Taşcı Firuzbay is a Salt board member (Garanti BBVA release, 22 May 2026; translated from Turkish).
- Wikipedia states that Garanti BBVA owns Salt's three exhibition spaces, including the Ankara space.
- Salt's supporters page lists, under corporate contributing institutions, Global Ports Holding and Global Yatırım Holding, with L'Internationale. Its halls are named for Gregory Michael Kiez, Mastercard (Mastercard Exhibition Hall) and Doğuş Holding A.Ş. (Ferit F. Şahenk Hall). Under Salt Research it lists Kalebodur, Kurukahveci Mehmet Efendi, Hande Bozdoğan, Murat Çağlar, Haldun Dostoğlu, Feride İkiz, the Hitay Foundation and Nilüfer Küçükoğlu. Services come from Jotun (paint), Eureko Sigorta (insurance) and Bankerhan Hotel.
- Salt Research Funds, a grants programme since 2013, has supported more than 80 projects; in 2025 the Garanti BBVA Economic History Fund expanded it (Garanti BBVA release).
- ArtReview (Laura McLean-Ferris, 2012) says Istanbul has almost no public sector funding, so wealthy families and the foundations they leave sustain institutions like Salt.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces. One questions Salt Beyoğlu's 2016 closure and 2018 reopening; the other is a structural point about private funding in Istanbul and does not criticise Salt itself.
1. Hyperallergic: Dorian Batycka, "Istanbul's Leading Contemporary Art Space Reopens in a Very Different Turkey" (23 May 2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/443986/salt-beyoglu-reopening-istanbul-turkey/
- Reports that many speculated Salt Beyoğlu's January 2016 closure was censorship of a progressive institution; Kortun said he could not speculate on other reasons and that permits were needed before programmes could resume.
- Asks whether the April 2018 reopening is something to celebrate, arguing that Turkey had entered a second, more dangerous state of censorship: self-censorship.
- Notes that Kortun announced his retirement in October 2016 and that curator November Paynter's departure was announced a few months later; a correction note on the page says the retirement had been planned internally but was not publicly known.
2. ArtReview: Laura McLean-Ferris, "Istanbul" (1 December 2012)
URL: https://artreview.com/research-and-rescue-istanbul/
- Argues that with almost no public sector in Istanbul, private money sustains institutions such as Salt and brings "an undertow of flux, whim and insecurity".
- Does not criticise Salt or Garanti; it presents Salt as a research-driven institution.

BOTTOM LINE
Salt is a bank-founded institution: Garanti BBVA created it in 2011, has a deputy general manager on its board, and Salt thanks it as its founder for its support; Wikipedia says Garanti BBVA owns Salt's exhibition spaces. Salt's supporters page names Global Ports Holding, Global Yatırım Holding, Mastercard, Doğuş Holding A.Ş. and individual donors. Hyperallergic reported in 2018 that many speculated the January 2016 to April 2018 closure of Salt Beyoğlu was censorship; Kortun said he could not speculate on other reasons.
