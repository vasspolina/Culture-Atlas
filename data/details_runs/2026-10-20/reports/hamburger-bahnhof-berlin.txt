HAMBURGER BAHNHOF, NATIONALGALERIE DER GEGENWART: Berlin, Germany
ID: hamburger-bahnhof-berlin
Website: https://www.smb.museum/museen-einrichtungen/hamburger-bahnhof (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art of the Nationalgalerie, part of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, in a former railway terminus.
- The Berlin Senate decided in 1987 to make a contemporary art museum in the station after the entrepreneur Erich Marx offered his collection; it opened in November 1996 with a Sigmar Polke exhibition.
- Named for the Berlin to Hamburg railway, whose terminus it was: built to plans by Friedrich Neuhaus in 1846/47, it stopped working as a station in 1884 and housed a transport museum from 1906 to 1944.
- Josef Paul Kleihues won the 1989 competition and converted the building from 1990 to 1996; the Rieckhallen, a former freight depot, were rebuilt in 2004 to show the Friedrich Christian Flick Collection.
- The Marx collection includes Beuys, Kiefer, Rauschenberg, Twombly and Warhol; a Dan Flavin neon has lit the façade since 1996.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, Thursday until 8 pm, closed Monday; under-18s free and a 50 percent discount for students, apprentices, volunteers, ALG I recipients and people with a severe disability.

FUNDING
Hamburger Bahnhof: mainly public: the federal government and the Länder through the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz; BMW sponsors the museum's prize
- The museum belongs to the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz (SPK). Under the funding agreement of 12 March 2025, in force from 1 January 2026, the federal government pays 75 percent (€101,234,100) of a €134,978,800 base for operating costs and the Länder 25 percent (€33,744,700); Berlin's share is €11,754,600. The federal government alone pays for new buildings and renovation (Brandenburg treaty register).
- The federal government paid €66 million for the Hamburger Bahnhof building by November 2022; the state of Berlin bought the Rieckhallen for about €100 million through funds and a land swap, after the owner CA Immo planned to demolish them (Wikipedia).
- The Preis der Nationalgalerie, shown at the museum, is made possible by the Freunde der Nationalgalerie; BMW Group has supported it as exclusive partner since 2006 (BMW press release, 26 April 2023). No amount is published.
- Friedrich Christian Flick lent about 1,500 works from 2004 and paid €8 million to renovate the Rieckhallen; he ended the loan in 2021 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of other corporate sponsors of the museum's programme.

Red flags: protest over the Flick Collection
- The Flick Collection opened at the museum in September 2004 with Chancellor Gerhard Schröder; former slave labourers and others protested outside (World Jewish Congress, 22 September 2004).
- Friedrich Flick, the collector's grandfather, built a fortune in the Second World War by backing the Nazis and using forced labour; victims' groups said "blood money" financed the collection, and Flick was criticised for not paying into compensation funds (World Jewish Congress).
- The loan ended in 2021; the flag rests on the protest at this museum.

Red flags: protest disrupted the Tania Bruguera performance
- Pro-Palestine activists disrupted Bruguera's 100-hour reading of Hannah Arendt's "The Origins of Totalitarianism" twice on 10 February 2024; the reading had begun on 7 February and was due to end on 11 February (The Art Newspaper).
- Protesters asked Bruguera "why do you give a platform to Zionists?"; the museum said they used violent hate speech toward a reader and a museum director, and Bruguera closed the work (The Art Newspaper).
- Directors Sam Bardaouil and Till Fellrath backed her decision and rejected all hate speech and violence (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution itself. One e-flux review criticises the museum's handling of the Bruguera protest; the Flick dispute of 2004 was covered mainly in general press.
1. e-flux Criticism: Eugene Yiu Nam Cheung, "Tania Bruguera's 'Where Your Ideas Become Civic Actions (100 Hours Reading The Origins of Totalitarianism)'" (2024)
URL: https://e-flux.com/criticism/592489/tania-bruguera-s-where-your-ideas-become-civic-actions-100-hours-reading-the-origins-of-totalitarianism
- The reading was more performative than serious and detached from the present, leaving out Arendt's critiques of Israel's treatment of Palestinians.
- Defends the disruptions as bringing material stakes back into the event; the museum's open forum had limits.
- Notes the institution filed a police report and that it was unclear which language was judged "hate speech"; places this in a German climate shaped by Erinnerungskultur and Staatsräson that he calls "censorious".
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Tania Bruguera's reading at the Hamburger Bahnhof shut down after pro-Palestine protestors storm event" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/02/12/tania-brugueras-reading-at-the-hamburger-bahnhof-shut-down-after-pro-palestine-protestors-storm-event
- Not criticism: a news report with the museum's statement and the protesters' slogans.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's running money is federal and Länder money through the SPK, which is not a flag. The weak points are the history of the Flick loan, protested in 2004 and ended in 2021, and the 2024 Bruguera dispute; BMW's sponsorship of the museum's prize, with no published amount, is worth watching.
