SCULPTURECENTER: New York, USA
ID: sculpturecenter
Website: https://www.sculpture-center.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting contemporary art museum at 44-19 Purves Street, Long Island City, Queens (sculpture-center.org, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1928 by the sculptor Dorothea H. Denslow as The Clay Club in Crown Heights, Brooklyn, first as a studio shared with local children and later other artists; it later moved to the West Village (1932) and to a carriage house on East 69th Street (1948), where it ran an artists' school (Wikipedia).
- In 2001 the board closed the school, sold the carriage house for $4.75 million and moved to Queens as a European-style kunsthalle (Wikipedia).
- Building: Maya Lin and David Hotson converted a derelict brick building into a 6,000 square foot exhibition space; Andrew Berman led a $4.5 million expansion in 2014 that raised exhibition space to 6,700 square feet and added a courtyard of 1,500 square feet (Wikipedia).
- What is there: about three exhibition cycles a year and work by more than 750 artists shown (Wikipedia); the "In Practice" series is named on the support page. Current exhibitions to 28 December 2026: Aziz Hazara, Cevdet Erek and Yining Fei (sculpture-center.org).
- Visiting: open Thursday to Monday, 12pm to 6pm (sculpture-center.org). No reporting found of the admission price or accessibility details from the pages read.

FUNDING
SculptureCenter: mixed: private foundations and individuals, with New York City and New York State arts money
- Finances (Form 990, ProPublica): for the year to June 2025, expenses $2,609,276, revenue $2,134,666 and contributions $2,068,454; net assets $6,819,397. In the year to June 2024 revenue was $3,620,043 and contributions $3,518,731.
- Leadership donors for exhibitions and programmes: the Anna-Maria and Stephen Kellen Foundation, the Teiger Foundation, Carol Bove and Barbara von Portatius. Major donors for exhibitions and programmes include the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Richard Chang, Jill and Peter Kraus, Miyoung Lee and Neil Simpkins, Eleanor Heyman Propp and Jacques Louis Vidal (sculpture-center.org support page, undated).
- Annual operations: Lambent Foundation Fund of Tides Foundation (leadership), Hartwig Art Foundation, the A. Woodner Fund and Irene and Allen H. Brill (major).
- Government: New York City Department of Cultural Affairs with the City Council, and the New York State Council on the Arts with the Governor's office and the State Legislature. The page gives no amounts.
- Collaborative Arts Network New York funders: Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, Hearthland Foundation, David Rockefeller Fund, Arison Arts Foundation. In Practice: the Elaine Graham Weitzen Commissioning Fund and the Pollock-Krasner Foundation (major).
- The page lists an International Council of individuals, including Füsun Eczacıbaşı of SAHA.
- Board officers: Helen Warwick (chair), Anicka Yi (president), Justin Beal and Eleanor Heyman Propp (vice chairs), Andreas Waldburg-Wolfegg (treasurer) and Michael Quinn (secretary). Director: Sohrab Mohebbi (sculpture-center.org). Eleanor Heyman Propp, and Jane Hait with Justin Beal, also appear on the donor list.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder among the names listed.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the institution found in art media. The two reviews read criticise individual shows.
1. Hyperallergic: Seph Rodney, "When the Language Meant to Prop Up Art Makes It Fall Over" (2017)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/when-the-language-meant-to-prop-up-art-makes-it-fall-over/
- Argues the group show "In Practice: Material Deviance" does not meet its own curatorial statement.
- Says the show's press release promises more than the works deliver, and calls SculptureCenter "a captivating space." Criticises the exhibition, not the institution.
2. Hyperallergic: Benjamin Sutton, "An Exhibition of Sea Creatures and Beach Gear Treads Water" (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/an-exhibition-of-sea-creatures-and-beach-gear-treads-water/
- Finds the maritime-themed sculptures visually appealing but "conceptually aimless", and the curatorial idea too vague to carry the show.
- Criticises the exhibition, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a mix of private foundations, collectors and New York City and State arts funds, with Form 990 figures public and no flagged funder in the names listed. The weak points are that donors are listed without dates or amounts, and that board members Eleanor Heyman Propp and Justin Beal also appear on the donor list. Worth watching: the donor page, which is undated, and the Hartwig Art Foundation and Lambent Foundation lines.
