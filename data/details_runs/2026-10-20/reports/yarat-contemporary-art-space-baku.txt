YARAT CONTEMPORARY ART SPACE: Baku, Azerbaijan
ID: yarat-contemporary-art-space-baku
Website: https://yarat.az/ (checked 2026-10-24; working, but it lists no hours, admission, funders or board on the page opened)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in a former Soviet naval building on the Baku seafront by the Caspian Sea (name: Azerbaijani for "to create"), about 1.5 miles along the waterfront (The Art Newspaper, 30 April 2015).
- Founded in 2011 by the artist Aida Mahmudova; the building opened on 24 March 2015. Its 2013 show was held in an abandoned air-conditioning factory on the city's outskirts (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- About 2,000 square metres of exhibition space (Wikipedia).
- Other sites: the Painting Museum, ARTIM Project Space in the Old City (opened October 2015), YAY Gallery and YARAT Studios; programmes include an art school and a residency (Wikipedia, YARAT website).
- No reporting found of opening days or admission.

FUNDING
YARAT Contemporary Art Space: undisclosed: no funder list or accounts published; the founder says private, government help acknowledged
- In a 2015 Forbes interview Mahmudova said YARAT receives "technical support" from the government but is not government-funded (as reported by Hyperallergic, 2 November 2020). The Art Newspaper (30 April 2015) says she received government help with the site but did not name the Heydar Aliyev Foundation as a backer.
- Hyperallergic (28 February 2021) says "there are no public records for YARAT" and that Mahmudova reportedly claimed an unnamed bank backs it.
- Unverified: Meydan TV (27 January 2017, unsigned) says YARAT receives "large contributions" from telecom companies, and that the daughters of President Aliyev are beneficiaries of Azercell and Azerfon. The text gives no evidence.
- March 2026: APA (18 March 2026) lists YARAT among the partners of the "Beyond the Frame" exhibition at the Stone Chronicle Museum, with bp, PASHA Holding and the Regional Development Public Union of the Heydar Aliyev Foundation; SOCAR Midstream Gas Operations was initiator and supporter.

Red flags: ties to the Aliyev family and state
- Family: Hyperallergic (2020) calls Mahmudova the niece of Ilham Aliyev; The Art Newspaper (2015) says First Lady Mehriban Aliyeva, who leads the Heydar Aliyev Foundation, is her aunt.
- State help: The Art Newspaper (2015) reports that Mahmudova received government help with the site.
- Unverified: Hyperallergic (2021) argues YARAT is funded "through the Aliyevs", an inference it draws from the absence of records.
Red flags: protest by an exhibiting artist
- On 25 October 2020 YARAT's Instagram showed its facade with an Azerbaijani flag and the hashtag #KarabakhIsAzerbaijan. After failed talks, the artist Ahmet Öğüt published an open letter objecting that his work was being used politically (Hyperallergic, 2 November 2020: letter and YARAT's agreement to end the show on 29 October; image kept up). ArtReview (30 October 2020) says the show, due to run to 20 November, was terminated on 28 October at his request, and quotes his accusation that the centre used it as a "propaganda tool".
Red flags: fossil fuel partners
- bp (partner) and SOCAR Midstream Gas Operations (initiator and supporter) were named alongside YARAT in that APA report; no payment to YARAT is stated.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is recurring and centres on state ties and the 2020 Öğüt episode. Hyperallergic's 2021 essay is advocacy.
1. Hyperallergic: Dorian Batycka, "Artist Accuses Azerbaijani Art Center of Using His Work as 'Propaganda Tool'" (2 November 2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/598624/yarat-contemporary-art-space-propaganda-tool
- Argues YARAT is politically aligned with the government despite claiming independence.
- Cites the 2017 exhibition "Neither War, Nor Peace" (press statement: "strengthen patriotic moods") and the Öğüt dispute.
2. Hyperallergic: Nevdon Jamgochian, "Artwashing a Dictatorship" (28 February 2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/615519/artwashing-a-dictatorship/
- An advocacy essay. Argues "it is impossible to see how this organization is funded in any way but through the Aliyevs"; says YARAT had not responded to requests for comment.
- Presents this as inference, not documents.
3. ArtReview: unsigned news item, "Baku art centre pulls exhibition amid political controversy" (30 October 2020)
URL: https://artreview.com/baku-art-centre-pulls-exhibition-amid-political-controversy/
- Reports Öğüt's request to close the show and notes some in the art world are concerned the centre is funded through the founder's family link to President Aliyev.
- No statement from YARAT in the article.
4. Meydan TV: "When Authoritarianism and Contemporary Art Cross Paths" (27 January 2017, unsigned)
URL: https://www.meydan.tv/en/article/when-authoritarianism-and-contemporary-art-cross-paths/
- Quotes former YARAT assistant curator Elene Kapanadze: "there is definitely a lot of self-censoring present among the contemporary Azerbaijani artists". Not an art-press outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
YARAT publishes no funder list or accounts, so its money cannot be checked. The documented weak points are the founder's family tie to the Aliyev family, government help with the site, the 2020 Öğüt protest and the naming of bp and SOCAR Midstream Gas Operations beside YARAT on a 2026 exhibition. Worth watching: published accounts.
