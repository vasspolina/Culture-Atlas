FONDATION ZINSOU: Cotonou, Benin
ID: fondation-zinsou-cotonou
Website: https://fondationzinsou.org/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation in Benin with two sites: the Ouidah Museum in the Villa Ajavon, behind the Basilica of Ouidah, and the Cotonou LAB, an exhibition space (the foundation's website).
- Founded by Marie-Cécile Zinsou, who conceived it in 2003 after noticing that Benin had no structure for contemporary artists; a free exhibition space opened in Ouidah's Villa Avajon in 2005 (Carenews, 11 March 2015). The foundation marks its 20th anniversary in 2025 (the foundation's website).
- By 2015 the Ouidah space had hosted 24 exhibitions, and travelling shows had gone to Belgium, the United States and the Paris metro (a 2011 photo project), the foundation had six libraries in working-class neighbourhoods and partnered with 364 schools; the libraries had more than 115,000 readers in 2014 (Carenews, 2015).
- A bus collects children across Cotonou at no cost (Carenews, 2015).
- 2025 programme: "Promesse", a carte blanche to the artist Joël Andrianomearisoa for the anniversary; earlier shows include the Lagos Photo Festival's first Benin edition in 2023 at the Ouidah museum and the LAB (the foundation's website).
- Ouidah Museum: open Wednesday to Sunday 09:00 to 18:00, Tuesday 13:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; Cotonou LAB: Monday 14:00 to 19:00, Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 19:00, closed Sunday; entry to both is free (the foundation's website).

FUNDING
Fondation Zinsou: private patronage: the Zinsou family and a circle of patrons
- The Zinsou family finances the project, and patrons helped fund the children's bus (Carenews, 2015). Wikipedia says Marie-Cécile Zinsou set up and ran the foundation "with funding from her family", and names her father as Lionel Zinsou.
- The foundation's patrons page lists names in two circles. Circle of Patrons and Friends, individuals: Marie-Cécile Zinsou, Paul-Emmanuel Reiffers, Kamel Mennour, Elena and Pierre Tattevin, Agnès Dumas-Perpitch, Béatrice Charon, Michèle and Olivier Picot, Cécile and Clyde Fakhoury, Hassanein Hiridjee, Sandrine Aholou, Sandra Terdjman, the Bakayoko family, Agnès Schmitz-Schweitzer, Denise Vilgrain and India Mahdavi.
- Companies and institutions in that circle: Scil Bénin Peinture, Masathis, Ancelle et Associés, La Mode en Images, Fondation H, AKAT, BNP Paribas and Institut Français Paris with the Agence Française de Développement.
- Circle of Major Patrons: Lionel and Marie-Christine Zinsou, Christian Langlois-Meurinne, Mercedes Villardell, Marie-Sophie Eiché Demester, Joël Andrianomearisoa, Professor René Zinsou, Open Society Foundations, and Karine and Laurent Gosseaume.
- Fondation H is the Antananarivo art foundation set up by Hassanein Hiridjee, who is also listed as an individual patron.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Unnamed critics doubted the project early on, as one 2015 profile reports.
1. Carenews PRO (no individual author): "[D'AILLEURS] Une fondation culturelle active depuis dix ans au Bénin" (11 March 2015, updated 17 March 2015)
URL: https://www.carenews.com/fr/news/2556-d-ailleurs-une-fondation-culturelle-active-depuis-dix-ans-au-benin
- Early critics asked why a poor country should open a culture foundation, and whether Africa should prioritise contemporary art given war, famine and AIDS; one person said that if it were a good idea, it would already exist (translated).
- Zinsou points to the foundation's results and to a collective effort.
- A profile, not an art-press piece; the critics are not named.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In August 2019 a Benin court gave Lionel Zinsou a suspended six-month jail term and a five-year ban from standing for election, for using false documents and breaking spending limits in his 2016 presidential campaign. Vincent Foly, editor-in-chief of La Nouvelle Tribune, said President Patrice Talon takes seemingly minor decisions that target opponents (Al Jazeera, AFP, 2 August 2019). The article does not mention the foundation.
- Lionel Zinsou's relation to the foundation: the foundation's patrons page lists "Lionel et Marie-Christine Zinsou" in its Circle of Major Patrons, and Wikipedia names Lionel Zinsou as Marie-Cécile Zinsou's father. The ruling concerned his campaign, not the foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the foundation is the Zinsou family's funding plus donors for specific projects (Carenews, 2015). The foundation's patrons page names BNP Paribas, Open Society Foundations and Institut Français Paris with the Agence Française de Développement among its patrons. Entry to both sites is free.
