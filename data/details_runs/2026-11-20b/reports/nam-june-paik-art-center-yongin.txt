NAM JUNE PAIK ART CENTER (백남준아트센터): Yongin, South Korea
ID: nam-june-paik-art-center-yongin
Website: https://njp.ggcf.kr/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public art centre at 10 Paiknamjune-ro, Giheung-gu, Yongin, Gyeonggi Province (Gyeonggi Province English site); Wikipedia names Giheung station as the nearest stop.
- The basic plan was drawn up with the artist Nam June Paik (백남준) in November 2001; building ran from 29 August 2006 to 30 April 2008, and the centre opened with a Nam June Paik Festival on 8 October 2008 (Gyeonggi Province). Paik died in 2006 without seeing it (Korea JoongAng Daily; Kyeonggi Ilbo).
- Named for Paik, whose work forms the permanent collection; it also holds temporary shows and awards the Nam June Paik Art Center Prize (Wikipedia).
- Building: by Kirsten Schemel and Marina Stankovic of KSMS Schemel Stankovic Architects, Berlin, winners of a 2003 international competition of 430 entries; 5,600 square metres on three floors above ground and two below, behind a facade of layered reflective and partly printed glass; galleries, a video archive and multi-purpose spaces (centre website).
- Director: Park Namhee (박남희), who opened the first Nam June Paik Media Art Festival, "Waiting for UFO", on 16 July 2026, marking 20 years since Paik's death (Newsis; Kyeongin Ilbo).
- "현대 트랜스로컬 시리즈: 하늘의 잔물결을 따라" (own translation: Hyundai Translocal Series: Following the Ripples of the Sky) runs from 5 November 2026 to 28 March 2027 (centre website).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:00; closed Mondays. Admission is free, though special exhibitions can charge; volunteer docent tours, sign-language tours, free wheelchair and pushchair loan (centre website).

FUNDING
Nam June Paik Art Center: public: Gyeonggi Province through the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation, with Hyundai Motor money for exhibitions
- Operator: the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation (경기문화재단), a registered donation body and professional arts corporation; donations to the centre are receipted in the foundation's name (centre website).
- Building and collection: Gyeonggi Province spent 13 billion won buying Paik's works and 39 billion won building the centre (Kyeonggi Ilbo editorial, June 2025).
- Running budget: the 2025 exhibition budget was 510 million won, against about 300 million won per show; the collection-purchase budget was zero in 2025, down from about 300 million won in 2018 and 2019 (Kyeonggi Ilbo editorial).
- Hyundai Motor: the 2025 programme reached four exhibitions "thanks to Hyundai Motor's sponsorship" (Kyeonggi Ilbo, own translation). Hyundai Motor runs the Hyundai Translocal Series, launched in 2025, and supports partner institutions' multi-year research, commissions, exhibitions and publications; the centre and the Pinacoteca de São Paulo co-organised its November 2026 exhibition (Hyundai Motor Group, 23 June 2026). Amounts are not published.
- The centre invites individual donations and corporate and organisational sponsorship for exhibitions, education, research, acquisitions and publishing (centre website).

Red flags: Hyundai Motor and child labour in its Alabama supply chain (2022 to 2024)
- July 2022: Reuters reported that SMART, then a Hyundai subsidiary in Luverne, Alabama, employed children as young as 12; SMART supplies Hyundai's Montgomery plant (Wikipedia, citing Reuters).
- 29 September 2022: the US Department of Labor fined SL Alabama, another supplier, $30,076, and a federal consent decree barred it from shipping goods made with underage labour. Neither labour department then accused Hyundai or Kia of wrongdoing (Wikipedia).
- 24 February 2023: Hyundai CEO Jae Hoon Chang told shareholders Hyundai would divest its controlling stake in SMART (Wikipedia).
- 30 May 2024: the Department of Labor sued Hyundai Motor Manufacturing Alabama, SMART Alabama and the staffing firm Best Practice Service, alleging they jointly employed a 13-year-old girl for up to 50 to 60 hours a week. Hyundai called it "an unprecedented legal theory" (CBS News). The suit has not ended in a ruling against Hyundai.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism of the centre; one regional newspaper editorial criticises the province for underfunding it.
1. Kyeonggi Ilbo (editorial): "백남준 예술, 이 훌륭한 자산이 뭉개지고 있다" (2025; own translation: Paik's art, this fine asset, is being crushed)
URL: https://www.kyeonggi.com/article/20250622580269
- Argues the province bought the works and built the centre, then let it shrink: falling budgets, too few shows, cramped galleries and storage.
- Notes the centre could not buy Paik's "Hacker Newbie" (1994) at a February 2025 Seoul Auction sale for 150 million won.
- Suggests opening the centre to private ideas and partners.

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly public provincial money, with a growing reliance on Hyundai Motor for exhibitions. That reliance on a company with an open child-labour case, at undisclosed amounts, is the weak point.
