LUNDS KONSTHALL: Lund, Sweden
ID: lunds-konsthall-lund
Website: https://www.lundskonsthall.se

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art hall at Mårtenstorget 3 in Lund, "part of Lunds kommun" (the hall's website, title translated).
- Opened on 21 September 1957 with the exhibition "Svensk nutidskonst"; it mainly shows Swedish and Nordic contemporary art, with one or more international exhibitions most years (Swedish Wikipedia).
- Designed by architect Klas Anshelm, with high and low rooms, separate spaces for sculpture and large paintings, and open balconies (Swedish Wikipedia).
- Gamla Sparbanken, a savings bank, donated the money for the building to the city of Lund; the bank set up a foundation in 1931 to create a concert hall and art salon in Lund (Swedish Wikipedia).
- Swedish Wikipedia lists Åsa Nacking as director from 2003.
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 12:00 to 17:00 and Thursday 12:00 to 20:00; closed Monday (the hall's website).

FUNDING
Lunds Konsthall: municipal: Lund municipality
- The hall's website states that it is part of Lunds kommun; Swedish Wikipedia calls it a municipal art hall.
- Lund's Board of Culture and Leisure took the December 2015 decision on the hall's future. Kunstkritikk reports that a conservative minority with support from the Sweden Democrats intended to save 3 million Swedish kronor from the budget; board chairman Joakim Friberg (Social Democrat) claims the reconstruction is unrelated to the savings of around 3 million kronor (Frida Sandström, 17 December 2015).

Red flags: 2015 protest over the municipal decision to reconstruct the hall
- The protest was aimed at the hall's own owner: the Board of Culture and Leisure of the City of Lund, which runs the hall. Swedish Wikipedia states that on 10 December 2015 the board voted to close the hall with the aim of reopening it later in reconstructed form.
- Kunstkritikk reports that the board announced the hall would be "reconstructed" to suit "the present and future Lund", which critics read as a shutdown (Frida Sandström, 17 December 2015).
- Director Åsa Nacking said: "An artspace with sixty years of history is being shut down without discussion. The staff and myself are shocked." The artist Matts Leiderstam called the decision another attempt by the board to get rid of Nacking (Kunstkritikk).
- An open letter signed by museum directors, university professors and deans appeared in Sydsvenska Dagbladet on 15 December 2015 and was posted on e-flux on 16 December, translated by Anders Kreuger. It calls the plan a "hurried decision" and says the City of Lund "would commit a serious error" (e-flux).
- Board vice chairman Peter Bergwall (Green Party) said he could not understand how the director saw the decision as a shutdown (Kunstkritikk, 17 December 2015). Kreuger wrote on 3 February 2016 that the politicians said they never meant to close the hall and planned to hire an outside consultant to study how it could reach more target groups (Kunstkritikk).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The three pieces date from 2015 and 2016 and are aimed at the municipal board's decision on the hall.
1. Kunstkritikk: Frida Sandström, "The End of Lund's Konsthall as We Know It" (17 December 2015)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/lunds-konsthall-to-be-discontinued/
- Reports that Nacking said the decision was made without prior communication, a point Bergwall disputes, and quotes Kreuger and the artist Matts Leiderstam on it; Leiderstam calls it another attempt to get rid of Nacking.
- Quotes Bergwall and Friberg; it could not reach Director of Culture Chris Schenlaer.
2. e-flux: "An open letter in support of Lunds konsthall" (16 December 2015, first published in Sydsvenska Dagbladet on 15 December; translated by Anders Kreuger)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/28536/an-open-letter-in-support-of-lunds-konsthall/
- Ten signatory entries, museum directors, academics and an art academy dean among them, call the decision unfortunate for both the cultural field and democracy, and say there should be investment in professional development instead of discontinuing activities.
- Links the decision to the government's October report on museums, which proposes keeping museums "at an arm's length" from political power.
3. Kunstkritikk: Anders Kreuger, "Lund's Konsthall: Time and Again" (3 February 2016)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/lunds-konsthall-time-and-again/
- Argues the conflict is political, between visual art's demand for autonomy and "cultural animation" aimed at lowering the threshold for everyone.
- Describes Nacking as a "seasoned institutional practitioner" with a "critically acclaimed exhibition program" and says the conflict is political, not about her competence.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Lunds Konsthall is municipal: Lund municipality owns and runs it, from a building paid for by a savings bank's donation. The documented issue is the 2015 protest against the municipal board's decision to reconstruct the hall.
