PINCHUKARTCENTRE: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: pinchukartcentre
Website: https://pinchukartcentre.org/en/about (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre in a historic architectural complex in Kyiv's Besarabka area, with interiors by the French architect Philippe Chiambaretta (centre website).
- Founded by the businessman Victor Pinchuk in September 2006 (centre website); it opened on 16 September 2006 (Wikipedia).
- It occupies six floors: exhibition space on four, more than 3,000 square metres in all, plus a library, an education room and a video lounge (centre website).
- Programmes: the Research Platform, an archive of Ukrainian art from the early 1980s on, launched in 2016; the PinchukArtCentre Prize and the Future Generation Art Prize for artists aged 35 or younger (centre website). It counts more than 3.5 million visitors since opening (centre website).
- It closed for 143 days after Russia's full-scale invasion and reopened on 17 July 2022 (Wikipedia). In 2026 it presented "Still Joy" at the Venice Biennale (centre website).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 pm to 9 pm, last entry 8:30 pm; entry and events are free; the centre closes during air alerts (centre website).

FUNDING
PinchukArtCentre: self-funded: founder Victor Pinchuk and his foundation
- The centre names Pinchuk as its founder and charges no admission (centre website).
- Pinchuk founded Interpipe, a producer of seamless pipes and railway wheels, in 1990, and the London investment firm EastOne in 2006; before the invasion he and his wife were the largest shareholders of the broadcaster StarLightMedia (Wikipedia).
- The Victor Pinchuk Foundation set up the Future Generation Art Prize in 2009, a $100,000 award of which $40,000 must go to new work, with the centre organising it (Wikipedia).
- The 2022 reopening show, "When Faith Moves Mountains", was organised with M HKA in Antwerp and the Flemish Government (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2004 Pinchuk and Rinat Akhmetov bought the Kryvorizhstal steel plant for about $800 million; Pinchuk's father-in-law, President Leonid Kuchma, authorised the sale, which competitors said was far below market rate (Wikipedia).
- The plant was returned to the state in June 2005 after the government challenged the privatisation, which President Viktor Yushchenko called a theft; on 24 October 2005 Mittal Steel bought it at a televised auction for more than $4.8 billion (AP, via NBC News).
- A 2007 cable by US ambassador William B. Taylor said the Nikopol Ferroalloy Plant tenders of 2003 and 2004 were "rigged in favor of" Pinchuk, "like the subsequent sale of the Kryvorizhstal steel plant" (Kyiv Post).
- Guides who formed a union in October 2019 protested near the centre on 25 December 2019; their fixed-term contracts were not renewed and on 9 February 2020 the centre abolished the post (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper counted 28 guides dismissed, the Kyiv Post about 20. Artists Valentina Petrova and Pavlo Grazhdanskij withdrew from the 2020 PinchukArtCentre Prize in solidarity (The Art Newspaper).
- The Trump Foundation's 2015 tax return showed $150,000 from Pinchuk's London-based charity; his press service said in November 2016 that it paid for Donald Trump's video call to the Yalta European Strategy conference, which the Victor Pinchuk Foundation organises (UNIAN, citing Fast Company and Ukrayinska Pravda).
- Unverified: Ihor Kolomoisky told a parliamentary hearing in March 2015 that Pinchuk took $5 million a month for the right to manage Ukrnafta; the National Anti-Corruption Bureau opened criminal proceedings on 18 March 2015 (Wikipedia).
- These concern the founder's business and politics, and the centre's own staff before November 2021.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre runs on one man's money, which carries no current flag. Its weak point is how that fortune grew under his father-in-law's presidency.
