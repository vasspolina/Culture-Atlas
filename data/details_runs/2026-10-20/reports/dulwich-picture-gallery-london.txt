DULWICH PICTURE GALLERY: London, UK
ID: dulwich-picture-gallery-london
Website: https://www.dulwichpicturegallery.org.uk (checked 2026-10-20; returned a "Just a moment" block page to every fetch, so its own pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Independent art gallery on Gallery Road, Dulwich, south London SE21, which Wikipedia calls the oldest public art gallery in England; it opened to Royal Academy students in 1815 and to the public in 1817.
- Founded on the collection Noël Desenfans and Sir Francis Bourgeois assembled from 1790 for King Stanisław August Poniatowski of Poland; the partitions of Poland meant it was never delivered, and Bourgeois bequeathed it to the College of God's Gift (Wikipedia).
- John Soane designed the top-lit building and a mausoleum where the founders are buried. A V1 flying bomb damaged it on 12 July 1944; it reopened in 1953. Rick Mather added a café, education rooms, a lecture theatre and disabled access in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Over 600 old master paintings (the New York Times, quoted in the charity accounts), exhibitions, learning programmes, and from 2025 a three-acre Sculpture Garden with the Lovington Sculpture Meadow by Kim Wilkie and an ArtPlay Pavilion (accounts 2025). Jennifer Scott has been director since April 2017; Dame Janet Vitmayer chairs the trustees.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, and bank holiday Mondays; National Art Pass holders enter free, and there is no reduced-price entry for exhibitions (Art Fund).

FUNDING
Dulwich Picture Gallery: private: donations, admissions, trading and a £26 million endowment
- In the year to 31 March 2025 income was £5,425,000: donations and legacies £2,222,000 (of which £1,152,000 for its Open Art and Future Plan projects), admissions £1,166,000, trading £1,127,000 and investment income £809,000 (charity accounts).
- Endowment funds stood at £26,299,000; the gallery draws 3.5% of the portfolio a year, £945,000 for 2025-26 (charity accounts).
- Open Art's principal supporter is The Lovington Foundation; major supporters include the Julia Rausing Trust, Manton Foundation, Wolfson Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Kusuma Trust, National Lottery Heritage Fund, Gatsby Charitable Foundation, Linbury Trust and Swire Charitable Trust (charity accounts).
- Corporate supporters include Bloomberg Philanthropies, Rathbones, Sotheby's, Japan Airlines and Mylands; public money includes Heritage Lottery Fund Catalyst endowments (£2,797,000 together), an Arts Council England/V&A purchase grant and the Government Indemnity Scheme (charity accounts).
- Deputy director Chantelle Culshaw chairs the Christina Smith Foundation, which gave £75,000 to the Future Plan (related-party note).

Red flags: Sackler endowment still held
- The Director fund, £3,430,000 at 31 March 2025, "represents funding received from Dr. Mortimer & Teresa Sackler Foundation in support of the Director role" (charity accounts).
- The gallery quietly dropped "Sackler Director" from the director's title on 1 April 2022, without an announcement; Scott declined to disclose the sum given (The Art Newspaper).
- The £1,414,000 Sackler Education fund was renamed the Education fund in the 2025 accounts.
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter aimed at Dulwich.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the gallery's programme found; the one critical news item concerns the quiet handling of the Sackler name.
1. The Art Newspaper: Martin Bailey, "Dulwich Picture Gallery latest to drop Sackler name" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/06/02/dulwich-picture-gallery-drops-sackler-name
- Reports that the gallery did not answer questions about the director's title in March and April 2022 and removed the name without notice.
- Sets the change beside the National Portrait Gallery, Serpentine, Tate, British Museum and National Gallery, which had dropped or refused Sackler money.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery runs on admissions, trading, donations from British trusts and a large endowment. The weak point is that a £3.4 million endowment paying for the director's post came from the Dr. Mortimer & Teresa Sackler Foundation and is still held, after the name was dropped quietly. Worth watching: whether the gallery says how it uses the Sackler funds.
