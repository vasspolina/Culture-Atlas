SCHAULAGER: Basel, Switzerland
ID: schaulager
Website: https://schaulager.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Art storage building, museum and research institute at Ruchfeldstrasse 19, Münchenstein, just outside Basel; tram 11 from Basel SBB station stops at "Schaulager" in about 10 minutes (museenbasel.ch).
- Completed in 2003, built under commission of the Laurenz Foundation; it holds the Emanuel Hoffmann Foundation collection, started in 1933 by Maja Hoffmann-Stehlin, later Maja Sacher (archiweb.cz, Wikipedia).
- A hybrid of storage facility and museum, built to hold parts of the collection that are normally not visible to the public (archiweb.cz).
- Building: Herzog & de Meuron designed a block of 70 by 70 by 20 metres, dug more than 10 metres into the ground, with outer walls covered in earth excavated on site and very few windows. A gable-roofed entrance house holds a bookshop, ticket office and cafe (archiweb.cz).
- Extension: in October 2024 Herzog & de Meuron were named for an extension for large installations and multimedia works rarely lent, with an auditorium, seminar rooms and conservation studios, linked to the existing building. Cost, size and opening date were not stated (Art Newspaper France).
- Visiting: closed to the general public now. The opening-hours page says it "will reopen to the public in 2027" with an exhibition dedicated to the Albanian artist Anri Sala; until then it offers school and university visits, research visits, tours on the last Wednesday of each month and library access by reservation. Exhibition rooms and storage rooms are wheelchair accessible, with a lift to all floors (schaulager.org).

FUNDING
Schaulager: privately funded: the Laurenz Foundation of Maja Oeri
- The Laurenz Foundation was founded in 1999 by Maja Oeri and her husband Hans U. Bodenmann in memory of their first son, Laurenz Jakob, who died young. It does not give grants; it starts and runs its own projects, and building and running Schaulager is its main one (schaulager.org).
- archiweb.cz says the foundation pays for Schaulager's equipment, concept and operation, and its reviewer gives the building cost as 80 million francs.
- Oeri is president of the Emanuel Hoffmann Foundation, whose collection Schaulager houses. The Art Newspaper reported in 2010 that Oeri's family founded the Swiss pharmaceutical company Roche and that the Laurenz Foundation gave 50 million Swiss francs toward the Kunstmuseum Basel extension. The foundation's own page does not mention Roche.
- No reporting found that Roche or any company funds Schaulager.
- No reporting found of annual accounts, donors or a foundation board: no board page was found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little. The one piece read is a building commentary and does not question the funder. An Artforum column on the Schaulager is paywalled and was not read.
1. archiweb.cz: Petr Šmídek, commentary on the Schaulager building (undated)
URL: https://www.archiweb.cz/en/b/schaulager
- Says he cannot forgive the architects for the small, formal entrance house.
- Praises the interior layout and the experiments with materials.
- Notes the cost of 80 million francs; says a design by Frank Gehry would have cost three to four times more. Criticises one feature of the building, not the institution or its funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Schaulager is clean on what is published: one family foundation, with no corporate sponsor, public funder or named flagged donor reported. The weak points are the lack of any published accounts, donor list or board information, and that the founder's family is tied to Roche, though no page links Roche money to the building. Worth watching: the cost and funding of the announced extension.
