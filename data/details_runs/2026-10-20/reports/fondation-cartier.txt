FONDATION CARTIER: Paris, France
ID: fondation-cartier
Website: https://www.fondationcartier.com (checked 2026-10-20; the old /en/about page returns 404)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation at 2 place du Palais-Royal in the 1st arrondissement, across the road from the Louvre (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- Opened in October 1984 at Jouy-en-Josas, founded by the Maison Cartier and its president Alain Dominique Perrin, with Marie-Claude Beaud as director (The Art Newspaper, 2024; Wikipedia).
- From 1994 to 2025 it occupied Jean Nouvel's glass-and-steel building at 261 boulevard Raspail, on the site of the former American Center; a cedar planted by Chateaubriand in 1823 in its garden was felled in 2020 after drought (Wikipedia).
- Opened its new home in the former Louvre des Antiquaires, rebuilt by Jean Nouvel, on 25 October 2025: 8,500 square metres, 6,500 of them for exhibitions, with five mobile platforms rising to 11 metres (Wikipedia; Le Claireur; The Art Newspaper).
- A collection of almost 4,000 works; Chris Dercon has been managing director since 1 June 2023, after Hervé Chandès (1994 to 2023) (The Art Newspaper; Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday 11 am to 10 pm, Wednesday to Sunday 11 am to 8 pm, closed Monday; €15, reduced €10 (Sortir à Paris). No reporting found on accessibility beyond a link on listings pages.

FUNDING
Fondation Cartier: corporate: Cartier, a jewellery Maison of the Richemont group
- Created by Cartier in 1984 and still carries its name; Richemont lists Cartier as one of its jewellery Maisons (Wikipedia; Richemont).
- Perrin speaks of a "radical separation" between the foundation and Cartier's commercial business (The Art Newspaper, 2024); Dercon describes "un cordon sanitaire" between the brand and the institution (Le Claireur, citing Libération).
- The landlord SFL delivered the space in its Louvre Saint-Honoré complex to "Cartier (groupe Richemont)" on 21 July 2023, under a lease signed in 2018 for a firm 20 years, extendable by 20; Petit, a Vinci Construction France subsidiary, did the works, with Ateliers Jean Nouvel (SFL press release, 6 September 2023).
- Time Out Paris (2023) reported an annual rent of 18 million euros. The cost of the conversion was not disclosed (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- No reporting found of an annual budget, accounts or other funders.

Red flags: Cartier's gold and the Yanomami (2023)
- Cartier's website showed a photo of Yanomami children and described a rainforest project that never took place; Yanomami leaders had not approved the image (AP, 15 May 2023).
- Júnior Hekurari of the Yanomami health council asked how a gold jewellery company "which we, the Yanomami people, are against" could use their image; the Roraima Indigenous Council said it broke their right to free, prior and informed consent (AP).
- Anthropologist Bruce Albert, who linked the foundation with the Yanomami in 2003, said they had not given permission (AP).
- Cartier removed the photo and called the description "a regrettable oversight on our part" (AP).
- Dário Kopenawa of the Hutukara Yanomami Association set the foundation apart, saying it supports Yanomami protection, while "anyone who buys a gold ring is part of the crime" (AP).

Not related: the Fondation Henri Cartier-Bresson, 79 rue des Archives, set up in 2003 by the photographer, Martine Franck and their daughter. It shares only the surname (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation runs on one company's money, Cartier's, inside the Richemont luxury group, and it publishes no budget, accounts or cost for its new building. The weak point is Cartier's gold: the 2023 Yanomami objection reached the jeweller while praising the foundation. Worth watching: whether the reported 18 million euro annual rent is paid by Cartier and how long Richemont keeps paying.
