NEUE NATIONALGALERIE: Berlin, Germany
ID: neue-nationalgalerie-berlin
Website: https://www.smb.museum/museen-einrichtungen/neue-nationalgalerie (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of 20th-century modern art at the Kulturforum in Berlin, south of the Tiergarten park (Wikipedia). It is one of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin.
- Opened on 15 September 1968 in West Berlin (Wikipedia).
- Architect Ludwig Mies van der Rohe designed the building: a black-painted pre-stressed steel roof plate 1.8 m thick on eight cruciform columns and a granite floor (Wikipedia).
- David Chipperfield Architects renovated it; the museum closed in 2015 and reopened in August 2021 with a Calder exhibition. The project cost €140 million against a €101 million budget (Wikipedia).
- The collection focuses on 20th-century art, with strengths in Cubism, Expressionism, the Bauhaus and Surrealism (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Monday, open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00 (Thursday until 20:00); fully wheelchair accessible, with a step-free entrance on the left side at Reichpietschufer (museum website). No reporting found of current ticket prices on the museum's pages.

FUNDING
Neue Nationalgalerie: mainly public: federal government and the German states through the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation
- The Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz receives annual federal allocations under the federal budget law, plus state grants set in federal-state financing agreements (§ 3 StiftPKG). No reporting found of a budget line for this museum alone.
- Volkswagen Group sponsors the Art4All programme (free admission on the first Thursday of each month, 16:00 to 20:00), and the Mercedes-Benz Art Collection provides a long-term loan of works (museum website).
- No reporting found of any other corporate sponsor or donor list.

Red flags: 2024 dispute over a Nan Goldin slide
- The Goldin retrospective "This Will Not End Well" had its opening evening on 22 November 2024 (Flash Art) and ran from 23 November 2024 to 6 April 2025 (museum website). At the opening Goldin criticised Israel's conduct in the Gaza war and Germany's stance on it; protesters drowned out director Klaus Biesenbach, and Culture Minister Claudia Roth said she was "appalled" (taz, dpa report, 23 November 2024; translation).
- Goldin said she was told to remove a credit slide expressing solidarity with Gaza, the West Bank and Lebanon (Hyperallergic, Hanno Hauenstein, 11 December 2024, from an interview first published in Frankfurter Rundschau on 28 November).
- Unverified: Goldin's claim, in a 3 December 2024 email to Biesenbach as Hyperallergic reports it, that the museum warned the original slide "could jeopardize the institution's funding"; no document in the piece shows a funder's instruction.
- The museum said on 6 December 2024 that the slide was inserted without consultation, did not originally name Israeli victims, and was later removed by Goldin's studio team (Hyperallergic).
- Leaked internal emails show Biesenbach consulting senior staff at the culture ministry and the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation on whether to insert the slide now that it also named Israeli victims; on 4 December the museum confirmed to Goldin that her slide would be included (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2024 Goldin affair; most coverage reports the dispute, and one review praises the show.
1. Hyperallergic: Hanno Hauenstein, interview with Nan Goldin (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/nan-goldin-speaks-out-on-censorship-of-berlin-show
- Goldin says the museum wanted no sign of her politics in the show; the article says her email correspondence with Biesenbach "tells a more nuanced and ultimately different story" than the museum's 6 December statement.
- Names Biesenbach, the culture ministry and the foundation.
2. Flash Art: Gea Politi, review of "This Will Not End Well" (2024)
URL: https://flash---art.com/2024/11/nan-goldin/
- Praises the show as the first comprehensive Goldin survey in Germany and a fitting venue given her ties to Berlin.
- Reports that Goldin, Hito Steyerl, Candice Breitz and Eyal Weizman withdrew from a planned symposium; Biesenbach said it was planned independently of Goldin.
3. The Left Berlin: "The Fabrication of a Scandal: Nan Goldin at Berlin's Neue Nationalgalerie" (2024)
URL: https://theleftberlin.com/the-fabrication-of-a-scandal-nan-goldin-at-berlins-neue-nationalgalerie/
- Argues German institutions and media enforce a state-backed narrative (Staatsräson) that silences critics of Israel, and calls the museum's symposium a "prophylactic" (a claim it attributes to Goldin).
- Portrays Biesenbach's on-stage response as submission to Staatsräson. Describes itself as a journalistic project run by international progressives; argues Goldin's side.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Roth, the federal culture minister, rejected Goldin's "intolerably one-sided views" and said the museum and the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation were right to contradict her (taz; translation).
- The museum belongs to the foundation that receives the federal allocations.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public; Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz are named only for Art4All and a loan. The weak point is the Goldin dispute, where the leaked emails show officials consulted on a slide that the museum says Goldin inserted without consultation; Goldin calls this coercion. Watch for any published account of the ministry's role.
