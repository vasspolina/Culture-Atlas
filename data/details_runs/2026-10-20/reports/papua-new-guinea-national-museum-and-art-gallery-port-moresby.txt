PAPUA NEW GUINEA NATIONAL MUSEUM AND ART GALLERY: Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea
ID: papua-new-guinea-national-museum-and-art-gallery-port-moresby

OVERVIEW
- National museum on Independence Drive, Waigani, Port Moresby, holding tens of thousands of artefacts from PNG's 22 provinces (Business Advantage PNG, Robert Upe, 18 May 2020). Wikipedia says it holds over 50,000 ethnographic objects.
- Wikipedia traces its origins to a Board of Trustees formed in 1954 and the Public Museums and Art Galleries Ordinance of 1956; collections sat in disused government buildings and an old hospital from 1960. Construction of a purpose-built museum began in 1975 with partial Australian government funding, and it opened to the public on 27 June 1977.
- The original 1974 building was by Martin Fowler (Architectus, 14 November 2018).
- Refurbished by the Australian firm Architectus, with work that stripped the spaces back to the original building. The refurbished museum had its official reopening on Friday 12 October 2018 and opened to the public on Monday 15 October 2018. The Australian High Commission describes the refurbishment of the gallery spaces and external entrance area. Wikipedia says the work improved disability access and renamed the galleries after Papuan figures, and dates the reopening to 12 October 2017.
- Directors named by Wikipedia include Soroi Eoe from 1985 to 2005 and Andrew Moutu from 2011 to 2016 and again from 2018 to 2022.
- Visiting: open Monday to Saturday 9am to 3pm and Sunday 1pm to 3pm; free on weekdays, K10 at weekends for non-residents (Business Advantage PNG, 2020).

FUNDING
Papua New Guinea National Museum and Art Gallery: statutory body: Australian grants paid for the building and its upgrade
- PNGi Central calls the museum the statutory custodian of PNG's cultural artefacts and says its senior appointments are governed in part by the Regulatory Statutory Authorities (Appointment to Certain Offices) Act 2004 (6 February 2018).
- Australia: the original construction was funded through an Australian cultural grant (Australian High Commission, speech of 14 September 2015); Wikipedia says the Australian government partly funded construction from 1975. On 11 September 2015 Prime Minister Tony Abbott announced US$17.5 million over four years to upgrade the museum, to mark the 40th anniversary of PNG's independence (RNZ, "PNG Museum funding announced by Australia", republished by the Council of Australasian Museum Directors). The 2015 speech names a masterplan and an upgrade ahead of APEC 2018. The Australian High Commission says the refurbishment was supported by the PNG-Australia partnership.
- Audit findings quoted by PNGi Central (staff reporter, 6 February 2018) from the Auditor General's August 2017 report on the museum, which covered almost K8 million in assets: the museum had not maintained a proper accounting system or updated its Fixed Assets Register; the Auditor General could not place reliance on total wages of K469,850; travel payments of K322,837 to staff were not acquitted; and a contract worth K1,356,500 was awarded without the public tendering process. PNGi Central links that contract to the renovation of the museum's amphitheatre and calls for a criminal investigation into the museum's assets and finances.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a PNG statutory body whose original building was funded through an Australian cultural grant and which received an announced US$17.5 million from Australia in 2015 for upgrades. The documented issue is the Auditor General's August 2017 report on the museum's accounts.
