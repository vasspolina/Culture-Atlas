VICTORIA GALLERY: Samara, Russia
ID: victoria-gallery-samara
Website: https://gallery-victoria.ru/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art gallery on the Volga embankment in the historic centre of Samara, at ul. Nekrasovskaya 2 (gallery website).
- Founded in 2005 by Leonid Mikhelson, a businessman from Novokuybyshevsk; the first director was Lyudmila Patrati. It began by showing touring collections, such as prints by Dali, Dix and Fuchs in 2005 (VK long read, Vse v Samare).
- Named for Mikhelson's daughter Victoria, a name shared with the V-A-C Foundation (Kommersant FM).
- The building closed for reconstruction in spring 2023. Since the reopening on 6 March 2026 the gallery occupies the whole three-storey building; before it held one third (Snob, Kommersant FM). The architect is not named in the pages read.
- On site: three exhibition halls, a lecture hall, a library the site calls the largest on contemporary art in Samara, a bookshop, a cafe, art and children's studios, ceramics and silkscreen workshops, and a roof exhibition space. A second space, Victoria Underground, was added in the 2020s under curator Anastasia Albokrinova (VK long read).
- Visiting: exhibition halls open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 21:00; the shop and cafe daily; the library Tuesday to Saturday, 13:00 to 21:00. Ticket prices are not listed. The site lists tours for blind and visually impaired visitors and tours with Russian Sign Language interpretation.

FUNDING
Victoria Gallery: owner-funded: Leonid Mikhelson, whose wealth is in gas
- The gallery is private, founded and owned by Mikhelson (gallery website, Kommersant FM). Kommersant FM calls him the third-richest person on the Russian Forbes list and says the gallery and the Victoria Art Foundation are linked to his daughter's name.
- Mikhelson is board chairman of Novatek, described as Russia's second-largest gas company (The Art Newspaper, 2022).
- Neither the gallery's site nor the press pieces read give a budget, a partner list or other donors. No reporting found of a state or corporate funder of the gallery.
- V-A-C link: the V-A-C Foundation's exhibition "City of Winners" was shown at the gallery, and its first invited curator Andrey Parshikov came from V-A-C (VK long read).

Red flags: owner's gas wealth and sanctions
- Fossil fuels: the owner's fortune comes from Novatek, a gas company (The Art Newspaper; Coda Story).
- Sanctions: the UK sanctioned Mikhelson on 6 April 2022 and imposed a director disqualification on 9 April 2025; Australia, New Zealand and Ukraine also list him; the OpenSanctions page lists Canada without a date (OpenSanctions).
- Coda Story reports that the US sanctioned Novatek over Russia's annexation of Crimea. It lists a $18.4 to $25 billion wealth range for Mikhelson.
- Unverified: the ACF List of War Enablers says he controls companies that help the Russian Ministry of Defence recruit contract soldiers, and the Dossier Center lists him as a person of interest over a possible bribe. Both are advocacy or investigative sources, quoted by OpenSanctions, not sanctioning authorities.

- Notes V-A-C's 26 February 2022 statement "cannot turn a blind eye" stopped short of condemning the war. Does not name the Samara gallery.

WIDER CONTEXT
- After the February 2022 invasion V-A-C ended its Instagram account, cancelled some exhibitions, and its artistic director Francesco Manacorda resigned in March 2022 (Coda Story). V-A-C's Venice palace was suspended and occupied by protesters (The Art Newspaper).
- The Samara gallery is Mikhelson's earlier project and is linked by name and by staff to V-A-C (VK long read, Kommersant FM); campaigners targeted V-A-C in Venice, not Samara.
- The gallery's own news page says an exhibition, "Полный Сюр", closed early on 24 July 2026 because it could not meet fire safety requirements (stated by the space). No independent reporting found.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one person's: Mikhelson, chairman of Novatek, who is under UK, Australian, New Zealand and Ukrainian sanctions. The gallery posts no accounts, board or partner list, so nothing beyond his ownership can be confirmed. Worth watching: how the gallery treats the war and sanctions in its programme, and the early-closed July 2026 show.
