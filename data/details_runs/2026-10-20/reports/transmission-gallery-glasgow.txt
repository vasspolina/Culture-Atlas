TRANSMISSION GALLERY: Glasgow, Scotland, United Kingdom
ID: transmission-gallery-glasgow
Website: https://www.transmissiongallery.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run gallery at 28 King Street, Trongate, Glasgow; it calls itself "Scotland's longest-standing artist-run gallery" (Transmission).
- Established in 1983 by graduates of Glasgow School of Art; its first address was 13 to 15 Chisholm Street, Trongate (Wikipedia).
- Moved to 28 King Street in 1989; the first show there was the Fifth International Festival of Plagiarism, in August 1989 (Wikipedia).
- A membership organisation of about 300 members run by six voluntary administrators (ArtReview, 2018); members of the committee serve at least two years (Wikipedia).
- Past committee members include Douglas Gordon, Claire Barclay, Christine Borland, Martin Boyce, Simon Starling, Lucy Skaer and Alberta Whittle (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: no general opening hours are published, only hours for individual exhibitions; exhibitions and talks are free (Transmission).

FUNDING
Transmission Gallery: artist-run, no funder list published; Creative Scotland regular funding ended in 2018
- The gallery's site lists no funders or sponsors. Its pages offer a PayPal donation link and a membership form, and name no charity number, accounts or annual report.
- Creative Scotland: in the 2018 to 2021 round it rejected Transmission's application, listed by The Art Newspaper as £210,000 (27 February 2018). Wikipedia says Transmission was dropped from Creative Scotland's portfolio of regularly funded organisations in 2018. No page read gives the years or amount of the earlier regular grant, shows a later reinstatement, or confirms any Creative Scotland grant today.
- At its start the gallery was supported by the Scottish Arts Council and Glasgow City Council (Wikipedia). No reporting found of current Glasgow City Council funding.
- The gallery says publication costs for its funding-call exhibitions are "fully covered by Transmission". One call is "in partnership with the Lovelace-Hodgkin Symposium 2026" and a residency runs "in conjunction with the Scottish Association of Marine Sciences, at Oban"; neither is called a funder.
- No reporting found of a corporate, Gulf state, fossil fuel, arms or other flagged funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of Transmission itself found in art media. The coverage criticises Creative Scotland's 2018 decision to drop it, and Transmission's own committee made most of the charges.
1. ArtReview: staff, "Glasgow's artist-run Transmission gallery decries funding cut" (2 February 2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-2-februay-2018-transmission-gallery-decries-funding-cut/
- Reports Transmission's statement: the committee called the cut discriminatory and political and said the gallery was penalised for its institutional framework and for having a committee led by people of colour. This is Transmission's own claim.
- Quotes Creative Scotland's assessment, as relayed by Transmission: the application was "assessed as fundable" and its approach to EDI "is exceptionally strong and sectorally relevant at this time".
2. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Creative Scotland chief apologises for funding backlash, though questions remain" (27 February 2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/02/27/creative-scotland-chief-apologises-for-funding-backlashthough-questions-remain
- Chief executive Janet Archer said she was "profoundly sorry that the delivery of this process has been such a negative one for many"; the funder dropped 20 organisations from its regular funding for 2018 to 2021.
- Lists Transmission among the rejected applications (£210,000). Committee members said Creative Scotland's credibility was "severely damaged". Criticises the funder, not the gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
Current funding is not documented. The only amount found is the £210,000 Creative Scotland application rejected in 2018, when the gallery was dropped from regular funding. The weak points are the lack of any published accounts, annual report or funder list on the site. Worth watching: whether Creative Scotland or other funders are named on future calls and in its accounts.
