MUSEUM DE LAKENHAL: Leiden, Netherlands
ID: museum-de-lakenhal-leiden
Website: https://www.lakenhal.nl/en/story/funds-and-sponsors

OVERVIEW
- Leiden's municipal museum of fine art and history, holding early works by Rembrandt, Lucas van Leyden's Last Judgement, De Stijl works, contemporary art and a collection of Dutch Golden Age fijnschilder paintings (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1874 as a municipal museum (Wikipedia).
- The building was erected in 1640 by Arent van 's-Gravesande as a cloth hall, a guild hall for cloth merchants. An exhibition hall was added at the end of the 19th century and a new wing in 1922 (Wikipedia).
- The museum closed on 17 October 2016 for restoration and expansion. Julian Harrap Architects restored the classical building and Happel Cornelisse Verhoeven designed the expansion, built by the contractor group Nieuw Laecken (De Lakenhal press page). King Willem-Alexander opened the renovated museum on 19 June 2019 and it opened to the public on 20 June 2019 (CODART).
- The director is Tanja Elstgeest and the museum had 29,281 visitors in 2021, a year affected by Covid-19 lockdowns (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Museum De Lakenhal: mainly public: Gemeente Leiden, with state and provincial money and a long list of funds and donors
- Gemeente Leiden: on 12 October 2016 Kassa (BNNVARA, credit ANP) reported the renovation and expansion as a 23.3 million euro project, with the municipality contributing 20.5 million euros and third parties 2.8 million euros. Wikipedia gives the cost of the 2016 to 2019 renovation as about 19 million euros. Leiden's council decided to make additional funds available (De Lakenhal press release, 12 October 2016).
- Budget: about 5 million euros as of 2021 (Wikipedia).
- The museum's thanks page lists the Municipality of Leiden, the central government through the Ministry of OCW and the Province of South Holland as government funders. Among its funds and companies it lists the BankGiro Lottery, Mondriaan Fund, Ammodo, BNG Culture Fund, VSB Fund and Fonds 21.
- The same page lists Prince Bernhard Culture Fund, Niemeijer Fund, Turing Foundation, Dioraphte Foundation, Stokroos Foundation, the Rijksdienst voor het Cultureel Erfgoed, Creative Industry Stimulation Fund and the Rembrandt Association with its BankGiro Lottery funds. The page says it covers donations and subsidies since the museum's foundation.
- Individual donors named with a purpose: Leendert Bosch (bequest for restoring the earth and nail collection), Aty van der Drift (inheritance for restoring and expanding the building), Carla van Steijn (gifts and legacies for the building and the collection), Gail and Ernst von Metzsch (restoring the University Room).
- Friends and patronage groups listed: Lucas van Leyden Patronage and the American Friends of Museum De Lakenhal.
- The BankGiro Lottery is a charity lottery and ordinary public funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mainly public: the city of Leiden contributed 20.5 of the 23.3 million euros budgeted in October 2016 for the 2016 to 2019 rebuild, and the state and province are listed as funders. The remainder comes from lottery money, arts funds and private donors named on the museum's thanks page.
