WIDER CONTEXT
- On 30 October 2019 about 30 professional staff (curators, registrars, educators and information workers), members of the Syndicat de professionnelles et professionnels du gouvernement du Québec (SPGQ), held a one-day picket outside the museum on the day of an exhibition opening. A flyer handed out at the strike said they had been without a collective agreement since 2015 and that 54 per cent of the museum's professional staff were on temporary contracts without benefits; union representative Adi Jakupović called it the first time the museum's members had gone on strike (Canadian Art, Saelan Twerdy).
- Deputy director Yves Théoret said the museum had put forward "a reasonable offer, within the parameters set by the Conseil du trésor" for the negotiations (Canadian Art).
- The staff then announced a four-day strike from 19 to 22 November 2019, timed with the "EMERGE" performance programme; the participating artist Bridget Moser wrote on Twitter that she was "deeply frustrated [and] angry at the management" of the museum. The museum, the Quebec treasury and the union reached a tentative agreement late on 19 November, which curator Mark Lanctôt said moved nine jobs from temporary to permanent (Canadian Art, Saelan Twerdy).
- The collective agreement was signed on 18 February 2020, with a 5.25 per cent pay rise over five years and retroactive increases for the years without a contract. Lanctôt said 15 staff had left since 2015 and only six had been replaced (Canadian Art, Saelan Twerdy).
- On 15 July 2020 the museum ended its Jon Rafman exhibition, which was to run until 6 September, two days after allegations concerning the artist were posted on Instagram; the museum did not comment on why it ended the show (Canadian Art news roundup, July 2020).
