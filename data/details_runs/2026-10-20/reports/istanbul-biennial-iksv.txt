ISTANBUL BIENNIAL (İKSV): Istanbul, Turkey
ID: istanbul-biennial-iksv
Website: https://bienal.iksv.org/en (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennial organised by the Istanbul Foundation for Culture and Arts (İKSV), which also runs the city's film, music, jazz and theatre festivals, the Istanbul Design Biennial and Türkiye's pavilion in Venice.
- First held in 1987 as "Contemporary Art in Traditional Spaces"; İKSV has organised it since then (Wikipedia).
- The 18th edition, "The Three-Legged Cat", curated by Christine Tohmé, ran in three phases from 2025: the first from 20 September to 23 November 2025 at eight venues on the Beyoğlu to Karaköy axis (İKSV 2024 report, e-flux).
- Liu Ding and Carol Yinghua Lu curate the 19th edition, 18 September to 14 November 2027; the site names Artistanbul Feshane as main venue.
- Kevser Güler has directed the Biennial since January 2024, after Bige Örer (2008 to 2024). İKSV's board is chaired by Bülent Eczacıbaşı (Diken, Serbestiyet).
- Visiting: free admission; the 2025 venues opened 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays (e-flux).

FUNDING
Istanbul Biennial: corporate-sponsored: Koç Holding, through İKSV
- Koç Holding is listed as "2007–2036 Biennial Sponsor". In September 2022 chairman Ömer Koç announced a 10-year extension of the 2007 to 2026 deal to 2036 (Diken). No reporting found of the amount.
- Koç Holding's 2024 revenue was $65.8 billion. Its refiner Tüpraş runs most of Turkey's oil refineries and makes up almost half the group's revenue; the group includes Otokar Otomotiv ve Savunma Sanayi, a defence vehicle maker (Wikipedia).
- The Ministry of Culture and Tourism and Eczacıbaşı also appear as logos on the Biennial site, roles not stated.
- İKSV 2024 accounts: activity revenue TL 428.4 million; sponsorship 50 percent (TL 212.1 million), tickets 15 percent, Cultural Centre contributions 16 percent, commercial activities 13 percent, Tulip Card members 4 percent and public contributions 2 percent (TL 7.25 million) (İKSV 2024 report).

Red flags: Koç Holding money from oil refining and defence
- The Biennial's sole named sponsor through 2036 is a group whose largest business is oil refining (Tüpraş) and which builds military vehicles (Otokar) (Wikipedia, Biennial site).

Red flags: protests against Koç sponsorship, 2009 and 2013
- In 2009 the Direnal-Istanbul (Resistanbul) movement criticised the Biennial's Koç sponsorship and its ties to neoliberal policy (Afterall, Tom Snow).
- On 10 May 2013 activists wearing Koç Holding banners and the names of gentrifying neighbourhoods lay down during a Biennial performance by Vermeir & Heiremans; security removed them (ArtInfo, Ben Davis and Sara Roffino).
- Curators Fulya Erdemci and Andrea Phillips called the action "psychological violence, harassment, and violation of individual rights". More than 100 people in the Turkish art world signed a 25 May 2013 letter condemning that response (ArtInfo).

Red flags: curator selection row, 2023 to 2024
- In 2023 the advisory board unanimously recommended Defne Ayas; İKSV appointed Iwona Blazwick, then a member of that board (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris).
- Critics believe Ayas was judged too risky after her 2015 Venice pavilion catalogue, with an essay naming the "Armenian genocide", was withdrawn after a Turkish government complaint (The Art Newspaper).
- Venice pavilion curator Esra Sarıgedik Öktem resigned in protest on 16 August 2023 (Biennial Foundation). Four artists withdrew in October 2023 and more than 80 artists and curators signed an open letter asking for transparency (Apollo).
- Blazwick stood down on 19 January 2024 and the edition moved to 2025; İKSV now chooses from a three-name shortlist set by the advisory board (Apollo).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism exists, aimed at the Koç sponsorship, the 2013 edition's retreat from public space, and İKSV's governance in the 2023 curator row.
1. Afterall: Tom Snow, "13th Istanbul Biennial: A Retreat to the Institution" (2013)
URL: https://www.afterall.org/articles/13th-istanbul-biennial-a-retreat-to-the-institution/
- Argues the curators' choice to withdraw from urban space after Gezi was "too easily compatible" with state repression of public politics.
- Cites Hito Steyerl's work on Koç and Siemens funding of biennials and Direnal-Istanbul's 2009 criticism of Koç.
- Faults the show for missing the chance to address the city's political situation.
2. ArtInfo, republished by ArtLeaks: Ben Davis and Sara Roffino, on the 2013 protests (2013)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2013/06/12/istanbul-biennial-protests-foreshadowed-battle-for-gezi-park/
- Reports the Koç banner protest and the curators' response; frames the protests as a precursor to Gezi.
3. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Iwona Blazwick stands down as Istanbul Biennial curator, event postponed until 2025" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/01/19/iwona-blazwick-stands-down-as-istanbul-biennial-curatorevent-postponed-until-2025
- Sets out İKSV's rejection of the board's choice and the suspicion that Ayas was passed over for political reasons. İKSV blamed "undesired divisions in art circles".

WIDER CONTEXT
- At the 2025 opening Ömer Koç said he could not believe some people still support and justify "this terrible genocide" in Gaza (Serbestiyet; translation).

BOTTOM LINE
The Biennial runs on Koç Holding money locked in to 2036, and Koç's largest earner is oil refining, with a defence arm beside it. Artists protested that sponsorship in 2009 and 2013, and the 2023 curator row raised questions about İKSV's independence from political caution. The public share of İKSV's income is small, at 2 percent in 2024.
