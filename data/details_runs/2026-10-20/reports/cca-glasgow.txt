CCA GLASGOW (CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ARTS): Glasgow, United Kingdom
ID: cca-glasgow
Website: https://www.cca-glasgow.com

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre at 350 Sauchiehall Street, Glasgow. It closed on 30 January 2026, entered liquidation and made its 39 staff redundant (The Art Newspaper). A provisional liquidator was appointed on 6 February 2026 (Civil Society).
- Grew out of the Third Eye Centre, a multimedia arts centre opened in 1974 and founded by the Scottish Arts Council; the Centre for Contemporary Arts was established in 1992 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Grecian Chambers, a Category A listed building designed by Alexander "Greek" Thomson in 1867 to 1868. The Scottish Arts Council bought it in the 1970s and Page & Park renovated it in 1998 (Wikipedia).
- The centre closed for four months after the 2018 fire at the nearby Glasgow School of Art (The Art Newspaper). Wikipedia states it reopened in October 2018.

FUNDING
CCA Glasgow: Creative Scotland funding and a Creative Scotland building
- Creative Scotland owns the building and leased it to the CCA for £1 a year (The Art Newspaper).
- The CCA won three years of funding worth £3.4 million from Creative Scotland in January 2025 (The Art Newspaper).
- Creative Scotland said £832,000 was awarded for 2025/26 and £1.28 million was planned for each of the next two years (Civil Society).
- On 30 January 2026 Creative Scotland suspended further payments, saying the CCA "is unable to demonstrate its ongoing viability" (The Art Newspaper; ArtReview).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, June 2025
- The Telegraph (Samuel Montgomery, 14 August 2025) reports that the centre announced on Instagram that its board had not come to a consensus on publicly endorsing the boycott of Israel, and that Art Workers for Palestine Scotland occupied the building on 24 June 2025.
- The Skinny (Jamie Dunn, 30 January 2026) reports that the group had planned a week-long fringe programme of workshops and screenings in the courtyard, that the board arranged for police officers to remove the group from the building, and that a 63-year-old woman taking part was arrested and injured.
- The Telegraph reports the centre said it "sincerely regret[s] the outcome of our decisions on 24 June" and would revisit endorsing the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel under new leadership.
- After the 2026 closure Art Workers for Palestine Scotland wrote that it was "years of mismanagement and ethical rot and a final destructive act by the board" (Instagram statement, quoted by The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The Art Newspaper and ArtReview reported the closure; The Art Newspaper quotes Art Workers for Palestine Scotland criticising the centre's governance.
1. The Art Newspaper: Joe Ware, "Glasgow's Centre for Contemporary Arts to close permanently" (2026)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/02/02/glasgows-centre-for-contemporary-arts-to-close-permanently
- Reports that the CCA called police to clear protesters, apologised and replaced senior staff and board members.
- Quotes Art Workers for Palestine Scotland criticising the lack of public consultation before the layoffs.
- Programme manager Annie Hazelwood called the closure "deeply painful".
2. ArtReview: "Glasgow's Centre for Contemporary Arts to permanently close" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/glasgow-centre-for-contemporary-arts-to-permanently-close/
- Reports Creative Scotland's statement that the CCA board had decided to cease operations and that the organisation had been placed in administration.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2023: Saramago Cafe Bar, the CCA's business tenant, was accused of firing three members of staff mid-shift on 3 March for taking part in union-endorsed strike action over staff shortages, and demonstrations took place outside the venue. The owners deny the claim and say the staff took part in an unannounced work stoppage in breach of their contracts. The CCA said it was ending its relationship with Saramago, and the cafe bar would close with immediate effect, because the dispute had become "untenable" for its artists, programme partners and staff (Museums Association, Geraldine Kendall Adams, 21 April 2023).
- The Scottish Artists Union said in a public statement that it was "deeply concerned" by the "unexpected" liquidation, which was "preceded by systemic failures in governance and risk management" (Civil Society, George Hayes, 9 February 2026).
- Unverified: Ali Hassan, who created a petition to save the CCA, claims in a letter to MSPs and ministers that the closure decision "was made by just two board members" (Civil Society).

BOTTOM LINE
The CCA was funded by Creative Scotland, which owns the building it leased for £1 a year. Creative Scotland awarded it £3.4 million over three years from January 2025 and suspended payments on 30 January 2026; a provisional liquidator was appointed on 6 February 2026. In June 2025 Art Workers for Palestine Scotland occupied the centre after its board did not reach consensus on endorsing a boycott of Israel, and the board arranged for police to remove the group.
