OVERVIEW
- State art museum in Minsk, the largest art museum in Belarus, with more than 30,000 works in about 20 collections, mainly Belarusian art (Wikipedia).
- Founded as the State Art Gallery on 24 January 1939 by resolution of the Council of People's Commissars of Belarus, in 15 halls of a former girls' gymnasium; nearly all of its roughly 2,711 early works were lost in the Second World War. Renamed the National Art Museum in 1993 (Wikipedia).
- Early holdings came partly from the Vitebsk Museum of Modern Art (1919 to 1925) (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Uladzimir Prakaptsou (1998 to 2023), then Ganna Konanava (2023 to present) (Wikipedia).
- Nasha Niva (7 July 2026) reports that Marc Chagall's "Stroll" ("Progulka"), one panel of a triptych, was brought to the museum earlier and that its display was extended to October 2026 after talks between the Belarusian and Russian prime ministers. The article does not say who lent it.

FUNDING
National Art Museum of the Republic of Belarus: public: a state museum, with a past partnership with Belgazprombank
- No reporting found of the museum's budget, accounts or a sponsor list.
- Chagall exhibition: no source opened names a sponsor. The Nasha Niva article names no organiser or sponsor. No reporting found of Sber Bank as a sponsor of the museum or the Chagall exhibition.
- Belgazprombank: its Art-Belarus project, which has a public-private partnership agreement with the Ministry of Culture signed on 17 June 2015, hosted "Ten Centuries of Belarusian Art" there from 27 March to 10 July 2014 (Art-Belarus site). On 24 July 2012 the bank gave the museum two Chagall prints, "Vision of the Apocalypse" and "The Way of the Cross" (myfin.by). The bank's "Autumn Salon" is a sales show for young artists run with the Belarusian Union of Artists (Art-Belarus site); a search result places recent editions at the Palace of Arts, not this museum.
- Belgazprombank ownership in 2020: Gazprom and Gazprombank held 49.66% each (Prime Press, 20 June 2020).

Red flags: staff dismissed over a political poem, 2020
- July 2020: guide Nikita Monich was dismissed from the museum after writing a poem addressed to Lukashenko about the seizure of about 150 paintings from Belgazprombank's collection; the director asked him to remove it "not to embarrass the museum in this pre-election time" (translation), he refused and offered to be dismissed instead, and he was fired (Current Time, 13 July 2020). The museum is a state museum (Wikipedia: created by a resolution of the Council of People's Commissars of Belarus), so the dismissal was by a state-run employer.
- Context: Current Time says the seizure of the paintings was part of the criminal case against Viktar Babaryka, a potential presidential candidate; Myfin (2012) calls him chairman of Belgazprombank's board. Prime Press (20 June 2020) dates his questioning by the financial investigations department to 18 June 2020.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Belarusian state money, and no source opened shows a sponsor for the current Chagall show. The weak points are the museum's place in a state where staff were dismissed for political speech in 2020, its earlier ties to Belgazprombank, which Gazprom and Gazprombank owned in 2020, and the lack of any published budget or sponsor list. Worth watching: who sponsors the Chagall show and whether the museum publishes accounts.
