DEUTSCHES SPIONAGEMUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: deutsches-spionagemuseum-berlin
Website: https://www.deutsches-spionagemuseum.de

OVERVIEW
- Interactive museum on the history of spies and intelligence services at Leipziger Platz 9, 10117 Berlin, on the former death strip at Potsdamer Platz (stated by the space).
- Opened on 19 September 2015 as the Spy Museum Berlin, created by the former journalist Franz-Michael Günther; it reopened on 29 July 2016 as the Deutsches Spionagemuseum under a new operator (museum website; Wikipedia).
- Robert Rückel is managing director of the operating company, Deutsches Spionage Museum DSM GmbH (museum imprint).
- Leipziger Platz was chosen as the site in 2014; the building was originally built for a logistics company and refurbished as a museum (stated by the space).
- On site: about 1,000 exhibits across roughly 3,000 square metres, including a Fialka cipher machine, an Enigma machine, agent radios, spy gadgets and original James Bond props, with a laser course, code-breaking, a lie detector and a safe-cracking challenge (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Visiting: open daily from 10:00 to 20:00, 365 days a year; timed tickets are sold online, including a combination ticket with the Deutschlandmuseum.

FUNDING
Deutsches Spionagemuseum: private museum: private money and admission revenue
- The museum states that it was "funded entirely with private money and without any government assistance" (stated by the space).
- The museum says its funding model ran into difficulties, a new operator relaunched it on 29 July 2016, and ticket prices were reduced after the relaunch (stated by the space).
- Unverified: the original private operating company was insolvent, from dark-tourism.com (Peter Hohenhaus, a travel guide site), which writes "Apparently there have been some goings-on in the background (incl. insolvency of the original private company)" and says the admission price was cut from 18 euros to 12 euros after the first ten months.
- The operating company is Deutsches Spionage Museum DSM GmbH, registered at Amtsgericht Berlin-Charlottenburg under HRB 176723 B (museum imprint).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private money and admission revenue, run through Deutsches Spionage Museum DSM GmbH under managing director Robert Rückel. The museum states it was "funded entirely with private money and without any government assistance".
