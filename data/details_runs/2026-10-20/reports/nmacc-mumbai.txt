NITA MUKESH AMBANI CULTURAL CENTRE (NMACC): Mumbai, India
ID: nmacc-mumbai
Website: https://nmacc.com

OVERVIEW
- Performing arts and exhibition centre in G Block, Bandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai, inside the Jio World Centre retail and business complex (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper, Kabir Jhala, 9 February 2024).
- Opened on 31 March 2023, founded by Nita Ambani, chairperson of Reliance Foundation; the architect was TVS Design (Wikipedia).
- Four venues: the 2,000-seat Grand Theatre, the 250-seat Studio Theatre, the 125-seat Cube and the four-storey, 16,000 square foot Art House (Wikipedia). The National Herald essay says the Grand Theatre is studded with 8,400 Swarovski diamonds.
- The Art House is "open to all" (The Established, Shruti Thacker, 31 March 2023). Its opening shows were "Sangam/Confluence", curated by Ranjit Hoskote and Jeffrey Deitch, and "India in Fashion"; Hans Ulrich Obrist and Jessica Morgan sit on its advisory board (National Herald, Rashmi Sawhney, 8 July 2023).
- "Pop: Fame, Love, Power" ran until 11 February 2024, curated by collector and dealer Lawrence Van Hagen, with loans including works from Isha Ambani Piramal; the centre's late opening hours draw office workers after hours (The Art Newspaper).
- Public art includes Kamal Kunj, a 56-foot Pichwai painting, and Clouds, mirror structures by Yayoi Kusama (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include workshops, outreach for underserved communities and school and college collaborations (The Established).

FUNDING
NMACC: corporate-owned: Reliance Foundation, the charitable arm of Reliance Industries
- NMACC is part of Reliance Foundation, the philanthropic branch of Reliance Industries; Nita Ambani is the foundation's founder-chairperson (The Established).
- Wikipedia says Reliance Foundation, founded in 2010 by Mukesh Ambani, is "wholly owned by Reliance Industries Limited". Mukesh Ambani is the owner of Reliance Industries (Wikipedia).
- The cost of the centre was estimated at about 120 crore rupees, or 13 million dollars, in 2023 (Wikipedia).
- The Art Newspaper places the centre in the Jio World Centre retail hub, where the mall setting "encourages a flow of shoppers into the shows"; it quotes Darshan Mehta, president of Reliance Brands, at the India Art Fair in New Delhi saying art is central to "the theatre of luxury."
- Reliance Industries' Integrated Annual Report 2024-25 lists segment revenue in crore rupees: Oil to Chemicals 6,26,921, Retail 3,30,943, Digital Services 1,54,119, Oil and Gas 25,211, Media and Entertainment 20,696 (Reliance annual report).
- Wikipedia gives 2024 revenue shares of Oil to Chemicals 53.12%, Retail 26.68%, Digital Services 11.05%, Others 6.77% and Oil and Gas 2.38%.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Reliance Industries)
- The centre belongs to the Reliance Foundation, which Wikipedia says is wholly owned by Reliance Industries. Oil to Chemicals is the largest of the five segments in the company's annual report (6,26,921 crore rupees of revenue in 2024-25), and Wikipedia puts it at 53.12% of 2024 revenue.
- Wikipedia calls the Jamnagar petrochemical complex in Gujarat "the largest refinery in the world".
- Russian oil: Wikipedia reports that in December 2024 Reliance agreed to buy 500,000 barrels of oil per day from Rosneft for 10 years, about 13 billion dollars a year, and that Reliance is listed on Leave Russia, a platform tracking companies still active in the Russian market.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is mostly neutral reporting, with two critical pieces.
1. The Art Newspaper: Kabir Jhala, "Blockbuster Pop art show in Mumbai marks a new type of exhibition for India" (9 February 2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/02/09/blockbuster-mumbai-show-of-pop-art-portends-future-of-art-and-luxury-in-india
- Calls "Pop: Fame, Love, Power" a "safe, surface-level survey of Pop art", whose "lone attempt" to link the show to its Indian context is an Ed Ruscha painting of the Himalayas.
- Says the mall setting encourages a flow of shoppers into the shows and that visitors are surrounded by the gift shop; before the centre opened, the family "had not been leading patrons of the nation's visual arts".
2. National Herald: Rashmi Sawhney, "Does big business beggar art?" (8 July 2023)
URL: https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/art-and-culture/does-big-business-beggar-art
- Argues corporate patronage is not inherently wrong but can compromise art's capacity to dissent, and says nothing about the centre or the family gives hope that it will be a space where "capitalistic greed and state violence can be critiqued through art forms". It closes: "Art can be funded by big business, but art must not be appropriated by big business."
- Writes that big businesses provide political parties with funds for their electoral campaigns. Names the Ambani family and Mukesh Ambani; does not name Reliance.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre belongs to the Reliance Foundation, which Wikipedia says is wholly owned by Reliance Industries, a group whose largest segment is oil to chemicals, and it sits inside the Jio World Centre retail complex. The documented weak points are the fossil fuel parentage and, according to Wikipedia, the December 2024 Rosneft supply contract.
