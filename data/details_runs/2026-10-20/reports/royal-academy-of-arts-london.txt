ROYAL ACADEMY OF ARTS: London, United Kingdom
ID: royal-academy-of-arts-london
Website: https://www.royalacademy.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led institution and gallery at Burlington House, Piccadilly, London, where it has been since 1868; it moved there from Pall Mall, Somerset House and Trafalgar Square (Wikipedia).
- Founded through a personal act of King George III on 10 December 1768, with Joshua Reynolds as first president (Wikipedia).
- Rebecca Salter was elected president on 10 December 2019, the first woman in the post (Wikipedia).
- The Royal Academy Schools are the oldest art school in Britain; their three-year postgraduate programme is free to every applicant offered a place (Wikipedia).
- David Chipperfield Architects were appointed in 2008; the site closed in 2016 and the "New RA" reopened on 19 May 2018 after a £56 million project (Wikipedia).
- The annual Summer Exhibition shows public submissions and works by Royal Academicians; the majority of works are for sale, and sales support the exhibiting artists and the Academy's charitable work, including training artists at the Royal Academy Schools. The 257th edition ran from 17 June to 17 August 2025 (Royal Academy press notice, Insight Investment website).
- Visitor numbers were 696,000 in 2022, 710,000 in 2023 and 622,000 in 2024, against 1.25 million before the pandemic (The Art Newspaper, 27 February 2025).

FUNDING
Royal Academy of Arts: independent charity: tickets, donations, sponsorship, commercial income and membership, with no direct government funding
- Wikipedia states that the Academy "receives funding from neither the State nor the Crown and operates as a charity"; The Art Newspaper states that it receives no direct government funding and relies on ticket sales, donations, sponsorship, commercial activities and its membership scheme (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 27 February 2025).
- Summer Exhibition sponsor: Insight Investment sponsored the 2025 edition (Royal Academy press notice on the Insight Investment website). House of Creed was headline partner of the 2026 Summer Exhibition Preview Party, which supports the Royal Academy Schools (Retail Boss, Shipra Bohara, 15 June 2026).
- Finances: the 2024-25 budget forecast a net loss of £7.1 million before any measures; without action the Academy would breach its credit facilities by the year ending 31 August 2026 and would need to draw on £10.3 million of unrestricted funds held by the separate Royal Academy Trust charity (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 27 February 2025).
- Sackler name: the Academy has a Sackler wing. In December 2021 a Royal Academy spokesperson told Museums Journal that the Academy has not received donations from the Sackler Trust or the Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation, and that it has received support from Jillian and Arthur M Sackler, which the spokesperson called "a different branch of the Sackler family"; the wing kept its name (Museums Journal, Michael Rudling, 14 December 2021).

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- 15 March 2025: about 50 members of the IWGB union demonstrated at the Royal Academy against its second round of mass redundancies in five years, and 78% of the 118 staff who took part in a vote expressed no confidence in the president, the interim chief executive and the treasurer to lead the restructure (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 17 March 2025).
- The Academy's proposal was to cut up to 60 roles, about 18% of the workforce, almost half of them current vacancies. The IWGB called the cuts evidence of "reckless mismanagement of an historic institution" (The Art Newspaper, 27 February 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns the Academy's management and finances in 2025, as reported by The Art Newspaper.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Royal Academy staff face redundancy amid plans to cut 18% of workforce" (2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/02/27/royal-academy-staff-face-redundancy-amid-plans-to-cut-18-of-workforce
- Reports that the Royal Academy Council approved the redundancy plans, for up to 60 roles, and that the union blames "reckless mismanagement".
- Quotes interim chief executive Natasha Mitchell, who says the Academy faces "a serious financial challenge".
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Protestors descend on London's Royal Academy over planned job cuts" (2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/03/17/protestors-descend-on-londons-royal-academy-over-planned-job-cuts
- Reports the 15 March protest, the union's demand to stop all mandatory redundancies and its no-confidence figures.
- The Academy disputed the union's figure of almost 100 jobs at risk and said no decisions had been made.

BOTTOM LINE
The Royal Academy is an independent charity with no direct government funding, living on tickets, donations, sponsorship, commercial income and membership. The documented sponsors are Insight Investment for the Summer Exhibition and House of Creed for its preview party; the documented dispute is the 2025 redundancy plan, which brought a staff no-confidence vote and a union protest.
