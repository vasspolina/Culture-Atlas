VICTORIA GALLERY: Samara, Russia
ID: victoria-gallery-samara
Website: https://gallery-victoria.ru/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art gallery at Nekrasovskaya ulitsa 2, in the historic centre of Samara on the Volga embankment (gallery website).
- Founded in 2005 by Leonid Mikhelson, a businessman from Novokuybyshevsk, with Lyudmila Patrati as its first director (VK long read, Vse v Samare).
- Named, like Mikhelson's other art bodies, after his daughter Victoria (Kommersant FM).
- The building closed for reconstruction in spring 2023 and reopened on 6 March 2026; the gallery now uses all 1,200 square metres of the three-storey building, where it once held a third (Snob).
- Samara studios RBTY and link.bureau designed the new building and interiors, with 600 square metres for three exhibition halls with museum-grade climate control (Snob).
- On site: three exhibition halls, a lecture hall, a library, a bookshop, art and children's studios, ceramics and screen-printing workshops and a seasonal roof space (gallery website).
- Programme: an annual prize for regional art and curatorial projects, two festivals and the Underground hall opened after 2020 (VK long read).
- Lyudmila Patrati is president and Darya Kolpashnikova general director (gallery website); Kolpashnikova came from the Pushkin Museum of Fine Arts (Sobaka.ru).
- Sergei Balandin, curator since 2014, was general director from March 2026 and left on 31 July 2026 (gallery news).
- Visiting: exhibition halls open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 21:00; tickets are sold online (gallery website).

FUNDING
Victoria Gallery: owner-funded: Leonid Mikhelson, whose fortune comes from gas and chemicals
- The gallery describes itself as a private institution founded by Mikhelson (gallery website); Kommersant FM calls him number three on the Russian Forbes list and the owner of all the art bodies named after his daughter.
- The UK Sanctions List (FCDO) gives his posts as chairman of the management board and board member of Novatek and chairman of the board of directors of Sibur.
- The works in the reopening show came from private collections (Snob).

Red flags: owner's gas fortune, current in 2026
- The owner's wealth comes from Novatek, which The Art Newspaper (2022) calls Russia's second largest gas company, and from Sibur; the UK Sanctions List says his interests in the Russian energy and chemicals sectors make him one of Russia's richest men.

Red flags: owner under UK sanctions since 2022
- The UK designated Mikhelson on 6 April 2022 with an asset freeze and travel ban, added trust services sanctions on 21 March 2023 and a director disqualification on 9 April 2025, on the ground of his directorships and shareholdings in Novatek and Sibur (UK Sanctions List, FCDO, checked 20 November 2026).
- Australia, New Zealand and Ukraine also list him (OpenSanctions); Coda Story says Canada sanctioned him and the US sanctioned Novatek over Crimea.
- Unverified: the Anti-Corruption Foundation (advocacy) says he controls companies that help the Russian Defence Ministry recruit contract soldiers, and the Dossier Center (investigative) lists him as suspected of giving a bribe, both as quoted by OpenSanctions.

WIDER CONTEXT
- V-A-C left Instagram on 1 March 2022 and its artistic director Francesco Manacorda resigned in March 2022 (Coda Story); V-A-C suspended its activities at Palazzo Zattere after the occupation (The Art Newspaper).
- Kommersant FM traces the line from the Samara gallery to V-A-C and the GES-2 House of Culture in Moscow; the 2022 campaign targeted V-A-C in Venice, not Samara.
- The gallery closed its show "Polny Syur" ("Полный Сюр", our romanisation) early on 24 July 2026 because it could not meet fire safety rules for the works (stated by the space).

BOTTOM LINE
Not clean: the gallery rests on a single owner's fossil-fuel fortune, and that owner is under sanctions. Worth watching: who paid for the 2023 to 2026 rebuild.
