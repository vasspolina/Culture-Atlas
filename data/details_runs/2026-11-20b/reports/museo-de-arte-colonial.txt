MUSEO DE ARTE COLONIAL: Havana, Cuba
ID: museo-de-arte-colonial
Website: http://www.ohch.cu/ (checked 2026-11-20; returned a 502 error)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of colonial furniture and decorative art in the house facing the Plaza de la Catedral in Old Havana, which the Historian's Office calls the oldest building around the square (Historian's Office, archive copy of 28 June 2017; Spanish Wikipedia).
- Building: a house of 1720 built for Luis Chacón, then governor of Cuba; it takes the name Casa Bayona from his grandson José de Bayona y Chacón, first Count of Casa Bayona (Spanish Wikipedia; OnCuba).
- From 1856 the house held the Real Colegio de Escribanos, the newspaper La Discusión and the Arechabala liquor firm; the National Commission of Museums and Monuments restored it in 1963 (Historian's Office).
- The museum opened on 30 July 1969 (Historian's Office; OnCuba).
- An annex in the Palacio de Lombillo, at Empedrado 151, shows a collection of tableware (Historian's Office).
- On show: furniture, decorative arts, architectural elements, Cuban stained glass and colonial carriages, with furnished rooms recreating noble houses (Historian's Office). OnCuba counts four rooms, three permanent and one for temporary shows.
- UNESCO listed Old Havana as World Heritage on 14 December 1982 (Historian's Office).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Colonial: mainly public: Office of the City Historian, on the state budget, ticket and shop income and authorised donations
- Decree-Law 368 of 2018 (Gaceta Oficial No. 24, 3 April 2019) says the Historian's Office system runs on the State budget and other authorised funding, answers to the Council of Ministers, can hold bank accounts in national and convertible currency, and can manage donations and agree collaboration with Cuban and foreign bodies.
- The Office says Decree-Law 143 of 1993 let it create its own income sources, and that museum entrance fees and shop sales went into its funds at once (Historian's Office, archive copy of 24 June 2017).
- From 1981 the state named the Office investor for the first restoration plan; the Revolutionary Armed Forces paid for its 1986 to 1992 restoration of El Morro and La Cabaña. When state funding stopped after 1989, the Spanish cooperation agency backed the recovery of the San Francisco convent, and the Ateneo de Caracas and the painter Oswaldo Guayasamín paid for single restorations (Historian's Office).
- In August 2016 the military conglomerate GAESA took over the Office's business operations, including Habaguanex with some 20 hotels, 30 shops and more than 25 restaurants; officials told AP the Office would keep only museum entry fees and souvenir shop income.
- Decree 357 of 2019 abolished the Grupo Empresarial Centro Histórico, placed its entities under the Historian and transferred its assets free of charge to the Office's system (Gaceta Oficial No. 24, 2019).

WIDER CONTEXT
- GAESA, the armed forces' business group, runs hotels, the Mariel port and the Banco Financiero Internacional (AP, 2016). The US State Department's Cuba Restricted List of November 2017 names GAESA, Compañía Turística Habaguanex S.A. and Habaguanex hotels in Old Havana (Federal Register 2017-24449).
- The connection: Habaguanex was the Historian's Office's business arm until GAESA took it over in 2016; the museum's entry fees and shop income stayed with the Office (AP).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is clean in the atlas's terms: public money and its own takings, with no flagged funder. The military link is indirect. Worth watching: how the Office's businesses are run after the 2019 decree, and who gives the donations the law allows.
