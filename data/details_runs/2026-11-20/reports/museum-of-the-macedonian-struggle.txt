MUSEUM OF THE MACEDONIAN STRUGGLE FOR INDEPENDENCE: Skopje, North Macedonia
ID: museum-of-the-macedonian-struggle
Website: https://mmb.org.mk (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A national history museum on the left bank of the Vardar in central Skopje (Prizma), between the Archaeological Museum, the Holocaust Museum and the Stone Bridge, on the former site of the Skopje branch of the National Bank of Yugoslavia (Wikipedia).
- The government founded it as a National Institution by a decision of 31 July 2008; building work began that year and took three years, and it opened on 8 September 2011, the 20th anniversary of independence (Museum for All).
- On opening day President Gjorge Ivanov placed the declaration of independence in the lobby, where it occupies the central place (Museum for All).
- The building has 6,435 square metres of net floor space, 2,000 of them for exhibitions; the permanent exhibition covers Macedonian history of the 19th and 20th centuries (museum).
- In 2014 the museum described its holdings as 109 wax figures, 25 portraits, 85 paintings of mass scenes and battles, and 1,500 items including weapons and documents (quoted by Florian Bieber); Bieber wrote that most of the paintings were by artists from Russia and Ukraine.
- It runs a subsidiary, Porta Macedonia, which rents out space for conferences, concerts, receptions and weddings (museum).
- Acting director Anche Ilievska (museum).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10 to 18, closed Mondays and public holidays; tickets 100 denars for Macedonian visitors and 300 denars for foreign visitors, with a guided tour included; free for everyone on the first Saturday of each month; lifts, an accessible entrance and a Braille catalogue (museum, Museum for All).
- Visits used to be by compulsory guided tour, with photography banned (Florian Bieber, 2014; Foreign Policy, 2016); the museum now also offers individual visits with English texts (museum).

FUNDING
Museum of the Macedonian Struggle: state-funded: built and run by the North Macedonian government
- A National Institution set up by government decision (Museum for All); the Ministry of Culture heads its list of partner institutions (museum).
- Prizma's Skopje 2014 database records 2,110,940 euros (129,822,831 denars) spent on the museum building, and 2,175,946 euros in all on projects with the museum as investor (Prizma).
- Its own income comes from tickets and from renting Porta Macedonia (museum).

Red flags: Party-political national story in a state museum, from 2011
- Its permanent exhibition ended with Dragan Bogdanovski, an exile and 1990 co-founder of the then governing party VMRO-DPMNE, whose New Jersey licence plate reading "VMRO 1" was on show; Bieber wrote that this made "the merger between the nation, the national movement and the governing party" complete (Florian Bieber, "The museum with the longest name", 2014).
- Foreign Policy wrote that its mission "isn't pedagogical so much as patriotic", with guides there "to ensure that the Macedonian history on display is properly interpreted", and noted that it makes no mention of the country's ethnic Albanians, estimated at 25 to 30 percent of the population (Valerie Hopkins, Foreign Policy, 2016).
- A 2022 paper in the journal EthnoAnthropoZoom argues that this museum and the other Skopje 2014 museums were a deliberate effort to reshape historical interpretation and collective memory into a new post-communist national narrative (Jasmina Pijanmanova; only the abstract was read).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Foreign Policy described Skopje 2014 as a 730 million dollar building programme initiated by Nikola Gruevski, prime minister from 2006, whose structures came to symbolise a decade "marked by wasteful spending and a lack of transparency"; the 2016 protests centred mostly on allegations of corruption (Foreign Policy, 2016).
- The museum was one of the programme's centrepieces (Foreign Policy).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is North Macedonian public money and carries no funder flag. The weak point is what that money bought: a state museum shaped around one party's national story.
