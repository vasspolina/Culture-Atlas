THAPONG VISUAL ARTS CENTRE: Gaborone, Botswana
ID: thapong-visual-arts-centre-gaborone
Website: https://contemporaryand.com/en/places/thapong-visual-arts-centre (checked 2026-10-25; this is a directory listing, the centre has no site of its own; the listing points to facebook.com/thapongarts.bw)

OVERVIEW
- Membership-based visual arts organisation in Gaborone with a gallery, studios and workshops, and an international residency programme that began in 2002 (Contemporary And).
- Established in 1998 according to Contemporary And. Mmegi (2016) dates its origin earlier: the Thapong International Artists' Workshop was established in 1989, and in 1999 it set up a centre in an old building in Gaborone Village donated by the National Museum and Art Gallery.
- Thapong began as an international artists' workshop programme in Botswana that grew out of the Triangle Arts Trust (Wikipedia, in its entry on the artist Ann Gollifer).
- Building and site: a colonial-era house, formerly a residence for colonial officials, became the gallery; a coffee shop was built in front of it and six studios were added (Mmegi, 2014).
- On site: gallery, six studios and a coffee shop.
- It runs the Thapong Artist of the Year Awards (TAYA), introduced in 2004 (Mmegi, 2016).
- Visiting: no opening hours, admission or accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Thapong Visual Arts Centre: mainly public and corporate: Botswana's culture ministry for the buildings, a diamond trading company and a bank foundation for the refit and awards
- 2013 to 2014 refit: Ministry of Youth, Sports and Culture, P1.5 million for new art studios; DTC Botswana, P654,908 for the gallery refurbishment; First National Bank Foundation, the coffee shop (Mmegi, 30 October 2013). Mmegi's July 2014 piece names the Department of Youth Sports and Culture, DTC, Plascon, Collegiums and the FNB Foundation as funders, gives no amounts for them, and reports the colonial-era building was renovated at a cost of P250 000.
- Awards: the Botswana Telecommunications Corporation (BTC) Foundation worked with the centre on the BTC phonebook cover and TAYA competitions. The phonebook cover winner usually receives P50,000 including the exchange programme. In 2021 neither competition was held: the centre said its sponsors were hit by the COVID-19 pandemic, and that one sponsor, the Ministry of Youth, Sport and Culture (MYSC), did not host the President's Day competitions (Mmegi, 17 October 2021).
- The Daily News (dailynews.gov.bw) pages on the centre did not load (expired security certificate). Funders and sums that appear only there are not stated in this report.
- No reporting found of audited accounts, an annual report or a current board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is a mix of Botswana ministry funds and corporate sponsors (a diamond trading company, a telecoms company, a bank foundation) at amounts that were published only at the time of the 2013 refit. The weak point is that no accounts, reports or current board were found, and the centre has no site of its own. Worth watching: whether sponsors of the awards return after the 2021 pause.
