KUNSTHAUS BASELLAND: Münchenstein, Switzerland
ID: kunsthaus-baselland-münchenstein
Website: https://www.kunsthausbaselland.ch/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at Helsinki-Strasse 5 on the Dreispitz site in Münchenstein near Basel, a few minutes by public transport from Basel SBB station (the space's own pages).
- Its roots are in the Basellandschaftliche Kunstvereinigung, founded in 1944 and now the Kunstverein Baselland, a private association that is the institution's sponsor (Träger). In 1997 the association bought a former factory in Muttenz and used it until 2024.
- Ines Goldbach has directed it since August 2013 (German Wikipedia; the article does not say whether she still holds the post).
- Building: a former champagne warehouse converted by Buchner Bründler Architekten, who won the competition in 2015 with a plan to keep part of the old hall. Limestone outer walls, steel roof trusses and the asphalt floor were kept, with three new concrete light chimneys (Bauwelt). About 1,500 square metres of exhibition space (Bauwelt). The new building opened in April 2024 with the exhibition "Rewilding".
- On site: exhibition halls, a 400 square metre foyer, a free-access library and a shop. The site belongs to the Christoph Merian Stiftung (Bauwelt) and sits among the art school, the House of Electronic Arts and artists' studios.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 11:00 to 18:00 (Thursday to 20:00), Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Monday. Adults CHF 12, reduced CHF 9, free under 26.

FUNDING
Kunsthaus Baselland: mainly public with corporate and foundation partners: the canton of Basel-Landschaft, the Christoph Merian Stiftung, Novartis and the Basellandschaftliche Kantonalbank
- The canton's culture office pays an annual subsidy (stated by the space); German Wikipedia says the canton provides most of the funding, for artistic operations only. No reporting found of the amount.
- Partners listed on the space's page: the canton, the municipalities of Münchenstein and Muttenz, Basellandschaftliche Kantonalbank (BLKB), Novartis, Habasit, Jenzer Fleisch + Feinkost AG and Hotel Gasthof zum Ochsen in Arlesheim. No amounts or years are given.
- Novartis calls Kunsthaus Baselland a long-standing partner on its sponsoring page; the page gives no amount.
- The new building was funded by the canton, the Christoph Merian Stiftung and BLKB, plus donations from foundations, companies and private donors the space does not name (its architecture page). No reporting found of the construction cost.
- Private members pay from CHF 30 a year (students and apprentices up to 25; single membership CHF 90); the Art Club for individuals starts at CHF 1,000.
- The Stiftung Kunsthaus Baselland, led by Jan Bangert, owns the new building (the space's page).

Red flags: Novartis settlement
- On 1 July 2020 Novartis agreed to pay $678 million to settle US civil False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute allegations that it paid doctors to prescribe its drugs through speaker events, from 2002 to 2011. The US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York handled the case, which began with a 2011 whistleblower complaint. Novartis also signed a corporate integrity agreement with the HHS Office of Inspector General (Skadden).
- Novartis is a current partner of the Kunsthaus. No reporting found of a protest at the Kunsthaus over it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Kunsthaus, its funders or its partners found in art media. The one evaluative piece found is a mostly admiring architecture review.
1. Bauwelt: Kaye Geipel, "Mischt mit" (2024, issue 14)
URL: http://www.bauwelt.de/dl/2114251/38_bis_41_5_Baseland_jl_p1-4.pdf
- Praises the conversion and Goldbach's programme; notes that outside groups pay CHF 2,100 to hire the foyer.
- Criticises the architecture in one respect only: heavy use of concrete, which Geipel ties to a competition won ten years earlier.
- Does not name Novartis.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly public and local, and the Kunsthaus publishes no amounts for any funder. The weak point is Novartis, a named long-term partner that paid $678 million in 2020 over doctor payments; no objection to it at the Kunsthaus is on record. Watch for the first annual report with partner amounts.
