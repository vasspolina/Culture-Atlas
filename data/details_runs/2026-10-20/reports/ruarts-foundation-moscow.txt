RUARTS FOUNDATION: Moscow, Russia
ID: ruarts-foundation-moscow
Website: https://ruarts.foundation/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art foundation and exhibition space at Trubnikovsky Lane 6, Moscow; its stated mission is to "study, systematise, examine and promote Russian art".
- Founded in 2003 by collector Marianna Sardarova, who told The Blueprint the idea came to her in 2003; art director is Catherine Borissoff (spelled Katrin Borisov in Russian press).
- The foundation's six-storey building opened on 17 April 2021 with the exhibition "Soft Homeland" (Moskvich Mag, The Blueprint; names of the architect and floor area not given in either).
- The building holds exhibition halls, a library, an educational centre, a bookshop and a café (The Blueprint, 2021).
- Collection of more than 2,000 works, focused on unofficial Soviet art and contemporary Russian art; sections of the site cover Now, Archive, Publishing, Auction and Grants (stated by the foundation).
- Its older gallery on Ostozhenka is closing after 21 years; the last show runs from 18 September to 4 October 2026, and the foundation continues at Trubnikovsky Lane with a programme booked to 2028 (Moskvich Mag, 10 September 2026).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 20:00; admission was paid at the 2021 opening, and no current price was found.

FUNDING
Ruarts Foundation: private: the founder's money; the source of that money is not published
- No funding source, partner list, board, annual report or accounts appears on the foundation's About page.
- Marianna Sardarova founded the foundation (Moskvich Mag, 2021).
- Transparency International Russia names Rashid Sardarov's wife as Marianna Sardarova, citing a February 2024 French company registry document. The Ruarts founder has the same name. No source opened states that the two are the same person or that his money pays for the foundation.
- Rashid Sardarov owns JSC South Ural Industrial Company, which extracts and processes oil and gas (Transparency International Russia, profil.at).
- No reporting found of a state, corporate or other named funder behind Ruarts.

Red flags: Rashid Sardarov, Russian oil and gas owner
- Sardarov is not on the EU sanctions list (profil.at, 22 March 2022); no sanctions designation is described in Transparency International Russia's 2025 piece. No source opened places him on a US or UK list.
- The 2016 Panama Papers linked Sardarov to dozens of offshore companies (Transparency International Russia, advocacy source).
- Novaya Gazeta (Irek Murtazin, 2 March 2016) described a structure in which Kazakh gas condensate was processed through Gazprom units; the article does not accuse Sardarov of a crime, and he denied close ties to Gazprom officials, saying he had only "production relations" with Gazprom (translation).
- Unverified: that Sardarov's business profited from exclusive access to Gazprom processing and moved profits abroad to avoid Russian tax, from Transparency International Russia, an anti-corruption group.
- Unverified: that French authorities seized a former Martin Gray estate of his in 2023 in a fraud case, from Transparency International Russia, citing Nice Matin.
- Transparency International Russia states he held a Bosnian passport and, as of February 2024, a St Kitts and Nevis citizenship with his wife.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Ruarts is a private foundation whose money is not disclosed. It publishes no accounts, board or donor list. The only issue is the matching name of Rashid Sardarov's wife, as Transparency International Russia gives it; Sardarov is a Russian oil and gas owner named in the Panama Papers and questioned by Russian investigative outlets, and he is not on the EU sanctions list. Watch for any published funding statement and for whether any source ties his money to the foundation.
