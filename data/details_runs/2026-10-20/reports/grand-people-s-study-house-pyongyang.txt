GRAND PEOPLE'S STUDY HOUSE: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: grand-people-s-study-house-pyongyang
Website: http://gpsh.edu.kp (checked 2026-10-20; down: returns 502 Bad Gateway from outside North Korea)

OVERVIEW
- North Korea's national library and centre of Juche studies, on Kim Il Sung Square by the Taedong River, opposite the Juche Tower (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 1 April 1982, partly to mark Kim Il Sung's 70th birthday; construction began in December 1979 (Wikipedia, Koryo Tours).
- Successor to the Pyongyang City Library (opened 13 November 1945), rebuilt after the Korean War as the National Central Library (15 August 1954) and renamed the Central Library in 1973 (Wikipedia).
- Ten storeys in neo-traditional Korean style with a green-tiled roof, about 100,000 m² of floor space and 600 rooms; Kim Jong Il insisted on the style (Wikipedia).
- Reading rooms, computers that reach the domestic intranet, lecture theatres, language classes in English, Russian, German and Chinese, and a music room; North Korean sources claim space for 30 million books (Wikipedia, Koryo Tours).
- Browsing hours 09:00 to 18:00 except holidays; free for citizens aged 17 and over. Foreigners visit with a local guide as part of organised tours (Koryo Tours, 2018).

FUNDING
Grand People's Study House: state-funded: the government of North Korea
- A state institution: Kim Il Sung proposed a library for the site in the winter of 1973 (Koryo Tours). No budget, accounts or funder list is published.
- International organisations have donated books, among them The Asia Foundation from 1996 (College & Research Libraries News, 2019).
- A 1989 Pyongyang Broadcasting Station report claimed more than 10 million visitors a year (Wikipedia). No independent figure found.
- No reporting found of the Library Law the record cites, or of any non-state funder.

Red flags: owned by a government under US sanctions
- Executive Order 13722, signed by President Obama in March 2016, blocks "the property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea" (Conventus Law, Federal Register).
- The library is the country's centre of Juche studies (Wikipedia). No source found names a funder other than the North Korean state.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The UN Commission of Inquiry reported on 17 February 2014 an "almost complete denial of the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion" in North Korea, and found that the State uses propaganda to manufacture absolute obedience to the Supreme Leader (UN News).
- The Commission found that state surveillance reaches into private life and that almost no criticism of the political system goes unnoticed or unpunished (UN News).
- The Study House is the state's national library and Juche study centre; its computers reach only the domestic intranet, and readers must request items from staff (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The funding is North Korean state money, and the state is under US blocking sanctions; the only outside support found is donated books. Nothing about its budget or collection can be checked from outside. Worth watching only as a sanctions case: any foreign donor, partner or exchange would deal with a blocked government.
