DAADGALERIE: Berlin, Germany
ID: daadgalerie-berlin
Website: https://www.berliner-kuenstlerprogramm.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition and event space of the Berliner Künstlerprogramm (BKP) of the DAAD, the German Academic Exchange Service, a residency programme that invites about 20 international artists, writers, filmmakers and composers to Berlin each year (stated by the programme).
- The programme began in 1963 as a Ford Foundation initiative in West Berlin; the DAAD took it over in 1965 (Wikipedia). It says it has supported more than 1,200 artists (stated by the programme).
- The gallery opened in 1978 in the silent film star Henny Porten's former villa on Kurfürstenstraße and moved to Zimmerstraße near Checkpoint Charlie in 2005 (Wikipedia; taz, 17 January 2017).
- In January 2017 it moved to Oranienstraße 161 in Kreuzberg, a former shoe store built in 1910 to 1912 by the theatre architect Oskar Kaufmann, with about 500 m² over two floors: a 250 m² ground-floor gallery and a first-floor studio for 100 visitors (taz, Linda Gerner, 17 January 2017).
- The programme left Oranienstraße 161 on 30 September 2026; since 3 October its season runs in the Grüner Salon of the Volksbühne on Rosa-Luxemburg-Platz and at other Berlin venues (stated by the programme).
- Programme: exhibitions, readings, concerts, film screenings and performances by current and former fellows; in 2026 the fellow Lina Lapelytė showed "We Make Years Out of Hours" at the Hamburger Bahnhof from 1 May 2026 to 10 January 2027 (stated by the programme).
- Head of the programme: Silvia Fehrmann (programme team page).
- Visiting: the Grüner Salon is not yet wheelchair accessible (stated by the programme).

FUNDING
daadgalerie: public: the German Federal Foreign Office and the State of Berlin, through the DAAD
- The programme states that it is institutionally funded from the budgets of the Federal Foreign Office and the Berlin Senate; the DAAD itself is an association of German universities funded by the Foreign Office (programme imprint).
- Berlin's budget plan shows the programme's income for 2026 at 2,398,300 euros: 1,800,000 euros from the federal government, 563,300 euros from the State of Berlin and 35,000 euros of its own revenue. In 2024 it received 1,875,708 euros from the federal government and 610,500 euros from Berlin (Berlin budget plan 2026/2027, Einzelplan 08).
- Berlin cut its grant by 60,000 euros a year for 2026 and 2027 to consolidate the budget, while moving 177,000 euros into the line from another title (same budget plan).
- Ford Foundation: created the programme in 1963 with an initial donation of three million US dollars, of which 590,000 dollars paid for artists, writers, academics, scientists and composers to live in Berlin (Wikipedia, citing the Ford Foundation annual report for 1963).
- Moontower Foundation: with the programme it funds the Rebecca Horn fellowship, first awarded to Lina Lapelytė as the 2026 music and sound fellow (stated by the programme).
- With Artis, a New York and Tel Aviv non-profit, the programme offers funded stays of up to three months to Israeli artists and curators; the grants come from Foreign Office money (stated by the programme).

Red flags: 2024 refusals of DAAD fellowships over the president's Nancy Fraser decision
- In April 2024 the Dutch artist Jonas Staal rejected his nomination for the DAAD artists' residency in an open letter to DAAD president Joybrato Mukherjee, citing Mukherjee's withdrawal, as rector of the University of Cologne, of Nancy Fraser's Albertus Magnus Professorship after she signed a pro-Palestinian letter (ArtReview, 19 April 2024; e-flux notes, 19 April 2024).
- In July 2024 the Irish writer Joanna Walsh declined a DAAD fellowship for the same reason, writing to the heads of the Artists-in-Berlin Program; her letter says Eva Giannakopoulou, Jonas Staal and China Miéville had already withdrawn from the 2024 programme (Spike, 10 July 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: German federal and Berlin state money through the DAAD. Worth watching are artists' boycotts aimed at the DAAD leadership.
