ISRAEL MUSEUM: Jerusalem, Israel
ID: israel-museum
Website: https://www.imj.org.il/en (checked 2026-10-20; down: the page failed a certificate check and returned no content)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of art, archaeology and Jewish life on a 20-acre campus in Givat Ram, Jerusalem, near the Knesset, the Supreme Court and the Hebrew University (Wikipedia).
- Opened in May 1965; Jerusalem mayor Teddy Kollek was the driving force behind it (Wikipedia).
- Wings are named for donors: the Samuel and Saidye Bronfman archaeology wing, the Edmond and Lily Safra fine arts wing, the Mandel Wing for Jewish Art and Life, and the Ruth Youth Wing (Wikipedia).
- Alfred Mansfeld designed the original buildings, Armand Bartos and Frederick Kiesler the Shrine of the Book, and Isamu Noguchi the Billy Rose Art Garden; a $100 million renovation by Efrat-Kowalsky Architects and James Carpenter finished in July 2010 (Wikipedia).
- It holds about 500,000 objects and runs the Rockefeller Archaeological Museum and Ticho House off site. Jacob (Yasha) Grobman became director on 1 March 2026, after interim director Suzanne Landau (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily, Tuesday 16:00 to 21:00 and Friday 10:00 to 14:00; adults NIS 54, students NIS 39, ages 5 to 17 free on Tuesdays, Saturdays and in August (Time Out listing dated March 2023; current prices not confirmed).

FUNDING
Israel Museum: mainly private donors abroad, with a minority state and city grant
- Government and city sources provide 10 to 12 percent of the operating budget; the museum raises the rest and pays 17.5 percent VAT and property tax (Wikipedia).
- American Friends of the Israel Museum raised $270 million in cash, including $47 million for the endowment, and gave $210 million in art from 1972 to 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Mandel Foundation: support since 1971; a $12 million gift in 2009 for the Mandel Wing; a 2024 grant for the Bezalel gallery; a three-year $4.5 million matching grant in 2025 for exhibitions (Mandel Foundation).
- Edmond J. Safra Philanthropic Foundation: $12 million in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- The endowment stood at $200 million (Wikipedia, undated).
- The Israeli register page for the museum (company 520028275) showed no figures when opened on 20 October 2026, so the 2025 income split in the record could not be checked.

Red flags: PACBI boycott letter, 2009
- On 5 July 2009 PACBI, part of the BDS National Committee, published "Open Letter to Yinka Shonibare: Beheading Justice?", urging him not to guest-curate at the museum.
- It called the museum a state institution in occupied Jerusalem and said taking part would help whitewash the occupation, coming after the 2008 to 2009 Gaza war (PACBI, an openly pro-boycott campaign).

Red flags: West Bank antiquities in the 2013 Herod exhibition
- "Herod the Great: The King's Final Journey" showed more than 250 artefacts dug at Herodium and Jericho, both in the West Bank (Al Jazeera, Jonathan Cook, 2013).
- Hamdan Taha, the Palestinian Authority official for antiquities, denounced it as an assault on Palestinian heritage; former Israel Antiquities Authority archaeologist Yonathan Mizrachi said the 1954 Hague Convention bars such digs. Director James Snyder said the objects were on loan and would return to the site (Al Jazeera).
- In 1968 the museum bought 1,000 items from Moshe Dayan's collection for $1 million; some had been acquired illegally (Wikipedia).

Not related: the Bible Lands Museum Jerusalem, a separate museum next door in Givat Ram, whose 2019 show of West Bank finds Hyperallergic criticised.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism of the museum itself. The main objections come from Palestinian officials and the boycott campaign, covered above.

- Ai said the museum covered some of his works during Donald Trump's speech there on 23 May 2017.
- A museum spokeswoman, unnamed, said finished works stayed in place and works still being installed were protected from the crowds.
- A news report, not an argued critique.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2016 the Tel Aviv Museum of Art pulled a planned show by Ai Weiwei and Miki Kratsman that was to include portraits of Palestinians; the museum cited scheduling (The Art Newspaper).
- The Israel Museum hosted Ai's 2017 show a year later; Ai told AFP that simply endorsing a boycott was too easy and "My voice should be heard" (Times of Israel).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly private: American Friends of the Israel Museum and family foundations such as Mandel and Safra, with a small state and city share. No fossil fuel, arms or other flagged funder is on record. The weak points are the boycott campaign aimed at the museum and its showing of antiquities dug in the occupied West Bank.
