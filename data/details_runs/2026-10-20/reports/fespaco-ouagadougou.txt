FESPACO: Ouagadougou, Burkina Faso
ID: fespaco-ouagadougou
Website: https://fespaco.bf

OVERVIEW
- Panafrican Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou, held every second year in Ouagadougou. The 29th edition ran from 22 February to 1 March 2025 and the 30th is scheduled for 20 to 27 March 2027 (FESPACO).
- Founded in 1969 and renamed FESPACO in 1972, when a government decree recognised it as an institution; its top prize, the Étalon de Yennenga, dates from 1972 (Wikipedia).
- A new headquarters, begun in 1994, was inaugurated in 2005; opening night takes place at the Stade du 4-Août (Wikipedia).
- Alex Moussa Sawadogo has been delegate general since 2020 (Wikipedia). The Agence Burkinabè de la Cinématographie et de l'Audiovisuel issues the festival's call for films and a call for sponsors and donors to fund special prizes (FESPACO).
- The 2025 edition had 235 films in the official selection, 17 of them competing for the Étalon d'or de Yennenga, with Chad as guest country of honour (Le360, AFP).
- Programmes include the MICA market, where about 100 stands were rented at roughly 500,000 FCFA each (Africultures), and FESPACO-Professionnel (EEAS).

FUNDING
FESPACO: mainly public: the Burkina Faso state, with the European Union, the state lottery LONAB and a telecom sponsor
- Delegate general Sawadogo said the forecast budget for 2025 was about 2 billion FCFA and the executed budget 1.3 to 1.5 billion FCFA, excluding technical support. The state's share was about 70 percent and rose to nearly 80 percent (Africultures, Olivier Barlet, interview "Alex Moussa Sawadogo tire un bilan du Fespaco 2025", 3 April 2025).
- European Union: 195 million FCFA for the 29th edition, paying for selection committee experts, FESPACO-Professionnel activities, two awards, 35 trophies and printed programmes (EEAS delegation to Burkina Faso, 21 February 2025).
- LONAB, the Loterie nationale burkinabè: 50 million FCFA as national major sponsor, signed on 27 February 2025 by its director general Ibrahim Ben Harouna Zarani and by Fidèle Tamini, president of the national organising committee, for the Ministry of Culture; it includes the 5 million FCFA "Prix de la chance" (Wakat Séra, Daouda Zongo, 27 February 2025).
- Moov Africa Burkina is the official sponsor of the Bronze Étalon de Yennenga for fiction features, a prize worth 5,000,000 FCFA (Sidwaya, Wamini Sidwaya, 27 February 2025).
- The 22 special prizes, worth 97 million FCFA in total, come from 19 donors (Fraternité Matin, "FESPACO 2025 : 22 prix spéciaux d'une valeur de 97 millions FCFA seront décernés", 26 February 2025).
- AFP lists the European Union and UNESCO among the 2025 financial partners (Le360).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces on the 2025 edition. One is an interview in which the festival's own delegate general lists problems; the other is supportive of the festival and criticises neither its organisation nor its funders.
1. Africultures: Olivier Barlet, "Alex Moussa Sawadogo tire un bilan du Fespaco 2025" (3 April 2025)
URL: http://africultures.com/alex-moussa-sawadogo-tire-un-bilan-du-fespaco-2025-16324/
- Sawadogo lists problems: crowds at screenings of Burkinabè films left some ticket holders unable to enter, poor acoustics and limited visibility in some city hall rooms, insufficient air service with a last-minute Royal Air Maroc daily quota, and visa delays from an electronic system bug.
- He reports the criticism that placing the national section apart put it in a "ghetto", and answers that Burkinabè films play to full houses.
- Asked about President Ibrahim Traoré's speech, he says panafricanism "was not an empty word" this year.
2. Contemporary And: Makella Ama, "FESPACO 2025 Made Space for the Ceremony of African Cinema" (8 May 2025)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/fespaco-2025-made-space-for-the-ceremony-of-african-cinema
- Presents the festival as rooted in protest against Eurocentric dictatorship, and describes Traoré's pledge to African sovereignty as a visible presence at the opening and closing ceremonies he attended.
- Says calls for better pay, rights and working conditions in the film industry often come as "knee jerk reactions" from people with money. It does not criticise FESPACO's organisation.

WIDER CONTEXT
- AFP, in Le360 on 22 February 2025 ("Burkina Faso: début du Fespaco, grand festival de cinéma africain, malgré le contexte sécuritaire"), writes that since the 2022 coup the junta led by Captain Ibrahim Traoré has silenced critics through arrests, kidnappings and forced conscription.
- Connection: the Burkina Faso state pays about 70 to 80 percent of FESPACO's executed 2025 budget (Africultures), and Traoré attended the opening and closing ceremonies (Contemporary And). The AFP passage concerns the junta's treatment of critics in general.

BOTTOM LINE
FESPACO is mainly state-funded: its delegate general says the Burkina Faso state paid about 70 to 80 percent of the executed 2025 budget, with 195 million FCFA from the European Union and 50 million FCFA from the state lottery LONAB. AFP describes the government as a military junta that has silenced critics.
