LA VIRREINA CENTRE DE LA IMATGE: Barcelona, Spain
ID: la-virreina-barcelona
Website: https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/lavirreina/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Publicly owned centre for contemporary art and the image in the Palau de la Virreina, La Rambla 99, which it describes as "effectively a passageway into the Raval neighbourhood" (La Virreina website).
- Its stated programme runs along three lines, the city, the archive and images, through exhibitions, seminars, courses and publications (La Virreina website, mission page).
- Named for the palace, which took its name from the viceroy's widow, Maria Francesca de Fivaller-Clasquerí i de Bru, who lived there after the death of her husband Manuel d'Amat, Viceroy of Peru, in 1782 (Catalan Wikipedia; Amics de la Rambla).
- Building: a palace between baroque and rococo built from 1772 to 1778 for Manuel d'Amat. Catalan Wikipedia names Josep Ausich i Mir as architect; Amics de la Rambla says the plans are traditionally attributed to him, with facade decoration by Carles Grau and Francesc Serra. It was declared a national monument in 1941 (Catalan Wikipedia).
- Earlier uses: the first exhibition was in 1940 (Amics de la Rambla), the Museu d'Arts Decoratives opened there in 1949 (Catalan Wikipedia), and the City Council's Department of Culture has been based there since 1986 (Amics de la Rambla). It also houses the Gabinet Numismàtic de Catalunya and the Museu Postal (Catalan Wikipedia).
- Direction: Valentín Roma directed the centre from 2016 to May 2026 (La Virreina website). On 10 June 2026 the MACBA consortium named him director of the MACBA after a competition (Ara, 10 June 2026).
- The Institut de Cultura de Barcelona advertised the post of "Director de la Virreina Centre de la Imatge i coordinació de centres d'art" (file 145/2026) as a merit-based contest open to career civil servants of grade A1 and, as an exception, other graduates; applications closed on 29 July 2026 and the call was marked resolved on 27 October 2026 (CIDO, Diputació de Barcelona).
- Season: the 2026 programme has eight exhibitions, led by a Jean-Luc Godard show curated by Manuel Asín (28 March to 4 October 2026), and shows on Gràcia Territori Sonor and the artist Juana Dolores (Betevé, 14 January 2026; La Virreina website).
- Visiting: free admission; open Tuesday to Sunday and public holidays, 11 am to 8 pm; closed 1 and 6 January, 1 May, 24 June and 25 and 26 December; groups of more than 10 book in advance (La Virreina website).

FUNDING
La Virreina Centre de la Imatge: public: Barcelona City Council through its Institut de Cultura (ICUB)
- Owner of the building: in 1944 the City Council, then led by mayor Miquel Mateu, bought the palace from the Carreras family for 3.9 million pesetas (Catalan Wikipedia). Amics de la Rambla also dates the City Council's purchase to 1944.
- Operator: the centre sits on the City Council's own website, and the palace is the headquarters of the Institut de Cultura de Barcelona (ICUB), the City Council's culture institute (La Virreina website; Amics de la Rambla; English Wikipedia).
- Budget: 1.3 million euros for 2026, of which 750,000 euros go to exhibitions and public programmes (Betevé, 14 January 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public money from Barcelona City Council. Worth watching is the choice of Valentín Roma's successor.
