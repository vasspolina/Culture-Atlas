JÜDISCHES MUSEUM BERLIN: Berlin, Germany
ID: juedisches-museum-berlin
Website: https://www.jmberlin.de

OVERVIEW
- Museum of German-Jewish history from the Middle Ages to the present, in Kreuzberg, Berlin (Wikipedia, museum visit page).
- A first Jewish Museum opened in Berlin on 24 January 1933 and was shut by the Gestapo on 10 November 1938. Daniel Libeskind's design was chosen in 1989, construction began in 1992, and the new museum opened on 9 September 2001 (Wikipedia).
- The Libeskind Building has a zinc facade and houses the core exhibition; the baroque Old Building holds the main entrance and temporary exhibitions. The W. Michael Blumenthal Academy across the street houses the library and reading room and the entrance to ANOHA, the children's museum (museum visit page).
- The permanent exhibition "Jewish Life in Germany: Past and Present" opened on 23 August 2020 and replaced "Two Millennia of German Jewish History" (Wikipedia).
- Directors: W. Michael Blumenthal (1997 to 2014), Peter Schäfer (2014 to 2019) and Hetty Berg (from 2020) (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm, closed Mondays. The core exhibition is free for everyone; temporary exhibitions in the Old Building cost 10 euros, or 4 reduced, and under-18s enter free. The entire exhibition space is wheelchair accessible (museum visit page).

FUNDING
Jüdisches Museum Berlin: German federal government subsidy
- The museum is subsidised by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (museum website footer).
- The federal commissioner for culture and media announced that about 24.5 million euros would be available for 2025, roughly 550,000 euros more than before (Jüdische Allgemeine, citing epd, "Mehr Bundesmittel für Jüdisches Museum", 30 June 2025). Wikipedia says a federal grant covers around three-quarters of the budget, with the rest mainly from donations and ticket sales.
- In November 2020 the Bundestag budget committee finalised 3.2 million euros more so the museum could offer free admission (Jüdische Allgemeine, citing epd, 27 November 2020).
- The museum says its collections grow "thanks also to many donors from Germany and abroad" and lists donations, membership, a Friends of the JMB programme and a Companies page for sponsors (museum about page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The 2019 pressure on the museum and the resignation of director Peter Schäfer; criticism came from Israel's ambassador and the Central Council of Jews in Germany, and museum professionals defended the museum.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Berlin's Jewish Museum Director Resigns After Criticism Over BDS-Related Tweet" (18 June 2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/jewish-museum-resign/
- Schäfer resigned on 14 June 2019 after the museum tweeted on 6 June an article from TAZ about an open letter by 240 Jewish and Israeli scholars against a Bundestag resolution condemning the BDS movement as antisemitic.
- Israel's ambassador to Germany, Jeremy Issacharoff, called the post "shameful"; Josef Schuster of the Central Council of Jews in Germany said the museum had lost the council's trust and later called the resignation "an important step".
- TAZ reported in December 2018 that the Israeli government had sent a letter to Chancellor Merkel's office asking Germany to cut funding to the museum and 11 other organisations; the Israeli government did not confirm sending it. The Prime Minister's Office told Yedioth Ahronoth that Israel "will continue fighting these organizations."
2. Museums Journal: Simon Stephens, "Concerns raised over resignation of Jewish Museum Berlin director" (2 July 2019)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2019/07/02072019-concerns-raised-over-resignation-of-jewish-museum-berlin-director/
- More than 80 museum professionals signed a letter calling Schäfer "a man of great integrity", rejecting the accusation that the museum was "anti-Jewish", and arguing his resignation came from a lengthy campaign. They warned of growing interference from governments and campaign bodies in the independence of cultural institutions.
- Culture Minister Monika Grütters, chair of the museum's board of trustees, said she respected Schäfer's decision to resign "with immediate effect".

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is subsidised by the German federal culture commissioner, with about 24.5 million euros announced from the federal culture budget for 2025. Its website lists donors, members, a Friends programme and sponsors. In 2019 the museum's tweet drew criticism from Israel's ambassador and the Central Council of Jews in Germany, and director Peter Schäfer resigned.
