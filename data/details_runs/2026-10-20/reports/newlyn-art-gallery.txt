NEWLYN ART GALLERY: Penzance, United Kingdom
ID: newlyn-art-gallery
Website: https://newlynartgallery.co.uk

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery in Newlyn, Cornwall, with a second venue, The Exchange, in Penzance; the two are about 2 km apart, a 20 to 30 minute walk along the seafront (gallery visit page).
- Opened in 1895 with the Le Grice family giving the land and the philanthropist John Passmore Edwards supporting it (gallery website); Wikipedia adds that it was designed by James Hicks of Redruth and was conceived as a home and exhibition venue for the Newlyn School of Art.
- In 2007 it redeveloped the Newlyn site, after a RIBA design competition, and took over Penzance's former telephone exchange as The Exchange (gallery website, Wikipedia).
- Run as an educational charity, number 273785; it describes itself as an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation and a Cornwall Council Strategic Arts Client (gallery website).
- James Green is Director and in 2025 began his 20th year in the post (gallery review "2025 in Reflection, 2026 in Sight").
- 2025 programme: Hew Locke and Chantal Joffe in summer, and Abigail Reynolds's "Walking A Cappella" across both venues; a partnership with the homelessness charity St Petrocs delivered artist-led workshops, and the gallery became an Active Member of the Gallery Climate Coalition (gallery review).
- Open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 17:00, at New Road, Newlyn (TR18 5PZ) and Princes Street, Penzance (TR18 2NL). Annual passes cover both venues; children's entry is free; the National Art Pass gives 50% off standard admission. Both venues are fully accessible, with a lift to the upper gallery at Newlyn, wheelchairs available at both venues and quieter visits on Fridays 10:00 to 12:00 (gallery visit page).

FUNDING
Newlyn Art Gallery: mainly public: Arts Council England and Cornwall Council, plus trusts and earned income
- Arts Council England and Cornwall Council together give 50% of its funding; the rest comes from admissions, shops, cafes and fundraising (gallery support page).
- Income was 733,736 pounds and spending 842,543 pounds in the year to 31 March 2025 (Charity Commission data via OpenCharities).
- Grants recorded on 360Giving between 2004 and 10 February 2026: Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport 7 grants totalling 2,080,586 pounds; Paul Hamlyn Foundation 149,700 pounds; The Headley Trust 140,719 pounds (2 grants); Garfield Weston Foundation 128,544 pounds; National Lottery Heritage Fund 78,000 pounds (3 grants); Foyle Foundation 62,000 pounds (2 grants); Wolfson Foundation 28,000 pounds (2 grants) (GrantNav).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies: the gallery says in its review "2025 in Reflection, 2026 in Sight" that it joined the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator programme in 2025, which it says "will transform our systems" to improve audience connection. The support page shows a Bloomberg Philanthropies logo, among logos of the Headley Trust, Linbury Trust, Wolfson Foundation, Ashden and DASH, with no text, date or amount.
- DASH: the gallery review says a partnership on the Future Curator's Programme launches fully in 2026.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 The New Arab (staff report) described an open letter signed by more than 260 British artists and published by Artists for Palestine UK, urging the National Theatre, Royal Shakespeare Company, Young Vic, Royal Court and Almeida to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies. The letter calls the organisation "complicit in grave war crimes" against Palestinians and cites the Bloomberg-Sagol Center at Tel Aviv University.
- The same report says Bloomberg's Regional Recovery and Repair Initiative contributed up to 27.8 million dollars to Israeli communities affected by the war in Gaza.
- The connection to the gallery: the gallery says it joined the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator programme in 2025. The letter names five London theatres, not Newlyn.

BOTTOM LINE
Newlyn is a charity funded half by Arts Council England and Cornwall Council, with grants from DCMS, trusts such as Headley, Paul Hamlyn, Garfield Weston and Wolfson, and earned income. The gallery joined the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator programme in 2025, and the May 2026 artists' letter against Bloomberg named five London theatres.
