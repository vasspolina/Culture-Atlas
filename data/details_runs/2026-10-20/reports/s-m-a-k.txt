S.M.A.K.: Ghent, Belgium
ID: s-m-a-k

OVERVIEW
- S.M.A.K. is the Municipal Museum of Contemporary Art Ghent, at Jan Hoetplein 1 in Citadelpark, opposite the Museum of Fine Arts.
- The Society for the Museum of Contemporary Art was founded on 8 November 1957 under Karel Geirlandt; the museum was founded in 1975, housed first in the Museum of Fine Arts, with Jan Hoet as director from 1975 (stated by the museum).
- It moved to a depot in a converted fair wing in 1996 and received its own building, a converted casino, renamed S.M.A.K. (Stedelijk Museum voor Actuele Kunst) in 1999 (stated by the museum).
- Philippe Van Cauteren succeeded Hoet as director in 2005 (stated by the museum).
- The building is outdated: it sometimes rains inside and the climate system failed in summer 2025; Ghent set aside 3 million euros for urgent works (VRT NWS, 9 September 2025).
- Visiting: no opening days or admission prices were readable in the pages opened; the homepage shows the museum closing at 18:00 and says it aims to be "accessible and inclusive".

FUNDING
S.M.A.K.: mainly public: City of Ghent, passing to Flanders on 1 January 2029; with corporate partners
- The City of Ghent currently pays the museum's structural operating subsidy (City of Ghent, 27 May 2026).
- Flanders takes the subsidy over: the City, Flanders and S.M.A.K. signed a cooperation agreement for a 2026 to 2028 transition and the museum becomes a Flemish museum on 1 January 2029; Ghent keeps more than 3 million euros a year and Flanders sets a 950,000 euro transition budget (City of Ghent, VRT NWS).
- Corporate tier: structural partners Stichting Willame, Jan De Nul, Interparking and Square Group commit for three years at 25,000 euros a year; the page does not say what each pays (S.M.A.K.).
- ENGIE: has supported the museum's workshops for young people with a difficult school path since 2012, run with DUO²-Ghent; no amount published (S.M.A.K., 19 December 2021).
- Belfius, the National Lottery, Christie's and Hiscox appear as logos with no tier or amount; Duvel is not named on the pages opened.
- No reporting found of an annual report, accounts or board list on the museum's site.

Red flags: ENGIE sponsorship, fossil fuels
- ENGIE funds the museum's youth workshops and the partnership won third place at the 2021 Belgian sponsorship awards (S.M.A.K.).
- No documented objection to the sponsorship was found.
- No source opened says whether ENGIE still pays in 2026.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of its funding found in art media. The criticism found is in Belgian general news and concerns the director's conduct and the museum's record on women.
1. VRT NWS: Ward Schouppe, report on complaints against director Van Cauteren (2025)
URL: https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/02/26/gent-cultuur-smak-van-cauteren-toxisch-leiderschap-ugent-kunstwe/
- Reports allegations of toxic leadership since May 2024; Ghent University's art history department put museum internships on hold and unions demanded his dismissal (translation).
- Van Cauteren declined to comment and in May 2024 filed a complaint against unknown persons; the city said it took this "very seriously".
- Not an art outlet.
2. VRT NWS: Ward Schouppe, "Geen straffen voor laster tegen SMAK-directeur in Gent" (2026)
URL: https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/17/gent-laster-eerroof-smak-philippe-van-cauteren-personeel-ophef-t/
- The Ghent chamber of indictments ruled the defamation offences time-barred; the whistleblowers again asked for an external audit (translation).
- The whistleblowers had also alleged questionable purchasing in May 2024.
3. VRT NWS: Lucas Vanclooster, report on the #smakistestil protest (2019)
URL: https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2019/10/25/protest-tegen-seksisme-en-discriminatie-van-vrouwen-in-smak-en-a/
- About 30 artists and students said S.M.A.K. had never had a female director and that the sector tolerates abuse of power (translation).
- Van Cauteren said the museum was willing to talk but regretted the "pamphlet-style" approach; he noted 30 of 50 employees are women.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mainly city money, moving to the Flemish government in 2029, with corporate partners at a published 25,000 euro tier. The weak points are the ENGIE sponsorship and the lack of any published accounts or donor amounts. Watch how the Flemish takeover and the outcome of the governance complaints affect the director and the sponsor list.
