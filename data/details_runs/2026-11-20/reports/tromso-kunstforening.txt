TROMSØ KUNSTFORENING (TROMSØ CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART): Tromsø, Norway
ID: tromso-kunstforening
Website: https://www.tromsokunstforening.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Members' association and free space for contemporary art, also named Romssa Dáiddasiida and, since its centenary, the Kven name Tromssan Taitopiiri; it calls itself the oldest art institution in northern Norway (tromsokunstforening.no; annual report 2025).
- Founded in 1924; registered as an association with a founding date of 31 December 1924 (tromsokunstforening.no; Brønnøysund register).
- Since 1981 its home has been Muségata 2 in Muséparken, a listed, municipally owned neoclassical building from 1894 built for Tromsø Museum (annual report 2025).
- Since October 2023, while Muségata 2 is renovated, it has worked from the second floor of the former Hvilhaug care home at Mellomvegen 82 (annual report 2025).
- Programme: exhibitions, public-space projects, performances and workshops, with the bookshop Mondo Books and the micro-cinema KINOBOX (tromsokunstforening.no).
- Director: Camilla Fagerli, hired by the elected board for a four-year term; chair: Kolbjørn Engeseth (tromsokunstforening.no).
- In 2025 it worked with 173 artists and held 154 events; 5,630 people saw its exhibitions and events, 14,555 counting KINOBOX and the park installations; it had 129 ordinary and 3 business members (annual report 2025).
- The centenary show "Historie er drømmen om det som kan bli" ran from 11 October to 21 December 2025 (annual report 2025).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 17:00, during exhibition periods; admission free (tromsokunstforening.no).

FUNDING
Tromsø Kunstforening: mainly public: state, county and city operating grants, plus a rent-free city building
- Fixed operating grants from the state, Troms county and Tromsø municipality came to NOK 3,789,000 in 2025, 67 per cent of income. By share of income: Ministry of Culture and Equality 33.8 per cent, Tromsø municipality 25.7 per cent, Troms county 7.9 per cent, other sources 32.7 per cent (annual report 2025).
- Tromsø municipality ended all fixed culture agreements in 2024; new four-year agreements were adopted in 2026 with the association's grant at the same level as before (annual report 2025).
- Tromsø municipality gives it premises without rent, under a fully subsidised lease dating from 1981 that is to be renegotiated before it moves back (annual report 2025).
- Other 2025 grants: Ministry of Culture and Equality NOK 250,000 for artist fees and an extra NOK 355,000 for exhibitions; VAT compensation NOK 385,000; the Directorate for Culture NOK 140,000 for the festival exhibition and NOK 100,000 for a centenary book; Fritt Ord NOK 40,000; Samfunnsløftet NOK 134,000 for outreach; Norske Kunstforeninger NOK 20,000 (annual report 2025).
- Grants awarded but not yet paid: KORO NOK 500,000 and Bildende Kunstneres Hjelpefond NOK 150,000 for the outdoor project Goahtegieddi, Finngamsletta; Kulturdirektoratet Arena and Samfunnsløftet NOK 300,000 each to fit out more space at Hvilhaug, conditional on a long-term lease (annual report 2025).
- Samfunnsløftet is SpareBank 1 Nord-Norge's community dividend strategy, launched in 2018 (Vista Analyse).
- Income was NOK 5,629,932 in 2025, with a surplus of NOK 62,675 and equity of NOK 1,527,624 (Brønnøysund accounts register; annual report 2025).
- Muségata 2 and 4 passed to Tromsø municipality in 2019, when a foundation of the city and the association, short of money for repairs, was dissolved. Renovation began in 2023 and stood still through 2025 because of the city's budget deficit; in 2026 the city began looking at shared ownership with UiT, the Arctic University of Norway (annual report 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The art press supports the association and aims its criticism at Tromsø municipality's handling of the building.
1. Hakapik: Hilde Sørstrøm and Carina E. Beddari, "Hakapik mener: Saken om Muségata 2 handler ikke bare om fasade" (2026)
URL: https://www.hakapik.no/home/2026/10/26/hakapik-mener-saken-om-musgata-2-handler-ikke-bare-om-fasade
- Editorial: the city, under state financial supervision, has not sought outside money such as the NOK 64 million that Kunstnernes Hus in Oslo got from the national culture buildings scheme, and did not send input to the government's culture arena strategy, while the association did.
- Under the city's model, the city would lease space from UiT and sublet it to the association; in the worst case the association and much of the independent art field could be pushed out.
- Backs a sale to UiT only with a binding long-term lease that gives the association enough space without UiT steering its programme.
- Names Tromsø municipality and UiT; criticises the building process, not the association.
2. Hakapik: Silvia Colombo, interview with Camilla Fagerli and Ruth Alexander Aitken (2025)
URL: https://www.hakapik.no/home/2025/12/19/100-years-of-fostering-communities-and-supporting-artists-in-troms
- Not criticism: the director and curator say membership has fallen to about 140 people and that Tromsø still lacks a Sámi art institution.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money, almost all of it public. Worth watching: the city's decision on its building, which sets where and on what terms the association can work.
