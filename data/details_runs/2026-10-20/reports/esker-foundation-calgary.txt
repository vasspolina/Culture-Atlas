ESKER FOUNDATION: Calgary, Canada
ID: esker-foundation-calgary
Website: https://eskerfoundation.com/ (checked 2026-10-20; a Cloudflare challenge blocked automated reading, so visit details come from other sources)

OVERVIEW
- Privately funded, non-commercial contemporary art gallery at 1011 9 Ave SE in the Inglewood neighbourhood of Calgary (Avenue Calgary; Travel Alberta).
- Founded in May 2012 by Calgary collectors Jim and Susan Hill, who planned it for about 15 years (CanadaHelps; Larry's List).
- The gallery fills the fourth floor of the Atlantic Avenue Art Block, a four-storey building Hill developed on land he bought on Ninth Avenue in 1998, when Inglewood was full of derelict warehouses; construction began in 2007 (Canadian Art).
- Kasian Architecture designed the gallery and Clark Builders completed it in 2012 for the client JD Hill Investments (Clark Builders).
- Three exhibitions a year in a gallery of about 15,000 square feet (Clark Builders lists 12,000), a street-level Project Space for emerging Calgary artists, the Lantern Library lounge, free workshops, talks and seminars with artists (Avenue Calgary, 2018), and no permanent collection (Canadian Art).
- Visiting: admission, educational programmes and tours are always free; Travel Alberta lists the gallery as fully accessible. No reporting checked of current opening days.

FUNDING
Esker Foundation: self-funded: founders' money from the oil-services firm Pason Systems, and rent from the building's tenants
- Jim and Susan Hill "made a fortune in the oil and gas industry" and had spent more than $80 million over 15 years by 2012, with an endowment planned (The Globe and Mail, 2012).
- Hill became CFO of Badger Drilling in 1980 and in 1987 bought its subsidiary Pason Systems (Canadian Art, 2014).
- Hill was Pason's president and CEO until 2011 and its board chair until 1 October 2020; Pason describes itself as "a leading global provider of specialized data management systems for drilling rigs" (Pason press release, 2020).
- Hill spent about $100 million on the Atlantic Avenue Art Block (Canadian Art, 2014).
- Rent from commercial tenants, including the agency Critical Mass, covers the building's running costs and Esker's programme budget (Avenue Calgary; Larry's List).
- Registered charity, business number 802236315RR0001 (CanadaHelps). No reporting found of its filed accounts, a donor list or a board; Canadian Art (2014) reported it had no board.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor or government grant.

Red flags: fossil fuels (founder money)
- The founding money comes from Jim Hill's career in oilfield services: Badger Drilling and then Pason Systems, which describes itself as a provider of data management systems for drilling rigs (Canadian Art; Pason press release, 2020).
- The Globe and Mail (2012) says the Hills "made a fortune in the oil and gas industry"; Avenue Calgary (2018) calls the gallery privately funded. No reporting found of any other source of capital.
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or boycott aimed at Esker or its founders.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the institution found in art media. The coverage found is admiring and centres on the private funding model.
1. Canadian Art: Sara Angel, "Art and Commerce Find Harmony at the Esker Foundation" (2014)
URL: https://canadianart.ca/features/esker-foundation-filling-the-void/
- Not criticism: describes the building, where leased commercial space pays the gallery's operating costs, as Hill's "new fiscal model for the cohabitation of art and commerce".
- Records that when Esker opened, people asked whether a privately funded space could be taken seriously; Potter of Esker said the question no longer came up.
- Hill says art and commerce "don't cohabit very easily" and that many wealthy Calgarians took their money to "Palm Springs, or the Bahamas, or somewhere outside of Calgary".

BOTTOM LINE
Esker runs on one family's money, made in oilfield services through Pason Systems, plus rent from the building that family owns. There is no state or corporate sponsor, but there are no published accounts and no board. Worth watching: whether the planned endowment exists and what it holds.
