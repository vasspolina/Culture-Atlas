SFMOMA: San Francisco, USA
ID: sfmoma-san-francisco
Website: https://www.sfmoma.org (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum at 151 Third Street, San Francisco, on a former parking lot (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1935 as the San Francisco Museum of Art under director Grace L. McCann Morley; it rented the fourth floor of the Veterans Building for its first sixty years, and "Modern" was added to the name in 1975 (Wikipedia).
- The Botta building, designed by Swiss architect Mario Botta, opened in January 1995. Snøhetta designed the expansion; the museum closed in 2013 and reopened on 14 May 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Since 2009 it has held the Doris and Donald Fisher collection of about 1,100 works on a 100-year loan. Gap Inc. is the source of the Fisher family wealth (Wikipedia).
- Collection of about 33,000 works and about 170,000 sq ft of exhibition space (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Monday, Tuesday and Friday to Sunday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., Thursday noon to 8 p.m., closed Wednesday. Adults $30, seniors $25, students $23; members and visitors 18 and under free (SFMOMA website).

FUNDING
SFMOMA: private non-profit: foundation grants and public agencies, plus trustee and donor gifts; no corporate sponsor list published
- Foundation and government list (updated 24 June 2025, no amounts or years): Ford, Andy Warhol, Henry Luce, Hearst, Koret, Osher, Walter & Elise Haas Fund, Evelyn and Walter Haas Jr. Fund, Robert Lehman, Frankenthaler Climate Initiative, Jay DeFeo, Japan Foundation and Fundación Jumex, among 25 named funders, plus Grants for the Arts, the National Endowment for the Arts and the Institute of Museum and Library Services, and three anonymous donors (SFMOMA website).
- The 2016 expansion cost $305 million (Studio International).
- Robert J. Fisher chairs the board (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual operating figures in the sources opened.

Red flags: Epstein-linked
- ComplexCity: in 2001, under director David A. Ross, SFMOMA took a 20% interest in John F. Simon Jr.'s ComplexCity (2000), described in a document as a "fractional and promised gift of Jeffrey Epstein" (San Francisco Standard, 5 February 2026, citing Justice Department files).
- Thank-you letter: director Neal Benezra wrote to Epstein on 17 December 2009 thanking him for his support (San Francisco Standard).
- SFMOMA's response: it has no records of Epstein support after 2001; ComplexCity was deaccessioned in October 2019, the interest returned to the artist, and the work was never exhibited (stated by SFMOMA, via the Standard). The Standard could not confirm the work's details or status.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little sustained criticism of the institution in art media. The pieces found cover the 2020 staff and former-staff protests and, in a mostly favourable review, the Fisher collection.
1. The Art Newspaper: Scarlet Cheng, "SFMoMA releases a plan for reform" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/sfmoma-releases-a-plan-for-reform
- After a 30 May Instagram post, former employee Taylor Brandon called the museum's response to the police-brutality protests hypocritical; the museum disabled her comment. The groups No Neutral Alliance and xSFMOMA called for changes in hiring, collecting and programming and for Benezra to step down.
- Five senior staff left, including chief curator Gary Garrels, who resigned after remarks about collecting white artists. The museum published a diversity plan acknowledging "longstanding inequities".
- Its own people answered through the plan; Benezra apologised publicly.
2. Studio International: Jill Spalding, review of the Snøhetta expansion (2016)
URL: https://studiointernational.com/index.php/san-francisco-museum-of-modern-art-new-extension-sf-moma-review
- Mostly a positive review. It reports that the Fisher collection has been "faulted as predominantly white, male and blue-chip", and that pushback against the contract rotating the collection back to its installation every 10 years through 2116 "has been vocal".
- Criticises the collection's profile, not the funders.
3. Mission Local (local news, not art press): Sam Lefebvre, "'Toxic donors' are coming under fire at art museums, but not in liberal San Francisco. What will SFMOMA do?" (2019)
URL: https://missionlocal.org/2019/05/toxic-donors-are-coming-under-fire-at-art-museums-but-not-in-liberal-san-francisco-what-will-sfmoma-do/
- SEC filings tie at least 13 trustees or spouses to senior roles at firms invested in military contractors or arms dealers.
- Names trustees and donors including James and Penny Coulter, George Roberts of KKR, Jim Breyer and Charles Schwab. SFMOMA declined to comment: it "will not be participating in your piece".

WIDER CONTEXT
- In June 2017 and December 2017, Neri Oxman thanked Epstein for supporting her "Vespers" death mask series and asked him to fund a further project; he replied "25k". "Vespers II" appeared in SFMOMA's 2022 show "Nature x Humanity" (San Francisco Standard). The Standard says the documents suggest rather than prove these connections.
- David A. Ross, director 1998 to 2001, resigned a department chair at the School of Visual Arts in February 2026 after his friendship with Epstein surfaced (San Francisco Standard).

BOTTOM LINE
The operating money comes mainly from named foundations and public agencies, with no amounts published. The weak point is the documented Epstein link: a 20% interest in a work he gave in 2001 and a 2009 letter thanking him for support, which SFMOMA says ended in 2001 and was reversed in 2019. Watch for any further Justice Department disclosures and for amounts behind the funder list.
