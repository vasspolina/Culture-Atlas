NATIONAL MUSEUM OF THE REPUBLIC OF KAZAKHSTAN: Astana, Kazakhstan
ID: national-museum-of-the-republic-of-kazakhstan-astana
Website: https://nmrk.kz/en/

OVERVIEW
- State history and culture museum at Tauelsizdik Avenue 54, Astana (museum website).
- Built in 2014 and designed by architect Vladimir Laptev; the design began with "the idea of a waving flag" (Wikipedia, citing QazaqCulture).
- The building has seven blocks of one to nine storeys in blue glass and white marble, with about 14,000 square metres of exhibition space across 11 halls (Wikipedia).
- Collections run from prehistory to the present; the halls include Gold, Ancient and Medieval History, Ethnography and Modern Art, and the museum houses the Issyk Golden Cataphract Warrior (Wikipedia). The museum's site calls the "Golden Man" "a symbol of Independent Kazakhstan".
- Wikipedia lists a children's museum, a scientific library with a reading room and a conference hall among its facilities.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; tickets are sold through ticketon.kz (museum website).
- Exhibition "From Athens to Astana: Olympic artifacts from the V. Potanin collection": 140 artifacts, including Olympic medals, torches and diplomas, shown with items on Kazakh athletes from the museum's own collection, as part of the cultural programme of the "Games of the Future 2026"; the museum news item dated 1 August 2026 says it runs until 30 September 2026.

FUNDING
National Museum of the Republic of Kazakhstan: state budget: Kazakh Ministry of Culture and Sports
- In 2015 the Accounts Committee found that the Ministry of Culture and Sports had allocated 514.7 million tenge to the National Museum, which is not eligible for subsidies, and called the allocation unjustified (Informburo, 3 December 2015; no author named).
- A Decree of the Head of State appointed Darkhan Mynbay as director (Wikipedia).
- The 2026 Olympic exhibition was organised jointly by the Ministry of Culture and Information, the Ministry of Tourism and Sports, the National Museum, the National Olympic Committee and the "V. Potanin Olympic Collection" project (museum news item).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Vladimir Potanin, whose collection supplied the show, was sanctioned by the UK on 29 June 2022 under the Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, with a full asset freeze (UK government). The same page reports the UK Charity Commission opened an inquiry into The Potanin Foundation that day.
- Wikipedia lists sanctions on Potanin from the United States (15 December 2022) and Canada (6 April 2022).
- The connection to the museum: the museum's news item of 1 August 2026 says the 140 objects come from Vladimir Potanin's collection and lists the "V. Potanin Olympic Collection" project among the organisers of the exhibition, which runs until 30 September 2026.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Kazakh state budget, paid through the culture ministry; the documented 2015 finding concerned 514.7 million tenge allocated to it without justification. The sanctions link is the 2026 Olympic show, which draws on Vladimir Potanin's collection and lists his collection project among its organisers; no page names him as a funder.
