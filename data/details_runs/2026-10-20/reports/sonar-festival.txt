SÓNAR FESTIVAL: Barcelona, Spain
ID: sonar-festival
Website: https://sonar.es/en (checked 2026-10-24; live, showing the 2026 edition on 18 to 20 June and the next on 17 to 19 June 2027)

OVERVIEW
- Annual electronic music, art and technology festival in Barcelona, run by Advanced Music SL (Sónar website).
- Founded in 1994 as the "Festival of Advanced Music and Multimedia Art" by Ricard Robles, Enric Palau and Sergio Caballero; the first edition ran from 2 to 4 June 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Sónar by Night moved to the Fira Gran Via area in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat in 2001, and Sónar by Day to Fira Montjuïc in 2013, when the Sónar+D congress began (Wikipedia). Attendance was about 126,000 in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of ticket prices or accessibility in the pages opened.

FUNDING
Sónar Festival: private: owned by Superstruct Entertainment, itself owned by KKR; also small public grants and brand partners
- Ownership: Superstruct Entertainment acquired Sónar in 2018, and KKR bought Superstruct in June 2024 (Mixmag, 13 October 2025).
- Sónar says it has "no direct relationship" with KKR and that "not a single euro" of festival revenue goes to KKR (Catalan News, June 2025).
- Public money: Sónar received EUR 552,245 in 2025 and EUR 717,618 in 2024, about EUR 6.6 million over 11 years (El Salto, Crític, 6 July 2026). The Generalitat gave about EUR 300,000 in 2025, up from the usual EUR 250,000; the Barcelona City Council gave EUR 230,000 to EUR 300,000 a year through the Institut de Cultura; the Catalan culture institute ICEC and the State's INAEM gave smaller grants without figures. Public aid was 5.41 percent of Sónar's income in 2023.
- Brand partners shown on the site (undated logos): Estrella Damm, Fritz-Kola, OCB, Vieta, Cutty Sark, Negrita, The Social Hub, Fujifilm and Grey Goose (Sónar website).
- No reporting found of a budget or accounts.

Red flags: Protest or boycott (artists withdrew over KKR's ownership)
- In May 2025, 70 artists who had played Sónar signed an open letter pledging not to perform and backing the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement (Wikipedia). On 17 May 2025 Spain's culture minister Ernest Urtasun said KKR is "not welcome" in Spain (Wikipedia).
- By 4 June 2025, 34 acts had withdrawn: 28 earlier, undated in the article, and six confirmed on 3 June: Arca, Rone (with (LA)HORDE), Asia, 15 15, Paquita Gordon and Shaun J. Wright (Catalan News). Wikipedia also names Juliana Huxtable.
- Unverified: activist groups, including BDS-aligned ones, say KKR's investments extend to companies that directly or indirectly support the Israeli military, and that KKR is a major shareholder in firms marketing real estate in Israeli settlements, which Amnesty International calls illegal (Catalan News). Wikipedia names Axel Springer, whose Yad2 platform lists properties in occupied Palestinian territories, Optiv and the Coastal GasLink pipeline; Mixmag adds weapons manufacturers.
- Sónar said it condemns the "genocide of the Palestinian people", has "no direct relationship" with KKR and sends "not a single euro" to it (Catalan News). Superstruct said festival proceeds stay within Superstruct (Wikipedia). The founders and the CEO stepped back in October 2025; Superstruct called it a "planned exit" agreed in 2018 (Mixmag).

BOTTOM LINE
The festival is private and owned through Superstruct by KKR, and it also takes about EUR 550,000 a year in public grants. The weak points are KKR's investments, which BDS-aligned groups contest, and the artist withdrawals of 2025; Sónar says no money goes to KKR. Watch for published accounts and for what happens to the ownership after the founders' exit.
