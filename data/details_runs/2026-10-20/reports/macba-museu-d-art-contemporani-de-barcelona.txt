MACBA (MUSEU D'ART CONTEMPORANI DE BARCELONA): Barcelona, Spain
ID: macba-museu-d-art-contemporani-de-barcelona
Website: https://www.macba.cat

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on Plaça dels Àngels in El Raval, in Barcelona's Ciutat Vella district (Wikipedia).
- Opened to the public on 28 November 1995. The MACBA Foundation was set up in 1987 under the entrepreneur Leopoldo Rodés, and the consortium that runs the museum was created in 1988 (MACBA).
- Closed Tuesday; open Saturday 10:00 to 20:00 and Sunday 10:00 to 15:00. Entry is 15 euros at the desk and 13.50 euros online; free on the first Sunday of each month, from 18:00 on Thursday, Friday and Saturday, and for under 16s. Expansion works run from January 2025 to February 2027; access for people with reduced mobility is guaranteed.

FUNDING
MACBA: mainly public: Generalitat de Catalunya, Barcelona City Council and the Spanish Ministry of Culture, with private money raised through Fundació MACBA
- The consortium has four members: the Generalitat de Catalunya, the Ajuntament de Barcelona, the Ministry of Culture (a member since 2007) and Fundació MACBA. The three public administrations fund day-to-day operations; the Foundation raises capital to build the permanent collection (MACBA).
- Fundació MACBA's page lists nine founding companies and six corporate benefactors: Fundació Damm, Grífols, Fundación Mutua Madrileña, Fundación Repsol, Fundación Telefónica and Reig Capital (MACBA, page copyright 2026).
- Fundación Repsol: the partnership began in 1998 with the permanent donation of Tàpies's "Rinzen"; since 2001, 41 works by 20 artists have been added to the collection (Fundación Repsol, 24 May 2019). MACBA's collection page says the foundation collaborates in the digital dissemination of the collection.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Fundación Repsol
- Fundación Repsol is listed as a corporate benefactor of Fundació MACBA (MACBA, page copyright 2026). It has supported the museum's Accessibility Programme since 2015, and a contract renewed in 2019 provides for 41 works to be delivered over the next 20 years (Fundación Repsol, 24 May 2019).
- The foundation's statutes name Repsol, S.A. as its founder, and its 2025 accounts record a 16,000,000 euro grant from Repsol, S.A. for 2025. Wikipedia describes Repsol as operating across the oil and gas industry, from exploration and production to refining.

Red flags: Censorship of an artwork, 2015
- In March 2015 the director, Bartomeu Marí, asked the curators to remove Ines Doujak's sculpture the day before "The Beast and the Sovereign" opened. When they refused, he cancelled the show. Three days later he reversed the decision and resigned, and he fired two of the four curators, Valentín Roma and Paul B. Preciado (Hyperallergic, Claire Voon, 12 November 2015). ArtLeaks, which published the curators' statement on 19 March 2015, also attributes the decision to the director.
- Marí said he learned of the sculpture the day before the opening (El País, cited by Hyperallergic); Doujak showed local media a loan form signed by the former director and dated 28 February, three weeks earlier (Hyperallergic).
- Unverified: some participating artists suspected that Queen Sofía's position as honorary president on MACBA's board influenced the decision, from artists cited by Hyperallergic. Marí denied any relation between his reasoning and the board's roster and said the sculpture was not consistent with the museum's usual showings.

Red flags: Protest against the museum's expansion
- Residents of the platform "No to the extension of the MACBA" and skateboarders marched through the Raval on 20 March 2024; about a hundred people took part. They oppose the museum's expansion, which residents say takes about 1,000 square metres of Plaça dels Àngels, and criticised the city council's "unilateral decision" to give the museum that space. The council presents the project as an extension of public space (El Nacional, Jordi Palmer, 20 March 2024).
- Ara reported in November 2025 that the expansion has drawn opposition from some thirty organisations (Ara, Antoni Ribas Tur, 9 November 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One art-press account, of the 2015 censorship row and its effect on CIMAM.
1. Hyperallergic: Claire Voon, "Three Resign from International Museum Committee Board, Demand New Leadership" (2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/252613/three-resign-from-international-museum-committee-board-demand-new-leadership/
- Charles Esche, Vasif Kortun and Abdellah Karroum resigned from the board of CIMAM, the international museum committee whose president was Marí. Esche said that after the censorship of Doujak's work at MACBA, CIMAM's historic commitment "was compromised by the position its president had taken in relation to Doujak's work".

BOTTOM LINE
MACBA is run by a consortium of the Generalitat de Catalunya, Barcelona City Council, the Spanish Ministry of Culture and Fundació MACBA, with the three public administrations paying for day-to-day operations. The documented private money comes through Fundació MACBA's corporate members, among them Fundación Repsol, which has supported the accessibility programme since 2015 and added works to the collection since 2001.
