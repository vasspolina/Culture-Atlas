MUDAM (MUSÉE D'ART MODERNE GRAND-DUC JEAN): Luxembourg, Luxembourg
ID: mudam-mus-e-d-art-moderne-grand-duc-jean
Website: https://www.mudam.com

OVERVIEW
- National museum of contemporary art on the Kirchberg plateau, Luxembourg City, on the site of the old Fort Thüngen (Wikipedia).
- Prime Minister Jacques Santer championed the museum in 1989; the site in Dräi Eechelen Park was agreed in 1997. Grand Duke Jean inaugurated it on 1 July 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Architect I. M. Pei designed the building, which Wikipedia puts at about 100 million dollars. The Lëtzebuerger Journal (Pascal Steinwachs and Lex Kleren, "The Mudam is no scandal-ridden place", 4 September 2026) says the project was politically contested over cost, location, the intervention in the fort, size and choice of stone, and was built much smaller than first planned.
- Bettina Steinbrügge has been director since 2022 (Lëtzebuerger Journal, 4 September 2026). The museum became a fully public institution on 1 January 2024 under a law passed in July 2023, replacing its foundation status (Paperjam).
- After the change, an external audit found structural problems and tensions among staff, and board chair Patrick Majerus resigned in January 2025 (Paperjam).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 18:00; on Wednesdays it stays open until 21:00 and admission is free from 18:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Mudam: mainly public: the Luxembourg State through the Ministry of Culture, with corporate patrons
- Paperjam's interview with director Bettina Steinbrügge ("La transformation engendre souvent des frictions") describes Mudam as "Financé à plus de 80% par l'État". Mudam received 7.3 million euros from the State for its 2019 budget (Paperjam, Céline Coubray, "Big benefactor, tax breaks behind Mudam donors group").
- Patrons: Mudam's patrons page thanks the Ministry of Culture, the members of the Cercle des collectionneurs, all donors and patrons, and "especially for their exceptional support": the Leir Foundation, M. and Mme Norbert Becker-Dennewald, Cargolux, Luxembourg High Security Hub, A&O Shearman, Clearstream and JTI. Under "as well as" it lists ArcelorMittal SA, Arendt & Medernach, Banque de Luxembourg, CA Indosuez Wealth (Europe), Elvinger Hoss, PwC Luxembourg, Swissquote Bank Europe, the Loo & Lou Foundation, ATOZ, Bank Pictet & Cie (Europe), Soludec, Dussmann Services, Les Amis des Musées d'Art et d'Histoire Luxembourg and American Friends of Mudam.
- JTI (Japan Tobacco International), a tobacco company headquartered in Geneva (Tobacco Tactics), appears only as a name in that list, with no year or amount beside it: a listing, not a documented funding line.
- Clearstream, the post-trade business of Deutsche Börse Group, launched a partnership with Mudam on 13 March 2024 for an initial three years (Clearstream press release, "Mudam – Musée d'Art Moderne Grand-Duc Jean and Clearstream launch partnership to promote contemporary art in Luxembourg").
- Degroof Petercam Luxembourg became a patron in 2021, backed the Kentridge (2021), Yiadom-Boakye (2022) and Halley (2023) exhibitions, and renewed the partnership for three years (the bank's press release, 28 March 2023).
- Banque de Luxembourg supported the project from the beginning (Paperjam, "The road to Eldorado").
- American Friends of Mudam is a US charity that lets American donors claim tax deductions; it grew from collector Raymond Learsy's wish to donate works to Mudam (Paperjam, Céline Coubray).

BOTTOM LINE
Mudam is a state museum: the Luxembourg State pays more than 80 percent of its budget through the Ministry of Culture. Private money comes from companies and banks, including Clearstream (partnership from March 2024) and Degroof Petercam (patron since 2021). JTI and ArcelorMittal are names on the patrons page with no year or amount, which makes them listings, not documented funding.
