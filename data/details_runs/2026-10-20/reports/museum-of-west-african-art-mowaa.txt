MUSEUM OF WEST AFRICAN ART (MOWAA): Benin City, Nigeria
ID: museum-of-west-african-art-mowaa
Website: https://wearemowaa.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads; says visits are by appointment only)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural campus in Benin City, within the ancient moats of the Benin Kingdom (The Art Newspaper, Catherine Hickley, 1 November 2024).
- Designs by Adjaye Associates for the Edo Museum of West African Art were revealed in November 2020 (Wikipedia); "Edo" was dropped after a 2023 federal ruling that the Benin Bronzes belong to the Oba of Benin (Museums Association). Phillip Ihenacho is executive director and chair.
- The first building is about 4,000 square metres of rammed earth, with an auditorium, conservation lab, library, exhibition space and amphitheatre (The Art Newspaper). NAN links the land to the former Central Hospital.
- The public opening was planned for 11 November 2025 (Wikipedia). After the 9 November preview was disrupted, the museum closed to the public indefinitely and carried on with research and archaeology (Apollo, 15 December 2025).
- Visiting: by appointment only (museum site).

FUNDING
MOWAA: mixed: governments, foundations and Nigerian corporate and individual donors; ownership disputed
- Founding partners (support page, undated): Edo State Government, Germany's Federal Foreign Office, Mellon Foundation, PI Trust, A. G. Leventis Foundation, British Museum.
- About $20 million raised by November 2024 from the Edo state government, the German and Nigerian governments, Gerda Henkel Foundation, GIZ, Open Society Foundation and Ford Foundation, towards a campus expected to cost around $100 million; Mellon granted $3 million for training (The Art Newspaper). Apollo (15 December 2025) says MOWAA has attracted more than $25 million in total.
- A committee chaired by Adams Oshiomhole, set up by Governor Okpebholo in November 2025, reported in August 2026 that the Obaseki administration spent N3.8 billion, and listed donations of €4 million from Germany and $1.5 million from Leventis (ThisDay, 22 August 2026); NAN says receipt is not confirmed. MOWAA management said it received more than $18 million for land and excavation; the Edo Accountant-General said the treasury knew of no international donor income, and the committee called the claim false (NAN, 11 September 2026).
- No reporting found of audited accounts. The museum names donors by tier without years.

Red flags: fossil fuel donors
- The museum's support page lists NNPC Seplat Energy, Lekoil Nigeria Limited and Azura Power West Africa Limited among its Leadership Circle donors. The page carries no date.
- The page gives tier prices only (Leader $100,000, Senior Leader $500,000, or a quarter of each per year over four years) and does not say which tier any donor is in. No gift amounts or years found for these three.

Red flags: protest and dispute with the Oba of Benin
- Two days before the preview, the Oba asked Governor Monday Okpebholo to stop the opening, calling MOWAA "fraught with greed, deceit, mischief and lack of transparency". On 9 November 2025 some 40 protesters singing their allegiance to Oba Ewuare II stormed the complex during the preview (Apollo).
- 10 November 2025: Museums Association reports that Governor Okpebholo revoked the land's Certificate of Occupancy, citing "overriding public interest"; Apollo reports he later told Germany's ambassador this was a "social media scam" and nothing had been revoked.
- 22 December 2025: the Edo House of Assembly urged the state to take over ownership and control. 20 August 2026: the Oshiomhole committee recommended renaming the museum the Benin Royal Museum of African Art and returning it to the Oba to manage (NAN).
- Unverified: a relative of the Oba said MOWAA "deceived the Europeans", from Apollo; he is on one side of the dispute. MOWAA denies any illegality in its land acquisition (Apollo) and says it never presented itself as the Benin Royal Museum to secure funding (Museums Association).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage concerns the political and ownership row, not the art or architecture. Apollo says Germany "still seems committed to MOWAA".
1. Apollo: Barnaby Phillips, "What has gone wrong at the Museum of West African Art?" (15 December 2025)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=1036066
- The crisis grew from a long rivalry between the Oba's circle and the museum's backers; an independent trust reassured European institutions but suggested to the Oba that he was losing control.
2. Museums Association (Museums Journal): Geraldine Kendall Adams, "Political row delays opening of Nigeria's Museum of West African Art" (2025)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2025/11/political-row-delays-opening-of-nigerias-museum-of-west-african-art/
- News report: some of the public and Edo's traditional rulers felt a local project had been "hijacked" when it was rebranded.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a mix of European and Nigerian state funds, large foundations and named Nigerian donors, including three energy companies. The weak points are the disputed ownership and land, the Edo committee's figures against management's claim of more than $18 million received, and the lack of published accounts. Worth watching: the Edo white paper on the Oshiomhole report.
