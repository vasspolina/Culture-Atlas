ASHKAL ALWAN: Beirut, Lebanon
ID: ashkal-alwan-beirut
Website: https://ashkalalwan.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit for the plastic arts in Beirut, registered with Lebanon's Ministry of Interior and Municipalities under number 249/AD (stated on its site). Also known as the Lebanese Association for Plastic Arts.
- Founded in 1994 by Christine Tohmé, Marwan Rechmaoui, Rania Tabbara, Mustapha Yamout and Leila Mroueh, after the Lebanese war (Wikipedia). The Sigrid Rausing Trust gives 1993, and Tohmé's 2012 Ibraaz interview mentions both years. Tohmé is its director.
- Name means "forms and colours" in Arabic (Wikipedia).
- Home Workspace opened in 2011 in a former industrial building next to the Beirut Art Center (Wikipedia). Tohmé told Ibraaz in 2012 that the space was provided by the Philippe Jabre Association and that Youssef Tohmé (no relation) designed the refurbishment.
- Programmes: the Home Works Forum (launched 2002, held every three years per the Warhol Foundation), the Home Workspace Program (a ten-month, tuition-free course for 10 to 15 international fellows with a stipend, per e-flux), video production grants, a residency, a publishing initiative and a library.
- Currently: an exhibition, "Afteruse" by Ali Merhi, from 16 October 2026 to 15 January 2027.
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening hours, an address or admission on its site.

FUNDING
Ashkal Alwan: grant-funded: foreign foundations and a Norwegian ministry, plus private donors
- Major benefactors listed on its supporters page (undated): the Robert A. Matta Arts & Culture Association, the Foundation for Arts Initiatives, the Andy Warhol Foundation, Norway (Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs) and the Teiger Foundation (linked to Home Works X). Patron: Sfeir Semler Gallery.
- Andy Warhol Foundation: $100,000 multi-year program support in the fall 2018 grant cycle.
- Sigrid Rausing Trust: supports it from 2022 to 2026 under its Open Societies programme; its page lists £320,000 in previous grants.
- Tohmé thanked "the Lebanese community who committed themselves to be donors" (Ibraaz, 2012).
- Honorary board members named on the supporters page: Zaza Jabre, Robert A. Matta, Carla Chammas and Hoor Al Qasimi.
- A 2015 annual report PDF is hosted on its site, but its text could not be read. No reporting found of accounts, total budget or trustees on the pages opened.
- No reporting found of Lebanese state funding, or of Sharjah Art Foundation funding.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the institution. The coverage found is sympathetic, and the one critical thread concerns the format of its school.
1. e-flux Education: Hicham Awad, "Navigating Volatility: Home Workspace Program at Ashkal Alwan" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/education/features/6732768/navigating-volatility-home-workspace-program-at-ashkal-alwan
- The "emerging artist" selection criterion is unstable, and mixing practitioners of different training caused discomfort.
- Open studios were "perhaps the program's most contested tradition": critics objected to their conventional exhibition format, and audience members quoted from an Artforum piece called the 2012-13 X-Apartments project "poverty tourism"; Awad reports this and does not endorse it.
- Opens by asking how an experimental programme can evolve without replicating what it was designed to circumvent. Does not discuss funders.
2. Hyperallergic: Hrag Vartanian, "Lebanese Art Community Joins Unprecedented Protests" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/524105/lebanese-art-community-joins-unprecedented-protests/
- Not criticism: reports that Ashkal Alwan postponed Home Works indefinitely on 20 October 2019, three days into the protests, and quotes its statement "Today, it is our turn."
- Says nothing about its funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Ashkal Alwan is arts foundations and a Norwegian ministry, with named benefactors and no flagged industry or state body found. The weak point is transparency: the supporters page is undated, no amounts or accounts are published on the pages opened, and the donor behind the Home Workspace building is named only in an interview. Worth watching for current funder amounts.
