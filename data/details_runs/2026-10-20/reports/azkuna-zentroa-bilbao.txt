AZKUNA ZENTROA (ALHÓNDIGA BILBAO): Bilbao, Spain
ID: azkuna-zentroa-bilbao
Website: https://www.azkunazentroa.eus

OVERVIEW
- Culture and leisure centre in a former wine market in Bilbao, converted by the French designer Philippe Starck (Wikipedia).
- The original building, the Alhóndiga, was designed by the Basque architect Ricardo Bastida and inaugurated in 1909 (Wikipedia). A new warehouse was planned in the 1970s and the building was abandoned; proposals including public housing and a modern art museum were dropped, and in 1994 the decision was made to convert it into a sports and culture centre (Wikipedia).
- Starck, working with Thibaut Mathieu, designed the conversion (English Wikipedia). Demolition of the interior began in May 2001 and the centre opened on 18 May 2010 (Spanish Wikipedia); the English edition says it opened in stages between 18 May and 24 October 2010. Bilbao Ría 2000 managed the works (Bilbao Ría 2000).
- Named Alhóndiga Bilbao until March 2015, when it was renamed for Iñaki Azkuna, the late mayor of Bilbao (Wikipedia).
- Building: three brick-lined cubes hung over 43 different columns (Spanish Wikipedia); the Guggenheim Bilbao site says Starck designed the 43 columns.
- On site: a multimedia library (Mediateka), exhibition room, auditorium, cinemas, multi-purpose hall, a swimming pool and physical activity centre, restaurants, cafeterias and a shop (Guggenheim Bilbao). Bilbao Ría 2000 gives the surface as 40,000 m2 with an underground car park of 987 spaces.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 7 pm (Guggenheim Bilbao's visitor page).

FUNDING
Azkuna Zentroa: mainly public: the Bilbao city council
- Wikipedia (English) gives the conversion cost as 71 million euros; the Spanish edition cites an investment of 75 million euros. Bilbao Ría 2000, the public regeneration company, managed the works and contributed 6 million euros from its Abandoibarra action.
- The centre is run under the Bilbao city council: its director Fernando Pérez, in post from 2 May 2018, submitted his resignation to the mayor Juan Mari Aburto on 12 December 2024 (Orain, Onda Vasca).
- Onda Vasca's headline of 13 December 2024 attributes the resignation to "disagreements with the city council" (translation); in the article Pérez thanks the council and says he is proud of the centre he leaves, and Orain quotes him saying he is "muy orgulloso" of its programming.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is public: the City of Bilbao and, for the building works, Bilbao Ría 2000. No flag applies.
