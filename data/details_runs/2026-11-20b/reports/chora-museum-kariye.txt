CHORA MUSEUM (KARIYE): Istanbul, Turkey
ID: chora-museum-kariye
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Former Byzantine monastery church of St Saviour in Chora in the Edirnekapı neighbourhood of Fatih, Istanbul, outside the Theodosian walls; since 2020 it is the Kariye Mosque (Daily Sabah; Bianet).
- Built as a monastery in 534; most of the present building dates from 1077 to 1081, and Theodore Metochites rebuilt and decorated it between 1316 and 1321 (Bianet; Daily Sabah).
- Made a mosque in 1511 under Sultan Bayezid II's grand vizier Atık Ali Paşa; a museum by a Council of Ministers decree in 1945, opened to the public in 1958 (Bianet).
- What is there: the city's most extensive cycle of 14th-century Christian mosaics and frescoes, including Christ in Majesty and the Anastasis fresco (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- After restoration it reopened on 6 May 2024 as a mosque that also takes visitors; Erdoğan opened it by video link from Ankara (The Art Newspaper; Haber7, 19 August 2024).
- Visiting: since 19 August 2024 foreign tourists pay 20 euros; Turkish citizens and Muslims coming to pray enter free; on Fridays it is open for worship only, and visits stop 15 minutes before each prayer (Haber7, 19 August 2024).

FUNDING
Chora (Kariye): state: owned by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's General Directorate of Foundations, used for worship under the Presidency of Religious Affairs
- Owner: the General Directorate of Foundations (Vakıflar Genel Müdürlüğü) of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism (Daily Sabah, 7 May 2024).
- The August 2020 decree gave control of worship to the state Religious Affairs Directorate (The Art Newspaper, 2020; Wikipedia).
- Restoration: the General Directorate of Foundations carried out the four-year restoration completed in 2024 (Haber7); The Art Newspaper dates the programme to 2013 and a committee member to 2012. No cost for Chora was published.
- Income: ticket sales to foreign tourists at 20 euros, plus headphones at 5 euros and body covers and scarves sold at the ticket booth (Haber7).
- History: Thomas Whittemore and Paul A. Underwood of the Byzantine Institute of America and the Dumbarton Oaks Center for Byzantine Studies sponsored the restoration programme begun in 1948 (Bianet).

Red flags: conversion to a mosque, decreed in 2020 and carried out in May 2024
- On 11 November 2019 the Council of State ruled the building's allocation as a museum "contrary to law"; a presidential decree opening it to worship was published on 21 August 2020 (Bianet).
- Three images of the Virgin Mary and Christ in the nave, now the men's prayer hall, are covered with curtains during prayers (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- In May 2024 Greek prime minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said he would raise his "strong dissatisfaction" with Erdoğan, and the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of New York urged Turkey to reconsider (The Art Newspaper, 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Steady criticism of the conversion since 2019, on the mosaics' safety and the politics of the decision.
1. The Art Newspaper: Ayla Jean Yackley, "Turkey reopens former Byzantine Chora church as a mosque amid muted objections" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/10/turkey-reopens-chora-museum-as-a-mosque-amid-muted-objections
- Most mosaics stayed visible, but the heritage expert Tuğba Tanyeri-Erdemir says that once museum status ends the conservation structures go and unmonitored visitors speed decay.
- Places it in the government's campaign to turn former Byzantine churches back into mosques.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "First Hagia Sophia, now Turkey's 'Sistine Chapel of Byzantium' will be turned back into mosque" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/08/24/first-hagia-sophia-now-turkeys-sistine-chapel-of-byzantium-will-be-turned-back-into-mosque
- Quotes Robert G. Ousterhout calling the decision a "blatant attempt to erase Istanbul's rich Byzantine heritage" and "a purely political manoeuvre".
- Warns the narthexes and funeral chapel would have to be veiled or closed during services.
3. Apollo: Merih Danalı Cantarella and Anthony Cutler, "The Kariye Museum in Istanbul: a Byzantine masterpiece under threat" (2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/kariye-museum-istanbul-byzantine-cultural-heritage/
- Calls the court decision populist, aimed at the AKP's pious, nationalist base, and part of its neo-Ottomanist cultural policy.
- Warns that shutters or panels over the mosaics could trap humidity and damage the wall surfaces.

WIDER CONTEXT
- An association filed suit against the museum status in 2005 (Bianet).
- In 2020 Erdoğan dropped plans to pray in person at Chora after UNESCO warned that unilateral changes to Chora and Hagia Sophia could affect their World Heritage standing (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- Erdoğan's 2020 prayers at Hagia Sophia drew an outcry from Russia, the EU, the US, Pope Francis and Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew; the Chora decree followed Hagia Sophia's by a month (The Art Newspaper, 2020 and 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
Turkish state money and control throughout; there is no private funder. The issue is the state's own decision to end its museum status, which foreign governments and heritage specialists opposed.
