DES MOINES ART CENTER: Des Moines, USA
ID: des-moines-art-center
Website: https://desmoinesartcenter.org/

OVERVIEW
- Museum, art school and sculpture park in Des Moines, Iowa; the museum is at 4700 Grand Avenue in Greenwood Park (stated by the space).
- The Art Center traces its roots to 1916, when the Des Moines Association of Fine Arts opened gallery space in the Public Library of Des Moines; it became the Des Moines Art Center in 1945 and the museum opened in 1948 (Wikipedia).
- Planning for a permanent building began in 1943 after a sizeable donation from the trust of James D. Edmundson (Wikipedia).
- The main campus has three buildings designed by Eliel Saarinen, I. M. Pei and Richard Meier (stated by the space); Wikipedia dates them 1948, 1968 and 1985.
- The John and Mary Pappajohn Sculpture Park opened in 2009 in Western Gateway Park (stated by the space).
- Visiting: admission to the galleries is always free (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Des Moines Art Center: private nonprofit funded by contributions and investment income
- The Art Center is operated by Edmundson Art Foundation Inc. For 2024 it reported revenue of 9,381,811 dollars: contributions 4,536,649 dollars (48.4%), investment income 1,852,058 dollars (19.7%) and other revenue 2,504,582 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 data).
- The Art Center says Annual Fund gifts "provide essential operating support" (stated by the space).
- Greenwood Pond: Double Site by Mary Miss, installed in 1996, cost the museum nearly 1 million dollars in maintenance since it was unveiled, by the museum's account; the museum estimated at least 2.6 million dollars to stabilise and restore it, a figure Miss disputed (The Art Newspaper).
- In the settlement announced on 14 January 2025 the Art Center agreed to pay Miss 900,000 dollars and to remove the work (Iowa Public Radio, Josie Fischels, 14 January 2025). Miss pledged the money to a Public Art Advocacy Fund set up by The Cultural Landscape Foundation (ArtReview).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Both pieces concern the museum's care of a commissioned artwork, and both quote Charles A. Birnbaum of The Cultural Landscape Foundation.
1. The Art Newspaper: Benjamin Sutton, "Mary Miss and Des Moines Art Center settle lawsuit over Land art piece's demolition" (14 January 2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/01/14/mary-miss-des-moines-art-center-lawsuit-settlement-land-art-demolition
- Charles A. Birnbaum, president of The Cultural Landscape Foundation, said the institution "appears to have failed as a proper custodian and steward" of the work and that the outcome "could have and should have been prevented".
- Maxwell Anderson said the settlement "isn't an ideal outcome, but it is the best that could be achieved", and that long-term preservation "cannot be an afterthought".
- The museum claimed it could only afford to tear the work down; it was contractually blocked from demolishing it without Miss's consent.
2. ArtReview: "Mary Miss and Des Moines Art Center settle lawsuit" (15 January 2025, no byline)
URL: https://artreview.com/mary-miss-and-des-moines-art-center-settle-lawsuit/
- Birnbaum, president and CEO of The Cultural Landscape Foundation, said the sculpture's loss "could have and should have been prevented".
- On 3 May 2024 Judge Stephen H. Locher ruled the installation could not be demolished without the artist's consent, and found the museum's decision not to maintain it reasonable under a contract giving it "unilateral discretion" over repairs.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Art Center is contributions and investment income, with a founding gift from the Edmundson trust. The documented controversy is the Greenwood Pond: Double Site dispute, which ended in January 2025 with the Art Center agreeing to pay the artist 900,000 dollars and remove the work.
