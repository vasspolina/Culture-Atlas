OWENS ART GALLERY: Sackville, Canada
ID: owens-art-gallery-sackville
Website: http://mta.ca/owens/

OVERVIEW
- University art gallery at 61 York Street, Sackville, New Brunswick, part of Mount Allison University (Owens Art Gallery website).
- Opened on 28 May 1895 to house the collection of the Owens Art Institution, which had been established in Saint John, New Brunswick, in 1884; about 400 paintings and 100 plaster casts moved to Sackville (Mount Allison library guide). CTV News (Alana Pickrell, 17 May 2025) calls it the oldest university art gallery in Canada.
- Designed by Edmund Burke in the Beaux-Arts style, with a light olive sandstone facade, stone and brick, and terra-cotta friezes on three sides. The 1972 renovation by Gustavo Da Roza & Brown, Brisley & Brown added an expanded entryway at the rear and replaced the atrium skylights with upstairs gallery space (Mount Allison library guide).
- Built with instruction space for Mount Allison Ladies' College; instruction moved out of the Owens in 1965 (Mount Allison library guide).
- Holds 4,061 works, valued at 31,413,086 Canadian dollars, and supports student internships and course visits (2023-24 annual report).
- Open weekdays 10:00 to 17:00 and weekends 13:00 to 17:00, closed on holidays; admission is free (Owens Art Gallery website).

FUNDING
Owens Art Gallery: mainly public: Canada Council for the Arts, the New Brunswick government and Mount Allison University
- Public sector grants in 2023-24 (annual report): Canada Council for the Arts, 180,000 Canadian dollars (Engage and Sustain, Artistic Catalysts); New Brunswick Department of Tourism, Heritage and Culture, 52,000 (Core Support Program) and 6,900 (Strategic Initiatives Fund, Arts Wellness Component); Town of Sackville, 1,000 (operational grant).
- Mount Allison University hosts the gallery, which takes donations through the university's giving page (Owens Art Gallery website).
- Internship donors above 1,000 dollars in 2023-24 (annual report): Heather and Ian Bourne (Colville House Summer Internships), the Jean and Peter Flemington Trust (Art Conservation Lab Internships) and the J.E.A. Crake Foundation (ArtsWork Internships).
- The J.E.A. Crake Foundation is a Sackville charity set up by Dr. John Ernest Alexander Crake, a Mount Allison classics professor from 1946 to 1976, which the Mount Allison Record (winter 2019 issue) says has given more than 5 million dollars to arts and humanities at the university.
- The Friends of the Owens is the gallery's giving programme; its money goes to education, outreach and mentorship (Owens Art Gallery website).
- The annual fundraiser "The Sweetest Little Thing" is held with Struts Gallery, with works donated by artists (annual report).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Owens is public grants from the Canada Council, New Brunswick and the Town of Sackville, with the gallery hosted by Mount Allison University and donations above 1,000 dollars for student internships from the Bournes, the Flemington Trust and the J.E.A. Crake Foundation.
