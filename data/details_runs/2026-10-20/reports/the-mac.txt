THE MAC: Belfast, UK
ID: the-mac
Website: https://themaclive.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre with galleries, theatres and a cafe bar at 10 Exchange Street West, in Belfast's Cathedral Quarter, opposite St Anne's Cathedral (MAC website; Wikipedia).
- It is the successor to the Old Museum Arts Centre, which opened in 1989 and closed at the end of January 2010; the Metropolitan Arts Centre (the MAC) opened on 20 April 2012 (Wikipedia). It is a registered charity, NIC100808.
- Building: designed by the Belfast firm Hackett Hall McKnight, completed in February 2012, with intersecting brick volumes and a basalt tower topped by glazing. It won a 2013 RIBA National Award and the 2014 Downes Medal (Wikipedia). The capital development cost £18m (Museums Association).
- On site: art exhibitions, theatre, dance, comedy, a family programme, a cafe bar, creative learning and venue hire. Its one permanent artwork is "The Permanent Present" by Mark Garry (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday from 9am, galleries 11am to 5pm; the page lists no admission prices and links to a separate accessibility page. Wikipedia says the old centre's limited wheelchair access was a factor in the decision to build anew.

FUNDING
The MAC: mainly public: Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Belfast City Council, plus foundations and trading income
- Income for the year to 31 March 2025 was £2,986,762 against expenditure of £3,302,232, with 108 employees (Open Charities). The accounts show a net deficit of £315,470, mainly from restricted capital depreciation, and an operating surplus of £10,621.
- Public funding in 2025 from the two funders the trustees call core funders: Arts Council of Northern Ireland £1,100,500 (restricted funds) and Belfast City Council £120,000 (unrestricted) (2025 accounts, Note 5). The accounts do not label the £1,100,500 as core, programme or capital funding.
- Other 2025 grants: Freelands Foundation £125,815, Art Fund Weston Loan Programme £30,000, Dormant Accounts £28,500, Cultural Bridge £19,500; Henry Moore Foundation £4,000, Creative Ireland £5,963 and the Public Health Agency £5,000 (2025 accounts, Note 5).
- Trading: £1,365,947 from commercial trading, £24,150 from sponsorship (sponsors are not named in the accounts), £34,772 rental, £824,421 in ticket sales, and £100,105 theatre, museum and gallery tax relief.
- Capital development cost: £18m (Museums Association, 2018). The supporters page has a "Capital Funders" heading with logos only; the image file names suggest Belfast City Council and the Department of Communities, but the page names no one in text.
- Going concern (year to 31 March 2025): the auditor's opinion is not modified, but its report has a section headed "Material uncertainty related to going concern". It says the conditions explained in note 2 "indicate the existence of a material uncertainty which may cast significant doubt" on the ability to continue. Note 2 says the MAC relies on the Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Belfast City Council, that funding from both is guaranteed for the next financial year, and that it rests on single-year commitments. Unrestricted reserves were £166,958 against a £750,000 target (2025 accounts).
- Past rescue funding: in the 2017-18 year the Department for Communities, through the Arts Council, gave £275,000 and Belfast City Council £25,000 after a £40,000 council payment in October (Museums Association, 9 January 2018).
- Chair: Raymond Hutchinson (2025 accounts). Eleven trustees are listed on Open Charities.
- No reporting found naming the sponsors behind the £24,150 of sponsorship income; the supporters page shows logos without names.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the MAC's programme or governance found in art media. Press coverage found concerns its repeated funding gaps.
1. Apollo: unsigned news item, "Belfast's MAC seeks emergency funding" (20 December 2017)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/belfasts-mac-seeks-emergency-funding/
- Reports the MAC asked for an extra £300,000 from the council and the Arts Council before the end of 2017-18, plus £270,000 for 2018-19, citing "urgent cash flow problems".
- Reports that closure was a risk if the requests were not met, and that the MAC had already received £40,000 from Belfast City Council in October.
- DUP councillor Graham Craig said: "All the parties that are involved in this have to come to the table and provide the finances necessary."
- Not criticism of the art or the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the MAC is public funding from the Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Belfast City Council, with foundation grants and its own trading. The weak point is financial: the auditor's report on the 2025 accounts has a material uncertainty section on going concern, and unrestricted reserves stand at £166,958 against a £750,000 target. Worth watching: whether the single-year guarantees from its two core funders are renewed.
