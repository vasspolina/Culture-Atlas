NATIONAL MUSEUM OF CHINA: Beijing, China
ID: national-museum-of-china
Website: https://www.chnmuseum.cn (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The state's museum of history and art on the east side of Tiananmen Square, where the leadership built the Museum of the Chinese Revolution and the Museum of Chinese History after 1949; the Party centre merged the two in February 2003 (museum website).
- Both predecessor museums were in the Central Propaganda Department's first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases in July 1997 (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- A German architectural firm directed the rebuilding (Time), completed in 2012 by the museum's account with nearly 200,000 square metres of floor space and 48 halls; the collection holds more than 1.43 million items (museum website).
- Its core displays are "Ancient China" and "The Road to Rejuvenation" (museum's 2024 data report); visiting the latter on 29 November 2012 with the Politburo Standing Committee, Xi Jinping said national rejuvenation was the greatest dream of the Chinese nation (Xinhua, state media).
- In 2024 it was open 317 days, received 6,956,830 visits and staged 58 exhibitions, including six from abroad such as a Kremlin Museums show (museum's 2024 data report).
- Visiting: free with real-name booking up to seven days ahead on the museum's website or WeChat; open 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:00, extended to 17:30 from 1 June to 31 October, closed Mondays except public holidays; over-60s and disabled visitors use a priority entrance (museum booking rules, from 1 August 2025).

FUNDING
National Museum of China: state-funded: Ministry of Culture and Tourism
- A second-level budget unit of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism; the ministry's museum budget line pays staff, running costs, programmes and building work at the Palace Museum and the National Museum (ministry's 2025 final accounts).
- Time put the cost of the refurbishment at 380 million US dollars (2011).
- In 2024 it signed 14 strategic cooperation agreements with 11 partners, including the Supreme People's Procuratorate, the Shenzhen city government and the technology company Tencent (museum's 2024 data report).
- It licenses its name and collection to consumer brands, among them Balabala, with which it developed 12 children's New Year outfits, Lengsuanling and Wuyutai; these are clients, not funders (museum's 2024 data report).

Red flags: 2024 agreements with Qatar Museums and the Saudi Museums Commission
- In 2024 the museum signed ten cooperation documents with museums and cultural authorities abroad, among them the Qatar Museums Authority, the Tokyo National Museum, the Saudi Museums Commission and the Moscow Kremlin Museums (museum's 2024 data report).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is of the museum's state narrative, chiefly in a 2011 Time report and in academic work on Chinese museums.
1. Time: Hannah Beech, "Party Piece" (2011)
URL: https://time.com/archive/6595687/party-piece/
- Calls the renovated museum impressive in size and hardware but "sanitized, pro-government" in its narrative.
- Notes that "The Road of Rejuvenation" covers the Cultural Revolution with one photograph, makes no direct mention of the Great Leap Forward and omits the 1989 Tiananmen democracy movement; David Clarke of the University of Hong Kong calls its ideological weight "crushing".
- Reports that a museum employee said she would not be let in unless she wrote something positive, and that Ai Weiwei, once a consultant on the renovation, was detained less than a day after the German foreign minister opened the "Art of Enlightenment" show.
2. MCLC Resource Center: Joseph R. Allen, review of Kirk A. Denton, "Exhibiting the Past" (2015)
URL: https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/allen
- Allen writes that official ideology has openly driven museology throughout the history of the People's Republic, and summarises Denton's finding that the revolution museum had to "whitewash" the upheavals of the Mao era.
- Notes Denton's account of the 2011 reopening, when "The Road to Revival" and the Enlightenment show were followed by displays of Louis Vuitton luggage and Bvlgari jewellery.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Chinese public money from the culture ministry and carries no flag of its own. The weak points are its role as the state's display of Party history and its agreements with Qatari and Saudi museum bodies. Worth watching: what those agreements commit the museum to.
