UZBEKISTAN ART AND CULTURE DEVELOPMENT FOUNDATION: Tashkent, Uzbekistan
ID: uzbekistan-art-and-culture-development-foundation-tashkent
Website: https://acdf.uz/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the page carries mostly news headlines and no funding or board information)

OVERVIEW
- State cultural body in Tashkent that commissions the Bukhara Biennial and runs a wider programme of heritage restoration, architecture, education and exhibitions.
- Founded by presidential decree in 2017 (The Spectator Australia). Its site calls it the "Foundation for the Development of Culture and Art under the Administration of President of the Republic of Uzbekistan".
- Chaired by Gayane Umerova, who is also commissioner of the Bukhara Biennial. The Korea Herald names her and Saida Mirziyoyeva, head of the presidential administration and eldest daughter of the president, as central figures in shaping "Uzbekistan's cultural landscape".
- Programmes listed by e-flux: the Aral Culture Summit (2025), the Center for Contemporary Arts in Tashkent and a new National Museum designed by Tadao Ando.
- Bukhara Biennial: first edition from 5 September to 23 November 2025 (biennial site; e-flux, Arab News and Times of Central Asia give 20 November), theme "Recipes for Broken Hearts". The biennial site reports 1.8 million visitors; Pin-Up says the count includes people who crossed the free urban zone.
- Visiting: the foundation has no single venue, and no admission details were found.

FUNDING
Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation: state-funded: a presidential foundation, with Saudi partners on part of the biennial
- Hyperallergic describes it as state-funded; Pin-Up calls it a "deep-pocketed state body". No budget, accounts or donor list was found.
- The foundation produced all 2025 commissions (Bukhara Biennial site).
- AlMusalla Prize display, 5 September to 20 November 2025: Saudi companies ACWA Power and Vision Invest made it possible, and the Diriyah Biennale Foundation organised the prize (e-flux). No amount is stated.
- ACWA Power also supported the "Central Asian Women" panel on 6 September 2025 (Times of Central Asia).
- Timur Karpov, quoted by New Eastern Europe, calls the spending "exorbitant by Uzbek standards" (his claim; no figure).
- No reporting found of Bukhara Oil as a biennial partner: the biennial site and Times of Central Asia name no partners.

Red flags: Gulf state partner
- Diriyah Biennale Foundation: it organised the AlMusalla Prize shown in Bukhara. On 4 November 2025 Saudi culture minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah bin Farhan and Umerova welcomed the collaboration between the foundation and the Bukhara Biennale (Arab News).
- ACWA Power and Vision Invest: the Saudi companies that made the display possible (e-flux). On 8 September 2025 ACWA Power's chairman said Uzbekistan is its largest market outside Saudi Arabia (idrawater.org).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Several pieces argue the biennial is state image-building; one calls that charge weak because the audience was local. Little criticism of the foundation as an organisation.
1. Hyperallergic: Nevdon Jamgochian, "The Bukhara Biennial Will Break Your Heart" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1052634/the-bukhara-biennial-will-break-your-heart
- Calls the biennial "a part of this system" of state image-building, compares it to Azerbaijan's YARAT Center and Desert X AlUla, and says "Its very existence depends on a restrictive autocratic structure."
- Says the biennial did not answer its questions about government involvement.
- Praises the art as "important, valid, and validating".
2. New Eastern Europe: Anna Gzubicka, "Bukhara delights with art, while the authorities count political dividends" (2026)
URL: https://neweasterneurope.eu/2026/02/20/bukhara-delights-with-art-while-the-authorities-count-political-dividends/
- Frames the event as artwashing; cites Uzbekistan's 148th place in the 2025 Reporters Without Borders index and Karpov: the biennale is "exclusively about international PR and artwashing".
- Says the artworks were "very loosely related to the theme, if at all".
3. The Spectator Australia: Alex Diggins, "Am I a useful idiot visiting Uzbekistan's first art biennial?" (2026)
URL: https://www.spectator.com.au/2026/01/am-i-a-useful-idiot-visiting-uzbekistans-first-art-biennial/
- Trip sponsored by the foundation. An artist calls the project "icky" and says it "feels like it's for the government, not the people"; Umerova answers "It's not about trying to change international perceptions."
4. PIN-UP: Andrew Pasquier, "STATECRAFT" (undated on the page)
URL: https://www.pinupmagazine.org/articles/bukhara-biennial-uzbekistan-architecture-art-and-culture-foundation
- Calls the biennial "soft power" but argues the artwashing charge is weaker because the audience was local. The author was hosted by the foundation.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hyperallergic (27 October 2025) cites Amnesty International on widespread censorship in Uzbekistan and says the 2022 Karakalpakstan protests were violently suppressed.
- The foundation operates under the president's administration, whose head is the president's daughter (Korea Herald), so these criticisms attach to its patron.

BOTTOM LINE
This is state money, directed from the presidency; no accounts, donor list or board were found. The one documented outside funding is Saudi: the Diriyah Biennale Foundation, ACWA Power and Vision Invest at the 2025 biennial. Worth watching: the next edition's partner list, and any fossil-fuel partner, of which none was confirmed.
