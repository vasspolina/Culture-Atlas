SALVADOR DALÍ MUSEUM: St. Petersburg, USA
ID: salvador-dali-museum
Website: https://thedali.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the work of Salvador Dalí at One Dali Blvd on the downtown waterfront of St. Petersburg, Florida; it calls itself The Dalí.
- Built on the collection of Reynolds and Eleanor Morse, who began buying Dalí's work in 1942 and opened a first museum in Beachwood, Ohio in March 1971.
- The collection moved to a renovated marine warehouse in St. Petersburg, which opened on 7 March 1982.
- The present building, by Yann Weymouth of HOK, opened on 11 January 2011 with concrete walls 18 inches thick against hurricanes; it cost over $30 million (Wikipedia).
- A 501(c)(3) nonprofit; Hank Hine is executive director.
- More than 10 million people have toured the museum since 1982 (WUSF).
- Visiting: daily 10:00am to 6:00pm, Thursdays to 8:00pm; adults USD 32, students USD 22, ages 6 to 12 USD 12, 5 and younger free.

FUNDING
Salvador Dalí Museum: self-earning nonprofit: ticket and programme income, with tourist tax money for its expansion
- Fiscal year to June 2025: revenue $22,409,456 and expenses $12,582,030 (Form 990 data, ProPublica).
- Programme services brought $9,767,807 (43.6%), sales of assets $5,573,627, contributions $3,020,615 (13.5%), investment income $2,543,309 and net inventory sales $1,500,462.
- Net assets stood at $123,841,730.
- In March 2026 the museum announced a $65 million expansion of about 35,000 square feet on the south side of the building, with a proposed 2028 opening.
- Much of the expansion money came from Visit St. Pete-Clearwater's "From Visitors With Love" programme, which reinvests tourist development tax collected on hotel stays (WUSF, St. Pete Catalyst).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum largely pays its own way from tickets and programmes, and the public money behind its expansion is a local hotel tax. No funder on record is objected to. Worth watching: how much tourist tax the expansion finally takes and who gives the private share.
