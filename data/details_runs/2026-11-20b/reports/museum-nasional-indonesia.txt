MUSEUM NASIONAL INDONESIA: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: museum-nasional-indonesia
Website: https://www.museumnasional.or.id (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Archaeological, historical, ethnological and geographical museum on Jalan Medan Merdeka Barat, Central Jakarta, on the west side of Merdeka Square; known as Museum Gajah after the bronze elephant King Chulalongkorn of Siam gave Batavia in 1871 (Wikipedia).
- It grew from the Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen, a private Dutch learned society founded on 24 April 1778; the colonial government decided on the present building in 1862 and it opened in 1868 (Wikipedia).
- The Indonesian government took it over on 17 September 1962 as Museum Pusat, and it was renamed Museum Nasional by a 1979 ministerial decree (Wikipedia).
- It holds about 141,000 objects, from prehistory, archaeology and numismatics to ceramics, ethnography, history and geography; a north wing, Gedung B, opened in 2007 (Wikipedia).
- A fire on 16 September 2023 damaged six rooms in Gedung A, the old wing; the museum reopened on 15 October 2024 with a show of more than 2,500 repatriated objects (The Star; The Art Newspaper).
- It marked its 248th anniversary on 24 April 2026; it had 770,000 visitors in 2025 and targets 800,000 in 2026, with free entry under the Aramsa programme on the second Wednesday of each month from 16:00 (RRI).
- Since 1 January 2026 tickets cost Rp50,000 for adults (from Rp25,000), Rp30,000 for school pupils (before free) and Rp150,000 for foreigners (from Rp50,000); children under three enter free (Metro TV).

FUNDING
Museum Nasional Indonesia: public: Indonesia's Ministry of Culture, through a public service agency that keeps ticket income
- Owner: the Ministry of Culture, which decided the 2026 ticket rise; the head of Museums and Cultural Heritage, Indira Estiyanti Nurjadin, said the museum relies on government money, philanthropy and tickets, and that ticket income pays for collection care and visitor services (Antara, 5 January 2026).
- Operator: the Indonesian Heritage Agency, a public service agency (BLU) running 18 museums and 34 heritage sites, which keeps ticket income and investor support instead of paying it all to the treasury (Fortune Indonesia, April 2024). In April 2026 the museum still shared one BLU with the national gallery (RRI).
- Under the BLU the museum can earn its own income from tickets, venue hire, collaborations and sponsorship; Culture Minister Fadli Zon wants public-private partnerships with philanthropists to fund museums (RRI, 24 April 2026).
- Fire repairs: the acting head of the agency, Ahmad Mahendra, said full restoration would take three years (The Art Newspaper, 15 October 2024); in January 2026 the museum said renovation of the six burned rooms would continue with support from government, private and public sources (Antara).

Red flags: 2023 fire in Gedung A
- The fire began at about 8pm on Saturday 16 September 2023, lasted around three hours and damaged six rooms in Gedung A; an official said most destroyed items were replicas (The Star, citing The Jakarta Post).
- Ahmad Mahendra said on 19 September 2023 that police found the fire started behind Gedung A, not inside it; some evacuated objects were intact and others damaged to varying degrees (Kompas).
- The museum's companion show "Beating Nekara, Watering the Fire" set out to "transparently answer public curiosity" about the fire and its preparedness for future disasters (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: colonial-era loot in the collection
- Part of the collection came from Dutch colonial military campaigns against kingdoms in Java, Aceh, Lombok and Bali; most of the royal regalia in its ethnology treasure room was acquired or looted in those campaigns (Wikipedia).
- Returns continue: about 300 selected collections came back from the Netherlands between 1978 and 2023, including the Lombok treasure in July 2023 (The Art Newspaper), and the museum received Eugene Dubois's fossil collection from the Netherlands before January 2026 (Antara).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is news of the fire and the reopening; the criticism found is of the 2026 ticket rise, in national press.
1. Metro TV: "Harga Tiket Masuk Museum Nasional Naik Drastis, Pegiat Pendidikan Mengeluh" (2 January 2026)
URL: https://www.metrotvnews.com/read/NleC9jEx-harga-tiket-masuk-museum-nasional-naik-drastis-pegiat-pendidikan-mengeluh
- Nurfahmi Budi of Sanggar Askala Edukasi said doubling the price without careful thought burdens citizens who want to learn.
- Argues a state-owned museum should not act like a private company chasing profit, and that service improvements should come from the state budget. National television news, not art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Indonesian state money plus ticket income. The weak points are the slow repair of the 2023 fire damage and the colonial origins of part of the collection; worth watching is how much the museum comes to rely on higher tickets and private donors.
