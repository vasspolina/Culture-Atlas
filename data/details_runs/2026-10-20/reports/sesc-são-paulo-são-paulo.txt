SESC SÃO PAULO: São Paulo, Brazil
ID: sesc-são-paulo-são-paulo
Website: https://www.sescsp.org.br (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Network of cultural, sports and social centres in São Paulo state, run by the regional administration of the Serviço Social do Comércio (Sesc), a private non-profit institution (Wikipedia); the regional administration for the state is named on sescsp.org.br.
- Sesc was created on 13 September 1946 by Decree-Law 9,853, signed by President Eurico Gaspar Dutra, and belongs to the "Sistema S" group of business-created bodies with Senac, Senai and Sesi (Wikipedia, Portuguese and English).
- Its centres offer culture, education, health, leisure and sport; it is aimed at workers in commerce, services and tourism and their families but open to the public (Wikipedia, sescsp.org.br). Its Credencial Plena is a free benefit for workers in those sectors (sescsp.org.br).
- Best-known centre: Sesc Pompeia, at Rua Clélia 93, converted from a 1938 oil-drum factory by Lina Bo Bardi between 1977 and 1986; it opened on 22 January 1982 with two concrete towers of sports halls joined by walkways, a theatre, library, pool, exhibition rooms and workshops (Wikipedia).
- Sesc 24 de Maio, Rua 24 de Maio 109, showed "HIP-HOP 80'sp" with free admission, extended to 29 March 2026 (e-flux). Luiz Galina is named as Director of Sesc São Paulo (e-flux).
- Visiting: hours and prices vary by centre. No reporting found of a single admission or accessibility policy from the pages read.

FUNDING
Sesc São Paulo: mainly a levy on companies in commerce, services and tourism
- Sesc's revenue comes from a tax on companies, at rates between 0.2% and 2.5% (Wikipedia, English). It is a non-profit private institution kept by businessmen in trade, services and tourism (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of São Paulo revenue figures: the transparency portal page read showed no totals.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors of the São Paulo regional administration.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Judith Butler seminar, 2017: Sesc Pompeia hosted Butler's seminar "Os fins da democracia" on 7 to 9 November 2017. An anonymous online petition asking Sesc to cancel her visit reached 88,821 signatures, and about 386 negative reviews appeared on the Sesc Pompeia Facebook page in about 24 hours from 26 October. About 3,000 people posted positive reviews in a counter-campaign (Vermelho, 1 November 2017, a partisan outlet that frames the episode as censorship).
- The actors were the petitioners and the reviewers. Vermelho does not report that Sesc cancelled the seminar or censored anything; Sesc representatives declined to comment.
- In December 2018, before taking office, Paulo Guedes called for cuts of up to 50% in Sistema S funding; the Confederação Nacional da Indústria rejected the idea (Congresso em Foco).
- A 2019 bill by deputy Eli Borges, PL 6505/19, would make contributions to the Sistema S, including Sesc, optional and cap them at 1% of payroll, arguing that they burden employers (Jornal Contábil, 3 May 2021).
- Provisional Measure 932/2020 cut contributions to the Sistema S entities, Sesc among them, by 50% until 30 June 2020 to relieve payrolls during the pandemic (Congresso em Foco, 1 April 2020).
- Sesc São Paulo is funded through this levy, so the proposals concern its main income. The Butler petition targeted Sesc Pompeia's programme, not the levy.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a levy on Brazilian companies in commerce, services and tourism, with no corporate sponsor reported. The weak points are that no São Paulo revenue figures were found, and that the levy has been the target of political proposals to cut or make it optional. Worth watching: published accounts, and any change to the levy.
