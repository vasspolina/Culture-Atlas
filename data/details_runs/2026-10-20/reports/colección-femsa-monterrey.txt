COLECCIÓN FEMSA: Monterrey, Mexico
ID: colección-femsa-monterrey
Website: https://coleccionfemsa.com/

OVERVIEW
- Corporate art collection of the Mexican company FEMSA, based in Monterrey, focused on modern and contemporary Latin American art with an emphasis on Mexican work (CIMAM).
- It began in 1977, when the founding family of FEMSA donated "El maizal (Milpa seca)" by Gerardo Murillo (Dr. Atl) (CIMAM).
- Its history is tied to the Museo de Monterrey (1977 to 2000); after the museum phase ended the collection became itinerant, working through loans and collaborations with museums (FEMSA press release, 8 December 2025).
- Bienal FEMSA, founded in 1992, began as a competition with prizes and now moves to a different venue for each edition; it has commissioned nearly 50 works and added about 20 to the collection (CIMAM).
- To mark the 50th anniversary, "Constelaciones y derivas: arte de América Latina desde la Colección FEMSA" shows around 170 works by more than 115 artists at the Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Monterrey from 20 March to 9 August 2026 (FEMSA press release).
- Current exhibitions include "El hogar que habito" at Museo Amparo, Puebla, from 26 September 2026 to 25 January 2027 (collection website).
- The curatorial team is Laura Pacheco, Paulina Bravo, Eugenia Braniff and Beto Díaz (CIMAM).
- It runs temporary exhibitions, a works-on-loan programme and educational activities (CIMAM).

FUNDING
Colección FEMSA: corporate: part of FEMSA (Fomento Económico Mexicano)
- The collection is part of FEMSA (CIMAM).
- FEMSA runs the OXXO convenience store chain and drugstore chains in South America, and the Garza Lagüera family took control of FEMSA in 1974 (Wikipedia). Coca-Cola FEMSA's Form 20-F for 2025 states that FEMSA indirectly owns 47.2% of its outstanding capital stock, representing 56.0% of its voting shares.
- Laura Pacheco, manager of Colección and Bienal FEMSA, said that FEMSA "for 135 years" has kept a commitment to the arts (translation, FEMSA press release).

WIDER CONTEXT
- SIPAZ, "Chiapas: Protest to cancel Femsa-Coca Cola concession in San Cristobal de las Casas" (posted by the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre on 11 April 2017): on 10 April 2017 organisations and neighbourhoods of San Cristóbal de las Casas, Chiapas, marched to demand that the concession of Femsa Coca-Cola be cancelled or its water extraction capped; protesters said the plant extracts more than 750,000 litres of water a day.
- Al Jazeera, Peter Yeung, "'Forgotten': How one Mexican city struggles against big industry for water" (8 January 2025): the Femsa-run bottling plant in San Cristóbal has permits to extract more than 300,000 gallons of water a day from the basin of the Huitepec volcano, and a 2017 report by a coalition of environmental and civil rights groups says Femsa pays 2,600 pesos for each water permit in Mexico. Coca-Cola Mexico's spokesperson, Daniela Puerta, said "We always abide by local laws and regulations."
- Connection: the collection is part of FEMSA, which indirectly owns 47.2% of Coca-Cola FEMSA. The protest and the article name FEMSA and its bottler, not the collection.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the collection is corporate: it is part of FEMSA, the Monterrey company behind OXXO and owner of 47.2% of the Coca-Cola bottler Coca-Cola FEMSA, and it began with a donation from the FEMSA founding family in 1977. The criticism on record concerns the bottler's water use in Chiapas, raised by residents in 2017 and reported by Al Jazeera in 2025.
