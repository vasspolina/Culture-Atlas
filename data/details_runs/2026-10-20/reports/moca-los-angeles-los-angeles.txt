MOCA LOS ANGELES: Los Angeles, USA
ID: moca-los-angeles-los-angeles
Website: https://www.moca.org/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum with two buildings: MOCA Grand Avenue at 250 South Grand Avenue, and the Geffen Contemporary at 152 North Central Avenue (MOCA).
- Established in 1979 after philanthropist Marcia Simon Weisman argued at a fundraising event that Los Angeles needed a contemporary art museum. Eli Broad was founding chairman (Wikipedia).
- The Grand Avenue building, designed by Arata Isozaki, was completed in 1986 at a cost of $23 million as part of a deal with the California Plaza developer. The Temporary Contemporary, a former hardware store renovated by Frank Gehry, opened in 1983 and was renamed the Geffen Contemporary in 1996 after a $5 million gift from the David Geffen Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Grand Avenue is open Tuesday to Friday 11am to 5pm and Saturday and Sunday 11am to 6pm, closed Monday, and open until 8pm on the first Thursday of each month. General admission is free; Michael Heizer's Double Negative in Nevada is also free, on view all hours (MOCA).

FUNDING
MOCA Los Angeles: mainly private donations: contributions were $22.2 million of $29.6 million revenue in the year to June 2025
- Form 990 for the year to June 2025: revenue $29,554,913, expenses $26,532,037, contributions $22,168,637, net assets $219,694,934. Contributions were $18,029,335 in 2024 and $12,949,052 in 2023 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Wikipedia says MOCA receives minimal government funding and relies on donors to pay about 80% of its expenses.
- Free general admission: Apollo (20 May 2019) reported that a $10 million gift from Carolyn Powers, then board president, prompted MOCA to drop admission charges; the museum's visit page says general admission is free "courtesy of Carolyn Clark Powers".
- 2008 rescue: after the endowment lost $44 million of $50 million over nine years, Eli Broad offered $30 million in staggered payments, $15 million as matching donations. He missed payments in 2012 and the payments expired in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Board 2026 to 2027: Carolyn Clark Powers (Chair), Eugenio López Alonso and Karyn Kohl (Vice-Chairs), Clifford J. Einstein and Maurice Marciano (Chairs Emeriti), Jeffrey Soros (President Emeritus), 15 further trustees including Barbara Kruger, Tala Madani, Rodney McMillian, Sean Parker and Christina Quarles, and Jonathan Abbott, Karen Bass and Council President Marqueece Harris-Dawson as ex-officio members (MOCA).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critical coverage dates from 2012 and 2013, when the museum's governance and programming under director Jeffrey Deitch were attacked after the firing of chief curator Paul Schimmel.
1. Art F City: Corinna Kirsch, "The A-Z MOCA Scandal Index" (14 March 2013)
URL: https://artfcity.com/posts/2013/03/the-moca-scandal-index-a-z/
- Presents MOCA as in financial trouble despite a board packed with millionaires and billionaires; says "Except for Eli Broad, it seems like nobody's emptying their pockets" and that Broad had missed the last two payments of his promised $30 million.
- Faults Deitch's "increasingly entertainment-driven program", citing a Dennis Hopper show that knocked a Jack Goldstein retrospective off the schedule.
- Describes Schimmel's firing or dismissal as a turning point and says critics were outraged at the lack of transparency.
- Its entry "Cohen, Steve" calls him a "Billionaire board member" and says, as of March 2013, that his hedge fund company was under federal investigation for insider trading while he gave millions of dollars in art to MoMA. Cohen is not on the museum's 2026-27 board roster.
2. The Chronicle of Philanthropy: "Artists on Board of L.A. Museum Resign to Protest Curator's Ouster" (17 July 2012), reporting from the Los Angeles Times
URL: https://www.philanthropy.com/news/artists-on-board-of-la-museum-resign-to-protest-curators-ouster/
- No author is named; the piece attributes its information to the Los Angeles Times. All four artist trustees (John Baldessari, Catherine Opie, Barbara Kruger and Ed Ruscha) left the board after the firing of chief curator Paul Schimmel, and it says the ouster deepened divisions among trustees and supporters over the museum's direction under Deitch.
- General philanthropy press, not art press.
3. The Chronicle of Philanthropy: "L.A. Museum's Trustees Seek Unity After Summer of Disputes" (4 September 2012)
URL: https://www.philanthropy.com/news/la-museums-trustees-seek-unity-after-summer-of-disputes/
- No author is named; the piece cites the Los Angeles Times. It reports that some trustees and donors said Broad and a select group of board members had too much influence, and that major donors, Broad among them, publicly disputed the museum's direction under Deitch.
- General philanthropy press, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
MOCA is funded mostly by private donations: $22.2 million in contributions in the year to June 2025, a $10 million gift from Carolyn Powers in 2019 that prompted free admission, and Eli Broad's 2008 offer of $30 million in staggered payments (Wikipedia). The documented criticism concerns the 2012 and 2013 governance disputes under Deitch.
