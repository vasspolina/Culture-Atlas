BIENAL DE CUENCA: Cuenca, Ecuador
ID: bienal-de-cuenca
Website: https://biennialfoundation.org/biennials/bienal-de-cuenca/ (the biennial's own site was not opened; checked 2026-10-24 via the Biennial Foundation listing)

OVERVIEW
- International contemporary art biennial in Cuenca, Ecuador, founded in 1985 as an "eminently pictorial biennial" and since widened to other media (Biennial Foundation).
- Organised by the Fundación Municipal Bienal de Cuenca, at Bolívar 13-89 y Estévez de Toral, Cuenca (Biennial Foundation).
- The 17th edition, "The Game", marked the 40th anniversary: inauguration on 24 October 2025, exhibitions until 31 January 2026 (El Mercurio).
- It used 13 official venues in Cuenca, museums, heritage houses and galleries, plus 10 alternative spaces; a congress on new pedagogies of contemporary art took place in Loja on 27 and 28 October (El Mercurio).
- Seventeen curators each chose three artists, at least one Ecuadorian, for 51 artists in total. Hernán Pacurucu became executive director of the foundation in 2023 (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting: Primicias reported that entry to the 16th edition was free; El Mercurio's 2025 article does not say whether entry to the 17th was paid.

FUNDING
Bienal de Cuenca: mainly public: the municipality of Cuenca, with private-company agreements and self-raised funds
- The 17th edition had a budget of US$1.2 million, the most expensive in four decades, from municipal contributions, agreements with private companies and self-generated income (El Mercurio, September 2025).
- The municipality of Cuenca provides $500,000 per edition, about a third of which covers operations and staff (The Art Newspaper).
- For the 16th edition, Primicias reported that the foundation lived on contributions from the Cuenca mayor's office, embassies and private sponsors, with an annual budget of $700,000 and a hope of reaching $1 million with central government support.
- No reporting found of the names of the private companies or of the amounts each paid.
- No reporting found of accounts or a board list; a 2023 annual report posted on Google Drive could not be opened (sign-in screen).

Red flags: protest or boycott: demands to remove one work
- November 2025: director Hernán Pacurucu reported that in recent days the biennial had received in-person and electronic warnings demanding that "Son de las Malvinas" by Cuenca artist Fernando Falconí stay off view. The work is about the case of four missing children in the Las Malvinas neighbourhood of Guayaquil (El Mercurio, 25 November 2025).
- One email was signed by a movement calling itself "Dios, Patria, Orden y Tradición" (DPOT). It demanded the work's removal, called it an "afrenta a las Fuerzas Armadas" (an affront to the Armed Forces, translation), said it could "tomar acciones" (take action) if ignored, and asked for new selection criteria. El Mercurio gives no further detail on who DPOT is.
- The biennial called the messages "presiones directas" (direct pressure, translation) that put the artist, the organising team and the event at risk, and reported them to the Fiscalía del Azuay. Falconí said the work would stay at the Museo Universitario de Cuenca until the biennial closed.
- Not state censorship on the page: El Mercurio names no state body as a sender and reports no action against the work by the municipality, the Armed Forces or the national government. The only state body it mentions is the Fiscalía del Azuay, which received the complaint.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review in the art press, mostly admiring, with criticism of organisation. No criticism of the funding found.
1. The Art Newspaper: Veronica Pesantes and Charmaine Picard, "Ecuador's Bienal de Cuenca marks 40th anniversary with a playful theme but a serious tone" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/12/16/bienal-de-cuenca-ecuador-17th-edition-review
- Calls the overall quality strong but says the lack of organisation, absence of maps and outdated website make navigation difficult for visitors.
- Curators were taken to Loja for a conference during opening weekend, leaving artists little time for dialogue; several artists said the curators barely saw completed works in situ.
- Does not mention the DPOT threats.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the biennial is mainly the City of Cuenca's, with private-company agreements whose names and amounts are not published in anything found; no flagged funder was found. The weak points are the missing sponsor names, the absence of accounts or a board list in anything opened, and the threats of November 2025 against one work. Worth watching are whether the Fiscalía complaint produces a result and whether future editions publish their agreement partners.
