LA CASA ENCENDIDA: Madrid, Spain
ID: la-casa-encendida-madrid
Website: https://www.lacasaencendida.es

OVERVIEW
- Cultural and social centre at Ronda de Valencia 2, Madrid, run by Fundación Montemadrid (the centre's website).
- Inaugurated on 3 December 2002 as a centre of Caja Madrid's Obra Social; José Guirao, until then director of the Museo Reina Sofía, was its first head (the centre's website).
- Named for the book "La casa encendida" by the poet Luis Rosales Camacho (English Wikipedia).
- Building: a neo-Mudéjar edifice by architect Fernando Arbós y Tremantí, with the foundation stone laid on 1 May 1911 and an official inauguration on 1 May 1913. It was first a branch and pawnshop warehouse of the Caja de Ahorros y Monte de Piedad de Madrid, known as the Casa de Empeños. Carlos Manzano and Associates carried out the conversion between 1999 and 2002.
- On site: about 6,500 square metres over four floors and a roof terrace, with an auditorium for 175 people and an audiovisual room for 72 in the basement, five exhibition rooms, a courtyard of about 300 square metres and a shop (the centre's website). Programmes cover culture, solidarity, the environment and education.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 10 am to 9 pm and Sunday 10 am to 4 pm. Screenings cost 4 euros, family sessions 5 euros, concerts and performances 8 to 20 euros, with extra discounts for under-30s, over-65s, the unemployed and large families on certain activities. The renovation aimed to eliminate architectural barriers and make the facilities accessible (the centre's website).

FUNDING
La Casa Encendida: foundation-funded: Fundación Montemadrid, a private non-profit foundation
- The transparency page says Fundación Montemadrid provides "about 89%" of the annual budget; space rentals and collaborations with other public and private entities make up a small percentage of the rest. Ticket and course prices are subsidised by the foundation, covering on average up to 75% of the final price (the centre's transparency page).
- The opening hours and tickets page says: "Fundación Montemadrid, a private non-profit entity, finances 100% of the cost of activities at La Casa Encendida" (the centre's website).
- Where the foundation comes from: Spanish Wikipedia calls Fundación Montemadrid the heir of Caja Madrid, whose social work was carried out through Obra Social Caja Madrid. Dividends from the Banco Financiero y de Ahorros (BFA), formed in 2010 around Caja Madrid, were meant to supply the capital for that social work; the state nationalisation of BFA was formalised on 27 June 2012 through the FROB, and Caja Madrid lost its stake (Spanish Wikipedia).
- When Caja Madrid merged into BFA (later Bankia) in 2010, its non-financial assets, including La Casa Encendida, were excluded; they were renamed Fundación Especial Caja Madrid in 2012 and the name Fundación Montemadrid was finalised in 2014 (the centre's website).
- The Fundación Especial Caja Madrid had a budget of 31.6 million euros in 2013, and the foundation is under the protectorate of the Madrid regional government (Spanish Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is a single private non-profit foundation, Fundación Montemadrid, which supplies about 89% of the budget by the centre's own account. Spanish Wikipedia calls the foundation the heir of the savings bank Caja Madrid.
