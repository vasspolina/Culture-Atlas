PLUG IN ICA: Winnipeg, Canada
ID: plug-in-ica
Website: https://plugin.org (checked 2026-10-25; loaded)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art institute at Unit 1, 460 Portage Avenue, Winnipeg, in the Buhler Centre at Memorial Boulevard and Colony Street, a building it shares with the University of Winnipeg (plugin.org; The Manitoban, 2012).
- Established in 1972 as a not-for-profit exhibition space run by a board of practising artists; Lorraine Raboud and Suzanne Gillies were the founding directors (plugin.org/history; The Manitoban). It started in a loft at 90 Albert Street and later moved to 175 McDermot Street, with earlier addresses on Arthur and Donald Streets (The Manitoban).
- Earlier name: Plug In; plugin.org says a survey of ICA models in the UK and US found its mission fits "most comfortably within the ICA model," and it does not state a formal designation.
- Building: Canadian Heritage's 19 February 2010 announcement describes a new 37,500 square foot building with four exhibition areas, with Plug In ICA occupying about 20 percent, built in partnership with the University of Winnipeg. No reporting found of its opening date.
- On site: exhibitions, the STAGES series, education, a video archive, a bookstore and library, and gallery rentals (plugin.org). The Manitoban says it fostered the creation of Video Pool (1983) and launched Manitoba Artists for Women's Art (MAWA), and that its 2001 Paradise Institute represented Canada at the Venice Biennale and won the Golden Lion.
- Visiting: free; open Tuesday and Wednesday noon to 6pm, Thursday noon to 8pm, Friday noon to 6pm and Saturday noon to 5pm, closed Sunday and Monday and between exhibitions; wheelchair accessible; paid parking on site and nearby (plugin.org).

FUNDING
Plug In ICA: mainly public: federal government grants, with private donors, sponsors and a gala
- Canada Arts Training Fund (Canadian Heritage): $100,000 contribution to Plug-In Inc., 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2027 (Government of Canada open data). plugin.org credits the same fund and the Johnston Group for its 2026 Fall Institute.
- Capital: on 19 February 2010 Canadian Heritage's Canada Cultural Spaces Fund announced $1.4 million for phase three of the capital expansion (Government of Canada).
- Andy Warhol Foundation: $80,000 to support programming over two years (Canadian Art, 22 January 2016).
- The site's operational-support and program-support banners are logos with no text, so other funders could not be named. Its sponsors page lists sponsorship opportunities for tours, the Summer Institute, STAGES, the gala, exhibition openings and the Prairie Art Book Fair, with no amounts.
- No reporting found of RBC Foundation, Canada Council, Manitoba Arts Council or Winnipeg Arts Council grants. No reporting found of annual reports or accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

- Notes that some programming drew controversy, including the 1993 Festival du Voyeur and a decision to display work by John Wayne Gacy; director Anthony Kiendl defends such work in his quoted remarks as a public forum. The article does not say whether the Gacy works were shown.
- Criticises the art shown, not the institution; a student newspaper rather than art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Plug In ICA is mostly federal public grants plus foundations and private sponsors, with no flagged-industry funder found. The weak points are that sponsor logos are unlabelled, annual reports and accounts are not posted, and no source found names RBC Foundation, the Canada Council or the Manitoba and Winnipeg arts councils as funders. Worth watching the 2025 to 2027 federal agreement and the new sponsor names on its support page.
