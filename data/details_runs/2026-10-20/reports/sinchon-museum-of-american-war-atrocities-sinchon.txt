SINCHON MUSEUM OF AMERICAN WAR ATROCITIES: Sinchon, North Korea
ID: sinchon-museum-of-american-war-atrocities-sinchon
Website: none found for the museum. The address on file, https://kcnawatch.org (checked 2026-10-24; live), is an aggregator of North Korean state media and does not mention the museum.

OVERVIEW
- War museum in Sinchon County, South Hwanghae Province, reached on a day trip from Pyongyang (Jeppesen), dedicated to the Sinchon massacre of October to December 1950 (Wikipedia; Daily Beast).
- Established on 26 March 1958; rebuilt and moved to a new site in July 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Sinchon, the county where the killings took place; it attributes them to US and South Korean forces (Wikipedia).
- What is on show: remains, belongings, images and paintings; the grounds include a grave of 400 mothers and children killed in the area (Wikipedia). A visitor writing in 2013 counted about a dozen rooms with survivor testimony, victim counts by district, paintings and photographs of bodies (Patrick Tapy, Sino-NK).
- The museum shows a toll of over 35,000 dead between 17 October and 7 December 1950 (Wikipedia); the Korean Central News Agency gave 35,383 in 1998 (Wikipedia, Sinchon Massacre).
- Visiting: the site is rarely on group itineraries for foreigners; nearly every North Korean visits at least once on a mandatory educational trip (Travis Jeppesen, Daily Beast, 2018). No reporting found of opening hours or admission.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Historians outside North Korea dispute the museum's account. The Institute for Korean Historical Studies (South Korea) concluded that both communists and anti-communist vigilantes carried out killings, and that the 19th Infantry Regiment did not take part but failed to stop the South Korean secret police (Wikipedia).
- Novelist Hwang Sok-yong attributes the massacre to local rivalry that used the chaos of war as cover (Wikipedia). Cathcart reports that the historian Bruce Cumings said he found nothing in the archives about a US occupation of Sinchon.
- Adam Cathcart (University of Leeds), 19 July 2015: he found no evidence that an American unit entered Sinchon, and wrote that "no serious writing outside of North Korea has been made to determine if the body count is accurate"; he did not reach a firm conclusion on who did the killings.
- Patrick Tapy (Sino-NK, 17 September 2013) says the visit gave "the impression of complete and unquestionable US guilt being used as a propaganda tool", and that North Korean historians "do not possess any more evidence of actual US responsibility" than independent scholars such as Bruce Cumings or Hwang Sok-yong.
- A 1952 report by the International Association of Democratic Lawyers, prepared in Pyongyang, accused US forces; it relied on North Korean oral testimony (Wikipedia). No independent international investigation is described.
- Connection to the space: these disputes concern the account the museum presents. The death toll and the attribution to US forces are the museum's own claims.

BOTTOM LINE
No page opened names who owns, runs or pays for the museum, so no sanctions listing can be tied to it. The weak points are the lack of any published ownership, budget or board, and an official account of the killings and the toll that outside historians dispute and that nobody outside the state has been able to verify. Watch for any independent access to the site or archives.
