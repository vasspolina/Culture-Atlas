SHARJAH ART FOUNDATION: Sharjah, United Arab Emirates
ID: sharjah-art-foundation-sharjah
Website: https://sharjahart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; live, listing Sharjah Biennial 17, 21 January to 13 June 2027)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation in Sharjah with its Information Centre at Al Mureijah Square; venues include converted heritage buildings and public courtyards (SAF website).
- Founded in 2009 by Hoor Al Qasimi, daughter of Sultan bin Muhammad Al-Qasimi, ruler of Sharjah; Wikipedia places it under the Department of Culture and Information, Government of Sharjah.
- It took over the Sharjah Biennial, first held in 1993 under the Department of Culture and Information, in 2009; the March Meeting of regional art practitioners began in 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Buildings include the Rain Room, a reconstruction of The Flying Saucer, the Al Jubail Vegetable Market and the Kalba Ice Factory, which opened in February 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: the Biennial, the March Meeting, residencies, production grants, commissions, publications, a library and SAF Radio (SAF website, Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Saturday to Thursday 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., Friday 4 p.m. to 9 p.m. Most exhibitions and events are free; wheelchair-accessible washrooms in every venue and free guided tours in Arabic, English, Farsi, Hindi and Urdu (SAF website).

FUNDING
Sharjah Art Foundation: state-backed: the Sharjah ruler and Sharjah's government; no accounts published
- Ghalya Saadawi wrote in e-flux Journal (June 2011) that the Foundation, founded in 2009, is funded by the ruler and Sharjah's Ministry of Culture.
- Hoor Al Qasimi describes it as "the independent public arts organisation she established in 2009" (SAF website). Sheikh Sultan Al Qasimi decided to dismiss director Jack Persekian in 2011 (e-flux Journal; Jadaliyya).
- No reporting found of annual budgets, a sponsor list or a board list.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The Foundation is a Sharjah public body under the Government of Sharjah (Wikipedia), led by the ruler's daughter, Hoor Al Qasimi, as President and Director (SAF website).

Red flags: state censorship and dismissal, 2011
- On 6 April 2011 the ruler of Sharjah dismissed director Jack Persekian without notice, after public outcry over Mustapha Benfodil's installation "Maportaliche" in the 10th Sharjah Biennial (Sharjah Call for Action, e-flux; The National).
- The work was removed. Other works were altered, removed or placed "under review", including an audio piece adjusted so it did not overlap the call to prayer (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Hoor Al Qasimi said the work paired sexually explicit language with religious references in a busy courtyard, and "the public took enormous offense" (Jadaliyya; ArtAsiaPacific).
- The Foundation called the boycott petition "deliberately misinforming the public" and said no staff had resigned (spokeswoman Sara Fitzmaurice, The National). The petition, signed by 1,627 people by 2 May 2011, called for public acknowledgment of the censorship and for guarantees of the Foundation's independence (Jadaliyya).
- Persekian apologised for not examining the piece closely and said he opposed boycotts (Jadaliyya; ArtAsiaPacific).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Substantial criticism, almost all about the 2011 dismissal and censorship. The pieces found are 15 years old; no later critique of the Foundation's own conduct found.
1. e-flux Journal: Ghalya Saadawi, "Post-Sharjah Biennial 10: Institutional Grease and Institutional Critique" (2011)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/journal/26/67970/post-sharjah-biennial-10-institutional-grease-and-institutional-critique
- Says the ruler decided to remove Persekian and that the Foundation and its PR firm ran a campaign defending the decision.
- Argues that large institutions everywhere depend on state, private or capital interests, and that some critics treated the Gulf as a single entity; calls for a renewed critique of institutions, not only moral condemnation.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: unsigned news item, "Director's Ouster Jeopardizes Sharjah Art Foundation's Future" (2011)
URL: https://www.artasiapacific.com/news/director-s-ouster-jeopardizes-sharjah-art-foundation-s-future/
- Argues the dismissal threatens the Foundation and Biennial as regional platforms and risks self-censorship; says the Foundation has alienated key figures in the regional scene.
- Quotes curators Rasha Salti and Haig Aivazian: "To shock was never our strategy, and offense never our intention."
3. Jadaliyya: author not named, "Boat Rocking in the Art Islands: Politics, Plots and Dismissals in Sharjah's Tenth Biennial" (2011)
URL: https://www.jadaliyya.com/Details/23938
- Presents the dismissal as the ruler's decision and questions the international art world's ties to the UAE. The Foundation's reply is quoted.
4. Sharjah Call for Action, posted on e-flux: "An unwarranted dismissal in Sharjah" (2011)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/35699/an-unwarranted-dismissal-in-sharjah/
- Open letter, not a critical essay: calls the dismissal disproportionate and threatens a boycott of the Foundation, the Biennial and the emirate's cultural activities unless its demands are met.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In May 2015 Gulf Labor member Walid Raad was turned back at Dubai airport on his way to the Foundation's March Meeting; Gulf Labor campaigns for migrant workers on Abu Dhabi's Saadiyat Island (The Art Newspaper, Javier Pes). More than 40 artists in Sharjah Biennial 12 backed the banned artists (The Art Newspaper).
- The Art Newspaper does not report a Foundation response. The bans were by UAE authorities; the link to the Foundation is that the trip was to its event.

BOTTOM LINE
The Foundation is a Sharjah government body, so the money behind it is the ruler's and the state's, with no accounts or funder list published. The documented weak point is 2011, when the ruler dismissed the director over an artwork and other works were altered; the Foundation disputed the petition. Watch for any published accounts or governance changes.
