STATE RUSSIAN MUSEUM: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: state-russian-museum-st-petersburg
Website: https://rusmuseum.ru (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of Russian art in the historic centre of St Petersburg, with more than 450,000 items from the 10th to the 21st century (stated by the museum).
- Founded by decision of Alexander III; Nicholas II signed the decree in 1895 and the museum opened to visitors on 19 March 1898 (7 March, old style) as the Russian Museum of Emperor Alexander III (stated by the museum).
- The main building is the Mikhailovsky Palace, built in 1819 to 1825 by Carlo Rossi as the residence of Grand Duke Michael Pavlovich; Vasily Svinyin rebuilt the interiors for the museum (Wikipedia, the museum).
- The complex also includes the Benois Building, the Marble Palace, the Stroganov Palace, St Michael's Castle, the Summer Palace and Cabin of Peter I, and the Summer and Mikhailovsky gardens (stated by the museum).
- Directors: Vladimir Gusev from 1988 to 2023, then Alla Manilova since 25 April 2023 (Wikipedia). Gusev became the museum's president.
- Visitors: over 3.6 million in 2024 (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
State Russian Museum: state-run, with the sanctioned state-owned bank VTB as general sponsor
- State museum under Russian federal control; no reporting found of its budget, annual report or accounts.
- VTB Bank is general sponsor and has been general partner since 2024, supporting exhibitions of Konchalovsky, Surikov, Grabar, Repin, Petrov-Vodkin, Vereshchagin, Bryullov, "Our Avant-garde", and the Summer Garden Day (stated by the museum).
- No amount is given. Russian state bodies held over 90 percent of VTB's capital as of February 2022 (Wikipedia).
- The sponsors page names only VTB in text; no other sponsor is named on it.

Red flags: sanctions
- Manilova, named director in April 2023, was placed on Canada's cultural sanctions list in early August 2023. The listing's general rationale is that Russia uses cultural figures to promote Kremlin propaganda about the invasion of Ukraine; the article gives no individual grounds, and does not say the museum itself was sanctioned (The Art Newspaper, 31 August 2023).
- VTB, the general sponsor, was sanctioned by the EU and UK from 2014, and by the US, UK and others again on 24 February 2022; it was removed from SWIFT in March 2022 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: state censorship of an exhibition
- July 2023: the museum cancelled the Timur Novikov show "Klin Novikova" the day before its 19 July opening. Artists and an art historian said Manilova personally banned it; the museum said it breached technical rules and held too few Novikov works from its own collection (Meduza, Moscow Times).
- It opened on 26 July with works swapped or covered. Curator Alexander Borovsky said sexually explicit works were replaced and a donkey costume's genitals covered to protect the museum "from idiots": "We chose to conceal." Olga Tobreluts's "Adam and Eve" was removed (The Moscow Times, The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: museum employees told Sever.Realii the ban cited pornographic content, reported by Meduza, which did not confirm it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism of the institution; the main case is the 2023 Novikov censorship.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "'Cancelled' St Petersburg show honouring Timur Novikov finally opens, with censorship" (2023)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2023/08/15/st-petersburg-show-honouring-timur-novikov-finally-openswith-censorship
- Partial censorship of the Novikov show is evidence that cultural restriction in Russia has intensified since the invasion of Ukraine.
- Former New Academy colleagues said works were altered or removed; the museum cited technical reasons.
- Describes Manilova as a Kremlin-connected official; she declined to comment.
2. Novaya Gazeta: editorial staff, "Zhenshchina-prazdnik na poroge Russkogo muzeya" (2023)
URL: https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2023/04/24/zhenshchina-prazdnik-na-poroge-russkogo-muzeia
- News outlet, not art press. Presents Manilova as a poor fit: not a museum professional, with a record of festival contracts. Anonymous staff called her candidacy "the worst of the possible" (translation).
- Unverified: allegations about contracts, from Novaya Gazeta, which cites court testimony and anonymous sources; Manilova is not quoted.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-run and its only named sponsor is VTB, a state-owned bank under sanctions from several governments. The director is on Canada's sanctions list, and the 2023 Novikov episode is documented censorship. The museum publishes no accounts or board, so the scale of state and sponsor money cannot be checked.
