VAN SCHIJNDEL HOUSE: Utrecht, Netherlands
ID: van-schijndel-house
Website: https://www.iconichouses.org/specials/mart-van-schijndel/visit-mvs (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- House at Pieterskerkhof 8 in the old centre of Utrecht, built on an inner courtyard behind the houses and gardens of the Pieterskerkhof and the Kromme Nieuwegracht, and largely hidden from the street (DUIC, 2016; Iconic Houses).
- The architect Mart van Schijndel (1943 to 1999) bought the former Stichtse Glashandel in 1988, built the house inside the walls of its old business hall from 1992 and finished it in 1993 (DUIC, 2016; Iconic Houses). He designed it for himself and lived there with his wife, the architectural historian Natascha Drabbe, until his death on 30 September 1999 (DUIC, 2024).
- He turned the 1939 gatehouse in front, designed by J.F. Berghoef as a garage with living space, into four postmodern apartments with a pink-and-white striped front (DUIC, 2016 and 2024).
- Inside, a triangular living hall sits between two patios, with glass facades partly of sandblasted glass and doors that hinge on silicone sealant; Van Schijndel designed most of the furniture. It won Utrecht's Rietveld Prize in 1995 and became a municipal monument in 1999 (DUIC, 2016; Iconic Houses).
- In March 2026 the house was on the market for EUR 1,900,000 (The Spaces, 5 March 2026). The sales page hosted by Iconic Houses now gives EUR 1,795,000 through Burgersdijk Makelaars Utrecht and says the architect's partner, who has lived there since 1992, is selling it (Iconic Houses).
- Visiting: tours on the first Sunday of each month by appointment, up to 10 people, for a donation of EUR 45 with coffee or tea; students, Docomomo members, friends of the foundation and Iconic Houses members pay EUR 22.50. Private tours on other days need a donation of at least EUR 350 (Iconic Houses).

FUNDING
Van Schijndel House: self-funded: tour donations and friends' gifts to a volunteer-run foundation in a privately lived-in house
- Van Schijndel financed the build in 1992 by turning the gatehouse into four apartments (DUIC, 2016).
- The Mart van Schijndel Foundation, set up in 2008 to manage and spread knowledge of his work, opens the house for tours, holds lectures and concerts, and sells the monograph on the house (foundation brochure, 2009; Iconic Houses).
- Tour donations go to the foundation, which Iconic Houses calls a volunteer-run non-profit whose support pays for programming and the preservation of the house.
- Friends' scheme: from EUR 20 a year in 2009, with the book for gifts of EUR 50 and invitations to friends' gatherings for EUR 100 or more, described as support for the upkeep of the house (foundation brochure, 2009).
- Natascha Drabbe opens it to visitors (DUIC, 2024); she founded the Iconic Houses network, which hosts the foundation's visiting page (DUIC, 2016; Iconic Houses).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the house is small, private and clean, and no funder belongs to a flagged industry. Worth watching: whether the tours continue if the house changes hands.
