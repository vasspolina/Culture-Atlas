THE ROOMS: St. John's, Canada
ID: the-rooms-st-johns
Website: https://www.therooms.ca/ (checked 2026-10-24; the admission page opened; /about and /visit returned "not found")

OVERVIEW
- Provincial museum, art gallery and archives in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, with three regional museums: Demasduit in Grand Falls-Windsor, the Provincial Seamen's Museum in Grand Bank and the Labrador Interpretation Centre in North West River (annual report 2024-25).
- Opened on 29 June 2005, combining the Art Gallery of Newfoundland and Labrador, the Provincial Archives and the Provincial Museum (Wikipedia). It is a Category 1 Crown Corporation of the provincial government (annual report).
- The name is not explained on the pages opened.
- Built on the site of Fort Townshend, an 18th-century fort designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1951; archaeologists and historians opposed the location (Wikipedia). The fort's outline is marked in granite pavers; the remains largely lie under the building.
- Galleries, archives reading room, a cafe on Level 4 and school programmes; 150,324 visitors in 2024-25 (annual report).
- Admission $13.80 adults, $8.97 seniors 60+, students and Canadian military, $6.90 youth 6 to 17, free for children 5 and under and for Indigenous Peoples; free on Friday for students and 5 to 9pm on the first Wednesday of each month. Hours and accessibility details could not be read on the pages opened.

FUNDING
The Rooms: mainly public: provincial government, with federal grants, corporate sponsors and earned income
- Annual report 2024-25 (year to 31 March 2025): total revenue $11,375,873 and expenses $11,630,927, a deficit of $255,054. Provincial operating grants were $7,362,409, with $779,213 for employee benefits and a $239,971 capital grant. Commercial operations earned $2,191,649. The province committed $1M a year for five years for capital upgrades from 2024-25.
- Federal: the Canada Council for the Arts gave $250,000 in 2024-25 as part of a grant first awarded in 2019 ($945,000 over four years); Young Canada Works gave $37,757.
- Corporate sponsorship was $347,367 in 2024-25: Chevron Canada $185,000, BMO $100,000 and Cenovus Energy $20,000. BMO's $100,000 was the final instalment of a $1M pledge made over ten years for the First World War campaign.
- Chevron funded the school programme from 2009: the original 2008 agreement was more than $375,000 over three years (Government of Newfoundland and Labrador release). The 2024-25 report says Chevron completed its commitment and the programme, Chevron Open Minds, ended in June 2025 and became Curious Minds.
- Elinor Gill Ratcliffe gave $250,000 to buy a sculpture by Billy Gauthier. The International Grenfell Association gave $490,000 in total.

Red flags: oil and gas sponsors of school programmes
- Chevron Canada paid for the school programme from 2009 to June 2025 (annual report 2024-25).
- Cenovus Energy gave $20,000 for the Celebrating Diversity Through Identity Edukit in 2024-25.
- In March 2025 the Rooms asked BP Canada Energy Group for $30,000 for workshops in Curious Minds (annual report).
- No reporting found that the Chevron sponsorship ended under pressure; the report gives no reason.

Red flags: Innu exhibit dispute with the provincial government
- The Innu Pakassiun exhibit, assembled by Innu over several years, was cancelled in June 2026. Jodie Ashini of the Innu Nation said executives of The Rooms relayed a provincial request that it leave out an Innu timeline or the link between stone tools and Innu; the Innu cancelled rather than change it (Canadian Press, 26 June 2026).
- Premier Tony Wakeham apologised for matters related to the exhibit and committed to free Indigenous cultural expression at provincial institutions (Canadian Press).
- Unverified: that the provincial government asked for the changes, from the Innu Nation, a party to the dispute. The Canadian Press and The Independent report no response from The Rooms.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found. The criticism is in news reporting on the 2026 Innu exhibit.
1. The Canadian Press: "Archeologists accuse N.L. government of 'colonial logic' in dispute with Innu" (2026)
URL: https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/06/26/archeologists-accuse-nl-government-of-colonial-logic-in-dispute-with-innu/
- Seven Memorial University archaeologists called the province's theory that Innu arrived 300 years ago "colonialist logic" and said it dismisses oral history.
- Reports no response from The Rooms.
2. The Independent: Heidi Atter, article on the Provincial Archaeology Office's theory (2026)
URL: https://theindependent.ca/news/inside-the-provincial-archaeology-offices-theory-that-europeans-inhabited-labrador-before-innu/
- Innu Grand Chief Simon Pokue said the position "amounts to the erasure of Innu history". Quotes no statement from The Rooms.

BOTTOM LINE
The Rooms is mostly provincial money, plus a small share of oil-company sponsorship: Chevron through June 2025, and Cenovus, with a BP Canada request. The weak points are those school-programme sponsors and the 2026 cancellation of an Innu exhibit after a timeline dispute. Watch the Curious Minds funding and the outcome of the Innu talks.
