FIRSTSITE: Colchester, UK
ID: firstsite-colchester
Website: https://firstsite.uk

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and cinema in Lewis Gardens, High Street, Colchester CO1 1JH (Firstsite).
- Established in 1993 and in its current building since 2011; the site says over 1 million visitors have come since the move (Firstsite).
- Wikipedia says the building was designed by Rafael Viñoly, cost £25,542,701 to build, and that Colchester Council owns the freehold.
- The Berryfield Mosaic, dating to AD200, is set into the floor, and the building has a 190-seat cinema (Firstsite).
- Programmes: exhibitions, films, workshops, events and community outreach (Firstsite).
- Open Tuesday to Wednesday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday to Saturday 10:00 to 22:00, Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; admission is free (Firstsite).
- The 2026 exhibition "Duncan Grant: Worlds Through Desire: From Drawing to Cruising to DMs" (11 July to 8 November 2026) carries a content warning for nudity, keeps sexually explicit works in a separate room for visitors aged 18 and over, and is free to enter (Firstsite).

FUNDING
Firstsite: mainly public with charitable trusts: Arts Council England, Colchester Borough Council and Essex County Council, plus Garfield Weston Foundation and Essex Community Foundation
- Firstsite's thank-you page lists as core funders Arts Council England, Colchester Borough Council and Essex County Council. It lists as programme supporters Trevor Fenwick, Coode-Adams Firstsite Trust, John Talbot and the Firstsite Collector's Group; as project supporters Essex Community Foundation, Garfield Weston and the East Anglian Art Fund; and as a corporate supporter Attwells Solicitors.
- 360Giving data (GrantNav) records 37 grants to Firstsite totalling £6,440,723 between 14 April 2005 and 5 March 2026: Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport £5,760,878 (10 grants), Essex Community Foundation £226,354 (16), National Lottery Heritage Fund £106,300 (2), Esmée Fairbairn Foundation £100,000 (1), Foyle Foundation £100,000 (1), Garfield Weston Foundation £80,000 (2), Youth Music £47,684 (2) and National Lottery Community Fund £19,507 (3).
- Arts Council England awarded £815,000 a year for 2015/16 to 2018/19. In February 2015 it expressed concern about the centre's viability and moved to a one-year grant; in June 2017 it reinstated two years of funding from April 2018 (The Art Newspaper, 2018). Wikipedia gives the annual subsidy as £814,512 for four years from 2018.
- Bloomberg Philanthropies: Firstsite was selected in January 2025 as one of 200 non-profit cultural organisations in the US and UK for the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator programme, aimed at upgrading its digital tools and website, digital fundraising and online shop (Firstsite, 30 January 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece, a 2018 news report on a conflict of interest at the board.
1. The Art Newspaper: Martin Bailey, "Conflict-of-interest scandal at publicly funded UK arts centre Firstsite" (20 July 2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/07/20/conflict-of-interest-scandal-at-publicly-funded-uk-arts-centre-firstsite
- Firstsite paid £91,040 between September 2015 and November 2016 to Friary West, a Maldon human-resources consultancy. Chairman Noorzaman Rashid (appointed July 2015) and trustee Robert Surman (trustee since September 2015) both became directors of Friary West in January 2016, while Firstsite was paying the firm.
- Both resigned on 16 March 2018. Firstsite appointed Mazars to review the matter in March 2018; its report, submitted in June 2018, found that both men had declared their interests and said they received no financial reward from the company, and recommended that Firstsite review its procurement policy and define criteria for selecting suppliers. Neither man responded to requests for comment.
- The Charity Commission called the payments "a serious incident." Arts Council England reinstated two years of funding in June 2017, starting April 2018.
- Names Firstsite, its chairman and its trustee.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 more than 260 British artists published an open letter on the Artists for Palestine UK platform urging the National Theatre, Royal Shakespeare Company, Young Vic, Royal Court and Almeida to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies, saying the partnerships made the theatres "complicit in grave war crimes" through the foundation's involvement with illegal Israeli settlements (The New Arab, "Over 260 UK artists urge theatres to end ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies over Israel", The New Arab Staff, 29 May 2026). The letter does not name Firstsite.
- The connection: Firstsite announced on 30 January 2025 that it had been selected for the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator programme (Firstsite).

BOTTOM LINE
Firstsite's core funding is public: Arts Council England, Colchester Borough Council and Essex County Council, with the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport the largest funder in the 360Giving record. Charitable trusts add smaller sums, and the documented governance episode is the 2018 payments to a consultancy tied to its chairman and a trustee.
