BRÜCKE-MUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: brucke-museum
Website: https://www.bruecke-museum.de/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the Brücke expressionist group at Bussardsteig 9 in Dahlem, Berlin (museum website).
- Karl Schmidt-Rottluff initiated it in 1964 with a donation to the State of Berlin; it opened on 15 September 1967 in a building by Werner Düttmann and has always been run by the Berlin culture department (museum website).
- Founding gifts: Schmidt-Rottluff gave 75 works in 1964 and kept adding until his death in 1976; Erich Heckel arranged a gift of more than 900 works in 1966 (museum website; Proveana project record).
- Collection: about 5,000 works entered after the museum was founded, 2,937 of them made before 1945 (Proveana project record).
- Schmidt-Rottluff's estate, held by the Karl und Emy Schmidt-Rottluff Stiftung, includes 87 paintings, more than a thousand sculptures, applied art and works on paper, archives and about 100 objects from colonial contexts (museum website).
- Directors: Leopold Reidemeister from 1967 until his death in 1987, then Eberhard Roters as provisional director and Magdalena M. Moeller from 1988; Lisa Marei Schmidt since October 2017 (museum website).
- Current exhibition: "Swedish Modernism", works from the Moderna Museet in Stockholm, 9 November 2026 to 28 February 2027 (museum website).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Monday 11 am to 5 pm; 6 euros, reduced 4 euros, annual ticket 25 euros; step-free entry by a ramp, with accessible toilets in the neighbouring Kunsthaus Dahlem (museum website).

FUNDING
Brücke-Museum: public: the State of Berlin, with help from two private foundations and a friends' association
- State of Berlin: the museum has its own chapter (0812) in the Berlin budget. For 2026 it plans 2,038,200 euros of spending and 286,000 euros of own income, leaving 1,752,200 euros for the State to cover; spending is 5.9 percent below the 2025 figure of 2,165,300 euros (Berlin budget plan 2026/2027, Einzelplan 08).
- In 2024 the museum actually spent 3,165,645 euros and took in 1,258,730 euros, including 1,060,890 euros of grants and donations: 500,000 euros each from the Kulturstiftung der Länder and the Ernst von Siemens foundation towards compensation for a Kirchner painting, and 60,000 euros from the Ferdinand Möller Stiftung for a curator's post (same budget plan).
- The Senate funded the museum's project to decolonise its work, confirmed in 2021 (taz, 5 March 2021), and funds the online collection (museum website).
- Karl und Emy Schmidt-Rottluff Stiftung: income from its assets pays for publications and acquisitions; its four-member board is Gerold Dahl of Deutsche Bank, the business economist Thomas Erb, Christine Regus of the Senate's museums division and the museum director (museum website).
- Ferdinand Möller Stiftung: pays for the museum's communication and registrar posts (museum website, team page).
- Förderverein Brücke-Museum e.V.: founded in 1968, it pays towards purchases, exhibitions and research, and since 2020 lends the museum Erich Heckel's painting "Brandstätte" (museum website).
- Provenance research on 58 works from November 2018 to October 2019 found nine with gaps between 1933 and 1945 judged not suspicious and two judged suspicious and kept under review (Proveana project record).

Red flags: colonial-context objects in Schmidt-Rottluff's estate, reappraised since 2021
- Since January 2021 the museum has worked on the critical reappraisal and digitisation of the roughly 100 objects, with experts from the regions of origin and decolonial activists, and publishes images and data on Wikimedia Commons; some come from Germany's former colonies (museum website).
- taz reported that where Schmidt-Rottluff obtained the objects is largely unknown; the director said about 20 regions of origin had been identified; the museum was in contact with a Cameroonian-German research project led by Bénédicte Savoy and Albert Gouaffo (taz, 5 March 2021).
- Director Lisa Marei Schmidt told taz that colonialism had been "vollkommen vernachlässigt" (completely neglected, own translation) in expressionism research.
- The curators Dorthe Aagesen and Beatrice von Bormann, quoted by taz, said Brücke artists depicted colonial objects with a "karikaturistischen oder rassischen Beigeschmack" (a caricaturing or racial undertone, own translation).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ferdinand Möller (1882 to 1956), whose daughter set up the Ferdinand Möller Stiftung in 1995 with the dealer Wolfgang Wittrock, dealt in "degenerate art" seized from German museums under the Nazi regime and sold such works to German collectors until 1943 (Berlinische Galerie).
- The Ferdinand Möller Stiftung named under FUNDING carries his name.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Berlin state money with modest private support. Worth watching is where the provenance work on the colonial and Nazi-era holdings leads.
