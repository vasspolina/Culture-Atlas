MUSEO NACIONAL DE ARTES VISUALES: Montevideo, Uruguay
ID: museo-nacional-de-artes-visuales
Website: https://mnav.gub.uy/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads, lists an exhibition running to 15 November 2026)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at Tomás Giribaldi 2283, corner of Julio Herrera y Reissig, in Parque Rodó, Montevideo.
- Created by Law 3,932 of 10 December 1911 as the Museo de Bellas Artes (the Spanish Wikipedia gives the name Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes); it later became the Museo Nacional de Artes Visuales. Its first year was in the left wing of the Teatro Solís.
- Its original collection was 234 pieces, mostly foreign. Its collection traces back to the Museo y Biblioteca Nacional, founded on 18 July 1838 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a pavilion put up in the late 19th century for a Hygiene Exhibition. The museum closed for almost eleven years (1952 to 1962). The Argentine architect and artist Clorindo Testa led the last major transformation, from the 1970s. The front garden was designed in the 1990s by the landscape architect Leandro Silva Delgado.
- On site: temporary and permanent collection galleries, a specialised library of more than 8,000 volumes, a 174-seat auditorium, a video department and storage depots. The museum's page gives both "more than 7,000" and "about 6,500" works; Wikipedia says about 6,500, mostly by Uruguayan artists including Torres García, Figari and Blanes.
- Directors: Ángel Kalenberg 1969 to 2007, Enrique Aguerre 2010 to 2025, Roxana Fabius since 28 August 2025 (Ministry of Education and Culture).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 13:00 to 20:00; admission is free. No accessibility information found on the museum's site.

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Artes Visuales: mainly public: the Uruguayan state, through the Ministry of Education and Culture
- The museum's own page states that its division depends on the Dirección Nacional de Cultura of the Ministry of Education and Culture, and that its annual objectives, tasks and budget are organised under it. Wikipedia likewise says the ministry administers it.
- No reporting found of a budget figure for the museum. Neither the El Observador interview with Fabius (undated on the page; given after her 28 August 2025 start) nor the ministry's note on the August 2025 handover gives one.
- Fabius told El Observador that some building changes need resources the museum does not have "por el momento" (translation: "for now").
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, a friends association or a donor list. The museum's site names none.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Uruguayan state money, with no private funder or sponsor named in any source found. The weak point is opacity: no museum accounts, funder list or board are published, and the budget is not reported. Worth watching are the building changes Fabius said need resources the museum lacks.
