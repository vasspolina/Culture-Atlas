HAUS DER KULTUREN DER WELT (HKW) – Berlin, Germany
Website: https://www.hkw.de (not checked 2026-10-20: fetch blocked by a permission prompt that timed out)

OVERVIEW
- Venue for contemporary art and culture in the former Kongresshalle, which the United States gave to the city. Hugh Stubbins designed it in 1957 for the Interbau exhibition; John F. Kennedy spoke there in 1963; the roof collapsed on 21 May 1980, killing one person; rebuilt and reopened 1987 (Wikipedia).
- Address John-Foster-Dulles-Allee 10. The building is rented from the Federal Real Estate Agency (BImA); about 9,205 sq m; 148 staff on average in 2024 (KBB environmental statement 2025).
- Formerly Haus der Kulturen der Welt GmbH; merged into KBB – Kulturveranstaltungen des Bundes in Berlin GmbH from 2002 (KBB history page).
- Director, opening hours, admission and accessibility: not retrieved this run.

FUNDING
HKW: mainly public (federal).
- Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (BKM) and the Federal Foreign Office (Auswärtiges Amt) support HKW as one of the federally funded "lighthouses of culture" (Wikipedia).
- KBB, its operator, has the Federal Republic of Germany as sole shareholder and is funded by BKM (KBB environmental statement 2025, stated by the operator).
- The federal government fully finances KBB, including HKW (Berlin Senate culture page, official).
- No separate HKW budget found; KBB reports €18.8M event revenue for 2024 across all its venues.
- No reporting found this run of private sponsors or partners (the partner page could not be retrieved).

BOTTOM LINE
HKW is a federally owned and funded venue run through KBB, with support from BKM and the Foreign Office; no private funders of concern turned up in the sources retrieved. This run was limited (no press search possible), so programme controversies were not checked; the €18M figure in the existing record could not be confirmed.
