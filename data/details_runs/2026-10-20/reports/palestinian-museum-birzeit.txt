THE PALESTINIAN MUSEUM: Birzeit, Palestine
ID: palestinian-museum-birzeit
Website: https://www.palmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Palestinian history, society and culture in Birzeit, on a large site next to Birzeit University (Masri Foundation; Wikipedia).
- Began as a project of the London-based Welfare Association (Taawon), which Wikipedia says initiated the idea in 1997, first to commemorate the Nakba. The building opened on 18 May 2016, inaugurated by Mahmoud Abbas, without an exhibition or collection (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 17 May 2016; Wikipedia). The first exhibition, "Jerusalem Lives", opened on 26 August 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Became administratively independent from Taawon in 2021, registered with the Palestinian Ministry of Interior as an independent non-governmental association (Masri Foundation).
- Building: designed by heneghan peng architects of Dublin on a 40,000 square metre hillside of dry-stone agricultural terraces, clad in limestone quarried near Bethlehem, with design in 2012 to 2013 and completion in 2016 (Aga Khan Development Network). It has 3,500 square metres of exhibition and educational space on a plot leased long term from Birzeit University (The Art Newspaper). It won the 2019 Aga Khan Award for Architecture (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include an online archive of 419 collections and 363,574 digitised items as of March 2025, and exhibitions and education work for Palestinians in Palestine and the diaspora (Wikipedia; Masri Foundation).
- Directors: Jack Persekian until his resignation in December 2015, Mahmoud Hawari from 2016, Adila Laidi-Hanieh from 2018 to 2023, and Amer Shomali as General Director from October 2023 (Wikipedia; ArtAsiaPacific).

FUNDING
The Palestinian Museum: privately funded: Palestinian families and institutions, the Qattan family, Bank of Palestine and foundations
- Building cost: US$30 million per The Art Newspaper, US$24 million per ArtAsiaPacific (Sylvia Tsai, 20 May 2016); Wikipedia gives an estimate of US$24 to 30 million.
- Donors: about US$25 million had been raised by May 2016 from individuals and institutions, including the Al-Qattan family, the Bank of Palestine, and the construction companies Projacs and Consolidated Contractors Company (The Art Newspaper, 17 May 2016). In December 2015 The Art Newspaper (Gareth Harris, 11 December 2015) cited The Economist's figure of about US$21 million raised from more than 30 Palestinian individuals and institutions, and said the Welfare Association (Taawon) had funded the museum's capital and operational costs.
- Wikipedia lists funds from "over 30 private Palestinian families and institutions", including the A. M. Qattan Foundation, the Bank of Palestine and the Arab Fund for Social and Economic Development.
- The Munib and Angela Masri Foundation states that it contributed to supporting the establishment of the museum.
- In 2022 the Aliph Foundation gave 480,000 US dollars for a textile conservation studio at the museum, with the Victoria and Albert Museum (Hyperallergic, Valentina Di Liscia, 13 March 2022; Wikipedia).
- Omar Al-Qattan, chairman of the A. M. Qattan Foundation, led the museum as board chairman and acting director in 2016 (The Art Newspaper; ArtAsiaPacific).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Press criticism concerns the board's handling of the first director and the opening without exhibitions.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Jack Persekian steps down as director of new Palestinian Museum" (11 December 2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/12/11/jack-persekian-steps-down-as-director-of-new-palestinian-museum
- Chairman Omar Al-Qattan said Persekian "resigned as a result of differences over planning and management issues"; Persekian declined to comment.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: Sylvia Tsai, "Palestinian Museum Opens Despite Management Disputes" (20 May 2016)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/palestinian-museum-opens-despite-management-disputes
- Reports that Persekian resigned in December 2015 after disagreements with board members and never officially disclosed the cause; reports described differing visions, with more conservative leaders wanting the museum to commemorate those affected by the Palestinian Exodus while Persekian wanted it also to address contemporary Palestine.
- Quotes Persekian telling openDemocracy that the museum should not be "merely acting as a national museum to vindicate what the authorities want". Names no external critics.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is privately funded: the building was paid for by Palestinian families and institutions, with the Qattan family, the Bank of Palestine and the Arab Fund named, and it has been independent of Taawon since 2021. The documented disputes concern the 2015 departure of the first director and the opening without an exhibition.
