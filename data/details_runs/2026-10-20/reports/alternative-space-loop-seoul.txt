ALTERNATIVE SPACE LOOP: Seoul, South Korea
ID: alternative-space-loop-seoul
Website: http://altspaceloop.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art space in Seoul. E-flux notices from 2024 and 2026 give its address as 3rd Floor, 172-1 Cheonggyecheon-ro, Jung District. K-ARTNOW (23 December 2025) reports a move to the first floor of the same address, along the Cheonggyecheon stream, and a reopening on 16 December 2025.
- Opened in 1999 in the Hongdae area of Seoul and is described as Korea's first alternative space (e-flux directory). K-ARTNOW says it was founded in Mapo-gu, Seoul.
- Became a non-profit organisation in 2019 for its 20th anniversary; it has nine board members and eighty general members (e-flux directory).
- Director Ji Yoon Yang joined as a curator in 2006 and became director in autumn 2017 (Whitewall, Katy Donoghue, 20 November 2024).
- Programme: exhibitions, an annual open call for artists and independent curators, and research and workshops; its 2026 to 2027 mission statement says it will challenge the financialisation of art and use feminist and communal approaches (K-ARTNOW).
- Visiting: Monday to Sunday, 10 am to 7 pm; activities are free and open to everyone (e-flux).

FUNDING
Alternative Space LOOP: member-funded non-profit
- Director Yang says board members and other members sponsor LOOP monthly, which she says makes it more stable; she calls it a middle-sized organisation with a stronger public role than at the start (Whitewall, 2024).

Red flags: public questionnaire over the Euljiro move
- On 15 December 2025 the Cheonggyecheon-Euljiro Preservation Alliance, Listen to the City and the Feminist Designer Social Club posted an open questionnaire on Instagram asking LOOP to answer publicly (K-ARTNOW, 23 December 2025). The questionnaire was addressed to LOOP.
- The groups say Sanlim-dong, where LOOP moved, is one of the few remaining metal manufacturing clusters in Euljiro and faces redevelopment and gentrification, and that cultural spaces can speed up gentrification.
- They ask for LOOP's reasons for moving, its consideration for local residents and the existing trades, and its stance on gentrification.
- In its 2026 to 2027 mission statement LOOP pledges not to use Euljiro's workshops, labour, housing or redevelopment tensions as "cultural material" (stated by the space, via K-ARTNOW).

- Not criticism: an interview of 20 November 2024 in which Yang describes how the 2008 financial crisis forced LOOP to reshape nearly everything to survive, and how the pandemic closed it.

BOTTOM LINE
LOOP is a small non-profit sustained by monthly contributions from board members and other members. Its weak point is the public challenge over its December 2025 move to Euljiro. Worth watching: LOOP's answer to the December 2025 questionnaire about gentrification in Euljiro.
