BANGKOK ART BIENNALE: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: bangkok-art-biennale-bangkok
Website: https://www.bkkartbiennale.com (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale held across Bangkok temples, museums, malls and public spaces over about four months. The 2026 edition, "Angels and Mara", runs 29 October 2026 to 28 February 2027 (Tourism Authority of Thailand).
- Run by the Bangkok Art Biennale Foundation, which says it was founded in 2017. Its page lists as board members Thapana Sirivadhanabhakdi, Group CEO of ThaiBev, and Prof. Dr. Apinan Poshyananda, chief executive and artistic director and a former Acting Minister of Culture. The first biennale was held in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the city.
- Its venues page lists 11 venues, including Wat Pho, Wat Arun, the National Museum, the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC), One Bangkok and CentralWorld.
- Collateral event of the 61st International Art Exhibition in Venice: "The Spirits of Maritime Crossing", at Palazzo Rocca Contarini Corfù, on view until 2 August 2026 (BAB).
- Visiting: no biennale-wide ticket prices on its venues page, which notes entrance fees at Wat Pho, Wat Arun and the National Museum.

FUNDING
Bangkok Art Biennale: corporate-sponsored: ThaiBev and its group, with Bangkok city and the Tourism Authority of Thailand
- ThaiBev has been the major supporter since 2018 (BAB Foundation page). Its chief executive Thapana is a co-founder and pledged support for the 2020 and 2022 editions (The Nation, 12 July 2017).
- 2018: Bt150 million in seed funding; backers were ThaiBev and the mall operators Central Group and Siam Piwat, with City Hall, the Tourism Authority of Thailand and the Thailand Convention and Exhibition Bureau (The Nation). Artribune (Umberta Genta, 8 November 2018, citing The Art Newspaper) gave company donations of $67 million, partly in kind such as free hotel rooms; the figure is not reconciled with The Nation's.
- ThaiBev owns OneBangkok and the Queen Sirikit National Convention Centre; both are used as venues (Cornwel-Smith, Koktail, 2025). ThaiBev is an alcohol and beverage group: the Sirivadhanabhakdi family holds about 30% and its subsidiaries include Fraser and Neave and Sabeco (Wikipedia).
- Venice 2026 show: "supported by ThaiBev and One Bangkok" (BAB). The Tourism Authority of Thailand supports BAB 2026 with promotion. No reporting found, in pages opened, of BEM, Ch. Karnchang or Generali as supporters.
- Koktail (Cornwel-Smith, 2025) says BAB5 "is already funded". No reporting found of accounts or amounts for 2026.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism of the sponsor's influence. Reviewers in ArtReview and e-flux argue the biennale is tied to ThaiBev's property interests and to the state.
1. e-flux Criticism: Max Crosbie-Jones, "Bangkok Art Biennale 2022, 'CHAOS : CALM'" (2022)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/508133/bangkok-art-biennale-2022-chaos-calm
- Links ThaiBev's "ties with the state apparatus" and a government with "fervently royalist and dictatorial instincts" to the exclusion of antagonistic art activism, and says the curators glossed over the intimidation of prisoners of conscience, most of them young adults facing lèse-majesté charges.
- Says the city route is designed to acclimatise viewers to ThaiBev's "questionable paradigm of sustainable progress"; most works at three temples are "photogenic decoys" indifferent to place.
2. ArtReview: Max Crosbie-Jones, "'Why Are They Here?'" (2021)
URL: https://artreview.com/why-are-they-here-bangkok-art-biennale-escape-routes-review/
- Says a sister company of the sponsor is building One Bangkok, and cites ThaiBev's "symbiotic ties to the palace, government and military".
- Says Apinan, questioned about the suppression of student protests, showed "careful defensiveness".
3. ArtReview: Max Crosbie-Jones, "Bangkok Art Biennale 2024 Review: What, Exactly, Is Being Nurtured?" (2024)
URL: https://artreview.com/bangkok-art-biennale-2024-nurture-gaia-review-max-crosbie-jones/
- Opening week coincided with the launch of One Bangkok, built by the sponsor's sister company; calls the biennale business-as-usual.
- Calls "sustainability" a One Bangkok "buzzword"; says the event's environmental footprint is glossed over.
4. Koktail: Philip Cornwel-Smith, "Is Bangkok Art Biennale a success?" (2025)
URL: https://www.koktailmagazine.com/2025/02/03/is-bangkok-art-biennale-a-success/
- Reports a panel on sponsor influence; asked about censorship, Apinan pointed to political works shown, including a BAB4 artist who critiqued ThaiBev.
- Not an art-press outlet; some artists worry corporate caution affects their work.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2025 the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre, a BAB venue, altered the show "Constellation of Complicity" after pressure from Chinese Embassy officials (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan, 8 August 2025). Bangkok's Metropolitan Authority was the exhibition's main supporter.
- The 2025 censorship was not at the biennale, and the Hyperallergic article does not mention it. City Hall supported the biennale in 2018 (The Nation).

BOTTOM LINE
The biennale is paid for by one beverage group whose property arm also hosts it, with city and tourism-authority backing. No flagged funder was found; ThaiBev's business is alcohol and beverages. The weak points are the sponsor's control of venues and the lack of published accounts. Worth watching: how the 2026 edition is funded and whether political work is shown.
