ARMENIAN CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY EXPERIMENTAL ART (ACCEA/NPAK): Yerevan, Armenia
ID: armenian-center-for-contemporary-experimental-art-accea-npak-yerevan
Website: https://www.accea.info/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at Pavstos Buzand 1/3, Yerevan, in a 1,823 square metre building (ACCEA); Wikipedia places it in walking distance of Republic Square.
- Founded by Sonia Balassanian, Edward Balassanian and Arne Z. Balassanian (ACCEA About page). The founding year differs by source: the About page says activities began in 1992 and that ACCEA and NPAK were incorporated in 1995 as a New York 501(c)(3) non-profit, with a charitable foundation in Yerevan; Mirror-Spectator (2022) and Wikipedia give 1992 as the founding year; a biography of Edward Balassanian on the centre's site says he and Sonia Balassanian co-founded it in 1995.
- The facility was formally inaugurated on 25 April 1996 with a group exhibition of Armenian avant-garde artists (ACCEA).
- Building: a Government of Armenia decree of 22 February 2002 provided the building free of charge for an unlimited period (ACCEA). The site holds five exhibition spaces including a 552 square metre Grand Hall, and a small theatre of 130 seats (ACCEA).
- A bronze cast of Yervand Kotchar's "Melancholy" stands at the entrance, a project sponsored by Rouben H. Avanessian and begun in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- ACCEA/NPAK organised the Armenian Pavilion at the Venice Biennale, which the Ministry of Culture entrusted to it; Wikipedia gives 1995 to 2009 (ACCEA; Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Armenian Center for Contemporary Experimental Art: diaspora-funded: family funds and foundations, with a free building from the Armenian state
- The site lists major benefactors as at 5 February 2022: the Sonia and Edward Balassanian Fund, the Cafesjian Museum Foundation, the Boghossian Foundation, Rouben Avanessian, Carolyn Mugar, the JHM Charitable Foundation, and the Hirair and Anna Hovnanian Fund (ACCEA About page).
- State support is the building, provided free since 2002 (ACCEA).
- Venice pavilions: a Diaspora Council of Supporters of the Armenian Pavilion was formed in the United States for each Biennale from 1995 to raise the costs, so that the pavilion was not "any financial burden on the national budget of Armenia" (ACCEA, stated by the centre). Council chairs named include Joan Agajanian Quinn (1999, 2001, 2003) and Sonia Balassanian (2005, 2009).
- Edward Balassanian was Acting Executive Director of the Cafesjian Museum Foundation, one of the benefactors, from June 2002 through February 2009, and the centre's chief executive from 1992 to 2024 (ACCEA biography page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is positive; the one art-press piece on the centre is a favourable exhibition review.
1. Mirror-Spectator: Christopher Atamian, "'Hrant Dink: Here and Now' NPAK Celebrates Armenian Hero" (11 August 2022)
URL: https://mirrorspectator.com/?p=44088
- Not criticism of the centre: a positive review of the exhibition (7 May to 11 September 2022), calling it "a well-curated and thorough overview" of Dink's life and work.
- Its one reservation is that a similar corner on memory, justice and minority rights in Armenia would be welcome.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is diaspora and family philanthropy plus a free state-provided building; none of the named benefactors belongs to a flagged industry. The weak points are Edward Balassanian's former post at the Cafesjian Museum Foundation, which is also a benefactor, and the centre's own pages giving both 1992 and 1995 as the founding year.
