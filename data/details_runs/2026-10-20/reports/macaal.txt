MACAAL: Marrakech, Morocco
ID: macaal
Website: https://macaal.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of African Contemporary Art Al Maaden (MACAAL, in French Musée d'Art Contemporain Africain Al Maaden) at Al Maaden, Sidi Youssef Ben Ali, Marrakech, on the other side of town from the Medina (macaal.org; The Art Newspaper).
- Founded by the private collector Othman Lazraq with his father Alami Lazraq; the Art Newspaper dates its opening to 2018, Euronews and The National say it was created in 2016. Its first exhibition, "Africa Is No Island", opened alongside the first Marrakech 1-54 art fair (Galerie, The Art Newspaper). Fondation Alliances runs it; Lazraq is president of both (The National).
- Named for the Al Maaden district where it sits.
- Building: designed by the French architect Didier Lefort, with geometric mosaics, local stone, terracotta and earth-toned façades and central courtyards with mashrabiya-style screens (The Art Newspaper). In 2018 it spread across two floors in a walled compound (Galerie).
- Closed in spring 2023 and reopened on 2 February 2025 after an 18-month transformation, with a new permanent exhibition, "Seven Contours, One Collection", of more than 150 works in seven galleries, plus a media library and an Artist Room (Euronews). The museum holds more than 2,000 works (The National).
- The collection began with Alami Lazraq more than 40 years ago and first focused on Moroccan modernist pioneers (Euronews). In October 2026 it shows Daoud Aoulad-Syad's "Le temps d'un battement de cil" (to 29 November 2026) and installations by Yassine Balbzioui and others (macaal.org).
- Visiting: macaal.org lists practical information but its pages read did not show hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
MACAAL: privately funded: the Lazraq family, through Fondation Alliances
- The museum is a project of Fondation Alliances (macaal.org). Euronews says Othman Lazraq established the foundation in 2009 and that the foundation gave significant support to the 2023 to 2025 overhaul; Art Basel (3 February 2025) says the foundation was created by his parents in 2009.
- Alami Lazraq is a real estate developer (Galerie); Lazraq links the museum to his father's real estate work (Art Basel). Financial Afrik reported on 15 April 2015 a second suspension of trading in the listed property company Alliances within ten days, requested by the Moroccan securities regulator CDVM after a protective seizure by a Holmarcom subsidiary against a company of Alami Lazrak; it wrote in the conditional that Alliances had negotiated staggered repayment of part of its debt with two banks. The article does not mention Fondation Alliances or MACAAL, and no opened page links this episode to the museum's money.
- Al-Monitor (6 October 2025) quotes Lazraq saying MACAAL "is no longer just a private initiative"; no details given.
- No reporting found of a state, corporate or foundation funder other than Fondation Alliances, of amounts, a donor list, a board or published accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No full critique found. Coverage is admiring, with two passing reservations about access.
1. The Art Newspaper: Tom Seymour, "After 2023 earthquake, Marrakech's pioneering art museum reopens" (2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/01/27/after-2023-earthquake-marrakech-pioneering-art-museum-reopens
- Not criticism of the museum: notes that its grand architecture can be a "psychological barrier" for local visitors, which artistic director Meriem Berrada acknowledges.
- Notes that the 1-54 fair is held at the luxury La Mamounia hotel and suggests it serves wealthy international visitors more than local communities.
2. Art Basel: Florence Derieux, interview with Othman Lazraq (2025)
URL: https://www.artbasel.com/stories/moroccan-collector-othman-lazraq-macaal-marrakech
- Not criticism: Lazraq says few Moroccans have visited a museum, so MACAAL has to move away from an elitist image; he calls the museum "a story of handing-down from father to son".
- Does not discuss funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is one family's, channelled through a foundation and tied to its property business; no flagged funder is named. The weak points are the lack of any published accounts, board or funding breakdown, and the 2015 trading suspensions at the family's property company, which Financial Afrik does not link to the museum. Worth watching: Lazraq's statement that MACAAL is "no longer just a private initiative" and any public funding that follows.
