BARBICAN ART GALLERY & THE CURVE: London, United Kingdom
ID: barbican-art-gallery-the-curve
Website: https://www.barbican.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- The Barbican Art Gallery (Level 3) and The Curve (Level G) are the visual arts spaces of the Barbican Centre, on Silk Street in the City of London, designed by Chamberlin, Powell and Bon in the Brutalist style and opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 3 March 1982 (Wikipedia).
- The Curve is a free gallery of site-responsive commissions; the current one is by Yuko Mohri until 24 January 2027. The centre cost £161 million, the City of London's gift to the nation (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Art Gallery is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm, until 8pm on Thursday and Friday, closed Mondays and bank holidays. The Curve is open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 7pm. The gallery admission price is not stated on the pages read; the Mohri show is free.

FUNDING
Barbican Art Gallery & The Curve: mainly public: the City of London Corporation, with Arts Council England and earned income
- The City of London Corporation calls itself "the founder and principal funder of the Barbican Centre" (Barbican website).
- 2022/23: City of London funding £18,991,000 (40% of income), Arts Council England £753,000, donations and other grants £2,295,000, total income £46,996,000 (Barbican season review finance pages).
- 2024/25 business plan: funding from the Corporation of £20,511,000 against £17,573,000 the year before (Barbican business plan, City of London committee papers).
- The Barbican says it must earn 60 per cent of its income itself; its support page names no sponsor or trust.
- No reporting found of an oil, arms or tobacco sponsor of the gallery.

Red flags: protest or boycott aimed at the Barbican
- Exhibit B (September 2014): journalist and activist Sara Myers started a change.org petition that gathered more than 8,000 signatures in two weeks against Brett Bailey's installation, staged at The Vaults with performers in chains and cages. The Barbican called off the five-day run, citing "the extreme nature of the protest" (Artlyst, 24 September 2014).
- Unravel (29 February 2024): collectors Lorenzo Legarda Leviste and Fahad Mayet removed two Loretta Pettway quilts from the show after the Barbican decided not to host a London Review of Books lecture series that included a talk by Pankaj Mishra. Leviste accused the Barbican of censorship (Hyperallergic).
- Barbican Stories (June 2021): the Barbican Stories Collective published a book of more than 100 accounts of discrimination dating back to 2014. Managing director Nicholas Kenyon was due to step down the next month, and the City of London Corporation commissioned an external review by Lewis Silkin LLP (The Art Newspaper, 10 August 2021).
- Open letter (reported 20 February 2026): more than 170 cultural figures asked the Barbican Board and the City of London Corporation to explain the departure of Devyani Saltzman, director for arts and participation, publish senior-leadership diversity data and commission an independent review of senior appointments and exits (ArtReview).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism in art media targets the centre's handling of race, speech and staffing, not its funding.
1. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Lenders Withdraw Works From Barbican Show, Alleging 'Censorship'" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/875873/lenders-withdraw-works-from-barbican-show-alleging-censorship/
- Two lenders pulled quilts from Unravel, calling the lecture cancellation censorship and repression of speech about Palestine.
- Leviste warned of a chilling effect on other UK arts institutions and noted the irony at an exhibition with anti-colonial themes.
- The Barbican said the lecture had been publicised prematurely and cited its hosting of the London Palestine Film Festival.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "London's Barbican shakes up staff following racism allegations published in tell-all book" (2021)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/barbican-staff-shake-up-following-racism-allegations
- Contributors to Barbican Stories describe the work culture as "subtle and insidiously" racist; one account says interns of colour were not usually hired long term while white interns were.
3. ArtReview: "Cultural figures sign open letter over departure of Barbican arts director" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/cultural-figures-sign-open-letter-over-departure-of-barbican-arts-director
- The letter questions commitment to global majority leadership and asks for diversity data and an independent review of senior appointments and exits.
4. Artlyst: "Barbican Installation Closed After Racism Accusation" (2014)
URL: https://artlyst.com/barbican-installation-closed-after-racism-accusation/
- Critics called Exhibit B offensive and racist; the Barbican said it confronted "the abhorrent historical attitudes to race during the colonial era", then cancelled it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly public: the City of London Corporation pays a large share of the budget, and no fossil fuel, arms or tobacco sponsor was found. The weak points are the pattern of protests and withdrawals over race and speech since 2014, and the dependence on one municipal funder. Worth watching is the Board's response to the 2026 open letter.
