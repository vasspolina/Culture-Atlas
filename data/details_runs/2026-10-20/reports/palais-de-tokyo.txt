PALAIS DE TOKYO: Paris, France
ID: palais-de-tokyo
Website: https://palaisdetokyo.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the west wing of the Palais de Tokyo in Paris; the French state owns the west wing and the City of Paris the east wing (Wikipedia).
- The building was inaugurated on 24 May 1937 and designed by André Aubert, Marcel Dastugue and Paul Viard. Nicolas Bourriaud and Jérôme Sans launched the "Site de création contemporaine" in March 2002 (Wikipedia).
- Anne Lacaton and Jean-Philippe Vassal overhauled and expanded it in 2012. Guillaume Désanges has been director since 2022 (Wikipedia).
- It is "reputed to be the biggest non-collecting contemporary art museum in Europe" (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 12:00 to 22:00. Full price 13 euros, reduced 9 euros (18 to 25, students, over 60); free for under 18, job seekers and people with disabilities and a companion (museum website).

FUNDING
Palais de Tokyo: mixed: French state plus corporate patrons
- The museum's home page says it "enjoys the annual support of" the French Republic, Richard Mille and Fondation Engie, shown as logos with no amounts or year. No reporting found of the state grant's size.
- Season partners: Revolut and Tessi. Exhibition partners include Havaianas, Vlisco and ADAGP (museum website).
- "Sustainable Palais" patrons: Richard Mille, Fondation Engie, Ruinart, Matmut pour les arts and Swiss Life (museum website). Benefactor members: Hopscotch, HBSS France, Peclerc Paris.
- No reporting found of amounts for any partner.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Fondation Engie)
- Fondation Engie is listed on the museum's supporters page "for inclusive outreach" and in the home page's annual-support line; neither page gives a year (museum website).
- Engie describes its gas business as covering "the whole gas chain"; natural gas made up 55% of its installed power capacity in 2018, the French state held 23.64% of its shares at 31 December 2018, and long-term Gazprom contracts accounted for about 20% of its gas sales and consumption in 2021 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gulf state body (Qatar 2020)
- "Notre monde brûle", a guest exhibition organised by Doha's state-run Mathaf, Arab Museum of Modern Art, was due to run from 21 February to 17 May 2020 as part of the Qatar-France 2020 Year of Culture (ArtAsiaPacific, 10 January 2020).
- Money: a Palais spokeswoman told The Art Newspaper (Nikita Dmitriev, 8 January 2020) that the Qatari government has never donated to the museum and that production costs are shared between the Palais and Mathaf. No reporting found of the amounts.
- Yves Michaud called the partnership an attempt to "bribe French society and soften its stance on human rights issues in the Persian Gulf region" (ArtAsiaPacific). The museum said on 9 January 2020 it would not cancel the show and that it partners with a museum, not a state.

Red flags: protest over a sponsor (La Poste, 2018)
- La Poste set up a temporary post office in the entrance hall, presented as an artwork by Sabrina Vitali. On 16 August 2018 about twenty people occupied the hall for barely half an hour, with a banner reading "La Poste finance des expos mais ferme ses bureaux !" (translation: La Poste funds exhibitions but closes its offices). La Poste's press service called it "une opération de mécénat entre deux institutions publiques" (Carenews, 19 November 2018; the year of the occupation is inferred from the article date).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little critique of the Palais's funding. The 2024 row is a patron's objection to its politics; the 2020 row concerns a Qatari guest show.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Palais de Tokyo patron row intensifies after French art world figures sign letter of support" (2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/14/palais-de-tokyo-patron-row-intensifies-after-french-art-world-figures-sign-letter-of-support
- Sandra Hegedüs left the Amis du Palais de Tokyo and withdrew her funding after 15 years, saying she no longer wanted to be linked to the "new, very political direction" of the Palais de Tokyo,.
- More than 100 figures, among them Chris Dercon and Camille Henrot, signed an open letter published in Le Monde in support of the Palais, rejecting a patron's claim that its programming is driven by "wokeism".
- Criticism of the programme by a patron, not of its funding. Does not quote Désanges.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: Kylie Yeung, "Artists protest Palais de Tokyo Mathaf collaboration over LGBTQ issues" (2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/artists-protest-palais-de-tokyo-mathaf-collaboration-over-lgbtq-issues
- Artists and critics objected that an LGBTQ-friendly French museum was partnering with a museum of a state that criminalises homosexuality. Names the Palais and Mathaf. The article does not say who paid for the show.

BOTTOM LINE
The Palais is funded by the French state and named corporate patrons, none with a published amount. The weak points are Fondation Engie, the 2020 Qatari guest show and the 2018 protest over La Poste's sponsorship.
