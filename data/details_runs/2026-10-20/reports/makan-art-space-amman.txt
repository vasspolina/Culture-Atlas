MAKAN ART SPACE: Amman, Jordan
ID: makan-art-space-amman

OVERVIEW
- Independent, artist-run contemporary art space at 21 Nadim Al-Mallah Street, Jabal Al-Weibdeh, Amman (Fillip, Spring 2012; TransArtists).
- Founded by Ola Khalidi (Ola El-Khalidi), who opened it in April 2003 as an alternative space for young artists free of restrictions; before launching she worked at the Amman cultural café Blue Fig and the NGO Beirut DC and spent about eight months researching the project (Bidoun, Summer 2004).
- Khalidi said she did not want to call it a "gallery": its purpose was to develop young artists by hosting projects and building connections (Bidoun, 2004).
- By 2012 it was a collective: a core team of Khalidi, Diala Khasawnih and Samah Hijawi, with Hijawi running the Amman space and part of the team working from San Francisco. It is registered as a not-for-profit company supporting the arts under Jordan's Ministry of Industry and Trade (Fillip, 2012).
- In 2011 the space had three studios, a documentation space, an office and a nearby residency house, Beit Makan (Interartive, Herman Bashiron Mendolicchio, 28 November 2011).
- Programmes: an artist exchange and residency programme, workshops, exhibitions, performances, film screenings, music events and studio provision, with a focus on social issues and public art (TransArtists). In October 2004 it was one of five venues for Amman Meeting Points, organised with the Brussels-based Young Arab Theatre Fund (Bidoun, Diala Khasawneh, Hair issue 3, Winter 2005).
- TransArtists states that residency applications are not accepted until further notice.

FUNDING
Makan Art Space: self-funded at the start, later supported by grants: founder's own money first, then a 2008 AFAC-backed programme and other grants
- Khalidi said in 2004 that Makan was self-funded, which is why its projects were "very low budget" (Bidoun, Summer 2004).
- In Fillip (Spring 2012), Ola El-Khalidi says Makan was set up as a not-for-profit company supporting the arts, and a participant in the 2009 Shatana workshop writes in a footnote that Makan "got a grant that will make it survive for a while longer". Diala Khasawnih says "I am tired of fundraising" and proposes food-based projects to bring in money. The 2011 pilot "LBC-Makan Restaurant", presented at the Congress of Collectives, drew share purchases from other participating collectives; its business plan proved unfeasible and Makan continued on its own (Fillip, Ola El-Khalidi and Diala Khasawnih, Spring 2012).
- The Arab Fund for Arts and Culture (AFAC) supported "Makan House", the Arab Artists Residency and Exchange Programme announced on 6 February 2008 with the A.M. Qattan Foundation, Zico House in Beirut, Townhouse Gallery in Cairo and the Alexandria Contemporary Art Forum (A.M. Qattan Foundation).

BOTTOM LINE
Makan began as a self-funded, artist-run space. The documented outside funder is AFAC, which supported the 2008 Makan House exchange programme; a 2009 workshop participant records a further grant.
