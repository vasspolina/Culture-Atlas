ESKENAZI MUSEUM OF ART: Bloomington, USA
ID: eskenazi-museum-of-art
Website: https://artmuseum.indiana.edu (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of Indiana University Bloomington, at 1133 East Seventh Street on the Bloomington campus.
- Opened in 1941 as the Indiana University Museum of Art under Henry Radford Hope, in a gallery in Mitchell Hall; renamed the Sidney and Lois Eskenazi Museum of Art in May 2016 (Wikipedia, museum news release).
- Named for Sidney and Lois Eskenazi, Indianapolis-based philanthropists (museum news release).
- The I.M. Pei and Partners building, dedicated in 1982, is two concrete triangles joined by a glass-roofed atrium. It closed in May 2017 for a renovation by Susan T. Rodriguez of Ennead Architects with Browning Day Mullins Dierdorf and reopened in November 2019 (Wikipedia, museum news release).
- About 45,000 objects, including ancient jewellery and paintings by Picasso and Jackson Pollock, with a café and shop. Mindy N. Besaw was hired as director in 2025, after David A. Brenneman (from 2015) (Wikipedia).
- Admission is free. Open Tuesday to Sunday, closed Monday; Thursdays to 7pm. Two lifts serve all galleries, wheelchairs can be borrowed, and there is a weekly sensory-friendly hour and ADA parking behind the building.

FUNDING
Eskenazi Museum of Art: university-run: Indiana University, with private naming and renovation gifts
- Sidney and Lois Eskenazi gave $15 million in 2016, the largest cash gift in the museum's history, as the lead gift for the renovation, plus nearly 100 prints including 34 works by Miró; IU added $20 million through its bicentennial campaign match (museum news release).
- No reporting found of Sidney Eskenazi's business or of a controversy involving the Eskenazis.
- No reporting found of other named donors or of the museum's own budget within the university's accounts.

Red flags: university cancelled a Palestinian artist's retrospective
- On 20 December 2023 Indiana University cancelled "Samia Halaby: Centers of Energy", the first US retrospective of the Palestinian artist and IU alumna, due to open on 10 February 2024 after three years of work; IU cited "concerns about guaranteeing the integrity of the exhibit for its duration" (Indiana Public Media, Hyperallergic).
- Halaby said: "Clearly the intent is to suppress Palestinian voices at this very time" (The Art Newspaper).
- In November 2023 US Representative Jim Banks had written to IU president Pamela Whitten about antisemitism on campus, calling pro-Palestinian protests "pro-terrorist" (Indiana Public Media).
- Provost Rahul Shrivastav called the show a "lightning rod" for protests and said IU would "err on the side of caution" when there was a risk of violence (Indiana Public Media).

Red flags: protest over the cancellation
- A petition by Halaby's grandniece Madison Gordon passed 13,500 signatures by 18 January 2024 (Indiana Public Media).
- PEN America's Artists at Risk Connection called it "an alarming affront to free expression", and the National Coalition Against Censorship wrote to Whitten on 11 January 2024 (Indiana Public Media, Hyperallergic).
- On 7 February 2024, 75 faculty and staff signed a letter criticising IU's lack of transparency; three artists withdrew from the Eskenazi School's McKinney Visiting Artist Series (Herald-Times).
- The Broad Art Museum at Michigan State University hosted the retrospective in June 2024 (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Strong criticism in the art press, all about the 2023 Halaby cancellation; it targets the university's decision more than the museum's staff.
1. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Indiana University Cancels Palestinian Artist Samia Halaby's Exhibition" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/865825/indiana-university-cancels-palestinian-artist-samia-halaby-exhibition/
- Unverified: that the decision was tied to Halaby's pro-Palestine advocacy and social media, from Hyperallergic citing emails and a petition; the university cited concerns about the exhibit's integrity.
- Quotes the petition: "the University is canceling the show to distance itself from the cause of Palestinian freedom."
2. The Art Newspaper: Theo Belci, "US museum under fire for cancelling Palestinian artist's retrospective" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/01/12/indiana-university-cancels-palestinian-artist-samia-halaby-retrospective
- Reports the petition's claim that IU gave "no evidence of threats" to the artwork, the campus or Halaby.
- Notes Halaby asked why the university did not speak up "during the three long years of preparation".

BOTTOM LINE
No funding controversy was found behind the museum: its university and an Indianapolis couple's naming gift. The weak point is the university's December 2023 cancellation of the Halaby retrospective, which PEN America called "an alarming affront to free expression" and which drew a petition, faculty protest and artist withdrawals. Worth watching: whether the new director restores the show or changes how such decisions are made.
