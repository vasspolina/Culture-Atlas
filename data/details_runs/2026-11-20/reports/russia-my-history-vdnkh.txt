RUSSIA, MY HISTORY (VDNKH): Moscow, Russia
ID: russia-my-history-vdnkh
Website: https://myhistorypark.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Multimedia history park in Pavilion 57 of the VDNKh exhibition grounds, Prospekt Mira 119 (myhistorypark.ru).
- The project grew out of the Orthodox Rus exhibitions the Russian Orthodox Church began in 1995, which Archimandrite Tikhon Shevkunov later took under his wing (RFE/RL, 2019).
- A Romanov dynasty show in the Manezh in 2013 drew more than 300,000 people in 20 days, and the first permanent exhibition opened in Pavilion 57 in December 2015 (RFE/RL).
- The network spans 26 cities, and the site stated in February 2026 that more than 29 million people had visited the project (myhistorypark.ru).
- Permanent displays cover the Rurikids (862 to 1598), the Romanovs (1613 to 1917) and the period "From the Great Upheavals to the Great Victory", with a show on Peter the Great (myhistorypark.ru; title translated by the atlas).
- In 2017 the Education Ministry recommended the parks as teaching material for students and teachers (RFE/RL).
- In 2015 the operator sent its texts to historians of the Institute of Russian History; senior researcher Vladislav Aksyonov told RFE/RL that only a tiny fraction of their changes were adopted.
- From 11 December 2025 Pavilion 57 showed "Znanie. Heroes", an exhibition by the Znanie society on Heroes of Russia that includes participants of the war in Ukraine (Znanie society; title translated by the atlas).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 19:00. Under-18s enter the permanent displays free, as do disabled visitors of groups 1 and 2, large families and combat veterans; the site states that every hall is reachable for disabled visitors (myhistorypark.ru).

FUNDING
Russia, My History: Gazprom-funded foundation with state backing
- The Humanitarian Projects Foundation runs the parks. Its managing director is former Gazprom lawyer Ivan Yesin, and Shevkunov heads its expert council (RFE/RL, 2019).
- RFE/RL reported in 2019 that the foundation gets most of its funding from the state-controlled Gazprom and from government grants, and lists as partners the presidential administration, the Moscow Patriarchate, the Culture Ministry and the Russian Military-Historical Society.
- Gazprom states that since 2016 it has co-financed the creation of the parks with Russia's regions, signing a contract in each region with an authorised non-profit that carries out the project (Gazprom sustainability report 2019).
- Unverified: Bloomberg reported in 2017, as cited by RFE/RL, that most of Gazprom's record 26.3 billion roubles of charitable giving was earmarked for the parks. The foundation denied it and said it spent no more than 120 million roubles on each centre; RFE/RL's own analysis of open sources put total spending at 10 billion roubles or more.
- Since 2025, on the President's instruction, the parks have been part of the federal project "We Together" within the national project "Youth and Children", which runs to 2030 (myhistorypark.ru; names translated by the atlas).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bumaga, reporting in 2023 on the St Petersburg park, wrote that Shevkunov told The Bell that Gazprom allocated 360 million roubles in 2018 for three new parks (Bumaga, citing The Bell).
- Bumaga also reported that Gazprom Neft's Rodnye Goroda foundation gave more than 1 billion roubles in 2019 and 3 billion in 2021 to the My History foundation, which organised an exhibition on Ukraine at the St Petersburg park (Bumaga, citing Vazhnye Istorii).
- The US designated Gazprom chief executive Alexei Miller on 6 April 2018, and Gazprom Neft and more than two dozen of its subsidiaries on 10 January 2025 (US Treasury press releases).
- The UK designated Miller on 10 March 2022 and Gazprom Neft on 10 January 2025; its list names Gazprom Neft, not the parent PJSC Gazprom (UK Sanctions List). OpenSanctions, a secondary source, records Gazprom Neft in EU sanctions acts against Russia published in the Official Journal.
- In 2017 the Free Historical Society published an open letter criticising the parks' "panegyric tone" towards "every tsar without exception" and asked the Education Ministry to stop recommending them until a professional review was done; Pavel Kuzenkov, a former content author, called the project "a clear example of the use of historical science in the service of political propaganda" (RFE/RL).
- Parks of the network run in occupied Melitopol, open since autumn 2023, and Luhansk, and one opened in Donetsk in January 2026 (myhistorypark.ru). Bumaga reported in 2023 that the St Petersburg park staged exhibitions supporting the invasion of Ukraine.
- The Moscow park shares the network's operator, name and content, so these points bear on it.

BOTTOM LINE
Fossil fuel and Russian state money stands behind the park, through the foundation that runs the whole network. Its weak points are sanctioned companies and executives in the Gazprom group and a programme historians call propaganda; the network's growth in occupied Ukraine is worth watching.
