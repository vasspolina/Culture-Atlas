MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART SAN DIEGO: La Jolla, USA
ID: museum-of-contemporary-art-san-diego-la-jolla
Website: https://mcasd.org/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 700 Prospect Street in La Jolla, San Diego, with a separate downtown site (museum website; La Jolla Light).
- In 1941 the founding trustees bought the residence of the philanthropist Ellen Browning Scripps, designed by Irving J. Gill, for 10,000 dollars, and formed The Art Center in La Jolla as a nonprofit. It was renamed La Jolla Museum of Art in 1964, La Jolla Museum of Contemporary Art in 1971 and Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego in 1992 (museum timeline).
- Additions to the Gill house: formal galleries by Mosher Drew in 1950, Sherwood Auditorium in 1960, and in 1996 an expansion that added Axline Court, the Edwards Sculpture Garden and the Museum Cafe, with a facade restoration by Venturi, Scott Brown and Associates (museum timeline).
- A downtown location opened in 1993 in the One America Plaza building, and the Joan and Irwin Jacobs and David C. Copley buildings opened downtown in 2007, designed by Richard Gluckman (museum timeline).
- A capital campaign was announced in 2016 for 75th anniversary expansion by Annabelle Selldorf, growing gallery space from 10,000 to 40,000 square feet. The La Jolla site closed in 2017, broke ground on 18 October 2018 and reopened on 9 April 2022 (museum timeline).
- The renovation and expansion cost 105 million dollars, and most La Jolla spaces are named for donors, among them the Strauss galleries, Foster gallery, Pfister gallery, Prebys Learning Center, Axline Court and Jacobs Hall (La Jolla Light, Elisabeth Frausto, 30 November 2021).
- Board: Maryanne Pfister is president; the bylaws set a maximum of 40 trustees (museum website, who we are).

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego: private nonprofit: trustees, donors, endowment, with a federal construction grant
- Scale: in the fiscal year ending June 2025 the museum reported revenue of 1,721,132 dollars, expenses of 13,688,922 dollars, contributions of 4,857,103 dollars and net assets of 124,604,727 dollars. Net assets were 135.3 million dollars in 2024. The chief executive's compensation was 399,331 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 data).
- Endowment: Wikipedia states a permanent endowment of over 40 million dollars, and that most of the support traces to a 1999 bequest of more than 30 million dollars from Rea and Jackie Axline.
- Federal grant: on 30 January 2020 the National Endowment for the Humanities announced a 750,000 dollar grant in matching funds under its Infrastructure and Capacity Building Challenge for the La Jolla expansion. NEH put building costs at 55 million dollars in 2016, risen to 95 million dollars by 2019 (National Endowment for the Humanities).
- Corporate: the museum's Corporate Partnership Program has levels from Supporter at 2,500 dollars to Visionary at 100,000 dollars (museum website, corporate partners page).
- Donor-named galleries and rooms in the expanded La Jolla building are listed under Overview. The board supports operations and the 75th Anniversary Campaign (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private: trustees and donors, an endowment built largely on the Axline bequest, and a 750,000 dollar NEH matching grant announced in 2020 toward the 105 million dollar expansion that reopened in 2022.
