URAL INDUSTRIAL BIENNIAL OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Yekaterinburg, Russia
ID: ural-industrial-biennial-of-contemporary-art-yekaterinburg
Website: https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/5924952

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennial held in factory and other non-gallery spaces in Yekaterinburg and other cities of the Ural region (Biennial Foundation).
- Founded in 2010 and organised by the Ekaterinburg branch of the National Centre for Contemporary Arts (NCCA); the 2019 edition was listed as part of ROSIZO (Biennial Foundation, e-flux).
- Editions: 2010, 2012, 2015, 2017, the fifth ("Immortality") on 12 September to 1 December 2019, and the sixth, "Thinking Hands, Touching Each Other", on 2 October to 5 December 2021 with 52 artists and collectives from 23 countries and regions (Biennial Foundation).
- Alisa Prudnikova was the commissioner of the 2019 and 2021 editions (e-flux).
- For the sixth edition the organiser was named as the Ural Branch of the Pushkin State Museum of Fine Arts, with the Center for Support and Development of Contemporary Art ZA ART as co-organiser (e-flux).
- The final exhibition of the sixth edition was held at the UGMK Museum of Automotive Technology in Verkhnyaya Pyshma (E1.ru, 30 September 2021).
- Kommersant reported on 7 April 2023 that the seventh edition would not take place, an announcement made by the city administration's press service citing the culture department.
- In September 2023 the team launched a research platform presenting archival materials from the first six editions and relaunched its residency programme as a research laboratory (The Art Newspaper Russia, 7 September 2023).

FUNDING
Ural Industrial Biennial: mixed public and sponsor money: a state organiser plus sponsors
- Sixth edition (2021): the budget was about 100 million rubles, 40 percent covered by the state and 60 percent by sponsors (Kommersant, 7 April 2023).
- Fifth edition (2019): held "with the support of the Ministry of Culture of the Russian Federation" (e-flux).
- The organiser is a state museum branch (the Pushkin State Museum of Fine Arts) for the sixth edition, and the NCCA Ural branch, listed as part of ROSIZO, for the fifth (e-flux, Biennial Foundation).

Red flags: open letter against the circus venue, 2021
- 24 August 2021 (The Blueprint): artists, activists and cultural workers signed an open letter with more than 300 signatures asking the biennial's organisers not to work with a circus. The letter was aimed at the biennial's choice of the Yekaterinburg Circus as one of its venues, not at the city.
- The organisers replied on Instagram that the circus choice drew attention to "exploitation and cruel treatment of circus animals in Russia", that they are against violence against animals, and that they called the circus "an ambiguous and in its own way problematic place" they intend to examine (The Blueprint, title and quotations translated).
- Artist Alisa Goroshenina (Alice Hualice) withdrew from the biennial in late July 2021 over the circus venue and accused the organisers of inhumanity (The Blueprint).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the biennial is state and sponsor money: in 2021 about 40 percent state and 60 percent sponsors of a budget of about 100 million rubles, with the Russian Ministry of Culture supporting the 2019 edition. The seventh edition was cancelled in April 2023 by an announcement of the city administration.
