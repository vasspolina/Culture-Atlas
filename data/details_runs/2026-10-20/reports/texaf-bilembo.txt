TEXAF BILEMBO: Kinshasa, DR Congo
ID: texaf-bilembo
Website: https://www.texaf-bilembo.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural and educational space in Kinshasa, in the Nouvelle Concession UTEX on rue Esengo, with its entrance at 372 Avenue Colonel Mondjiba, Gombe commune (Kino Congo). The centre's own site gives the address as Rue Esengo (Utexafrica).
- Inaugurated on 7 November 2013; conceived by art historian Chantal Tombu, writer and photographer Alain Huart and artist Christine Decelle (stated by the centre).
- Texaf, the Brussels-listed company, founded the space and is its main patron. Former Texaf chief executive Philippe Croonenberghs gave the site to the project (stated by the centre).
- Building: a former textile factory of more than 1,000 m² inside the Utexafrica concession.
- On site: a permanent collection (the "Petit Musée"), an exhibition hall, a contemporary art gallery, an art shop, a bookshop and a library. Kino Congo also lists a cafe and restaurant.
- Programmes: temporary exhibitions of contemporary Congolese artists, and workshops with partner schools, built around the didactic book "SOS Planète Congo", which deals with the biodiversity of the Congo forest.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 9:00 to 16:30 and Sunday 10:00 to 13:00; admission free. No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Texaf Bilembo: corporate-funded: Texaf, a Brussels-listed Congolese property and quarry group, is founder and head sponsor
- Texaf says it is founder and head sponsor of the centre (texaf.be). No reporting found of the annual amount Texaf spends on it.
- Where Texaf's money comes from: in 2025, 85.2% of net sales were Kinshasa property rental, 11.7% a sandstone quarry and 3.1% digital. All net sales arise in the DR Congo, and the company had 212 employees (MarketScreener).
- Texaf shareholders: Société Financière Africaine SA holds 62.88% and the Cha family 10% (MarketScreener). Chairman is Dominique Moorkens since May 2023; Jean-Philippe Waterschoot is chief executive since May 2017 (MarketScreener).
- Texaf history: founded on 14 August 1925 in the Belgian Congo as Société Textile Africaine; textile manufacturing in the DR Congo ended with the 2007 liquidation of the Congotex joint venture, after which Texaf moved to real estate (Wikipedia).
- Partners listed on the centre's "Qui sommes-nous" page: TMB, Bracongo, Afrissur, SFA, CFAO, Global Broadband Solution, Canalbox, the Belgian Embassy, the European Union, the Académie des beaux-arts de Kinshasa, IMNC, Enabel, Weyrich Africa, Angalia gallery, the Africa Museum, Louvain Coopération and the King Baudouin Foundation, with Texaf as founder and main patron. The page gives no amounts, years or roles for any of them.
- TotalEnergies: the centre's home page partner strip shows a logo labelled "UE" whose link points to totalenergies.cd; the "Qui sommes-nous" page names the European Union, not TotalEnergies, and no page opened names TotalEnergies in text. No amount, year or role is given, so this is an unquantified logo listing, not documented funding.
- Angalia gallery is a listed partner; its news page presents "Papiers de société" (16 April to 30 May 2021, curated by Chantal Tombu) as an exhibition held at the centre. The page does not give the terms of any partnership (Galerie Angalia).
- The centre's site invites public donations. No reporting found of public accounts for the centre itself.
- Kin ArtStudio, a collective of artists, is housed in the Texaf concession; it is a separate group (texaf.be, Dutch-language page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the founding company's own rental, quarry and digital income, with a long partner list whose amounts and terms are not published; a home page logo links to TotalEnergies, but no page shows funding from it. The weak points are the lack of centre accounts or a named board and the unstated terms of the partnerships. Worth watching: whether the partners' sums and the centre's governance are ever published.
