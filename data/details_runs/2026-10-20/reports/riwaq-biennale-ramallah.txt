RIWAQ BIENNALE: Ramallah, Palestine
ID: riwaq-biennale-ramallah
Website: https://www.riwaq.org

OVERVIEW
- Art biennale run by Riwaq, the Centre for Architectural Conservation, a non-governmental, non-profit organisation in Ramallah that protects Palestinian cultural heritage (e-flux, OPEC Fund project page).
- Suad Amiry founded Riwaq in 1991 (Wikipedia); the OPEC Fund page gives June 1991 as its establishment and says it carried out an architectural survey from 1994 to 2007.
- Riwaq launched the first biennale in 2005, named after the institution itself rather than a place; Riwaq's director Khaldun Bshara writes that the biennale gave Riwaq exposure beyond the heritage world and "opened new funding possibilities", and that there was no fourth edition in 2011 after resistance within Riwaq (PARSE Journal, Khaldun Bshara).
- Editions listed on Riwaq's site: Biennale III (2009), IV (2012), V (2014) and the Chicago Biennial 2019 (Riwaq website). The third edition, "A Geography: 50 Villages", ran in Palestine from June to October 2009 and was curated by Reem Fadda and Charles Esche as tours, curated conversations and temporary interventions (Bidoun, Beth Stryker).
- In 2019 Riwaq showed "Secrets of a Digital Garden: 50 Flowers...50 Villages", a speculative installation with Yara Sharif and Nasser Golzari, in the Chicago Architecture Biennial; the 50 flowers stand for the 50 villages of Riwaq's 50 Villages Project (Riwaq website).
- Riwaq's Registry of Historic Buildings, published in 2006, records 50,320 buildings in 122 villages, towns and cities; Riwaq received the Prince Claus Award in 2011 and the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 2013 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Riwaq Biennale: NGO-funded: European and international development funders, foundations and heritage funds
- The biennale is organised by Riwaq, whose "Donors & Partners" page thanks these funding partners for operational and programmatic support: the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development, ALIPH, Arts Collaboratory, the Cultural Protection Fund of the British Council, UNDP, the Doen Foundation, the European Union (including EuroMed Heritage and the ENPI-CBC tool), Germany through KfW, the French Consulate, the Ford Foundation, the French Development Agency, the Foundation for Arts Initiatives, the Goethe Institute, Hivos, the OPEC Fund for International Development, the Open Society Foundation, the Prince Claus Fund, the Tamari Foundation, the Tides Foundation, the Swedish Government, UNESCO, Wallonie-Bruxelles International, the Walloon Heritage Institute and the Welfare Association.
- OPEC Fund: a grant of US$0.15 million, approved on 5 October 2011 and signed on 27 December 2011, later extended to support rehabilitation of the old centre of Mazare Al Nobani (OPEC Fund project page).
- Operating partners named on the same page include Ramallah Municipality, the Hebron Rehabilitation Committee, Oxford Brookes University and Columbia University's GSAPP.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review, analytical rather than hostile, of the third edition's framing.
1. Bidoun: Beth Stryker, "3rd Riwaq Biennale" (2010, first published in Bazaar, issue 20)
URL: https://bidoun.org/articles/3rd-riwaq-biennale
- Reads the biennial as the second half of a "dual presentation" with Khalil Rabah's "Palestine c/o Venice", a collateral event of the 53rd Venice Biennale, and says the tours often became the main event and the biennial negotiated the space between fact and fiction.
- Says the Jamma'een community centre performance, with children in traditional costume, "threatened to cater to guests' expectations of an 'authentic' experience", but that the critical engagement of the curatorial framework kept it from defaulting to the maudlin; adds that, unlike many biennials criticised as tourist attractions, this one turned the tour into an object of critical reflection.
- Says whether Riwaq achieves its goals of protecting, utilizing and promoting cultural heritage in Palestine "may take time to fully discern"; names Rabah, Fadda and Esche.

BOTTOM LINE
The Riwaq Biennale is funded through Riwaq, a Palestinian non-profit supported by European governments and agencies, UN bodies, foundations and heritage funds, with a US$0.15 million OPEC Fund grant approved in 2011. Riwaq's site lists the biennale editions of 2009, 2012 and 2014 and the 2019 Chicago Architecture Biennial exhibition.
