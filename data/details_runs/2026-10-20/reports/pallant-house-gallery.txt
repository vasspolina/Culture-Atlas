PALLANT HOUSE GALLERY: Chichester, UK
ID: pallant-house-gallery
Website: https://pallant.org.uk (checked 2026-10-25; loaded)

OVERVIEW
- Art gallery in a Grade I listed Queen Anne townhouse built in 1712 in Chichester, West Sussex, with a modern wing added in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Established in 1982; the house was council offices from 1919, restored in 1979, and has been run by an independent trust since 1985 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the house itself, Pallant House; the collection began with works that Walter Hussey, Dean of Chichester Cathedral, left to Chichester in 1977 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Colin St John Wilson and Long & Kentish designed the wing, which opened in June 2006 and won the 2007 Gulbenkian Prize; Christopher Bradley-Hole designed the courtyard garden. The £8.6 million extension was funded by the Heritage Lottery Fund, Arts Council England, the local council and other donors (Wikipedia).
- On site: collections of 20th-century British art, including later gifts from Charles Kearley (1989) and Colin St John Wilson (2006), with temporary exhibitions; current shows include "British Landscapes: A Sense of Place" and "Pop and the Figure" (pallant.org.uk).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 10am to 5pm and Sunday 10am to 4pm, closed Monday; adults £17 including a £2 Gift Aid donation, under-18s free, ages 18 to 25 and Art Fund members £8.50, Friends free (pallant.org.uk). No reporting found of accessibility details on the pages read; the site links to a separate access page.

FUNDING
Pallant House Gallery: mixed: charity income from visitors, Friends and patrons, with a district council grant and corporate partners
- The gallery says it must raise £2.75 million a year and that only 7% of annual income comes from public subsidy (stated by the gallery).
- Chichester District Council: Cabinet and Council approved a grant of £130,000 a year plus inflation for four years from April 2026, subject to a funding agreement. Unverified: this rests on a web search extract of council records dated July 2025; the council pages returned HTTP 403 when opened.
- Corporate partners named on the gallery's pages: Dreweatts (Principal Partner) and Chichester Council (Civic Partner). Evelyn Partners signed a three-year partnership on 13 March 2025 to sponsor the annual exhibition programme; no amount was disclosed (Wealth Briefing).
- Grants recorded on GrantNav, 2007 to 2025: National Lottery Heritage Fund £1,121,600 (including £1 million in 2012 toward an endowment fund); Arts Council England £300,000; DCMS £181,518; Paul Hamlyn Foundation £243,266; Foyle Foundation £105,000; National Lottery Community Fund £92,910; Esmée Fairbairn Foundation £25,000; Wolfson Foundation £19,000; Paul Mellon Centre £2,500 (total £2,090,794).
- Individuals: Friends, Patrons, legacies and Supporters' Circles (stated by the gallery).
- No reporting found of a Fidelity Foundation grant of £260,000 or of £3.0 million income for FY2025; neither appears on the pages opened. No reporting found of any fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is ordinary public and charitable funding, with visitors and private supporters paying most of its costs, and nothing flagged was found. The weak points are reliance on a local council grant that is renewed in four-year cycles (unconfirmed, see above) and a thin public financial record on the gallery's own site. Worth watching the council's annual monitoring reports and the Charity Commission accounts.
