KAMLOOPS ART GALLERY – Kamloops, Canada
Website: https://kag.bc.ca (loaded 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery incorporated in 1978. Since 1998 it has been in a purpose-built civic building by Peter Cardew and Nigel Baldwin, shared with Thompson-Nicola Regional District (TNRD) offices and the downtown TNRD Library branch. It was designated a Category "A" institution under the Cultural Property Export and Import Act in 2006 (gallery About page).
- Open Tuesday to Saturday 10–5 and Thursdays 10–8. Admission is free on Thursdays. The About page gives no accessibility information.
- Margaret Chrumka, executive director for 12 years, left at the end of 2024 for the Art Gallery of Greater Victoria (Castanet, 22 Oct 2024).

FUNDING
Kamloops Art Gallery: mainly public, plus individual donors and local corporate sponsors
- Government and foundation donors named on the gallery's supporters page (undated tiers, donors "since January 2024"): City of Kamloops, Canada Council for the Arts, BC Arts Council, Province of British Columbia, Vancouver Foundation, Audain Foundation, Hamber Foundation, TNRD, BC Interior Community Foundation, Tourism Kamloops and the Provincial Employees Community Services Fund. The provincial acknowledgment refers to Gaming Policy and Enforcement Branch funding.
- Corporate sponsors: BCLC (sponsors free Thursday admission and a community ticketing programme), GK Sound, Kelson Group, Best Western Plus Kamloops Hotel, Artistic Sign Service, KA Media, Twisted Spirits, Bright Eye Brewing and Paladin Security.
- Individual donors are named by tier. The $25,000+ tier includes Jane Mastin and James Funk, Min Fang Zhang and Michael Black, the Fedoraks, Jane Irwin and Ross Hill, Donald Lawrence, Kenneth W. Lepin and Tricia Sellmer.
- No figures are available for this run: the 2025 annual report PDF could not be retrieved.

Red flags: Sponsor criticised by a public inquiry
- BCLC, a BC Crown corporation, sponsors free Thursday admission (gallery site; no start date given).
- The Cullen Commission, reporting in June 2022, found that BCLC ignored repeated law enforcement warnings about casino money laundering until 2015. It described BCLC's approach as "a completely unacceptable and unreasonable risk tolerance" (Wikipedia, citing CTV News).
- An Independent Gambling Control Office was set up to oversee BCLC from 13 April 2026 (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is mainly publicly funded and names its donors. The one item worth watching is BCLC's sponsorship of free admission, given the Cullen Commission's findings about BCLC. No reporting found of protest or criticism aimed at the gallery itself.
