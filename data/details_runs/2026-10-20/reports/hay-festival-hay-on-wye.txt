HAY FESTIVAL: Hay-on-Wye, UK
ID: hay-festival-hay-on-wye
Website: https://www.hayfestival.com/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual literature festival in Hay-on-Wye, Powys, Wales, on the Dairy Meadows site in the Bannau Brycheiniog National Park.
- Founded in 1988 by Peter Florence and his parents, Rhoda and Norman Florence; it used venues around the town, including the primary school, until it moved to one site south of Hay in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- Hay Festival Global also runs editions in Arequipa, Jericó, Cartagena, Querétaro and Segovia, and smaller forums elsewhere (Wikipedia); Julie Finch was its chief executive in 2024.
- The 2026 festival, the 39th spring edition, ran from 21 to 31 May with more than 500 events (Brecon & Radnor Express).
- The site has a bookshop, the BBC Marquee, a Wild Garden, a family garden, cafés and market stalls; entry to the site is free, events are ticketed.
- No reporting found of accessibility details in the sources checked.

FUNDING
Hay Festival: mainly earned income: ticket and event revenue, with corporate and foundation partners
- The charity Hay Festival Foundation Ltd (no. 1070073) reported income of £6,883,201 for the year to 31 December 2025: £6.43 million from charitable activities, £400,620 from donations and legacies, £51,670 from investments, and £122,616 from government grants (Charity Commission).
- Baillie Gifford and Tata Group were among the principal sponsors from 2017 (Wikipedia); no amount was published.
- 2026 supporters and contributors named: British Council (Literary Pairs), Hawthornden Foundation, Swansea University, Royal Society of Literature, the BBC, MUBI and the UK Government's National Year of Reading (Brecon & Radnor Express).
- Earlier main sponsors were The Sunday Times, The Guardian (2002 to 2010) and The Daily Telegraph (from 2011) (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a principal sponsor replacing Baillie Gifford.

Red flags: Baillie Gifford suspended under campaign pressure
- On 24 May 2024 Julie Finch announced that Hay had "taken the decision to suspend our sponsorship from Baillie Gifford", citing claims by campaigners and "intense pressure on artists to withdraw" (stated by the space).
- The campaign group Fossil Free Books demanded that Baillie Gifford divest from fossil fuels and from companies profiting from Israel's occupation, citing £2.5 billion to £5 billion in fossil fuel holdings (figures from Urgewald and The Ferret); over 800 authors and book workers signed its statement (Fossil Free Books, advocacy source).
- Dawn Butler, Shami Chakrabarti, Grace Blakeley, Nish Kumar and Charlotte Church withdrew from the 2024 festival (Fossil Free Books).
- Fossil Free Books then asked Hay for a fundraising policy ruling out sponsors that invest in fossil fuels or Israel's occupation; no reporting found of such a policy.

Red flags: Abu Dhabi edition with the UAE Ministry of Tolerance
- Hay Festival Abu Dhabi ran from 25 to 28 February 2020 at Manarat Al Saadiyat, "supported by the UAE's Ministry of Tolerance" under its minister Sheikh Nahyan Mabarak Al Nahyan (stated by the space).
- An open letter signed by over 50 organisations and individuals, including Amnesty International, PEN International and Stephen Fry, urged the UAE to free jailed rights defenders such as Ahmed Mansoor (The New Arab).
- Caitlin McNamara, who helped organise the edition with the ministry, said the minister sexually assaulted her; his lawyers deny it (The Spectator). The Crown Prosecution Service declined to prosecute, citing jurisdiction (Wikipedia).
- In October 2020 chair Caroline Michel said Hay would not return to the UAE while he remained in post (The Spectator).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found; the critique is in the literary and political press, about the Abu Dhabi edition and the Baillie Gifford decision.
1. The Spectator: Sam Leith, "The Hay has become the Starbucks of literary festivals" (2020)
URL: https://spectator.com/article/the-hay-festival-s-uneasy-dance-with-the-uae/
- Compares taking part in such events to "constructive engagement" with a government that has a poor human rights record, and says the UAE hosted the festival for the prestige it brings.
- Director Peter Florence mishandled McNamara's account: he knew of it, the festival opened with the minister on stage, and her request to circulate her account to colleagues and the board was not carried out.
- Calls the chair's statement, issued about eight months after the incident, "defensive PR".

BOTTOM LINE
Hay's money is mostly its own earned income. The weak points are the 2020 Abu Dhabi edition paid for by a Gulf state ministry whose minister was accused of sexual assault, and the 2024 loss of Baillie Gifford under a campaign aimed at the festival. Worth watching: who replaces Baillie Gifford as principal sponsor.
