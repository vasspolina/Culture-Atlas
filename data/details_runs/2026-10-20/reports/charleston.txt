CHARLESTON: Firle and Lewes, UK
ID: charleston
Website: https://www.charleston.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the former farmhouse home of the artists Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant at Firle in East Sussex, with a second site, Charleston in Lewes (Wikipedia for the farmhouse, Visit Lewes for the Lewes site).
- Bell and Grant moved to Sussex in 1916 and Charleston became the country meeting place of the Bloomsbury group (Wikipedia).
- The Charleston Trust, a charity, was set up in 1980 to restore and maintain the house; the collection has been open to the public since 1986 (Wikipedia).
- Bell and Grant decorated the walls, doors and furniture, and created the walled garden to designs by Roger Fry (Wikipedia).
- A suite of galleries designed by Jamie Fobert Architects opened in September 2018, alongside two restored barns: the Threshing Barn café and the Hay Barn for events and workshops (Wikipedia).
- Charleston in Lewes, at Southover Road, has changing exhibitions, a shop, a café and a community space for workshops and schools (Visit Lewes).
- The collection holds around 20,000 works, objects, books and archive items; more than 120,000 people visit each year across both sites, and Arts Council England has awarded the collection Designated status (Artlyst, September 2026).
- Visiting, Lewes site: Wednesday to Sunday and bank holiday Mondays, 10am to 5pm; adult gallery ticket £14, students £6.25, under-18s and carers free; a combined ticket with Firle saves 15% (Visit Lewes).

FUNDING
Charleston: mixed: ticket, shop and café income, National Lottery Heritage Fund and other public grants, and charitable gifts
- Wikipedia states that Charleston relies on ticket sales and spend in its shop and café. For the year to 31 December 2020 the Trust's income was £2,078,352, including £722,303 from five government grants (Wikipedia).
- National Lottery Heritage Fund: on 28 March 2011 the Heritage Lottery Fund announced £2.4 million, 38% of the costs of a £6.3 million Charleston Barn Project that restored the barn, recreated the granary and created an auditorium, a studio learning space and storage for the reserve collection, and expanded the café and shop (National Lottery Heritage Fund, "Home of the 'Bloomsbury Set' wins £2.4million").
- National Lottery Heritage Fund: £567,738 awarded on 27 November 2025 to the Trust for "Charleston in Lewes: Strengthening Resilience, Serving Community, Safeguarding Collections" (National Lottery Heritage Fund). Artlyst states the fund supports a new Collection Store at the Lewes site.
- Sigrid Rausing Trust: a £30,000 grant from April 2026 to the Charleston Barn Development Project, after grants since 2016 totalling £255,500 (Sigrid Rausing Trust).
- Getting Building Fund: £89,293 in November 2020 and £240,542 approved in July 2021 for a new cycle path and resurfacing of the access road. Wikipedia says the road is reported to be privately owned by the eighth Viscount Gage and his wife, and that the grant "gave rise to criticism", citing a Guardian article.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Charleston is charity income: tickets, shop and café, National Lottery Heritage Fund grants of £2.4 million in 2011 and £567,738 in 2025, the Sigrid Rausing Trust and government grants. Wikipedia records criticism of the 2021 Getting Building Fund grant for an access road reported to be owned by Viscount Gage.
