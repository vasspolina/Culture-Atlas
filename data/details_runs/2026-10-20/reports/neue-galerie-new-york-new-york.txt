NEUE GALERIE NEW YORK: New York, USA
ID: neue-galerie-new-york-new-york
Website: https://www.neuegalerie.org (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of early 20th-century German and Austrian art and design at Fifth Avenue and 86th Street, Manhattan (Wikipedia).
- Founded by art dealer Serge Sabarsky and collector Ronald Lauder, who met in 1967; Lauder carried on after Sabarsky died in 1996. It opened on 16 November 2001 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the former William Starr Miller House, a Louis XIII and Beaux-Arts structure, renovated by Selldorf Architects (Wikipedia).
- On site: Austrian art and Wiener Werkstätte objects on the second floor, German art on the third, shops and Café Sabarsky. In 2006 Lauder bought Klimt's Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I from Maria Altmann for the museum; the press reported $135 million (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the museum's site says it reopens on 12 November 2026 with its 25th Anniversary Exhibition. Open Monday and Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm, closed Tuesday. Adults $28, seniors $18, students, educators and visitors with disabilities $15, members free; children under 12 are not admitted during regular hours (museum website).

FUNDING
Neue Galerie New York: founder-funded: Ronald Lauder, with admissions, members and corporate members
- Form 990-PF for 2024: revenue $21,344,921, of which contributions $9,531,377; expenses $24,685,946; net assets $112,052,503. Ronald S. Lauder is President and Trustee, unpaid (ProPublica).
- Corporate members at $25,000: The Estée Lauder Companies, Gagosian, Orentreich Family Foundation and Zezé Flowers. At $10,000: Bank of America, Bloomberg Philanthropies, J.P. Morgan Chase & Co, Cartier, Ralph Lauren Corporation, Onyx Renewable Partners, Sidley Austin and others. At $5,000: Christie's and Sotheby's among six listed. The page gives tiers, not each gift, and no date (museum website).
- Hyperallergic puts Lauder's estimated net worth at $4.9 billion (merger article) and calls him heir to the Estée Lauder fortune (MoMA protest article).
- Merger: announced 14 May 2026 and due to take effect in 2028, subject to approvals; the Neue Galerie becomes a branch of the Met and is to be renamed The Met Ronald S. Lauder Neue Galerie (Finestre sull'Arte). Lauder and his daughter Aerin Lauder Zinterhofer will give 13 paintings and an undisclosed endowment gift (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is a 2016 report on a restitution claim and 2026 coverage of its founder's politics and associations; no critique of its programme found. Coverage of the merger was factual.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Protest at MoMA, 12 November 2024: about 40 people protested at MoMA against Lauder, its honorary board chair and World Jewish Congress president, calling for the museum to disaffiliate from "dirty money" (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan). The article does not report protests at the Neue Galerie.
- Epstein documents: The Daily Pennsylvanian, citing Justice Department releases, reports Lauder kept in touch with Jeffrey Epstein as recently as 2017: a February 2017 email places Lauder at Epstein's pool, a visit to Epstein's home was scheduled for 24 February 2017, and Epstein's assistant arranged a museum tour for Lauder, Epstein and "Jim Watson"; the article does not name the museum. It also reports a 2013 agreement signed by Lauder for a joint purchase between the Neue Galerie and Leon Black, with the Galerie selling five artworks for $19 million, and a 2014 purchase with Black of a Schwitters painting for $25 million. The paper says Lauder "has not been accused of any wrongdoing in connection with Epstein or his crimes" and did not respond to a request for comment (Luke Petersen, 11 February 2026). The article does not report any payment between Epstein and the museum.
- Two ARTnews pieces on Epstein's art-world connections, one on Lauder, could not be read (paywalled).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded by its founder, Ronald Lauder, plus admissions and corporate members. The weak points are the Epstein documents that put Lauder in contact with Epstein through 2017, a 2013 museum sale to Leon Black, protests aimed at Lauder at MoMA, and the 2016 restitution claim. Watch the Met merger terms and whether the Epstein documents lead to any museum disclosure.
