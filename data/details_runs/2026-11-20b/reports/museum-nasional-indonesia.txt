MUSEUM NASIONAL INDONESIA: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: museum-nasional-indonesia
Website: https://www.museumnasional.or.id (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Archaeological, historical, ethnological and geographical museum at Jalan Medan Merdeka Barat 12, Central Jakarta, on the west side of Merdeka Square (Museum Nasional; Wikipedia).
- It grew from the Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen, an independent learned society set up in Batavia on 24 April 1778; the colonial government decided on the present building in 1862 and it opened to the public in 1868 (Museum Nasional).
- Known as Museum Gajah after the bronze elephant King Chulalongkorn of Siam gave in 1871 (Museum Nasional).
- The society handed the museum to the Indonesian government on 17 September 1962 as Museum Pusat; a ministerial decree of 28 May 1979 renamed it Museum Nasional (Museum Nasional).
- It holds about 190,000 objects in seven collections, from prehistory and Hindu-Buddhist archaeology to numismatics, ceramics, ethnography, geography and history; Gedung B opened on 20 June 2007 (Museum Nasional).
- A fire from about 8pm on 16 September 2023 burned for around three hours and damaged six rooms in Gedung A; an official said most destroyed items were replicas (The Star, citing The Jakarta Post). Police found it started behind Gedung A, not inside (Ahmad Mahendra, Kompas, 19 September 2023).
- It reopened on 15 October 2024 with a companion show, "Beating Nekara, Watering the Fire", that the museum said would "transparently answer public curiosity" about the fire (The Art Newspaper).
- It had 770,000 visitors in 2025 and targets 800,000 in 2026; entry is free under the Aramsa programme on the second Wednesday of each month from 16:00 (RRI, 24 April 2026).
- Since 1 January 2026 tickets cost Rp50,000 for adults (from Rp25,000), Rp30,000 for school pupils (before free) and Rp150,000 for foreigners (from Rp50,000); children under three enter free (Metro TV).

FUNDING
Museum Nasional Indonesia: public: Indonesia's Ministry of Culture, through a public service agency that keeps ticket income
- Owner: the Ministry of Culture, which decided the 2026 ticket rise; the head of Museums and Cultural Heritage, Indira Estiyanti Nurjadin, said the museum relies on government money, philanthropy and tickets, and that ticket income pays for collection care and visitor services (Antara, 5 January 2026).
- Financial status: a Finance Minister's decree of 22 March 2021 made the museum a public service agency (BLU) under the education ministry (Museum Nasional).
- Operator: the Indonesian Heritage Agency, which in April 2024 was set to run 18 museums and 34 heritage sites as one BLU, keeping ticket income and investor support instead of paying it to the treasury (Fortune Indonesia, 3 April 2024). In April 2026 the museum still shared one BLU with the national gallery (RRI).
- As a BLU it earns income from tickets, venue hire, collaborations and sponsorship; Culture Minister Fadli Zon wants public-private partnerships with philanthropists to fund museums (RRI, 25 April 2026).
- Fire repairs: the acting head of the agency, Ahmad Mahendra, said full restoration would take three years (The Art Newspaper, 15 October 2024); in January 2026 the museum said renovation of the six burned rooms would continue with support from government, private and public sources (Antara).

Red flags: colonial-era loot in the collection, returns up to 2024
- The reopening show presented more than 2,500 objects that The Art Newspaper calls looted heritage repatriated over 70 years, among them the looted "Lombok treasure" the Netherlands returned in July 2023; about 300 selected collections came back from the Netherlands between 1978 and 2023, and 760 objects returned in 2023 and 2024 (The Art Newspaper, 15 October 2024).
- Most of the royal regalia in the ethnology treasure room were acquired or looted in Dutch East Indies military campaigns of the 19th and early 20th centuries, with treasures from Java, Aceh, Lombok and Bali (Wikipedia).
- Before January 2026 the museum received Eugene Dubois's fossil collection back from the Netherlands (Antara).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is news of the fire and the reopening; the criticism is of the 2026 ticket rise, in national press.
1. Metro TV: "Harga Tiket Masuk Museum Nasional Naik Drastis, Pegiat Pendidikan Mengeluh" (2 January 2026)
URL: https://www.metrotvnews.com/read/NleC9jEx-harga-tiket-masuk-museum-nasional-naik-drastis-pegiat-pendidikan-mengeluh
- Nurfahmi Budi of Sanggar Askala Edukasi said doubling the price without careful thought burdens citizens who want to learn.
- Argues a state-owned museum should not act like a private company chasing profit, and that service improvements should come from the state budget. National television news, not art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Indonesian state money plus ticket income. The weak point is the colonial origin of part of the collection; worth watching is how far the museum comes to rely on higher tickets and private donors.
