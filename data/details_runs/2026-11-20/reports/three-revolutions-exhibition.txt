THREE REVOLUTIONS EXHIBITION: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: three-revolutions-exhibition
Website: none found (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State exhibition of North Korea's economic and technical work in the Ryonmot-dong area of the Sosong District, in north Pyongyang near the Victorious Fatherland Liberation War Martyrs' Cemetery (korea-dpr.com, Koryo Tours).
- Began as non-permanent exhibits in August 1946, became a permanent Industrial and Agricultural Exhibition in May 1956, took its present name in September 1983 and was enlarged in April 1993 (korea-dpr.com). KCNA dates its founding by Kim Il Sung to 6 August 1946.
- Named for the ideological, technical and cultural revolutions. Koryo Tours says every development shown is presented as a success of the state ideology, Juche.
- Has six halls (Koryo Tours). Wikipedia lists electronics, heavy industry, agriculture, class education and technology among the subjects.
- The North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification describes a site of more than 100 jongbo, laid out along a central road over 100 metres wide, with a hall for works on the Juche idea, halls for heavy industry, electronics, light industry and agriculture, an outdoor display ground and a monument at the far end. It dates the start of construction to the end of February 1989.
- The central building is shaped like Saturn and holds a planetarium, and the grounds show North Korean-made vehicles outdoors (Koryo Tours, Wikipedia).
- The state uses the site for fairs: it held the 16th Pyongyang Spring International Trade Fair in May 2013 and a national exhibition of information technology from 15 to 19 October 2018 in its New Technology Innovation Hall (North Korea Information Portal, citing state media).
- The exhibition marked its 80th anniversary with a report meeting on 5 August 2026, at which its director, Son Chol Jin, gave the report and which called for renovating and modernising the exhibition (North Korea Information Portal, citing Rodong Sinmun).
- Visiting: open to foreign visitors, a short drive from the city centre (Koryo Tours).

FUNDING
Three Revolutions Exhibition: state-funded: the Government of North Korea
- Party and Cabinet oversight: at the 60th anniversary meeting on 23 August 2006, attended by party secretary Choe Thae Bok and vice-premier Ro Tu Chol, a message from the Central Committee of the Workers' Party praised the staff and called the exhibition a "base of mass education" (KCNA).
- The same KCNA report says the exhibition hosted science and technology festivals and "many national and international exhibitions and fairs". Exhibitors at such fairs are clients of the venue, not funders.
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: Sanctions on the state
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).

Red flags: Self-Defence 2021 weapons exhibition held at the site
- KCNA reported that a defence development exhibition titled "Self-Defence 2021" was staged at the Three Revolutions Exhibition on 11 October 2021, for the 76th anniversary of the Workers' Party, with a speech by Kim Jong Un (Hankyoreh, 13 October 2021).
- Hankyoreh lists new tank models, a super-large multiple rocket launcher, intermediate-range and short-range missiles, the Hwasong-8 hypersonic missile and an intercontinental ballistic missile among the weapons shown.
- South Korea's Ministry of National Defense said on 12 October 2021 that South Korean and US intelligence were analysing the equipment on display (Hankyoreh).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One travel guide entry from an outlet opened discusses the exhibition; it is sceptical of the technical displays and is not art criticism.
1. Lonely Planet: "Three Revolutions Exhibition" (undated entry)
URL: https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/three-revolutions-exhibition/435030
- Compares the complex to a theme park and says the technical exhibits look more like antiques than advances.
- Criticises the displays, not the exhibition's funding.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia says the Three Revolutions Movement has been compared to China's Cultural Revolution, and cites the author Jiyoung Song that its goal was to turn every North Korean into a "Juche-style communist".
- The exhibition is named for that movement and presents its results.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the exhibition is the North Korean state's, and the party addresses it as a tool of mass education. The weak points are the sanctions basis under Executive Order 13722 and the use of the halls in 2021 to show the state's missiles. Worth watching: which fairs and weapons shows the site hosts next.
