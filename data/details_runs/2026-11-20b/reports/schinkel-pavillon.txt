SCHINKEL PAVILLON: Berlin, Germany
ID: schinkel-pavillon
Website: https://www.schinkelpavillon.de/ (checked 2026-11-20; the page loads its content by script and showed only "Loading" to the automated check)

OVERVIEW
- An octagonal, fully glazed exhibition building on Oberwallstrasse in Berlin-Mitte, in the park of the Kronprinzenpalais near Unter den Linden (German Wikipedia; Berliner Zeitung, 27 November 2024).
- The East German architect Richard Paulick built it in 1969; in GDR times it housed the restaurant Schinkelklause, and its entrance holds a cast-iron portal of about 1835 from Schinkel's Bauakademie, demolished in 1962 (German Wikipedia).
- The art association Schinkel Pavillon e.V., founded in 2007, runs it as a platform for experimental contemporary sculpture, installation and media art, curated by the artist Nina Pohl (German Wikipedia; Schinkel Pavillon website).
- The programme picks up the Kronprinzenpalais's history: from 1919 to 1937 it housed the Nationalgalerie's "Gallery of the Living" (Schinkel Pavillon on e-flux, 2022).
- Artists shown include Mike Kelley, Isa Genzken, Franz West, Paul McCarthy, Cyprien Gaillard and Thomas Hirschhorn (German Wikipedia); by 2022 over 400 artists had taken part, and the performance series "Disappearing Berlin" has run at sites slated for demolition since 2019 (e-flux, 2022).
- Autumn 2026: a Helmar Lerski solo show, the first since the 1980s, from 10 September to 20 December 2026, part of "Projections onto the East" with the Neuer Berliner Kunstverein, conceived by Fabian Bechtle and Leon Kahane (Kulturstiftung des Bundes).

FUNDING
Schinkel Pavillon: run by an art association, with Berlin public money and federal project grants
- 2024: the Pavillon said it could lose 50 per cent of its funding and close for good under the Berlin Senate's plan to cut arts funding by 13 per cent, about 130 million euros, in 2025 (ArtDog Istanbul, 10 December 2024).
- "Projections onto the East" (2026) is funded by the Kulturstiftung des Bundes and by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (e-flux, 26 August 2026).
- In 2013 the Berlin Senate gave the association its 30,000-euro prize for artistic, self-organised project spaces (German Wikipedia).
- In 2015 a benefit auction of works given by 54 artists, among them John Baldessari, Cindy Sherman and Rosemarie Trockel, paid for converting the former Schinkelklause on the ground floor and secured the institution's future (German Wikipedia).

Not related: the Neuer Pavillon in the Charlottenburg palace gardens, also called the Schinkel-Pavillon, a building by Karl Friedrich Schinkel from 1824 and 1825 (German Wikipedia). It is a different building, not run by this association.

WIDER CONTEXT
- KW Institute, Künstlerhaus Bethanien, n.b.k. and nGbK warned that the Senate's cuts would set off a "domino effect"; culture senator Joe Chialo called them "very drastic and brutal" (ArtDog Istanbul, 10 December 2024).
- Der Freitag (Laura Ewert, 13 December 2024) reported that the CDU-SPD Senate planned to cut about 12 per cent of the culture budget and that the Schinkel Pavillon "stellt sein Aus in Aussicht" (translated: "has raised the prospect of its end").
- Berliner Zeitung (Ingeborg Ruthe, 27 November 2024; commentary, paywalled, only the opening was read) asked under the headline "Gibt es für Berlins beliebteste Kunstorte bald nur noch die halbe Ration?" (translated: "Will Berlin's favourite art venues soon get only half rations?") whether the cuts would starve such spaces.
- The Pavillon depends on the Berlin culture budget these cuts target.

BOTTOM LINE
The public money behind the Pavillon is clean. The weak point is its dependence on a shrinking Berlin culture budget; watch whether it keeps its full programme.
