ID: museo-nazionale-del-cinema

WIDER CONTEXT
- In November 2012 Ken Loach refused the Turin Film Festival's Gran Premio Torino lifetime achievement award in protest at a labour dispute at the National Film Museum, the festival's parent organisation, after cleaning and security services at the museum were outsourced, with cut wages and several workers fired (The Hollywood Reporter, 21 November 2012).
- Loach said: "The lowest-paid workers, the most vulnerable, thus lost their jobs for their opposition to a pay cut" (The Hollywood Reporter).
- The museum replied that Loach had been "badly informed", that the outsourcing had been carried out as legally prescribed after a competitive bidding process, and that "The museum cannot be held responsible for the actions of third parties, either directly or indirectly" (The Hollywood Reporter).
- In an open letter of 22 November 2012 the USB union said workers of the Rear cooperative, outsourced at the museum for 12 years, had their pay cut by 10 percent in June 2011 (later 6 percent), that five were dismissed, and that a Turin labour judge ruled on 6 June 2012 that the dismissal of one union representative was unlawful (USB Piemonte, 22 November 2012).
