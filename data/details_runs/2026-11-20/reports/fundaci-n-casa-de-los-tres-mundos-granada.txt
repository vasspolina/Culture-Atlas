FUNDACIÓN CASA DE LOS TRES MUNDOS: Granada, Nicaragua
ID: fundaci-n-casa-de-los-tres-mundos-granada
Website: https://c3mundos.org (checked 2026-11-20; an archive copy of the foundation's site, last post dated 28 May 2019)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre in the Casa de los Leones (House of the Lions) on the Plaza de los Leones in Granada, a house dated to 1720 and named for the two stone lions at its portal (Casa de los Tres Mundos, Confidencial).
- The Austrian actor Dietmar Schönherr and the Nicaraguan poet Ernesto Cardenal acquired the building from the heirs of Julio Cardenal in 1987 and created the foundation, according to its own site. Pan y Arte, its German support association, and La Verdad Nica date the centre's founding to 1989.
- Julio Cardenal Argüello rebuilt the house in 1924 with a neoclassical facade and a second floor, and it served as a residence, shop, factory, toy workshop and private primary school until 1987 (Casa de los Tres Mundos).
- The main building and first two patios were restored by 1992 using traditional methods, the auditorium was finished in 1994 and the old Granada courthouse was added and restored (Casa de los Tres Mundos).
- In 1992 the centre was inaugurated and "given to the city with the condition of always being a cultural center" (Casa de los Tres Mundos).
- Programmes listed on the archive site: a music school, the Infantilarte art school, an artists' studio, a graphic studio, a school of scenic arts, LoCreo, local development in Malacatoya, Música en los Barrios, Radio Volcán, a municipal archive and an art library.
- Dieter Stadler was director and Johannes Kranz deputy director in 2023, when LoCreo reached more than 300 children a month and Infantilarte had 40 pupils (Pan y Arte annual report 2023).
- The house has been closed since 23 August 2024 (Pan y Arte). Radio Volcán, shut with it, employed about 20 workers (La Verdad Nica, Augusto Cermeño, 27 August 2024).

FUNDING
Fundación Casa de los Tres Mundos: funded by German donors through the association Pan y Arte, after a restoration paid by European governments and the City of Granada
- Restoration: the foundation's history page thanks the German Foreign Ministry (funds for the conservation of monuments), the federal government of Austria (North-South Institute), the Swiss government (COSUDE), the Dutch government (News Project) and the City of Granada (stated by the space).
- Stadler put the cost of the restoration at more than 500,000 dollars in March 2015; in October 2015 the foundation's authorities gave about one million dollars (La Prensa).
- Running costs: Pan y Arte e.V. of Münster, a registered association since 1994, founded by Schönherr, paid 85,752 euros of project funding to the Casa in 2023 and 135,396 euros to Música en los Barrios, whose head also ran the Casa's music school (Pan y Arte annual report 2023).
- Pan y Arte's income in 2023 was 574,717 euros: 415,481 euros of donations, 61,517 euros of bequests and 4,525 euros of grants. The report calls private individual donations its main source of income.
- The Dietmar Schönherr und Luise Scherf-Stiftung für Pan y Arte, set up in Bremen on 30 May 2006, held 1.78 million euros at the end of 2023 and gave Pan y Arte 90,000 euros that year, including 35,000 euros for the two music programmes and 15,000 euros for LoCreo.
- Own income: the foundation rented out a seminar hall for 45 people, an auditorium for up to 250 and the whole house for events (stated by the space).
- After the closure Pan y Arte kept paying the salaries of its project staff in Nicaragua until the end of 2024 (Pan y Arte annual report 2024).

Red flags: Court case over one eighth of the house, 2004 to 2015
- Beatriz Cardenal, one of eight heirs, did not sign the sale, and the land registry entered only seven eighths of the house in the foundation's name (Dieter Stadler, quoted by La Prensa, 5 March 2015).
- A lawsuit to divide the property began in 2004. On 4 March 2015 Judge Iveth Toruño of Granada's second civil district court put her son, Roberto Fuentes Cardenal, in possession of the disputed part, chained two doors and closed the upper floor used by the music school (La Prensa).
- The foundation's lawyer, Ernesto Zambrana, said the Granada appeals court had recognised Fuentes's share but ruled that a heritage building cannot be split, only sold or auctioned (La Prensa, 5 March 2015).
- The judge set 30 October 2015 for handing over the eighth. The board rejected the order, and artists held a vigil outside the house on 29 October, where people signed against the ruling (La Prensa, 29 and 30 October 2015).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 19 August 2024 the Nicaraguan Executive outlawed the foundation along with 1,499 other nonprofit associations "for alleged noncompliance with the law" and ordered their movable and immovable assets transferred to the State (Confidencial, 12 February 2026).
- Pan y Arte says the same decree dissolved its second partner, the Deutsch-Nicaraguanischer Kulturverein Bertolt Brecht, which ran a library and a book bus. It moved its work to Costa Rica.
- La Verdad Nica reported that the government had begun removing NGOs from circulation about three years before the closure.
- On 11 February 2026 a presidential decree declared the seized building National Cultural Heritage, and Granada City Hall sources told Confidencial an event centre with a restaurant would open there.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the foundation was clean: European public grants for the restoration, then German private donations collected by Pan y Arte and its Bremen foundation, with accounts published each year. The weak point was the title to the house, part of which a Granada court awarded to a Cardenal heir. The Nicaraguan state dissolved the foundation and holds the building; worth watching is who runs the house now.
