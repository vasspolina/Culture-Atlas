KUNSTHAL GENT: Ghent, Belgium
ID: kunsthal-gent
Website: https://kunsthal.gent

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art space at Lange Steenstraat 14 in the Patershol district of Ghent, inside a former Carmelite monastery (stated by the Kunsthal).
- Kunsthal Gent opened in January 2019. In 2018 the hall church and the Old House (Oud Huis) came back into the hands of the City of Ghent, after the Provincial Cultural Centre Caermersklooster used them as exhibition space from 1998 (stated by the Kunsthal).
- The Carmelites bought the site in 1287 and consecrated the church on 11 September 1329; the City of Ghent bought the church in 1881 and used it as a museum until 1925 (stated by the Kunsthal).
- On weekends it is an exhibition space; on weekdays a workplace for artists and arts organisations that also hosts talks, workshops, screenings and performances. It runs three exhibition openings a year, a continuing programme called Endless Exhibition and a development programme, Permanently Practising, that gives a work-period grant each year to six artists, collectives or organisations.
- It shares its rooms with the programmes of Art Cinema OFFoff, the Curatorial Studies programme of KASK & Conservatorium, and das Kunst.
- Boycott declared by the space on its Apartheid Free Zone page: Kunsthal Gent has joined the Apartheid Free Zone campaign of Palestina Solidariteit vzw and Association Belgo Palestinienne, and states that it joins the cultural boycott of Israel together with more than 875 Dutch and Belgian artists and nearly 300 cultural institutions. It says it will not work on art projects financed by the Israeli state or complicit organisations and will not start new collaborations with such parties.
- Visiting: open at weekends 11:00 to 18:00; ticket price is pay what you can. The entrance has a ramp, and the toilets and cinema are on the ground floor; the Old House is accessible only with assistance, and the pavilion above the cinema is reached by stairs.

FUNDING
Kunsthal Gent: supported by the City of Ghent and the Flemish government, stated by the space
- The Kunsthal's pages carry the line "Gesteund door" (supported by) with two logos, labeled for Gent and, by image file name, for Vlaanderen (stated by the space).
- The City of Ghent took back the hall church and the Old House in 2018, and the Kunsthal opened there in January 2019 (history page).
- Weekend admission is pay what you can: visitors set the ticket price themselves.

BOTTOM LINE
Kunsthal Gent is housed in the former Carmelite monastery, which the City of Ghent took back in 2018. Its own site states support from the City of Ghent and the Flemish government.
