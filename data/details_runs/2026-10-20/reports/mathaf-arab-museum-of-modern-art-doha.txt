MATHAF: ARAB MUSEUM OF MODERN ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: mathaf-arab-museum-of-modern-art-doha
Website: https://www.mathaf.org.qa (checked 2026-10-24; live, carries a "© Qatar Museums 2026" notice)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art from the Arab world in Education City, Doha, with over 9,000 objects; it is a member of Qatar Museums (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Grew from the collection Sheikh Hassan bin Mohamed bin Ali Al Thani began in the early 1990s, first kept in two private villas in Madinat Khalifa; donated to Qatar Foundation and later acquired by the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia).
- Opened 30 December 2010 in a former school building converted by the architect Jean-François Bodin; 5,500 square metres (Wikipedia).
- Director since November 2021: Zeina Arida (Wikipedia). On site: permanent collection, temporary exhibitions, an online collection and an encyclopedia of Arab art (museum website).
- Visiting: Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday 9 am to 7 pm; Thursday 9 am to 9 pm; Friday 1:30 to 7 pm; closed Monday. Tickets are sold through the museum's website.

FUNDING
Mathaf: state-funded: Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity
- Qatar Museums was founded in 2005 as a Qatari government entity, formerly the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia). Mathaf's site lists no other supporters.
- Bloomberg reported on 23 October 2013 that Sheikha Al-Mayassa's annual acquisition budget for Qatar Museums is estimated at $1 billion (Wikipedia, citing Bloomberg).
- For Mathaf's January 2020 show at the Palais de Tokyo, Paris, the Palais said the Qatari government never donated to it and production costs were shared with Mathaf (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of Mathaf's own accounts, donors or board.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Mathaf opened on 30 December 2010 and is a member of Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity founded in 2005 and chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa (museum website, Wikipedia).

Red flags: state censorship
- November 2024: Mathaf pulled İnci Eviner's work Harem (2009) at the last minute from "Seeing Is Believing: The Art and Influence of Gérôme", which opened on 3 November 2024. Eviner says director Zeina Arida told her the Qatari ministry of culture ordered the removal. Mathaf confirmed the removal to ARTnews without detail (The Art Newspaper, 11 December 2024).
- 2013: Adel Abdessemed's film Printemps was not shown at Mathaf; officials cited technical issues (openDemocracy, 16 October 2013). Unverified: social media reports said Sheikha Al-Mayassa intervened, from openDemocracy, which calls them unverified.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism of Qatar Museums and its control over what is shown; the Mathaf-specific pieces concern censorship and its use abroad.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anni Irish, "Harem work removed from show at Qatar's Arab Museum of Modern Art" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/12/11/work-removed-from-show-at-qatars-arab-museum-of-modern-art
- Reports the removal of Eviner's Harem on the ministry's order, per the artist, from a show organised with Lusail Museum.
- Eviner calls it censorship.
2. The Art Newspaper: Nikita Dmitriev, "Artists criticise Palais de Tokyo partnership with Qatari museum over LGBTQ rights" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/01/08/artists-criticise-palais-de-tokyo-partnership-with-qatari-museum-over-lgbtq-rights
- Philosopher Yves Michaud called the partnership part of a "shameless and long-term strategy" by the Qatari government to win over French society. Activist Babi Badalov said the gay community will fight to cancel the show.
- The Palais said it partners with a museum, not the Qatari state. Contains no statement from Mathaf.
3. e-flux Criticism: Ana Teixeira Pinto, "PR Kingdom: Press Debriefings and a Bit of Art in Doha. 'Tea with Nefertiti' at Mathaf" (2012)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/234065/pr-kingdom-press-debriefings-and-a-bit-of-art-in-doha-tea-with-nefertiti-at-mathaf
- Describes a press trip organised by PR agencies on behalf of the Qatar Museums Authority, calls the Mathaf show's curatorial concept "somewhat flimsy" and Qatar's sponsorship laws "a euphemism for indentured servitude", and asks whether the collections belong to private sponsors or the state.
4. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Examines 2013 disputes over art in Qatar, including Printemps; says questions remain about public spending and unannounced acquisitions, and calls the authority a "private entity for public good". Not an art outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Amnesty International (16 October 2019): Qatar hosts about two million migrant workers and has consistently failed to protect their rights. Qatar announced kafala reform, which Amnesty said matters if workers can freely leave or change jobs. Its September 2019 report found hundreds of workers waiting for unpaid wages.
- Connection: Mathaf belongs to Qatar Museums. No reporting found of labour abuses on Mathaf's own conversion or operation.

BOTTOM LINE
Mathaf is paid for by the Qatari state through Qatar Museums, with no private donors named; the question is state control. The weak points are the removal of a work in 2024, which the artist says the ministry ordered, and the 2013 case of a withheld film. Worth watching: whether Mathaf publishes accounts and who decides its programme.
