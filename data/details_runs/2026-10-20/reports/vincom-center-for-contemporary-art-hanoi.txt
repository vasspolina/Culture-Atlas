VINCOM CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Hanoi, Vietnam
ID: vincom-center-for-contemporary-art-hanoi
Website: https://vccavietnam.com/en/vingroup-ra-mat-trung-tam-nghe-thuat-duong-dai-vcca (checked 2026-10-24; loads; the page carries no general opening hours)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art centre of about 4,000 square metres in the basement of the Vincom Megamall at Royal City, B1-R3, 72A Nguyen Trai, Thanh Xuan, Hanoi (VCCA website, Viet Nam News).
- Opened on 6 June 2017 with the group show "The Foliage", with 19 Vietnamese and foreign artists including Yoko Ono's "Wish Tree" (VietnamNet, Viet Nam News).
- Artistic director is Mizuki Endo (Viet Nam News). Viet Nam News (November 2017) says VCCA was launched by Dong A Gallery and Vincom.
- Programme in four seasons of about three months, with a Vincom Art Development Fund for conservation and artists' exhibitions. Facilities include a library, tea house, video studios, a creative studio, an art class and climate-controlled storage (VCCA website).
- Visiting: no general opening hours or admission policy on the page read; one exhibition listing says "Free entry".

FUNDING
Vincom Center for Contemporary Art: corporate-funded: developed and financed entirely by Vingroup, a property, retail and car conglomerate
- The centre says it was "developed and financed entirely by Vingroup" (stated by the space). Viet Nam News (2017) says Vingroup, which it calls Viet Nam's biggest real estate developer, sponsored and financed it, and that Deputy Chairwoman Le Mai Lan spoke at the launch.
- Vingroup's founder, largest shareholder and chairman is Pham Nhat Vuong. Its businesses include Vinhomes (housing), Vincom Retail (malls), VinFast (cars), Vinpearl, VinWonders, VinMec, Vinschool and VinUniversity (Wikipedia).
- Vingroup's finances are under strain: Asia Times (opinion) reports liabilities of $31 billion by Q2 2025, and says Moody's and Fitch downgraded Vinhomes' debt to junk in January 2025.
- No reporting found of any other donor, grant or partner of the centre, or of a published budget.

Red flags: Vingroup's 2025 defamation suits
- On 8 September 2025 Vingroup announced civil suits against 68 individuals and organisations over what it called false information about its finances, product origin and leadership. It named the Cybersecurity Law and the Penal Code as the laws it says the posts breach (CleanTechnica).
- Some targets live abroad; Vingroup planned to work with foreign lawyers and notify embassies (CleanTechnica).
- Critics: Asia Times opinion writer Nguyen Ngoc Nhu Quynh calls such suits companies "enlisting the Ministry of Public Security to intimidate critics" under the 2018 Cybersecurity Law; she does not document the ministry's role in these cases.
- Vingroup disputes rumours of near-bankruptcy and of VinFast being a disguised Chinese product (CleanTechnica).
- The suits concern the group, not the art centre. No censorship at the centre was found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the centre found in art media. Coverage is mostly launch reporting and one curator interview.

- Do co-curated "The Foliage III" at VCCA, hesitated because of the mall's capitalist setting, and said the organisers "don't really understand" the subversive content.
- Not criticism of the institution; it describes no censorship or formal restrictions.
2. Viet Nam News: "Yoko Ono project at new art centre" (2017)

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is paid for entirely by Vingroup, so its money is clean of any outside patron but not of its owner's record: the group's 2025 lawsuits against 68 critics and its heavy debt. No censorship at the centre was found, and the centre publishes no budget or board. Worth watching: Vingroup's finances and whether the centre's programme continues.
