NATIONAL TAIWAN MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS (國立臺灣美術館): Taichung, Taiwan
ID: national-taiwan-museum-of-fine-arts-taichung
Website: https://www.ntmofa.gov.tw (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum at No. 2, Section 1, Wuquan West Road, West District, Taichung, known as NTMoFA; Chinese name Guoli Taiwan Meishuguan (國立臺灣美術館; romanisation ours) (museum website; English Wikipedia).
- Opened on 26 June 1988 under the Taiwan Provincial Government's education department as the Museum of Art; it closed in 1999 after damage in the 921 earthquake and reopened in July 2004 (English Wikipedia).
- It came under the Council of Cultural Affairs, restructured as the Ministry of Culture on 20 May 2012 (English Wikipedia).
- Building: four storeys with 37,953 square metres of building area, 16 galleries and 13,525 square metres of exhibition space, in a 102,000-square-metre outdoor area with a sculpture park and a forest of 50 calligraphy stone tablets (English Wikipedia).
- Programme: it runs the Asian Art Biennial (from 2007), the Taiwan Biennial (from 2008) and the International Biennial Print Exhibition, and has organised the Taiwan Pavilion at the Venice Architecture Biennale since 2000 (English Wikipedia).
- Director: Chen Kuang-yi (陳貺怡), an art historian with a doctorate from Paris X, who thanks Premier Chen Chien-jen and Culture Minister Shih Che for her appointment (museum website, director's page dated 3 July 2023).
- Visiting: free admission; Tuesday to Friday 09:00 to 17:00, weekends 09:00 to 18:00, closed Monday (museum website).

FUNDING
National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts: mainly public: Taiwan's Ministry of Culture, with small outside gifts
- Central government: in 2025 the museum and its affiliated units, the Living Art Centers in Hsinchu, Changhua, Tainan and Taitung, received NT$681,048,554 from the treasury out of total income of NT$776,752,075; donations and gifts made up NT$74,994,150 (2025 audited final accounts, income and expenditure statement).
- Spending: the museum's own programme spent NT$289,215,701 in 2025 (2025 audited final accounts).
- Earned income: property yields were NT$8,688,967 in 2025, of which NT$6,704,801 were concession fees; the catering concession and a parking concession in Changhua brought in more than budgeted (2025 audited final accounts).
- Cash gifts in 2025: NT$5,642,970 in 27 gifts. 成幼方 (Cheng You-fang; romanisation ours) gave NT$1,000,000 for collection care and purchases. 築遠工程顧問有限公司, 富盛建材股份有限公司, 台灣引興股份有限公司, 財團法人紀慧能藝術文化基金會 and named individuals gave to the 國美藝術大使計畫 (Art Ambassador programme; translation ours). 呂永豐, 張秋墩 and 浩瀚開發建設股份有限公司 paid towards the Liao Chi-chun exhibition. The academic and cultural section (CCAC) of 法國在台協會, France's office in Taipei, paid artists' flights and a catalogue (museum donation list, 2025; names as listed). Some donor names are masked.
- Cash gifts in January to June 2026: NT$2,411,873, including NT$200,000 from the E.SUN Cultural and Educational Foundation (財團法人玉山文教基金會) for a volunteer education programme (museum donation list, 2026).

WIDER CONTEXT
- August 2026 naming dispute at the 16th Gwangju Biennale: the pavilion's curator Chen Xiang-Wen and nine artists said on 15 August that the proposal the museum submitted as "Taiwan Pavilion" had been renamed "NTMoFA" without their consent (The Art Newspaper, 26 August 2026).
- The Gwangju Biennale Foundation said the museum signed on 12 January 2026 as an institutional participant, so the pavilion bore its name; Taiwan's Ministry of Culture said the name had been agreed in an agreement signed on 2 April and called the foundation's account wrong (Central News Agency, 19 August 2026; The Art Newspaper).
- The artists, the ministry and twenty other contributors called the change "political censorship"; the organisers said they considered the listing an administrative convention (ArtReview, 2026). On 25 August the foundation approved the "Taiwan Pavilion" name (The Art Newspaper).
- Gwangju art groups backed the Taiwanese side and covered "NTMoFA" with "Taiwan" on a poster (udn, Central News Agency, 24 August 2026).
- Connection: the objection was aimed at the biennale foundation; the museum was the pavilion's organiser, and its acronym replaced Taiwan's name.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public money, with small private gifts that the museum publishes. No funder carries a flag.
