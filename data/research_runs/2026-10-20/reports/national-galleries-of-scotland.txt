NATIONAL GALLERIES OF SCOTLAND – Edinburgh, UK
Website: https://www.nationalgalleries.org (support page did not load on 2026-10-20; the Baillie Gifford statement page and the 2023-24 accounts PDF did load)

OVERVIEW
- Scotland's national art collection, a public body and charity (SC003758), with four Edinburgh sites. 2.63 million visits in 2024-25 (Edinburgh Evening News, July 2026).
- Chair: Benny Higgins. Director-general: Anne Lyden, in post since January 2024 (2023-24 accounts; The Art Newspaper).

FUNDING
National Galleries of Scotland: mainly public (Scottish Government), plus corporate sponsors, trusts and patrons.
- Scottish Government grant-in-aid in 2023-24: £22.1M cash (£19.1M running costs, £3.1M capital). That is 68% of income; the remaining 32% was self-generated. Membership income was £419,942 (2023-24 accounts). The Scotsman (July 2024) put government funding at around £26M a year.
- £56M of Scottish Government money over three years for "The Art Works", a collections building in Granton costing at least £100M. The rest is sought from the UK government and philanthropists (The Art Newspaper, June 2026).
- Baillie Gifford: corporate sponsor; length and value not disclosed (stated by the space, 1 July 2024).
- The 2023-24 accounts name the Art Fund, the Walton Fund and the American Patrons of the National Library and National Galleries of Scotland. They name no corporate sponsors.

Red flags: Kept Baillie Gifford despite campaigns
- On 1 July 2024 NGS said it would keep Baillie Gifford, which it said meets its ethical standards after an ethics committee review and consultation with the Scottish Government (NGS statement; The Art Newspaper; Arts Professional).
- Campaigners (Fossil Free Books; Art Workers for Palestine Scotland, an activist group) object to the firm's fossil-fuel and Israel-linked holdings. Unverified: activist claims about the scale of those holdings, which the firm disputes; Baillie Gifford says 2% of client money is in fossil-fuel-linked companies.
- Baillie Gifford's own sponsorship directory did not list NGS when checked on 2026-10-20.

Red flags: BP Portrait Award hosting ended 2019
- In Nov 2019 NGS said it would no longer host the BP-sponsored BP Portrait Award exhibition, citing the climate emergency. BP's sponsorship was with the National Portrait Gallery, London, which organised the award; NGS was the host (The Art Newspaper; The Ecologist).

Not related: The National Gallery (London) and the National Portrait Gallery (London) — separate UK national museums.

BOTTOM LINE
Funding is mainly Scottish Government money. The main point of dispute is that it chose to keep Baillie Gifford in 2024, when smaller Edinburgh galleries dropped the firm. The record's "dropped BP 2019" should read "stopped hosting the BP Portrait Award exhibition", because NGS was not itself BP's sponsee.
