KUNSTHALLE MÜNSTER: Münster, Germany
ID: kunsthalle-münster-münster
Website: https://www.kunsthallemuenster.de/de/ueber/

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art venue on the fifth floor of Speicher II, a former grain store, at Hafenweg 28 in the Stadthafen (harbour district) of Münster; it has been in these rooms since 2004.
- Opened on 19 September 1991 as the municipal Ausstellungshalle am Hawerkamp under its first director Gail B. Kirkpatrick; renamed AZKM in 2004 and Kunsthalle Münster in 2012 (stated by the Kunsthalle; German Wikipedia gives the 1991 opening and the 2012 name).
- Part of the Kulturamt (culture office) of the City of Münster. Merle Radtke has led it since 1 July 2018, and her first programme opened on 14 December 2018 with a solo exhibition by Mary Beth Edelson (Wikipedia).
- It has no collection of its own but manages the city's collection of art in public space, which grows with each Skulptur Projekte edition (every ten years) and holds works by Daniel Buren, Claes Oldenburg, Donald Judd, Rebecca Horn, Per Kirkeby, Maria Nordman and Oscar Tuazon. Since June 2018 the Kunsthalle has been responsible for the content care of Gerhard Richter's installation "Zwei graue Doppelspiegel für ein Pendel" in the Dominikanerkirche (council paper V/0899/2018).
- The building also holds the Galerie Mike Karstens; the council paper names Speicher II among the city's large studio houses. The Kunsthalle hosts Residence NRW+, begun in 2020: four 12-month scholarships a year for visual artists up to age 40 with ties to NRW at EUR 1,450 a month, and four 6-month scholarships for curators up to age 35 at EUR 1,250 a month.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 12:00 to 18:00, free admission. No fully barrier-free entrance: a six-step staircase or a ramp about 110 m away; a lift serves the floors and staff arrange step-free access to the fifth-floor exhibition space; once there, the rooms are step-free and doorways are 195 cm wide. Assistance dogs are welcome.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Münster: municipal institution: City of Münster budget, with project grants and a friends' association
- City of Münster: the Kunsthalle is part of the Kulturamt and the council paper below calls it a "städtische Institution" (municipal institution).
- Budget: a council paper dated 9 November 2018 (V/0899/2018, "Stärkung des Fachbereichs Bildende Kunst", answering a motion of the CDU and Bündnis 90/Die Grünen/GAL groups) states that about EUR 84,000 from the city budget and 1.0 full-time post were available for the visual arts department, whose programme therefore depends heavily on third-party funds. Third-party money had raised the budget to an average of EUR 160,000 a year: about EUR 25,000 in donations from Freundeskreis members and about EUR 55,000 from private initiatives, corporate-linked foundations and public bodies, among them the British Council, Pro Helvetia, the Mondriaan Fund, the Kunststiftung NRW and the NRW culture ministry.
- The same paper splits the EUR 160,000 as about EUR 70,000 for staff and fee contracts, about EUR 85,000 for the international exhibition programme of three shows a year plus the Kunstakademie Münster prize exhibition, and about EUR 5,000 for education programmes. The upkeep of the sculpture collection is paid by the city's property management office.
- Residence NRW+: the artist scholarships are paid equally by the NRW Ministry of Culture and Science and the Kunststiftung NRW; the page states that Pro Helvetia has co-awarded the residency scholarships since 2020 (stated by the Kunsthalle).
- Freundeskreis der Kunsthalle Münster e.V.: a registered non-profit association that supports the Kunsthalle financially; membership is EUR 20 a year.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Kunsthalle Münster is a city budget line plus project grants from the NRW culture ministry, the Kunststiftung NRW, Pro Helvetia and other public and foundation funders, and members of the Freundeskreis. The budget figures are from the council paper of 9 November 2018.
