CENTRAL LENIN MUSEUM: Moscow, Russia
ID: central-lenin-museum
Website: https://lenin.shm.ru (checked 2026-11-20; a virtual museum of the collection run with the State Historical Museum)

OVERVIEW
- Former central museum of Vladimir Lenin in the old Moscow City Duma building by architect Dmitry Chichagov, on Revolution Square next to Red Square (ru.wikipedia; The Moscow Times).
- On 31 May 1924 the Moscow Committee of the Communist Party decided to set up a Lenin Institute with a museum department; the Central Lenin Museum was created in September 1935 and opened on 15 May 1936 (lenin.shm.ru).
- It was rebuilt on a large scale for Lenin's centenary in 1970 and received the Order of Lenin in 1974 (lenin.shm.ru). Branches opened from 1937 to 1990, from Leningrad, Kyiv and Tbilisi to Tashkent and Kuibyshev (ru.wikipedia).
- It was disbanded in 1993 by order of the Russian president (lenin.shm.ru), in November after the October 1993 events in Moscow; from December 1993 its collections and buildings became a branch of the State Historical Museum (ru.wikipedia).
- The collection holds over 100,000 items, long closed to the public (lenin.shm.ru).
- In 2014 the building held "The Myth of the Beloved Leader", about 1,000 items from the archive, some shown for the first time (The Moscow Times).
- Since 4 September 2012 the site at Ploshchad Revolyutsii 2/3 has housed the State Historical Museum's Museum of the Patriotic War of 1812; adult tickets cost 2,000 roubles (shm.ru; ru.wikipedia).

FUNDING
Central Lenin Museum: closed; its building and collection are held by the State Historical Museum under Russia's Ministry of Culture
- Until 1991 it was an institution of the Communist Party, whose activities were suspended that year (lenin.shm.ru).
- The State Historical Museum has held the building and collection since December 1993 (ru.wikipedia).
- Russia's Ministry of Culture extended the contract of the State Historical Museum's director, Alexei Levykin, for five years in February 2023; he has led it since 24 June 2010 (BFM).

Red flags: built to house the Lenin cult (1936 to 1991)
- The museum was created on a Communist Party decision and opened in 1936 as the main Lenin museum of the USSR (lenin.shm.ru).
- After the 1956 campaign against Stalin's personality cult, Sergei Mikhalkov cut all mention of Stalin from later editions of his poem about the museum; Stalin items were kept in the holdings despite orders to destroy them (ru.wikipedia).

Red flags: sanctions on Russia's culture minister (2022 to 2023)
- The EU listed culture minister Olga Lyubimova in December 2022, saying her ministry gave money and programme support to the so-called "Donetsk People's Republic" (The Art Newspaper).
- The US Treasury listed her on 24 February 2023 (The Art Newspaper), and the UK on 17 July 2023, with asset freezes (Interfax).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is of the 2014 archive exhibition, which looked at the Soviet cult critically.
1. The Moscow Times: Kevin O'Flynn, "Red Square Exhibit Sheds New Light on Soviet Cult of Lenin" (2014)
URL: https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2014/10/26/red-square-exhibit-sheds-new-light-on-soviet-cult-of-lenin-40765-a40765
- The new Russian state closed the museum after brief perestroika-era displays, and the building stood closed for much of two decades.
- Quotes staff member Olga Grankina, there since 1976: the aim is for people to think about what happened in the country.

BOTTOM LINE
A party museum that no longer exists; what remains is a federal holding of a ministry whose head is under Western sanctions. Worth watching: how the State Historical Museum uses the Lenin collection.
