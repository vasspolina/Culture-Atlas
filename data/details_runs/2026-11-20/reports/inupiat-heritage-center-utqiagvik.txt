IÑUPIAT HERITAGE CENTER: Utqiaġvik, Alaska, USA
ID: inupiat-heritage-center-utqiagvik
Website: https://www.north-slope.org/departments/inupiat-history-language-culture/inupiat-heritage-center/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural centre at 5421 N Star St, Utqiaġvik, on Alaska's North Slope (National Park Service; North Slope Borough).
- Dedicated in February 1999 (National Park Service).
- An affiliated area of New Bedford Whaling National Historical Park in Massachusetts: one of several partners that tell the story of commercial whaling and of the Alaska Natives who crewed, fed, clothed and sheltered the Yankee whalers (National Park Service).
- Houses exhibits, artefact collections, a library, a gift shop and a traditional room where elders and artists in residence teach crafts (National Park Service). The traditional room is also used to build and repair whaling boats and other subsistence tools (North Slope Borough).
- Its multipurpose room and classroom are rented out for events (North Slope Borough).
- Staff in the 2026 to 2027 budget: five posts, a museum curator, a collection management technician and three museum interns (North Slope Borough budget).

FUNDING
Iñupiat Heritage Center: public: the North Slope Borough, funded mainly by taxes on oil and gas infrastructure
- Owner and operator: the North Slope Borough, through its Department of Iñupiat History, Language and Culture (National Park Service; North Slope Borough).
- Running costs: the centre's cost centre, 2805.IHCC, is budgeted at US$707,803 for fiscal 2026 to 2027, down from $714,623; $599,343 goes on staff and $88,000 on electricity, gas, water and sewer (North Slope Borough budget). The whole department is budgeted at $5,332,296 (same).
- Where the borough's money comes from: taxes on oil and gas infrastructure pay for more than 90 percent of its annual budget, then $400 million (Alaska Public Media, 2019).
- In 2019 Governor Mike Dunleavy proposed moving that taxing power to the state, which would have cut $370 million from the borough's budget; the borough's director of government and external affairs, D.J. Fauske, said it would destroy the borough (Alaska Public Media, 2019).
- Federal grant: in fiscal 2016 the Institute of Museum and Library Services gave the borough, for the centre, $150,000 to digitise 1,648 audiovisual recordings made by Iñupiaq people, with the digitising done by Summit Day Media (IMLS, award MA-30-16-0478-16).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Alaska Public Media reported in 2019 that ConocoPhillips was pushing three new oil projects in the National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska, Willow and Greater Moose's Tooth 1 and 2, and that royalty and property tax revenue from the reserve could reach $700 million a year for the borough and its villages.
- Connection: the borough budget that pays for the centre grows with that oil development.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean of corporate sponsors, but not of oil: the public money behind the centre depends on North Slope drilling, and any change in how that revenue is shared would reach its budget.
