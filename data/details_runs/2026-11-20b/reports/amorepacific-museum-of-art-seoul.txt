AMOREPACIFIC MUSEUM OF ART: Seoul, South Korea
ID: amorepacific-museum-of-art-seoul
Website: https://apma.amorepacific.com/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Corporate art museum in the Amorepacific headquarters at Hangang-daero 100, Yongsan-gu, Seoul (museum site), David Chipperfield's "luminous cube" (W Magazine, 27 August 2025).
- Founded in 1979 on the art collected by Amorepacific's founder Suh Sung-whan (서성환), opening as a cosmetics museum in Daebang-dong; renamed Pacific Museum (태평양박물관) in 1997 and moved to Yongin, The Amore Museum in 2005 and Amorepacific Museum of Art in 2009, and reopened in Yongsan in 2018 (Encyclopedia of Korean Culture).
- It collects Korean historical art and Korean and international contemporary art, and supports researchers, scholarly societies and young artists (Encyclopedia of Korean Culture).
- Directed by Jeon Seung-chang (전승창) (museum press releases of December 2025 and of 21 May 2026, the latter on Hankyung).
- 2026 programme: "APMA, Chapter Five", about 50 works by over 40 artists from the collection, 1 April to 2 August (press release, 23 December 2025), then "Sol LeWitt: Open Structure", the artist's first large solo show in Korea, with 45 works including 13 wall drawings, a touring show organised with the Sol LeWitt Foundation and the Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (exhibition page).
- The December 2025 programme had announced a Jonas Wood survey for 1 September 2026 to 28 February 2027; the museum's site now lists the LeWitt show for the second half of 2026 (press release; exhibition page).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; closed Mondays, 1 January and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays; the LeWitt show takes online bookings and on-site tickets, with priority for bookings at busy times (museum site). W Magazine (2025) described admission as free.

FUNDING
Amorepacific Museum of Art: corporate-owned: Amorepacific Corp., a cosmetics company
- The museum site is run by Amorepacific Corp. (㈜아모레퍼시픽), CEO Kim Seung-hwan (김승환), at the headquarters address (museum site footer), and the museum sits in the company's headquarters (W Magazine).
- Amorepacific grew from the camellia oil business of Yun Dokjeong; her son Suh Sung-whan took it over in 1945 and handed it to his second son Suh Kyung-bae in 1997, who chairs it (Wikipedia).
- The parent is Amorepacific Holdings; 2025 revenue was 4.25 trillion won and net income 247.3 billion won (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Korea Fair Trade Commission fined Amorepacific 500 million won in August 2014 for forcing door-to-door sales agencies to move about 3,480 high-performing sales staff to new or struggling agencies from 2005 to June 2013; the commission said this was the highest fine it could impose (Korea JoongAng Daily, 18 August 2014).
- In April 2020 the commission fined Amorepacific Group and its affiliate Cosvision 48 million won each, 96 million won in all, over 75 billion won in fee-free fixed deposits that let Cosvision take five low-interest Korea Development Bank loans worth 60 billion won; the article also prints 9.6 billion won, at odds with its headline (Korea Times).
- Both cases concern the museum's owner, not the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: a company museum paid for by a cosmetics business, with nothing under a flag.
