TOKYO ARTS AND SPACE: Tokyo, Japan
ID: tokyo-arts-and-space-tokyo
Website: https://www.tokyoartsandspace.jp

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre of the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture, with an exhibition venue, TOKAS Hongo, at 2-4-16 Hongo, Bunkyo-ku, and an artist residency, TOKAS Residency, at 2-14-7 Tatekawa, Sumida-ku (Artsticker, Internet Museum).
- Established in 2001 as Tokyo Wonder Site, an initiative for supporting young artists, and renamed Tokyo Arts and Space in 2017 (ASEF culture360, Artsticker). Japanese Wikipedia says the project started on Governor Shintaro Ishihara's initiative and opened in 2001 as Tokyo Wonder Site Hongo.
- The Creator-in-Residence Program launched in 2006 and moved to the Sumida site in 2014 (Artsticker).
- The Hongo building dates from 1928 and was used for a long time as a vocational school and an education office (Internet Museum).
- The Tokyo Contemporary Art Award, an award for mid-career artists active in Japan, was established in 2018 by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and Tokyo Arts and Space (ASEF culture360; e-flux). Each of the two recipients gets a prize of 3,000,000 yen and up to 1,000,000 yen for overseas activities, plus an exhibition at the Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo (Tokyo Metropolitan Government award outline, 2021).
- Visiting: TOKAS Hongo is open 11:00 to 19:00, last entry 30 minutes before closing, closed Mondays (Tuesday if Monday is a holiday); admission is listed as free but varies by event. There is no parking; the nearest stations are a 7-minute walk away (Internet Museum).

FUNDING
Tokyo Arts and Space: run by the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture, whose parent body is the Tokyo Metropolitan Government (Japanese Wikipedia)
- The operator is the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture, formed in 1995 by the merger of the Tokyo Culture Promotion Foundation (1982) and the Edo-Tokyo Historical Foundation (1990). It became a public interest foundation in April 2010 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Japanese Wikipedia states that the foundation's parent body is the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's Bureau of Culture, Sports and Lifestyle and that it is designated a Tokyo policy-partner organisation.
- Tokyo Wonder Site's budget: Japanese Wikipedia states that its budget rose more than eightfold, from 55.89 million yen to 471.52 million yen, while the budgets of the Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum and the Tokyo Bunka Kaikan were cut by nearly 40 percent over the same period.
- The Tokyo Contemporary Art Award is a joint creation of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and Tokyo Arts and Space (e-flux, 5 April 2019).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Assembly, 2006: in the fourth regular session of 2006, Japanese Communist Party member Nobuo Yoshida told the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly that Tokyo Wonder Site was run as a personal project of the governor's family, citing Ishihara's fourth son, the painter Nobuhiro Ishihara, and the appointment of Imamura and his wife as director and deputy director. The Culture Bureau director-general answered that no remuneration had been paid to Nobuhiro Ishihara for a stage backdrop, that public spending on him was about 600,000 yen in travel costs, and that 830,000 yen in adviser fees had been paid to the account of Kayoko Iemura, whom it had commissioned as a facility adviser in 2001. Yoshida had said that 730,000 yen reached the account of Imamura's wife in March 2002, before her formal appointment. Ishihara answered that he would correct and apologise if any spending were illegal, and called the line of questioning "a poor way of thinking, typical of the Communist Party" (Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly net report; translation).
- J-CAST News, 12 December 2006: reported that the Tokyo government paid about 550,000 yen for Nobuhiro Ishihara's travel on a 2003 Europe research trip for Tokyo Wonder Site, and that about 1.2 million yen of public money paid to a performer covered a January 2004 trip to paint a backdrop. It quotes Ishihara at a 24 November 2006 press conference: "If someone irreplaceable were needed, I'd use anyone" (translation).
- Japanese Wikipedia states that Imamura, a Tokyo government adviser, and his wife took the director and deputy director posts through their acquaintance with Ishihara.
- Connection: this concerns the programme as it ran under its earlier name, Tokyo Wonder Site, which the governor started and the Tokyo government funded and controlled.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Tokyo Arts and Space is Tokyo government money, channelled through the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture (Japanese Wikipedia). The documented controversy is the 2006 row in the Tokyo assembly and in J-CAST over Governor Ishihara's son and the director's appointment at Tokyo Wonder Site.
