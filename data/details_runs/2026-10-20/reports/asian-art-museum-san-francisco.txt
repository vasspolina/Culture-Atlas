ASIAN ART MUSEUM: San Francisco, USA
ID: asian-art-museum-san-francisco
Website: https://asianart.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- City-owned museum of Asian art at 200 Larkin Street in San Francisco's Civic Center; the City owns the building and the permanent collection.
- Grew from the collection of Chicago industrialist and IOC president Avery Brundage; it opened in 1966 in a bond-funded wing of the de Young, and Brundage gave more than 7,700 objects by his death in 1975.
- Full name: Asian Art Museum of San Francisco, Chong-Moon Lee Center for Asian Art and Culture, for the Silicon Valley entrepreneur who gave $15 million in 1995 to start the building campaign.
- Housed since 20 March 2003 in George Kelham's 1917 Beaux-Arts former Main Library, rebuilt by Gae Aulenti with base isolation for earthquakes; the Akiko Yamazaki and Jerry Yang Pavilion opened in July 2023.
- Led since 2025 by Soyoung Lee, after Jay Xu.
- Open Thursday 1pm to 8pm and Friday to Monday 10am to 5pm, closed Tuesday and Wednesday; adults $20, 65 and over $17, ages 13 to 17 and students $14, 12 and under free; general admission free on the first Sunday of each month.

FUNDING
Asian Art Museum: mixed: City of San Francisco and private donors through the Asian Art Museum Foundation
- Roughly a third of the budget has historically come from the City; after the mayor's office asked institutions to plan for a 15 percent cut, the museum faced laying off 13 security guards, which would save the City $1.7 million (The Art Newspaper, March 2025).
- Asian Art Museum Foundation of San Francisco (EIN 94-1704765), FY to June 2025: revenue $28,079,494, of which contributions $20,319,541; expenses $39,437,939; net assets $164,254,424; Jay Xu was paid $661,595 (Form 990 via ProPublica).
- Corporate partners include Bank of America, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Google, Kaiser Permanente, Pacific Gas and Electric Company, Prologis, TPG Capital and COSCO Shipping Global Exhibition Services (Beijing); membership runs from $2,500 to $25,000 (stated by the space, undated).
- Foundation partners include the Koret Foundation, Mellon Foundation and Lilly Endowment (stated by the space).

Red flags: PG&E, a gas and electric utility
- PG&E is a corporate member; it supplies natural gas and electricity to 5.2 million households in northern California (Wikipedia).
- PG&E pleaded guilty on 16 June 2020 to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter over the 2018 Camp Fire (Wikipedia).

Red flags: COSCO Shipping, on the US list of Chinese military companies
- COSCO Shipping Global Exhibition Services (Beijing) is a corporate partner (stated by the space).
- On 7 January 2025 the US Department of Defense added COSCO to its list of Chinese military companies; the listing carries no specific penalties (Hellenic Shipping News).

Red flags: founder Avery Brundage
- Activists demanded removal of Brundage's bust, on show since 1972; the artist Chiraag Bhakta called it a "mixed message" beside the museum's Black Lives Matter post (ArtAsiaPacific, 2020).
- Jay Xu wrote on 4 June 2020 that Brundage "espoused racist and anti-Semitic views," and the bust went to storage; he pledged a provenance review.
- Unverified: Brundage's collection was based mainly on stock confiscated from Japanese-American dealers during the Second World War, from ArtAsiaPacific (2020), which gives it as a report without naming a source.

Red flags: Thai lintels
- The Department of Justice filed a forfeiture complaint in October 2020 for two sandstone lintels from temples in northeastern Thailand, after a Homeland Security Investigations probe; the case settled in February 2021 and they returned in May 2021 (Hyperallergic).
- The museum called the complaint "unmerited" and said there was "no affirmative evidence" of illegal removal.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the founder and the lintels, in news reports; no critic found makes a case against the museum's current funding.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Jae Lamb, "Asian Art Museum Removes Bust Of Racist Founder" (2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/asian-art-museum-removes-bust-of-racist-founder
- Reports activists' demands and Bhakta's charge that honouring a "Nazi sympathizer" sent a mixed message.
- Cites Brundage's 1936 move against a boycott of the Nazi Olympics and the 1968 expulsion of Tommie Smith and John Carlos.
2. Hyperallergic: Cassie Packard, "After Five Decades in San Francisco, Two Lintels Repatriated to Thailand" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/after-five-decades-in-san-francisco-two-lintels-repatriated-to-thailand
- Archaeologist Tanongsak Hanwong found the lintels in 2016 and campaigned for their return.
- Research found no export licences, and photos show the lintels in place as late as the 1950s; the museum does not answer that point.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on City money and private philanthropy, and it discloses its 990 and partners. The weak points are a gas utility with a manslaughter plea and a Pentagon-listed Chinese shipping company among corporate partners, and a founding collection whose provenance the museum pledged to review in 2020. Worth watching: the City budget cuts and any further restitution claims.
