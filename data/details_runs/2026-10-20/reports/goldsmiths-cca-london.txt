GOLDSMITHS CCA: London, UK
ID: goldsmiths-cca-london
Website: https://goldsmithscca.art/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery on the campus of Goldsmiths, University of London, at St James', New Cross, SE14 6AD, a 3-minute walk from New Cross Gate station (stated by the space).
- Opened on 8 September 2018 with an exhibition by Mika Rottenberg (Wikipedia); Sarah McCrory is director (stated by the space).
- Occupies the water tanks and plant-works of the Grade II-listed Laurie Grove Baths, a former Victorian bathhouse, converted by the architecture collective Assemble; the refurbishment cost £4.6 million (stated by the space, Wikipedia).
- Just under 1,000 m² with just under 700 m² of exhibition space across eight galleries, including a converted iron-lined Victorian water tank and the double-height Oak Foundation Gallery; a café sits at the rear (stated by the space).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 6pm; no admission charge is listed; step-free entry by a 1:10 ramp, a hearing loop, large-print texts, an accessible toilet and monthly relaxed viewings (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Goldsmiths CCA: university-run, built with private gifts: lead benefactors Damien Hirst and the Oak Foundation
- Goldsmiths said in January 2015 that the gallery needed £2.8 million, all from external donations (Goldsmiths).
- Alumni Antony Gormley, Damien Hirst, Sarah Lucas, Julian Opie, Sam Taylor-Johnson and Steve McQueen gave works for a Christie's sale on 12 February 2015 to raise money for it (Goldsmiths). No reporting found, among pages that could be opened, of the sum raised.
- Opening donors: lead benefactors Damien Hirst and the Oak Foundation; major benefactors Christie's, Dimitris Daskalopoulos, the Garcia Family Foundation, the Garfield Weston Foundation, The Goldsmiths' Company, Antony Gormley, Sarah Lucas, the Bridget Riley Art Foundation and the Roden Family Foundation; benefactors include the Fidelity UK Foundation (stated by the space, undated).
- Programme partners listed include Arts Council England, the Terra Foundation for American Art, the Danish Art Foundation, the Austrian Federal Chancellery, White Cube and David Zwirner (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of a separate CCA budget or accounts.

Red flags: 2024 occupation and boycott over donors Candida and Zak Gertler
- Goldsmiths for Palestine occupied the CCA from 30 May 2024 for 27 days and launched a boycott; the CCA closed until October, saying it could not meet its insurers' terms for keeping artworks safe (ArtReview, The Art Newspaper).
- The group demanded that Goldsmiths revoke Zak Gertler's honorary fellowship, remove the Gertler name from one of the CCA's galleries and cut ties with the couple (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: the Gertlers are personal friends of Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, donors to his campaigns and supporters of illegal settlements, from Goldsmiths for Palestine, a pro-Palestinian student campaign group.
- Artists Matt Connors and Mark Corfield-Moore backed the occupiers; Connors's show closed early, and Galli's exhibition, due on 20 June 2024, was postponed (The Art Newspaper, ArtReview).
- The CCA removed the Gertlers' names from the gallery and the donor board and said it had received no Gertler funds since 2017; the boycott then ended (The Art Newspaper, 25 November 2024).
- On 29 November 2024 Candida Gertler resigned from Outset Contemporary Art Fund and all voluntary UK arts roles, calling it "an act of principled protest against the alarming rise of antisemitism" (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The coverage is news reporting on the 2024 occupation and its outcome. No critical essay or review of the CCA's programme or funding was found.

2. The Art Newspaper: Joe Ware, "Goldsmiths CCA closes until autumn amid pro-Palestine protests" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/06/14/goldsmiths-cca-closes-until-autumn-amid-pro-palestine-protests

URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/11/25/pro-palestine-boycott-goldsmiths-cca-ends-after-gallery-cuts-ties-donors
- Reports the removal of the Gertler names and the CCA's statement that no Gertler money arrived after 2017.
- The Gertlers did not answer a request for comment sent through Outset.

BOTTOM LINE
The CCA was built with artists' gifts, a Christie's sale and foundations, and now runs inside the university. The weak point was the Gertler naming, which a 2024 occupation forced the CCA to drop. Worth watching: the CCA publishes no budget or dated donor list of its own.
