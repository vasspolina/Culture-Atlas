JIM THOMPSON ART CENTER: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: jim-thompson-art-center-bangkok
Website: https://www.jimthompsonartcenter.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at No. 6 Soi Kasemsan 2, Rama 1 Road, Bangkok, in the same compound as the Jim Thompson House Museum (Jim Thompson Art Center; ASEF Culture360).
- Run by the James H.W. Thompson Foundation, legally established in 1975, which also operates the house museum; the house and collection are registered as a national museum (James H.W. Thompson Foundation). The foundation is under the royal patronage of Princess Maha Chakri Sirindhorn (Wikipedia).
- Named for Jim Thompson, the American who founded the Thai Silk Company in 1951 and disappeared in Malaysia's Cameron Highlands on 27 March 1967; his nephew and heir Henry B. Thompson III gave the house and antiques to the foundation (James H.W. Thompson Foundation).
- The centre reopened on 27 November 2021 after three years of building work on a new four-storey building of 3,000 square metres by the Bangkok studio Design Qua, founded by Malina Palasthira and John Erskine (ArtAsiaPacific, Pamela Wong, 2021). ArchDaily gives 3,800 square metres and says the site was the house museum's outdoor car park.
- On site: two galleries, a rooftop space, the William Warren Library (free to use), a cafe and a shop (ArtAsiaPacific; James H.W. Thompson Foundation).
- Artistic director: the curator Gridthiya Gaweewong (Jim Thompson Art Center; Artforum). Since 2021 its Future Project has presented art on the Cold War in Thailand and Southeast Asia (Jim Thompson Art Center).
- Current show: "And Still, Another After", six emerging Thai artists, 10 October 2026 to 29 March 2027 (Jim Thompson Art Center).
- Visiting: open every day 10am to 6pm; 50 baht for the art centre, 250 baht with the house museum; students under 10 free; the centre sells a J.H.W. membership (Jim Thompson Art Center).

FUNDING
Jim Thompson Art Center: self-funded by its foundation (stated by the foundation), with grants and partners on single shows
- The foundation says it is a non-profit that "relies entirely on its own funding to carry out its activities". It runs the Jim Thompson House Museum, which charges admission (James H.W. Thompson Foundation; Jim Thompson Art Center).
- The foundation's assets came from Thompson's estate: the court-appointed administrator vested the house and collection in it in 1975 (James H.W. Thompson Foundation).
- Governance: a seven-member Board of Trustees; the site lists six: Vudthi Nathamethikul, Pinaree Sanpitak, Pawapun Bunnag Booth, Punthumadee Katawandee, Rachaporn Choochuey and Devid Teh (James H.W. Thompson Foundation).
- "On-Air, Off-Air" (2025), with Ho Rui An and Sung Tieu, was supported by the foundation, the Goethe-Institut, A+ Works of Art and GroundControl (Jim Thompson Art Center).
- "Living in an Elastic Time" (2026), first shown as the Thailand Pavilion at the 2025 Cheongju Craft Biennale, was partly supported by the Contemporary Art Promotion Fund of the Office of Contemporary Art, Thai Ministry of Culture, the Cheongju Craft Biennale and the foundation (Jim Thompson Art Center).
- "MO NUM EN TS" (2025 to 2026), Som Supaparinya's show, came from the Han Nefkens Foundation's Southeast Asian Video Art Production Grant 2024, which commissioned the work (Jim Thompson Art Center; e-flux).
- "The Shattered Worlds" (2025) was co-presented with the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre for the foundation's 50th anniversary (Jim Thompson Art Center).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is reviews and listings; one review raised a curatorial gap.
1. Artforum: Li Qi, review of "The Shattered Worlds: Micro Narratives from the Ho Chi Minh Trail to the Great Steppe" (2025)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/events/shattered-worlds-jim-thompson-bangkok-art-culture-centre-review-1234735728/
- The show, co-organised with the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre, arrived during the Thailand and Cambodia border dispute with no works by Cambodian artists, which the critic calls a shortcoming.
- Otherwise favourable; criticises the selection, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: an estate-founded foundation with no flagged funder. Worth watching: accounts showing how far house-museum admission pays for the art centre.
