M+: Hong Kong, China
ID: m-hong-kong
Website: https://www.mplus.org.hk/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of visual culture in the West Kowloon Cultural District on Victoria Harbour, covering twentieth and twenty-first century art, design, architecture and moving image (Wikipedia, Hong Kong Free Press).
- Administered by the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority (WKCDA), a statutory authority that began operating on 11 July 2008. Lars Nittve was inaugural director; Suhanya Raffel has led it since January 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 12 November 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Building by Herzog & de Meuron with Farrells, chosen in 2013; construction began in 2014 and finished in December 2020. The building covers 65,000 square metres, or 700,000 square feet (Wikipedia).
- It recorded about 2.6 million visitors in 2024 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of opening days or prices; the visit page did not load.

FUNDING
M+: public: the Hong Kong government through the WKCDA, with bank and corporate sponsors
- The museum cost HK$5.9 billion (Wikipedia).
- HSBC became M+'s first Lead Partner from 1 November 2022, covering select special exhibitions, NGO tours and family days; no amount or end date is stated, and M+ Board chair Bernard Chan called it "long-term" (M+ press release, 5 August 2022).
- Today's corporate page lists Programme Sponsors Julius Baer, Ritz Carlton, Rosewood and UBS; Corporate Patrons Bloomberg Philanthropies, Citadel and Morgan Stanley; Travel Partner Cathay. The Lead and Major Partner names are not in the page text. No amounts or years are posted (stated by the museum).

Red flags: State censorship or punishment
- In March 2021 Henry Tang, described as the government official in charge of the cultural district, said no work deemed to breach the law would be shown, welcomed vetting by the police's national security unit, and confirmed Ai Weiwei's Tiananmen photograph from the "Study of Perspectives" series would not be in the opening display; he denied censorship (AFP via Hong Kong Free Press).
- Ai Weiwei told Reuters "The museum is clearly under censorship". Uli Sigg told ART Magazine: "Can the museum work according to its scholarly ethos, or do politicians curate what it can show?" (Outlook India, 11 November 2021).
- On 21 April 2022 nine works were off view, including Wang Xingwei's "New Beijing" (2001) and a Wang Guangyi Mao painting. The WKCDA called it routine "rotation" for conservation reasons (CNN).

Red flags: banks with documented settlements
- HSBC, the Lead Partner from November 2022, entered a five-year deferred prosecution agreement with the US Justice Department on 11 December 2012 over money laundering and sanctions violations, forfeiting $1.256 billion and paying $665 million in civil penalties to the Comptroller of the Currency and the Federal Reserve, under the Bank Secrecy Act, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the Trading with the Enemy Act (US Attorney's Office, Eastern District of New York).
- UBS, a Programme Sponsor: on 5 May 2025 Credit Suisse Services AG pleaded guilty in a US Justice Department prosecution to conspiring to hide more than $4 billion from the US tax authority in at least 475 offshore accounts, and will pay a fine of more than $510 million. UBS said it was not involved in the conduct, which began before it bought Credit Suisse in 2023 (Reuters via Yahoo Finance).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns political pressure on what M+ shows. None found of its sponsors in art media.
1. ArtReview: news desk, "Future of museums in Hong Kong threatened with censorship" (2021)
URL: https://artreview.com/future-of-museums-in-hong-kong-threatened-with-censorship/
- Reports Chief Executive Carrie Lam saying authorities were on "full alert" for exhibitions that could undermine national security, answering lawmaker Eunice Yung, who argued upcoming M+ shows could breach the National Security Law.
- Notes Raffel had said days earlier there would be "no problem" showing Ai Weiwei. An M+ spokesperson said the museum would follow Hong Kong law.
- Names M+, Lam, Yung and Raffel.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Reuters, via Finance News Network (20 February 2026), reported US Justice Department documents showing Morgan Stanley handled accounts for Epstein-linked trusts from 2015 to 2019; Reuters found no evidence of wrongdoing by the bank.
- SRF (10 February 2026), citing Reuters, reported UBS held several accounts for Ghislaine Maxwell from 2014, up to $19 million; UBS declined to comment.
- Both are M+ sponsors; the reports describe bank accounts, not gifts to M+.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money runs the museum, and a statutory authority chaired by a government figure controls what it shows. The weak points are the 2021 and 2022 removals, HSBC's 2012 settlement (it became Lead Partner in 2022), and UBS and Morgan Stanley in the sponsor list. No reporting found of a campaign aimed at M+'s sponsors. Watch the unnamed Lead and Major Partner slots.
