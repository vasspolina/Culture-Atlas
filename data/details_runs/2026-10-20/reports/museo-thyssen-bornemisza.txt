MUSEO NACIONAL THYSSEN-BORNEMISZA: Madrid, Spain
ID: museo-thyssen-bornemisza
Website: https://www.museothyssen.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in the Villahermosa Palace at Paseo del Prado 8, Madrid (museum website; museum accounts, 2024).
- Opened to the public on 10 October 1992 to show the collection of Baron Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza, installed in Spain under a loan agreement; the Kingdom of Spain bought the collection by a contract of 21 June 1993 (museum accounts, 2024). Wikipedia dates the baron's collection to the 1920s and says Spain paid €300 million for 775 works; The Art Newspaper (Gareth Harris, 31 December 2020) gave the price as $350 million.
- Named for the Thyssen-Bornemisza family, whose collection forms its core. Baroness Carmen Thyssen-Bornemisza (Carmen Cervera), who married the baron in 1985, has lent a second collection since 1999 (accounts; Wikipedia).
- Building: the Villahermosa Palace, which the state handed over free as the museum's home; two neighbouring buildings on Calle Marqués de Cubas were added, opened on 8 June 2004 (accounts).
- The museum is run by the Fundación Colección Thyssen-Bornemisza, registered in 1989 and part of the Spanish state public sector since Law 50/2002 (accounts).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 19:00; combined ticket €14; Friends enter free, membership from €90 a year; free access on Saturdays 21:00 to 23:00 under the "Noches Thyssen" programme (museum website).

FUNDING
Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza: mainly public: the Spanish Ministry of Culture and Sport, with ticket sales and corporate and foundation sponsors
- 2024 accounts: income from the museum's activity was €26,535,764: grants €19,672,346, ticket sales €5,461,007, membership fees €714,301 and sponsorship and partnerships €688,110, down from €1,430,004 in 2023. Shop and services brought in a further €4,932,146.
- The Ministry's Directorate General of Fine Arts covered the operating deficit with €18,566,850 in 2024. Under the Real Decreto-Ley 11/1993 of 18 June, the state must cover any gap between the foundation's income and its costs (accounts).
- The Patronato is chaired by the Minister of Culture, Ernest Urtasun, with Baroness Carmen Thyssen-Bornemisza as vice president; other seats go to state officials, Thyssen family members and government-appointed trustees including Juan Antonio Pérez Simón (accounts, 31 December 2024).
- The Baroness's collection is leased: a 15-year contract signed on 9 February 2023 pays €6,500,000 a year, indexed to inflation; the 2024 cost was €7,786,363 with VAT, funded from the ministry's annual contribution (accounts).
- Named private and regional funders in 2024 (accounts): Fundación Mutua Madrileña €300,000 for conservation; Comunidad de Madrid €300,000 across four exhibitions and programmes; Lucerne Foundation €159,320 for the Gabriele Münter show; Ayuntamiento de Madrid €65,000; Fundación Mª Cristina Masaveu Peterson €50,000; Fundación Iberdrola €35,000 for education; Fundació Privada Banc Sabadell €30,000; Terra Foundation €22,353; Fundación ACS €20,000. Multi-year grants also came from Fundación La Caixa (2018 to 2028) and the BBVA foundation (2020 to 2023).
- Mastercard sponsors "Los lunes de Mastercard", free permanent-collection visits on Mondays (museum website, apoyo page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece names the museum: a report on the court case over a Pissarro painting in its collection. It is a news report of a ruling, not an essay on the museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Martha Lufkin, "US court rules Nazi-looted Pissarro painting belongs to Spain" (12 January 2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/01/12/nazi-looted-pissarro-painting-spain
- The parties are the heirs of Claude Cassirer, sole heir of Lilly Cassirer, and the Thyssen-Bornemisza Collection Foundation, which keeps the painting at the Villahermosa Palace. The heirs claim Pissarro's "Rue Saint Honoré, après-midi, effet de pluie" (1897), which Lilly Cassirer was forced to sell in 1939 and for which she was denied the proceeds. The baron bought it in 1976 and Spain acquired it with the collection in 1993.
- On 9 January 2024 the US Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals, in an opinion by Judge Carlos T. Bea, applied Spanish law and ruled the foundation owns the painting. The Cassirer legal team said it would seek en banc review and argued the ruling "fails to explain how Spain has any interest in applying its laws to launder ownership of the spoils of war"; the foundation's lawyers called it "a welcome conclusion".
- Wikipedia adds that the US Supreme Court vacated the 2024 judgment on 10 March 2025 after California passed a new statute in September 2024, and the case returned to the lower court.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state foundation: the Ministry of Culture and Sport paid €18.6 million of its 2024 deficit, the foundation pays €6.5 million a year to lease the Baroness's collection, funded by the ministry, and ticket sales and named foundations add the rest. The documented dispute is the Cassirer family's claim to the Pissarro in the museum's collection.
