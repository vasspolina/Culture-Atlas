MUSÉE D'ORSAY: Paris, France
ID: musée-dorsay-paris
Website: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/ (checked 2026-10-24; returned HTTP 403 to automated requests, so its pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of art in the former Gare d'Orsay railway station on the Left Bank of the Seine in Paris (Wikipedia).
- The station was built from 1898 to 1900 for the Paris-Orleans railway and listed as a historic monument in 1978; the museum opened in December 1986, inaugurated by President Francois Mitterrand (Wikipedia).
- Architects of the station: Lucien Magne, Emile Benard and Victor Laloux; the museum conversion was won by the ACT Architecture team in a 1978 competition, with interiors by Gae Aulenti (Wikipedia).
- Since 2010 it shares a public body with the Musee de l'Orangerie, the Etablissement public du musee d'Orsay et du musee de l'Orangerie (French Wikipedia).
- Visitors: 3,785,134 in 2025 (French Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 9:30 to 18:00, Thursday until 21:45, closed Monday, 1 May and 25 December. Entry ticket EUR 16 and same-day desk ticket EUR 14. Free for under-18s, EU and EEA visitors under 26, job seekers and disabled visitors with one companion, and for everyone on the first Sunday of each month. From 10 March 2026 until summer 2028 the guide says booking a time slot online is strongly recommended, not required. All galleries stay open during building work, but entrances shift (artvisitguide.com, a third-party guide).

FUNDING
Musee d'Orsay: mainly public: a French state museum, with private patronage for acquisitions and building projects
- The museum is a national museum run by a state public body (French Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the museum's annual budget, state grant or own-income figures in sources that could be opened.
- LVMH: funded the January 2023 acquisition of Gustave Caillebotte's "La Partie de bateau" (1877), priced at EUR 43 million against an acquisition budget of around EUR 3 million a year (The Conversation; LVMH announcement, 2 January 2023).
- The Conversation's author states that the sponsorship carries a tax deduction of 90% of the cost of the painting, so "precisely 90%" of LVMH's communication campaign is paid by the taxpayer.
- An anonymous US patron gave EUR 20 million toward the "Orsay Grand Ouvert" renovation, through the American Friends of the Musees d'Orsay et de l'Orangerie (Wikipedia).
- Daniel Marchesseau gave EUR 5 million from the sale of 18 sculptures by Francois-Xavier and Claude Lalanne (French Wikipedia).
- Spencer and Marlene Hays donated 293 works in 2016 and 2019 (French Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself found in art media. The strongest critical pieces concern LVMH's patronage and its tax treatment, written by an academic and a general magazine rather than art press.
1. The Conversation: Tanguy Gatay, "The group Louis Vuitton (LVMH), a gift to the Musee d'Orsay and a huge tax incentive: business as usual?" (2025)
URL: https://theconversation.com/the-group-louis-vuitton-lvmh-a-gift-to-the-musee-dorsay-and-a-huge-tax-incentive-business-as-usual-265403
- Argues the purchase is mainly public relations for LVMH at small cost to the company, while "it is the taxpayer who is bankrolling LVMH's communication campaign".
- Argues donations can create a sense of indebtedness that gives the group diffuse influence over the museum.
- Names Bernard Arnault and LVMH; reports no response from the museum. The author is a PhD student at Universite Paris Nanterre.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Epstein visit: ARTnews carries the headline "Jeffrey Epstein Apparently Got Private Access to the Musee d'Orsay with Woody Allen" (listed as 4 February 2026 in a search result). The article is paywalled and was not opened, so the date of the visit and its circumstances are not confirmed here.
- Leon Black loan: in 2014 the financier Leon Black lent Van Gogh's "Quarry near Saint-Remy" to the museum for "Van Gogh/Artaud: Le Suicide de la Societe" (The Art Newspaper, Martin Bailey, first published 13 February 2026). Epstein files released by the US Department of Justice in January show Epstein gave Black financial advice; Black has denied wrongdoing in his dealings with Epstein and says he was unaware of Epstein's crimes before 2019.
- Neither item is a gift or funding link between Epstein and the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is French state money plus patronage, and no source found shows a funder of the museum with a documented controversy. The weak points are the tax cost of the LVMH gift, which a critic says falls about 90% on the public, and the absence of any budget figures that could be checked. Worth watching: how future sponsored purchases are priced against public subsidy.
