IZOLYATSIA: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: izolyatsia
Website: https://izolyatsia.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit cultural platform, now based in Kyiv on the site of the Kyiv Shipbuilding and Ship Repair Plant on Naberezhno-Luhova Street (Wikipedia). It describes itself as a "non-profit, non-governmental platform for cultural initiatives" (IZOLYATSIA).
- Founded in 2010 in a former insulation works in Donetsk (IZOLYATSIA). Wikipedia names Lyubov Mikhailova, daughter of the last Soviet director of an insulation materials plant on that territory, as founder.
- On 9 June 2014 armed members of the Donetsk People's Republic took over the foundation's premises, and it relocated to Kyiv. Only part of the exhibits left Donetsk; the library, equipment, shop and works that could not be moved were destroyed or looted (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia's "Izolyatsia prison" article says representatives of the Donetsk People's Republic (DPR) seized the site and converted it into a closed site of the DPR's State Security Ministry, where prisoners were held. IZOLYATSIA's website states: "Russian illegal prison on IZOLYATSIA premises has been operational for 12 years."
- Programmes: research, site-specific projects, exhibitions and residencies (IZOLYATSIA). IZONE, a creative space in Kyiv with workshops in photography, silkscreen decoration and lithography, launched in 2015 (Kyiv Post, 1 September 2016).
- In 2021 the foundation marked its anniversary in Soledar, Donetsk region, rather than in Kyiv (Promote Ukraine, 8 November 2021).
- Far-right groups targeted the platform for attacks between 2014 and 2019 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
IZOLYATSIA: grant-funded with private backing: EU programmes, international grants and its owner Liubov Mykhaylova (Kyiv Post, 2016)
- Before 2014, donors included Donetsk enterprises, which the foundation chose itself. After 2014 those enterprises stopped working and money came mostly from international grants, including EU and US programmes. IZONE's profits went to IZOLYATSIA projects; its main income came from leasing premises, which has ended (Promote Ukraine, Marta Barandiy, interview with the development director, 8 November 2021).
- Kyiv Post (Nataliya Trach, "Donetsk art center finds ways to flourish in exile", 1 September 2016): IZOLYATSIA is owned and sponsored by Liubov Mykhaylova, a businesswoman; in Donetsk it accepted donations from local businesses but never from Rinat Akhmetov; since moving to Kyiv it has sought grants from the European Union and private donations.
- EU Creative Europe: co-funds ZMINA: Rebuilding, a project of IZOLYATSIA, Malý Berlín (Trnava, Slovakia) and Trans Europe Halles (Sweden) for displaced Ukrainians and the Ukrainian cultural and creative sectors; the project launched on 7 June 2023 (IZOLYATSIA, ZMINA page).
- Kyiv city administration cooperated with the foundation on the "Social Agreement" project on the site of the Lenin monument demolished in 2014 (Promote Ukraine).

BOTTOM LINE
IZOLYATSIA describes itself as a non-profit, non-governmental platform; it lost its Donetsk home to armed members of the Donetsk People's Republic in 2014. After 2014 it was financed mostly by grant programmes including EU and US initiatives (Promote Ukraine, 2021), with the EU Creative Europe programme co-funding ZMINA and, according to the Kyiv Post in 2016, private backing from its owner Liubov Mykhaylova.
