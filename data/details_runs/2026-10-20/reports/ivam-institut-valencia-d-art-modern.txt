IVAM INSTITUT VALENCIÀ D'ART MODERN: Valencia, Spain
ID: ivam-institut-valencia-d-art-modern
Website: https://ivam.es/en/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public museum of modern art at Guillem de Castro 118 in Valencia; the main building, the Centre Julio González, sits on remains of the city's medieval walls (Wikipedia, IVAM).
- The Valencian government created the institute in 1986 and it opened in February 1989, the first modern art centre in Spain; Tomàs Llorens was its first director. A second site in the Centre del Carme ran until 2002 (Wikipedia).
- The collection began with the Julio González holdings bought before opening and now has about 10,000 works, mainly 20th-century art and photography (Wikipedia).
- Blanca de la Torre has directed it since 1 April 2025, after an international public competition (Valencia News).
- In 2025 it had 202,258 visitors, 48 percent from abroad, and bought 16 works by 14 women artists for about €1 million (Valencia News).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 19:00, closed Mondays; the home page gives no prices.

FUNDING
IVAM: mainly public: the Generalitat Valenciana's culture department
- IVAM is run by the Valencian Community's culture department (Wikipedia). Its budget was €9.7 million in 2020 and €11.1 million in the 2021 plan, the rise mostly a €3 million investment line for a new headquarters (Valencia Plaza, 2020).
- No reporting found of the current operating budget.
- The website names Spain's Ministry of Culture as sponsor and the Fundación Banco Sabadell as partner; no amounts given.
- In 2025 it received three works by Soledad Sevilla valued at over €250,000 as a donation (Valencia News).

Red flags: funding government reported the director to prosecutors, 2024
- In February 2024 the Generalitat's first vice-presidency and culture department, led by Vicente Barrera of Vox, filed a complaint with prosecutors over director Nuria Enguita's donation of land to the Todolí Citrus Fundació of Vicente Todolí, who had sat on the jury that chose her (Ara, Valencia Plaza).
- Enguita resigned on 21 February 2024, calling the attacks a "campaña difamatoria" and saying she resigned to avoid further harm to the museum (Ara).
- Prosecutors closed the case in March 2024 (Valencia Plaza). Barrera denied any persecution and said he had asked her not to resign; Compromís deputy Verònica Ruiz called it "una caza de brujas" (Valencia Plaza).

Red flags: former director convicted of embezzlement, 2021
- In August 2021 former director Consuelo Císcar admitted misusing museum funds from 2008 to 2014 to promote the career of her son, the artist Rafael Blasco Císcar, and accepted a suspended 18-month prison term and two and a half years' disqualification (Valencia Plaza).
- She and two co-defendants were ordered to pay IVAM €75,000 in total (Valencia Plaza).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The 2024 resignation and the Císcar case were covered by Spanish and Valencian news outlets, not the art press.

BOTTOM LINE
IVAM runs on Valencian regional public money, with a small Ministry of Culture and Banco Sabadell foundation presence and no flagged corporate funder. The weak points are political: the regional government that funds it reported its director to prosecutors in 2024, and prosecutors closed the case in March 2024; a former director was convicted in 2021 of misusing its funds. No current budget is published in the sources found.
