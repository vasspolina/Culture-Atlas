KAESONG KORYO MUSEUM: Kaesong, North Korea
ID: kaesong-koryo-museum-kaesong
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the Koryo dynasty (918 to 1392) in eastern Kaesong, open every day and a common stop on the Kaesong tourist route (Koryo Tours, December 2018). Wikipedia puts the site 2 km northeast of the city centre.
- It has occupied the former Songgyungwan (Sungyun Academy), the highest Confucian academy of the Koryo dynasty, since 1987 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia dates the academy's opening to 992 and its designation as principal teaching institute to 1089; Koryo Tours says it moved to Seoul in 1394.
- The buildings burned in the Imjin War of 1592 to 1598 and were rebuilt from 1602; Koryo Tours dates the reconstruction 1602 to 1610. Koryo Tours counts 12 main and six auxiliary buildings on 20,000 square metres; Wikipedia counts Taesong Temple, Myongnyun Hall and 20 other buildings.
- The Koryo Songgyungwan is one of the 12 components of the UNESCO World Heritage property Historic Monuments and Sites in Kaesong, inscribed in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Koryo Tours counts over 1,000 artefacts in four halls: politics, economy and society; science and culture, including movable metal type from the Manwoldae palace site and celadon; a 1.6 m iron Buddha from Chokjo Temple; and metalwork, sculpture and painting. An open-air exhibition by a stream holds stone pagodas and stupas from former temple sites around Kaesong. Seoul Shinmun put the display at about 600 relics in 1991.
- Wikipedia, citing a 2005 study by Elisabeth Chabanol, says the old buildings lack proper conditions for conserving and presenting works of art and are ill-suited to receiving visitors.
- Visiting: Koryo Tours says Hall 3 with the iron Buddha is usually left out of tours for non-Korean visitors unless the guide is asked to open it, and that local weddings are sometimes held on the site.

FUNDING
Kaesong Koryo Museum: state-run: a North Korean state museum
- Staff: South Korea's Ministry of Unification, in its 2022 directory of North Korean institutions, lists Paek Sol (백솔) as director of the Koryo Museum (고려박물관) (my romanisation).
- Seoul Shinmun reported in October 1991 that the North Korean authorities pay attention in their own way to preserving and managing Kaesong's historic sites, though visitors called many of them sites "without fragrance" (my translation).
- Joint exhibition, 2015: the museum hosted a special exhibition of about 100 finds from the joint North and South Korean excavation of the Manwoldae palace site, organised by South Korea's Cultural Heritage Administration, the Ministry of Unification and the South-North Historians Council, from 15 October to 15 November 2015 (Ajunews). That joint dig began in 2007.
- Cost of the joint dig: National Assembly foreign affairs and unification committee chair Na Kyung-won wrote in November 2015 that about 3.7 billion won had gone into the excavation, including 2.2 billion won from South Korea's Inter-Korean Cooperation Fund that year (Seoul Shinmun). The money paid for the dig, not the museum. She wrote that the Kaesong show was laid out in a makeshift tent hall on the museum grounds, with no control of humidity or sunlight.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).

Not related: Sungkyunkwan University in Seoul, which Koryo Tours names as the southern successor of the same Koryo academy; it is a separate South Korean university.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a North Korean state museum, and the US blocking order on that state is its one Red flag.
