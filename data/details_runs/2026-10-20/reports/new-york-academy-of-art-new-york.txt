NEW YORK ACADEMY OF ART: New York, USA
ID: new-york-academy-of-art-new-york
Website: https://nyaa.edu/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Graduate school of figurative and representational art at 111 Franklin Street in Tribeca, New York (school website).
- Instruction began in 1980 as the New York Drawing Association; Stuart Pivar is credited as primary founder and Andy Warhol as a key founder and funder (Wikipedia).
- Building: a five-storey, 42,000 square foot landmark building designed by Benjamin Warner in 1861. The school began operating there in 1990, and a 2001 fire damaged half the interior (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: a two-year MFA with about 100 students, a one-year Certificate of Fine Art, a four-week summer programme and continuing education classes (school website; Wikipedia).
- Public events: the Tribeca Ball, first held in 1994, and the Take Home a Nude auction at Sotheby's (Wikipedia). Chubb Fellows exhibitions are listed on the school site.
- No reporting found of gallery visiting hours or admission: the school's site lists studio tours booked online.

FUNDING
New York Academy of Art: privately funded: tuition, fundraising events, foundations and individual donors
- Warhol's bequest after his death in 1987 let the school buy its Franklin Street headquarters, and the Warhol Foundation had given more than $1 million by 2004. Pivar gave key early support (Wikipedia).
- The Tribeca Ball is an important fundraiser; Van Cleef & Arpels was title sponsor of the April ball in 2025 (Tribeca Citizen, a post labelled as sponsored by the Academy).
- No reporting found of a published donor list or annual report that could be opened.

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein
- Epstein sat on the Academy's board from 1987 to 1994 (Hyperallergic, Rhea Nayyar, 16 March 2026).
- After his 2008 Florida guilty plea, Epstein paid for events and scholarships: a $25,000 Tribeca Ball table in each of 2012 and 2014 and a $30,000 Portrait Scholarship gift in 2014. The Academy total for Epstein-linked funds is $65,900 (Hyperallergic). The article does not reconcile these examples with the $65,900 total.
- In March 2026 the Academy sent $65,900 to Girls Educational and Mentoring Services, a charity for sexually exploited and trafficked young people. Board members agreed to match the sum so student resources were not cut (USA Art News, 16 March 2026).
- The Academy's board cited "serious failures in judgment and governance" in its relationship with Epstein, apologised, and said it would create an ethics committee to review donor-support policies (Hyperallergic).
- Board chair Eileen Guggenheim stepped down on 1 April 2026, a month earlier than planned, and remains on the board (USA Art News).
- Unverified: alumna Maria Farmer alleges Guggenheim introduced her to Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell in 1995 and told her to sell Epstein a painting at a discount; Guggenheim denies introducing them (Hyperallergic; USA Art News). Farmer filed an FBI report in 1996 (Hyperallergic).
- A 2020 petition to remove Guggenheim as chair collected more than 2,100 signatures; the Academy called it "filled with false information, innuendo, and lies". Its organiser later retracted it (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 2020; editor's note 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
The school's regular money is private and little of it is published. The documented problem is Epstein: he sat on the board from 1987 to 1994 and paid for Academy events after his 2008 plea, which the Academy now calls a failure of judgment and has redirected to a charity. Watch the new ethics committee and whether the Academy publishes a donor list.
