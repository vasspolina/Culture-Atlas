COPENHAGEN CONTEMPORARY: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: copenhagen-contemporary-copenhagen
Website: https://copenhagencontemporary.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre on the Refshaleøen peninsula, close to the city's opera house (The Art Newspaper), in the former B&W welding halls (Copenhagen Contemporary).
- The Danish collector Jens Faurschou initiated the project (The Art Newspaper) and founded the Copenhagen Contemporary Foundation in 2014; he was vice-chairman of the board until 2024, and the board was established on 2 December 2014 (Copenhagen Contemporary). It began with an 18-month pilot programme at a temporary venue in June 2016, and the Refshaleøen site opened on 28 June 2018 (The Art Newspaper, Clemens Bomsdorf, 27 June 2018).
- Building: a refurbished shipyard with 7,000 square metres in total and 4,500 square metres of exhibition space (The Art Newspaper).
- On site: large-scale installations, the permanent James Turrell work Aftershock and the cafe Darcy's. In 2024 Aftershock became the first permanent acquisition and founded a new collection of 21st-century art (annual report 2024, translated from Danish).
- Visiting: closed Monday; open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11 am to 6 pm and Thursday 11 am to 9 pm. Adults 140 DKK, students 95 DKK, ages 3 to 17 25 DKK, under 3 and Copenhagen Card holders free.
- Accessibility: the building is accessible by wheelchair with ramps, automatic doors, lifts on all floors and accessible toilets; wheelchair users bring a companion free; Aftershock cannot be experienced in a wheelchair.

FUNDING
Copenhagen Contemporary: private foundations, with state and city operating grants
- Net revenue for 2024 was DKK 41,056,544. The annual report states foundation support of DKK 25,799 thousand for operations and exhibitions, plus in-kind sponsorships, and public grants of DKK 10,852 thousand (annual report 2024, translated).
- Public grants in 2024: the Ministry of Culture DKK 5,308,334 for operations, the City of Copenhagen DKK 5,000,000 for operations, and the Danish Arts Foundation (Statens Kunstfond) DKK 543,672 for the exhibition programme (annual report 2024).
- Its About page lists 22 supporters for 2026: 15. Juni Fonden, Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond, A.P. Møller og Hustru Chastine Mc-Kinney Møllers Fond til almene Formaal, Augustinusfonden, Beckett-Fonden, C.E. Jensens Fond, Det Obelske Familiefond, Dinesen, Foreningen Roskilde Festival, Fredericia Furniture, Knud Højgaards Fond, Konsul George Jorck og Hustru Emma Jorck's Fond, Kulturministeriet, Københavns Kommune, Le Fix, Lemvigh-Müller Fonden, Nordea Fonden, Ny Carlsberg Fondet, Refshaleøens Ejendomsselskab, Spar Nord Fonden, Statens Kunstfond and William Demant Fonden.
- Augustinus Fonden: the annual report 2024 states that, thanks to a donation from the foundation, Aftershock became the museum's first permanent acquisition in 2024 (translated from Danish).
- Start-up: at the end of 2017 several Danish foundations gave about EUR 4.5 million for the first three years at the new site, and the Municipality of Copenhagen gave EUR 400,000 for 2018 (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: tobacco money through two funders
- Augustinus Fonden: a Danish cultural foundation established in 1942 whose investment company is Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (Augustinus Fonden). Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group as of 1 May 2025 (Scandinavian Tobacco Group shareholder page).
- Det Obelske Familiefond: a foundation established in 1956 by Asta Obel that is the sole owner of the investment company C.W. Obel A/S (lex.dk). C.W. Obel holds more than 10% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group (C.W. Obel).
- Scandinavian Tobacco Group makes cigars and fine cut tobacco, sells in more than 100 markets and has been listed in Copenhagen since February 2016 (C.W. Obel).
- Both foundations appear on Copenhagen Contemporary's 2026 supporter list, and Augustinus Fonden's 2024 donation is recorded in the annual report.

BOTTOM LINE
In 2024 Copenhagen Contemporary received DKK 25.8 million in foundation support and DKK 10.9 million in public grants, against net revenue of DKK 41.06 million. Two of its listed foundations, Augustinus Fonden and Det Obelske Familiefond, are tied to Scandinavian Tobacco Group through Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (more than 25 percent) and C.W. Obel (more than 10 percent), and a donation from Augustinus Fonden made the 2024 Aftershock acquisition possible.
