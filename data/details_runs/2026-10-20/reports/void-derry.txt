VOID: Derry, UK
ID: void-derry
Website: https://www.derryvoid.com/about-us/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at 10 Waterloo Place, Derry city centre, which it moved to in March 2021 (Void website, Open Charities).
- Artists formed DADA (Derry's Artists for Derry's Art) in January 2003 to create a contemporary art space; Void Art Centre opened in January 2005 as a non-profit limited company with charitable status, with the inaugural exhibition "Peripheral Vision" (Void website).
- Home was first the basement of the Old City Shirt Factory; it moved to a first-floor space there in 2015 (Void website, Photoireland wiki).
- Void is a registered charity in Northern Ireland, registered 6 March 2015 (Open Charities).
- Programme: exhibitions curated by the Director and invited curators, plus free educational and outreach programmes (Photoireland wiki, Open Charities).
- Artists shown include Amanda Coogan, John Gerrard, Niamh O'Malley, Andrei Molodkin, Helen Cammock and George Shaw (Wikipedia).
- Vivianna Chechia has been director since May 2023 (Wikipedia); the Void website lists Anne Kelly as Interim Director.
- Visiting: the Waterloo Place gallery is described as fully accessible (Void website).

FUNDING
Void: public and charitable funders: Arts Council of Northern Ireland, Derry City and Strabane District Council and foundations
- The Void website names as funders the Arts Council of Northern Ireland, Derry City and Strabane District Council, Future Screens NI, Garfield Weston Foundation, Halifax Foundation for Northern Ireland, the Henry Moore Foundation and Ulster University.
- The early project secured Peace II funding through the Local Strategy Partnership (Void website, Photoireland wiki).
- Income was £329,093 and expenditure £177,056 in the year ending 31 March 2025, with 6 employees (Open Charities).
- In 2021 it received a grant of over £123,000 from the Arts Council of Northern Ireland (Wikipedia).
- Exhibition "Nothing But Longing" (14 January to 11 March 2017): Circa Art Magazine names Artis International as its funder; Void's disclaimer said all the artists in the exhibition condemn Israel's occupation of Palestine (Circa Art Magazine).
- Trustees: Mary Scally (chair), Diane Greer, Brian Bridges, Paul Connolly, Selina Horshi, Adrian Kelly, Una McNally and Helen Quigley (Void website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One essay examines a Void exhibition and its funder; it comes from a curator at CCA Derry~Londonderry and is not a review.
1. Circa Art Magazine: Sara Greavu, "Pushback" (29 March 2017), headed "Nothing But Longing, Void Art Centre and the LD50 Gallery controversy"
URL: https://circaartmagazine.net/sara-greavu-pushback/
- Treats Void's show "Nothing But Longing", curated by Sagit Mezamer with emerging Israeli artists who had held residencies in the Curfew Tower in Cushendall, County Antrim, as an example of the "contact hypothesis" that bringing divided groups together can ease conflict; the essay also discusses the LD50 gallery controversy in London.
- Writes that the funder Artis International's agenda "appears to align closely with the artwashing of 'Brand Israel'".
- Records that Void consulted the Ireland Palestine Solidarity Campaign and held a panel on the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement, and that its disclaimer said the artists condemn Israel's occupation of Palestine.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Void is public and charitable: the Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Derry City and Strabane District Council, with foundations and Ulster University, run by a board of eight trustees. The one documented objection is Greavu's March 2017 criticism of Artis International as funder of the exhibition "Nothing But Longing".
