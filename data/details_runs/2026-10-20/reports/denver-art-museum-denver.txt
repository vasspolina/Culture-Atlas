DENVER ART MUSEUM: Denver, USA
ID: denver-art-museum-denver
Website: https://www.denverartmuseum.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Denver, Colorado, with more than 80,000 works of art in 13 collections (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1893 as the Denver Artists Club; it became the Denver Art Association in 1917 and the Denver Art Museum in 1923 (Wikipedia).
- The North Building, now the Lanny and Sharon Martin Building, opened in 1971; Gio Ponti designed it with the local firm James Sudler Associates (Wikipedia).
- The Frederic C. Hamilton Building, designed by Daniel Libeskind with Davis Partnership Architects, opened on 7 October 2006 (Wikipedia).
- A renovation of the Martin Building was announced in December 2016; the Sie Welcome Center, named for Anna and John J. Sie, who pledged 12 million dollars, opened in October 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Christoph Heinrich is the museum's director; Sarah S. Anschutz is listed as Chairwoman, Board of Trustees (museum website).

FUNDING
Denver Art Museum: private nonprofit funded by contributions, the Scientific and Cultural Facilities District sales tax and City of Denver bond money
- The museum website lists the Scientific and Cultural Facilities District (SCFD) as a supporter; Wikipedia describes the SCFD as a 0.1% sales tax.
- The Elevate Denver Bond Program contributed 35.5 million dollars to the North Building project (Wikipedia).
- Lanny and Sharon Martin gave a 25 million dollar lead gift in 2016 for the building's renovation; the building was renamed for them (museum blog, 4 September 2023).
- Denver Art Museum Inc reported revenue of 87,033,570 dollars for the fiscal year ending September 2025, of which contributions were 75,528,401 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 filed 1 May 2026).
- Sarah Shaw Anschutz is a co-presenting sponsor of the museum's Delight gala on 5 June 2026, which supports Learning and Engagement programmes. UMB Bank is also a co-presenting sponsor (museum website).
- Emma Bunker and her family gave 185,000 dollars under a 2018 naming agreement; the museum announced in 2023 that it was returning the money and removed her name from a gallery (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece, on antiquities linked to a former donor and scholar.
1. The Art Newspaper: Benjamin Sutton, "Officials from three countries have called on Denver Art Museum to return eight artefacts in its collection" (16 August 2023)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/08/16/denver-art-museum-cambodia-thailand-vietnam-restitution-emma-bunker
- Officials from Cambodia, Thailand and Vietnam asked the museum to return eight antiquities they say were looted; six of the eight were donated by the late scholar Emma C. Bunker.
- Bunker helped the museum acquire more than a dozen objects from the antiquities smuggler Douglas Latchford, mostly Cambodian pieces smuggled out during the Khmer Rouge era and the civil war that followed.
- A museum spokesperson said the museum received courtesy copies of letters the three countries sent to US Department of Homeland Security officials, has not been contacted by the countries directly, and is working with US authorities. In March 2023 it deaccessioned five Bunker-linked works and began working with the US Department of Justice to return them.

WIDER CONTEXT
- KCUR (Frank Morris, "The Kemper Museum Of Contemporary Art Caught Up In National Immigration Debate", 31 August 2019) reported that the Fang Collective was running social media campaigns against UMB Bank and the Kemper Museum of Contemporary Art in Kansas City over the bank's role in financing the Wyatt Detention Center in Rhode Island, which began holding ICE detainees in spring 2019.
- UMB said it is third-party indenture trustee for the bondholders who financed the prison and has no operational control of or financial stake in it (KCUR). KCUR ties UMB to the Kemper Museum through trustee Mariner Kemper, UMB's chief executive; it does not mention the Denver Art Museum.
- UMB Bank is a co-presenting sponsor of the Denver Art Museum's Delight gala on 5 June 2026 (museum website); the 2019 campaign targeted UMB and the Kemper Museum, not the Denver Art Museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a private nonprofit with public support from the SCFD sales tax and Denver bond money, and with named private donors such as the Martin family and Sarah Shaw Anschutz. The documented controversy is the provenance of antiquities connected to the former donor Emma Bunker; the museum has returned her family's money and deaccessioned five works, and says it is working with US authorities.
