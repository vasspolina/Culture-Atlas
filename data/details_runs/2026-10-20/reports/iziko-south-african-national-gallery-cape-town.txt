IZIKO SOUTH AFRICAN NATIONAL GALLERY: Cape Town, South Africa
ID: iziko-south-african-national-gallery-cape-town
Website: https://www.iziko.org.za/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National art gallery on Government Avenue in the Company's Garden, Cape Town; one of Iziko's 11 museums, with an Annexe that houses the Art Library (Iziko).
- Iziko was set up in 1998 when five national museums in the Western Cape merged; it was renamed Iziko Museums of Cape Town in 2001 and Iziko Museums of South Africa in 2012 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the gallery's own founding year, architect or earlier names in the pages opened.
- Friends of the Iziko South African National Gallery formed in 1968 to support the gallery (Iziko).
- Shows in 2026 included "Modernism and its Discontents: South African Art from the Permanent Collection, 1925-2025" and Steven Cohen's "Long Life", which runs to 30 June 2026 (Iziko, Brooklyn Rail).
- Visiting: open daily 09:00 to 17:00, closed Christmas Day and Workers' Day; local adults R60, children 5 to 17 R40, pensioners and students R40 with a card, international visitors R100; Friends members free; public programmes free. No accessibility information on the page.

FUNDING
Iziko South African National Gallery: mainly public: South African national government
- Iziko is an agency of the national Department of Arts and Culture, now Sport, Arts and Culture (Wikipedia).
- National Treasury's fourth-quarter 2025/26 report for Iziko Museums of Cape Town shows approved receipts of R269.7 million: transfers R235.8 million (90.4 percent of the year's receipts to date), sales of goods and services R23.7 million approved, interest and dividends R10.2 million. R233.7 million of transfers had been received. The report's five largest transfer lines include departmental transfers, local non-government sources and other transfers; the text extract does not give their labels with amounts reliably.
- This is for all of Iziko, not the gallery alone. No reporting found of a figure for the gallery.
- The Iziko site names no donors or corporate sponsors.

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, Cohen retrospective, December 2025
- On the night before the 11 December 2025 opening, Iziko's senior management emailed curator Anthea Buys, just after 10pm, that works would come out of Steven Cohen's retrospective "Long Life"; eleven works were then covered with black cloth (TimesLIVE; the Brooklyn Rail counts twelve).
- The covered works include video and photographs from "Golgotha" (2007 to 2009), shoes made from human skulls, and depictions of performances involving the late Nomsa Dlamini, Cohen's former domestic worker and collaborator (TimesLIVE).
- Iziko cited the historical representation of Black women and racialised display, the dignity of elders, power and authorship in depicting individuals, and its commitments on ancestral human remains (TimesLIVE, Mail & Guardian).
- Buys says Iziko acted unilaterally, in breach of a contract requiring final curatorial decisions to be agreed by both parties (Mail & Guardian). Cohen called the museum's decision a disservice to the public (TimesLIVE).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, Mthethwa work, December 2016
- The 2016 show "Our Lady", co-organised with the New Church Museum, included a work by Zwelethu Mthethwa, then on trial for the 2013 murder of sex worker Nokuphila Kumalo (GroundUp, Ashleigh Furlong).
- SWEAT called the inclusion "not only in bad taste but deeply offensive"; SWEAT and Sisonke activists protested outside with placards naming murdered sex workers (GroundUp).
- After talks Iziko agreed to a public dialogue, a joint statement beside the work and a portrait of Kumalo in a later show (GroundUp).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns Iziko's handling of the Cohen retrospective; it comes from a South African newspaper and a US art magazine.
1. Mail & Guardian: Lesego Chepape, "Navigating ethics and art" (2025)
URL: https://mg.co.za/friday/2025-12-19-navigating-ethics-and-art/
- Argues the fault was process and timing: the ethical questions were foreseeable after a development period of more than a decade, so late intervention risks acting as control rather than care.
- Presents the black cloths as a signal that something was interrupted.
- Reports Buys's account that Iziko acted unilaterally and against the contract.
2. The Brooklyn Rail: Norman L. Kleeblatt, "Steven Cohen: Long Life" (2026)
URL: https://brooklynrail.org/2026/04/artseen/steven-cohen-long-life/
- Says installers cut cloths in haste after management demanded removal, and that the gallery had known the content for months.
- Quotes Chepape: "What changed was not the work but the moment at which the institution chose to act."
- Notes some voices approve of the censorship.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public: the South African government funds Iziko, and no corporate or private funder is named on the gallery's site. The weak points are the state-run gallery's late removal of Cohen's works in December 2025 and the 2016 protest over a murder-accused artist's work. Iziko publishes annual reports with donor sections; those were not opened for this report.
