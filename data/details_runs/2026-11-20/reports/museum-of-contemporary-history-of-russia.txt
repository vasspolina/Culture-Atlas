MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY HISTORY OF RUSSIA: Moscow, Russia
ID: museum-of-contemporary-history-of-russia
Website: https://www.sovrhistory.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The State Central Museum of Contemporary History of Russia, at Tverskaya 21 in a late 18th-century mansion listed as a federal heritage site; the Moscow English Club met there from 1831 to 1917 (museum brochure, fessl.ru).
- A 1922 exhibition "Red Moscow" in the building grew into a museum of revolutionary history; the Central Executive Committee of the USSR founded the Museum of the Revolution of the USSR on 9 May 1924, its charter calling it a scientific and agitational institution (museum brochure).
- It became the Central Museum of the Revolution of the USSR in 1968 and took its present name in 1998 (museum brochure).
- The collection holds about 1.4 million items and the library about a million volumes (museum brochure).
- Besides the main building it runs the Presnya museum, the 1905 to 1906 underground printing press, Krzhizhanovsky's flat, the Yevtushenko gallery in Peredelkino and the memorial complexes of Katyn and Mednoye (museum brochure; sovrhistory.ru).
- Director general: Irina Velikanova (sovrhistory.ru).
- Shows on in November 2026 include one on the 250th anniversary of the Bolshoi Theatre; the Presnya site closes for restoration from 5 October 2026 (sovrhistory.ru).

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary History of Russia: state-funded: Russia's Ministry of Culture
- A federal state budgetary institution of culture whose founder's powers are exercised by Russia's Ministry of Culture (financial plan 2019).
- The 2019 plan put income at 353,520,564.70 roubles: a state assignment subsidy of 280,101,072.80 roubles, other budget subsidies of 15,562,213.90 roubles and about 57.9 million roubles from paid services and other income (financial plan 2019).
- In 2017 the museum and the Russian Military Historical Society carried out a reconstruction at Katyn that added a new museum and exhibition centre (sovrhistory.ru).

Red flags: 2022 exhibition "NATO: Chronicle of Cruelty"
- Opened in April 2022, weeks after Russia invaded Ukraine; its central display held a Ukrainian bulletproof vest, uniform and insignia described as "unique exhibits" from the Russian Defence Ministry, and a guide linked a helmet with SS insignia to Ukrainian soldiers (The Moscow Times).
- Entry was free; the museum reported almost 20,000 visitors in under two months, and similar shows opened in other regions (The Moscow Times).

Red flags: 2024 war exhibition guidelines for Russian museums
- The museum helped compile, with the culture ministry and the Russian Historical Society, 28 pages of "Methodological Recommendations" for exhibitions on the war, following Putin's April 2023 order to create museums about it (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- The document calls Ukraine's government a "Nazi regime" and suggests showing "children's toys with traces of blood" (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: sanctions on Russia's culture minister (2022 to 2023)
- The EU listed culture minister Olga Lyubimova in December 2022, saying her ministry gave money and programme support to the so-called "Donetsk People's Republic" (The Art Newspaper).
- The US Treasury listed her on 24 February 2023 (The Art Newspaper), and the UK on 17 July 2023, with asset freezes (Interfax).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism in the art press treats the museum as a tool of the state's war propaganda.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Russia opens up new museum front in its war against Ukraine" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/07/russia-opens-up-new-museum-front-in-its-war-against-ukraine
- Frames the guidelines and new war museums as a Kremlin campaign to use museums to rewrite history and justify the war.
- Names "the contemporary history museum" as co-author of the guidelines.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 10 April 2026 the Russian Military Historical Society, chaired by Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky, put up an exhibition on "Polish Russophobia" outside the Polish war cemetery at Katyn; Notes from Poland reported that it downplays Soviet responsibility for the 1940 massacre.
- The museum runs the Katyn memorial complex, which holds the graves of more than 4,000 executed Polish prisoners of war (sovrhistory.ru).

BOTTOM LINE
Federal money through a ministry whose head is under Western sanctions. Since 2022 the museum has served as a model for war propaganda in Russian museums, and its Katyn branch is worth watching.
