ORDRUPGAARD: Charlottenlund, Denmark
ID: ordrupgaard-charlottenlund
Website: https://museumordrupgaard.dk (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- State-owned art museum in Charlottenlund, north of Copenhagen (Dezeen), with a park, the Finn Juhl House and a café (museum website).
- Wilhelm Hansen, an insurance executive, and his wife Henny had the country house built in 1916 to 1918 to a design by Gotfred Tvede; it opened on 14 September 1918. After Henny Hansen died in 1951 she left the collection, house and park to the state, and the museum opened to the public in 1953 (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Danish Golden Age works and French Impressionist paintings. After the 1922 collapse of Landmandsbanken, Hansen sold about 82 French works to cover his debts and bought new French paintings from 1923 to 1933 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a 1,150-square-metre extension by Zaha Hadid opened on 30 August 2005, and a largely underground extension by Snøhetta opened in August 2021 (Wikipedia, Dezeen).
- Finn Juhl House: built by the furniture designer Finn Juhl in 1941 to 1942, it joined the museum in 2008 through a private donation from Birgit Lyngbye Pedersen (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: exhibitions, the Finn Juhl House, café and shop open 11:00 to 17:00; the art park 8:00 to 18:00; an annual pass is sold. The museum site lists no admission prices, discounts or accessibility details on the pages read and links an accessibility statement.

FUNDING
Ordrupgaard: mainly state, with private foundations paying for building and exhibitions: state ownership, plus Augustinus Fonden, Det Obelske Familiefond and others
- State: Ordrupgaard is state-owned (Wikipedia). No reporting found of its annual state grant on the pages read.
- Snøhetta extension (2021): "Tilbygningen blev finansieret af A.P. Møller Fonden, Realdania, Det Obelske Familiefond, Knud Højgaards Fond" and Augustinus Fonden (Augustinus Fonden project page; translation: the extension was financed by those foundations). No amounts found.
- Augustinus Fonden: its project page lists a grant ("Bevilling") of DKK 2.5 million dated 2024. The page also states that the foundation's support for Ordrupgaard goes back to 1958, that it supports the museum's outreach and contributed to the 2024 exhibition "Impressionismen og dens oversete kvinder", and that the museum received support for two main exhibitions in 2025 (translations). The page does not say which activity the DKK 2.5 million covered.
- Det Obelske Familiefond was established in 1956 by Asta Obel and is the sole owner of the investment company C.W. Obel A/S (lex.dk). It gave to the 2021 extension (Augustinus Fonden page).
- Finn Juhl House: private donation from Birgit Lyngbye Pedersen (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of ticket income, a board or a list of current corporate sponsors.

Red flags: tobacco money (Augustinus Fonden and Det Obelske Familiefond)
- Augustinus Fonden owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, which held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group as of 1 May 2025 (Wikipedia, Scandinavian Tobacco Group investor page).
- C.W. Obel A/S, owned by Det Obelske Familiefond, held more than 10% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group on the same date; the investor page lists no other holder above 5% (Scandinavian Tobacco Group).
- Augustinus Fonden's page lists a DKK 2.5 million grant dated 2024 and support from 1958; both foundations co-financed the 2021 extension (Augustinus Fonden).
- No reporting found of a campaign or objection against either foundation's gifts to Ordrupgaard, or of a sponsor leaving the museum under pressure.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Ordrupgaard is a state museum, and its extensions and exhibitions are paid for by named private foundations. The weak points are two of them: Augustinus Fonden and Det Obelske Familiefond, whose holding companies together held more than 35% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group in May 2025. No reporting found of the state grant, ticket income or other sponsors on the pages read.
