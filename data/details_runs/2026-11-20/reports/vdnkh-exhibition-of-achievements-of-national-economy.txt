VDNKH (EXHIBITION OF ACHIEVEMENTS OF NATIONAL ECONOMY): Moscow, Russia
ID: vdnkh-exhibition-of-achievements-of-national-economy
Website: https://vdnh.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition grounds, park and museum complex of about 237 hectares in the north of Moscow (Calvert Journal, 2015).
- It opened on 1 August 1939 and has been named VSKhV, VDNKh of the USSR and VVC; in 1992 it became the joint-stock company "GAO VVC" (vdnh.ru).
- It was built with a pavilion for each Soviet republic and each major industry, and its 29 surviving pavilions are listed monuments (Calvert Journal).
- The VDNKh name came back after a public vote in which 90% of Muscovites backed it (Calvert Journal).
- Museums on the site include the Cosmonautics and Aviation Centre and the Atom museum (vdnh.ru).
- Pavilion 57 houses the history park Russia, My History (myhistorypark.ru).
- The site quotes Mayor Sergei Sobyanin: about 33 million people visit a year. Sergei Shogurov is general director of JSC VDNKh (vdnh.ru).
- Visiting: a free circular bus runs from 9:00 to 21:00, and visitors can borrow a wheelchair free by booking at the information centre in the main entrance arch (vdnh.ru).

FUNDING
VDNKh: public: City of Moscow, with private investors
- The operator is JSC "Exhibition of Achievements of National Economy" (OGRN 1027700008874) (vdnh.ru).
- In June 2013 the federal government held 70% and the city of Moscow 30% (Vedomosti, 2013). In November 2013 the federal government said it was handing its 70% share to the Moscow city government within six months (The Moscow Times, 2013).
- Sobyanin ordered a city subsidy of 3.09 billion roubles for 2014, more than 1.5 billion of it for emergency repairs to 118 pavilions and structures (BFM, 2014).
- In 2019 the first stage of reconstruction was costed at more than 100 billion roubles: almost 60 billion from the Moscow budget and about 47 billion from private investors, among them Samvel Karapetyan's Tashir and the Mutsoev family's Regiony. Net profit in 2017 was 8.8 million roubles (Snob, citing Vedomosti).

Red flags: Investment by Gennady Timchenko's companies, 2019
- Snob, citing Vedomosti, reported in 2019 that companies of Gennady Timchenko were among the private investors in the reconstruction and that Timchenko planned to reopen the Golden Kolos restaurant on the site.
- The UK designated Timchenko on 22 February 2022 (UK Sanctions List).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art press coverage; one design and culture magazine weighed the renovation against the site's propaganda past.
1. The Calvert Journal: Anya Filippova, "Grand designs: can a Stalinist propaganda park become an appealing public space?" (2015)
URL: https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/3568/vdnkh-moscow-stalinist-renovation-urbanism-public-space
- Reports that the project had been dubbed "hipster Stalinism" and that the revival could be read as a new empire remaking the old Soviet empire's sacred seat in its own image.
- Notes that the ice rink cost about 784.5 million roubles, which drew questions from the anti-corruption campaigner Alexei Navalny.
- Concludes that the new VDNKh will still be a welcome public space for Moscow.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The UK designated Mayor Sergei Sobyanin on 15 March 2022 for speaking in favour of the "special military operation" in Ukraine (UK Sanctions List).
- In 2015 the Culture Ministry ordered VDNKh redesignated as a "tourist zone"; Konstantin Mikhailov of the preservation group Arkhnadzor said the rules legalised buildings put up in the previous three to four years and left room for new construction (The Moscow Times, 2015).
- Sobyanin heads the city government that owns VDNKh, and the Culture Ministry rules on its heritage status.

BOTTOM LINE
VDNKh runs on Moscow city money, with private capital in its rebuilding. The weak point is a sanctioned investor among those private backers; who owns each new project on the site is worth watching.
