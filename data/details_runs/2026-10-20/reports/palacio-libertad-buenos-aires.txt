PALACIO LIBERTAD: Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: palacio-libertad-buenos-aires
Website: https://palaciolibertad.gob.ar/ (checked 2026-10-24; returned an access error to the reader here, so the site's own text was not read)

OVERVIEW
- National cultural centre in the former Central Post Office building (Palacio de Correos) in central Buenos Aires; it depends on the national Secretaría de Cultura (La Nación, 11 September 2025).
- History of the building: the architect Norbert Maillart was commissioned in 1889; the post office opened in 1928 as an eight-storey, 60-metre building, then Argentina's largest public building. It was named a National Historic Monument in 1997 and its last postal bureau closed in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- Néstor Kirchner proposed converting it into a cultural centre in 2005; the centre opened in May 2015 as the Centro Cultural Kirchner (CCK), in a design by the Bares siblings that uses glass and stainless steel against the original interior (Wikipedia).
- Renamed: the Milei government announced a name change in March 2024 and chose "Palacio Libertad" in May 2024; decree 897/24 of 10 October 2024 made the name "Palacio Libertad, Centro Cultural Domingo Faustino Sarmiento" official (Wikipedia). In April and May 2024 the new management covered a plaque naming Cristina Fernández de Kirchner and others and removed signs bearing the Kirchner name (La Nación, Diego Cabot, 7 May 2024).
- On site: a symphonic hall for 1,950 people with a Klais pipe organ of about 8,000 pipes, exhibition halls and a sixth-floor exhibition space (Wikipedia, Perfil).
- Visiting: Wikipedia states admission is free. No reporting found of opening days, hours or accessibility on pages read.

FUNDING
Palacio Libertad: state: the Argentine federal government through the Secretaría de Cultura
- A federal state centre; Wikipedia gives no construction cost or funding breakdown. No reporting found of an operating budget, sponsors or a board.
- La Nación (7 May 2024) reported that the new management left some Kirchner-era signs in place because there was no budget to replot them.
- No reporting found of corporate or private funders.

Red flags: state censorship
- Fileteadores show, September 2025: hours before the opening of the 14th Encuentro Anual de Fileteadores, the Asociación de Fileteadores announced the show would not take place. It said Palacio Libertad authorities had asked for three works supporting Palestine and one defending public education to come down; the show had 160 works by 152 artists. The curator, Ayelén Fagiani, said authorities told them the works "herían la susceptibilidad del Gobierno" (translation: offended the government's sensitivities). Culture sources rejected the censorship claim, said no national cultural venue would host political expression of any kind, and said withdrawing the show was the association's decision (La Nación, Daniel Gigena, 11 September 2025).
- Indigenous Art Biennial show, early 2026: according to Laura Isola in Perfil, authorities pulled the Biennial's exhibition at Palacio Libertad close to the opening, citing "un cambio de programación" (a change of programming), and asked that the word "indigenous" not appear, that there be no curatorial text and that the organising network's logos be removed; a Chaco-region show planned for the sixth floor was also blocked (Perfil, 22 March 2026). Perfil's teaser line for a related piece also reports complaints of "censura y ensañamiento" over the cancellation (Perfil, 20 February 2026). The only official position Isola reports is the programming explanation.

Red flags: protest by artists
- The Asociación de Fileteadores publicly denounced the cancellation and said the show would go ahead at another venue, moving all works within 24 hours (La Nación).
- The Biennial was held instead at the Pabellón de Bellas Artes of the Universidad Católica Argentina in Puerto Madero, open until 12 April 2026 (Perfil, Laleska Villanueva, 20 February 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Palacio Libertad is the Argentine federal government's, with no sponsors or budget published on the pages read. The weak points are two exhibitions cancelled or blocked on officials' instructions in 2025 and 2026, and the renaming and removal of Kirchner-era signs, all under the same state that runs the centre. Watch whether the Secretaría de Cultura publishes programming criteria.
