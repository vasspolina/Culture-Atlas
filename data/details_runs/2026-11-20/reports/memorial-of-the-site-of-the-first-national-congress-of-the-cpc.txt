MEMORIAL OF THE SITE OF THE FIRST NATIONAL CONGRESS OF THE CPC: Shanghai, China

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the founding of the Communist Party of China at 76 Xingye Road in Xintiandi, Huangpu district, in the shikumen house of founding member Li Hanjun where the First Congress met in July 1921 (Wikipedia, China Daily).
- A Shanghai Museum cadre traced the site in 1950, when it was a noodle shop; the Shanghai Party committee rented it from September 1951 and bought it in May 1952 (Wikipedia, citing historian Denise Ho).
- It has been open free to the public since 1952; it was named a national cultural relic under state protection in 1961, closed from 1966 to 1968 in the Cultural Revolution, and added previously excluded founders such as Chen Duxiu to its displays in 1980 (China Daily, Wikipedia).
- The Propaganda Department of the Party Central Committee listed it in July 1997 in its first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- Xi Jinping took the other six members of the Politburo Standing Committee there on 31 October 2017 to review the Party oath, and called the site the "spiritual home" of Chinese communists (Xinhua via gov.cn, state media).
- After nearly two years of works Shanghai Party secretary Li Qiang opened a new memorial hall across the street on 3 June 2021, for the Party's centenary, whose exhibition "Epoch-Making Beginnings" covers 3,700 square metres, more than three times the old display, with 1,168 exhibits and 612 relics (China Daily, China News Service, both state media).
- The complex comprises the congress site, the former Bowen Girls' School where most delegates stayed, the oath hall and the new hall; the restored dormitory site opened on 24 March 2026 (China News Service, Xinhua, state media). The museum also manages the Zhou Gongguan site (Shanghai budget 2026).
- In 2022 it reported more than 128,000 objects in its collection and over 5.6 million visits in five years; Xue Feng was Party secretary and director (China News Service, state media).
- Visiting: at the 2021 opening, Tuesday to Sunday 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:30, closed Mondays except public holidays, with real-name booking through the museum's WeChat mini program; the hall and the congress site are booked separately (The Paper, government channel).

FUNDING
Memorial of the Site of the First National Congress of the CPC: state-funded: Shanghai municipal budget
- A public institution fully funded by the Shanghai Municipal Finance Bureau, founded in 1952 (Shanghai 2026 municipal unit budget).
- 2026 budget: income 116.03 million yuan, of which 86.79 million in public budget appropriations, 24.74 million in operating income and 4.50 million in other income (Shanghai 2026 municipal unit budget).
- 2026 spending: 69.18 million on projects, 40.26 million on staff and 6.59 million on running costs; appropriations rose by 970,000 yuan on 2025 because of added staff (Shanghai 2026 municipal unit budget).
- Since 2016 Shanghai has run a "birthplace of the Party" programme to restore and open revolutionary sites, centred on "one hall and five sites" (China News Service, state media).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the memorial is Chinese public money and carries no funder flag. The weak point is purpose: it is run as a shrine to the Party's own founding.
