GROENINGEMUSEUM: Bruges, Belgium
ID: groeningemuseum
Website: https://www.museabrugge.be/en/visit/locations/groeningemuseum (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum at Dijver 12 in the centre of Bruges, showing six centuries of Belgian visual art.
- One of the 13 city museums and monuments run as Musea Brugge, which the Flemish government recognises as a cultural heritage institution.
- Built on the site of the medieval Eekhout Abbey (Wikipedia).
- The collection runs from Flemish primitives such as Jan van Eyck, Hans Memling and Gerard David to René Magritte, Roger Raveel and Raoul De Keyser.
- The museum closes on 11 January 2027 for a renovation of four years and reopens in 2031, and the core of the collection moves to BRUSK, the new exhibition hall next door that opened on 8 May 2026 (stated by the space).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00 from 1 April to 2 November and 9:30 to 17:00 from 3 November to 31 March, closed Monday. Admission is EUR 15, EUR 13 under 26, EUR 7 under 18, free under 13.

FUNDING
Groeningemuseum: public: the City of Bruges, with Flemish government money for the new museum site
- The City of Bruges runs the museum through Musea Brugge, whose brochure calls its 13 sites municipal museums and whose site carries the city's copyright line.
- The Flemish government pledged EUR 27.2 million for BRUSK, 60 per cent of an estimated cost of EUR 45 million, the largest sum it had given a project in Bruges, and the same master plan covers the Groeningemuseum renovation (VRT, 25 January 2021).
- Belfius Private, a Belgian private bank, is main sponsor of BRUSK and lends works from the Belfius Art Collection (BRUSK brochure, 2026).
- Other logos on the Musea Brugge partner page: Flanders, the Province of West Flanders, the Vrienden van Musea Brugge, Visit Bruges and the media partners VRT Radio 1, Klara and De Standaard.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and local: the city pays for the museum and the Flemish government for most of the new building beside it. The one corporate name is a Belgian bank. Worth watching: the four-year closure from January 2027 and the cost of the renovation.
