KUNSTNERNES HUS: Oslo, Norway
ID: kunstnernes-hus-oslo
Website: https://kunstnerneshus.no

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run exhibition house at Wergelandsveien 17 in Oslo. Its own site calls it Norway's oldest artist-run institution, non-commercial and independent since 1930.
- Norwegian artists founded and built it. It is owned by Norway's artists, organised in the professional artists' organisations (Kunstnernes Hus). A council of members from eight artists' organisations appoints the board of the foundation Stiftelsen Kunstnernes Hus (annual report 2024).
- It opened on 1 October 1930 with the Autumn Exhibition (Høstutstillingen), attended by King Haakon. It had cost 650,000 kroner (museum history page).
- The architects were Gudolf Blakstad and Herman Munthe-Kaas, whose entry "Felix" won a competition of 62 entries held after the site deed was signed in 1928. The building is concrete with the upper part clad in patterned brick.
- On site: exhibition halls, a cinema established in 2016, the café and bar Juret, a shop, education rooms and events. The Autumn Exhibition, a juried show, runs every year.
- The Wehrmacht seized the building in 1942 and it reopened on 22 November 1945 (museum history page). In 1938 Picasso's Guernica was shown there as part of the French exhibition.
- Visiting: exhibitions cost 140 NOK, or 70 NOK for students and under 30s; under 18s, artists, art students and members enter free. Companions with a companion card enter free. There is no ramp; wheelchair users use a back elevator (240 x 88 cm) that serves the exhibition halls and the cinema. The upper light halls and cinema are reachable, but the education rooms and Atelier Felix are not step free.

FUNDING
Kunstnernes Hus: operating grants from the Ministry of Culture and the City of Oslo, with foundation support; Sparebankstiftelsen DNB gave the start of the rehabilitation funding
- The foundation says it is partly financed ("dels finansiert") by operating grants from the Ministry of Culture and the City of Oslo (annual report 2024). The public operating subsidy in 2023 was 13,823,000 NOK against operating costs of 18,185,858 NOK to open the building without a programme (annual report 2024).
- Sparebankstiftelsen DNB gave 40 million NOK in 2024 as the start of the rehabilitation before the 2030 centenary. It also supports summer exhibitions, education programmes and the cinema (annual report 2024).
- Fritt Ord funded education activities and the cinema series in 2024. Bergesenstiftelsen supports the cinema and the exhibition series Underskogen, and Talent Norge also supports Underskogen (annual report 2024).
- The annual report also thanks Kulturrådet, Pro Helvetia, Goethe-Institut Norwegen, Lindbäck Langaards Stiftelse and Norsk filminstitutt in its list of partners and supporters.
- On 1 October 2026 Culture and Equality Minister Lubna Jaffery announced a state commitment of 64.6 million NOK from the national cultural buildings scheme, one third of the estimated rehabilitation cost of about 194 million NOK (house news item).
- The annual report put the rehabilitation at 186 million NOK. The house is on the city antiquarian's yellow list, and the board wrote that it cannot borrow because the building is not valued at market price.

BOTTOM LINE
The house is partly financed by operating grants from the Ministry of Culture and the City of Oslo, plus Sparebankstiftelsen DNB (40 million NOK in 2024), Fritt Ord, Bergesenstiftelsen and Talent Norge for programmes. The state added a 64.6 million NOK commitment to the rehabilitation in October 2026. The house is owned by Norway's artists, organised in their professional organisations (annual report 2024).
