INSTITUTO TUNGA: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
ID: instituto-tunga-rio-de-janeiro
Website: https://institutotunga.org (checked 2026-10-25; shows only the notice "Em manutenção", under maintenance, with no other content)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit institute created in 2017 "to study, preserve and disseminate" the work of the Brazilian artist Tunga (Fórum Permanente, October 2023).
- Antônio Mourão, the artist's only son, is its director, and Clara Gerchman is co-founder and manager of the institute's collection (Arte!Brasileiros, Eduardo Simões, 13 January 2023; Cahiers d'Art Institute).
- Named for the artist Tunga (Antonio José de Barros Carvalho e Mello Mourão, 8 February 1952 to 6 June 2016) (Wikipedia).
- Its main project is the catalogue raisonné "Tunga: The Two-Dimensional Works", started in 2021 by Mourão and Gerchman and covering over 10,000 works; the first volume, on the 1960s and 1970s, came out in December 2024, with later volumes released in 2025 and 2026 (Cahiers d'Art Institute).
- The institute has started certifying two-dimensional works by Tunga through an identification and authentication process (Fórum Permanente).
- No reporting found of a public building, opening hours or admission.

FUNDING
Instituto Tunga: artist's estate institute; the catalogue raisonné is corporate-sponsored by Banco Itaú and Sabesp
- The Cahiers d'Art Institute page says the catalogue raisonné is sponsored by Itaú Bank and Sabesp, the Basic Sanitation Company of the State of São Paulo, through the Rouanet Law. Fórum Permanente (October 2023) says the catalogue is developed "with the support of" Banco Itaú and Sabesp and does not mention the Rouanet Law.
- The Rouanet Law is a Brazilian tax incentive that lets businesses and individuals reduce their income tax by a portion of the amount they give to approved cultural projects; Wikipedia, citing Folha de S.Paulo, puts the cost to Treasury revenue at about R$1 billion.
- Tunga is represented by Luhring Augustine, New York, which showed "Vê-nus" in January 2023 (Arte!Brasileiros).
- No reporting found of how the institute itself is funded, of accounts, a funder list or a board; the institute's site did not load.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia reports that critics of the Rouanet Law say the state stopped directly funding culture and let companies "themselves decide which kind of culture deserved to be sponsored". It also lists misappropriation of funds among the criticisms, citing an Estadão report on Petrobras money reaching shell companies. It names no speaker for either point, and none of it mentions Tunga or the institute.
- The catalogue raisonné is financed through this law, according to the Cahiers d'Art Institute page.

BOTTOM LINE
The sponsors of the catalogue are named: a bank and a state water utility paying through a tax incentive. No flagged funder was found. The weak points are that the institute's own funding, accounts and board are not published anywhere that could be opened, and its site shows only a maintenance notice. Worth watching: how the estate institute is funded beyond the catalogue.
