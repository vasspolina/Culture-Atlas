TRAPHOLT: Kolding, Denmark
ID: trapholt-kolding
Website: https://www.trapholt.dk (checked 2026-10-24; opens, and says the museum is closed for rebuilding)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern art, craft and design at Æblehaven 23 in Kolding, by Kolding Fjord, in a city that belongs to UNESCO's Creative Cities Network (the museum).
- Closed for rebuilding: the last opening day was 30 December 2025 and the museum expects to reopen in spring 2027 (the museum).
- The land was a summer residence built in the 1930s for the dentist Gustav Lind and his wife. In 1972 the Linds set up Trapholtfonden and gave the land to Kolding Municipality, which committed to create an art museum there (the museum).
- The museum opened on 2 June 1988 in buildings by architects Boje Lundgaard and Bente Aude; Lundgaard and Lene Tranberg designed a 1996 extension (the museum, Augustinus Fonden). It began with works from Kolding Kunstforening and holds about 7,000 works; it is a state-recognised museum.
- Furniture collection of more than 500 chairs from the 20th century, the largest in Denmark; a sculpture park; a hall of paintings by Richard Mortensen; and Arne Jacobsen's Kubeflex summer house, moved there in 2005 (the museum, Wikipedia). Cafe Gustav Lind and a design shop.
- Wikipedia reports that in 2000 Marco Evaristti's installation "Helena", blenders holding live goldfish, led to the museum's director being fined for animal cruelty after refusing a police request to turn the blenders off; he and the museum were later acquitted and the fine was retracted.
- Visiting: closed until spring 2027. The museum states it is wheelchair accessible, with free parking at the door. Admission prices were not on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Trapholt: mixed: state and municipal support, foundations and earned income
- The rebuild has a total grant of DKK 102.4 million from Augustinus Fonden, Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond, Villum Fonden and Kolding Kommune, announced by Augustinus Fonden on 21 February 2025. Neither that item nor Byggeri & Arkitektur (29 April 2025) gives each funder's share. 3XN won the design competition from five teams (Byggeri & Arkitektur).
- The scope is a new guest reception, a sculpture lounge, an education and CRAFT centre for up to 500 people, and an upgraded park.
- Kolding Municipality received the land as the Linds' gift in 1972, and Trapholt is a state-recognised museum (the museum). The museum's documents page links to annual reports; their figures were not read.
- No reporting found, on the pages opened, of the origin of the Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond capital or of operating grant amounts.

Red flags: Augustinus Fonden, tobacco-built foundation
- Augustinus Fonden was set up in 1942 by Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, one of the three predecessor companies of Scandinavian Tobacco Group (Wikipedia).
- It owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker fully; Wikipedia lists that company's subsidiaries as Skandinavisk Holding A/S (65%), which holds Scandinavian Tobacco Group (51%).
- The foundation is one of the four funders of the DKK 102.4M rebuild, announced on 21 February 2025. Its own share of the sum is not stated.
- No reporting found of a protest or objection to its gift to Trapholt.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Trapholt's rebuild is paid by three Danish foundations and the municipality, and the one flagged name is Augustinus Fonden, which was founded by a tobacco company and holds its Scandinavian Tobacco Group stake indirectly. The weak point is that Augustinus Fonden's share of the DKK 102.4M, given in 2025, is not published in the pages opened. Watch the 2025 accounts and the reopening in 2027.
