QUEENS MUSEUM: New York, USA
ID: queens-museum
Website: https://queensmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum and educational centre in the New York City Building in Flushing Meadows Corona Park, Queens, on the site of the 1939-40 and 1964-65 World's Fairs; the building is owned by the City of New York (museum website).
- Opened in November 1972 as the Queens County Art and Cultural Center and was renamed the Queens Museum in 1973 (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by Aymar Embury II as the New York City Pavilion for the 1939 World's Fair; it housed the UN General Assembly from 1946 to 1951 (Wikipedia). The 1947 UN vote on the partition of Palestine took place on the site (Hyperallergic).
- Open Wednesday to Friday 12 pm to 5 pm and Saturday and Sunday 11 am to 5 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday (museum website). Wikipedia states that admission is pay what you want.

FUNDING
Queens Museum: public funds from New York City, New York State and Queens officials, plus members and trustees
- The museum website states that its support comes in part from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs, with assistance from the Queens Borough President and the New York City Council, and from the New York State Council on the Arts. It also lists the Board of Trustees, members and friends.
- Capital money: Wikipedia lists $10.6 million from Queens Borough President Helen Marshall in 2006, $26.4 million from the city in 2021, and $8.5 million each from the city (2023) and the state (2025) for the Suna Children's Museum wing. Hyperallergic confirms the $26.4 million city award of 2021 for the second phase of expansion.

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- 2017: the museum cancelled, then reinstated, an on-site event organised by Israel's ambassador to the UN, Danny Danon, to commemorate the 70th anniversary of Israel's founding with a reenactment of the 1947 UN partition vote. Hyperallergic, citing the Jerusalem Post, reported that director Laura Raicovich told Danon she was reversing her approval, citing concerns from "Palestinian friends of the museum", and that her message said the board had voted not to hold a "political event." Danon called for her immediate dismissal. Councilman Rory Lancman and Assemblyman Michael Simanowitz called the decision "a disgrace and a violation of law." After what the museum described as a "productive conversation" with Danon, it reversed its decision and said it would work with the Israeli Mission on the commemoration (Hyperallergic, Claire Voon, 18 August 2017).
- 2023: in October, 17 Queens Museum employees signed a letter asking leadership to address the building's history as the site of the 1947 UN vote and to speak publicly on Palestine. Several signatories joined a strike for Palestine on 20 October. Staff then described heightened surveillance, and the director said circulating the letter and strike call by museum email broke museum policy. At a staff meeting on 16 November Tallant said the museum "is not able to make political statements" and cited its nonprofit status, its bylaws and the American Alliance of Museums code of ethics (Hyperallergic, Maya Pontone, 17 November 2023).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Hyperallergic reported two episodes in which the museum was criticised: by Israeli and New York officials in 2017, and by its own staff in 2023.
1. Hyperallergic: Claire Voon, "Queens Museum Cancels, Then Reinstates Israel-Sponsored Event After Accusations of Antisemitism" (2017)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/396426/queens-museum-israel-event-antisemitism/
- Reports that the museum cancelled an on-site event for the 70th anniversary of Israel's founding, organised by Danon, and reversed the decision after objections from Danon and New York officials, including Comptroller Scott Stringer, who said it sent "a disappointing message."
- Reports the museum's statement that it would work with the Israeli Mission on the commemoration of the 1947 vote.
2. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Queens Museum Director Declines to Issue Statement on Gaza" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/857430/queens-museum-director-declines-to-issue-statement-on-gaza/
- Reports a staff letter signed by 17 employees, a strike on 20 October 2023 and staff accounts of surveillance.
- The museum declined to issue a statement on Palestine.

BOTTOM LINE
The documented money behind the Queens Museum is a city-owned building, public funds from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs, the Queens Borough President, the City Council and the New York State Council on the Arts, capital grants from the city and state, plus members and trustees. The documented points are the two protest episodes, the 2017 pressure over the cancelled Israeli Mission event and the 2023 staff letter and strike over the museum's silence on Palestine.
