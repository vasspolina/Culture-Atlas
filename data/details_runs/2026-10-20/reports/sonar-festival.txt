SÓNAR FESTIVAL: Barcelona, Spain
ID: sonar-festival
Website: https://sonar.es/en (checked 2026-10-24; live, showing the 2026 edition on 18 to 20 June and the next on 17 to 19 June 2027)

OVERVIEW
- Annual electronic music, art and technology festival in Barcelona, run by Advanced Music SL (Sónar website).
- Founded in 1994 as the "Festival of Advanced Music and Multimedia Art" by Ricard Robles, Enric Palau and Sergio Caballero; the first edition ran from 2 to 4 June 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Early venues were the Centre de Cultura Contemporània de Barcelona (by day) and the Apolo club (by night); Sónar by Night moved to the Fira Gran Via area in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat in 2001, and Sónar by Day moved to Fira Montjuïc in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Sónar+D, a business and technology congress, has run alongside the day programme since 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Attendance was about 126,000 in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of ticket prices or accessibility in the pages opened.

FUNDING
Sónar Festival: private: owned by Superstruct Entertainment, itself owned by KKR; also small public grants and brand partners
- Ownership: the founders sold their majority stake to Superstruct Entertainment in 2018. Superstruct was founded by James Barton in 2017 and acquired by KKR in June 2024 (Mixmag, 13 October 2025).
- Sónar says it has "no direct relationship" with KKR and that "not a single euro" of festival revenue goes to KKR (Catalan News, June 2025).
- Public money: Sónar received EUR 552,245 in 2025 and EUR 717,618 in 2024 from public bodies, about EUR 6.6 million over 11 years (El Salto, Crític, 6 July 2026). The Generalitat gave about EUR 300,000 in 2025, up from the usual EUR 250,000; the Barcelona City Council gave EUR 230,000 to EUR 300,000 a year through the Institut de Cultura; the State gave smaller grants without figures. Public aid was 5.41 percent of Sónar's income in 2023.
- Brand partners shown on the site (undated logos): Estrella Damm, Fritz-Kola, OCB, Vieta, Cutty Sark, Negrita, The Social Hub, BCN Music Lab, Fujifilm and Grey Goose, with the Generalitat de Catalunya and Barcelona City Council (Sónar website).
- No reporting found of a budget or accounts.

Red flags: Protest or boycott (artists withdrew over KKR's ownership)
- In May 2025, 70 artists who had played Sónar signed an open letter pledging not to perform and calling for support of the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement (Wikipedia).
- By 4 June 2025, 34 acts had withdrawn, among them Arca, Rone (who was to perform with (LA)HORDE and the National Ballet of Marseille), Juliana Huxtable and others (Catalan News, which counts 28 earlier plus 6 announced 3 June).
- Unverified: activist groups aligned with BDS say KKR holds investments that support the Israeli military and Israeli settlements; Catalan News and Wikipedia report the claim, and Wikipedia names Axel Springer (which operates Yad2), Optiv and the Coastal GasLink pipeline, citing El Salto and Billboard. Sónar said it condemns the "genocide of the Palestinian people" and distanced itself from KKR (Catalan News).
- Superstruct said festival proceeds would stay within Superstruct and not go to KKR (Wikipedia). The founders and the CEO stepped back in October 2025; Superstruct called it a "planned exit" agreed in 2018 (Mixmag).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is in music and news press, not art press, and concerns ownership and public subsidy, not the programme.

- Reports that Palau, Robles and Caballero stepped back after scores of artists withdrew, with François Jozic becoming CEO.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival is private and owned through Superstruct by KKR, and it also takes about EUR 550,000 a year in public grants. The weak points are KKR's investments, which BDS-aligned groups contest, and the artist withdrawals of 2025; Sónar says no money goes to KKR. Watch for published accounts and for what happens to the ownership after the founders' exit.
