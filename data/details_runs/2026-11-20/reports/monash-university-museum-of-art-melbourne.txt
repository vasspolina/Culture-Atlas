OVERVIEW
- University museum of contemporary art, known as MUMA, on the ground floor of Building F at Monash University's Caulfield campus, 900 Dandenong Road, Caulfield East (MUMA).
- Grew from a fund set up in 1961 by Vice Chancellor Louis Matheson to buy work by living Australian artists. Patrick McCaughey opened the Monash University Gallery in 1975; it took its present name in 2002 (MUMA).
- Moved in 2010 into a remodelled building first used by the Victorian Public Works Department; Kerstin Thompson Architects' redesign won two Victorian architecture awards in 2011 (MUMA).
- Dr Rebecca Coates has been director since July 2023, under an advisory committee chaired by Professor Mel Dodd, dean of Art, Design and Architecture (MUMA).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday; "Entry is always free" (MUMA).

FUNDING
Monash University Museum of Art: university museum: owned and run by Monash University, with private gifts for the building, commissions and programmes
- Monash University owns the museum (ABC, 26 March 2025).
- Building: MUMA credits philanthropic support for its Caulfield premises, naming the Ian Potter Sculpture Court, the Merlyn Myer Gallery, the Helen Macpherson Education Space and the Marc and Eva Besen Commission, Callum Morton's "Silverscreen" of 2010 (MUMA support page, archived 7 October 2024).
- MUMA Contemporaries: annual giving at four levels from $1,000 to $10,000; The Bruce Parncutt Family Foundation was on the 2020 donor list (MUMA, archived 7 August 2020).
- Research money: MUMA takes part in the Australian Research Council Linkage Grant project "Precarious Movements: Choreography and the Museum" (MUMA).
- Earned income: venue hire of its foyer, conference room, Education Lab and sculpture court, and a shop (MUMA).

Red flags: Postponement of "Stolon Press: Flat earth" by the university, 2025
- On 25 March 2025 Monash postponed the exhibition indefinitely. It was due to open on 8 May after 18 months of planning and included work by Khaled Sabsabi. The ABC understood it to be the first exhibition pulled in the gallery's 50 years (ABC, Nicola Heath and Daniel Browning, 26 March 2025).
- Monash said consultation had "identified there is a need for the Museum to deepen its collaboration and engagement on this exhibition" (ArtReview, 26 March 2025).
- Director Rebecca Coates later said: "Our recommendation was to put it on, but to manage it." Stolon Press said it was not consulted beforehand (Mojo News, Sue Kaufmann, 22 August 2025).
- Protest: Penelope Benton of the National Association for the Visual Arts (NAVA) called the decision "a devastating blow to artistic and academic freedom", and Ben Eltham, president of the NTEU Monash branch, said "a university censoring its own art gallery is a really bad idea" (NAVA, 27 March 2025). On 2 April students sent the Vice-Chancellor an open email with over 600 signatures (Mojo News).
- Unverified: Josh Milani, Sabsabi's gallerist, said MUMA's curators "started receiving pressure from their superiors" after Sabsabi's Venice commission was pulled (ABC; ArtReview).
- On 22 May Monash announced the show would open; it ran from 29 May to 12 July 2025 (Mojo News; Milani Gallery).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 13 February 2025 Creative Australia's board voted to drop Sabsabi and curator Michael Dagostino from the 2026 Venice Biennale, after Coalition arts spokesperson Claire Chandler raised in parliament works of his from more than 15 years earlier. Mikala Tai, Tahmina Maskinyar and board member Lindy Lee resigned (ABC).
- On 2 July 2025 Creative Australia reinstated the artistic team after a review (Mojo News).
- The MUMA postponement came a month after the Venice decision.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind MUMA is clean: a university budget and named private gifts. The weak point is the university's power over the programme, which it used against the museum's own advice in 2025 and reversed only under protest. Watch how far Monash's leadership steers what MUMA shows.
