WHITNEY MUSEUM OF AMERICAN ART: New York, USA
ID: whitney-museum-of-american-art
Website: https://whitney.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of American art at 99 Gansevoort Street in the Meatpacking District, Manhattan.
- Founded in 1930 by Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney after the Met declined her offer of over 500 American works; it opened on 18 November 1931 in three converted row houses on West 8th Street (Wikipedia).
- The current building, by Renzo Piano, opened on 1 May 2015 and cost about $422 million; from 1966 to 2014 it was in a Marcel Breuer building at 945 Madison Avenue (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 10:30 am to 6 pm Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday, to 10 pm Friday, closed Tuesday. Adults $30, seniors and students $24, 25 and under and members free; free Friday nights from 5 pm and second Sundays. Ramps, elevators and free wheelchairs.

FUNDING
Whitney Museum of American Art: privately funded: trustee and donor giving, earned income and corporate sponsors including Hyundai Motor
- Form 990 (ProPublica), year to June 2025: revenue $191,356,000, contributions $50,977,000, expenses $96,002,000, net assets $1,027,457,000.
- Hyundai Motor: a 10-year partnership announced on 6 February 2024, in which it is presenting partner of the Whitney Biennial and supports the annual Hyundai Terrace Commission. No amount is given.
- No reporting found of Tiffany & Co. or Genesis as Whitney supporters on the pages opened; neither is credited on the 2026 Biennial page.
- 2026 Biennial: presented by Fondazione Bvlgari, with support from Hyundai Motor; leadership support from David Cancel and from Stephanie March and Dan Benton; significant support from Sotheby's; endowments from Emily Fisher Landau, Leonard A. Lauder and Fern and Lenard Tessler (stated by the Whitney).
- Leonard A. Lauder's 2008 gift of $131 million was the largest donation in the museum's history (Wikipedia).

Red flags: arms and defence trustees
- Kanders: Warren Kanders was Whitney vice chairman, and chairman, chief executive and majority owner of Safariland, a maker of law enforcement and military equipment, including tear gas. In March 2019 Decolonize This Place began weekly demonstrations demanding his removal; staff had written to management in November 2018, and over 100 artists, critics and scholars signed an open letter in April 2019. He resigned on 25 July 2019 (The Art Newspaper).
- Biennial: eight participants, seven artists and Forensic Architecture, withdrew work from the 2019 Biennial; four of them cited the museum's "continued failure to respond in any meaningful way" (The Art Newspaper, 19 July 2019; Hyperallergic, 20 July 2019).
- Crown: on 23 May 2025 about 50 activists protested in the lobby, with pamphlets naming board member Nancy Carrington Crown, whose family members are major shareholders of General Dynamics, and sponsor Hyundai Motors, and banners reading "artwashing genocide". The Whitney did not immediately respond to a request for comment (Hyperallergic, 27 May 2025). The Whitney's board page, as of 6 October 2026, lists her as a vice president.
- The protest followed the museum's cancelling of a performance about Palestinian mourning on 14 May 2025 (Hyperallergic).
- Unverified: the protesters' pamphlets tie General Dynamics and Hyundai Motors to the Israeli military, a claim Hyperallergic reports.

Overall: Criticism centres on Kanders in 2019 and a smaller 2025 protest; the art press mostly reported rather than argued it. An Artforum essay on the 2019 Biennial was paywalled and not read.

WIDER CONTEXT
- David A. Ross, Whitney director from 1991 to 1998, resigned as chair of the MFA art practice department at the School of Visual Arts, effective 3 February 2026, after emails showed contact with Epstein from 2009 to 2016 (The Art Newspaper, 10 February 2026). The article says he sought to bolster relationships with donors including Epstein; it reports no Whitney statement and no gift from Epstein to the Whitney.

BOTTOM LINE
The Whitney is privately funded, with a billion-dollar balance sheet and named sponsors. Its weak points are trustees tied to arms companies: Kanders (Safariland), whose resignation as vice chairman followed the 2019 campaign, and the Crown family (General Dynamics shareholders), targeted in 2025. Worth watching: any Whitney statement on Ross.
