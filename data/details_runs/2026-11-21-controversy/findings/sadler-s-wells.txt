ID: sadler-s-wells

Red flags: 2024 dispute over Maguy Marin's Gaza statement
- Sir Alistair Spalding, director of Sadler's Wells, prohibited Maguy Marin from reading a statement on Israel's aggression in Gaza after performances of May B in May 2024, a statement she had read at earlier European performances; Spalding said an artist's message should be expressed in the work and cited neutrality and audience concerns (Writing about dance, Nicholas Minns, 13 July 2024).
- Company president Antoine Manologlou said the statement named a Sadler's Wells sponsor, Barclays Bank, over investments supporting arms purchases in Israel (Writing about dance).
- Unverified: security staff "intervened aggressively" when Marin and company members handed printed copies of the statement to the audience on 22 May 2024, from Writing about dance.

Red flags: 2024 complaint over a Breakin' Convention set
- UK Lawyers for Israel wrote to Spalding asking for an urgent investigation into possible breaches of the Equality Act over a set by Sasha Mahfouz Shadid at Breakin' Convention on 5 May 2024; a Sadler's Wells spokesman said: "We are aware of the complaint and are investigating it" (Jewish News, Jenni Frazer, 3 June 2024).

Red flags: 2025 withdrawal of associate artist Michael Keegan-Dolan over Barclays
- On 16 September 2025 Michael Keegan-Dolan, who signs as Sadler's Wells Associate Artist 2011 to 2025, said in a statement: "I can no longer remain affiliated with Sadler's Wells, particularly in light of the Theatre's partnership with Barclays and the lack of meaningful institutional response to the concerns raised around it" (Teaċ Daṁsa statement, 16 September 2025).
- He named Barclays' connections to the arms trade and to the Israeli government, and said that week's performances would be Teaċ Daṁsa's last at Sadler's Wells and that, unless the institution changed its position, The Only Tune in November would end his contribution as an Associate Artist (Teaċ Daṁsa statement).
