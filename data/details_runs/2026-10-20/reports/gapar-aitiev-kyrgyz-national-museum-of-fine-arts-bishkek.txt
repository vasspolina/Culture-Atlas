GAPAR AITIEV KYRGYZ NATIONAL MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS: Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan
ID: gapar-aitiev-kyrgyz-national-museum-of-fine-arts-bishkek
Website: No website for the museum was found or checked (2026-10-20); the record has none.

OVERVIEW
- National art museum on Sovietskaya Street, opposite the opera house and near Ala-Too Square in central Bishkek (SRAS Museum Studies Abroad).
- Grew from a picture gallery the Kyrgyz regional Communist Party committee opened in 1934 (Voices on Central Asia); it opened in January 1935 with 72 works chosen by Semyon Chuikov (SRAS).
- Named for Gapar Aitiev, an early Soviet Kyrgyz artist (SRAS).
- The current building, a brutalist design by Shailo Djekshenbaev, opened in 1974 (SRAS).
- Holds about 18,000 items, about 3,000 of them ethnographic, with Kyrgyz applied art (felt carpets, embroidery, silver jewellery, wood carving) the largest part, plus painting, graphics, sculpture and reproductions of European works (Voices on Central Asia; SRAS).
- The permanent display closed for repairs from 27 September 2025, with no reopening date given (24.kg). Earlier visitors reported a low entry fee (20 som for students in 2018) and guided tours in Russian, Kyrgyz and English (SRAS). No reporting found of accessibility provisions.

FUNDING
Gapar Aitiev Museum: public: Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Culture
- Owned and run by the Ministry of Culture, Information, Sports and Youth Policy (SRAS); the culture minister appoints and removes its director (24.kg).
- A 2022 visitor wrote that the old building "does not seem to have the facilities to keep its art in good condition", with damaged frames and works stored haphazardly (SRAS).
- No reporting found of a budget, sponsors, accounts or board.

Red flags: Feminnale censorship by the Culture Ministry
- The first Feminnale of contemporary art, with 56 artists from 22 countries, opened at the museum in late November 2019 (Human Rights Watch gives 28 November; New East Digital Archive).
- Culture Minister Azamat Zhamankulov met its opponents and ordered several works he called "provocative" and "amoral" removed (Human Rights Watch), including, in the New East Digital Archive's account, Zoya Falkova's punching bag shaped like a female body; the Danish artist Julie Savery's performance involving nudity was cancelled (ArtReview).
- On 2 December the minister said that "under the flag of feminism, campaigns with a naked woman were held in Parliament and in a church" and that a dedicated panel would review the exhibition (24.kg). On 5 December MP Kurmankul Zulushev called for a police investigation of some works (Human Rights Watch).

Red flags: threats and the director's departure
- Director Mira Dzhangaracheva left after threats against her and her staff, including a threat to "tear me apart, to rape me" (Human Rights Watch). The ministry said she was dismissed; she said she stepped down voluntarily (24.kg; ArtReview).
- She blamed the nationalist group Kyrk Choro, whose supporters objected to the show (Voices on Central Asia). On 6 December 2019 the government announced an investigation into the threats (Human Rights Watch).
- The ministry appointed deputy director and chief curator Aigul Mambetkazieva in her place; the Union of Artists asked for Dzhangaracheva's return (24.kg).

Overall: The coverage criticises the government's censorship and the nationalist campaign, not the museum's own conduct.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for and controlled by the Kyrgyz state, with no private funders on record. The weak point is that control: in 2019 the culture minister removed works from a feminist show and the director left under threats. Worth watching: when the permanent display reopens and who pays for the repairs.
