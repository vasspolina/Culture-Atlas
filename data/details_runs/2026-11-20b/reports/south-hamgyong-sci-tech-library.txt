SOUTH HAMGYONG SCI-TECH LIBRARY: Hamhung, North Korea
ID: south-hamgyong-sci-tech-library
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public science and technology library (함경남도과학기술도서관) in the centre of Hamhung, South Hamgyong Province (Wikipedia; NK News).
- NK News describes it as understood to be the first large-scale facility of its kind outside Pyongyang, which holds the country's biggest Sci-Tech centre.
- Google Earth imagery shows the building went up on the site of the former South Hamgyong Cultural Exhibition House between February and November 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Radio Free Asia reported in March 2018 that its exterior copies the Sci-Tech Complex in Pyongyang, opened in January 2016 (RFA Korean service).
- Wikipedia describes a mix of the styles of Ryomyong New Town and Mirae Scientists Street, with two roof features resembling stylised hydrogen atoms and a helium atom statue in front.
- On 31 December 2018 NK News reported, citing state media, an opening ceremony held the previous Sunday.

FUNDING
South Hamgyong Sci-Tech Library (Hamhung): state-run: a provincial science library built by the North Korean state
- The opening was announced by state media, including the party newspaper Rodong Sinmun (NK News, 2018).
- Curtis Melvin of the US-Korea Institute at Johns Hopkins told RFA in March 2018 that the Kim Jong Un government builds identical facilities in each province, such as orphanages and children's traffic parks, that it can promote as the leader's achievements, and that he expected sci-tech libraries to follow in every provincial centre (RFA Korean service, 31 March 2018; own translation).
- Japan's National Diet Library summarised the same reporting in April 2018: the library was confirmed as built by November 2016, the first provincial-level sci-tech library in a regional city after the Pyongyang complex opened (Current Awareness Portal, 3 April 2018).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA said on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for science and technology at 105.3 percent of last year's and for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the library.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two news reports, not art press, discuss how the library was built and announced.
1. NK News: Colin Zwirko, "New North Korean Sci-Tech Library officially opens after long delay" (2018)
URL: https://www.nknews.org/2018/12/new-north-korean-sci-tech-library-officially-opens-after-long-delay/
- Reports that state media announced the opening ceremony more than two years after the project first appeared nearly complete.
- Notes that the facade shown in the state reports is nearly identical to one aired earlier on state television.
- Paywalled; only excerpts were read.
2. RFA Korean service: "북 함경남도에 첫 '과학기술 도서관' 설립" (North Korea builds first "sci-tech library" in South Hamgyong; own translation) (2018)
URL: https://www.rfa.org/korean/in_focus/ne-jn-03312018140259.html
- Melvin expects the libraries to be used to spread knowledge of North Korean science while promoting the greatness of the Kim government (own translation).
- Criticises the state's use of the building, not its programme.

BOTTOM LINE
State money for a building the state presents as the leader's work. The sanctioned government is the only funding risk; worth watching is whether the copy-built provincial libraries actually open to readers.
