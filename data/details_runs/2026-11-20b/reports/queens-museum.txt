QUEENS MUSEUM: New York, USA
ID: queens-museum
Website: https://queensmuseum.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum and education centre in the New York City Building in Flushing Meadows Corona Park, Queens; the City of New York owns the building (museum website).
- The building was designed by Aymar Embury II as the New York City Pavilion for the 1939 World's Fair and housed the United Nations General Assembly from 1946 to 1951 (Wikipedia). The 1947 UN vote to partition Palestine was cast there (QNS).
- The museum opened on 12 November 1972 as the Queens County Art and Cultural Center and took the name Queens Museum in late 1973 (Wikipedia).
- After a $69 million expansion that reopened in November 2013 it covers 105,000 square feet; it holds the Panorama of the City of New York, built for the 1964 World's Fair, and about 10,000 objects from the 1939 and 1964 fairs (Wikipedia).
- Debra Wimpfheimer, at the museum since 2002 and most recently deputy director, was named executive director on 11 February 2026, succeeding Sally Tallant, who left to lead the Hayward Gallery in London (The Art Newspaper).
- Next: the 15,000 square foot Suna Children's Museum, due in 2028, with a World's Fair study centre and archive (The Art Newspaper).
- Open Wednesday to Friday 12 pm to 5 pm, Saturday and Sunday 11 am to 5 pm (museum website).

FUNDING
Queens Museum: mainly public: New York City and State, with trustees, members and friends
- The museum is supported in part by public funds from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs; the New York State Council on the Arts, the board of trustees, members and friends add to it (museum website).
- Form 990 for the year to June 2025: revenue $7,793,344, of which contributions and grants $7,449,736 (95.6 percent), program services $234,137 and rent $149,527; expenses $6,377,998; net assets $35,794,454 (ProPublica).
- Capital money: $10.6 million from Queens Borough President Helen Marshall in 2006; $26.4 million from the city in September 2021 for the second phase of expansion; $8.5 million from the city in August 2023 and $8.5 million from the state in July 2025 for the children's museum (Wikipedia).
- The children's wing is named for trustee Alan Suna (Wikipedia), who sits on the board chaired by Paula Kirby (ProPublica).

Red flags: 2023 staff dispute over a statement on Gaza
- Tallant told workers in November 2023 that the museum "is not able to make political statements" (Hyperallergic, 17 November 2023).
- Staff who worked on a pro-Palestinian statement reported "heightened surveillance" and "intimidation" (Wikipedia, citing Hyperallergic).

Red flags: 2023 artists' complaints about the residency programme
- In May 2023 a group of cultural workers published artists' complaints about the museum's residency programme and one participant's account of harassment (Hyperallergic, 8 May 2023).
- Wikipedia, citing Hyperallergic, says artists accused Tallant of failing to act on a harassment complaint.

Red flags: 2023 reuse of a Xaviera Simmons structure without permission
- A walled display structure that Simmons conceived for her solo show at the museum was painted pink and used in another exhibition; she said this was done without her permission (Hyperallergic, Elaine Velie, 20 September 2023).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In August 2017 the museum cancelled, then reinstated on the day the cancellation was reported, an event marking the 1947 UN partition vote organised by Israel's UN ambassador Danny Danon, after politicians including Congresswoman Grace Meng denounced the cancellation; StandWithUs co-sponsored the event (QNS).
- Director Laura Raicovich, who edited a book on the Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions movement, came under scrutiny (QNS); the board investigated her and her deputy, she resigned in January 2018 and the deputy was fired (Wikipedia).
- These are the older disputes over the building's Israel and Palestine history.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money: a city-owned building and city and state funds. The weak points are the 2023 disputes with staff and artists; worth watching is how the new director handles them while the children's wing is built.
