PIKENE PÅ BROEN (BARENTS SPEKTAKEL, TERMINAL B KUNSTHALL): Kirkenes, Norway
ID: pikene-pa-broen-kirkenes
Website: https://www.pikene.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A collective of curators and producers in Kirkenes, 15 km from the Russian border and 50 km from the Finnish border (Pikene på Broen).
- Bente Synnøve Andersen, Svea Andersen, Anne Mette Bjørgan, Inger Blix Kvammen and Solvor Vefsnmo set it up as an independent group in 1996; it became a limited company in 2001 (Pikene på Broen), registered on 3 April 2001 (Brønnøysund Register).
- It runs the festival Barents Spektakel, first held in 2004, the residency programme BAR International, and Terminal B, its project space, gallery, art shop and office on the pedestrian street in central Kirkenes since 2017 (Pikene på Broen).
- Barents Spektakel 2023, themed "Trust", had 88 events and 9,488 visitors (annual report 2023).
- Artistic director Astrid Fadnes and administrative director Franziska Kraiczy; the board for 2026 to 2028 is chaired by Amy Brox Webber (Pikene på Broen).
- In August 2026 Russia's Ministry of Justice added Pikene på Broen to its register of "undesirable" organisations; Kraiczy said this creates a "major problem" because it works with artists based in Russia (The Barents Observer, 29 August 2026).
- Visiting: Terminal B opens Monday to Friday 10 to 16 and Saturday 10 to 15 (Pikene på Broen).

FUNDING
Pikene på Broen: mainly public: the Ministry of Culture, the three northern counties and Sør-Varanger Municipality, plus yearly project grants
- In 2023 five current and former employees each owned 20 percent of the company, which pays no dividends and reinvests any profit (annual report 2023).
- It receives annual operating grants from the Ministry of Culture and Equality, Finnmark, Troms and Nordland counties and Sør-Varanger Municipality, and applies for one-year project grants from public and private funders, plus sponsor contributions (annual report 2023).
- Self-generated income was NOK 1.56M in 2023, 11 percent of its income (annual report 2023).
- In 2019 fixed operating money was 34 percent of income and about 70 percent came from project grants; Finnmark County gave four-year support to Barents Spektakel, a three-year Foreign Ministry agreement funded the festival and BAR International, the Barents Secretariat backed its Barents work, and SpareBank 1 Nord-Norge and Fritt Ord gave project support; a long-standing main sponsor of the festival pulled out that year (annual report 2019).
- Terminal B was set up with gaming funds and the Cultural Fund's Rom for kunst scheme; its 2019 programme was funded through Cultural Fund schemes and SpareBank 1 Nord-Norge's Samfunnsløftet (annual report 2019).
- Accounts for 2025: operating income NOK 14.54M, of which NOK 875,053 sales, and a profit of NOK 77,791 (Regnskapsregisteret).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money, mostly short-term project grants, with no flagged funder in the sources read.
