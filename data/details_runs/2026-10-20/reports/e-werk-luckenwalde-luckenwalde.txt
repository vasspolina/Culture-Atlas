E-WERK LUCKENWALDE: Luckenwalde, Germany
ID: e-werk-luckenwalde-luckenwalde
Website: https://www.kunststrom.com (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in a former brown coal power station at Rudolf-Breitscheid-Strasse 73, Luckenwalde, Brandenburg, about 50 km (40 minutes by train) south of Berlin (UBS Art; Reiseland Brandenburg).
- Artist Pablo Wendel founded the non-profit Performance Electrics in 2012; he and curator Helen Turner found the derelict station in 2017, and Performance Electrics took it over that year. The centre opened with Power Night on 14 September 2019 (UBS Art; Studio International; The Art Newspaper).
- Earlier use: a coal power plant built in 1913 that supplied Luckenwalde for over 60 years and stopped around 1989 (Reiseland Brandenburg).
- Building: a four-storey coal-era plant with low-cost artist studios, now generating "Kunststrom" (art electricity) from wood chips; the Art Newspaper (2024) says its generators run on local waste wood (UBS Art; The Art Newspaper).
- On site: exhibition galleries in the turbine hall, studios and a residency programme; the current show is "Electricity" by Asad Raza, running from 12 September 2026 to 25 April 2027 (e-flux; kunststrom.com).
- Visiting: open Saturday and Sunday 11am to 6pm, free admission; guided tours can be booked online (Reiseland Brandenburg; kunststrom.com). No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
E-WERK Luckenwalde: mixed public and self-earned: German federal and state culture money plus electricity sales that fund the art programme
- Public money for the "Electricity" exhibition: Kulturstiftung des Bundes, the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media, the Brandenburg Ministry of Science, Research and Culture, Teltow-Fläming district and a local savings bank (e-flux announcement, 2026). Amounts are not stated.
- Start-up: about €300,000 in state funding to convert the building and launch the programme (José da Silva, The Art Newspaper, 22 August 2019).
- Own income: clients across Germany buy Kunststrom, and that revenue partly finances the programme; the operator Performance Electrics gGmbH says it invests 100% of its profit in culture and art-electricity technology (Reiseland Brandenburg); e-flux says the Kunststrom supplier part-finances the art programme.
- Biomass firm Spanner Re2 gives additional support (The Art Newspaper, 2019).
- UBS partnered in 2022 for the exhibition "POWER NIGHTS: Being Mothers"; the page does not call UBS a sponsor or give an amount (UBS Art).
- Ownership and governance: Performance Electrics owns the power station (The Art Newspaper, 2019). The legal notice names Pablo Wendel as managing director and gives no register number. No reporting found of an annual report, accounts or board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage is admiring: Studio International (Anna McNay, "Power Night: the Launch of E-Werk Luckenwalde", 8 October 2019) calls the launch night "a huge success", and The Art Newspaper (Louisa Buck, 9 April 2024) reports the centre's "less is more" argument without objections. Flash Art (Philomena Epps, 15 November 2019) questions neither the funding nor the biomass process.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind E-WERK is clean: federal, state and district culture funding, a local savings bank, a biomass supplier, and its own electricity sales, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are the thin public detail on accounts and amounts and the dependence on one managing director, Wendel. Worth watching are the UBS partnership terms and whether the public funders named for the 2026 exhibition continue.
