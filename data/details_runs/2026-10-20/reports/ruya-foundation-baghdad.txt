RUYA FOUNDATION: Baghdad, Iraq
ID: ruya-foundation-baghdad
Website: https://ruyafoundation.org (checked 2026-10-24; working, but its About page carries a 2018 copyright notice)

OVERVIEW
- Iraqi non-profit, non-governmental organisation that supports culture in Iraq and commissions Iraq's national pavilion at the Venice Biennale; registered with the Iraq Commission for Civil Society Enterprises (stated by the foundation).
- Founded in 2012; Tamara Chalabi is Chair and co-founder and the only founder the site names. No office address is listed.
- Chalabi is the daughter of Ahmad Chalabi, a figure in the 2003 invasion who later became deputy prime minister (Gulf News, from the Guardian).
- Commissioned the Iraq pavilion in 2013 ("Welcome to Iraq"), 2015 ("Invisible Beauty"), 2017 ("Archaic") and 2019 (Serwan Baran's "Fatherland").
- Other work: a drama therapy project in Baghdad from March 2015, film co-productions, the Ruya Notebooks publishing initiative, a monograph on photographer Latif Al Ani and an online database of Iraqi artists, set up in 2016 (stated by the foundation).
- Visiting: no public venue or opening hours are listed on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Ruya Foundation: donor-funded: named supporters and sponsors, no amounts published
- The foundation says "As a non-profit, we rely on donations."
- The About page names H.E. Mr. Nechirvan Barzani and Mr. Khalid Khoshnaw as supporters, with sponsor logos including Prince Claus Fund, Rabee Securities, KAR, Hiwa, Miran Group, Rhein-Co., Mass and Halabja Group, and supporting organisations including a Ministry of Culture, DLA Piper, Mediawan, Ikon Gallery and Nuova Icona. The page gives no dates, amounts or purposes.
- The Prince Claus Fund supported the Baghdad drama therapy project from March 2015.
- No reporting found of the funders of the Venice pavilions: The Art Newspaper (2016), Iraq Business News (2015) and the foundation's own 2019 announcement name none.
- No annual report, accounts or board list is posted.

Red flags: KAR on the sponsor list, fossil fuels
- The sponsor logo labelled KAR sits on the About page. The page does not say "KAR Group"; Erbil-based KAR Group operates the Khurmala Dome oil field and, per Iraq Business News (2018), operated some Kirkuk oilfields after 2014 until Iraq's parliament banned it from them.
- Iraq Oil Report (2012) states Baghdad contracted KAR in 2004 for equipment at Khurmala Dome.
- No source opened gives the date or amount of any KAR payment to Ruya, or says KAR is a current sponsor.

Not related: no similarly named entity found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the foundation's funding; the one review found criticises the 2015 pavilion's curation.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: unsigned review, "National Pavilion of Iraq at the 56th Venice Biennale" (2015)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/shows/national-pavilion-of-iraq-at-the-56th-venice-biennale
- The focus on ISIS narrows the range of Iraqi art, and the lead curator was new to Iraqi culture and history.
- Iraqi diaspora curators and scholars were not used, and Ai Weiwei's role was unclear; artist Wafaa Bilal felt the show did not reflect the quality and complexity of Iraqi work.
- Calls Ruya's approach a "blockbuster" curator-artist duo; does not discuss funders.

BOTTOM LINE
Ruya's money cannot be traced: it says it relies on donations, shows undated logos and publishes no accounts or board. The weak point is the KAR logo, since the oil firm behind it operates a disputed field; no payment is documented. Watch whether the foundation posts dated funder information.
