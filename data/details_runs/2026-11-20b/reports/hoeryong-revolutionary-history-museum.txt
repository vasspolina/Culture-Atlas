HOERYONG REVOLUTIONARY HISTORY MUSEUM: Hoeryong, North Korea
ID: hoeryong-revolutionary-history-museum
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Two-storey white concrete museum in well-kept grounds in the centre of Hoeryong, a small city in North Hamgyong Province (Koryo Tours).
- It centres on the revolutionary activities of Kim Jong Suk, whom Koryo Tours calls the most famous daughter of the city: wife of Kim Il Sung and mother of Kim Jong Il.
- Korean Wikipedia's page on Hoeryong lists a Kim Jong Suk revolutionary museum (김정숙혁명사적관) that opened in October 1974 and shows material on her (my translation).
- Koryo Tours says the entrance hall holds a large painting of her holding her infant son on a horse beside her husband and other guerrillas, and the halls that follow show relics, tools and possessions of the guerrillas who operated around Hoeryong.
- Revolutionary history museums usually stand next to statues of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il; Hoeryong has a statue of Kim Jong Suk at its centre instead, which Koryo Tours calls unique in North Korea. The museum is a short walk from that statue and, over a hill, from her Native House.
- Koryo Tours says photos are allowed inside, unlike many other revolutionary history museums, that guides welcome questions, and that the building suffers from the power shortages common in this part of the country.

FUNDING
Hoeryong Revolutionary History Museum: state-run: a museum of the North Korean state
- Koryo Tours says many North Korean cities have revolutionary history museums with the same core narrative and a local story, which in Hoeryong is Kim Jong Suk.
- Staff: South Korea's Ministry of Unification, in its 2022 directory of North Korean institutions, lists Ri Sun Sil (리순실) as director of both the Comrade Kim Jong Suk Revolutionary Museum (김정숙동지혁명사적관) and the Hoeryong Revolutionary Museum (회령혁명사적관), with Jong Sin Hui (정신희) as a section chief at the latter (my translations and romanisations).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the museum.

Not related: the Native House of Kim Jong Suk, a separate memorial site in the same city (Koryo Tours).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state museum paid for by the North Korean state. Its weak point is the US blocking order on that state.
