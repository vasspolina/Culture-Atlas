CONTEMPORARY ART MUSEUM OF THE KASBAH: Tangier, Morocco
ID: contemporary-art-museum-of-the-kasbah
Website: not checked: no site of its own was found; its operator is the Fondation nationale des musées (FNM) and facts below come from press reports

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the former Kasbah prison, part of the Kasbah Palace in the medina of Tangier (Hespress).
- The prison dates from the 17th century and was a civil prison until the early 1970s (Hespress, 24 December 2021). The Journal des Arts says it was built under Sultan Moulay Ismaïl (1645 to 1727).
- Opened on 24 December 2021 (Hespress; the Journal des Arts says 23 December) to mark the tenth anniversary of the FNM. Omar Moro, regional council president, attended the opening (Hespress). FNM president Mehdi Qotbi said he was glad that a place that embodied despair, a prison, had become a place of hope (Journal des Arts, in French; my translation).
- Building: over 1,225 square metres, with lifts and ramps for accessibility (Hespress). No architect is named in the sources read.
- Opening exhibition: "The School of the North", over 70 works by artists of the Tetouan and Tangier schools, including Mohamed Serghini, Mohamed Ataallah and Saad Ben Cheffaj (Journal des Arts) and Mohamed Melehi and Mohamed Yacoubi (Hespress), drawn from the FNM collection and loans.
- Visiting: no reporting found of opening hours or admission for the contemporary art space.

FUNDING
Contemporary Art Museum of the Kasbah: public: Moroccan state bodies through the Fondation nationale des musées
- Restoration and scenography cost about 13 million dirhams, 1.2 million euros (Journal des Arts, 3 January 2022); fnh.ma gives the same 13 million dirham budget.
- The space was rehabilitated within a project to upgrade Tangier's medina, supervised by the Wilaya of Tangier, with the Ministry of Culture, the Agency for the Promotion and Development of the North (APDN) and the FNM contributing. Hespress (24 December 2021) says the Ministry of Culture, APDN and the FNM supported the work. fnh.ma names only the FNM and the 13 million dirham budget, and says the show draws on the FNM collection and loans, including from the Académie royale du Maroc or the Ministry of Culture.
- The FNM was established in 2011 under Law 01-09 and Dahir 1-10-21 as a non-profit with legal personality and financial autonomy that administers state-owned museums (Wikipedia). Its president is Mehdi Qotbi.
- No reporting found of private donors, a board, annual accounts or the museum's own running budget.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The Journal des Arts piece reports the opening positively and is paywalled; only the visible excerpt was read.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Moroccan state money, channelled through the Tangier Wilaya, the Ministry of Culture, APDN and the FNM; no private or foreign funder was found. The weak points are that the sources disagree on who paid what, and that neither the FNM's accounts nor the museum's running costs are published in anything read. Watch FNM budget disclosures.
