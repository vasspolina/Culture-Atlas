MUSEUM TINGUELY: Basel, Switzerland
ID: museum-tinguely
Website: https://www.tinguely.ch

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Paul Sacher-Anlage 1, Basel, on the banks of the Rhine, holding the world's largest collection of works by the Swiss artist Jean Tinguely (1925 to 1991) (museum website).
- Opened in 1996 as a gift from the pharmaceutical company Roche to the people of Basel, marking Roche's centenary. Niki de Saint Phalle gave over 50 machine sculptures and drawings and documents to a specially established Roche Centenary Foundation (museum website).
- Named for Jean Tinguely.
- Building: designed by the Ticino architect Mario Botta and surrounded by Solitude Park, whose upkeep Roche pays for (museum website).
- On site: a permanent display covering four decades of Tinguely's work, from 218 sculptures of which more than half are shown, an open restoration workshop on the second floor, an archive of about 1,500 works on paper, a library and documentation archive, a bistro and a shop. Temporary exhibitions of other artists and a programme of concerts, family Sundays and workshops run alongside; more than 120 exhibitions have been held since 1996 (museum website).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, until 21:00 on Thursday, closed Monday. Admission is 18 CHF, reduced 12 CHF. The site has an inclusion section for hearing, seeing, walking and learning needs (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum Tinguely: corporate-funded: Roche pays all operating costs
- The museum's operating costs and the upkeep of Solitude Park have been "funded entirely and exclusively by Roche since 1996" (museum website).
- Roche built the museum, contributed works from its own holdings and provided funding for additions to the collection (museum website).
- The museum names Roche Commissions and Roche'n'Jazz as other major Roche-sponsored cultural projects.
- Roche was founded in Basel on 1 October 1896 by Fritz Hoffmann-La Roche; descendants of the Hoffmann and Oeri families own slightly over half of the bearer shares with voting rights (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Roche price-fixing plea
- On 20 May 1999 the US Department of Justice announced that F. Hoffmann-La Roche agreed to plead guilty to a one-count criminal case of conspiring to fix and raise prices, and to divide sales volumes, for certain vitamins, and to pay a $500 million fine, which the release calls the highest criminal fine ever. The case was in the US District Court in Dallas and covered January 1990 to February 1999 (US Department of Justice). Wikipedia reports the same plea and $500 million in US criminal fines.
- Connection: Roche is the museum's sole funder; the museum's Roche page says Roche has funded its operating costs since 1996.

BOTTOM LINE
Roche, a pharmaceutical company, built the museum, opened it in 1996 as a gift to Basel and pays all of its operating costs. The documented weak point is the funder itself: Roche's 1999 guilty plea and $500 million US fine for the vitamin price-fixing conspiracy.
