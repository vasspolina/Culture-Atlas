FUNDAÇÃO SINDIKA DOKOLO: Luanda, Angola
ID: fundação-sindika-dokolo-luanda
Website: http://www.sindikadokolofondation.org (checked 2026-11-20; serves spam: it redirects through other domains to an unrelated site, 1-win.net.br)

OVERVIEW
- Luanda foundation that looked after Sindika Dokolo's contemporary African art collection and ran the Luanda Triennial (Wikipedia).
- Dokolo started the collection in 2004 with the Angolan artist and curator Fernando Alvim and formed the foundation in 2006 (The Art Newspaper, 2017); Ver Angola (2016) and Observador (2018) give 2003. Alvim was its vice-president (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder, a Congolese collector married to Isabel dos Santos, daughter of former president José Eduardo dos Santos. He died in Dubai on 29 October 2020, aged 48 (family statement in The Art Newspaper); Dubai Police said he died free-diving and found no criminal suspicion (Al Jazeera).
- The collection, about 3,000 works that The Art Newspaper (2020) called the couple's, administered by the foundation, grew from that of the German businessman Hans Bogatzke, bought from his widow after his death in 2005 (Observador, 2020; Dokolo to ArtDependence), and includes works by William Kentridge, Zanele Muholi and Edson Chagas.
- Programmes included "Check List", the African section of the 2007 Venice Biennale, drawn from the collection (ARTE.it) and the 2015 Porto show "You Love Me, You Love Me Not" (Ver Angola).

FUNDING
Fundação Sindika Dokolo: founder-funded: the private fortune of Sindika Dokolo and Isabel dos Santos
- Dokolo bought the Bogatzke collection for 330,000 euros, in a sale Alvim brokered with the widow (Observador, 2020).
- Restitution: the foundation paid, at undisclosed prices, for two Chokwe masks and a statue missing from the Dundo Museum in December 2015 and for most of the objects returned there in June 2018; Dokolo offered collectors what they had paid and no more (The Art Newspaper, 2015; Observador, 2018).
- It paid towards 16 artists' participation in Documenta 14 and drove a planned 2018 Luanda show with them (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- European base: in January 2016 the company Supreme Treasure bought the Casa Manoel de Oliveira in Porto at public auction for 1.58 million euros as the foundation's European headquarters (Ver Angola).

Red flags: 2019 and 2020 asset freezes in Angola and Portugal
- In December 2019 the Provincial Court of Luanda froze more than $1 billion of the couple's assets to recover state loans (The Art Newspaper, ICIJ).
- On 13 March 2020 the Portuguese judge Carlos Alexandre ordered the seizure of all of dos Santos's assets in Portugal (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: 2020 Luanda court ruling on the de Grisogono diamond deal
- In July 2020 the Provincial Court of Luanda called the state diamond company Sodiam's investment in the Swiss jeweller de Grisogono "fraudulent" and the "opportunity" for Dokolo and dos Santos to control it; Sodiam says it lost its entire $120 million (ICIJ).
- Dokolo's lawyers said he invested $115 million and later much more; dos Santos's lawyers said she had no interest in the jeweller (ICIJ).

Red flags: 2021 US State Department designation of Isabel dos Santos for corruption
- In December 2021 the US State Department designated dos Santos for "significant corruption by misappropriating public funds for her personal benefit", barring her and her immediate family from entering the United States (ICIJ).

Red flags: 2023 London freezing order against Isabel dos Santos
- On 20 December 2023 London's High Court granted the Angolan telecoms company Unitel a worldwide freezing order on up to £580 million of her assets; Unitel accuses her of procuring over £365 million in loans in 2012 and 2013 for her company UIH and her "own personal benefit", which she denies (ICIJ).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in the art press; the one sustained attack is a polemical 2009 opinion essay.
1. esferapública: Carlos Salazar, "Alfredo Jaar, Muxima y los Dokolo de Angola, Resucitados" (2009)
URL: https://esferapublica.org/alfredo-jaar-muxima-y-los-dokolo-de-angola-resucitados/
- Argues that the family's money contradicts the collection's claim to dignify Africans: it says the Banco de Kinshasa, founded by Dokolo's father, defrauded the Angolan state and small investors of over $80 million before collapsing in 1986, and links dos Santos's diamond business to abuses in the Lundas.
- Faults Alfredo Jaar for never speaking out against Dokolo after showing "Muxima" there in 2007, and quotes Barthélémy Toguo, who declined to take part: "Under no circumstances whatever should my name be associated with that of Sindika Dokolo".
- An opinion piece (March 2009) resting on press and NGO sources it links.
2. The Art Newspaper: Cristina Ruiz, "Africa's top collector returns ancestral works to Angola" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/12/22/africas-top-collector-returns-ancestral-works-to-angola
- Not criticism: reports the restitution purchases and his marriage to the president's daughter.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The ICIJ-led Luanda Leaks investigation of more than 715,000 documents reported that dos Santos built her $2.2bn fortune by exploiting Angola; she denies that it came from nepotism and corruption (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- Her father made her head of the state oil company Sonangol in 2016; President João Lourenço fired her the following year (The Art Newspaper). The foundation ran on the couple's fortune.

BOTTOM LINE
Not clean: the foundation's money came from a fortune that courts in Angola, Portugal and Britain have frozen and that the US government has designated for corruption.
