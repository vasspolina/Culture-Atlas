Red flags: 2024 return of five paintings to the Matthiesen gallery heirs
- In October 2024 the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz agreed to return five paintings from the Gemäldegalerie to the descendants of Franz Zatzenstein-Matthiesen, owner of the Berlin Matthiesen Gallery; a sixth work, "The Temptation of Saint Anthony" from the circle of Jan (Wellens) de Cock, stays in the Gemäldegalerie (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, 22 October 2024).
- The five works: a portrait of a man in armour by a follower of Anthony van Dyck, two Dutch ship paintings once attributed to Jan van de Capelle, a replica of Giovanni Battista Tiepolo's "Christ Carrying the Cross" and a portrait once attributed to Ambrosius Benson (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin).
- Zatzenstein-Matthiesen fled the Gestapo to Switzerland in 1933; in 1934 he gave works from his stock to Dresdner Bank to repay loans, and in 1935 they passed to the state museums in the bank's sale of about 4,400 works to Prussia (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin).
- The agreement takes account of the damage from Nazi persecution and of the fact that some debts predate 1933; the descendants thanked the Stiftung "for the responsible manner in which they have handled this particular situation" (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin).
