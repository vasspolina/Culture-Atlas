MADISON MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Madison, USA
ID: madison-museum-of-contemporary-art-madison
Website: https://www.mmoca.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 227 State Street in downtown Madison, Wisconsin, next to the Overture Center for the Arts.
- Founded in 1901 as the Madison Art Association; it became the Madison Art Center in 1964, moved into the Madison Civic Center in 1980 and took its present name in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Opened in its current building on 23 April 2006, after 105 years in borrowed and refurbished spaces. César Pelli designed it: 51,500 square feet inside plus a 7,100-square-foot rooftop sculpture garden. W. Jerome Frautschi made it possible (museum website).
- On site: galleries, a 230-seat lecture hall and a new-media gallery; the collection holds over 6,000 art objects (museum website).
- Open Wednesday, Friday and Sunday 12 to 6 pm, Thursday 12 to 9 pm, Saturday 10 am to 6 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday. Admission to exhibitions is free. Automatic door opener at the main entrance, one elevator across three floors, a free manual wheelchair; no on-site parking.

FUNDING
Madison Museum of Contemporary Art: mainly private giving: individuals, foundations, members and event sponsors, with free admission
- The museum states that it relies on individuals, corporations, private event rentals, foundations, memberships, fundraisers such as Art Fair on the Square, and private gifts (Wikipedia, summarising the museum).
- Form 990 data (ProPublica): in the year to April 2025 revenue was $5,254,356, of which $4,953,866 was contributions; expenses were $3,767,099 and net assets $10,021,335. In the years to April 2022, 2023 and 2024 revenue was $2.30 million, $1.96 million and $2.02 million, and expenses exceeded revenue each year.
- Art Fair on the Square sponsors, listed on the museum's page headed 2026 (its thank-you note refers to the 2025 fair): Lake Ridge Bank (Major), Wisconsin Lottery (Gold), Ho Chunk Gaming Madison and Wildwood Pro Acquisitions (Silver) and DoubleTree by Hilton (Official Hotel). The museum says sponsors help it offer free admission to nearly 100,000 visitors a year.
- No reporting found of a published list of the museum's major donors.

Red flags: casino sponsor
- Ho Chunk Gaming Madison, a casino owned and operated by the Ho-Chunk Nation on trust land inside Madison city limits (GGB magazine, 2019), is a Silver sponsor of Art Fair on the Square, a museum fundraiser (museum sponsorship page).
- No reporting found of an objection to the sponsor.

Red flags: 2022 Wisconsin Triennial open letter
- In August 2022, artists in the Triennial "Ain't I A Woman?", curated by Fatima Laster and showing Black women artists, published an open letter alleging "shameful mistreatment" of Black artists, contractors and staff and demanding the resignation of director Christina Brungardt (ArtReview).
- At least 11 of 23 artists withdrew by 23 August (Hyperallergic); the Daily Cardinal reported at least 16 before the show closed.
- The triggers were damage to Lilada Gee's unfinished installation on 24 June, and a verbal attack on Gee in March by a white Overture Center employee, as Gee describes it. The Overture Center shares the building (Hyperallergic).
- The museum's executive committee denied institutional racism (Wikipedia). In a September 2022 statement it apologised for the damage to Gee's work and announced a "truth and reconciliation" project led by three board members (Daily Cardinal, October 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One episode draws criticism in art media, the 2022 Triennial row; the pieces below report the artists' allegations. No criticism of the museum's funders found in art media.
1. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Liu, "Citing 'Institutional Racist Violence,' Half of the Wisconsin Triennial Artists Withdraw Their Work" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/755393/citing-institutional-racist-violence-half-of-the-wisconsin-triennial-artists-withdraw-their-work/
- At least 11 of 23 artists withdrew after what Gee describes as a March verbal attack at the shared building, slow leadership responses, and the June damage to Gee's work.
- Artists seek an apology, restitution, the director's removal and an independent audit.
- Names Brungardt, Gee, Laster and the Overture Center. The museum did not respond to repeated requests for comment.
2. ArtReview: unsigned, "Wisconsin Triennial artists pull work from show dedicated to Black women artists; cite 'shameful mistreatment'" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/wisconsin-triennial-artists-pull-work-from-show-dedicated-to-black-women-artists-cite-shameful-mistreatment
- Reports that 11 of 23 artists withdrew and relays the open letter calling for Brungardt to resign, and its claim that artists and the curator were underpaid.
- Names Brungardt, Gee and Laster. Reports no response from the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is mostly private giving, and its revenue was about $5 million in FY2025. The weak points are the casino sponsor of its main fundraiser and the 2022 Triennial dispute over its treatment of Black artists. Worth watching: the sponsor list and whether the museum publishes the findings of its reconciliation project.
