CENTER OF CONTEMPORARY ART TBILISI (TBILISI TRIENNIAL): Tbilisi, Georgia
ID: center-of-contemporary-art-tbilisi-tbilisi-triennial-tbilisi
Website: https://cca.ge

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit contemporary art centre in Tbilisi, founded by the artist and art educator Wato Tsereteli; it has existed since 1 October 2010 (Arterritory, 2013).
- Tsereteli describes it as an institution that "occupies abandoned places in the city" (On Curating questionnaire). In 2013 it paid no rent and was preparing to move, and some students documented the centre's events instead of paying tuition (Arterritory).
- Organises the Tbilisi Triennial, which covers visual art, art education and related research (Biennial Foundation). The 1st edition, "Offside Effect", was in 2012, curated by Henk Slager and Tsereteli.
- Runs a free public library of about 2,000 books on contemporary art, art history and art theory, and in December 2023 opened an educational space, The Studio Initiative (Danish Cultural Institute).

FUNDING
Center of Contemporary Art Tbilisi: grant-funded: foreign government agencies and organisations, with occasional Georgian state project support
- Arterritory (Laura Ķeniņš, 28 May 2013): the centre receives support from the Swiss Embassy and international financing, and the German organisation DVV International funded free places for ten students from socially disadvantaged families; the Georgian government sometimes supports specific projects. Tsereteli described "constructive relationships" with the Ministry of Culture and the Culture Department of Tbilisi city hall (On Curating questionnaire).
- Artribune (Roberto Ruta and Lisa Chiari, "Georgia: Do It by Yourself", 1 July 2014): Tsereteli's team collected money from artists and international government agencies, including the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation, to refurbish the spaces and open the centre.
- Danish Cultural Institute: the New Democracy Fund supported the opening of The Studio Initiative in December 2023.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Georgia's Law on Transparency of Foreign Influence (2024) requires organisations receiving over 20 percent of their funding from abroad to register as pursuing the interests of a foreign power; the Foreign Agents Registration Act (2025) adds criminal penalties of up to five years; and the Grants Law requires government approval before a foreign donor makes a grant to a Georgian recipient, with up to six years in prison for unauthorised funding after its 4 March 2026 amendment (Freemuse, Musa Igrek, 25 March 2026).
- Freemuse reports that Georgian contemporary art grew from foreign agencies, diplomatic missions, local civil society organisations and residencies rather than state patronage, and that most independent cultural organisations have shut down or suspended operations. The article describes the sector as a whole and does not name the centre.
- Connection: the Swiss agency, DVV International and the Danish New Democracy Fund are foreign funders of the centre, the kind of funding these laws regulate.

BOTTOM LINE
The money documented behind the Center of Contemporary Art Tbilisi comes from foreign sources: the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation and the Swiss Embassy, DVV International and the Danish New Democracy Fund, plus occasional Georgian state project support in 2013.
