MUZEUM SZTUKI NOWOCZESNEJ W WARSZAWIE (MSN): Warsaw, Poland
ID: msn-warsaw
Website: https://artmuseum.pl

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art on the north side of Plac Defilad in central Warsaw, next to the Palace of Culture and Science (The Art Newspaper, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 2005; Joanna Mytkowska has been director since 6 June 2007 (Wikipedia). It worked for nearly two decades from temporary homes, including offices at ul. Pańska 3 and the Museum on the Vistula pavilion designed by Adolf Krischanitz (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by New York firm Thomas Phifer and Partners, chosen in a 2014 competition, after an earlier design by Christian Kerez collapsed in 2012 (The Art Newspaper). It opened on 25 October 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Materials: poured-in-place white concrete inside and out, with regional ash wood cladding in the "city rooms" (Architectural Record, 2 December 2024). The square was built by the Soviets among the ruins of the Second World War (Architectural Record).
- On site: galleries, a cinema (Kinomuzeum), an auditorium, a publishing house and a shop. The museum collects Polish art of the 20th and 21st centuries in an international context (Wikipedia, museum website).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, closed Monday. Standard exhibition ticket 40 zł, reduced 30 zł, and 1 zł tickets are offered. Gallery A on the ground floor is free thanks to a partnership with Audi. The museum has an accessibility declaration and Polish Sign Language information (museum website).

FUNDING
Muzeum Sztuki Nowoczesnej w Warszawie: mainly public: the City of Warsaw
- Wikipedia states the museum was run jointly by the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage and the City of Warsaw, and has been a city cultural institution since 2023. The Art Newspaper (27 September 2024) places the end of joint oversight in 2022 and says talks on restoring it were under way.
- Building: the city pays for the whole project. In 2009 the then mayor Hanna Gronkiewicz-Waltz said the city would fund the entire construction from its own budget, after the Ministry of Culture had withdrawn as intermediary for EU funds (Wprost/PAP, 20 May 2009).
- Cost figures differ by outlet: about 145 million dollars (The Art Newspaper), 175 million dollars (Architectural Record), about 700 million zł against a plan of just over 400 million (RMF24/PAP, 12 February 2025).
- The Supreme Audit Office (NIK) began an audit of the building's financing and construction on 11 February 2025. The museum called it routine and said an earlier audit took place in 2015 (RMF24).
- Audi: the museum's visit page, which lists opening hours from 1 July 2026, states that admission to Gallery A is free thanks to its partnership with Audi.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: the City of Warsaw pays for the building and, since the city took sole oversight, for the institution, with the Ministry of Culture formerly a co-organiser. Documented private support is the Audi partnership for free admission to Gallery A.
