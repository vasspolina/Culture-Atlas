CONTEMPORARY ARTS MUSEUM HOUSTON: Houston, USA
ID: contemporary-arts-museum-houston
Website: https://camh.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting contemporary art museum at 5216 Montrose Boulevard in Houston's Museum District (Wikipedia; CAMH).
- Founded in 1948 by seven Houston citizens; its first professional director, Jermayne MacAgy, was hired in 1957 (Wikipedia).
- Building: trustees bought a site at Montrose and Bissonnet and Gunnar Birkerts designed the building, which opened in 1972 with the exhibition "Ten". It closed on 1 January 1997 for its first major renovation in 25 years and reopened on 10 May 1997 (Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Hesse McGraw was executive director until 31 December 2024; Melissa McDonnell Luján and Ryan N. Dennis became co-directors on 1 January 2025 (ProPublica; Wikipedia).
- Programme: temporary exhibitions and a Teen Council (Hyperallergic). It has no permanent collection.
- Visiting: admission is always free. Open Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11am to 6pm, Thursday 11am to 9pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. Wheelchair accessible; labels in English, Spanish and large print; free street parking.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum as an institution; the one substantial piece concerns its handling of a former director.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Former Houston Museum Director Accused of Sexual Harassment" (16 September 2020, updated 17 September)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/588480/bill-arning-contemporary-arts-museum-houston/
- Seven men, none named for fear of professional retaliation, told Hyperallergic they were victims of Bill Arning, director from 2009 until he stepped down in October 2018. They described unsolicited advances and messages, coercion and, in one case, an encounter at his home that the man considers assault. Hyperallergic could not independently verify the accounts.
- The museum's account of 2018: director Hesse McGraw, who joined in January 2020, told Hyperallergic the board began investigating in August 2018 after claims that Arning solicited artists with promises of exhibition inclusion, learned of a relationship with an artist from a Teen Council-linked exhibition, and gave him the choice of resigning or being fired; he chose to leave. The October 2018 announcement of his departure, from trustee chair Jereann Chaney, called him "a force".
- The museum's 2020 statement said "At that time, no illegal activity was alleged or uncovered" and that it reported the new allegations to the Houston Police Department. McGraw said the 2018 investigation did not uncover the grooming claims posted anonymously.
- Arning called the anonymous allegations "either false or so distorted as to require a great deal of effort to remember the actual stories". He acknowledged a brief romantic relationship with an artist, disputed that the artist was on the Teen Council, and apologised if he had made people feel manipulated or coerced.
- Unverified: the Instagram account @cancelartgalleries, which posts anonymous testimonies of abuse in the art world, alleged that Arning groomed Teen Council members; Hyperallergic did not verify the testimonies.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is foundation, trustee and member giving plus small public arts grants; no funder with a documented controversy was found among those named. The weak points are that no donor list or annual report is published and that the main criticism is the 2020 Hyperallergic report on Arning, to which the museum answered that no illegal activity was alleged or uncovered in 2018. Watch the Teiger and N2 Stone funding and any independent report on the Arning case.
