BAYERISCHE STAATSOPER: Munich, Germany
ID: bayerische-staatsoper
Website: https://www.staatsoper.de (checked 2026-11-20; refuses automated requests with a holding page)

OVERVIEW
- Bavaria's state opera, playing in the Nationaltheater at Max-Joseph-Platz 2 in Munich, with 2,101 seats (German Music Information Centre, miz.org).
- Ensembles and leadership: the Bayerisches Staatsorchester, chorus director Christoph Heil and ballet director Laurent Hilaire; Serge Dorny is Staatsintendant, Patrick Wasserbauer managing director and Vladimir Jurowski general music director, with Petr Popelka named from the 2029/30 season (miz.org).
- About 1,000 people work for the house on and behind the stage (Bavarian building ministry, 2 July 2021).
- Audience: 528,168 visitors in 2024 and 470,109 in 2025, a year with a longer summer break for building work in the Nationaltheater (Bavarian State Ministry for Science and Arts, Landtag printed paper 19/10025, 9 February 2026).
- The Nationaltheater needs a general renovation; the ministry said in December 2025 that no decision had yet been taken on where the company will play during the works from the mid-2030s, and that the planned Munich concert hall is being designed so it could take staged opera as one of several interim venues (Landtag printed paper 19/9423).
- Each summer the free open-air broadcast "Oper für alle" relays a performance to Max-Joseph-Platz; the 30th edition showed "Die Walküre" on 4 July 2026 (BMW Group press release, 3 June 2026).

FUNDING
Bayerische Staatsoper: public: the Free State of Bavaria, with BMW as main corporate partner
- Owner: the Free State of Bavaria is listed as its "Träger" (sponsoring body) under public sponsorship (miz.org).
- Box office: ticket sales brought in 31,552,216 euros in 2024 and 29,667,652 euros in 2025, up from 11,193,864 euros in 2020 (Landtag printed paper 19/10025).
- Donations and sponsorship: 6,678,099 euros in 2024 and 5,444,049 euros in 2025 (Landtag printed paper 19/10025).
- BMW: BMW calls itself the opera's Global Partner and its "größter und bedeutendster Partner" (largest and most important partner, own translation), has been a partner of "Oper für alle" since the first edition in 1997, and pays for free entry to it (BMW Group press release, 3 June 2026, read through a fetching tool).
- Buildings: the Free State paid for an extension of the opera's scenery store in Poing, an automated high-bay warehouse for about 450 containers of sets, to replace rented stores (Bavarian building ministry, 2021 and 2023).

Red flags: 2021 to 2023 cost rise of the Poing scenery store
- At the groundbreaking in July 2021 the Free State put its investment at about 24 million euros, with storage from mid-2023 (Bavarian building ministry, 2 July 2021).
- At the topping-out in June 2023 the total cost was about 35 million euros and handover was planned for spring 2024 (Bavarian building ministry, 19 June 2023).

Red flags: 2022 cancellation and 2026 re-invitation of Anna Netrebko
- The Staatsoper cancelled Anna Netrebko's engagement because of her closeness to Vladimir Putin; she had celebrated her 50th birthday with a gala in the Kremlin Palace the year before (Euronews, 1 March 2022).
- Netrebko wrote on Instagram that she was not a political person but that this was no time to sing, and withdrew from concerts (Euronews, 1 March 2022).
- On 15 March 2026 OperaWire reported that Netrebko returns for a recital on 23 July 2027. Dorny said he and Jurowski "have clearly sided with Ukraine. This position is non-negotiable", that she no longer performs in Russia and has distanced herself from the war, and that "Anna Netrebko will be invited to perform in opera productions in the future" (OperaWire, quoting Dorny's statement and Merkur).

Not related: the Munich Philharmonic, whose chief conductor Valery Gergiev the City of Munich dismissed with immediate effect in March 2022, is a city orchestra and not part of the Bayerische Staatsoper (Euronews, 1 March 2022).

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly clean: Bavarian state money, high box-office income and a car maker as main sponsor. Worth watching are cost control on the State's opera buildings ahead of the Nationaltheater renovation, and how the house handles artists close to the Russian state.
