OVERVIEW
- Exhibition and collateral event of the 61st Venice Biennale, from 9 May to 22 November 2026 at the Church of San Giovanni Evangelista, San Polo 2454, Venice (e-flux).
- Organised by Belarus Free Theatre, founded in Minsk in 2005 and based in London (Belarus Free Theatre website, Brooklyn Rail); curated by Daniella Kaliada with Natalia Kaliada MBE (e-flux).
- A collateral event, not a national pavilion. The UBBC post states that pavilions must be requested by a ministry of culture. The Brooklyn Rail notes that Belarus has not had a national pavilion since 2019.
- Artists: Sergey Grinevich (site-specific paintings in the church), Olga Podgayskaya (organ composition Sounds of Silence), Nicolai Khalezin (a sphere of books banned in Belarus), Vladimir Tsesler (metal sculptures reimagining the straw spider) and the chef Rasmus Munk (a sensory interpretation of a shared offering) (e-flux).
- Also on show: Surveillance Crucifix and Confessional of the System, a wheat installation, a candle scent, The Smell of Dictatorship, made with the Ukrainian studio ol.factory, and audio testimonies of recently released political prisoners voiced by Gillian Anderson, Stephen Fry, Jude Law (a Belarus Free Theatre trustee), Joanna Lumley and Ruth Wilson (e-flux).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; admission is free (project website).

FUNDING
Official. Unofficial. Belarus.: organised and commissioned by the exiled Belarus Free Theatre
- Belarus Free Theatre supports the maintenance of four antique paintings in the church, including Pietro Ricchi's Martirio di Santa Barbara and Antonio Vassilacchi's San Giacomo Apostolo (e-flux).
- A grant from MIT's Social and Ethical Responsibilities in Computing supports the code for Confessional of the System (project website).
- Unverified: more than half of the project is funded anonymously by Belarusian businesses, from UBBC (the Ukraine-Britain Business Council, by its web address), in a post dated 8 May and updated 12 May that names no author and no source for the figure.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review in art media, from the Brooklyn Rail, which raises reservations about who speaks for Belarus in Venice.
1. Brooklyn Rail: Monika Fabijanska, "Ukraine and Belarus in Venice" (June 2026)
URL: https://brooklynrail.org/2026/06/artseen/ukraine-and-belarus-in-venice/
- Calls it the first independent exhibition of Belarusian art at the Venice Biennale, and says the exhibition reflects a rift between exiles who speak for Belarus in the West and "those who stayed, muted for the world".
- Notes "the absence in Venice of the younger generation of artists".
- Contrasts the two countries directly: "Ukrainians have agency; Belarusians almost nothing."

BOTTOM LINE
The exhibition is organised and commissioned by the exiled Belarus Free Theatre, outside the Belarusian state pavilion process. The one claim about the main funding, that more than half of the project is paid for anonymously by Belarusian businesses, comes from UBBC alone.
