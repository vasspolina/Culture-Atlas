MUSÉE NATIONAL DE GUINÉE (SANDERVALIA): Conakry, Guinea
ID: mus-e-national-de-guin-e-sandervalia-conakry

OVERVIEW
- Guinea's national museum, in the Sandervalia quarter of Kaloum, Conakry, near Ignace Deen Hospital (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1960 to hold examples of traditional arts, linked to a state policy promoting traditional folklore (Wikipedia). Guinéenews (Mady Bangoura, 6 April 2023) says a sanitation campaign under Sékou Touré led to the site being designated the national museum.
- Named for the French explorer Aimé Olivier de Sanderval, who settled on the site; his house, built around 1897, and his forge remain, and the house was classified as a historic monument in 1960 (Guinéenews).
- The open-air courtyard holds monuments to figures including Noël Ballay, Karamoko Ibrahima N'Dama, Nèn Sira and Samory Touré (Guinéenews).
- The collection holds antique objects from several regions and colonial-era objects. A 2016 account said the building was nearly empty apart from one room of masks and musical instruments (Wikipedia). A wing was restored with support from the Embassy of Japan (Wikipedia).
- Amirou Conté is Director General (Guinéenews, 18 May 2026). The site of about 8,000 m2 is to be rehabilitated and extended (Ministry of Culture, 30 March 2026); Guinéenews (18 May 2026) says the AFD-backed extension, called "Territoire du Futur", is due by 2028.

FUNDING
Musée National de Guinée: state museum: rebuilding financed by a concessional loan from the French Development Agency (AFD)
- On 30 March 2026 the government signed a financing agreement with the AFD for the rehabilitation, extension and equipping of the museum, based on a sovereign concessional loan of 16 million euros (Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Crafts, "Patrimoine culturel : 16 millions d'euros pour la renaissance du Musée national de Guinée", 30 March 2026).
- The state will cover the project's counterpart funding and ensure the museum's viability; the museum is to become an administrative public establishment (Ministry of Culture).
- According to the AFD, as the ministry reports, this is its first sovereign financing for the cultural sector in Guinea at this scale; the ministry places the project within President Mamadi Doumbouya's vision (stated by the ministry).
- The project includes inventory, preventive conservation and digitisation of works, supported by the French embassy, and the museum is meant to receive Guinean works held abroad (Ministry of Culture).
- Culture Minister Moussa Moïse Sylla cited the 16 million euro agreement on International Museum Day, 18 May 2026; French Ambassador Luc Briard spoke on restitution at the same event (Guinéenews).

BOTTOM LINE
The rebuilding is financed by a 16 million euro sovereign concessional loan from France's development agency, signed on 30 March 2026, with the Guinean State covering the project's counterpart funding.
