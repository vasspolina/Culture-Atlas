NEDERLANDS FOTOMUSEUM: Rotterdam, Netherlands
ID: nederlands-fotomuseum-rotterdam
Website: https://nederlandsfotomuseum.nl/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- National photography museum in Pakhuis Santos, Brede Hilledijk 95, in the Katendrecht district of Rotterdam. It reopened there on 7 February 2026 (Dutch Wikipedia; NL Times).
- Formed in 2003 by merging the Nederlands Foto Instituut, the Nederlands Fotoarchief and the Nationaal Fotorestauratieatelier. The museum was founded from the bequest of Hein Wertheimer, who left 22 million guilders to the Prins Bernhard Cultuurfonds to set up a photography museum (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Building: a coffee warehouse completed in 1901 to 1902 and a national monument since 2000 (museum website); designed by Jacobus Pieter Stok and Jan Kanters. The old site closed on 24 May 2025 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibition spaces, a library, education rooms and a rooftop restaurant (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 17:00; adults €17.50, ages 18 to 25 €10.50, under 18 free. Wheelchair accessible; wheelchairs can be borrowed at reception (museum website).

FUNDING
Nederlands Fotomuseum: mixed: state and city subsidy plus private foundations, with Stichting Droom en Daad paying for the building
- Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW) and the Municipality of Rotterdam subsidised the predecessor institutes and are listed as founders and benefactors on the museum's website (Dutch Wikipedia; museum website). No reporting found of current subsidy amounts.
- Stichting Droom en Daad gave €38 million in 2023 to buy and renovate the Santos warehouse (Dutch Wikipedia; Network Cultures). NL Times gives the donation as €40 million. Droom en Daad says it was founded in 2016 and is a public benefit organisation (ANBI) (droomendaad.nl). Network Cultures names Wim Pijbes as its director and Martijn van der Vorm as the billionaire behind it.
- Other named supporters: Wertheimer Stichting, Vriendenloterij, Mondriaanfonds and Fonds21; no amounts found (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's funding in art media. The critique found is about Droom en Daad and arts philanthropy in general, plus coverage of a 2025 staff dispute at the museum.
1. Institute of Network Cultures: Sepp Eckenhaussen, "Six Theses Towards the Liberation of Arts Philanthropy" (2024)
URL: https://networkcultures.org/longform/2024/04/10/six-theses-towards-the-liberation-of-arts-philanthropy/
- Argues growing philanthropy in the Dutch visual arts weakens democratic governance of cultural institutions.
- Names Droom en Daad and the €38 million gift to the museum as an example; does not criticise the museum itself.
- Writes of "blatant art-washing, defended by the Mayor of Rotterdam" and says Droom en Daad has "direct access" to the Rotterdam municipal executive. Research outlet, not a news publication.
2. ArtReview: "Claims Nederlands Fotomuseum director created atmosphere of fear" (2025)
URL: https://artreview.com/claims-nederlands-fotomuseum-director-created-atmosphere-of-fear/
- Reports, from De Volkskrant, that staff alleged a "toxic work environment" under director Birgit Donker, who was suspended in June 2025; Donker said half the staff had supported her.
- Concerns management, not funding. Does not name Droom en Daad.
3. October Gallery (repost of The Art Newspaper, 1 July 2026): "Former Nederlands Fotomuseum director awarded €400,000 after unfair dismissal ruling" (2026)
URL: https://octobergallery.com/former-nederlands-fotomuseum-director-awarded-e400000-after-unfair-dismissal-ruling/
- A Rotterdam judge found Donker's dismissal in July 2025 unfair; the museum was ordered to pay €400,000, equal to two years' salary, and to publish a rectification.
- Says the Dutch Council for Journalism later found the Volkskrant allegations unverifiable and tendentious. The museum said it would not appeal.
- Repost; the author is not named on the page.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Droom en Daad offered about €40 million, reported as unconfirmed ("naar verluidt"), to the Boijmans Van Beuningen renovation in 2018. Unnamed city hall sources said Pijbes demanded two of seven seats on the supervisory board, which the city saw as too much influence, and the foundation withdrew the offer (NOS, 16 May 2019). Eckenhaussen gives the offer as €80 million (Network Cultures). Pijbes said oversight was logical for a project of seven years and that he never saw a counter-proposal.
- Unverified: Eckenhaussen alleges the Van der Vorm family's firm HAL avoids tax; he writes that the Rotterdam mayor called the fiscal constructions "totally irrelevant" (translation, Network Cultures).
- Unverified: Wijbrand Schaap, on the blog Cultureel Persbureau, claims the port barons behind Droom en Daad make up for tax flight through their gifts (18 February 2026); the post is openly critical of Pijbes.
- The museum is connected through the Santos gift: Droom en Daad paid for its building.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's running money is mainly state and city subsidy and private foundations, and the one large gift is a public-benefit foundation's purchase of the building. The weak points are the dependence on Droom en Daad for its premises, the foundation's attempt in 2019 to gain board seats at Boijmans, and the 2025 dismissal case that a court found unfair. Watch for any conditions attached to the Santos gift.
