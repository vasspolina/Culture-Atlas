MODERNA GALERIJA (MG+MSUM): Ljubljana, Slovenia
ID: moderna-galerija-ljubljana
Website: https://www.mg-lj.si

OVERVIEW
- Slovenia's national museum of modern and contemporary art, on two sites in Ljubljana: MG+ (Moderna galerija) at Cankarjeva 15 and +MSUM (Museum of Contemporary Art Metelkova) at Maistrova 3 (museum website).
- Established by a decree of the government of the People's Republic of Slovenia on 30 December 1947 and opened to the public on 3 January 1948 (Wikipedia).
- The main building was designed by Edvard Ravnikar and completed in 1948; the Metelkova museum opened as an expansion on 26 November 2011 (Wikipedia).
- The collection covers Slovenian art of the 20th and 21st centuries with works by foreign artists; the museum also runs debate, documentation, education and research programmes (Wikipedia).
- Zdenka Badovinac directed the museum from 1993 until 2020 (Apollo).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm; Thursdays in July and August until 8 pm (museum website).

FUNDING
Moderna galerija (MG+MSUM): public: a national museum whose founder is the Slovenian Ministry of Culture
- The Ministry of Culture, as the founder of Moderna galerija, issued the call for applications for the post of director (CIMAM, text of 10 March 2022).

Red flags: Ministry of Culture and the director's post
- Apollo (Dorian Batycka, 23 December 2020): the ministry advertised Zdenka Badovinac's post three times and in November 2020 named the poet and art historian Robert Simonisek (spelling as in Apollo) as interim director. Badovinac called her removal "absolutely political". The ministry rejected the criticism, saying that appointing new directors has been routine under left-wing governments for decades.
- CIMAM, in an unsigned text posted on 10 March 2022 beside the staff letter and written in the staff's voice: after Badovinac's mandate ended in 2020 the ministry repeated the call for the post three times instead of reappointing her; it changed the museum's statutes despite "loudly voiced opposition" from the professional public and changed the qualifications for the post. The text says these changes were made to enable the appointment of the ministry's "favoured candidate", Aleš Vaupotič, and calls it one of many "politically motivated changes" of directors. Badovinac had reapplied.
- Staff letter of 8 March 2022, addressed to the Government and the Ministry of Culture: 27 employees, 79.4 percent of all employees according to the letter, demanded the resignation or dismissal of Vaupotič. Their grievances: he removed part of the permanent exhibition without notifying its curators, mishandled contracts of external help and part-time employees, approved inflated budgets and pressured employees.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism in art and museum media concerns political control of the museum's leadership between 2020 and 2022, not its funding sources.
1. Apollo: Dorian Batycka, "Concerns rise about cultural freedom in Slovenia" (23 December 2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/slovenia-arts-government/
- Reports concern about cultural freedom under the ruling Slovenian Democratic Party led by Janez Janša, naming Badovinac's removal from Moderna galerija.
- Matevž Čelik says two of the three jurors selecting his successor were appointed by the ruling party and that earlier experience requirements were dropped.
- The ministry rejects the criticism.
2. CIMAM: "A Courageous Action by the staff of Moderna galerija, Ljubljana" (10 March 2022)
URL: https://cimam.org/museum-watch/museum-watch-actions/a-courageous-action-by-the-staff-of-moderna-galerija-ljubljana/
- CIMAM's Museum Watch Committee introduces the staff's public letter of protest of 8 March 2022 asking the Government and the Ministry of Culture for Vaupotič's resignation or dismissal.
- The ministry's repeated call and statute changes are described in the unsigned text beside the letter, which gives the staff's account.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Apollo: opposition member of parliament Violeta Tomić, acting head of the parliamentary culture committee, cites the cancelling of major subsidies for the public broadcaster and of financing for film production, and the firing of directors of several major cultural institutions.
- Apollo: an open letter of 17 November 2020 to Culture Minister Vasko Simoniti, with more than 1,000 signatures, criticised the director replacements and called the planned Museum of Slovenian Independence a "propaganda institution". The government said in a press release of 13 December that these characterisations were incorrect and that the museum would be curated by professional historians with no political affiliations.
- Connection: the letter's criticism of director replacements concerns the ministry that founded Moderna galerija and issued the call for its director.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the state: a national museum founded by the Slovenian Ministry of Culture. The documented issue is the ministry's handling of the director's post from 2020 to 2022: Badovinac called her removal "absolutely political", museum staff described politically motivated changes in a 2022 letter and accompanying text, and the ministry said that replacing directors is routine under left-wing governments.
