TE PAPA: Wellington, New Zealand
ID: te-papa
Website: https://www.tepapa.govt.nz

OVERVIEW
- New Zealand's national museum at 55 Cable Street, Wellington (stated by the space).
- Established in 1992 under the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa Act 1992, merging the Dominion Museum and the National Art Gallery; the official opening was on 14 February 1998 (Wikipedia).
- Te Papa means "the treasure box" in Māori (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Pete Bossley of Jasmax Architects and built by Fletcher Construction on reclaimed land owned by the Wellington Harbour Board; it covers 36,000 square metres, cost NZ$300 million by its opening and uses base isolation against earthquakes (Wikipedia).
- Holds natural history, art, history and Māori collections, including the permanent Te Taiao | Nature exhibition, a NZ$12 million nature zone that Te Papa announced would open to the public on 11 May 2019 (Te Papa press release).
- Visiting: open every day 10am to 6pm except Christmas Day. Entry is free for New Zealanders and people living in New Zealand; international visitors aged 16 and older pay NZ$35. A wheelchair can be reserved and guidebooks are offered in several languages, including NZ Sign Language (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Te Papa: mainly public: the New Zealand Government through the Ministry for Culture and Heritage, with sponsors, grant partners and earned income
- Annual report 2024-25: revenue from the Crown NZ$43.837 million of NZ$85.286 million total revenue; other revenue NZ$36.570 million, donated revenue NZ$1.816 million and interest revenue NZ$3.063 million (annual report 2024-25).
- Te Papa states that its primary funding comes from the New Zealand Government via the Ministry for Culture and Heritage, and that Wellington City Council has been a founding and destination partner since its inception (annual report 2024-25).
- A charge for international visitors began in September 2024 and generated over NZ$2.6 million in net contribution in its first year (annual report 2024-25).
- Corporate members listed on the current partners page: Panasonic, Wētā FX, ISS Facility Services Ltd and Bloomberg (undated page headed "Current partners"). Samsung supported the touring exhibition Bug lab (annual report 2024-25).
- Te Taiao | Nature, which cost close to NZ$12 million, was made possible with support from the New Zealand Government through the Ministry of Culture and Heritage. Its grant partners named in the 2019 press release were the New Zealand Lotteries Commission and the Lottery Grants Board, Pub Charity and The Lion Foundation; its core partners were Wellington City Council, EQC and GNS (Te Papa press release).
- Board: chair Christopher Swasbrook (appointed 5 May 2025) and deputy chair Jackie Lloyd (annual report 2024-25).

Red flags: gaming machine trusts as Te Taiao | Nature grant partners
- Pub Charity: its website says gaming machines in its member hotels and taverns raised NZ$44,107,348.14 in grants in 2024/25 (Pub Charity website). Te Papa's 2019 press release named it a grant partner of Te Taiao | Nature, and Te Papa's 2024-25 annual report (page 81, report signed 31 October 2025) shows its logo under "Grant partners" of Te Taiao | Nature, in a section headed "Te Papa acknowledges and thanks our Te Taiao | Nature partners".
- The Lion Foundation: its website describes its funding as the Charitable Gaming Model of pubs and hotels, which it calls "Class 4 Gambling". Te Papa's 2019 press release named it a grant partner of Te Taiao | Nature, and the 2024-25 annual report shows its logo under "Grant partners" in the same section.
- Te Papa's undated page headed "Current partners" also lists both among the Te Taiao | Nature exhibition sponsors, by logo only.

BOTTOM LINE
Te Papa is mainly paid for by the New Zealand Government, which supplied NZ$43.837 million of its NZ$85.286 million revenue in 2024-25, with the rest from its own earnings, a charge on international visitors, corporate members and sponsors. The documented weak point is Te Taiao | Nature, whose grant partners in the 2019 press release and the 2024-25 annual report include two gaming machine trusts, Pub Charity and The Lion Foundation.
