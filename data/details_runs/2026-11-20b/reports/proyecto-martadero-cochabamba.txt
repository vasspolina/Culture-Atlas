PROYECTO MARTADERO: Cochabamba, Bolivia
ID: proyecto-martadero-cochabamba
Website: https://martadero.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Social development and arts project on Calle 27 de Agosto y Ollantay in the Villa Coronilla neighbourhood of Cochabamba, a few blocks from the city centre, an area long seen as peripheral because of the slaughterhouse, rural migration and its image as a "red zone" (mARTadero, "Historia").
- Housed in the former municipal slaughterhouse, built in 1924 and awaiting heritage listing as early 20th century industrial architecture; the site is about 2,900 to 3,000 square metres (mARTadero, "Quienes somos" and "Historia").
- After 1992 the building served in turn as a makeshift school, a sports space and a depot for public lighting, unused municipal furniture and scrap, and it fell into decay (mARTadero, "Historia").
- In 2004 it hosted the II Concurso Nacional Bienal de Arte Contemporáneo (CONART), curated by Angélika Heckl; the then Oficial Mayor de Cultura, Jenny Rivero, and artists led by Heckl and Fernando García pushed for a loan of use from the municipal council. In December 2004 it hosted the 8th Bertolt Brecht Theatre Festival (mARTadero, "Historia").
- The association NADA generated the project and holds a 30-year concession on the building; Fundación Imagen manages it (mARTadero site footer and "Quienes somos").
- Rooms named after their slaughterhouse past, among them the Sala de Chanchos for exhibitions and the Trozadero for screenings, plus an open-air stage, a recording studio, a six-place residency flat and a mosaic workshop run by the women's organisation Kuska (mARTadero, "Espacios").
- Programmes: Formarte, residencies, Acción Urbana, cultural policy, a children's creativity workshop, an entrepreneurship incubator and a Maker Space, across performing arts, visual arts, architecture, audiovisual, literature and music; the project says some are dormant (mARTadero, "Quienes somos").
- The project counted almost 3,000 activities and more than 400,000 visitors in its first 14 years (stated by the project). Fernando García is its director (Voz de América).
- In 2022 it made augmented-reality murals for the Coronilla hill, part of a 31.6 million boliviano revitalisation run by the national fund FPS from 2019 to 2022 (Los Tiempos, 30 May 2022).
- In 2024 it marked the building's centenary and 20 years as a cultural centre (Los Tiempos, 9 May and 22 August 2024).
- Visiting: open Monday to Friday 10:00 to 13:00 and 15:00 to 19:00, until 23:00 on evenings with events; schools can book guided visits (mARTadero, "Participa"). A visiting dance company's two shows in May 2024 cost 20 bolivianos each (Los Tiempos).

FUNDING
Proyecto mARTadero: supporter-funded: Misereor and Oxfam listed as current supporters, in a municipal building held on a 30-year concession
- The Apoyos page, posted in February 2019, lists as current supporters Misereor and Oxfam International (mARTadero, "Apoyos").
- The same page lists as former supporters Hivos, Conexión, Solidar Suiza, Cooperación Italiana and CAF (mARTadero, "Apoyos").
- In kind: the Alcaldía of Cochabamba co-organised CONART 2004 and helped with the first clean-up of the building (mARTadero, "Historia").
- Fernando García describes mARTadero as a self-managed project (Voz de América, 27 October 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind mARTadero is foreign development aid, in a building the city has lent it. No flagged funder is on record; the supporters list is seven years old, so who pays now is the open question.
