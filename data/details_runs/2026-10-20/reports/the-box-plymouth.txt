THE BOX: Plymouth, UK
ID: the-box-plymouth
Website: https://www.theboxplymouth.com/ (checked 2026-10-24; the /supporters, /opening-hours and /access pages opened; /about-us and /visit returned "not found")

OVERVIEW
- Museum, gallery and archive on Tavistock Place in Plymouth city centre, free to enter.
- Opened on 29 September 2020, four months later than planned because of Covid-19 (The Art Newspaper, Blooloop). It replaced Plymouth City Museum and Art Gallery, which closed in September 2016 for redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- The museum was owned and operated by Plymouth City Council (Wikipedia). Its opening exhibition was "Mayflower 400: Legend and Legacy" (The Art Newspaper).
- Combines the 1907 to 1910 Edwardian Baroque museum by Thornely and Rooke, the former Central Library and St Luke's Church with a new extension designed by Atkins (Wikipedia).
- Nine permanent collection galleries, the Record Office, and the region's photographic, film and TV archives; a 6m concrete sculpture by Alexandre da Cunha in the foyer (The Art Newspaper). Fourteen restored figureheads hang in the atrium (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, plus selected bank holidays; entry is free, with free tickets to be booked for some exhibitions. Step-free entry, two lifts, a Changing Places toilet (radar key), free loan of two wheelchairs and a motorised scooter, audio loops and subtitles on moving-image displays.

FUNDING
The Box: mainly public: Plymouth City Council, Lottery and Arts Council funding, with a defence-contractor gallery sponsor
- Total cost £48m; National Lottery Heritage Fund almost £16m, Arts Council England more than £4m, Plymouth City Council about half of the opening costs plus an annual £1.4m subsidy (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- Founding Funders on the supporters page: Plymouth City Council, National Lottery Heritage Fund, Arts Council England, Coastal Communities Fund, Garfield Weston Foundation, Wolfson Foundation, University of Plymouth, a legacy from Norman Hine, EU Interreg MMIAH and Mayflower 400. No amounts or years.
- Principal Partners: Plymouth City Council, University of Plymouth, South West Film and Television Archive, South West Image Bank (Plymouth Barbican Trust). Partners include the British Film Institute and BBC.
- Sponsors: Babcock International (Gallery Partner, Port of Plymouth gallery), Plymouth Citybus and Willmott Dixon (figureheads), CityFibre (The Box on Screen), Art Fund (engagement and acquisitions). Patrons: Hilary Lade and Mark Wilson.
- The supporters and sponsorship pages give no amount, start year or length for the Babcock deal; the sponsorship page calls Babcock the first company to join as a major Gallery Partner. The museum's news page on it returned 404 and was not read.

Red flags: Babcock International, a defence contractor, sponsors the Port of Plymouth gallery
- Babcock sponsors the Port of Plymouth gallery as a Gallery Partner; the supporters page, read on 2026-10-24, gives no year for the sponsorship.
- What Babcock makes and does: it supports and maintains the Vanguard-class submarines that carry Trident missiles and is involved in designing the Dreadnought submarines, including the weapons handling and launch system. It provides fleet time engineering, facilities management, waterfront services, logistics and transport to Royal Navy bases, including Devonport. Its 2025 revenue was £4.8bn, 74% from defence clients (Campaign Against Arms Trade profile, last updated 28 April 2026, an advocacy source).
- The supporters page lists the Ministry of Defence, the National Museum of the Royal Navy and Devonport Naval Heritage Centre as Partners, by name only, with no description of their role.
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or campaign aimed at Babcock's sponsorship at The Box.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution. Coverage is descriptive or admiring, and no piece found takes up the Babcock sponsorship or the council subsidy.
1. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Inside The Box: Plymouth's £48m bid for cultural revival" (2020)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/news/inside-the-box-plymouth-s-gbp48m-bid-for-cultural-revival
- Reports no outside criticism. Covid disrupted construction and loans, some US loans were missing, and most Mayflower 400 events were postponed to 2021.
- A gallery on Plymouth's exploration history was revised late, after the Black Lives Matter movement, to acknowledge the part of Francis Drake and John Hawkins in the origins of the English slave trade; senior curator Emma Philip said it is not "a finished picture".
2. World Archaeology: staff byline, review of "Mayflower 400: Legend & Legacy" (2020)
URL: https://the-past.com/review/museum/mayflower-400-legend-legacy/
- Not criticism: presents the exhibition as a corrective to Mayflower myths and praises the partnership with the Wampanoag Advisory Committee.

BOTTOM LINE
The building was paid for mostly with public money (council, Lottery, Arts Council) and trusts, with no reporting found of anything wrong with those sources. The weak point is the Babcock gallery sponsorship: a company with majority defence revenue sponsors the Port of Plymouth gallery, and the deal's size and start year are not published. Watch for the sponsorship terms.
