LENBACHHAUS: Munich, Germany
ID: lenbachhaus
Website: https://www.lenbachhaus.de (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- The Städtische Galerie im Lenbachhaus und Kunstbau München, the City of Munich's art museum, in the former villa of the painter Franz von Lenbach beside Museumsplatz in the Kunstareal (Wikipedia, museum website).
- The villa was built in 1887 to 1891 by Gabriel von Seidl for Lenbach (Wikipedia); the city bought it from Lolo von Lenbach in 1924 and opened the Städtische Galerie there on 1 May 1929 (museum history page).
- Named for Franz von Lenbach, the painter who had the villa built.
- Building: renovated and extended by Norman Foster between 2009 and 2013; the museum reopened in May 2013 (Wikipedia, museum history page).
- What is there: the world's largest collection of Blue Rider (Der Blaue Reiter) art, built on Gabriele Münter's gift of more than a thousand works in 1957; also Joseph Beuys's "Show your wound" (bought in 1979) and contemporary art collected since the 1980s.
- Visiting: the website showed 10:00 to 18:00 opening on 25 October 2026; the pages read gave no weekly schedule, prices or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Lenbachhaus: mainly public: City of Munich, plus foundation gifts and a friends' association
- Run by the City of Munich as a municipal gallery; the City of Munich logo is the only funder in the website footer. No budget figure found.
- Gifts accepted by the council's culture committee on 18 September 2025: the Beisheim Stiftung, for the KulturSalon+ programme for isolated or poor older people; Pro Helvetia and the Art Mentor Foundation Lucerne, for the exhibition "Out of Focus. Leonore Mau und Haiti"; and the Herbert Schuchardt-Stiftung. Amounts are given only in a non-public session.
- April 2026 committee paper: the Deutsches Zentrum Kulturgutverluste, for research on 1944 purchases; the Jubiläums-Stiftung der Deutsche Bank AG Filiale München, for the project "Figurentheater"; and the Ingrid Werndl-Laue Stiftung, for free evening opening until 2029.
- July 2026 committee paper: the Karin und Uwe Hollweg Stiftung for a research project with the NS-Dokumentationszentrum on Franz von Lenbach.
- Förderverein Lenbachhaus e.V., founded 1993, whose board pledged in 2006 to raise €2.5 million for the renovation and met the goal a little over a year later; the KiCo Foundation (partner since the late 1990s) and the Förderverein support acquisitions. Deutsche Bank set up its Jubiläumsstiftung in 1992 with an initial endowment of DM 750,000 (museum website).

Red flags: Deutsche Bank penalty
- The Jubiläums-Stiftung der Deutsche Bank AG Filiale München funds the Lenbachhaus project "Figurentheater" from the income of its endowment (Munich city council paper, committee decision of 16 April 2026). The paper describes it as a private foundation set up in 1992 whose only purpose is to support the Lenbachhaus; the museum says Deutsche Bank set it up.
- On 7 July 2020 the New York State Department of Financial Services announced that Deutsche Bank had agreed in a consent order to a $150 million penalty for compliance failures in its relationship with Jeffrey Epstein and in its correspondent banking relationships with Danske Bank Estonia and FBME Bank (NY DFS).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. What exists comes from the city and the museum themselves.
1. Munich city council paper (not art media): Kulturausschuss, "Annahme von Zuwendungen" (16 July 2026)
URL: https://www.muenchen-transparent.de/dokumente/9729427/datei
- Launches, for the gallery's 100th anniversary in 2029, a project with the NS-Dokumentationszentrum on Franz von Lenbach as a founding member of the Pan-German League (Alldeutscher Verband), set up in 1891.
- Moves his political and social role into the foreground and the art history into the background.
2. Munich city council paper (not art media): Kulturausschuss (16 April 2026)
URL: https://www.muenchen-transparent.de/dokumente/9580468/datei
- Between 1925 and 1936 the gallery bought 77 works from the Jewish-owned Hugo Helbing auction house; Helbing was murdered in 1938. A project examines five works bought in 1944 from the Scheidwimmer gallery.
3. Wikipedia (not art press): "Lenbachhaus"
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenbachhaus
- In 2017 the heirs of Robert Gotschalk Lewenstein and his sister sued for the return of Kandinsky's "Colorful Life". In the same year the dispute over Paul Klee's "Swamp Legend" was settled: the city keeps the painting and the heirs of Sophie Lissitzky-Küppers are to be compensated.
- The museum's own history page says it "purged" its collection in the Nazi period and made purchases that are clearly illegitimate by today's standards.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mostly the City of Munich, with named foundation gifts that the council publishes but without public amounts. The weak points are the Deutsche Bank foundation's gift, given the bank's 2020 New York penalty, and the Nazi-era provenance record. Worth watching: the outcome of the Helbing and Lenbach research projects and any restitution claims.
