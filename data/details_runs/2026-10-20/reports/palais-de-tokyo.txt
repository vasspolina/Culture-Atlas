PALAIS DE TOKYO: Paris, France
ID: palais-de-tokyo
Website: https://palaisdetokyo.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the west wing of the Palais de Tokyo building in Paris; the City of Paris owns the east wing, which houses the Musée d'Art Moderne de Paris, and the French state owns the west wing (Wikipedia).
- The building was inaugurated on 24 May 1937 for the International Exposition and designed by André Aubert, Marcel Dastugue and Paul Viard. Nicolas Bourriaud and Jérôme Sans launched the "Site de création contemporaine" in March 2002 (Wikipedia). French Wikipedia states that Culture Minister Catherine Trautmann decided in 1999 to reopen the west wing as a contemporary art centre.
- Directors named by Wikipedia: Marc-Olivier Wahler (2006 to 2012), Jean de Loisy (2012 to 2018), Emma Lavigne (2021 to 2022) and Guillaume Désanges (2022 onwards).
- Building: Anne Lacaton and Jean-Philippe Vassal redesigned and expanded it in 2012, to 22,000 square metres (Wikipedia).
- It is described as "reputed to be the biggest non-collecting contemporary art museum in Europe" (Wikipedia). It also has a bookshop, restaurants and a programme of outreach, tours and workshops (museum website).
- Visiting: open 12:00 to 22:00; the admission page gives no closed day. Full price 13 euros, reduced price 9 euros (18 to 25, students, over 60); free for under 18, job seekers, people with disabilities and a companion, and others (museum website).

FUNDING
Palais de Tokyo: mixed: French state plus corporate patrons
- State: the footer of the museum's site says it receives the annual support of the French Republic, Richard Mille and Fondation Engie. No reporting found of the size of the state grant in the pages read; the activity reports are too large to read here.
- Season partners: Revolut and Tessi. Exhibition partners include Havaianas and the Brazilian embassy (Sonia Gomes), ADAGP and Copie privée, Vlisco (Paul Kodjo), and Dimosthenis N. Vardinogiannis and Christina Vatsella and the Elie Khouri Art Foundation (Ali Cherri) (museum website).
- "Sustainable Palais" patrons: Richard Mille (neurodiversity in contemporary art), Fondation Engie (inclusive outreach), Ruinart (ecodesign of exhibitions), Matmut pour les arts (mental health accessibility), Fondation ViSi0, Entreprendre pour aider, Ex Nihilo Paris and Swiss Life (museum website). The Ministry of Culture said in December 2023 that about a dozen companies are involved in the programme, among them Eco Adapt, Emerige, Ruinart, Fondation Swiss Life and Richard Mille.
- Benefactor members: Hopscotch, HBSS France, Peclerc Paris. Friend members of Tokyo Art Club Entreprises: Hugo Boss, G7, Arthur D. Little. Media partners include TV5 Monde, Libération, Radio Nova and RATP (museum website).
- Les Amis du Palais de Tokyo, an association with the Tokyo Art Club and an international board (museum website).
- No reporting found of amounts for any partner.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Fondation Engie)
- Fondation Engie is named in the museum's footer as an annual supporter and funds its inclusive outreach (museum website).
- Engie "covers the whole gas chain, from exploration and production to distribution"; in 2018 natural gas made up 55% of its installed capacity, and the French state held 23.64% of it on 31 December 2018 (Wikipedia). Long-term Gazprom contracts made up about 20% of its global gas sales and consumption in 2021 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gulf state body (Qatar 2020)
- "Notre monde brûle" (Our World is Burning), a guest exhibition organised by Doha's state-run Mathaf, Arab Museum of Modern Art, was due to run from 21 February to 17 May 2020 as part of the Qatar-France 2020 Year of Culture (ArtAsiaPacific, Kylie Yeung, 10 January 2020).
- Yves Michaud said the partnership aims to "bribe French society and soften its stance on human rights issues in the Persian Gulf region"; the artist Fyodor Pavlov-Andreevich called on the Palais to renounce funding from Qatar. The museum said on 9 January 2020 it would not cancel the show (ArtAsiaPacific). The article does not name Qatar Museums or state any amount.

Red flags: protest over a sponsor (La Poste, 2018)
- On 16 August 2018 about twenty striking postal workers from the Hauts-de-Seine occupied the entrance hall for about half an hour, with a banner reading "La Poste finance des expos mais ferme ses bureaux !" (translation: La Poste funds exhibitions but closes its offices). They targeted a temporary post office La Poste had set up in the museum. La Poste called it "une opération de mécénat entre deux institutions publiques" (Carenews, 19 November 2018).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Some press coverage of controversy around the Palais, but little critique of its funding by critics. The 2024 row concerns a patron's objection to its politics, and the 2020 row concerns a Qatari guest show.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Palais de Tokyo patron row intensifies after French art world figures sign letter of support" (2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/14/palais-de-tokyo-patron-row-intensifies-after-french-art-world-figures-sign-letter-of-support
- Sandra Hegedüs left the Amis du Palais de Tokyo and withdrew her funding after 15 years, saying "I don't want to be associated with the new, very political direction at the Palais de Tokyo", and singling out a Palestine-focused show thought to be "Past Disquiet", which she said "endorses a one-sided narrative on the Israel-Palestine conflict."
- An open letter in Le Monde on 13 May 2024 signed by more than 100 figures, among them Chris Dercon, Emma Lavigne and Camille Henrot, defended the Palais and its programming freedom.
- This is criticism of the Palais's programme by a patron, not of its funding. The article quotes no statement from Désanges.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: Kylie Yeung, "Artists protest Palais de Tokyo Mathaf collaboration over LGBTQ issues" (2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/artists-protest-palais-de-tokyo-mathaf-collaboration-over-lgbtq-issues
- Artists and critics objected that an LGBTQ-friendly French museum was partnering with a museum of a state that criminalises homosexuality.
- Names the Palais de Tokyo and Mathaf; the Palais answered that the guest curator, Abdellah Karroum, "has always fought against sexual discrimination."

BOTTOM LINE
The Palais is funded by the French state and a long list of named corporate patrons, none with a published amount. The weak points are Fondation Engie, a gas company's foundation, the 2020 Qatari guest show and the 2018 protest over La Poste's sponsorship. Watch whether the Palais publishes its state grant and patron amounts.
