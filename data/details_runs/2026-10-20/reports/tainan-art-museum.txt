TAINAN ART MUSEUM: Tainan, Taiwan
ID: tainan-art-museum
Website: https://www.tnam.museum/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- City art museum at No. 37 Nanmen Road, West Central District, Tainan, within walking distance southwest of Tainan Station (tnam.museum, Wikipedia).
- Planning began in 2010 after the city and county merged. Building 1 opened for trial operation on 17 October 2018 and the museum opened to the public on 27 January 2019 (Chinese Wikipedia, English Wikipedia). It was set up as an administrative legal entity, approved by the Ministry of Culture in November 2016, with its first board formed in March 2017.
- Building 1 was built in 1930 as the Tainan Police Department, designed by Sutejiro Umezawa in Art Deco style; the police left in 2011. Building 2 is a new structure by Shigeru Ban with Shih Chao-yung, whose pentagonal roof is based on the flame tree; it has 17 galleries (English Wikipedia).
- Leadership: the founding chair was the artist Chen Huei-dung (2017 to 2019; he died on 1 November 2024); the director since March 2025 is Kung Chuo-chun. Chinese Wikipedia lists five directors and five chairs since 2017, including a deputy mayor as chair in 2023 to 2025.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, Saturdays until 21:00; closed Mondays except national holidays (tnam.museum, checked 2026-10-25). Ticket prices appear only as an image. No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
Tainan Art Museum: mainly public: Tainan City, with central government and ticket income
- The museum is an administrative legal entity under a Tainan city ordinance. The city's Cultural Affairs Bureau supervises it and approves its goals, annual plans, budgets and results, and the city council reviews its budget (Da Cheng News, 21 August 2018, a city government statement).
- In 2018 Culture Bureau Director Ye Tse-shan said the museum's budget is set annually by need and that he hoped the museum would raise its self-financing rate (Da Cheng News, 2018).
- Its 2026 collection budget is NT$4 million, the lowest among Taiwan's publicly funded art museums (United Daily News, 27 February 2026).
- In 2024 Building 2 was approved for transfer to the Ministry of Culture as a planned national art museum. A preparatory office opened in March 2025 and took over Building 2 from 1 January 2026 (Chinese Wikipedia). Taiwan News reported the Cabinet's NT$1.4 billion art history plan for 2025 to 2028 includes the Tainan preparatory office (12 September 2024).
- The museum lists final accounts for 2017 to 2025, annual reports for 2019 to 2024 and yearly donor lists for 2016 to 2025 on its site. Those files did not open, so no revenue, subsidy or donor figures were verified. No reporting found of a named corporate donor.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the museum in art media. The reporting found is local news and government statements, and the main dispute is about oversight and the split of the museum between city and central government.
1. Da Cheng Daily (大成報, via Yam News): Du Zhongcong (杜忠聰), "Explanation of Tainan Art Museum Oversight and the Wumiao Guan Gong Statue Restoration" (21 August 2018; title translated)
URL: https://n.yam.com/Article/20180821847695
- A city government rebuttal to unnamed "outside doubts" about the museum's management; the statue half of the article is about a separate matter. It says the museum is supervised by the Cultural Affairs Bureau, assessed under the ordinance, reviewed by its board and the city council, and posts its accounts online.
- The critics are not named in the piece; at the council session on 21 August 2018, councillors questioned the city's ability to supervise the museum (second Da Cheng Daily report by Du Zhongcong, 21 August 2018, https://n.yam.com/Article/20180821982129). The Culture Bureau Director replied that independence was the reason for the legal-entity model.
- Local news, not art press; one-sided as a government statement.
2. United Daily News: Ho Ding-chao (何定照), "Unafraid of a Low Collection Budget, Tainan Art Museum Runs Cultural Experiments" (27 February 2026; title translated)
URL: https://udn.com/news/story/7266/9347298
- Not criticism. Director Kung Chuo-chun says contemporary art struggles to compete with commercial shows and the museum should not be an extension of an art academy.
- Reports the NT$4 million collection budget and that over 4,000 works are being redistributed with the national museum's preparatory office.
- Contains no outside criticism.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: the city of Tainan supervises and funds it, with central government now involved through the national museum project. No flagged funder was found. The weak points are that its accounts and donor lists did not open, repeated leadership changes, and the split of its buildings, which is worth watching through 2026.
