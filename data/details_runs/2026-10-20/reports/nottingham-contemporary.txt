NOTTINGHAM CONTEMPORARY: Nottingham, UK
ID: nottingham-contemporary
Website: https://www.nottinghamcontemporary.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at Weekday Cross in the Lace Market, Nottingham; formerly the Centre for Contemporary Art Nottingham (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 14 November 2009 with early works by David Hockney and recent works by Frances Stark. Alex Farquharson was founding director (2007 to 2015) and Sam Thorne took up the post in early 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Building by Caruso St John Architects, with Arup as services and structural engineers, over 3,000 square metres with four galleries and an auditorium, sunk into the sandstone cliff of the city centre; it won a 2010 RIBA Award (Wikipedia).
- A registered artistic and educational charity, number 1116670 (gallery website).
- Visiting: free entry with booking and an invitation to donate (gallery website).

FUNDING
Nottingham Contemporary: mixed: Arts Council England as principal funder, with trusts, galleries, individual patrons, Nottingham City Council and two universities
- The support page lists Arts Council England under Principal Funders.
- Headline Supporters: Bloomberg Philanthropies, the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation (UK Branch), Diane and Tariq Al-Ghussein, and the galleries Gajah Gallery, Gomide & Co, Jessica Silverman, Kasmin and Lisson Gallery.
- Bloomberg Philanthropies: the gallery joined its Digital Accelerator Program to build a new customer relationship management system (gallery website).
- Also on the support page: The Ampersand Foundation (Fund for the Future, in memory of John N Kirkland; Exhibitions Programme), Freelands Foundation, Wolfson Foundation, The Henry Moore Foundation, The Mildred Fund and The Big Give (Learning and Live Programme).
- Nottingham City Council (Learning and Live Programme), Creative New Zealand, the Lithuanian Council for Culture, the Lithuanian Culture Institute and the Embassy of the Republic of Paraguay (Exhibitions Programme) are listed. University Partners are Nottingham Trent University and the University of Nottingham.
- Jack Kirkland founded, funds and chairs The Ampersand Foundation, is executive chairman of the construction firm Bowmer and Kirkland, and was Chair of Nottingham Contemporary from 2015 to 2020 (Tate).

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Gulbenkian)
- The Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation (UK Branch) is a Headline Supporter on the gallery's support page. Its announcement of Gulbenkian Exhibitions Fund recipients, dated 18 June 2026, names a new commission and the first UK solo show of Sandra Poulson at Nottingham Contemporary; grants to the selected organisations range from £11,300 to £30,000 (Gulbenkian UK Branch).
- The Foundation was founded on 18 July 1956 under the will of Calouste Gulbenkian, whom Wikipedia calls "an Armenian Portugal-based oil magnate". Partex, a Portuguese oil extraction company, was fully owned by it until its sale in June 2019 (Wikipedia, Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation).
- Gulbenkian held 5 percent of the Turkish Petroleum Company, renamed the Iraq Petroleum Company in 1929, under the 1928 Red Line Agreement, and became a major shareholder of Royal Dutch Shell after its 1907 merger (Wikipedia, Calouste Gulbenkian).

Red flags: staff open letter, July 2020
- On 13 July 2020, 25 current and former staff published an open letter addressed to Director Sam Thorne, Finance and Operations Director Andrew Elliott and Chair Matt Symonds. It answered the gallery's anti-racism statement of 8 June 2020, which the writers said "comes too late and says too little" (open letter).
- Demands: publish the findings of two earlier reviews, The Bigger Picture and an Agency For Agency commission; diversify the four leadership roles and the board; democratic governance; end zero-hour and fixed-term contracts; bring cleaning staff in-house.
- Claims in the letter: a Diversity Board always led by a white senior staff member, and the 2017 exhibition The Place is Here curated by an all-white team.
- Unverified: the letter's writers, 25 current and former staff who gave no names, describe Soncell International as "the Homeland Security Division of Bowmer and Kirkland Ltd", link former chair Jack Kirkland to it and write that he "contributes to an inherently racist system". They say they are happy he no longer sits on the board.
- The gallery's response page welcomes the challenges, says some points were existing priorities it had not acted on quickly enough, and says the letter contains inaccuracies. It reports two board members appointed by open call in autumn 2020, ARE appointed as training provider in mid-2021 and training sessions in January 2022 (gallery website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Nottingham Contemporary comes from Arts Council England as principal funder, with Nottingham City Council, two universities, trusts, galleries and individual patrons. The Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation (UK Branch), a Headline Supporter with a 2026 exhibitions grant announcement, was endowed from Calouste Gulbenkian's oil fortune. On 13 July 2020, 25 current and former staff published an open letter, and the gallery answered it in writing.
