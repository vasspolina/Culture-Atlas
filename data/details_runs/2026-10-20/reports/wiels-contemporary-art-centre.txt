WIELS CONTEMPORARY ART CENTRE: Brussels, Belgium
ID: wiels-contemporary-art-centre
Website: https://wiels.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at Avenue Van Volxemlaan 354, 1190 Brussels, in Forest, a municipality of Brussels. It has no collection and shows temporary exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 2007; the centre says it was "initiated by art lovers in 2007" (WIELS). The Brussels-Capital Region acquired the building in 2001 (WIELS).
- The name comes from the Wielemans-Ceuppens brewery that built the site (WIELS).
- Building: the Blomme building, designed by Adrien Blomme and built by the Wielemans-Ceuppens family in 1931 to expand its brewing business. The brewery closed in 1988 and the building was given protected status in 1993 (Wikipedia, WIELS).
- On site: exhibitions, events, guided tours, a café, a bookshop, education and participation programmes, an artist residency programme and venue hire (WIELS website).
- Visiting: open 11:00 to 18:00 on the day checked; other days are not listed. Tickets €12, with reduced prices of €8, €5 and €0. No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
WIELS: mainly public with corporate and gallery partners: Flemish, French-speaking and Brussels public bodies, plus Duvel Moortgat, Phillips and others
- The website footer shows the Flemish Government, Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, Brussels-Capital Region, VGC and French Community Commission (CCF) and the Loterie Nationale (National Lottery), and the partners page lists the first four as public bodies; neither states what each pays (WIELS).
- The Brussels-Capital Region bought the building in 2001 (WIELS); no cost is stated on the pages opened.
- Structural partners: Aureus Ars & Scientia, Duvel Moortgat, Phillips and the WIELS Club.
- Exhibition and performance partners: the Netherlands embassy in Belgium, David Zwirner, Eeckman Art & Insurance, europalia, FABA, Fondation Willame Stichting, Fundacao Calouste Gulbenkian (Delegation en France), Galerie Buchholz, Goethe-Institut, Hauser & Wirth, Kurimanzutto, Lhoist Group, Loterie Nationale Loterij, Marian Goodman Gallery, Mondriaan Fonds, Richard Saltoun Gallery and the Terra Foundation for American Art.
- Education partners: Apheon, Cocof, GBL and PECA. The partners page gives no dates or amounts, and also lists private donors by name without amounts. Loterie Nationale (National Lottery) appears as an exhibition partner and in the footer; it is an ordinary public funder and stays under Funding.
- GBL (Groupe Bruxelles Lambert) is a holding company whose family shareholder base is the Frère and Desmarais (Power Corporation of Canada) families (Wikipedia). Wikipedia says that since 2012 GBL divested more of its stakes in Total and Engie, which were 4.2% of its portfolio on 31 March 2017, and lists its major holdings on 30 September 2023 as SGS, Pernod Ricard, adidas, Imerys, Concentrix + Webhelp and Ontex.
- GBL's own site, with net asset value as of 30 June 2026, names these holdings: adidas, Concentrix, Imerys, Ontex, Pernod Ricard, Recordati, SGS, Affidea, BUKO, Canyon, Rayner, Sanoptis, Parques Reunidos and Voodoo. TotalEnergies, Engie and other energy companies are not named. No reporting found of a current fossil fuel holding by GBL or of an objection to its WIELS support.
- No reporting found of annual accounts or a subsidy breakdown on the pages opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
WIELS rests on regional public money, the Loterie Nationale and corporate and gallery partners, none with a documented controversy in the pages read. GBL, which supports education, shows no fossil fuel holding on its own site as of 30 June 2026, and Wikipedia records its sell-down of Total and Engie from 2012. Worth watching: published accounts and a subsidy split, which the centre does not post.
