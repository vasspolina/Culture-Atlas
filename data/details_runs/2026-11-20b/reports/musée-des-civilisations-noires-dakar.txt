MUSÉE DES CIVILISATIONS NOIRES: Dakar, Senegal
ID: musée-des-civilisations-noires-dakar
Website: https://mcn-sn.com (checked 2026-11-20; did not respond)

OVERVIEW
- National museum in Dakar-Plateau (Wikipedia) of about 14,000 square metres with room for 18,000 pieces, in a building inspired by the round huts of Casamance (AFP via RTBF, 7 December 2018).
- It realises Léopold Sédar Senghor's plan for a museum of the histories and cultures of Black people (Wikipedia); Senghor first hired the Mexican architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez in the 1970s, with UNESCO paying his fee (Mail & Guardian).
- Abdoulaye Wade laid the first stone and President Macky Sall inaugurated it on 6 December 2018, seven years later (AFP via RTBF).
- A Chinese contractor built it; curator Ery Camara sees a Chinese pagoda in its circular form while others see Wolof houses (Mail & Guardian).
- Opening displays included the Toumaï skull, on exceptional loan from Chad, megaliths over 1,700 years old, Egyptian hieroglyphs, ritual masks, a sabre attributed to El Hadji Omar Tall brought from France, and Abdoulaye Konaté's "Non au jihad à Tombouctou" (AFP via RTBF).
- A French bill of 2020 provided for that sabre to leave the French national collections (French Senate, bill text).
- Directors: Hamady Bocoum at the opening (AFP via RTBF); Mouhamed Abdallah Ly from September 2024 (Le Soleil, 24 November 2025), who announced his resignation on Facebook in May 2026 (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica); Abdou Simbandy Diatta, formerly Director General of Culture, since 1 July 2026 (Vie Publique Sénégal).
- Ibrahima Wane has chaired the board since March 2025; the actor and director Awa Sène Sarr chairs the Friends of the MCN, and a 15-member international steering committee is coordinated by Amzat Boukari-Yabara (Le Soleil).
- It counted 49,308 entries in 2025, 19,408 of them school visits; the missions of China and Egypt held their national days there (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, 14 February 2026).

FUNDING
Musée des Civilisations Noires: Senegalese national museum; building paid for by China
- China financed the museum with more than 30 million euros (AFP via RTBF, 2018); the Mail & Guardian puts Beijing's share at $34 million.
- The museum's own revenue reached 353.9 million CFA francs in 2025, up 148 per cent on 2024, from visits, space rental and newly introduced merchandise (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica).
- The new steering committee is to propose fundraising and patronage strategies (Le Soleil).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical report, from the 2018 opening; later coverage concerns results and management.
1. Mail & Guardian: Sean O'Toole, "Senegalese artistic heritage gets a home" (2018)
URL: https://mg.co.za/friday/2018-12-20-senegalese-artistic-heritage-gets-a-home/
- Calls the museum principally a symbol, and most vulnerable as a symbol; Camara told Senegalese officials that a museum needs investment in people, training, networks, alliances and vision.
- Camara warns the seaside site makes climate control imperative and that Senegal lacks professional conservators.
- Chika Okeke-Agulu speaks of the "cultural recolonisation" of Africa and says new money "can be useful, but oftentimes it creates monstrosities and white elephants".
- Reports no permanent displays, galleries still empty two months before opening, Chinese masks in the first shows and safety signs in Chinese and English, not Wolof or French.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Sud Quotidien reported that Ly's resignation came as Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko was dismissed, and that Ly is identified as a cadre of Sonko's party PASTEF; Ly gave no reason.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: a state museum in a Chinese-financed building, with nothing under a flag. Worth watching: the new director's budget and any patrons the steering committee brings in.
