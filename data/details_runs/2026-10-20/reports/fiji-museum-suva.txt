FIJI MUSEUM: Suva, Fiji
ID: fiji-museum-suva
Website: https://fijimuseum.org.fj

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Fijian culture and history in Thurston Gardens, Cakobau Road, Suva (Fiji Museum).
- The idea of a museum was first discussed in 1904, when Sir William Allardyce gave his collection to the Suva Town Board. The Fijian Society, formed in 1908, included a museum in its aims, and the collection stayed in the Town Hall until a fire in 1919 destroyed much of it (Fiji Museum).
- Formally established in 1929 as "a Government Statutory Body with a Board of Trustees," governed by the Fiji Museum Act and the Preservation of Objects of Archaeological and Palaeontological Interest Act (Fiji Museum). Wikipedia says the Friends of Fiji Museum, a voluntary group, founded it in 1904.
- The current building opened in 1955, opened by Governor Sir Ronald Garvey, with sections added in 1972 and 1978. The archives, library, photographic studio and offices are in the former Nawela Hostel next to the main building (Fiji Museum).
- The collection includes archaeological material going back 3,700 years and galleries on history, masi, art and Indo-Fijian life. Wikipedia lists the 13-metre double-hulled canoe Ratu Finau and the rudder of HMS Bounty among its highlights.
- Open daily 9:00 to 16:30, including public holidays except Christmas Day, New Year's Day, Easter Sunday and Diwali. Admission (Fiji dollars): adults $10 local and $20 overseas; children $5 local and $10 overseas; entry with a guided tour $15 and $30 for adults (Fiji Museum). The museum gives school children free access (Fiji Parliament committee report).
- A parliamentary committee report records that the Fiji Independent Commission Against Corruption confiscated the museum's financial records for 2010 to 2014, and that they had not been returned by 25 July 2019, so it could not prepare accounts for those years. The 2015 audit carried a disclaimer of opinion and the 2016/17 audits were qualified.
- Wikipedia states that its director Sipiriano Nemani opposed a 2019 proposal to let the Indian High Commission develop part of Thurston Gardens, and that in 2021 a corruption acquittal of former director Timaima Sagale Buadromo was reversed, pending a new trial.

FUNDING
Fiji Museum: mainly public: Fiji government grants, with donor funding for digitisation from the US Embassy and the New Zealand High Commission
- The Fiji Parliament's Standing Committee on Social Affairs reports a government operating grant, in Fiji dollars, of $354,404 in 2016/17, $410,000 in 2017/18 and $697,525 in 2018/19, and a capital grant of $232,988, $466,500 and $500,000 in the same years.
- In 2015 the government allocated a $350,000 capital grant and a $330,000 operating grant, but the museum received only $127,298 of the capital grant and $213,006 of the operating grant (Fiji Parliament committee report).
- US Embassy in Suva: US$202,259 (FJ$420,698) over five years from the Ambassadors Fund for Cultural Preservation to digitise the collection; the museum's project was one of 22 chosen from 172 submissions (The Fiji Times, Timoci Vula, "US grant to help Fiji Museum complete digital documentation of its holdings", 13 November 2021).
- The Ambassadors Fund money runs out in September 2026, with about 40 percent of the collection digitised so far. The museum needs a further $300,000 to keep its interns on and finish digitising the collection by December 2028 (FBC News, Josefa Sigavolavola, "Museum faces funding cliff", 11 June 2026).
- New Zealand High Commission: a six-month extension of the digitisation project, covering September 2026 to March 2027. Chief executive Armando Lowe said the museum still needs more funding beyond March 2027 (MAI TV, Lusi Banuve, "Preserving History One Artifact at a Time; Fiji Museum Digitization Project Secures New Zealand Funding Extension", 18 June 2026).
- The museum shop assisted 26 local producers selling products worth $46,290 in 2019 (Fiji Parliament committee report).

BOTTOM LINE
The Fiji Museum is a statutory body paid for mainly by Fiji government grants, with the US Embassy's Ambassadors Fund and the New Zealand High Commission supporting its digitisation. The documented concerns are accounts gaps for 2010 to 2014 caused by the seizure of records and shortfalls in grants actually paid in 2015.
