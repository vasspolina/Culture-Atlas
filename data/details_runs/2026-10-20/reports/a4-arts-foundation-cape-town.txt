A4 ARTS FOUNDATION: Cape Town, South Africa
ID: a4-arts-foundation-cape-town
Website: https://www.a4arts.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private arts foundation and "laboratory for the arts" at 23 Buitenkant Street on the edge of District Six, Cape Town, near the District Six Museum.
- Started as a not-for-profit organisation in January 2015 and opened its public studio in 2017; it grew out of talks between collector Wendy Fisher and Josh Ginsburg, its first director (Business Day).
- Housed in a restored 19th-century industrial building that one of the cheap clothing outlets on the edge of District Six had used (Business Day, House and Garden).
- Three levels: a public library on the ground floor, the main exhibition space on the first floor and artists' work spaces above (House and Garden, 2017).
- Runs exhibitions, a residency programme, a library residency, an archive, a reading room and a book store; the October 2026 show is "Interior Design" (15 October to 7 November).
- Open Monday to Friday 10:00 to 17:30 and Saturday 10:00 to 14:00, closed Sundays and public holidays; admission is free. No accessibility information is published on the site.

FUNDING
A4 Arts Foundation: private: one patron, Wendy Fisher, with seed money through the Kirsh Family Foundation
- A4 states it is "founded and funded directly by Wendy Fisher" and is a private foundation with one patron, independent of government (stated by the space).
- In 2017 Business Day reported that the Kirsh Family Foundation covered about a third of operating costs, with the rest to come from private donors, companies or foundations.
- Fisher is president of the Kirsh Family Foundation (Business Day); the money ultimately comes from her father Nathan Kirsh (Mail and Guardian), whose Kirsh Group holds a majority of Jetro Holdings (Restaurant Depot, Jetro Cash and Carry). Sysco agreed in March 2026 to buy Jetro Restaurant Depot for $29.1 billion including debt (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the "independent institutional partners" named in the record, of amounts, or of published accounts.

Red flags: 2018 boycott call over the Kirsh family's Israel ties
- In April 2018 a Mail and Guardian comment piece called out Nathan Kirsh, the Kirsh Foundation and Wendy Fisher, citing the PACBI call for a cultural boycott of Israel.
- The piece named Kirsh as the largest shareholder and a former director of Magal Security Systems, which it said is responsible for perimeter control systems on the West Bank barrier and parts of the Gaza border.
- Kirsh bought Magal from Israel Aerospace Industries in the late 1970s, held 24.2% and was a director in 2009, and sold his 40% stake to FIMI in 2014, before the 2018 piece (Wikipedia).
- Unverified: Kirsh's Swaziland textile companies helped undermine UN sanctions against apartheid South Africa, from the same comment piece, written by activists supporting the boycott.
- No reporting found of any reply from A4 or Fisher, or of a later protest at A4.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One substantial critique, a 2018 opinion piece in the national press; later coverage is admiring profiles.
1. Mail and Guardian: Asher Gamedze and Mitchel Hunter, "The new A4 gallery's Israeli mirage" (2018)
URL: https://mg.co.za/article/2018-04-12-the-new-a4-gallerys-israeli-mirage/
- A4 looks like "a welcome alternative" to commercial galleries, but its money ties it to Israeli state image-building: "alas, it is a mirage."
- Names Fisher's roles at the Israel Museum and the Jewish People Policy Institute, and asks whether Black artists are being drawn into a propaganda project through A4.
- Announces a public forum on "Decolonising art institutions" held at A4 on 14 April 2018.
2. Business Day: Melvyn Minnaar, "Opening a space that's open to spaces opening" (2017)
URL: https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/life/arts-and-entertainment/2017-10-27-opening-a-space-thats-open-to-spaces-opening
- Not criticism: a favourable profile; Ginsburg speaks about money and commerce in the local art world in general terms.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one family's private fortune, from food wholesale in the United States, with no state or corporate sponsor on record. The weak point is the Kirsh family's past ownership of the Israeli security firm Magal, which drew a 2018 boycott call. There is no public reply from A4 and no published accounts.
