BUNKIER SZTUKI GALLERY OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Krakow, Poland
ID: bunkier-sztuki-krakow
Website: https://bunkier.art.pl (checked 2026-11-20; refused automated requests with a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art gallery at pl. Szczepański 3a in Krakow's Old Town, beside the Planty park (Polish Wikipedia).
- Its history goes back to 1950, when the Bureau of Art Exhibitions (BWA) was created. The gallery received municipal status in 1994 and took the name Bunkier Sztuki in 1995 (Polish Wikipedia).
- Krystyna Tołłoczko-Różyska designed the 1965 pavilion; Stefan Borzęcki and Antoni Hajdecki designed the facade of concrete cast in wooden formwork. PAP calls it the only brutalist building inside the Old Town (dzieje.pl).
- Directors: Krzysztof Głuchowski (1995 to 1999), Jarosław Suchan (1999 to 2002), Maria Anna Potocka (2002 to 2010 and 2019 to 2024), Piotr Cypryański (2010 to 2014), Magdalena Ziółkowska (2014 to 2018) and Delfina Jałowik (since 2024) (Polish Wikipedia).
- Attendance fell from 42,000 visitors at 15 exhibitions in 2015 to 27,000 at 13 exhibitions in 2018, figures the city gave in its 2019 merger resolution (PAP, via dzieje.pl).

FUNDING
Bunkier Sztuki: mainly public: the City of Krakow
- The gallery is financed by the City of Krakow (Polish Wikipedia: "finansowana przez Miasto Kraków"). The city is its organiser and is responsible for supervising it (Obywatelskie Forum Sztuki Współczesnej, quoted by OKO.press).
- The city paid 22 million zloty for the renovation of the pavilion, its first modernisation in 55 years (PAP, via dzieje.pl and Radio Kraków). PAP dates the start of works to autumn 2020; Polish Wikipedia gives 2021 to 2024.
- The money bought new ventilation, air conditioning, heating, security, lighting and sound systems, a connection to the municipal heating network and an extra exhibition room in the basement (RMF24, Radio Kraków).
- The city dropped a planned extension of four underground storeys, citing finances and the site beside the former city walls and moat (RMF24).

Red flags: 2019 plan to merge the gallery into MOCAK
- In January 2019 Mayor Jacek Majchrowski proposed combining the gallery with the Museum of Contemporary Art in Krakow under the MOCAK name (The Art Newspaper).
- Opponents organised as the Towarzystwo Osób Kibicujących Galerii Bunkier Sztuki and protested outside the gallery and City Hall. Artists Cecylia Malik and Monika Drożyńska withdrew from a MOCAK exhibition (The Art Newspaper).
- The presidium of ICOM Poland said the merger "godzi w wielość i pluralizm" (my translation: strikes at multiplicity and pluralism) of artistic life. Anna Baranowa of the Polish section of the International Association of Art Critics asked the mayor to drop it (PAP).
- On 24 April 2019 a clear majority of councillors voted the plan down (The Art Newspaper). OKO.press credited trade unionists of Inicjatywa Pracownicza, artists, critics and activists.

Red flags: 2024 labour court ruling on mobbing by the director
- Curator Lidia Krawczyk, at the gallery since 2006, was dismissed in the first wave of the coronavirus pandemic and went to the labour court (OKO.press).
- In July 2024 the court found that she was dismissed unlawfully and that Potocka "dopuściła się mobbingu" (my translation: committed mobbing). OKO.press calls the ruling final.
- Potocka denies mobbing and called the ruling the judgment of a "young and inexperienced" judge (her interview with Gazeta Wyborcza, quoted by OKO.press).
- The association Obywatelskie Forum Sztuki Współczesnej wrote that the mayor was told officially several times in 2019 of discriminatory practices against gallery staff, and asked the city for an immediate audit (OKO.press).
- On 2 October 2024 Mayor Aleksander Miszalski announced that Potocka would no longer direct MOCAK (OKO.press).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces criticise how the city has run the gallery and chosen its directors. Neither criticises the programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Richard Unwin, "Krakow City Council rejects mayor's plan to merge the city's two leading contemporary art spaces" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/04/29/krakow-city-council-rejects-mayors-plan-to-merge-the-citys-two-leading-contemporary-art-spaces
- In an open letter of 3 February 2019 to Culture Minister Piotr Gliński, Drożyńska and Iwona Demko warned of "a detrimental impact on the local, regional and national contemporary art scene" and asked for an open competition for the director.
- Majchrowski met his opponents on 12 February and three days later appointed Potocka, MOCAK's director, to lead the gallery. He cited a decline in its "position and prestige".
2. Magazyn Szum: editorial board, "Delfina Jałowik dyrektorką Bunkra Sztuki, czyli w Krakowie bez zmian. Komentarz redakcji" (2023)
URL: https://magazynszum.pl/delfina-jalowik-dyrektorka-bunkra-sztuki-czyli-w-krakowie-bez-zmian-komentarz-redakcji
- The city announced on 30 November 2023 that Jałowik, a long-time MOCAK curator and Potocka's "prawa ręka" (my translation: right hand), won the competition, one of five candidates. The editors write "wszystko zostaje w rodzinie" (my translation: everything stays in the family).
- Says the competition promised after Ziółkowska's dismissal in 2018 was held only five years later, and that Potocka was appointed in February 2019 without one.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery's money is clean: it is its own city's public money. The weak points lie in how the city has governed it, from the merger attempt and an appointment without competition to the mobbing ruling. Worth watching: how far the gallery under Jałowik stands apart from MOCAK.
