STEDELIJK MUSEUM AMSTERDAM: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: stedelijk-museum-amsterdam
Website: https://www.stedelijk.nl

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art and design on Museumplein, Amsterdam (Wikipedia).
- A society founded in 1874 to house art donated by Sophia Adriana de Bruyn led to the museum, which opened in 1895 in a building designed by Adriaan Willem Weissman (Wikipedia).
- The Benthem Crouwel extension reopened the museum on 23 September 2012 at a cost of 127 million euros (Wikipedia).
- It holds almost 90,000 objects, with clusters in De Stijl, Bauhaus, Pop Art and CoBrA (Wikipedia).
- It became a foundation on 1 January 2006. Rein Wolfs has been artistic director since 2019 (Wikipedia).
- 2024: about 574,000 visitors and 19 exhibitions (2024 annual report summary).

FUNDING
Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam: mainly public: the City of Amsterdam, with sponsor, fund and private contributions
- Total income in 2024: 37,540,888 euros, of which the City of Amsterdam subsidy was 23,228,444 euros, other public funding 332,558 euros and contributions from private sources 3,383,889 euros. Direct income was 10,595,997 euros, including 8,722,916 euros visitor-generated income and 513,465 euros sponsorship (2024 annual report summary).
- VriendenLoterij: called the Stedelijk's most important partner, and the City of Amsterdam is thanked alongside it (2024 annual report summary).
- Loyal partners in 2024: IMC, ABN AMRO, Mastercard, Polestar and Teijin (2024 annual report summary).
- Funds and foundations thanked for 2024 include the Mondriaan Fund, Ammodo, Fonds 21, the Cultuurfonds and the Bank of America Foundation (2024 annual report summary).
- The Don Quixote Foundation gave a gift through the Stedelijk Museum Fund that paid for the foyer refurbishment and a freely accessible sculpture hall (2024 annual report summary).
- Fossil fuels: the Museums Association reported on 18 September 2020 that the Stedelijk confirmed it does not receive any sponsorship from fossil fuel companies.

Red flags: ABN AMRO money-laundering settlement
- ABN AMRO is a loyal partner of the Stedelijk in 2024 (2024 annual report summary). ABN AMRO announced on 13 March 2026 that it and the Stedelijk were extending their partnership by two years from 11 March 2026 (ABN AMRO press release).
- On 19 April 2021 the Dutch Public Prosecution Service announced that ABN AMRO accepted a settlement of 480 million euros: a fine of 300 million euros and 180 million euros disgorgement of unlawfully obtained gains. The prosecution service accused the bank of violating the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter Terrorism Financing Act and of culpable money laundering. The release covers shortcomings in the years 2014 to 2020 and names three former members of the board of directors as suspects (Public Prosecution Service).

Red flags: protest over refusal to lend Bakunin's Barricade
- The Not Surprised Collective, a group of cultural workers, artists and activists, asked the museum on 28 May 2024 to lend Ahmet Ögüt's work Bakunin's Barricade to protect protesting students in Amsterdam from police violence. In a statement of 28 June 2024 it said the Stedelijk "has failed" (Network Cultures, the collective's own statement, an advocacy source).
- The collective says director Rein Wolfs offered a public conversation, which it declined, and that the museum proposed building the barricade from reproductions, which it rejected. The museum said (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 5 November 2024) that lending originals "clashes with an integral part of our mission and values".
- On 4 November 2024 Ögüt publicly demanded that the museum remove his work from display (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press criticism of the institution centres on the 2024 row over the refusal to lend Bakunin's Barricade.
1. Network Cultures: The Not Surprised Collective, "Stedelijk Museum Fails to Loan Artwork by Ahmet Ögüt to Protect Students in Amsterdam" (2024)
URL: https://networkcultures.org/ourcreativereset/?p=595
- Argues the museum refused to apply a contract clause that allows loans for use as a barricade in extreme social or political moments, and "fears loss of revenue and negative press coverage".
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Artist demands Stedelijk Museum remove his work amid row over 'refused' loan request" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/11/05/artist-demands-stedelijk-museum-remove-his-work-in-ongoing-row-over-loan-request-for-gaza-protest
- Reports the collective's June 2024 request, the museum's offer of reproductions, which the collective called "too performative", and Ögüt's demand that the work be taken off display.

BOTTOM LINE
The Stedelijk is funded mainly by the City of Amsterdam (about 62 percent of 2024 income), with the VriendenLoterij as its most important partner and a list of named corporate partners and funds. The documented weak points are ABN AMRO, a loyal partner whose partnership was extended in March 2026 and which paid 480 million euros in 2021 to settle a Dutch money-laundering case, and the 2024 protest over the refused loan of Bakunin's Barricade. The Museums Association reported in 2020 that the museum confirmed it takes no fossil fuel sponsorship.
