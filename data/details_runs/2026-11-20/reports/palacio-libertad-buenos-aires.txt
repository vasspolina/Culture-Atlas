PALACIO LIBERTAD: Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: palacio-libertad-buenos-aires
Website: https://palaciolibertad.gob.ar/ (checked 2026-11-20; answers automated requests with HTTP 403)

OVERVIEW
- National cultural centre in the former Central Post Office at Sarmiento 151, central Buenos Aires, a building by Norbert Maillart that opened in 1928 (Wikipedia).
- Néstor Kirchner proposed the conversion in 2005. A team led by the Bares siblings designed it, and it opened in May 2015 as the Centro Cultural Kirchner (Wikipedia).
- The Milei government renamed it by decree 897/24 of 10 October 2024 (Wikipedia); in April and May 2024 Culture Secretary Leonardo Cifelli had a Cristina Fernández de Kirchner plaque covered and Kirchner signs removed (La Nación, Diego Cabot, 7 May 2024).
- On site: the 1,950-seat Sala Sinfónica, home of the National Symphony Orchestra, five other auditoriums and 40 gallery rooms (Wikipedia).
- Valeria Ambrosio is director, and the centre hosts 180 to 300 activities a month (argentina.gob.ar, 19 June 2026). Cifelli counted more than 1.5 million visitors and more than 680 artistic projects in 2025 (argentina.gob.ar, 23 December 2025).

FUNDING
Palacio Libertad: state: the Argentine federal government through the Secretaría de Cultura
- Operator: the centre is a deconcentrated body under the Subsecretaría de Gestión y Desarrollo Cultural of the Secretaría de Cultura (Ámbito, 17 October 2024).
- Conversion: the works cost 3,811 million pesos, given as US$700 million at the 2015 opening; the first phase was built by Esuco (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Friends' association: the Asociación de Amigos del Palacio Libertad, founded on 16 December 2025 and chaired by Martín Cabrales with Gabriel Martino as treasurer, supports programming, heritage and the building (argentina.gob.ar).
- Concessions: in November 2024 the cabinet office announced a five-year tender, run by the state property agency AABE, for three food and event spaces (El Cronista, 7 November 2024).

Red flags: Removal of works from the fileteadores show, September 2025
- Hours before the opening of the 14th Encuentro Anual de Fileteadores, the Asociación de Fileteadores called the show off and denounced censorship: Palacio authorities had questioned four works, three supporting Palestine and one defending public education (La Nación, Daniel Gigena, 11 September 2025).
- Curator Ayelén Fagiani said authorities told her the works "herían la susceptibilidad del Gobierno" (translation: hurt the government's sensitivities), later accepted the education work, and still refused the Gaza works.
- Secretaría de Cultura sources replied that no national cultural venue would host political expression of any kind, and that cancelling was the association's decision, not the Secretaría's or the Palacio's.
- The association said the show would go ahead elsewhere with every work in it (Izquierda Diario, 10 September 2025).

Red flags: Cancellation of the Indigenous art biennial shows, February 2026
- The Secretaría de Cultura took the show "Desde el Corazón del Gran Chaco" off the programme two days before installation. Organiser Mercedes Avellaneda de Bocca said Palacio staff cited "órdenes de arriba" (orders from above), called it "un acto de discriminación y censura", and blamed second-tier officials rather than Cifelli (Tiempo Argentino, Patricio Féminis, 23 February 2026).
- Authorities dropped the Biennial's show close to its date, citing "un cambio de programación" (a change of programming), after asking that the word "indígena", a curatorial text and the organising network's logos not appear (Perfil, Laura Isola, 22 March 2026).
- Both shows opened elsewhere, at the Universidad Católica Argentina and at Vitriol (Perfil).

Red flags: Dismissal of staff and closure, 2016
- Under the Macri government, Hernán Lombardi let go 85% of the staff; in January 2016 about 450 employees were barred from the building, which stayed shut until 4 May 2016, and the measures met protests (Spanish Wikipedia).

Red flags: Claim of overpriced equipment, 2016
- Unverified: Lombardi, head of the Macri government's public media system, said the previous government paid 620 million pesos for equipment worth no more than 150 million, citing a provisional audit he would take to the Oficina Anticorrupción (El Cronista, 21 May 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Argentine federal government's, with a new friends' association and private concessions beside it. The weak point is that the state that pays also decides what is shown, and organisers accuse it of censorship. Watch who gives through the friends' association.
