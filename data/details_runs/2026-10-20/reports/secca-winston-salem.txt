SECCA: Winston-Salem, USA
ID: secca-winston-salem
Website: https://secca.org/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum formerly SECCA, now called the North Carolina Museum of Art, Winston-Salem (NCMA Winston-Salem). The Marguerite Drive campus (750 Marguerite Dr.) is closed in 2026 for construction improvements; a free downtown exhibition and program space at 400 W. Fourth St., Suite 130, was announced on 21 May 2026 to open on 16 July and is open Wednesday through Sunday (NCMA Winston-Salem).
- Founded in 1956 by Winston-Salem professionals and artists who wanted their city to be a place for Southeastern artists to show their work, first as the Gallery of Fine Art on Trade Street; it moved to Old Salem in the 1960s and later became the Winston-Salem Gallery of Contemporary Art (WFDD).
- In 1972 the industrialist James G. Hanes willed his 32-acre estate to the gallery (NCMA Winston-Salem history page).
- Hanes's English Hunt-Style mansion was renovated and an 8,896 square foot gallery addition was finished in 1976; a 24,500 square foot addition and a 300-seat auditorium followed in 1990, after which the museum focused on exhibiting major contemporary artists while still supporting southeastern artists (NCMA Winston-Salem history page).
- Planned upgrades to the campus include a new accessible main entrance, an elevator, a studio for visiting artists, a suite for artists in residence, new educational space, an outdoor performance pavilion and mobile gallery walls; the architects include the local firm Stitch Design Shop (WFDD, December 2024).
- The downtown space is open Wednesday through Sunday (NCMA Winston-Salem). Its inaugural exhibition series explores processes, practices and natural materials, and the inaugural exhibition is "Unboxed: Art beyond the Museum", which brings together artists who contributed to the Portable Gallery, an outreach programme that reached more than 2,000 students last year (NCMA Winston-Salem, 21 May 2026).

FUNDING
SECCA: mainly public: a North Carolina state agency, with private donors for the downtown space and programmes
- Since 2007 the museum has been an affiliate of the North Carolina Museum of Art and a division of the North Carolina Department of Natural and Cultural Resources (NCMA Winston-Salem history page). Its board then "really thought it was unsustainable" and worked a deal for it to become part of the state (WFDD, 30 December 2024).
- In 2024 the North Carolina General Assembly approved $15 million for capital projects (NCMA Winston-Salem history page); WFDD reports the $15 million appropriation came along with some additional fundraising. Corporations left the Piedmont as the economy weakened in the 1980s and 1990s (WFDD).
- Donors named for the downtown space: Anna Reilly and Matthew Cullinan; Borden and Ann Hanes; Twin City Development Foundation; Skip Dunn and Mia Celano; Don and Robin Flow; Stuart Parks; Joe Logan; Randall and Clare Tuttle; Z. Smith Reynolds Foundation; and EMBER Audio + Design (NCMA Winston-Salem, 21 May 2026).
- The Z. Smith Reynolds Foundation was established in 1936 by the children of RJ and Katharine Smith Reynolds as a memorial to their brother Zachary Smith Reynolds (foundation website); Wikipedia describes its endowment as the Reynolds family tobacco fortune.
- James G. Hanes's estate gave the campus site in 1972; the gallery's 1970s building was built with public and corporate support (WFDD).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind SECCA is a North Carolina state agency, which has run the museum since 2007, with a $15 million state appropriation approved in 2024 for the campus rebuild and a named list of private donors for the downtown space in 2026. The Z. Smith Reynolds Foundation is one of those donors; Wikipedia traces its endowment to the Reynolds family tobacco fortune.
