HALLE FÜR KUNST STEIERMARK: Graz, Austria
ID: halle-fuer-kunst-steiermark-graz
Website: https://halle-fuer-kunst.at (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition house for contemporary art in the Stadtpark at Burgring 2, Graz, operated by the Kunstverein Medienturm (association register number 542738445) (website imprint).
- Formerly the Künstlerhaus Graz: art associations collected for a building fund from 1903, the fund was confiscated after the 1938 Anschluss, and the house opened on 20 June 1952 (German Wikipedia).
- Building: an early-1950s modernist pavilion that the house calls one of the first white cubes in Austria, renovated in 2010 to 2012 by the Graz architect Alfred Bramberger with the Federal Monuments Office and adapted again from 2020 (website, German Wikipedia).
- It was a department of the Universalmuseum Joanneum from 2003 to 2012, has been run by the Kunstverein Medienturm since January 2013, and reopened as Halle für Kunst Steiermark in 2021 with "Europa: Antike Zukunft" (German Wikipedia, Kronen Zeitung).
- The Trigon biennial of art from Austria, Italy and Yugoslavia ran there from 1963 to 1995 (German Wikipedia).
- Sandro Droschl has directed it since 2013, with Helga Droschl as managing director (website, e-flux directory).
- Programme: Katherine Bernhardt (24 September 2026 to 10 January 2027), Günter Schimunek (21 January to 14 February 2027), and Josef Strau with Leroy Johnson (5 March to 9 May 2027) (website).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, open on public holidays, closed 23 December 2026 to 1 January 2027; 5 euros, 4 euros for seniors and disabled visitors, 3 euros for students aged 19 to 25, free under 19 and for artists; public tour every Wednesday at 17:00 (website).
- On 12 November 2026 the Land Steiermark and the City of Graz decided that the steirischer herbst festival will programme the house all year from 1 January 2028; the Kunstverein's contract ends in December 2027, and culture councillor Karlheinz Kornhäusl asked Droschl to apply for the festival directorship (Land Steiermark, ORF Steiermark, website).

FUNDING
HALLE FÜR KUNST Steiermark: public: Land Steiermark, the Austrian federal government and the City of Graz
- Running costs: at the 2021 relaunch the Land raised its budget for the house by 142,000 euros to 500,000 euros, the federal government contributed 140,000 euros (40 percent more) and the City of Graz kept its 102,000 euros; admission was made free at the time (Kronen Zeitung, Christoph Hartner, republished on the house's site).
- 5min.at reported in November 2026 that the house's annual operation is put at about 440,000 euros, against a steirischer herbst budget of about 4.5 million euros in 2025.
- Building: put up under the Land Steiermark as building client with Land and federal money, on land the city provided; a plaque removed in the 2020 works recorded the Land as owner (German Wikipedia).
- In 2024 the house switched to LED lighting and added outside shading with money from the EU's NextGenerationEU recovery fund; it names NextGenerationEU, the Klima- und Energiefonds, the federal arts ministry and the Land Steiermark as supporters (website).
- From 2028: the festival that takes over is the responsibility of the Land and the City; its directorship tender asks for "innovative Finanz- und Sponsoringkonzepte" (my translation: innovative financing and sponsorship concepts) because of tight public budgets (Land Steiermark).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2018 the Vereinigung Bildender Künstler Steiermark, the Sezession Graz and the Berufsvereinigung Bildender Künstler protested that their exhibition time in the house had been cut from half a year to 2.5 weeks for all associations together, and submitted 66 white canvases to a call for entries Droschl had issued (German Wikipedia, citing ORF Steiermark).
- profil (Stefan Grissemann and Wolfgang Paterno, 22 March 2025) reported cuts to Styrian culture funding under the new FPÖ-led Land government: Edith Draxl, removed as chair of the culture advisory board, gave about 2.5 million euros of annual and project grants in 2024 against 1.4 million euros in 2025, with cuts of 12 to 70 percent or refusals.
- In the same piece Kornhäusl said the culture budget rose from just over 60 million euros in 2019 to almost 81 million, most of it for the large institutions. The article does not name the Halle für Kunst; the Land is its owner and main funder.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money from all three levels of Austrian government. What is worth watching is the budget and direction of the house once the festival takes it over in 2028.
