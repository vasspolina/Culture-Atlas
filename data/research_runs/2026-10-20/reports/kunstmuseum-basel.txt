KUNSTMUSEUM BASEL – Basel, Switzerland
Website: https://kunstmuseumbasel.ch (not checked: fetch not permitted in this run, 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Grows out of the Amerbach Cabinet, bought by the city and university in 1661 and opened to the public in 1671. Main building by Paul Bonatz and Rudolf Christ (1931-36); Gegenwart (1980); extension by Christ & Gantenbein (April 2016), nearly 10,000 m2 of galleries in total. Wikipedia lists Elena Filipovic as director.

FUNDING
Kunstmuseum Basel: mainly public (canton), with foundations and corporate sponsors.
- The 2016 extension was funded by the Canton of Basel-Stadt and the Laurenz Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Existing record: Emanuel Hoffmann and Christoph Merian foundations; UBS partner; Novartis, KPMG, Trafina, Federal Office of Culture sponsors. The sponsors page could not be re-checked this run.

Red flags: Partner bank's fines
- UBS: $100M Federal Reserve fine in 2004 over dollar trading with Iran and other sanctioned states; $1.5bn Libor fine December 2012; $1.43bn US settlement August 2023 over mortgage-backed securities; $511M in May 2025 over Credit Suisse helping Americans hide offshore money; $125M FinCEN fine in 2026 for money laundering through a US subsidiary (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Sponsor's kickback settlement
- Novartis agreed to pay $678M in July 2020 over alleged payments to doctors through speaker programs (False Claims Act, Anti-Kickback Statute); also $233.9M (DOJ) and $112.8M (SEC) in June 2020 over Foreign Corrupt Practices Act investigations (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Restitution disputes
- The city rejected the Curt Glaser heirs' claim in 2008; an agreement came about twelve years later. Claims over Julius Freund works (2020) and a Henri Rousseau (2022) were refused (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is canton-run and mainly public. Its corporate partner UBS and sponsor Novartis carry repeated regulatory penalties, including a 2026 UBS money-laundering fine; these are worth tracking.
