WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2011 critics staged a protest near the gallery's grounds and circulated a petition calling for it to end its association with Tom Otterness, whom it had commissioned in April 2010 to make a sculpture for its Centennial Sculpture Park. The objection was to a 1977 work: Otterness adopted a dog from a shelter, tied it to a fence and shot it while filming it as "Shot Dog Film"; he has apologised, calling his actions "indefensible" (CITY Magazine, Rebecca Rafferty, 19 October 2011).
- The petition was just short of 2,000 names (CITY Magazine, 19 October 2011).
- The gallery kept the commission. Its statement said "We plan to move forward with our Otterness commission" and asked Otterness to renew his "contrition"; director Grant Holcomb said "We're an art museum, not a star chamber", and that walking away would have "substantial negative impact" because payments had been made and contracts signed (CITY Magazine, 19 October 2011).
- The park and its sculptures are paid for by private money from the Maurice R. and Maxine B. Forman Fund, not public funds; the gallery had lost fewer than 10 memberships over the dispute (CITY Magazine, 19 October 2011).
