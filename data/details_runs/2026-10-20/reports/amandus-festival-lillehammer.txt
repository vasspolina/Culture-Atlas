AMANDUS FESTIVAL: Lillehammer, Norway
ID: amandus-festival-lillehammer
Website: https://amandusfestivalen.no/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- National film festival by and for young people under 20, held in Lillehammer, usually the week before Easter; the next edition runs from 17 to 19 March 2027.
- First held in 1987 at the Norwegian Film Festival in Haugesund as a youth version of the Amanda Awards, started by Statens filmsentral; it moved to Lillehammer in 1999 (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- Run by Stiftelsen Amandusfestivalen, founded in 2005 by the Norwegian Film Institute, Lillehammer municipality, Oppland county and Lillehammer University College, and formally approved in 2006 (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- Merged with the Norwegian student film festival in 2016; since 2021 the foundation also runs Amandus Blikkfang for ages 20 to 26, plus a summer filmmaking course for ages 13 to 16.
- Lillehammer Kino is a partner; the site gives no main venue, admission or accessibility information.

FUNDING
Amandus Festival: mainly public: county, municipality and film bodies, with two private foundations
- The foundation's 2025 revenue was NOK 3.1 million, with a pre-tax result of NOK 4,000 and equity of NOK 344,000 (Proff, from the Brønnøysund accounts).
- Supporters shown as logos on the site: Innlandet county, Lillehammer municipality, Sparebankstiftelsen, Fritt Ord, Østnorsk Film and the Norwegian Film Institute (stated by the space; the logos carry no readable names, so these are read from the link domains).
- In June 2026 the foundation told the Norwegian Film Institute its total budget was below NOK 4 million, the threshold for the institute's new sustainability standard for festivals with operating grants (Amandus hearing response).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor or of amounts per funder.

Red flags: Epstein-linked royal patron, dropped in April 2026
- Crown Princess Mette-Marit was the festival's high patron from 2006 (NRK).
- The Epstein files released by the US Justice Department at the end of January 2026 showed extensive contact between her and Jeffrey Epstein from 2011 to 2014, including a stay at his Palm Beach house as late as 2013 (Wikipedia).
- NRK reported on 28 April 2026 that the board had unanimously decided to end the patronage; the festival's written statement said the aim was solely to protect the festival's values base and credibility (NRK).
- NRK quoted her, in a passage about her 18 patronages in general, saying she naturally wishes to continue the cooperation and understands if organisations want to end or pause their patronage. The patronage is not money; no reporting found of royal funding.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is small and mostly public, from local government, the film sector and two Norwegian private foundations, with accounts filed in the national register. The weak point was the royal patronage, which the festival itself ended in April 2026. Amounts per funder are not published.
