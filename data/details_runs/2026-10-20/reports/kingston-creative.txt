KINGSTON CREATIVE: Kingston, Jamaica
ID: kingston-creative
Website: https://kingstoncreative.org (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit arts organisation that runs the Kingston Art District, centred on Water Lane in Downtown Kingston, and the Artwalk Festival (stated by Kingston Creative).
- Founded in February 2017. Andrea Dempster Chung, now executive director, co-founded it with Alan Daisley and Jennifer Bailey (Jamaica Observer, 2024). Its first project was painting murals on Water Lane.
- No reporting found of an earlier name or of a namesake.
- Site and buildings: no building of its own for the district, which is a set of streets. Its office and coworking space, Kingston Creative Space, is at 107 Harbour Street, upstairs at F&B Downtown, and was backed by the Development Bank of Jamaica and TEF (Jamaica Observer).
- What is there: more than 108 murals in the district (its website) and 118 across Jamaica (Gleaner, 2026), mural and music tours, a coworking space with hot desks and a podcast room, the CreaTech Incubator for early-stage creative entrepreneurs, business and intellectual property registration help, and the Kingston International Festival scheduled for 6 to 13 December 2026.
- Visiting: mural, music and hop-on hop-off tours are booked through the Kulcha Connect app, TripAdvisor or tours@kingstoncreative.org. No reporting found of tour prices or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Kingston Creative: donor-funded: development banks, Jamaican companies, foundations and grants
- Inter-American Development Bank: its innovation lab IDB Lab invested US$1.3 million over three years in the CreaTech programme, which benefited more than 1,500 creative entrepreneurs (Gleaner, 1 February 2026). The Jamaica Observer (2024) and Kingston Creative's 2023 annual report give US$1.2 million for the same programme.
- Art District upgrade: the Gleaner (2026) reports $23 million in new funding, including $3 million each from Barita, Gore, National Bakery, Digicel Foundation and PricewaterhouseCoopers in July 2024, and a Bank of Jamaica pledge of $15 million over three years for managing and developing the district. The Gleaner prints no currency label. Neither the Observer (2024), the Gleaner's July 2024 report nor Kingston Creative's own site mentions the Bank of Jamaica. The Gleaner's July 2024 launch report names only Digicel Foundation and PwC at $3 million each and National Baking Company, which adopted a block, against a $30 million target for Phase II.
- UNESCO-Aschberg: one of 16 organisations chosen from 1,068 applications in 2026, and the first Caribbean non-governmental organisation to receive the grant, for the 18-month CARE Programme for artists in small island states. The amount is not stated (Jamaica Observer, 22 July 2026).
- Creative Resilience Fund after Hurricane Melissa: supported by the Clara Lionel Foundation and the Miami Foundation; it gave emergency stipends to more than 80 practitioners (Jamaica Observer, 2026).
- Partners listed by Kingston Creative: Jamaica Tourist Board, Caribbean Development Bank, National Gallery of Jamaica, Conecta Caribbean, CARIBEquity Facility, the European Union (CreaTech Incubator) and JAMPRO.
- Mural sponsors named by the critic Veerle Poupeye (2021): TEF, Sherwin Williams, ICD Holdings, GraceKennedy Foundation, F&B Downtown and Equality of Jamaica Foundation.
- Annual reports for 2021 to 2025 are posted. The 2023 report names more than 50 donors, among them the European Union, World Bank, Caribbean Development Bank, Development Bank of Jamaica, Digicel Foundation, Red Stripe, Sagicor, the Tourism Enhancement Fund and the Ministries of Culture and Tourism. Its financial statement pages are images, so no amounts could be read.
- No reporting found of fossil fuel, gambling, tobacco or arms funders among the sources named above.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One substantial critical piece, from 2021, on the mural campaign and the district plan. Other coverage found is news and profile writing.
1. Jamaica Monitor, reposted on Perspectives: Veerle Poupeye, "Painting the City" (2021)
URL: https://veerlepoupeye.com/2021/08/01/painting-the-city/
- Calls the Paint the City campaign, piloted by Kingston Creative and the Kingston Municipal Corporation, laudable but argues it is a "band-aid" over urban blight that does not address social and infrastructure problems.
- Argues it is driven by "uptown" organisers in a paternalistic way, that evidence of community consultation is limited and that many murals serve corporate interests; says art districts can contribute to gentrification and displacement.
- Argues many murals are by outside artists in a safe, superficial style, and that Downtown's street art tradition predates the campaign.
- Names Kingston Creative and the mural sponsors listed above.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Kingston Creative is mainly development-bank, foundation and Jamaican corporate donations, named in press reports, with no flagged industry found. The weak points are inconsistent figures between reports (the IDB sum and the Bank of Jamaica pledge), no readable financial statements (the 2023 report's statement pages are images), and the 2021 criticism that corporate-sponsored murals serve their sponsors and risk displacement. Worth watching: the Phase II district funding and who adopts blocks.
