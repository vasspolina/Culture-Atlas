WATTIS INSTITUTE: San Francisco, USA
ID: wattis-institute
Website: https://wattis.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art institute and gallery, part of the California College of the Arts (CCA), at 145 Hooper Street, San Francisco (Wattis).
- Founded in 1998 at CCA, initiated by Larry Rinder, as the CCAC Institute of Exhibitions and Public Programming; renamed the CCA Wattis Institute for Contemporary Arts in 2002 (Wattis, Wikipedia).
- Named for Phyllis C. Wattis; the 2002 renaming followed her death (Wikipedia).
- It absorbed the Capp Street Project, an artist residency founded in 1983 by Ann Hatch, renamed the Wattis Artist-in-Residence in 2024 (Wattis).
- Building: from 2013 it occupied 360 Kansas Street, redesigned by architect Mark Jensen, and has since moved to CCA's expanded San Francisco campus at 145 Hooper Street (Wikipedia, Wattis).
- Directors: Ralph Rugoff (2000 to 2006), Jens Hoffmann (2006 to 2012), Anthony Huberman (2013 to 2023), Daisy Nam (2024 to present, Wikipedia).
- Visiting: galleries and programmes are always free (Wattis); the current shows run to 21 November 2026.

FUNDING
Wattis Institute: parent-college and donor funded; the parent college is closing
- Its donate page lists, by tier and without amounts or years: founder Phyllis C. Wattis; Leadership Circle donors Mary and Harold Zlot, San Francisco Grants for the Arts, the Horace W. Goldsmith Foundation, Jonathan Gans and Abigail Turin, Katie and Matt Paige and Robin Laub; and about twenty Curator's Forum donors including The Richard Foundation, Helen and Chuck Schwab and Jeffrey Fraenkel (Fraenkel Gallery).
- San Francisco Grants for the Arts is labelled a funder on the donate page; no amount or year is given.
- Parent: CCA reported a 20 million dollar budget deficit in 2024 and laid off 23 staff on 27 September 2024 (CCA). Its 97.5 million dollar San Francisco expansion, which includes the Wattis's new home, was funded by donations from a capital campaign that raised more than 123 million dollars (The Art Newspaper, Tim Schneider, 28 August 2024).
- On 13 January 2026 CCA announced it will close by the end of the 2026 to 2027 academic year; Vanderbilt University will buy the San Francisco campus and has promised a "CCA Institute" to "preserve CCA's legacy" and manage its archives. KQED (Sarah Hotchkiss and Sydney Johnson) reports that Wattis programming is set to continue. Financial terms were not disclosed (KQED, 13 January 2026).
- No reporting found of accounts or a board list published by the Wattis itself.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Wattis is its parent college plus named foundations and individual donors, with no flagged-industry funder in the names listed. The main weak point is the future: CCA is closing by the end of the 2026 to 2027 academic year, Vanderbilt is buying the campus, and KQED reports that Wattis programming is set to continue.
