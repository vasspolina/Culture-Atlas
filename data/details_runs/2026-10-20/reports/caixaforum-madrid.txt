CAIXAFORUM MADRID: Madrid, Spain
ID: caixaforum-madrid
Website: https://caixaforum.org/es/madrid (checked 2026-10-20; refused automated access with HTTP 403, so details below come from the Madrid tourist office and press)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre of the Fundación "la Caixa" at Paseo del Prado 36, on Madrid's Paseo del Prado.
- Opened on 13 February 2008 (Flash Art).
- Converted by Herzog & de Meuron from an old power station from the 1900s called Central del Mediodía; Ferrovial built it between 2001 and 2007 (Wikipedia).
- About 10,000 square metres over seven floors, two of them underground, with a 460 square metre vertical garden by Patrick Blanc on the square outside (Flash Art).
- It has a 322-seat auditorium (esMADRID).
- Open daily 10:00 to 20:00. General entry is 6 euros; free for under-16s, Carnet Joven Europeo holders and CaixaBank customers. All spaces are wheelchair accessible, with Braille lift buttons, an induction loop in the auditorium and sign-language tours on request (esMADRID, updated February 2026).

FUNDING
CaixaForum Madrid: private foundation money: Fundación "la Caixa", funded by dividends from CriteriaCaixa's holdings
- The Fundación "la Caixa" runs the centre; Flash Art put the conversion cost at about 30 million euros (2008).
- The foundation's 2025 budget was a record 655 million euros; its 2025 to 2030 plan gives culture 15 to 20 percent of the budget (Ara, June 2025).
- The budget depends on dividends that its investment arm CriteriaCaixa receives from stakes in companies including CaixaBank, Naturgy, Telefónica and ACS. Isidre Fainé chairs both the foundation and Criteria (Ara, June 2025).
- Ticket income at 6 euros a visit, with CaixaBank customers entering free.
- No reporting found of the share of the Madrid centre's costs in the foundation's budget.

Red flags: gas dividends behind the foundation's budget
- Naturgy, a Spanish utility that works mainly in natural gas and electricity (Wikipedia), is one of the main holdings whose dividends fund the foundation's work, through CriteriaCaixa (Ara, June 2025).
- No reporting found of how much Naturgy dividends contribute, or of any protest at CaixaForum Madrid over them.

Red flags: dispute with contractor staff
- The front-of-house and education teams, employed by the contractor Magmacultura SL, called an indefinite strike from 11 December 2025 (SUT, the union that organised it).
- The union said education staff had been cut to 10 to 20 hours of work in August and September 2025, leaving wages of about 100 to 200 euros (SUT).
- On 9 December 2025 Magmacultura accepted all demands, including 90 percent of average hours guaranteed in 2026, schedules five working days ahead and a 3 euro an hour availability bonus (SUT).
- No reporting found of the dispute in independent press.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The coverage found is descriptive, such as Flash Art's 2008 opening report.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre runs on the Fundación "la Caixa" budget, which in turn rests on dividends from CriteriaCaixa's stakes in CaixaBank, Naturgy, Telefónica and ACS. The weak points are the gas money inside that dividend stream and the pay dispute with contractor staff settled in December 2025. Worth watching: whether the Magmacultura agreement holds in 2026 and what share of the budget Naturgy dividends supply.
