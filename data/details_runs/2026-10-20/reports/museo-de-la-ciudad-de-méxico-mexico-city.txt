MUSEO DE LA CIUDAD DE MÉXICO: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-de-la-ciudad-de-méxico-mexico-city
Website: https://www.cultura.cdmx.gob.mx/recintos/mcm

OVERVIEW
- City history museum at Pino Suárez 30 in the Centro Histórico, three blocks from the Zócalo, in the former Palacio de los Condes de Santiago de Calimaya (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The land went from Hernán Cortés to Juan Gutiérrez Altamirano; the palace was rebuilt between 1776 and 1779 by Francisco Antonio de Guerrero y Torres in Baroque style. It was declared national heritage in 1931 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The family that owned it lived there until 1964; after the building was expropriated they sold it to the city government (Spanish Wikipedia). It had become a vecindad with businesses on the first floor (Chilango).
- In 1960 the Departamento del Distrito Federal decreed it the seat of the official museum of Mexico City; Pedro Ramírez Vázquez remodelled it and the museum was inaugurated on 31 October 1964 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- On site: temporary exhibitions, a permanent studio of painter Joaquín Clausell, a 1923 cargo truck and viceregal carriages, the Biblioteca Jaime Torres Bodet and a Fondo de Cultura Económica bookshop; concerts, dance, theatre, workshops and talks (Chilango, Montse Quintana, 21 February 2023).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, general admission 38 pesos (Chilango, February 2023).

FUNDING
Museo de la Ciudad de México: mainly public: Mexico City government through its Secretaría de Cultura
- The venue belongs to the Secretaría de Cultura de la Ciudad de México, with the city government as administrator (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The city culture secretary, Ana Francis Mor, said at the April 2025 reopening of the Cháirez show: "no podemos permitir la censura nunca" (translated: we can never allow censorship) (El Financiero, 17 April 2025).

Red flags: protest outside the museum and court suspension of an exhibition
- Fabián Cháirez's show "La segunda venida del Señor", nine oil paintings made between 2018 and 2023, opened at the museum on 2 April 2025 for a run to 30 April (El Financiero, 17 April 2025; Vanguardia, 17 April 2025).
- Seven people protested outside the museum with the slogan "No ofendas mi fe, en nombre del arte" (Vanguardia, Mauro Marines, "En plena Semana Santa vuelven a suspender la exposición de Fabián Cháirez", 17 April 2025). El Financiero reported that Catholic groups had protested outside UNAM's Academia de San Carlos in February 2025 and that protests had returned.
- Religious groups filed an amparo suit on 15 April 2025, saying the show violates freedom of worship; the Juzgado Sexto de Distrito en Materia Administrativa granted a provisional suspension, notified to the museum on 15 April (Vanguardia). UnoTV calls the plaintiffs "un reducido grupo de ciudadanos" (a small group of citizens).
- The Asociación de Abogados Cristianos had earlier brought more than 30 amparos that led to the show's removal from the Academia de San Carlos in February 2025 (Vanguardia).
- The judge ordered a definitive suspension, reported on 28 and 29 April 2025, and the museum complied and removed the exhibition (UnoTV, 29 April 2025; La Razón, Julio Vázquez, "Suspenden definitivamente exposición de Fabián Cháirez en el Museo de la Ciudad de México", 29 April 2025).
- The city Secretaría de Cultura announced the provisional suspension and reaffirmed its commitment to artistic freedom of expression (Vanguardia). After the definitive order it said it would take steps to defend the right of access to culture, and called the ban a "retroceso" (setback, translated) in building a plural and democratic society (UnoTV; La Razón).
- The suit and the court order came from outside the museum and the city government. The court, not the city government, ordered the closure, and the city culture ministry opposed it.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a public institution of the Mexico City government, run through its Secretaría de Cultura. In April 2025 seven people protested outside it and religious groups won a court suspension of the Cháirez exhibition, which the city culture ministry opposed.
