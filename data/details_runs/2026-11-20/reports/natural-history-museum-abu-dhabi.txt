NATURAL HISTORY MUSEUM ABU DHABI: Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates
ID: natural-history-museum-abu-dhabi
Website: https://www.nhmad.ae/en (checked 2026-11-20; refused the automated check with a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Natural history museum of 35,000 square metres in the Saadiyat Cultural District on Saadiyat Island, next door to teamLab Phenomena Abu Dhabi and across from the Abrahamic Family House (Abu Dhabi Media Office; The National, 20 Nov 2025).
- Led by the Department of Culture and Tourism Abu Dhabi (DCT Abu Dhabi) and Miral; Al Mubarak said it took a little under five years from creative inception to opening (ALEC; The National, 30 Nov 2025).
- Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed attended the opening ceremony on 21 November 2025, and it opened to the public on 22 November 2025 (The National, 26 Nov 2025; Mecanoo).
- Building by the Dutch firm Mecanoo, shaped like rock formations; ALEC was main contractor, with its units handling the exhibition fit-out, building services and brass facades (Mecanoo; ALEC).
- Galleries run from the Big Bang to Earth's future: The Story of Earth, The Evolving World, Our World, Resilient Planet and Earth's Future, with side galleries including The PalaeoLab and The Human Story, and an Interactive Theatre (Abu Dhabi Media Office).
- Highlights: Stan, a Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton, a 25-metre blue whale, a piece of the Murchison meteorite with grains about seven billion years old, and the Abu Dhabi elephant species Stegotetrabelodon emiratus (Abu Dhabi Media Office). Al Mubarak said most of the fossils are over 50 per cent real, not resin (The National, 30 Nov 2025).
- Lucy, the 3.2 million year old Australopithecus afarensis found in Ethiopia in 1974, went on show in The Human Story on loan from the Ethiopian Heritage Authority (The National, 27 Nov 2025).
- The museum is also a research body in zoology, palaeontology, marine biology, molecular research and earth sciences (Abu Dhabi Media Office).
- Visiting: open 10am to 6.30pm Monday to Thursday and 10am to 8.30pm Friday to Sunday; general admission Dh70, free for under-18s, people of determination, UAE-based seniors and several professional groups; free parking (The National, 20 Nov 2025).

FUNDING
Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi: state: the Abu Dhabi government's Department of Culture and Tourism; no budget published
- Operator: run by DCT Abu Dhabi, the emirate's culture department (The Art Newspaper, 2 Dec 2025).
- Building: commissioned by DCT Abu Dhabi and Miral, an Abu Dhabi developer of visitor destinations that worked with Mecanoo on the design (Miral; Mecanoo). The planning consultant TBH lists the project value as AED 1.3 billion (TBH Consultancy, project page).
- Collection: DCT Abu Dhabi confirmed in March 2022 that Stan would go to the museum. National Geographic traced a 5.6 ton shipment worth $31,847,500, the price paid for Stan at Christie's New York in October 2020, from New York to the UAE in May 2021; the emirate also announced the purchase of the Murchison meteorite fragment (National Geographic, 2022).
- Acquisition committees include subject specialists and economists who assess significance and value (The National, 30 Nov 2025).
- Earned income: tickets at Dh70 for adults (The National, 20 Nov 2025).

Red flags: run by a Gulf state body, Abu Dhabi's Department of Culture and Tourism
- The museum is run by DCT Abu Dhabi, chaired by Mohamed Khalifa Al Mubarak, a department of the Abu Dhabi government (The Art Newspaper, 2 Dec 2025; Abu Dhabi Media Office).

Red flags: acquisition prices kept secret, 2025
- On the cost of its acquisitions, Al Mubarak said: "We don't like to be loud about things. Because all of a sudden, the prices go up by 15 per cent. So stay quiet in the shadows and get things with the right price" (The National, 30 Nov 2025).
- Asked about criticism of the Saadiyat district as a soft power project, he said: "This was never done for the critics" (The National, 30 Nov 2025).

Red flags: record price paid for Stan, 2020
- Paleontologists worried that the $31.8 million price could make it harder for museums with small budgets to buy specimens for research and could fuel the illegal fossil trade (National Geographic, 2022).
- Erin Thompson of John Jay College of Criminal Justice said a museum that signals it will spend vast sums risks encouraging looting or forgeries (National Geographic, 2022).

WIDER CONTEXT
- National Geographic reported in 2022 that the emirate had spent billions of dollars on the Saadiyat cultural district since 2007, that international observers questioned labour conditions for migrant workers, and that scholars raised concerns about political repression in the UAE.
- The museum is one of the district's state institutions, alongside Louvre Abu Dhabi and the Zayed National Museum (Abu Dhabi Media Office).

BOTTOM LINE
A state museum of the Abu Dhabi government, opened in November 2025, with no published budget and acquisition prices its chairman prefers to keep quiet. The only public figures are Stan's 2020 auction price and a consultant's AED 1.3 billion project value. Worth watching: whether DCT Abu Dhabi ever publishes the building cost or what it paid for its specimens.
