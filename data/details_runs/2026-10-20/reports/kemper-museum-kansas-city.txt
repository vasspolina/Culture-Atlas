KEMPER MUSEUM: Kansas City, USA
ID: kemper-museum-kansas-city
Website: https://www.kemperart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the about and visit pages returned only headings, so hours, admission and accessibility could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art at 4420 Warwick Boulevard, Kansas City, Missouri; Wikipedia calls it Missouri's first and largest contemporary museum.
- Opened in 1994; Bebe and R. Crosby Kemper Jr. gave the core of the collection. They left the board in May 2013 and their daughter Mary Kemper Wolf became chair (Wikipedia). The 2025 board lists Wolf as Chair Emerita and Bebe Kemper as Emerita.
- Architect Gunnar Birkerts designed the 23,200-square-foot building, built from 1992 to 1994 for $6.6 million, with a central atrium under a skylight (Wikipedia).
- More than 1,400 works from after the 1913 Armory Show to the present; the opening show was 28 O'Keeffe watercolours whose authenticity was challenged in 1999, after which the seller refunded the museum's $5 million (Wikipedia).
- Also on site: the restaurant Oil on Linen, opened November 2024, which shows Frederick J. Brown's 110-painting installation "The History of Art" (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: no reporting found from a page opened of hours, admission or accessibility. The 2025 board names Jessica May as Executive Director.

FUNDING
Kemper Museum: private: Kemper family trusts and foundations, other foundations and local corporations
- 2023 honor roll: the William T. Kemper Charitable Trust, with UMB Bank, n.a., as trustee, $1.664 million; the Bebe & Crosby Kemper Foundation and the R.C. Kemper Jr. Charitable Trust and Foundation, both with UMB Bank as trustee, $150,000 or more each (museum annual report).
- Also in 2023: The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, $100,000; Missouri Arts Council and the Arvin Gottlieb Charitable Foundation (UMB Bank trustee), $50,000 to $59,999 each; Gautreaux Family Foundation and Muriel McBrien Kauffman Family Foundation, $40,000 to $49,999; UMB Financial Corporation, $10,000 to $14,999 (museum annual report).
- Wikipedia gives an annual budget of about $5 million and about 75,000 visitors a year.
- No reporting found of a 2024 honor roll or of Form 990 figures after 2015 (ProPublica lists data only to 2015 under Kemper Museum Operating Foundation).

Red flags: trustee Mariner Kemper, UMB Bank and the Wyatt Detention Center
- Mariner Kemper, CEO and chairman of UMB Financial Corp and son of the museum's founders, was a Kemper Museum trustee in 2019 (Hyperallergic).
- In April 2019 UMB, as indenture trustee for the Wyatt Detention Center's bondholders, sued the city of Central Falls, Rhode Island, and the Wyatt board chair for $130 million and a restraining order after the city moved to stop holding ICE detainees there. UMB said it has no operational control of, or financial stake in, the prison (Hyperallergic, KCUR).
- The Fang Collective named five Kemper trustees with past or current UMB ties: Mariner Kemper, Sandy Kemper, L. Joshua Sosland, Clyde Wendel and Dennis Rilinger (Hyperallergic, an activist group quoted in the piece).
- Funding link: UMB Bank is trustee of the Kemper family funds that made the largest gifts on the 2023 honor roll, and UMB Financial itself gave $10,000 to $14,999 (museum annual report).
- On 3 October 2019 the Charlotte Street Foundation said Latinx artists had demonstrated at the Kemper over the trustee's ties to the lawsuit; it kept its 2019 Visual Artist Award exhibitions at the museum (Charlotte Street Foundation).
- Mariner Kemper does not appear on the 2025 trustee list; no reporting found of when or why he left, or of any museum policy change.

Not related: the Albrecht-Kemper Museum of Art in Saint Joseph, Missouri, a separate organisation with its own tax ID (ProPublica); no reporting found linking it to the Kansas City museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The largest listed gifts come from Kemper family trusts with UMB Bank as trustee, with a few arts foundations and public support from the Missouri Arts Council. The weak point is the 2019 link between UMB, the bank of a trustee and trustee of the family funds, and the Wyatt Detention Center, which drew artist protests, with no reporting found on how the museum responded. Worth watching: the 2024 honor roll and current ties between the board and UMB.
