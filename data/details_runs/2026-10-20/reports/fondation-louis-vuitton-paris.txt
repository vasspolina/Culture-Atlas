FONDATION LOUIS VUITTON: Paris, France
ID: fondation-louis-vuitton-paris
Website: https://www.fondationlouisvuitton.fr/en (checked 2026-10-20; it refused automated reading, so visit details come from listings)

OVERVIEW
- Corporate art foundation and museum at 8 avenue du Mahatma-Gandhi, in the Jardin d'Acclimatation in the Bois de Boulogne, 16th arrondissement (Wikipedia).
- A fondation d'entreprise of the LVMH group, backed by Bernard Arnault; the building was inaugurated on 20 October 2014, with Suzanne Pagé as artistic director (Wikipedia; USA Art News).
- Building by Frank Gehry: twelve glass sails of about 3,600 panels over white forms clad in about 19,000 Ductal concrete panels (Wikipedia).
- The land is held from the City of Paris under a 55-year agreement from 1 January 2007 at 100,000 euros a year; the building passes to the city at the end (Wikipedia).
- Exhibition galleries and a collection with works by Gerhard Richter, Pierre Soulages and Pierre Huyghe; 1,570,000 visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: during exhibitions open daily except Tuesday, 11 am to 8 pm, Friday to 9 pm, weekends from 10 am; full price €18, reduced €5 or €10, under-3s free; disabled visitors free between exhibitions; wheelchair access listed (Offi).

FUNDING
Fondation Louis Vuitton: corporate: LVMH companies, much of the building cost returned as tax breaks
- LVMH companies paid for the building. Estimates rose from 100 million euros in 2006 to 780 million euros reported by Marianne in May 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Under the 2003 Aillagon law, companies deduct 60% of cultural gifts from corporate tax. The Cour des comptes reported in November 2018 that LVMH companies cut their taxes by 518.1 million euros between 2007 and 2017 through payments to the foundation, about 47.1 million euros a year (Anticor; Wikipedia).
- That came to about 8% of the state's corporate patronage tax breaks for the period, and parliament then limited the law for the first time (The Conversation, 2025).
- The Conseil d'État ruled on 7 October 2022 that corporate foundations' accounts cannot be released, as they reveal their "situation financière" (Anticor). No reporting found of a budget or annual accounts.

Red flags: tax breaks and secret accounts
- The Cour des comptes report of November 2018 spoke of "le coût exorbitant pour les contribuables" of the LVMH exemption (Anticor).
- In November 2018 the anti-corruption group FRICC filed a complaint for fraud and tax evasion; it was dismissed in September 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Anticor went to the administrative court in 2020 to see the accounts; the Conseil d'État's 2022 ruling kept them closed (Anticor).

Red flags: LVMH spying settlement (2021)
- On 17 December 2021 a Paris court approved LVMH's payment of €10 million to end an inquiry into the hiring of former intelligence chief Bernard Squarcini, accused of spying on journalist François Ruffin (The Local).
- LVMH's legal director said the group accepted responsibility "including for the failings"; Ruffin asked: "Can justice be bought so cheaply?" (The Local).

Red flags: labour exploitation at an LVMH unit
- In 2024 a Milan judge placed Manufactures Dior SRL under an external administrator after police found workers at four Chinese-owned suppliers near Milan kept on "seamless day-night production cycles" (FashionUnited). Reuters, via Storyboard18 (11 June 2024), called it LVMH's Italian subsidiary and reported a one-year administration; the court said the unit did not take "appropriate measures to check the actual working conditions" of its contractors.
- A 2022 Mediapart investigation reported poor working conditions for the foundation's own security and reception staff (Wikipedia, citing Mediapart, which was not read).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is about money, not art: the tax cost to the public and the closed accounts. It comes from academic and national press and watchdog groups more than from the art press.
1. The Conversation: Tanguy Gatay, "The group Louis Vuitton (LVMH), a gift to the Musée d'Orsay and a huge tax incentive: business as usual?" (2025)
URL: https://theconversation.com/the-group-louis-vuitton-lvmh-a-gift-to-the-musee-dorsay-and-a-huge-tax-incentive-business-as-usual-265403
- Argues LVMH's patronage is largely paid by the public through tax deductions, with a €518 million rebate on the foundation.
- Says the gifts serve LVMH's image and give it diffuse influence over public institutions.
2. USA Art News: Claire Bennett, on Le Monde's investigation (2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/bernard-arnault-defended-his-use-of-tax-breaks-on-arts-patronage/
- Reports Le Monde's case that Arnault's arts spending brought LVMH tax and marketing gains.
- Arnault answered that he funded the foundation under the 2003 law: "I gave, and that seems suspicious." The Le Monde series itself was not read.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation runs on LVMH's money, and about half a billion euros of the building's cost came back to LVMH as tax relief, so the public paid for much of it. Its accounts are closed by court ruling. Worth watching: LVMH's legal record (the 2021 spying settlement, the 2024 Dior labour case) and any change to the Aillagon tax rules.
