BUSAN MUSEUM OF ART: Busan, South Korea
ID: busan-museum-of-art-busan
Website: https://art.busan.go.kr

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum at APEC-ro 58, Haeundae-gu, Busan; Hankyung calls it the Busan Municipal Museum of Art (부산시립미술관).
- Reopened in September 2026 after a three-year renovation costing 46.9 billion won (Hankyung, 28 September 2026).
- The reopening exhibition "Dear All The Yesterdays" runs from 17 September 2026 to 14 March 2027 and features the museum's collection (Busan Metropolitan City).
- Hankyung reports four exhibitions at the reopening, one of them showing about 260 works by artists who defected to North Korea or leaned socialist.
- Exhibition hours: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; closed Mondays and 1 January. Admission to the reopening exhibition is free (Busan Metropolitan City).

FUNDING
Busan Museum of Art: public: municipal museum
- Hankyung calls it the Busan Municipal Museum of Art, and the Busan Metropolitan City website carries its reopening exhibition.
- The museum's patrons' pages name two patron groups, the Busan Museum of Art Society (founded March 2016) and BMAN (founded April 2021).
- Supporting members listed on the museum's patrons' page without years or amounts: Centum Medical Foundation, Taekwang, Dongsung Chemical, Korens, Goryeo Development and Haeundae Beach C.C., Hwaseung Group, Songwol Towel and BNK Busan Bank.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One column, in a business daily rather than the art press, criticises the wall texts of a reopening exhibition as one-sided.
1. Hankyung (Korea Economic Daily): Sung Su-young, "[취재수첩] 편파 전시 논란 자초한 부산시립미술관" (28 September 2026; my translation: [Reporter's Notebook] Busan Municipal Museum of Art invites controversy with a biased exhibition)
URL: https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026092801331
- About the Busan Museum of Art's reopening show of about 260 works by artists who defected to North Korea or leaned socialist, including Lee Kwae-dae. The reporter says the artworks and scale are strong.
- Objects to the wall texts: they sharply criticise state violence such as the Bodo League massacre and the Jeju 4.3 incident but, the column says, do not address North Korea's war crimes or Soviet and Chinese involvement in the war; they suggest the South's separate elections caused the division and describe US aid and American culture negatively.
- Argues the texts ignore Lee Kwae-dae's experience under the North Korean regime, including forced portraits of Kim Il-sung and Stalin and his later purge.
- Reports that the museum said it has "no political intent" (my translation) and that director Seo Jin-seok told the reporter to look at the works rather than the texts.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is municipal, with supporting members including BNK Busan Bank, Hwaseung Group and the Centum Medical Foundation named without years or amounts. A September 2026 Hankyung column objects to the wall texts of the reopening exhibition on North Korea-linked and socialist-leaning artists.
