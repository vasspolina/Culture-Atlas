HAUS WITTGENSTEIN (BULGARIAN CULTURAL INSTITUTE): Vienna, Austria
ID: haus-wittgenstein-bulgarian-cultural-institute
Website: http://www.haus-wittgenstein.at (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Villa at Kundmanngasse 19 in Vienna's 3rd district, Landstraße, with the institute's entrance at Parkgasse 18 (Wien Geschichte Wiki, institute website).
- Built as a town house for Margaret Stonborough-Wittgenstein, who commissioned Paul Engelmann in 1925; her brother Ludwig Wittgenstein co-signed the plans approved in November 1926 and then took over all the detailed design, and the family moved in at the end of 1928 (institute website, Wien Geschichte Wiki).
- Also known as the Stonborough House and the Wittgenstein House (English Wikipedia).
- Her son Thomas Stonborough sold it in 1971 to the building contractor Franz Katlein, who planned a hotel high-rise and cut down the garden's trees; protests by architects and writers, started by Bernhard Leitner, stopped the demolition and the house was listed as a monument (Wien Geschichte Wiki, institute website, German Wikipedia).
- Bulgaria bought the house in 1975 with half the garden; a 14-storey office block of the Hauptverband der Sozialversicherungsträger went up on the other half (Wien Geschichte Wiki).
- The designer Carl Auböck brokered the sale through the Bulgarian ambassador Vladimir Ganovski and led the 1976 to 1977 renovation, which enlarged the basement for the large hall (institute website).
- It has served as the Bulgarian Cultural Institute since 1977, with exhibition rooms and halls for exhibitions, readings, concerts, film screenings and conferences, and acts as an extended cultural department of the Bulgarian Embassy (institute website).
- Pavel Naydenov is director (institute website).
- Visiting: individual house tours are booked through the partner Architekturerbe Österreich and groups by email (institute website).

FUNDING
Haus Wittgenstein (Bulgarian Cultural Institute): state-funded: the Bulgarian state owns the house and its Ministry of Culture pays for and runs the institute
- Owner: the Bulgarian state bought the building and courtyard in 1975 (institute website).
- Running costs: the institute states that it is financially and professionally managed by the Ministry of Culture of the Republic of Bulgaria (institute website).
- Restoration: the Bulgarian state restored the villa with the help of the Austrian Federal Monuments Office (Wien Geschichte Wiki).
- Exhibitions: the Austrian and Bulgarian culture ministries supported "Ludwig Wittgenstein: A Home for Gretl", a project of Austria's Wittgenstein Initiative opened by Bulgarian Culture Minister Nayden Todorov on 28 November 2024 (BTA).
- Concerts: the October 2026 concert of prizewinners of the Franz Schubert International Competition in Ruse was held with financial support from the Nationalfonds Kultur (National Culture Fund) under its Mobility programme (institute website).

BOTTOM LINE
Foreign state money, from Bulgaria's culture ministry, with no private or flagged funder.
