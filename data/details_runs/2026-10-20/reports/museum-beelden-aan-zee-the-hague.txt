MUSEUM BEELDEN AAN ZEE: The Hague, Netherlands
ID: museum-beelden-aan-zee-the-hague
Website: https://www.vriendenloterij.nl/goede-doelen/culturele-partners/museum-beelden-aan-zee

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary sculpture at Harteveltstraat 1 in Scheveningen, The Hague, on a side street off the boulevard; it calls itself the only museum in the Netherlands devoted exclusively to sculpture (Wikipedia, nl).
- Founded in 1994 by the collectors Theo Scholten (1927 to 2005) and Lida Scholten-Miltenburg (1922 to 2026) (Wikipedia, nl; the museum's site notes her death).
- Designed by the architect Wim Quist in sand-coloured concrete, glass, natural stone and wood. The museum lies in the dunes beneath the historic Paviljoen Von Wied, with a view of the sea (museum website).
- The building is fully underground. The Hague municipality required that it not be visible from the dune, so the dune was raised significantly (Wikipedia, nl).
- The Sculptuur Instituut, a research institute for modern sculpture, has been housed in the building since 2003. Tom Otterness's park of 23 fairytale sculptures on the boulevard has been open since 2004, free and around the clock (Wikipedia, nl; VriendenLoterij page).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 17:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum Beelden aan Zee: private foundation with lottery support: the VriendenLoterij, its own assets and donations
- Legal form: Stichting Museum Beelden aan Zee, run by a board of six unpaid members chaired by Isaäc Kalisvaart (ANBI page on the museum's website).
- Own assets: the foundation says its income comes from subsidies, donations, gifts, inheritances and legacies, that it has not actively raised funds so far, and that its assets, managed by an investment committee of board members, cover any operating losses of the museum and of Stichting De Onvoltooide (ANBI page).
- VriendenLoterij: the museum has been supported under a multi-year grant since 2008. The lottery's page shows a general contribution of 250,000 euros, 108,079 euros chosen by participants, and 579,767 euros received in 2025. At least 40 percent of the monthly stake for a chosen lot goes to the good cause. The VriendenLoterij is a charity lottery and ordinary public funding.
- Donations: the museum asks for gifts, bequests and "vriend" memberships to pay for exhibitions, educational projects and the care and growth of the collection (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private and charitable: a foundation started by the collector couple Scholten, with its own assets, donations, and a VriendenLoterij grant running since 2008 that is ordinary public funding. Annual reports for 2017 to 2024 and yearly accounts are posted on the museum's site, along with the named board.
