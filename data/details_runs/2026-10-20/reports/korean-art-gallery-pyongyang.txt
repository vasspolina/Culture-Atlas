KOREAN ART GALLERY: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: korean-art-gallery-pyongyang
Website: https://korean-books.com.kp (checked 2026-10-24; down, the address did not resolve from outside North Korea; no site of the gallery itself found)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum on the south side of Kim Il Sung Square, Pyongyang; Koryo Tours calls it the Korean Central Art Museum.
- Opening year: Koryo Tours says 1954; Wikipedia and Koryo Studio say 1948 (Wikipedia gives 11 August 1948 and also tags it 1954); the sources do not agree. Koryo Studio says it was renovated in 2010.
- No reporting found of a founder or of what the gallery is named for.
- Four storeys and 22 rooms (Koryo Studio, Wikipedia); Wikipedia adds an exhibition hall, preservation rooms and a study room, and over 10,000 square metres of floor space.
- The tour runs from reproductions of Koguryo tomb paintings through Korean history to socialist realist works; Koryo Studio says paintings of leaders giving on-the-spot guidance are shown and some displayed works are copies because the originals are stored elsewhere (Koryo Tours, Koryo Studio).
- Visiting: Monday to Saturday all year, closed on public holidays; free; a shop sells artworks and souvenirs; photography inside is not allowed (Koryo Tours, 13 April 2020).
- Visitors: Wikipedia, citing the state news agency KCNA, says over 6 million people had visited by 2020; the KCNA report itself was not opened.

FUNDING
Korean Art Gallery: state-run: North Korean state, owner and budget not published in any page opened
- No reporting found of a budget, accounts, donor list or board.
- The Atlas record gives the funder as the Workers' Party of Korea state budget; no page opened states this.
- Wikipedia gives the Ministry of Culture of North Korea and the National Tourism Administration as its sources, not as owners.

Red flags: sanctions on the North Korean government
- Executive Order 13722, signed by President Obama on 15 March 2016, blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea within US jurisdiction. It defines the Government of North Korea as including its "agencies, instrumentalities, and controlled entities" (Executive Order text, American Presidency Project).
- No reporting found that the gallery itself is named on a sanctions list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
No private funder is found behind the gallery; the Atlas classes it as a North Korean state museum, which brings the US sanctions order. Its owner, budget and board are unpublished. Worth watching: any published word on who runs it, and whether its own website is ever reachable.
