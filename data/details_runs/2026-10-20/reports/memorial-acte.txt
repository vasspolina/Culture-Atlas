MÉMORIAL ACTE: Pointe-à-Pitre, Guadeloupe
ID: memorial-acte
Website: https://memorialacte.fr (checked 2026-10-24; fails the secure connection: the certificate is issued for a hosting cluster, not this name, so no page was read)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial, museum, performing arts venue and congress centre on the former Darboussier sugar factory site in Pointe-à-Pitre; its official name is Centre caribéen d'expressions et de mémoire de la Traite et de l'Esclavage (French Wikipedia).
- The idea dates to 1998. Regional council president Victorin Lurel championed it, and the Comité international des peuples noirs proposed it (French Wikipedia, RCI). The competition was won in 2008 by the Guadeloupean studio BMC, and the first stone was laid on 27 May 2008.
- President François Hollande inaugurated it on 10 May 2015; it opened to the public on 7 July 2015. It became a public cultural cooperation body (EPCC) in 2019 (French Wikipedia).
- Building: 7,800 square metres on a 1.2-hectare site, a silver steel lattice over a black granite box; a 2.2-hectare hanging garden, Morne Mémoire, joins it by a 275-metre footbridge (French Wikipedia).
- Permanent exhibition traces slavery from antiquity to the present across six "archipelagos" of 39 islands. It won the Council of Europe Museum Prize in 2017.
- Directors: Jacques Martial (2015 to 2019), Laurella Yssap-Rincon (2019 to 2024), Isabelle Vestris (director general since December 2024); Raphaël Lapin has chaired the board since 2024.
- Visiting: no opening hours or prices were read. For 27 May 2026 entry to the permanent exhibition was free, and the museum offered its first critical tour of that exhibition (RCI).

FUNDING
Mémorial ACTe: public: mainly Région Guadeloupe, with European funds alleged
- Cost: €83 million; the construction budget rose from €21 million to €83 million, drawing criticism from the state audit court (The Art Newspaper, 2021; RCI, 2025).
- Victorin Lurel, then president of the Regional Council, drove the project (RCI). The €83 million cost was criticised as excessive with unemployment above 25% in Guadeloupe (RCI, 2025).
- The Région pays an operating subsidy; the then-director said in 2021 it paid only half of the 2020 subsidy (The Art Newspaper, her statement).
- Ticket sales and event hire add income; the Chambre régionale des comptes found that the Région uses the site for events such as the Route du Rhum (RCI, 2023).
- No reporting found of private donors, a sponsor list, or the shares paid by each member of the EPCC.

Red flags: audit findings and a financial investigation touching the Région
- 6 October 2023: the Cour des comptes prosecutor's 21-page request found "total disorganisation and flagrant disregard of administrative and financial rules" (my translation). Jacques Martial received a €91,382.87 termination payment without board approval; eleven staff who used a Covid withdrawal right were paid for over a year without working (Le Courrier de Guadeloupe, 5 February 2024).
- 20 November 2023: the regional audit chamber published a report finding serious irregularities that "paralyse" operation, "major risks of litigation and fraud", untraced cash and interference by the Région (RCI).
- 22 April 2024: the Parquet national financier searched the Région's offices, plus a social landlord, a property developer and a cultural engineering firm, in a preliminary inquiry into management since opening. It began with a 2019 Cour des comptes report on irregular public contracts and alleged use of European funds (RCI). RCI names no one and reports no outcome.
- Unverified: dismissed director Yssap-Rincon says ticket sales for late 2019 were reported at about €330,000 but exceeded €750,000, that €420,000 never returned, and that about €5 million may be missing since 2015. Regional president Ary Chalus called allegations an attempt to slander him; board chair Georges Bredent called her authoritarian (The Art Newspaper, 2021).
- RCI (July 2025) reports that the Basse-Terre administrative tribunal annulled the dismissal and that a director was convicted in January 2024 of favouritism in a public contract; it does not name that person in the sentence.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The art press covers the governance dispute, not the exhibition; the one critique of content is a scholarly blog.
1. The Art Newspaper: Vincent Noce, "Director of slavery memorial in Guadeloupe sacked after reporting €420,000 missing from museum's budget" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/08/03/director-of-slavery-memorial-in-guadeloupe-sacked-after-reporting-euro420000-missing-from-museums-budget
- Reports Yssap-Rincon's dismissal on 22 July 2021, her allegations of missing ticket money and the cost rise from €21 million to €83 million. Nearly 100 artists signed a petition calling the dismissal a witch hunt.
- Names Chalus and Bredent, who rejected her claims or declined to comment.
2. Bert Hoffmann, GIGA and Freie Universität Berlin, "Re-Colonizing Memory" (boasblogs, 2020; a scholarly blog, not art media)
URL: https://boasblogs.org/dcntr/re-colonizing-memory/
- Argues the museum is a French state project (Hoffmann's description) that builds a positive story of the Republic, relativises the transatlantic slave trade and avoids calling it a "crime".
- Opens with Columbus; sees it as still an important step that brings slavery into public debate.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public, driven by the Région, and the problem is how it was spent: the cost quadrupled, auditors found serious irregularities, and a national financial prosecutor is investigating. The weak points are untraced cash, the Région's influence over the site, and the unresolved allegations around ticket income. Worth watching: the outcome of the Parquet national financier inquiry.
