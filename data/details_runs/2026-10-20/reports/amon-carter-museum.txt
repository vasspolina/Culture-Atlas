AMON CARTER MUSEUM OF AMERICAN ART: Fort Worth, USA
ID: amon-carter-museum
Website: https://www.cartermuseum.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 3501 Camp Bowie Boulevard in Fort Worth's cultural district, on a hillside site overlooking downtown that Amon G. Carter Sr. chose in 1951 (Wikipedia).
- Founded through the will of Fort Worth newspaper publisher Amon G. Carter Sr. (1879 to 1955), which directed the Amon G. Carter Foundation to provide for it; it opened on 21 January 1961 as the Amon Carter Museum of Western Art, was renamed the Amon Carter Museum in 1977 and the Amon Carter Museum of American Art in 2011 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder. The core of the collection is over 400 works by Frederic Remington and Charles M. Russell, now joined by paintings, sculpture, works on paper and photography (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Philip C. Johnson in 1961, extended in 1977 and 2001; Schwartz/Silver Architects oversaw a 2019 renovation (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is free. Open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday 10am to 5pm, Thursday 10am to 8pm, Sunday noon to 5pm, closed Monday. Wheelchairs are lent free at the information desk; accessible parking is on Camp Bowie Boulevard and Lancaster Avenue.

FUNDING
Amon Carter Museum of American Art: mainly private: a founding family foundation, members and donors, with some public grants
- Amon G. Carter Foundation: funded the museum at its founding. In 1959 the foundation and the City of Fort Worth signed a contract under which the city supplied the rest of the land (Wikipedia). The foundation is a separate Fort Worth charity (EIN 75-6000331) with total assets of $553,053,361 in 2024 (ProPublica). Its grants to the museum were not published on the pages read.
- For the year ending September 2025 the museum's Form 990 shows revenue of $19,730,899, of which $16,657,042 (84.4%) was contributions and grants; expenses were $20,840,884 and net assets $93,595,341 (ProPublica).
- The museum states that it is supported "in part, by the generosity of Carter members and donors, and by grants from Arts Fort Worth, the City of Fort Worth, the Fort Worth Tourism Public Improvement District, and the Texas Commission on the Arts" (support page).
- "Current Corporate Leaders" on the support page include BNSF Railway, CBRE, Eagle Energy Systems, Fidelity Investments, Frost, Kelly Hart & Hallman, Lone Oak Trust Company, Luther King Capital Management and USI Insurance Services, with media outlets and local organisations. The page does not say what Eagle Energy Systems does.
- Officers on the 2025 Form 990: Karen Johnson Hixon (president), Mark L. Johnson, Robert M. Bass and John H. Robinson, with further trustees listed (ProPublica).
- The 2025 annual report was too large to open; donor names and amounts are not verified.
- No reporting found of a fossil-fuel, arms, tobacco or gambling funder behind the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One episode draws criticism, and it concerns the museum itself: its handling of the touring show "Cowboy" in October 2024. No criticism of its funders was found.
1. Southwest Contemporary: Emma S. Ahmad, "Cowboy Cuts Close to Home, Deep in Cowboy Country" (7 March 2025)
URL: https://southwestcontemporary.com/cowboy-amon-carter/
- Reviews "Cowboy", organised by the Museum of Contemporary Art Denver. Calls the "mature content" sign "the museum's remedy to visitor pushback" and says the reaction shows "why we need more exhibitions like Cowboy in cowboy country".
- Reports that less than two weeks after opening the show closed for a few days without explanation and reopened with a warning sign at the entrance.
- Names artists Gregg Deal, rafa esparza with Fabian Guerrero, and Angela Ellsworth; does not name any funder.
2. Dallas Voice: James Russell, report on the "Cowboy" exhibit and the warning (14 February 2025)
URL: https://dallasvoice.com/cowboy-exhibit-amon-carter-lgbtq-censorship-debate/
- News report, not an essay: the show opened on 28 September 2024, closed for two weeks in October, and reopened with a mature-content sign. The article says it is unclear which works prompted complaints.
- Cliff Vanderpool, the museum's director of development and communications, said: "We needed time to evaluate and get aligned on messaging for visitors."
- Artist rafa esparza said: "I see this as a form of censorship." The article links the case to the controversy over Sally Mann photographs at the Modern Art Museum of Fort Worth.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum looks clean on the sources read: a founder's family foundation, members, local public grants and a list of corporate supporters, with no flagged industry found. The weak points are the dependence on one founding foundation whose grants to the museum are not itemised, and the October 2024 closure and warning label on "Cowboy", which an exhibiting artist called censorship. Watch the donor list in the 2025 annual report and the Carter Foundation's grants to the museum.
