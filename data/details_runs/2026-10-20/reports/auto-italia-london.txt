AUTO ITALIA SOUTH EAST: London, United Kingdom
ID: auto-italia-london
Website: https://autoitaliasoutheast.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run gallery and commissioning organisation at 44 Bonner Road in Bethnal Green, London E2, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets. Nearest Tube is Bethnal Green, nearest Overground is Cambridge Heath.
- Founded in 2007 by Kate Cooper, Amanda Dennis and Rachel Pimm, who lived, worked and ran shows in a squatted car garage in Peckham, South London. It used donated buildings across the city for its first ten years (stated by the organisation). Wikipedia dates the Bonner Road space from 2016, after temporary spaces in Peckham (2007 to 2012) and Kings Cross (2013 to 2015).
- Named for the garage, whose sign read "auto-italia south east" (Wikipedia).
- Building: a gallery in a residential neighbourhood; no reporting found of architect or materials.
- Programme: major commissions, participation projects, exhibitions, performance and live work, international touring and creative learning for young people. Current show: "Sensual / Mutual", 17 July to 25 October 2026.
- Visiting: open Thursday to Sunday 12:00 to 17:00. The about page gives a step-free entrance to the first gallery (90 cm wide), a permanent ramp to the second gallery and accessible toilets; no reporting found of admission prices.
- Edward Gillman was director from 2019 to 2024; Maggie Matić has been director since May 2024.

FUNDING
Auto Italia South East: grant-funded: public arts funding plus international cultural agencies, trusts and private giving
- Arts Council England funds it as a National Portfolio Organisation, and Tower Hamlets Council funds it through the Local Community Fund (stated by the organisation). Wikipedia dates the NPO status from 2012. No amounts published on the pages opened.
- The organisation says it relies "entirely on fundraising" for its artistic commissioning costs and that it also relies on fundraising for over half its core running costs.
- International supporters listed on its partners page, undated and without amounts, include the British Council, the Goethe-Institut London, the Institut Français, the Mondriaan Fonds, the Kulturstiftung des Bundes, Create NSW, Culture Ireland, the US Embassy in London and the Swedish Embassy in London. The page says the list covers the past 15 years.
- The same list names the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation, with no year or amount. The foundation was created by the will of the oil magnate Calouste Gulbenkian, and it fully owned the Portuguese oil extraction company Partex until it sold it in June 2019 (Wikipedia). No reporting found of a dated gift from the foundation to Auto Italia, or of any objection to it.
- UK trusts and foundations listed, undated and without amounts: Art Fund, Backstage Trust, Bagri Foundation, Chelsea Arts Club Trust, David and Claudia Harding Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, The Elephant Trust, John Lyon's Charity, The Mercers' Company Charitable Foundation, The National Lottery Community Fund and Wellcome Trust, plus six more named on the page.
- Registration: the organisation gives charity number 1151979 and company number 07490255. The number 1172824 is not the one on its own about page.
- No reporting found of accounts or income figures; the Charity Commission register page could not be opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is admiring: Plaster magazine's guide to London artist-run spaces, by Annabel Downes (2026), calls it "the queen mother of artist-run spaces in London".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Auto Italia is mainly Arts Council England, Tower Hamlets Council, foreign cultural agencies and UK trusts, listed by name but with no years or amounts. The weak points are that the supporter lists are undated and that one listed supporter, the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation, has an oil-built endowment; no dated gift from it was found. Worth watching: published accounts and the dates of the supporter listings.
