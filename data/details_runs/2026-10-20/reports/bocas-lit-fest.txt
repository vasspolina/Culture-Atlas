BOCAS LIT FEST: Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago
ID: bocas-lit-fest
Website: https://www.bocaslitfest.com/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual literary festival held over four days at the end of April, based at the National Library (NALIS) and the Old Fire Station in downtown Port of Spain, with evening events at other venues (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Founded by Marina Salandy-Brown, now board president; the first festival ran from 28 April to 1 May 2011. Known as the NGC Bocas Lit Fest during its title sponsorship (Wikipedia; Salandy-Brown).
- Nicholas Laughlin is festival and programme director (stated by the space).
- Runs the OCM Bocas Prize for Caribbean Literature, whose overall winner gets US$10,000 from One Caribbean Media; Derek Walcott's "White Egrets" won the first prize in 2011 (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Also runs a youth programme, a national poetry slam, regional editions and The Writers Centre, a year-round literary hub (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Visiting: the 2026 festival ran 30 April to 3 May, with the poetry slam finals at the National Academy for the Performing Arts. No reporting found of admission prices; an accessibility statement is linked from the site.

FUNDING
Bocas Lit Fest: corporate-sponsored with a small state share: JB Fernandes Memorial Trust, OCM, the Ministry of Culture, formerly NGC
- 2012 to 2023: the National Gas Company (NGC), the state-owned gas company, was title sponsor for 12 years, committing in three-year blocks; it funded 2024 only in part and gave nothing in 2025 (Salandy-Brown, festival founder, 2025).
- 2024 main sponsors: NGC (title), OCM, First Citizens, the Ministry of Tourism, Culture and the Arts and the British Council; sponsors: Massy Foundation, UWI and the JB Fernandes Memorial Trust (stated by the space).
- 2026 main sponsors: the JB Fernandes Memorial Trust, OCM and the Ministry of Culture and Community Development; others: Massy Foundation, British Council, ANSA McAL Foundation, UWI and Methanex Trinidad Limited, which backs one-day community festivals later in 2026; the embassies of Colombia and Spain back Hispanophone writers (stated by the space).
- State money is under 3% of the operating budget, and the government had not paid its promised 2025 subvention by September 2025; the 2025 festival ran "on a shoestring" (Salandy-Brown).
- Laughlin called 2026 "the most difficult time in the past thirty years to try to fundraise for arts and culture in Trinidad and Tobago" (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of sponsorship amounts.

Red flags: fossil fuels (NGC and Methanex)
- NGC, created by the government in 1975, works in gas pipelines, gas production, natural gas liquids and LNG (Wikipedia); it was title sponsor until 2023 and part-funded 2024 (Salandy-Brown).
- Salandy-Brown wrote that NGC left because its corporate social responsibility priorities shifted toward sport (her column, first run in the Trinidad Express on 17 September 2025 and republished by Global Voices).
- Methanex Trinidad, a 2026 sponsor, runs the Titan and Atlas methanol plants at Point Lisas. Atlas was idled in September 2024 when its 20-year gas supply agreement expired; in June 2026 Methanex said it would idle Titan indefinitely after failing to secure a new gas contract with NGC (Guardian Media, 30 June 2026).
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter about either sponsor.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The published debate is the founder's own complaint about how the arts are funded in Trinidad and Tobago, not criticism of the festival.

BOTTOM LINE
For twelve years the festival ran on money from the state gas company, and it is now backed by a family trust, a media group and the Ministry of Culture, with Methanex among the smaller sponsors. The weak points are the fossil-fuel history and a thin, fragile funding base. Worth watching: whether Methanex stays after idling its Trinidad plants, and whether the state subvention is paid.
