CHARLESTON – Firle (near Lewes) and Lewes, UK
Website: https://www.charleston.org.uk (not checked: the fetch was blocked on 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Charleston is the former home of Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant in Firle, East Sussex, in Lewes District. The Charleston Trust was set up in 1980 and the house has been open since 1986. Galleries by Jamie Fobert Architects opened in September 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Charleston in Lewes occupies Southover House in Lewes, which Lewes District Council leases to Charleston Lewes Limited, registered as a separate charity on 2 May 2023. The lease requires free programming and an annual benefits report (council site; Charity Commission). It is open Wednesday to Sunday, 10–5 (Art Fund).

FUNDING
Charleston Trust: mainly self-generated income, plus lottery and government grants
- Charleston Trust income by year: £2.08M (2020), £2.50M (2021), £3.56M (2022), £2.96M (2023) and £2.71M (2024). For 2024 the register shows charitable activities £1.73M, trading £745k and donations £644k, with no government grants listed (Charity Commission).
- In 2020 it received about £722k from five government grants during COVID-19. It normally takes no regular public funding (Wikipedia; Artlyst, April 2020).
- The National Lottery Heritage Fund gave £2.4M in 2011 to the £6.3M barn project (Heritage Fund).
- Charleston Lewes Limited had income of £299k in 2023. Its trustees include Baroness Kay Andrews (Charity Commission).
- The Art Fund, Arts Council England and Bloomberg Connects items in the existing record were not re-checked this run.

Other finding: in July 2021 Charleston received about £240k in government grants for a cycle path and road resurfacing. Wikipedia reports this was criticised because the access road is reportedly privately owned.

BOTTOM LINE
Charleston is mostly funded by its own earned income and gifts, and its accounts are on the Charity Commission register. No reporting found of problematic funders. The Lewes gallery is a separate charity on a council lease, and the record should treat it as such.
