CAFESJIAN CENTER FOR THE ARTS: Yerevan, Armenia
ID: cafesjian-center-for-the-arts-yerevan
Website: https://www.cmf.am/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre inside and around the Yerevan Cascade, the stepped complex of fountains and terraces at 10 Tamanyan Street in central Yerevan.
- Run by the Cafesjian Museum Foundation. Wikipedia states the foundation was established in April 2002 with Armenian government support, which provided the half-built Cascade complex. The center opened in November 2009 (the center's own site); Wikipedia gives 17 November 2009.
- Named for its founder and principal benefactor, the Armenian-American philanthropist Gerard L. Cafesjian (1925 to 2013) (the center's own site).
- Building and site: the Cascade's first phase was finished in 1980, based on a design by Jim Torosyan, Aslan Mkhitaryan and Sargis Gurzadyan. The art center in it was designed by the New York architect David Hotson (Wikipedia).
- On site: the Cafesjian Sculpture Garden, the Escalator Gallery, exhibition galleries, a visitor center and a museum store. Wikipedia states the collection holds more than 5,000 works, mostly from Cafesjian's private collection, with a large glass collection.
- Visiting: exhibition galleries and visitor center open Friday to Sunday, 10:00 to 20:00; Escalator Gallery open daily, 8:00 to 20:00; the sculpture garden is always open. Admission is free. Closed on public holidays and on 12 July.

FUNDING
Cafesjian Center for the Arts: Gerard Cafesjian's own money through his foundations, with donations and membership
- Wikipedia states the Cafesjian Museum Foundation invested over $35 million to complete the Cascade's reconstruction.
- Cafesjian worked at West Publishing, rose to executive vice president and retired in 1996, when West was sold (the center's own site). He established the Cafesjian Family Foundation, which directed funds to projects in Armenia (the center's own site; Wikipedia).
- The center's site says its exhibitions include a selection of works from the Cafesjian Art Trust, and that individual membership and donations are open to the public.
- The City of Yerevan gave the unfinished Cascade area to the foundation with the right of property in 2002. In a news item dated 21 February 2022 the city said "no investments have been made by the owner" in the unfinished area and that Mayor Hrachya Sargsyan had told the city to restart negotiations with the foundation on a joint investment programme (City of Yerevan).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the center is Gerard Cafesjian's private fortune from West Publishing, channelled through the Cafesjian Museum Foundation and the Cafesjian Family Foundation, together with a city land and building transfer in 2002 and membership and donations. Cafesjian died in 2013.
