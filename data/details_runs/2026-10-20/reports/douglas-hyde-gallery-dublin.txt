DOUGLAS HYDE GALLERY: Dublin, Ireland
ID: douglas-hyde-gallery-dublin
Website: https://thedouglashyde.ie/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery in the Arts Building of Trinity College Dublin, on the campus in central Dublin.
- Co-founded by the Arts Council of Ireland and Trinity College Dublin; opened to the public on 1 March 1978 as the first publicly funded contemporary art exhibition space in Ireland (gallery website); Wikipedia describes it as Ireland's first university gallery.
- Named after Douglas Hyde, the first President of Ireland (gallery website); Wikipedia calls him a Trinity graduate.
- Gallery 1 was designed by Paul Koralek of ABK Architects; Gallery 2, designed by McCullough Mulvin Architects, opened in 2001 (gallery website).
- Programmes: exhibitions, an exhibitions and events archive, a book shop and venue hire (gallery website). Georgina Jackson is director, appointed in 2017 after John Hutchinson, who directed it for 25 years (Wikipedia).
- Open Wednesday 12 to 5, Thursday 12 to 6, Friday to Sunday 12 to 5; closed Monday and Tuesday. Admission is free (Visit Trinity).

FUNDING
Douglas Hyde Gallery: publicly supported: the Arts Council and Trinity College Dublin, with private patrons
- The gallery's support page says it is supported by the Arts Council (An Chomhairle Ealaíon) and Trinity College, Dublin.
- Ireland at Venice 2026: Isabel Nolan was selected to represent Ireland at the 61st Venice Biennale, with Georgina Jackson as curator and the gallery as partner. Ireland at Venice is an initiative of Culture Ireland in partnership with the Arts Council; Trinity College Dublin is Lead Partner and Dublin City Council is Principal Partner (Trinity College Dublin news, 6 November 2024). The Arts Council supports the 2027 tour of the work in Ireland.
- The law firm Mason Hayes & Curran says it is "among the patrons supporting Ireland at Venice" (Mason Hayes & Curran, 10 April 2026).
- Membership tiers on the support page: Champion €250 a year, Patron €750, Director's Circle €1,500, Commissions Circle €2,000 or more. The Director's Circle lists Emma Goltz, Kerlin Gallery (Dublin), Adrian and Jennifer O'Carroll, Ikumi and John Ryan, and K. Smith and A. Walsh. Patron: Michele McGuirk. Champions include Denis Byrne Architects and mother's tankstation. Benefactors: Tom Gallagher, Gordon McMillan, Pádraic E. Moore, Steve Wilson.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public: the Arts Council and Trinity College Dublin co-founded it and remain its institutional funders, with membership donations from named individuals, a gallery and an architecture practice on top. The Venice 2026 project is an initiative of Culture Ireland in partnership with the Arts Council, with Trinity and Dublin City Council as partners.
