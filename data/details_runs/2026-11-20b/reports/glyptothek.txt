GLYPTOTHEK: Munich, Germany
ID: glyptothek
Website: https://www.antike-am-koenigsplatz.mwn.de/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Greek and Roman marble sculpture on the north side of Königsplatz in Munich's Kunstareal, facing the State Collections of Antiquities; the museum calls itself Munich's oldest public museum and the only museum in the world dedicated exclusively to ancient sculpture (museum website; German Wikipedia).
- Built from 1816 to 1830 by Leo von Klenze, court architect to Ludwig I, King of Bavaria from 1825 to 1848, whom the museum names as its founder and benefactor; fourteen halls surround a square courtyard (museum website).
- Closed at the outbreak of the Second World War, hit by bombing in 1944, rebuilt from the 1960s to the plain brick look Johann Martin von Wagner had proposed, and reopened in 1972 (museum website).
- A general renovation began in October 2018; the interior reopened on 26 March 2021 (German Wikipedia), and the Bavarian art ministry announced the whole renovation, facade included, as complete in December 2021 (ORF, 19 December 2021).
- The collection runs from the Archaic period to Late Antiquity; Ludwig's agent Wagner bought most of it between 1810 and 1820. The museum dates the purchase of the Aegina pediment sculptures from the Temple of Aphaia to 1812; German Wikipedia gives 1813, the year Wagner also bought the Barberini Faun in Rome (museum website; German Wikipedia).
- The Discobolus bought in 1938 had to be returned to Italy ten years later under pressure from the American occupation authorities (German Wikipedia).
- The Glyptothek and the State Collections of Antiquities are run as one museum; Florian S. Knauß is Senior Collection Director, director since 2011 (museum website; German Wikipedia).
- Visiting: daily 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday to 20:00, closed Monday; a 6-euro day ticket, reduced 4 euros, covers both museums, 1 euro per building on Sundays, free under 18; all rooms wheelchair accessible via the northern entrance (museum website).

FUNDING
Glyptothek: public: a state museum of the Free State of Bavaria, with a friends' association
- The operator is the Free State of Bavaria, through the Staatliche Antikensammlungen und Glyptothek (German Wikipedia; museum imprint).
- The renovation finished in 2021 had a total budget of 17 million euros and stayed within cost and schedule, said Bavaria's building minister Kerstin Schreyer (ORF, quoting the Bavarian art ministry).
- The Association of Friends and Patrons of the Glyptothek and the State Collections of Antiquities has supported the museums since 1971 and has about 950 members; their money helps buy works, preserve the collections and pay for special exhibitions, lectures and tours (museum website).
- Ticket income: day tickets of 6 euros and annual passes of 20 euros (museum website).
- Founding: Ludwig I paid for the collection and the building out of his own pocket (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Bavarian state money with a members' association on top, and no corporate sponsor is named. Nothing in the funding draws a red flag. Watch the collection history: one 1938 purchase has already gone back to Italy.
