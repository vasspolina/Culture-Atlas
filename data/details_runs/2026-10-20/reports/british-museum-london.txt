BRITISH MUSEUM: London, UK
ID: british-museum-london
Website: https://www.britishmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-20; the visit page refused automated checks)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of human history in Bloomsbury, London, holding about 8 million objects; 6,440,120 visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Act of Parliament on 7 June 1753 on the collection of Sir Hans Sloane; opened on 15 January 1759 in Montagu House (Wikipedia).
- Neoclassical building by Sir Robert Smirke; the Queen Elizabeth II Great Court by Foster and Partners opened in 2000 (Wikipedia).
- A non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport, run by 25 trustees; chair George Osborne, director Nicholas Cullinan (Wikipedia; 2025/26 accounts).
- Masterplan: a new Energy Centre, a Visitor Welcome Programme of entrance pavilions backed by the Garfield Weston Foundation, and a rebuild of the Western Range, a third of the gallery floor space, by architect Lina Ghotmeh (stated by the space).
- Visiting: free and paid exhibitions (stated by the space). No reporting checked of opening hours or step-free access; the 2025/26 accounts mention a new fleet of wheelchairs.

FUNDING
British Museum: mainly public: DCMS grant-in-aid, with self-earned income, donors and a £50M BP deal
- 2025/26: £47.7M revenue and £33.2M capital grant-in-aid from DCMS (£44.5M and £29.7M in 2024/25) (2025/26 accounts).
- 2025/26: donations and legacies £14.3M; trading £23.3M; charitable activities £25.1M; total spending £126.8M (2025/26 accounts).
- 2024/25 donations of £934.3M were mostly the Sir Percival David Foundation collection, valued at £915M; in 2023/24 Sir Joseph Hotung bequeathed objects valued at £123M (2025/26 accounts).
- BP: £50M over ten years for the masterplan, announced 19 December 2023 (Museums Journal); the trustees put BP money and unspent income in a designated masterplan fund of £16.0M at 31 March 2026 (2025/26 accounts).
- Running costs grew 14% since 2023/24 against 6% growth in income (2025/26 accounts).

Red flags: fossil fuels (BP)
- BP has sponsored the museum since 1996; its exhibition deal expired in February 2023 before the new £50M deal (Hyperallergic; Museums Journal).
- The deal funds the masterplan, including the Energy Centre meant to cut the museum's carbon footprint (Museums Journal).
- Unverified: trustee Muriel Gray left the board in protest at the deal; Hyperallergic said the reasons were not confirmed.

Red flags: protest over BP
- Trustee Ahdaf Soueif resigned in 2019 (Museums Journal, 2023).
- November 2021: more than 120 museum, heritage and archaeology professionals asked the museum to end the deal, calling it "part of a strategy of reputational management" (Museums Journal).
- August 2023: about 80 artists and campaigners led by Nan Goldin asked director Hartwig Fischer to rename the BP Lecture Theatre and end fossil-fuel sponsorship (Hyperallergic).
- December 2023: Culture Unstained called the new deal "astonishingly out of touch and completely indefensible" and said it would seek legal advice; BP or Not BP? promised more protests (Museums Journal; Hyperallergic). No reporting found of how the legal challenge ended.

Red flags: Sackler name
- The Raymond and Beverly Sackler Foundation funded the museum from the 1990s until 2013; Raymond Sackler co-owned Purdue Pharma, maker of OxyContin (Hyperallergic, 2022).
- On 25 March 2022 the name came off the Sackler Rooms, Rooms 57 to 59 and the Egyptology lecture by mutual agreement; it stays on the Great Court donor list and the benefactors' board (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Steady criticism of the BP sponsorship since 2019, and of the Sackler name until 2022; the museum answers that it needs corporate money.
1. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Open Letter Calls On British Museum to Drop BP Name" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/837899/open-letter-calls-on-british-museum-to-drop-bp-name/
- The letter says BP's sponsorship gives it "an undeserved and dangerous social legitimacy and influence" and compares BP with the Sacklers.
- The museum answered that sponsors help it work while public funding is reduced, and declined to comment on the letter.
2. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "British Museum Announces $63M Partnership With Oil Giant" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/862948/british-museum-announces-63m-partnership-with-oil-giant-bp/
- Reports the activists' point that BP cut its 2030 emissions target, and notes that Tate, the National Portrait Gallery and the Royal Shakespeare Company had dropped BP.
- Quotes the museum: "we need corporate and private money to fulfill our public mission."
3. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Liu, "British Museum Drops Sackler Name, But Not Everywhere" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/british-museum-drops-sackler-name
- Points out that the name stays on the donor list; Chris Garrard said the museum should admit the link "crosses an ethical red line".

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on government grant-in-aid and its own earnings. The weak point is BP, renewed in 2023 for £50M over ten years after years of protest and a trustee's resignation. Worth watching: how much BP money reaches the masterplan each year and whether the Sackler name leaves the donor list.
