MIT MEDIA LAB: Cambridge, USA
ID: mit-media-lab-cambridge
Website: https://www.media.mit.edu (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Research laboratory and graduate programme (Media Arts and Sciences) at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, at 75 Amherst Street, Cambridge, Massachusetts (Media Lab website).
- Founded in 1985 by Nicholas Negroponte and former MIT president Jerome Wiesner, growing out of the Architecture Machine Group (Wikipedia).
- The Lab sits in the Wiesner Building (E15), designed by I. M. Pei, and in a second building (E14), designed by Fumihiko Maki and opened in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Negroponte until 2000, Walter Bender 2000 to 2006, Frank Moss 2006 to 2011, Joi Ito 2011 to 2019, then Dava Newman 2021 to 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Lab is an academic research laboratory and offers no general public tours; it accepts tour requests only from organisations considering membership, credentialed press and prospective donors (Media Lab website).

FUNDING
MIT Media Lab: corporate-sponsored: member companies, plus US government project grants
- Companies join as Consortium Lab Members for up to three years and receive licence-free, royalty-free rights to intellectual property created in the Lab during their membership (Media Lab website).
- Fourteen companies joined in 2024: Bangkok Bank, Compal, DeRUCCI, the Defence Science and Technology Agency (DSTA), GID, HP Inc., Kenvue, Kohler, MITRE, M7 Innovations, Murata Manufacturing, PMP Tech, SK Magic and Sony (Media Lab, 20 November 2024).
- Specific projects also receive US government funding from the NIH, NSF and DARPA (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of membership fees or of a current member list with amounts.

Red flags: Epstein-linked donations
- MIT's Goodwin Procter fact-finding report, released 10 January 2020, found Jeffrey Epstein gave MIT 10 gifts totalling USD 850,000 from 2002 to 2017, nine of them after his 2008 conviction, including USD 525,000 to the Media Lab in six gifts from 2013 to 2017 (MIT News).
- The report found Epstein's visits and all post-conviction gifts were driven by either Joi Ito, then Lab director, or professor Seth Lloyd, not by MIT's administration. It also found that three administrators created an informal framework in 2013, after learning of the Lab's first Epstein gift, that allowed gifts within guidelines meant to keep them small and unpublicised (MIT News).
- The New Yorker (Ronan Farrow, September 2019) reported that Ito and Lab development head Peter Cohen hid Epstein's contributions from MIT's central fundraising office, including by marking donations anonymous (TechCrunch, reporting the article). Ito told the New York Times the piece was "full of factual errors." Ito resigned on 7 September 2019.
- The Crimson (Malaika K. Tapper, October 2019) wrote that Ito accepted "more than $7.5 million in donations directed or bestowed by Jeffery Epstein". The Crimson names no source for the figure; MIT's report counts USD 525,000 given by Epstein to the Lab itself.
- On the "disqualified" label, the accounts differ. The New Yorker (Ronan Farrow, September 2019), as relayed by MIT Technology Review (Angela Chen, 12 September 2019) and the Boston Globe, reported that MIT had marked Epstein a "disqualified" donor and that the Lab took his gifts anyway. Former Lab fundraiser Signe Swenson told MIT Technology Review the label meant a donor was not worth pursuing and did not bar donations. MIT's January 2020 report says neither Epstein nor his foundations were ever coded "disqualified" in its donor database, and that the term denotes inactive donors.
- Boston Globe (Deirdre Fernandes, 2019): emails from 2014 and 2015, supplied by Whistleblower Aid, indicate at least two MIT fund-raisers and a finance administrator knew of Epstein's involvement. MIT did not answer questions about who knew.

Red flags: Saudi Misk Foundation membership
- Misk, the foundation of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, joined the Lab as a member company in 2017, with a minimum annual contribution of USD 250,000 for three years, according to a Guardian article of 30 March 2018 by Grif Peterson and Yarden Katz, cited by the MIT Faculty Newsletter. The editorial does not say how much Misk paid.
- The MIT Faculty Newsletter editorial board (March/April 2018) opposed MIT agreements with the Saudi government, citing the kingdom's human rights record, and asked President Reif to report to the faculty. Wikipedia states Misk was a Lab member until 2018.

BOTTOM LINE
The Lab's money is corporate membership dues plus federal project funding, and its biggest documented problem is Epstein: USD 525,000 to the Lab, a director who resigned, and donations hidden from MIT's central office. The weak points are that history, the Saudi Misk membership in 2017 to 2018, and no published member amounts. Worth watching: whether member fees and donor sources are published.
