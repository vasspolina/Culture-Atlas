MUSEUM OF WEST AFRICAN ART (MOWAA): Benin City, Nigeria
ID: museum-of-west-african-art-mowaa
Website: https://wearemowaa.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads; says visits are by appointment only)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural campus at 1 Benin Sapele Road, Oka, Benin City, inside the ancient moats of the Benin Kingdom (The Art Newspaper).
- Announced in 2020 as the Edo Museum of West African Art; the "Edo" was dropped in 2023 after the federal government ruled that the Benin Bronzes belong to the Oba (Museums Association). Phillip Ihenacho is executive director and chair (The Art Newspaper).
- Designed by Adjaye Associates; the first building is about 4,000 square metres of rammed earth, with an auditorium, conservation lab, library, exhibition space and amphitheatre (The Art Newspaper, 2024). The Edo state government's committee says the land was the former Benin Central Hospital site (NAN).
- The campus was costed at about $100 million including an endowment (The Art Newspaper).
- Public opening set for 11 November 2025 was postponed indefinitely after the 9 November preview was disrupted; the museum continues research and archaeology work (Apollo).
- Visiting: by appointment only, by email to info@wearemowaa.org (museum site).

FUNDING
MOWAA: mixed: governments, foundations and Nigerian corporate and individual donors, with ownership and the state's share disputed
- Founding partners (the museum's support page, undated): Edo State Government, Germany's Federal Foreign Office, Mellon Foundation, PI Trust, A. G. Leventis Foundation, British Museum.
- Mellon Foundation: $3 million for training programmes (The Art Newspaper).
- Raised about $20 million by November 2024 from Edo State, the German and Nigerian governments, Gerda Henkel Foundation, GIZ, Open Society Foundation and Ford Foundation (The Art Newspaper). Apollo puts the total at more than $25 million.
- Senior Leadership Circle includes Ford Foundation and Gerda Henkel Foundation; Leadership Circle includes Getty Foundation, the Embassies of Denmark and France and White Cube (support page).
- Edo State figures from the Oshiomhole committee set up by Governor Okpebholo: N3.8 billion spent by the Obaseki administration; Germany €4 million; Leventis $1.5 million (ThisDay, NAN). MOWAA management said it received more than $18 million (ThisDay).
- No reporting found of audited accounts. The museum names donors by tier without years.

Red flags: fossil fuel donors
- Leadership Circle donors include NNPC Seplat Energy, Lekoil Nigeria and Azura Power West Africa (support page, undated).
- The sources opened give no gift amounts or years for these three.

Red flags: protest and dispute with the Oba of Benin
- 9 November 2025: about 40 protesters loyal to Oba Ewuare II stormed the preview of "Nigeria Imaginary: Homecoming" (Apollo).
- The Oba had asked the governor to halt the opening, citing "greed, deceit, mischief and lack of transparency" (Apollo).
- 10 November 2025: Governor Monday Okpebholo announced revocation of the land's Certificate of Occupancy, citing "overriding public interest" and doubts about ownership and governance (Museums Association). Apollo reports his office later called some reports of the revocation a "social media scam".
- 22 December 2025: the Edo Assembly urged a state takeover (NAN). 20 August 2026: the Oshiomhole committee recommended renaming it the Benin Royal Museum of African Art and handing it to the Oba (NAN).
- Unverified: the Oba's circle says donors were misled (Apollo); MOWAA denies it presented itself as the Benin Royal Museum to raise money (Museums Association).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage concerns the political and ownership row, not the art or the architecture. The Apollo piece is sympathetic to MOWAA while recording missed warning signs.
1. Apollo: Barnaby Phillips, "What has gone wrong at the Museum of West African Art?" (2025)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=1036066
- The crisis grew from a long rivalry between the Oba's circle and the museum's backers, and warning signs were missed.
- Setting MOWAA up outside the National Commission for Museums and Monuments reassured European donors and alienated the Oba.
- Records that Germany gave millions of euros and that the Oba's circle claims donors were misled.
2. Museums Association (Museums Journal): Geraldine Kendall Adams, "Political row delays opening of Nigeria's Museum of West African Art" (2025)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2025/11/political-row-delays-opening-of-nigerias-museum-of-west-african-art/
- Reports local and traditional-ruler views that the project was taken over, and the accusation that it presented itself as the Benin Royal Museum to attract funding.
- The museum denies the accusation and says it has no claim to any Benin Bronzes. News report, not an essay.

3. BusinessDay NG (Nigerian press, not art media): Chido Nwakanma, "Social Listening 19 November 2025" (2025)
URL: https://businessday.ng/life-arts/article/social-listening-19-november-2025/
- Critics say shortening the name from EMOWAA to MOWAA distanced the project from state and traditional oversight.
- Raises an unresolved accountability question about the value of the state land given to the project.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a mix of European and Nigerian state funds, large foundations and named Nigerian corporate and individual donors, including three energy companies. The weak points are the disputed ownership and land, the state's N3.8 billion claim that MOWAA management disputes, and the absence of published accounts. Worth watching: the Edo white paper on the Oshiomhole report and the presidential committee's decision.
