THE BOX: Plymouth, UK
ID: the-box-plymouth
Website: https://www.theboxplymouth.com/ (checked 2026-10-24; the /supporters, /opening-hours and /access pages opened; /about-us and /visit returned "not found")

OVERVIEW
- Museum, gallery and archive on Tavistock Place in Plymouth city centre, free to enter.
- Opened on 29 September 2020, four months later than planned because of Covid-19 (The Art Newspaper, Blooloop). It replaced Plymouth City Museum and Art Gallery, which closed in September 2016 for redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- The museum was council-owned and its opening exhibition was "Mayflower 400: Legend and Legacy" (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- Combines the 1907 to 1910 Edwardian Baroque museum by Thornely and Rooke, the former Central Library and St Luke's Church, with a new extension designed by Atkins (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper reports a cantilevered building and does not name the architect.
- Nine permanent collection galleries, the Record Office, and the region's photographic, film and TV archives; a 6m concrete sculpture by Alexandre da Cunha in the foyer and a stained glass window by Leonor Antunes (The Art Newspaper). Fourteen restored figureheads hang in the atrium (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, plus selected bank holidays; entry is free, with free tickets to be booked for some exhibitions. Step-free entry, lifts to all public areas, a Changing Places toilet, free loan of wheelchairs and a mobility scooter, hearing loops and subtitles.

FUNDING
The Box: mainly public: Plymouth City Council, Lottery and Arts Council funding, with a defence-contractor gallery sponsor
- Total cost £48m; National Lottery Heritage Fund almost £16m, Arts Council England more than £4m, Plymouth City Council about half of the opening costs plus an annual £1.4m subsidy (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- The museum's supporters page lists Founding Funders: Plymouth City Council, National Lottery Heritage Fund, Arts Council England, Coastal Communities Fund, Garfield Weston Foundation, Wolfson Foundation, University of Plymouth, a legacy from Norman Hine, EU Interreg MMIAH and Mayflower 400. It gives no amounts or years.
- Principal Partners: Plymouth City Council, University of Plymouth, South West Film and Television Archive, South West Image Bank (Plymouth Barbican Trust). Partners include the British Film Institute, BBC, Ministry of Defence, National Museum of the Royal Navy and Devonport Naval Heritage Centre.
- Sponsors: Babcock International (Gallery Partner, Port of Plymouth gallery), Plymouth Citybus and Willmott Dixon (figureheads), CityFibre (The Box on Screen), Art Fund (engagement and acquisitions). Patrons: Hilary Lade and Mark Wilson. Wikipedia adds the Wolfson Foundation, Art Fund and V&A Purchase Grant Fund as grant givers.
- No reporting found of the value or length of the Babcock deal; the museum names Babcock as its first major Gallery Partner and gives no amount.

Red flags: Babcock International, a defence contractor, sponsors the Port of Plymouth gallery
- Babcock supports and maintains the Vanguard-class submarines that carry Trident missiles and is involved in designing the Dreadnought submarines, including the weapons handling and launch system. Its 2025 revenue was £4.8bn, 74% from defence clients (Campaign Against Arms Trade profile, updated 28 April 2026, an advocacy source).
- Babcock provides support services to Royal Navy bases at Devonport, the dockyard in Plymouth (CAAT).
- The Ministry of Defence, the National Museum of the Royal Navy and Devonport Naval Heritage Centre are listed as partners.
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or campaign aimed at Babcock's sponsorship at The Box.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution. Coverage in The Art Newspaper and archaeology magazines is descriptive or admiring, and no piece found takes up the Babcock sponsorship or the council subsidy.
1. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Inside The Box: Plymouth's £48m bid for cultural revival" (2020)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/news/inside-the-box-plymouth-s-gbp48m-bid-for-cultural-revival
- Reports no outside criticism. Concerns come from the museum itself: Covid disrupted construction and loans, some US loans were missing, and Mayflower anniversary events were scaled back.
- Gallery displays were revised late to address Elizabethan figures' role in the English slave trade; the article says the displays are not "a finished picture".
2. World Archaeology: staff byline, review of "Mayflower 400: Legend & Legacy" (2020)
URL: https://the-past.com/review/museum/mayflower-400-legend-legacy/
- Not criticism: presents the exhibition as a corrective to Mayflower myths and praises the partnership with the Wampanoag Advisory Committee.
- Criticises the myths, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The building was paid for mostly with public money (council, Lottery, Arts Council) and trusts, with no reporting found of anything wrong with those sources. The weak point is the Babcock gallery sponsorship: a company with majority defence revenue sponsors the Port of Plymouth gallery, and the deal's size is not published. Watch for the sponsorship terms.
