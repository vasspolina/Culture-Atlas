KHALIL SAKAKINI CULTURAL CENTER: Ramallah, Palestine
ID: khalil-sakakini-cultural-center-ramallah
Website: https://www.sakakini.org

OVERVIEW
- Palestinian arts and culture centre in Ramallah, housed in a renovated early-20th-century building in traditional Palestinian style (stated by the centre).
- Established in May 1996 as a branch of the Palestinian Ministry of Culture and registered as a non-profit NGO in 1998 (stated by the centre).
- Named for Khalil Sakakini, described by Wikipedia as a Jerusalemite scholar, poet and nationalist.
- Programmes listed on its about page: Visual Art, Literature, In Reach and Land.
- During Israeli-imposed curfews the centre moved some activities to Birzeit, Gaza City and Bethlehem (stated by the centre).
- Directors: Adila Laïdi-Hanieh was the first director (1996 to 2005, per Wikipedia); Yazan Khalili directed the centre from 2015 to 2019 (Makhzin interview, 2020).

FUNDING
Khalil Sakakini Cultural Center: donor-funded: international grants and foundation support
- In an interview published in Makhzin issue 3 (2020, interview by Rayya Badran, conducted in June 2019), Khalili described the centre's longevity and sustainability as "at the mercy of foreign aid", said private funders' engagement with Palestine had varied and more recently "came to a complete halt", and said the centre had not benefited from public cultural or funding institutions in Palestine.
- The Munib & Angela Masri Foundation says on its website that it established the Munib Rashid Masri Hall at the centre and provides funding that lets the centre host events such as exhibitions, concerts and poetry evenings.
- In 2004 Am Johal wrote in Scoop News ("Culture and Dissent in Palestine", 23 September 2004) that the centre had at different times received funding from the Japanese government, the UN Development Program, the Ford Foundation, the European Union and Dutch donors.
- The centre invites donations through its "Be Our Backbone" initiative and a "Sakakini Global Network" donation page (sakakini.org).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Jadaliyya (13 April 2022, a partisan pro-Palestinian outlet) reports that on 12 January 2022 a DIE ZEIT editor asked "Does the Documenta have an anti-Semitism problem?" and made allegations against Yazan Khalili, spokesman of The Question of Funding, tying the group in part to the Khalil Sakakini Cultural Center and calling Sakakini a "radical nationalist and Nazi supporter". Jadaliyya also reports that the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung published an article on 14 January 2022 about "Hetzkunst" (translation: incitement art) to be shown at documenta fifteen.
- Unverified: Ruhrbarone and a Dortmund website claimed that Sakakini supported National Socialism, from Jadaliyya, which says the claim feeds the newspaper columns and argues that his diaries do not support it.
- Connection: Khalili directed the centre from 2015 to 2019 (Jadaliyya, Makhzin interview). The ZEIT editor tied his group to the centre and to its namesake.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre has been funded through international grants and foundations, with the Munib & Angela Masri Foundation supporting its hall, and its former director says it received no help from Palestinian public cultural institutions.
