BULEGOA Z/B: Bilbao, Spain
ID: bulegoa-zb-bilbao
Website: https://bulegoa.org

OVERVIEW
- Independent "office for art and knowledge" at Solokoetxe 8 bajo, in the Solokoetxe district of Bilbao.
- Started in 2010; the founding team included Isabel de Naverán, who worked with it until 2018. Current core members are Beatriz Cavia, Miren Jaio and Leire Vergara, with a board of advisers (stated by the space).
- Full name: "Bulegoa zenbaki barik", shortened to Bulegoa z/b.
- Works on critical pedagogy, situated knowledge, collaborative methods, feminism and decolonial perspectives, through seminars, screenings, performances, talks and artistic projects (stated by the space).
- Programme lines on the site include Cine_ilegal, a film series (session 125 is listed for 12 November 2026), Formas de conocimiento informe, Producciones de una pared and open-call residencies.
- Partner of the Dutch Art Institute from 2017 to 2025; contributed to eight COOP study groups in the "Curating Positions" series, one per academic year (Dutch Art Institute).

FUNDING
Bulegoa z/b: public: Basque Government, Bizkaia Provincial Council, Bilbao City Council, Spain's Ministry of Culture and Sports, and the EU's Creative Europe programme
- The space's own pages list "With the support of" the Basque Government (Eusko Jaurlaritza), the Diputación Foral de Bizkaia, Bilbao City Council, the Creative Europe Programme of the European Union and the Ministry of Culture and Sports, without years or amounts.
- A 2020 post on the space's site says it was given the support of the Foundation for Arts Initiatives for a new project running from 2020 to 2023 (stated by the space).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is public culture funding from Basque, provincial, city, Spanish and EU bodies, plus one foundation's support for a 2020 to 2023 project. A 2026 article in Arte, Individuo y Sociedad by Irantzu Sanzo-San Martín and Concepción Elorza-Ibáñez de Gauna chooses the space as a case study of independent Bilbao initiatives that respond to the gentrification that followed the "Guggenheim effect"; its abstract says the space criticises the cultural, artistic and academic system.
