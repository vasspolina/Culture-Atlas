NATIONAL MUSEUM IN KRAKÓW: Kraków, Poland
ID: national-museum-in-krakow
Website: https://mnk.pl/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Poland's national art museum in Kraków, with its Main Building at al. 3 Maja 1 and branches that include the Sukiennice gallery, the Czartoryski Museum and the Arsenal.
- Established by a resolution of the Kraków City Council on 7 October 1879, linked to the opening of the renovated Cloth Hall on the Main Market Square.
- Henryk Siemiradzki's gift of the painting "Nero's Torches" became item number one in the inventory.
- The Czartoryski Museum branch shows Leonardo da Vinci's "Lady with an Ermine" (Radio Kraków).
- More than 2.2 million people visited the branches in 2025, after 1.8 million in 2024, 1.6 million in 2023 and 1.4 million in 2022 (Radio Kraków, 5 January 2026).
- The Main Building is open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. Tickets are PLN 45, reduced PLN 35, family PLN 90, and permanent exhibitions are free on Tuesdays.

FUNDING
National Museum in Kraków: mainly public: the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage, with state-controlled companies as patron and partner
- The Ministry of Culture and National Heritage pays the museum's operating subsidy. In June 2016, under minister Piotr Gliński, it raised the subsidy by PLN 1.2 million for staff pay, and the Solidarność union withdrew a collective dispute (Radio Kraków, 15 July 2016).
- The museum's site shows PGE as "Patron MNK" and PZU as one of two "Strategic Partners of MNK".
- The European Union's logo also appears on the site.

Red flags: a coal-based power group as patron
- PGE, Polska Grupa Energetyczna, is largely controlled by the Polish State Treasury (Wikipedia).
- Its power stations are fuelled mainly by hard coal and lignite, it runs two large lignite mines, and its Bełchatów plant is described as the fifth-largest coal-fired power station in the world (Wikipedia).
- In 2025 PGE generated 55.20 TWh of electricity, 28.50 TWh of it from lignite and 14.19 TWh from hard coal (Enerad, 16 April 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The state pays for the museum and that money is not a flag. The weak point is the patron: Poland's state-controlled power group, whose plants burn mainly coal and lignite. Worth watching: how much the patron and PZU give.
