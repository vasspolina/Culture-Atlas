MUNCH: Oslo, Norway
ID: munch-oslo
Website: https://www.munch.no (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum dedicated to Edvard Munch, in the Bjorvika waterfront district of Oslo at Edvard Munchs plass 1, with 13 floors of galleries and performance spaces (MUNCH website).
- Owned by Oslo municipality; the original Munch Museum at Toyen opened in 1963, the centenary of Munch's birth, and the new building opened on 22 October 2021, opened by King Harald V (Wikipedia).
- Named for Edvard Munch, who left his works to the city of Oslo at his death; the collection holds almost 28,000 artworks and more than 42,000 objects (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Juan Herreros and estudio Herreros, winners of a 2008 competition decided in 2009; construction began in September 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Tone Hansen is director (INPEX, 2023). The museum counted 746,146 visitors with tickets in 2024 (annual report 2024).
- Open Sunday to Tuesday 10:00 to 18:00 and Wednesday to Saturday 10:00 to 21:00 (MUNCH website). No reporting found of current admission prices or accessibility details on the pages read.

FUNDING
MUNCH: mainly public: Oslo municipality, with sponsors and own earnings
- In 2024 operating expenses were NOK 517.8 million and operating income NOK 190.9 million, of which sales NOK 153.1 million, leaving a net cost of NOK 326.9 million in the city's budget (annual report 2024).
- Annual report 2024: main sponsors carried into 2025 are INPEX Idemitsu Petroleum Norge, Canica, Viking Cruises and Deloitte, with Bergesenstiftelsen, Sparebankstiftelsen DNB and Talent Norge as supporters; its thanks list also names Aker BP, Multiconsult and YARA. The museum's sponsor page lists Idemitsu (under an INPEX Idemitsu Norge logo), Canica, Viking Cruises, Deloitte and those three supporters, with no years or amounts.
- Grant partners in 2024: Fritt Ord, the Norwegian Arts Council and Stiftelsen UNI; project support from the Asbjorn Lunde Foundation, White Cube and KLP (annual report 2024).
- Sparebankstiftelsen DNB gave NOK 15 million in 2020 for digital projects (annual report 2024).
- The museum did not disclose sponsorship amounts: Kunstkritikk's request for the INPEX Idemitsu and Aker BP agreements was rejected in 2021, and the 2020 annual report showed own revenue covering about 19.6% of operating costs (Kunstkritikk, 2021).
- No reporting found of annual amounts per sponsor.

Red flags: oil sponsorship
- INPEX Idemitsu Norge, through its predecessor Idemitsu Petroleum Norge, has sponsored the museum for more than 30 years; the company says it has contributed more than NOK 100 million since 1991. The agreement was renewed for five years, with a signing ceremony on 1 February 2023 (INPEX press release, 7 February 2023). The release states no start year for the sponsorship and no end year for the renewal.
- Kunstkritikk (19 November 2021) named Aker BP as a sponsor alongside Idemitsu; Aker BP is still thanked in the 2024 annual report. Equinor is not a direct sponsor but funds the Edvard Munch Award, a NOK 500,000 prize presented in 2015, 2017 and 2019.
- Protest: on 15 November 2021 the group Direct Action Theatre projected "Munch has blood on his hands" and the logos of Idemitsu, Aker BP and Equinor onto the museum facade (Kunstkritikk, 19 November 2021).
- The museum's management did not answer Kunstkritikk's questions about its dependence on the sponsors (Kunstkritikk, 2021).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism in art media is limited to two Kunstkritikk pieces on the oil sponsorship, from 2021 and 2022. No criticism of the programme was found.
1. Kunstkritikk: Mariann Enge, "Action Against Oil Sponsorship" (2021)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/action-against-oil-sponsorship/
- Reports the 15 November 2021 projection and a performance later that evening, part of the group's "Serve and Protect" series.
- Names Idemitsu and Aker BP as sponsors and Equinor as funder of the Munch Award; the museum refused to share the agreements.
2. Kunstkritikk: Mariann Enge, "Museums Must Become Active Allies in the Climate Struggle" (20 December 2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/museums-must-become-active-allies-in-the-climate-struggle/
- Argues museums should cut ties with fossil fuel sponsors, since sponsors seek honour and legitimacy in return.
- Notes Swedish and Danish museum heads (Moderna Museet, SMK) said they would not accept such sponsorship, while Norwegian directors said refusing would mean turning down state support; names Aker BP and Idemitsu as MUNCH sponsors.
- The Munch Museum did not respond to Kunstkritikk's queries.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city-owned institution paid for mostly by Oslo municipality, with a long, named oil sponsor in INPEX Idemitsu, whose gifts exceed NOK 100 million since 1991, and Aker BP. The weak points are the oil sponsorship, the refusal to disclose agreements and the unclear end year of the renewal. Worth watching: whether the museum publishes sponsor amounts and what it does when the 2023 renewal ends.
