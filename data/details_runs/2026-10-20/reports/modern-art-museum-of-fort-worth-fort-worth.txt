MODERN ART MUSEUM OF FORT WORTH: Fort Worth, USA
ID: modern-art-museum-of-fort-worth-fort-worth
Website: https://www.themodern.org/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of international modern and contemporary art in the Cultural District of Fort Worth, Texas, known as The Modern (Wikipedia).
- First chartered in 1892 as the Fort Worth Public Library and Art Gallery; later names included Fort Worth Art Museum, Fort Worth Art Center and Fort Worth Museum of Art (Wikipedia).
- Current building designed by the Japanese architect Tadao Ando, opened on 14 December 2002: three long pavilions set into a reflecting pond with about 53,000 square feet of gallery space (Wikipedia).
- Collection of more than 3,000 works, mostly from 1945 onward, including Picasso, Pollock, Rothko and Warhol (Wikipedia).
- Closed Mondays; open 10 am to 5 pm, Friday until 8 pm. Admission is $16 for adults, $12 for seniors, military and first responders, $10 for students, free under 18; Fridays are free and Sundays half price; free for up to four people with a SNAP EBT card. Galleries are closed 29 September to 30 October 2026 (museum website).

Richardson Foundation, Terrell and Patsy Small Family Foundation, Texas Commission on the Arts and Three G Foundation.
- Sid W. Richardson Foundation: set up in 1947 by Sid W. Richardson, who earned his fortune in the oil industry (Wikipedia). The foundation's own site says its grantmaking began in 1962, when income from assets of Richardson's estate became available. It had about $526.8 million in total assets in December 2024 and gave the Modern $750,000 for operational support in 2017 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: police seizure of Sally Mann photographs
- The Fort Worth Police Department seized Sally Mann photographs from the exhibition "Diaries of Home" by 7 January 2025 (Cardinal News, Charles Paullin, 27 March 2025). The museum's statement referred to four works (The Art Newspaper, Elena Goukassian, 9 January 2025).
- Complaint: The Art Newspaper reports that a complaint said the images show naked children and could be seen as pornographic, that a police report was filed against the museum and that the first story came from The Dallas Express on 23 December 2024. FIRE says several local officials complained in December 2024. A Tarrant County judge posted on X on 8 January 2025 urging police to investigate (Cardinal News).
- Museum: a spokesperson said the photographs had been published and exhibited for more than 30 years in leading cultural institutions (The Art Newspaper). The museum's attorney, Michael Anderson, thanked the grand jury for its review (Cardinal News).
- Outcome: a Tarrant County grand jury declined to take action against museum employees on 25 March 2025, and police said the photographs would be returned (Cardinal News); FIRE reports that police closed the case against Mann and the museum and returned the artwork in April 2025.
- The National Coalition Against Censorship and Artists At Risk Connection condemned the seizure (The Art Newspaper).
- Funding link: the museum's Foundation Support page lists the City of Fort Worth among its 2025-2026 funders; the Fort Worth Police Department made the seizure.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's listed funders are Texas foundations, bank charitable foundations, donor-advised funds and city and state arts bodies. One of them, the Sid W. Richardson Foundation, was set up in 1947 by an oilman, and its own site dates its grantmaking from his estate's income in 1962. In January 2025 city police seized Sally Mann photographs from a museum exhibition; the grand jury declined to take action in March 2025, and the City of Fort Worth is a listed funder.
