SURSOCK MUSEUM: Beirut, Lebanon
ID: sursock-museum
Website: https://sursock.museum (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in the Ashrafieh district of Beirut, forty metres from the top of the Saint Nicolas stairs, a few steps from Gemmayzeh Street (stated by the museum).
- Housed in the villa Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock built in 1912; on his death in 1952 he left it to the citizens of Lebanon as an art museum (waqf, a trust) (Wikipedia, stated by the museum).
- Nine years passed before the will was carried out: President Camille Chamoun turned the villa into a guesthouse for visiting heads of state. The museum opened on 18 November 1961 with the Salon d'Automne (stated by the museum).
- Closed in 2008 for renovation and expansion by Jean-Michel Wilmotte and Jacques Abou Khaled that raised the floor area from 1,500 to 8,500 square metres; reopened on 8 October 2015 (Wikipedia, stated by the museum).
- On site: galleries, a library and archives, a store and a restaurant.
- The 4 August 2020 port explosion, 800 metres away, severely damaged it; it reopened on 26 May 2023 and again on 20 December 2024 after a closure during the Israel-Lebanon conflict (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm; all galleries and facilities are wheelchair accessible; school visits are free (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Sursock Museum: donor-funded: Lebanese patrons, Friends memberships, foreign government and heritage grants
- Patrons in 2026: the Robert A. Matta Foundation and Kamal F. Tabet. Donors in 2026: Nicolas and Michele Garzouzi, the Alexis and Anne-Marie Habib Foundation, Philippe and Zaza Jabre, Omar and Ghalia Al Qattan. Friends of the Museum are tiered Platinum, Gold and Silver; the page gives no amounts (stated by the museum).
- 2026 operational sponsors: Commercial Insurance, Tinol, Mitsulift. Energy sponsors: Energy Assistance, Fondation CMA CGM, Pierre Dammous & Partners and Fondation Engie. 2025 grants: the Italian development agency and the Institut francais du Liban.
- After the 2020 explosion the museum raised 2,376,751 US dollars of a 3 million dollar reconstruction estimate: 2,086,031 from grants, 58,231 from institutions, 152,591 from fundraisers, 40,000 from corporations and 39,897 from individuals (stated by the museum).
- Grants named: ALIPH 500,000 dollars; French Ministry of Culture 500,000 euros; Government of Italy and UNESCO 965,000 dollars; Goethe-Institut 10,000 dollars; Arab Fund for Arts and Culture and Culture Resource 40,000 dollars. Corporate donor: DHL.
- No reporting found of a Lebanese state or municipal operating grant, or of published financial statements.

Red flags: Fossil fuel sponsor
- Fondation Engie is listed as an energy sponsor in 2026. Engie is a French utility that runs Europe's largest gas distribution network and one of the world's largest LNG portfolios; in 2018 natural gas made up 55 percent of its installed capacity (Wikipedia).
- The museum gives no amount or start year for the Engie support.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Shaikha Mai bint Mohammed Al-Khalifa, President of the Bahrain Authority for Culture and Antiquities, raised 98,231 dollars for the museum on behalf of the Arab Regional Centre for World Heritage, which the museum lists as an institutional donor for 2020 to 2022 (stated by the museum).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Sursock is mostly Lebanese private patrons and foreign heritage grants, with no state operating funder named. The weak points are Fondation Engie, a gas utility's foundation, and the Bahrain Authority for Culture and Antiquities fundraising of 98,231 dollars after the explosion. Worth watching: the missing financial statements and the size of the energy sponsorships.
