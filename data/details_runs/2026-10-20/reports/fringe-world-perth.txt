FRINGE WORLD: Perth, Australia
ID: fringe-world-perth
Website: https://fringeworld.com.au/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Open-access summer arts festival across Perth, run by ARTRAGE, a non-profit registered charity (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- ARTRAGE began in 1983 as the Festival Fringe Society of Perth; a Fringe pilot ran in February 2011, and the first full Fringe World in 2012 had more than 200 events in over 40 venues (stated by the space).
- About 100 venues in recent years; attendance was 592,000 in 2024 and 492,369 in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- 2026: the festival ran 21 January to 15 February and drew 487,416 attendees across free and ticketed events; $8,340,880 was paid to artists and venues in 2025/26 (ARTRAGE 2025/26 annual report).
- ARTRAGE also runs Rooftop Movies on the CPP Roe Street car park from October to March (ARTRAGE impact page).
- Visiting: the 2027 festival runs 20 January to 14 February, with ticketed and free events (stated by the space). No reporting found on accessibility.

FUNDING
Fringe World: earned income first, with WA state money and named sponsors
- Ticketing, food and drink and other earned income are more than 70% of ARTRAGE's revenue; unaudited 2026 revenue was $10.91 million (ARTRAGE 2025/26 annual report).
- State government: new multi-year investment of $4.3 million a year in the festival; government partners are Lotterywest (principal partner), the Department of Creative Industries, Tourism and Sport, and Tourism WA (ARTRAGE annual report).
- City of Perth: a three-year partnership for 2026 to 2028 (ARTRAGE annual report).
- 2026 sponsors: major partner K&L Gates, festival partner Pentanet, production partners Seamless AV and CCA Productions, and media partners including JCDecaux, PerthNow, Channel 7 and Nova 93.7 (Fringe World partners page).
- Board chair Noel Whitehead; CEO and creative director Jo Thomas (ARTRAGE annual report). ARTRAGE files with the ACNC.
- No reporting found of amounts paid by individual sponsors.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Woodside, ended)
- Woodside supported the festival from 2012 and was its principal partner in 2020 (Business News, 2020) and held naming rights for the festival hub until June 2021 (The Conversation; Wikipedia).
- After June 2021 ARTRAGE kept taking Woodside money as a direct sponsor; CEO Sharon Burgess said the organisation "is not in the business of making political statements" (The Conversation, 2021).
- Woodside ended its remaining sponsorship of ARTRAGE "in September", a company spokesperson told AAP, saying the decision was Woodside's. Perth Voice reported on 4 January 2024 that the final ties were cut and Woodside had gone from ARTRAGE's partner list.
- No Woodside or other fossil-fuel company is on the 2026 partner list (Fringe World partners page).

Red flags: protest over Woodside
- In January 2020 artists Ash Traylia, Petros Vouris and Noemie Huttner-Koros took the stage at the festival launch to read a protest against the Woodside partnership (Business News).
- Artists said a contract clause worked as a "gag order" against protest (The Conversation, 2021).
- Fossil Free Arts welcomed what it called Perth's first "fossil free" festival season after a five-year campaign; campaigner Anthony Collins said many WA artists stayed quiet about the sponsorships, fearing lost work (AAP).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is all about the Woodside sponsorship, from artists, campaigners and an academic; no criticism of the programme was found.
1. The Conversation: Jo Caust, "Principles or pragmatism: does it matter where arts sponsorship comes from?" (2021)
URL: https://theconversation.com/principles-or-pragmatism-does-it-matter-where-arts-sponsorship-comes-from-163430
- Argues the end of naming rights was a symbolic win because ARTRAGE kept Woodside funding.
- Says sponsorship from companies that contribute to climate harm or have harmed Indigenous lands has a real cost to an arts body's integrity.
2. AAP: Liz Hobday, "Festival season begins with fossil fuels on the fringe" (year not shown on the page)
URL: https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/festival-season-begins-with-fossil-fuels-on-the-fringe
- Reports the end of the deal and Woodside's line that it is "proud to be a global energy company".
- Names WA Ballet, WA Youth Orchestra and WA Symphony Orchestra as still Woodside-sponsored.

BOTTOM LINE
Fringe World now runs mostly on its own ticket and bar income, with WA state money through Lotterywest and the culture department, and a list of local sponsors with no fossil-fuel name on it. The weak point is history: Woodside money ran from 2012 until its final exit, reported in January 2024, and artists said they were gagged. Worth watching: whether any new energy sponsor joins.
