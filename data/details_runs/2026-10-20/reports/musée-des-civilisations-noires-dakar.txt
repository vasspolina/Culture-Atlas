MUSÉE DES CIVILISATIONS NOIRES: Dakar, Senegal
ID: musée-des-civilisations-noires-dakar
Website: https://mcn-sn.com

OVERVIEW
- National museum in Dakar-Plateau, covering about 14,000 square metres, with a design inspired by the round huts of Casamance and room for 18,000 pieces (AFP via RTBF, 7 December 2018); Wikipedia places it in Dakar-Plateau.
- Realises a vision of Senegal's first president Léopold Sédar Senghor for a museum on the histories and cultures of Black people (Wikipedia). Abdoulaye Wade laid the first stone; President Macky Sall inaugurated it on 6 December 2018, seven years later (AFP via RTBF; Mail & Guardian).
- Building: a Chinese contractor built it (Mail & Guardian). The original design came from the Mexican architect Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, contracted in the 1970s under Senghor, with UNESCO covering his fee. Curator Ery Camara sees a Chinese pagoda in the circular form while others see Wolof houses (Mail & Guardian).
- Opening exhibits (2018): the Toumaï skull, which came "exceptionally" from Chad, stone tools, megaliths more than 1,700 years old, Egyptian hieroglyphs, ritual masks and contemporary works including Abdoulaye Konaté's "Non au jihad à Tombouctou" (AFP via RTBF). The Mail & Guardian also reports Chinese masks among the inaugural exhibitions.
- Director: Hamady Bocoum at the 2018 opening (Wikipedia). Professor Mouhamed Abdalah Ly was the museum's director in 2025 (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, 14 February 2026) and announced his resignation as Director General on his Facebook page in May 2026 (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, 26 May 2026).
- 2025: 49,308 entries between 1 January and 31 December, of which 19,408 were school audiences; missions from China and Egypt chose the museum to celebrate their national days (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, Lamine Diedhiou, 14 February 2026).

FUNDING
Musée des Civilisations Noires: Senegalese national museum, building paid by China
- China financed the museum with more than 30 million euros (AFP via RTBF, 2018). The Mail & Guardian puts the figure at about $34 million, paid largely by Beijing.
- Own revenue reached 353.9 million FCFA in 2025, up 148% from 2024, from visits, rental of spaces and newly introduced merchandise (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, 14 February 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical report, written at the 2018 opening; later coverage is of results and management.
1. Mail & Guardian: Sean O Toole, "Senegalese artistic heritage gets a home" (20 December 2018)
URL: https://mg.co.za/friday/2018-12-20-senegalese-artistic-heritage-gets-a-home/
- Calls the museum principally a symbol and, in the piece's words, most vulnerable as a symbol. Camara says a museum needs investment in "soft infrastructure: people, training, networks, alliances and, most importantly, vision".
- Ery Camara says the seaside location makes climate control imperative and that Senegal has no professional conservators, so restoration work goes to Paris; he predicts a decline into obsolescence after the opening rush.
- Chika Okeke-Agulu links the project to "cultural recolonisation" and warns that new money can create "monstrosities and white elephants".
- Reports that the museum will not have any permanent displays, that its galleries were still empty two months before the opening, and that safety signs are in Chinese and English rather than Wolof or French.

BOTTOM LINE
The building was financed by China and the museum is a Senegalese national museum. Its own revenue was 353.9 million FCFA in 2025. The documented criticism concerns the Chinese-paid building, conservation capacity and empty galleries before the opening.
