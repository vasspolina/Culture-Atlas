UJAZDOWSKI CASTLE CCA: Warsaw, Poland
ID: ujazdowski-castle-cca-warsaw
Website: https://u-jazdowski.pl

OVERVIEW
- Centre for Contemporary Art in Ujazdowski Castle, Jazdów 2, Warsaw.
- The Centre has occupied the castle since 1985 (Wikipedia). It has organised over 600 exhibitions since 1990 (Wikipedia).
- A castle stood on the site from the 13th century; it was burnt out after the 1944 Warsaw Uprising and reconstruction to its 18th-century design was approved in 1975 (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions, a Project Room, a cinema, a library and bookshop, a Media Library, a collection, residencies, an education programme, a restaurant (Qchnia Artystyczna) and the Nobody Reads This art book fair (the Centre's website).
- Directors: Fabio Cavallucci from 2010 to 2016 (Wikipedia); Małgorzata Ludwisiak, whose five-year contract ran to December 2019 (Hyperallergic); Piotr Bernatowicz, picked by the Ministry of Culture for a seven-year term, who took up the post early in 2020 (Kunstkritikk).
- Visiting: exhibitions and bookshop open Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 19:00, Thursday until 20:00, closed Monday; admission is free on Thursdays; the cinema opens every day.

FUNDING
Ujazdowski Castle CCA: public cultural institution whose director is appointed by the Minister of Culture and National Heritage
- CIMAM calls it a public cultural institution and states that the Minister of Culture and National Heritage appointed Bernatowicz as its director (CIMAM, 27 November 2019).
- In November 2024 Minister Hanna Wróblewska announced a competition for the Centre's director (dzieje.pl, 19 November 2024).
- The website carries a page headed "Projects co-financed by the European Union".

Red flags: protest over the director's appointment and programme cancellations
- 6 November 2019 (Hyperallergic, Dorian Batycka): Minister of Culture and Heritage Piotr Gliński planned to nominate the new director without a public competition and stated his preference for Bernatowicz in a letter. A Polish-language online petition on naszademokracja.pl gathered over 2,500 signatures in its first weekend and an English version on change.org over 1,200; signatories included Charles Esche, Olga Tokarczuk, Lucy Lippard and Hal Foster.
- Two unions at the Centre, Inicjatywa Pracownicza and Solidarność, representing 44 curatorial and administrative staff, formally opposed the selection process and argued that Polish law requires their opinion on the choice of director (Hyperallergic).
- 27 November 2019: the Museum Watch Committee of the CIMAM Board asked for an open competition with a transparent jury of Polish and international professionals, while stating that the Ministry did not break existing rules (CIMAM).
- 16 March 2020 (Kunstkritikk, Robert Stasinski): the Polish Ministry of Culture hand-picked Bernatowicz. The article reports that PTV: Performance TV and a Miet Warlop exhibition produced with Kunst-Werke Berlin were cancelled, links the cancellations to the new management and cites budget cuts, which also hit the Centre-supported online journal Obieg. The collaboration with the Anti-fascist Year was cancelled after an 11 February 2020 seminar.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is concentrated in 2019 and 2020 and concerns the appointment of the director and the programme changes that followed, not the Centre's funding.
1. Hyperallergic: Dorian Batycka, "Polish Art Community Rebuffs Ministry of Culture's Controversial Choice for a Museum Director" (6 November 2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/polish-art-community-petition
- Reports that Minister Gliński stated his preference for Bernatowicz before any open call, and that Ludwisiak was told in August by letter from the Minister that her contract would not be renewed.
- The petition argues Bernatowicz has no relevant international experience, cites an open letter from 76 young artists against renewing his contract at the Arsenal Municipal Gallery in Poznań, and cites the 2015 exhibition "Strategies of Rebellion" at that gallery as misogynistic artwork.
2. Kunstkritikk: Robert Stasinski, "Ujazdowski Castle Takes a Right-Wing Turn" (16 March 2020)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/?p=156092
- Argues the Ministry hand-picked a relatively inexperienced art historian, and reports that the government was accused of undue political interference.
- Reports that Centre employees describe the atmosphere as "very difficult".
- Quotes Aneta Szylak, director of NOMUS in Gdansk, who calls this part of a larger project to convert institutions "for the people" into institutions "for the government".
3. CIMAM: Museum Watch Committee of the CIMAM Board, "A key institution for Polish cultural heritage of post-Cold War era under threat" (27 November 2019)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/key-institution-polish-cultural-heritage-post-cold-war-era-under-threat/
- Regrets that no competition was held and that artists and the institution's team were not consulted.
- Warns that the appointment could create tension in the art community and weaken the institution's authority.

BOTTOM LINE
The Centre is a public institution whose director is appointed by the Minister of Culture and National Heritage, with a page of EU co-financed projects on its website. The documented concern is the 2019 appointment of Bernatowicz without an open competition, the petition and union objections against it, and the programme cancellations reported in 2020.
