ACROPOLIS MUSEUM: Athens, Greece
ID: acropolis-museum
Website: https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en (checked 2026-11-20; refused the automated check with a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Archaeological museum at 15 Dionysiou Areopagitou, Athens, about 280 metres from the Parthenon, created to house the finds from the Sacred Rock of the Acropolis and its slopes (Greek National Tourism Organisation; Wikipedia).
- Opened on 20 June 2009, replacing a museum on the Acropolis completed in 1874 (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Bernard Tschumi Architects with Michael Photiadis of Athens, in glass, concrete and marble; Tschumi won the competition in 2001, after competitions in 1976, 1979 and 1989 produced no building (Dezeen; Wikipedia).
- The building stands over an excavated ancient Athenian neighbourhood, visible through glass floors, and has 14,000 square metres of exhibition space (Greek National Tourism Organisation; Yatzer).
- On show: the Gallery of the Acropolis Slopes, the Archaic Acropolis Gallery and, on the third level, the Parthenon Gallery with the frieze, metopes and pediments; pieces removed by Lord Elgin are shown as plaster copies (Greek National Tourism Organisation; The Art Newspaper).
- The General Director is Professor Nikolaos Stampolidis (Skai).
- 1,994,052 visitors in 2025, 38th in The Art Newspaper's list of the world's 100 most visited museums (Skai, 18 June 2026).
- Visiting: general admission 20 euros, reduced 10 euros; open daily 9am to 5pm from 1 November to 31 March, and until 8pm from Tuesday to Sunday in summer (Greek National Tourism Organisation).

FUNDING
Acropolis Museum: public: a Greek state legal entity, built with Greek state and European Union money
- Construction: the budget was 130 million euros, co-financed by the Hellenic Republic and the European Regional Development Fund; the client was the Organization for the Construction of the New Acropolis Museum (Yatzer; Dezeen).
- Status: the museum has been a legal entity under public law since it opened; a 2021 bill gave five other state museums the same status, with the aim of more financial autonomy and less state support (The Art Newspaper, 4 March 2021).
- Earned income: tickets, a ground-floor café and a second-floor restaurant (Greek National Tourism Organisation).
- Programme partner: Michael Rakowitz's "Lamassu of Nineveh", shown outside the museum until 31 October 2026, was curated by Stampolidis with Elina Kountouri, director of NEON (Skai), the organisation founded by the collector Dimitris Daskalopoulos (Protothema).

Red flags: 2009 cut of a Costa-Gavras film scene after a Church protest
- In July 2009 an animated scene was deleted from the film shown to visitors after the Greek Orthodox Church protested; it showed figures in black on ladders destroying part of the Parthenon frieze. The filmmaker Costa-Gavras demanded that his name be taken off the credits (Associated Press, 26 July 2009).
- In August 2009 the museum said it was reversing the cut, after days of picketing and the threat of a lawsuit (ArtsJournal, 5 August 2009).

Red flags: 2007 plan to demolish two listed buildings in front of the museum
- In 2007 the Greek government removed protection from two buildings at 17 and 19 Dionysiou Areopagitou, one Neo-Classical and one Art Deco, so that they could be demolished; INTBAU and ICOMOS protested (Wikipedia).
- The plan was dropped after reactions in Greece and abroad (Greek Wikipedia, citing The Sunday Times of 18 November 2007).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one critical piece criticises the museum's commercial model in passing, in a dispute about other Greek museums.

URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/03/04/archaeologists-appeal-to-greek-prime-minister-to-halt-restructuring-of-five-big-antiquities-museums
- Despina Koutsoumba, president of the Association of Greek Archaeologists, called the Acropolis Museum's model "only about tickets and restaurants".
- A letter of 23 February 2021 signed by 119 former archaeological service employees denounced "archaeocapitalism".
- Aimed at the bill and its five museums, not at the Acropolis Museum's management.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2026: The Daily Telegraph reported that talks between Greece and the British Museum over the Parthenon sculptures, which began in 2021, have stalled over ownership and over a long-term loan to Athens; no talks are under way (To Vima, 23 September 2026; iefimerida, 24 September 2026).
- British law allows only a loan, which would require Greece to recognise the British Museum's ownership, a condition Athens refuses; a "hybrid deal" exchanging long-term returns for rotating loans of other Greek objects also fell through (iefimerida).
- British Museum chairman George Osborne called the sculptures the institution's "most intractable problem" in December 2025 (To Vima); the British Museum says talks on a "new Parthenon Partnership" remain "constructive and ongoing" (iefimerida).
- The connection: Greece says the sculptures must be reunified at the Acropolis Museum, where the pieces in London are shown as plaster copies (iefimerida; Greek National Tourism Organisation).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a Greek state body, built with Greek and European Union public money. Its own disputes are about what it shows: in 2009 it cut a film scene after a Church protest, then restored it. Watch whether the talks over the Parthenon sculptures in London resume.
