ZABLUDOWICZ COLLECTION: London, UK
ID: zabludowicz-collection-london
Website: https://www.zabludowiczcollection.com/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art collection, founded in 1994; Poju Zabludowicz is founder and trustee (Wikipedia).
- Its London project space at 176 Prince of Wales Road, Chalk Farm, opened in 2007 in a former Methodist church and closed on 17 December 2023, after more than 100 free exhibitions and over 640 free events (The Art Newspaper).
- The collection now works through loans, commissions, residencies in Sarvisalo, Finland, and two mentoring programmes. The London building reopened in 2025 as Camden Arts Projects, a not-for-profit space (Time Out).
- Visiting: London viewing by email request; the New York space (1500 Broadway) is free, by appointment. No accessibility information.

FUNDING
Zabludowicz Collection: privately funded by the founders' family fortune through the Zabludowicz Art Trust
- The collection says all its activities are privately funded and it is principally owned by the Zabludowicz Art Trust; Zabludowicz Art Projects UK is charity number 1120067 (stated by the collection).
- The charity has "no regular source of income other than the donations" from the Art Trust; its income was £1,181,521 in 2019 and £1,114,769 in 2018 (The White Pube, citing Companies House filings).
- Poju Zabludowicz heads the holding company Tamares (Wikipedia), which sold its British Israel Property operation for $700m in 2011; the couple's 2022 net worth was £1.495bn (The Art Newspaper, citing The Sunday Times).
- His father, Shlomo Zabludowicz, built the family business around the Israeli defence contractor Soltam (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of any public funder, corporate sponsor or outside donor.

Red flags: boycott and protest over the founders' ties to Israel
- Boycott Divest Zabludowicz (BDZ), an anonymous group of artists and activists founded in 2014, urges a boycott (Hyperallergic, Wikipedia).
- 18 May 2021: Hyperallergic reported BDZ's call had more than 600 signatories and urged artists to withdraw ("de-author") work held by the collection. The collection's spokesperson called the petition "misinformed and upsetting".
- 26 July 2021: 25 artists and cultural workers sent signed letters to the Art Trust and Zabludowicz Art Projects withdrawing their work (Hyperallergic).
- The founders' statement of 21 May 2021 welcomed the ceasefire and backed a two-state solution (Hyperallergic). The collection said the 2023 closure was unrelated to the boycott or the war in Gaza (Time Out, 14 May 2025).

Red flags: arms and defence links in the family business
- Hyperallergic (2021) states that Poju Zabludowicz is CEO of Tamares Group, which holds a stake in Knafaim, the ultimate beneficiary of Maintenance Wings Limited Partnership, which provides military aircraft maintenance to the Israeli Air Force. No page opened gives the stake's size or shows it after 2021.
- Hyperallergic (May 2021) states that his father, Shlomo, founded Soltam Systems, which supplies weapons to the Israeli military and the US Army; Wikipedia says Shlomo built the family business around Soltam. Hyperallergic (July 2021) cites a 2013 University of Bath report that the lobby group BICOM, founded by Poju, was financed by his father's fortune.
- No page opened shows arms money paying for the Art Trust or the collection. Wikipedia describes Tamares as a $3 billion portfolio owning about 40% of downtown Las Vegas land.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is tied to the boycott campaign begun in 2014 and aimed at the founders' business and political ties, not the exhibitions.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "600+ Artists and Organizations Urge Boycott of Zabludowicz Art Trust Over Israel Ties" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/646541/artists-boycott-the-zabludowicz-art-trust-over-israel-ties/
- Reports BDZ's call (18 May 2021) to withdraw work, citing BICOM, the Knafaim stake, a West Bank shopping centre (from a 2009 Channel 4 Dispatches episode) and Soltam. "Artwashing" is BDZ's claim.
- Names signatories including Eyal Weizman and Claire Bishop.
2. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Citing Pro-Israel Ties, 25 Artists Disaffiliate From London Collection" (26 July 2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/665123/citing-pro-israel-ties-artists-disaffiliate-from-zabludowicz-collection/
- Reports 25 artists and cultural workers withdrawing work on 26 July 2021 and a BDZ member saying the aim is for the Trust to divest from the Israeli state's policies.
- Names Michelle Williams Gamaker, Harold Offeh, Amalia Pica and Jack Strange.
3. The White Pube: signed "ZM", "Boycott Zabludowicz" (20 June 2021)
URL: https://thewhitepube.co.uk/art-thoughts/bdz/
- Argues the collection is an "artwashing" front for money from arms dealing and West Bank property; this is the essay's own claim.
- Names Poju, Anita, Shlomo and Tiffany Zabludowicz and director Elizabeth Neilson. An openly partisan essay.

BOTTOM LINE
The collection runs on the founding family's private fortune; no outside funder was found. The weak points are the family business's links to Soltam and Knafaim (Hyperallergic, 2021), which no page opened ties directly to the collection's funding, and the boycott running since 2014. Watch the Art Trust's donations to the charity.
