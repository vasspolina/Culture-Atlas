RAK ART FOUNDATION: Riffa, Bahrain
ID: rak-art-foundation-ras-al-khaimah
Website: https://www.rakartfoundation.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art foundation in Riffa, in the centre of Bahrain, housed in the converted family home where its founder grew up (foundation website, Identity).
- Its founder, Shaikh Rashid bin Khalifa Al Khalifa, born in Riffa in 1952, is an artist, collector and patron who founded the Bahrain Arts Society in 1983 (foundation website) and a member of the royal family and cousin of the ruler (Identity, The Art Newspaper, 2018).
- He held the government posts of undersecretary for tourism and culture and for immigration, passports and nationality (The Art Newspaper, 2018). In November 2021 a royal decree named him chairman of the new National Council for Arts, a body attached to the Cabinet (Daily Tribune, 16 November 2021).
- Launched in 2020 as the Rashid Al Khalifa Art Foundation and opened to the public by appointment (Identity, Artribune); a white-cube gallery extension was completed in 2020 (foundation website).
- The house dates from the 1920s with a 1960s extension holding his childhood bedroom; he acquired it from his family in 2014 and restored and extended it with the Italian architect Davide Chiaverini, adding a two-storey courtyard pavilion with steel mashrabiya panels (Identity).
- The collection holds hundreds of works of fine and decorative art (foundation website), including Pierre Soulages, Damien Hirst, Fernando Botero, Mohammed Melehi and Fateh Al Moudarres (Identity).
- Programmes: exhibitions, The Art Station in Muharraq, and the RAK Art Foundation Prize, awarded at the ASIA NOW fair in Paris in October 2025 with a one-month residency at The Art Station (foundation website).
- Monocle reported in May 2025 that the foundation was preparing the Daima Museum of Middle East and North African Art, due to open that December, with Buckminster Fuller-designed domes to house the Bahrain headquarters of the United Nations Industrial Development Organization.
- Yasmin Sharabi is director (Artribune, Monocle).
- Visiting: private tours by booking; the house is private property (foundation website).

FUNDING
RAK Art Foundation: founder-funded non-profit: the collection and home of a member of Bahrain's royal family, with a US Embassy grant for one residency
- The foundation calls itself a non-profit and occupies a house the founder owns (foundation website, Identity).
- Its director told Artribune that Shaikh Rashid set up the foundation in his own name to give something back to his country (Artribune, 2023, paraphrased from Italian).
- The 2024 Art Station residency for US citizens (7 March to 30 May 2024, with housing, a monthly stipend of 1,500 USD and a 5,300 USD production grant) was "funded by the US Embassy in Manama" (e-flux announcement, 20 January 2024).
- Monocle states that Shaikh Rashid founded The Art Station, whose studios for artists are subsidised by the organisation (Monocle, May 2025).

Not related: Ras Al Khaimah Art (RAK Art), United Arab Emirates. The Al Qasimi Foundation runs the Ras Al Khaimah Art Festival, founded in 2013, and funds its residency and film grants; it is a different organisation in a different country and shares only the abbreviation "RAK" (Al Qasimi Foundation, Ras Al Khaimah Art website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is admiring; the one critical note is a 2018 comment on the founder as a royal artist, written before the foundation opened.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, "Why being a royal artist in the Gulf is a mixed blessing" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/10/30/why-being-a-royal-artist-in-the-gulf-is-a-mixed-blessing
- Argues that his title guarantees him respectful shows in the region but anchors him to his small state, and that Gulf artists lack the "frank criticism" of a diverse society.
- Notes that he held government posts while practising his art, which the author calls both an advantage and a disadvantage.
- Does not name the foundation, which opened in 2020; comments on the founder's position more than his art.

BOTTOM LINE
Private royal money with no flagged funder; its closeness to the Bahraini state runs through its founder rather than through any state grant.
