INSTITUTE OF MODERN ART: Brisbane, Australia
ID: institute-of-modern-art
Website: https://ima.org.au (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art gallery on the ground floor of the Judith Wright Arts Centre, 420 Brunswick Street, Fortitude Valley, Brisbane; it shows temporary exhibitions and holds no permanent collection (IMA; Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1975; the IMA calls itself Australia's oldest independent public art gallery. Robert Jadin de Fronenteau was the first director (Wikipedia).
- No earlier name or namesake is given in the sources opened.
- The gallery became a Resident Cultural Organisation at the Judith Wright Arts Centre in 2001, and the Berlin-based Studio Miessen refurbished its public areas in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- It runs exhibitions, including the Platform series that commissions emerging Queensland artists, and an annual gala first launched in 2015 for its 40th anniversary (IMA).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, entry free (IMA). No reporting found of an accessibility statement on the pages opened.
- Robert Leonard has been director since 2023, having also led the gallery from 2005 to 2013 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Institute of Modern Art: mainly public: Queensland and Australian governments, plus private partners and donors
- Principal funders listed on the IMA site: the Queensland Government through Arts Queensland, the Australian Government through Creative Australia, and the Visual Art, Craft and Design Partnership Framework, an initiative of the Australian federal, state and territory governments. The page gives no amounts or years (IMA).
- Arts Queensland: the IMA received continued multi-year organisational funding in the Organisations Fund 2022 to 2025, part of a $33.5 million package for 46 organisations announced on 8 September 2021. The IMA's own amount is not stated (IMA news, 20 September 2021).
- Creative Australia: its Plus1 Matched Funding program recognised the Platform series in February 2026 and will match donations dollar for dollar up to $50,000 given before 31 May 2026 (IMA news, 23 February 2026).
- Private partners: The Calile Hotel is identified among the partners and supporters. The other partner logos are not labelled on the pages opened, and no donors are named.
- Crikey (3 February 2006) put the IMA's public funding at over $600,000 a year on an annual turnover of nearly $1 million.
- No reporting found of annual reports or accounts on the site; the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission page did not load.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the IMA is government arts funding with a small number of private partners, and no flagged funder was found. The weak points are that the funders and partners appear only as logos, with no amounts, and that no accounts are posted on the site. Worth watching: the size of the private contribution against the public grants.
