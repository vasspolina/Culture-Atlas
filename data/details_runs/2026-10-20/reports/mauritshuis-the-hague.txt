OVERVIEW
- Art museum in a 17th-century town palace in The Hague, with 854 objects, mostly Dutch Golden Age paintings by artists such as Vermeer, Rembrandt and Frans Hals (Wikipedia).
- Opened to the public in 1822. It has been an independent non-profit foundation since 1995 and still receives government funding (Wikipedia).
- Named for Johan Maurits van Nassau-Siegen, who built the house between 1633 and 1644 with income from his time as governor-general of Dutch Brazil (Dutch Wikipedia). Jacob van Campen designed it and Pieter Post oversaw much of the work.
- DutchNews reports that Maurits paid for the house with profits from Brazilian sugar plantations that relied on enslaved labour.
- A fire destroyed the interior in 1704; a lottery organised by the heirs of the later owner helped fund rebuilding, finished in 1718 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Renovated 2012 to 2014 and reopened on 27 June 2014 with the neighbouring Plein 26 building and a new underground foyer by Hans van Heeswijk (Dutch Wikipedia). It drew 497,416 visitors in 2025. Martine Gosselink has been director since April 2020.

FUNDING
Mauritshuis: mixed: Dutch state funding and a corporate main sponsor, NN Group, with a supporting foundation
- Government funding continues alongside private income (Wikipedia). No reporting read of the amounts.
- NN Group has been "main sponsor of the Mauritshuis since 2016" and pays for the annual exhibition, the museum playlist "View the Mauritshuis with Your Ears" and the guest-director evenings Maurits&; no amount or terms are stated (NN Group website).
- Stichting Johan Maurits van Nassau, founded in 1957, supports acquisitions and exhibitions; it merged with the Stichting Vrienden van het Mauritshuis in 1986 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Shell Nederland was a partner for six years until July 2018 and backed technical research on paintings by Jan Steen (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).
- No reporting read of a current fossil-fuel funder.

Red flags: former Shell sponsorship
- The six-year Shell partnership ended in July 2018 and was not renewed, according to a museum spokesman. The museum's latest annual report at the time called support from Shell Nederland and two other partners "crucial for the long-term future of the museum" (The Art Newspaper, Martin Bailey, 29 August 2018).
- Shell Nederland said the end came through "mutual decisions" (The Art Newspaper).
- Fossil Free Culture and other groups campaigned against Shell's ties to Dutch museums, calling them "art-washing" (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic). No reporting found that the museum gave the campaign as a reason.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In January 2018 Prime Minister Mark Rutte called the museum's removal of a plaster bust of Johan Maurits from its foyer "crazy" and said that if it distanced itself from its founder it should change its name; he later said the Mauritshuis was a poor example (DutchNews).
- The museum tied the removal to the growing debate about the Dutch role in the colonial slave trade; director Emilie Gordenker said the bust was unnecessary once a gallery on Maurits was set up, and rejected a name change (DutchNews).
- Connection: the debate concerns the museum's founder and namesake.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum today is Dutch state funding and corporate sponsorship led by NN Group; no fossil-fuel funder is currently reported. The weak points are the six-year Shell partnership that ended in 2018, which the museum's own report called crucial, and the founder's fortune from enslaved labour.
