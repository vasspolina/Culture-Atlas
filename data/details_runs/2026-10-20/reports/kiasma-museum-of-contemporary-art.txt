KIASMA MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Helsinki, Finland
ID: kiasma-museum-of-contemporary-art
Website: https://kiasma.fi

OVERVIEW
- State museum of contemporary art at Mannerheiminaukio 2 in central Helsinki; one of three museums of the Finnish National Gallery, which falls under the Ministry of Education and Culture (stated by director Leevi Haapala in NO NIIN magazine).
- The collection began in 1990 as the Museum of Contemporary Art; the building opened in May 1998 (Wikipedia).
- Steven Holl won the 1993 design competition with a proposal titled Chiasma, chosen from 516 entries; construction began in 1996 (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, closed Mondays; tickets €23 and €21, discounted €13, under 18 free (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Kiasma: mainly public: Finnish state money through the Finnish National Gallery, plus ticket, shop and partner income
- The Finnish National Gallery is financed primarily by the state, with operations also compensated by lottery money; ticket sales, museum shops and collaboration with corporate partners bring in around 20 to 30 percent of the total annual budget (stated by the museum, Haapala in NO NIIN).
- Kiasma Support Foundation: an independent body that funded Kiasma exhibitions and publications and helped buy six artworks since 2008 (stated by the museum, Haapala in NO NIIN).
- Poju Zabludowicz and his wife Anita donated Ed Atkins's video installation Safe Conduct (requested in 2017, realised a year later), and Kiasma has worked with Zabludowicz Collections on loans for some temporary exhibitions (stated by the museum, Haapala in NO NIIN). Kiasma worked with the Zabludowicz Art Trust from 2009 (Yle News, 2 December 2022).
- In 2023 the Finnish National Gallery board announced ethical funding guidelines for Ateneum, Kiasma and the Sinebrychoff Art Museum: no donations from sanctioned entities or from organisations tied to oil and gas production, tobacco, weapons manufacturing or hazardous chemicals (ArtReview, 3 May 2023).

Red flags: Protest or boycott (Kiasma Strike, 2022 to 2023)
- October 2022: the artists Terike Haapoja and Eero Yli-Vakkuri published an open letter calling for the museum to cut ties with Poju Zabludowicz (Kunstkritikk, 13 January 2023; ArtReview, 3 May 2023).
- 2 December 2022: more than 40 Finnish artists campaigning as Kiasma Strike stopped working with Kiasma. They asked that a Zabludowicz Art Trust representative leave the Kiasma Support Foundation board and that Kiasma refuse support from people tied to the weapons trade (Yle News, 2 December 2022). A December 2022 petition also asked Kiasma to stop accepting money from the Zabludowicz Art Trust (Kunstkritikk).
- Grounds given: Kunstkritikk writes that Zabludowicz's investment company Tamares "has been linked to arms trade between Finland and Israel" and that the company says it works mainly in real estate. Yle News writes that his father Shlomo sold arms to Israel and later founded Tamares, which Poju now leads (Kunstkritikk; Yle News).
- Kiasma's reply: the National Gallery cannot decide who sits on the foundation's board and its museums cannot join boycotts directed at individual citizens (Yle News).
- Outcome: the boycott grew from 42 artists to 220. Kiasma cut ties with the Kiasma Support Foundation, which Zabludowicz cofounded, and director Leevi Haapala resigned from the foundation's board (ArtReview, 3 May 2023). The BDS Movement, an advocacy source, gives 27 April 2023 as the day the strike ended in negotiation.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism in art media concerns Kiasma's private funding, not its programme, and centres on the 2022 to 2023 Kiasma Strike.
1. Kunstkritikk: Robert Stasinski, "Artists Rally Against Kiasma" (13 January 2023)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/artists-rally-against-kiasma/
- Interview with strike spokesperson Jenna Jauhiainen: the petition asks that Zabludowicz be removed from the board, that Kiasma stop accepting money from the Zabludowicz Art Trust, that it refuse support from parties in arms trading or manufacturing, and that clearer ethical guidelines be set for support of Finnish arts institutions.
- Jauhiainen says it is unsustainable and unethical for a state-funded contemporary art museum to receive financial support linked to the arms trade.
2. ArtReview: "Kiasma to sever ties with Zabludowicz following artists' boycott [updated]" (3 May 2023; no author byline)
URL: https://artreview.com/kiasma-to-refuse-zabludowicz-patronage-after-five-month-artists-boycott/
- News report on the five-month boycott by 220 artists and its end: Kiasma severed ties with the foundation and the Finnish National Gallery board announced new funding guidelines.

BOTTOM LINE
Kiasma is mainly public: it belongs to the Finnish National Gallery, which is financed mainly by the Finnish state, with own income of 20 to 30 percent (stated by the museum). The documented private-money issue was the Kiasma Support Foundation and its link to the Zabludowicz Art Trust; Kiasma cut ties with the foundation in 2023 after a five-month artists' boycott.
