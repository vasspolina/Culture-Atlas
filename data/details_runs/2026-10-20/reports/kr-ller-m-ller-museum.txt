KRÖLLER-MÜLLER MUSEUM: Otterlo, Netherlands
ID: kr-ller-m-ller-museum
Website: https://www.krollermuller.nl (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum with a sculpture garden in Hoge Veluwe National Park in Otterlo, on the grounds of the Kröllers' former estate (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the collector Helene Kröller-Müller, who gave her whole collection to the Dutch state in 1935; the museum opened to the public on 13 July 1938 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder, Helene Kröller-Müller. Her husband Anton Kröller ran the trading firm Wm.H. Müller & Co (Hoge Veluwe park biography).
- Building and site: designed by Henry van de Velde; Wim Quist designed the exhibition wing that opened in 1977. The sculpture garden, added in 1961, covers more than 30 hectares (Wikipedia).
- On site: the second-largest group of Van Gogh paintings after the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam, works by Picasso, Mondrian, Seurat and Braque, the sculpture garden with Rodin, Henry Moore and Dubuffet, a shop and the restaurant MJ (Wikipedia; the museum's accessibility page).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday and public holidays, 10:00 to 17:00. A combined day ticket for museum and park costs €27.75 (adult), €20.80 (13 to 18) and €6.95 (6 to 12). Museumkaart holders enter the museum free but pay the €13.85 park ticket. Free wheelchairs are lent at the cloakroom; the museum states it is well accessible by wheelchair, with partly unpaved garden paths and two adapted toilets.

FUNDING
Kröller-Müller Museum: mainly public and lottery-funded: the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, the VriendenLoterij and the Helene Kröller-Müller Fund
- Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (OCW): the collection is part of the Dutch National Art Collection, and the museum receives a ministry subsidy, as do 37 other museums. The museum states no amount. It says the support of its three main benefactors together enables upkeep of the collection, acquisitions, exhibitions and educational projects (the museum's funds and foundations page).
- VriendenLoterij: a funder since 1988, paying an annual amount that is not specified. It has paid for acquisitions including Roni Horn's "Opposites of White" (2006 to 2007), Anish Kapoor's "Untitled" (2018) and Blinky Palermo's "Ohne Titel" (1969) (the museum). Players can direct at least 40% of their monthly stake to the museum (the museum).
- Helene Kröller-Müller Fund: founded in 2013, it has contributed more than €2 million in total to educational projects, conservation work and exhibitions (the museum).
- Other funds: the museum says the restoration of Dubuffet's "Jardin d'émail" was carried out with the support of "many funds", without naming them.
- Own income: ticket prices above; the museum reported 283,000 visitors in 2025, below the 2019 peak of 405,428 (Wikipedia).
- Origin of the founding collection: the Kröllers' fortune came from Wm.H. Müller & Co, a trading firm with grain interests and iron ore mines in four countries. After the First World War the firm ran into serious trouble, and Kröller had inflated its accounts; he was never prosecuted (Hoge Veluwe park biography). This is the history of the 1913 to 1938 collection, not current funding.
- Governance: supervisory board of Herman Dijkhuizen (chair), Henriette Bast, Fried van 't Hof, Wim Pijbes and Marije Hulshof; director Benno Tempel; about 115 employees (the museum's organisation page).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors in fossil fuels, arms, gambling or tobacco. The annual reports and accounts for 2017 to 2025 were not opened in full, so amounts per funder are not checked.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean on the evidence found: the Dutch ministry, a Dutch charity lottery and the museum's own fund, plus ticket income. The founding fortune came from a commodity trading firm whose accounts were inflated after the First World War, but that is the founders' history, not current money. Worth watching: the three named benefactors, whose amounts are not published on the pages read.
