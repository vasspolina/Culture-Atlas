MUSEUM MMK FÜR MODERNE KUNST: Frankfurt am Main, Germany
ID: museum-mmk-fur-moderne-kunst
Website: https://www.mmk.art/en/ (checked 2026-11-20; the pages load their content by script and returned only the contact line to the automated check)

OVERVIEW
- A city museum of art from the 1960s to today with three sites: the main building at Domstrasse 10, ZOLLAMT MMK at Domstrasse 3 and TOWER MMK near the Bahnhofsviertel (museumsufer.de).
- Founded in 1981 on the initiative of Heinrich Klotz and the critic Peter Iden, with backing from mayor Walter Wallmann and culture councillor Hilmar Hoffmann; Jean-Christophe Ammann opened the building on 6 June 1991 (German Wikipedia).
- The triangular building, nicknamed the "Tortenstück" (slice of cake), was designed by the Vienna architect Hans Hollein, who won the 1983 competition; building began in 1987 (German Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 5,000 works from the 1960s to the present (museumsufer.de). Its core came from 87 works the city bought in 1980 and 1981 from the collection of the Darmstadt industrialist Karl Ströher, mainly 1960s Pop Art (German Wikipedia).
- ZOLLAMT MMK, in the former main customs office, has shown younger artists since 2007; TOWER MMK opened on 19 October 2014 on the second floor of the Taunusturm (German Wikipedia).
- Directors: Udo Kittelmann (2002 to 2008), Susanne Gaensheimer (2009 to 2017), and Susanne Pfeffer since January 2018 (German Wikipedia).
- The main building is closed for fire-safety renovation; under-18s enter free (museumsufer.de, read 20 November 2026).

FUNDING
Museum MMK für Moderne Kunst: public: a museum of the City of Frankfurt, with private money for its tower venue and young-artist shows
- The City of Frankfurt runs the museum: museumsufer.de lists the Magistrat and the city's Kulturamt as responsible, and the museum uses a city e-mail address (museumsufer.de; MMK website). Culture councillor Ina Hartwig chaired the MMK from 2017 (ArtReview, 18 May 2022).
- TOWER MMK: the museum has more than 1,500 square metres in the Taunusturm free of charge for 15 years from October 2014, and the running costs are privately financed for that period (German Wikipedia article on the Taunusturm, citing FAZ, 25 September 2013).
- The entrepreneur Stefan Quandt, the Ernst Max von Grunelius Stiftung, Helaba and others share those costs, according to the developer as quoted by structurae.net, an engineering database, and the same Wikipedia article.
- The ZOLLAMT programme is substantially supported by the Jürgen Ponto-Stiftung, whose two annual grantees show there (German Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2022 staff letter on work culture under director Pfeffer
- About 80 per cent of employees anonymously signed a six-page letter to Pfeffer, seen by the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung, describing a toxic, abusive work culture, repeated crossing of boundaries and opaque decisions; a first letter in 2019 raised similar concerns (ArtReview, 18 May 2022).
- The second letter was sent on 5 April 2022 with a 14-day deadline and made public when management did not answer in time; Pfeffer told the FAZ she issued an internal reply on 13 May 2022 setting out "significant structural reforms" (ArtReview).
- At the city assembly's question time on 11 May 2023 a CDU member said staff had declared the mediation that followed "gescheitert" (failed); Hartwig answered that the Magistrat intends "neue Wege zu gehen" (translated: to go new ways) and owes a duty of care to staff and management alike (City of Frankfurt, Dezernat VII, answer to question 1652).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The art press covered the staff complaint rather than the programme.
1. ArtReview: unsigned news report, "Frankfurt Museum of Modern Art faces toxic workplace allegations" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/frankfurt-museum-of-modern-art-faces-toxic-workplace-allegations/
- Sets the staff claims against the museum's own curatorial focus on power imbalances and institutional violence, such as the show "crip time" (2021).
- Names Pfeffer and Hartwig.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is clean: city money with named private backers. The weak point is governance: a staff dispute with the director that the city had not settled by May 2023.
