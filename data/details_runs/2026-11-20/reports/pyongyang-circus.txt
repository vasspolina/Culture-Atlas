PYONGYANG CIRCUS: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: pyongyang-circus
Website: none found (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Circus theatre on Kwangbok Street, Mangyongdae District, completed in May 1989 for the 13th World Festival of Youth and Students (Korean Wikipedia).
- The site covers 80,000 square metres, with 54,000 square metres of floor space and 3,500 seats. A circus school stands in the grounds, and a magic theatre of about 300 seats opened beside it in 2002 (Korean Wikipedia).
- Renovation: Korean Wikipedia dates it to October 2011. English Wikipedia and Koryo Tours say 2012.
- Stages for ice, water and animal acts are mechanised (Koryo Tours; Korean Wikipedia).
- The company: the Korean Art website lists a National Acrobatic Troupe set up on 10 June 1952, based in the Pyongyang Acrobatic Theatre and Magic Theatre, with a Pyongyang Acrobatics School. The site claims over 90 appearances at international circus festivals and over a hundred gold and silver prizes.
- A May 2000 digest on the North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification says the troupe began with about 40 members, had grown to about 1,000 and had given about 23,400 performances at home.
- Koryo Tours says shows near anniversaries are themed on recent political and military events, with acrobats dressed as missiles.
- The theatre hosted the opening on 10 April and the closing on 17 April 2025 of the 9th April Spring People's Art Festival, chaired by Culture Minister Sung Jong Gyu (North Korea Information Portal, citing Rodong Sinmun and KCNA).
- Kim Jong Un watched a National Circus Troupe show there on 16 August 2026, for the 81st anniversary of liberation (North Korea Information Portal, citing Rodong Sinmun and KCNA).
- Visiting: Koryo Tours says it is open all year with regular performances. Photos are allowed only before and after the show, and animals appear in some shows.

FUNDING
Pyongyang Circus: state-run: the Government of North Korea, plus ticket fees from foreign visitors
- Operator: the troupe sits under the Stage Arts Guidance Bureau of the Ministry of Culture and uses the theatre as its own house (North Korea Information Portal, May 2000).
- The state built the People's Army Circus Theatre of 1,600 seats in 1964 and this theatre in 1989, and has trained performers at a circus school founded in 1972; the portal's November 1999 digest calls this support at state level.
- Foreign visitors pay 20 euros a person, handed to local guides who arrange the tickets (Koryo Tours).
- Touring: the May 2000 digest counts about 700 performances abroad, on three or four foreign tours a year, and a Monte-Carlo festival gold prize in January 2000.
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: Sanctions, state circus
- The Korean Art website lists the troupe under performing arts creative organs and says it holds the Order of Kim Il Sung.
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the circus.

Not related: the People's Military Circus, a second Pyongyang circus near the War Museum. Koryo Tours says it closed and its building became a concert auditorium.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces were read. A 2015 Daily NK report describes the troupe's training and prizes without criticism; a 2019 report carried by Al Bawaba attacks a bear act at a Pyongyang circus.
1. Daily NK: (no byline), "Pyongyang National Circus: Breaking Down Barriers" (2015)
URL: https://www.dailynk.com/english/pyongyang-national-circus-breaking/
- Says recruits are chosen from age nine for nine years of training, on criteria including facial symmetry, flexibility and height, then enter one of seven performance sections.
- Says the troupe won the Figueres golden elephant in March 2015 and a golden clown at Monte-Carlo in January, and that it went to Seoul in 2000.
- Argues that audiences can appreciate the art apart from politics. Does not criticise the institution.
2. Al Bawaba: (no byline), "What Should We Make of The N. Korea Dancing Bears?" (2019)
URL: https://www.albawaba.net/editors-choice/what-should-we-make-n-korea-dancing-bears-1280621
- An Australian tourist, Mark Woodman, filmed bears made to leapfrog their trainers, dance and skip with a rope at "the circus in Pyongyang".
- Jan Creamer, president of the campaign group Animal Defenders International, said such animals "endure a lifetime of suffering and deprivation".
- Does not name the Pyongyang Circus. The page says it was adapted from another source, which it does not name.

BOTTOM LINE
The circus is a state company under the Ministry of Culture in a theatre the state built. The points to watch are the euro ticket fees collected from foreign visitors and the troupe's tours abroad. The animal acts drew criticism from a campaign group in 2019.
