OKAYAMA ART SUMMIT: Okayama, Japan
ID: okayama-art-summit-okayama
Website: https://www.okayamaartsummit.jp/2025/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Triennial exhibition of contemporary, mainly conceptual, art in central Okayama, held at historic sites and cultural venues around Okayama Castle and Korakuen Garden (festival website).
- It was created, with public and private backing, to bring life back to the old castle town area; the first edition ran from 9 October to 27 November 2016 with Liam Gillick as artistic director (festival website).
- Later artistic directors were Pierre Huyghe and, in 2022, Rirkrit Tiravanija (e-flux).
- Philippe Parreno, appointed at the committee's general meeting on 24 October 2023, directed the 2025 edition, "The Parks of Aomame", from 26 September to 24 November 2025 (e-flux).
- Organiser: the Okayama Art Summit Executive Committee, chaired by Okayama's mayor Masao Omori (festival website).
- Team for 2025: executive producer Yasuharu Ishikawa, president of the Ishikawa Foundation; executive director Taro Nasu of the gallery TARO NASU; public programme director Chieko Kinoshita (festival website).
- Visitors: 424,917 visits in 2025 (preliminary figure), the most so far, including 5,800 pupils from 90 schools (executive committee press release, Kyodo News PR Wire).
- Visiting: admission to all venues was free in 2025, for the first time; venues closed on most Mondays (festival website; e-flux).

FUNDING
Okayama Art Summit: public and corporate: Okayama City and Prefecture, the Ishikawa Foundation, and regional sponsors
- The executive committee consists mainly of Okayama City, Okayama Prefecture and the Ishikawa Foundation (Tokyo Art Beat, Natsuko Fukushima, 19 October 2022).
- 2025 backers: Merco Group as special co-organiser; Ishikawa Holdings, the Sanyo Shimbun, Chugin Financial Group and the JR West Group as special sponsors; the Okayama Toyota dealers' group, Sapix, Tomiya Corporation, Nakashima Holdings, Japan Tobacco, Ryobi Group, RSK, OHK, TSC, ProdCo, Okayama University of Science and Maruzen-Yushodo as sponsors (2025 event report, March 2026).
- Grants in 2025: the Social Creation Arts Fund of the Association for Corporate Support of the Arts, and Institut français (2025 event report).
- Crowdfunding on CAMPFIRE from 5 August to 23 October 2025 raised 7,538,000 yen against a 5,000,000 yen target, towards the costs of the free edition (2025 event report).

Red flags: 2025 sponsorship by Japan Tobacco
- The executive committee's report on the 2025 edition lists 日本たばこ産業株式会社 (Japan Tobacco Inc.) among the sponsors (2025 event report, March 2026); the 2025 about page shows its "JT" logo, linked to jti.co.jp (festival website).

Red flags: citizen petitions of 2021 and 2022 over executive producer Yasuharu Ishikawa
- The Okayama Citizens' Association for Okayama Art Summit, formed in August 2021, asked that Ishikawa be replaced as general producer by an expert with ties to Okayama and that the festival concept be reviewed (Tokyo Art Beat, 24 November 2021 and 19 October 2022).
- Background: harassment accusations against Ishikawa, then head of the fashion company Stripe International, were reported for August 2015 to May 2018; he received a "stern warning" in December 2018 with no disciplinary action, did not admit harassment and stepped down as company president in March 2020 (Tokyo Art Beat, 19 October 2022).
- The group petitioned the city council on 19 November 2021 (withdrawn on 24 November), then the prefectural assembly on 22 February 2022 and the city council on 25 February; committees of the council and assembly rejected the petitions on 9 and 15 March 2022 (Tokyo Art Beat, 24 November 2021, updated 18 August 2022).
- On 8 July 2022 the group submitted a request with 400 signatures; on 15 July the city replied that it would look for another venue and did not address the allegations (Tokyo Art Beat, 19 October 2022).
- On 27 September 2022 the committee said an investigation committee at Stripe had found no harassment by Ishikawa and saw no need to explain to citizens or share information with the artists (Tokyo Art Beat, title translated). Ishikawa remained executive producer in 2025 (festival website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Tokyo Art Beat covered the petitions and the committee's answers; one essay criticises the organisers' lack of explanation.
1. Tokyo Art Beat: Natsuko Fukushima, "Controversy Brews Over Rirkrit Tiravanija's 'Okayama Art Summit': Who Does the 'Communication' Exclude?" (19 October 2022)
URL: https://www.tokyoartbeat.com/en/articles/-/controversy-brews-over-rirkrit-tiravanijas-okayama-art-summit
- Asks how citizens can be recognised and dialogue begin when the organisers claim no knowledge of the group.
- Reports that Tiravanija said at the 29 September 2022 press conference, "I am not aware of a group in Okayama", and that Nasu said he did not know any details about the citizens' group.

- Not criticism: records the committee's written answers.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 13 February 2025 the city of Okayama, whose mayor chairs the Summit's committee, signed a regional partnership agreement with Japan Tobacco covering passive-smoking consulting, street cleanliness posters, youth development and disaster response (Okayama City website).

BOTTOM LINE
Mostly local public and corporate money, with one current tobacco sponsor. The organisers' silence towards the petitioners remains the governance weak point.
