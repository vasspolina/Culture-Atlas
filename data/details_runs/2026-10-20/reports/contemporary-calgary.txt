CONTEMPORARY CALGARY: Calgary, Canada
ID: contemporary-calgary
Website: https://www.contemporarycalgary.com (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting public art gallery in the former Centennial Planetarium, a Brutalist building with a geodesic dome roof completed in 1967 for the Canadian Centennial, at the edge of downtown Calgary (Wikipedia).
- Formed in 2013 by the merger of three Calgary arts groups: the Institute of Modern and Contemporary Art, the Museum of Contemporary Art and the Art Gallery of Calgary. In June 2018 it agreed a 25-year lease of the building with the City of Calgary (Wikipedia).
- The building housed a science centre (now TELUS Spark) from the 1980s until 2011. KPMB Architects' Bruce Kuwabara, with Gibbs Gage Architects, was chosen in January 2019 to lead the renovation (Wikipedia).
- Opened two days a week in June 2019; officially opened on 23 January 2020 with the group show "Planetary" and Luke Jerram's "Museum of the Moon"; closed in March 2020 and reopened in September 2020 (Wikipedia).
- On site: the Observatory Gallery, the Dome Theatre and a smaller theatre, the Flanagan Family Gallery and the Ring Gallery around the Dome Theatre (Making & Meaning, 2024).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday 12pm to 7pm and Sunday 12pm to 5pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. The gallery says it aims to be "free of physical, social, and financial barriers"; no admission price or accessibility details are published on the pages read.

FUNDING
Contemporary Calgary: mixed: three levels of government for the building, plus donors and a yearly gala
- Phase 1 (base building renovations, finished 2019): $25 million from the City of Calgary. Phase 2: a $5 million investment from the City, PrairiesCan, Calgary Foundation and private donors (stated by the gallery).
- Phase 3, the Centennial Planetarium Transformation Project, costs $160 million: the Government of Alberta committed $40 million on 27 February 2025 and the Government of Canada $40 million on 3 July 2026. The gallery plans to seek City funding and to raise the rest from private and corporate donors. More than $110 million across all phases is raised or committed; construction is expected to start in fall 2027 (stated by the gallery).
- Gala: the LOOK25 gala raised over $1.2 million, with a record $633,300 from art auction sales, presented by Mawer. Holt Renfrew sponsored the VIP dinner, Concorde Entertainment Group the food, Heather Edwards the art auction, and Rogers Communications donated two works that sold for a combined $55,000 (press release, 6 June 2025).
- LOOK26 (2 May 2026) raised $1.2 million with $583,356 in auction sales; sponsors were again Mawer, Holt Renfrew, Concorde Entertainment Group, Cowboys Music Festival and Heather Edwards (press release, 4 May 2026).
- Individual donors: nine Leadership Circle donors are named, including Peter and Janet Tertzakian and Gordon and Jill Rawlinson, plus nine lifetime donors. Circle levels are $1,000, $5,000 and $10,000; no year or tier is given per name (stated by the gallery).
- Peter Tertzakian, a Leadership Circle donor and former chair of the gallery's board, is the founder of the ARC Energy Research Institute and a partner of ARC Financial Corp. (ARC Energy Institute). ARC Financial's own site says it runs two areas of funds, energy transition funds and oil and gas funds (arcfinancial.com). The gallery's pages give no year, tier or amount for the Tertzakians' gifts, and no reporting found of an objection to them.
- Board: co-chairs Carol Ryder and Kelly Streit, secretary Margaret-Jean Mannix, treasurer Krista Rabidoux, and directors including Rick Orman and Alan Ross; David Leinster is CEO (stated by the gallery). A 2024/2025 annual report is linked.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage found is admiring; no criticism found of the gallery.
1. Making & Meaning (Substack): Marie Leduc, "Contemporary Calgary" (2024)
URL: https://makingandmeaning.substack.com/p/contemporary-calgary
- Calls the Brutalist building a good fit for its new use and the exhibitions strong; the only reservation is that the gallery needs continued support from government, donors and members to become a major destination.
- The author says she has known one featured artist for over 20 years. Not criticism of the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is municipal, provincial and federal capital funding plus donors and an annual gala; no funder with a documented controversy was confirmed. The weak points are that donor names are undated and untiered, that no operating income figures were read, and that a former board chair and donor, Peter Tertzakian, is a partner of ARC Financial, which runs both energy transition and oil and gas funds, and the dates and size of his gifts are not published. Watch the Phase 3 fundraising and the donor list.
