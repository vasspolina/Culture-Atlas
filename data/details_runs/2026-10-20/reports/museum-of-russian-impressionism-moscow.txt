MUSEUM OF RUSSIAN IMPRESSIONISM: Moscow, Russia
ID: museum-of-russian-impressionism-moscow
Website: http://www.rusimp.su/

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum on the Bolshevik grounds at Leningradsky Prospekt 15, building 11, Moscow (museum website). The permanent exhibition on the second floor shows works by Konstantin Korovin, Valentin Serov, Stanislav Zhukovsky and Nikolai Bogdanov-Belsky (museum website).
- Founded by the collector Boris Mints (O1 Group); the museum opened to the public on 28 May 2016, and began in 2014 with exhibitions held by the MacDougall's auction house (Wikipedia).
- Building: the museum's own page says it was built in the 1960s to store flour and sugar and converted by the British architects John McAslan + Partners, keeping the cylindrical form and adding a perforated metal cladding, with five floors and about 1,000 square metres of exhibition space. Wikipedia says construction started in 2012 and cost $16.5 million, on the site of a former Bolshevik confectionery factory.
- Director: Yulia Petrova (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper Russia, 15 February 2024).
- Visiting: open 11:00 to 20:00 on Monday, Tuesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday and 12:00 to 21:00 on Wednesday and Thursday. The building is fitted for visitors with limited mobility, with sign-language tours, tours for blind and partially sighted visitors, and accessible restrooms (museum website).
- Recognition: longlisted for The Art Newspaper Russia Prize, Museum of the Year, in 2016, 2022 and 2024 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Museum of Russian Impressionism: private: a patron-trustee, ticket sales and commercial partners
- Founder Boris Mints: The Art Newspaper (Sophia Kishkovsky, 28 June 2018) reported that news of his family's departure for London emerged at the end of May 2018, and that it was unclear whether he would keep funding the museum from the UK; director Petrova declined to discuss his ongoing support. Unverified: the article cites reports that Mints left to avoid possible criminal investigation in Russia over bank dealings; it names no bank, and O1 Group referred questions to the museum.
- Since February 2024 the patron-trustee and head of the board of trustees is Vladimir Voronin, a businessman and shareholder of the FSK group of companies, which The Art Newspaper Russia (Sofia Bagdasarova, 15 February 2024) calls its founder and president (RTVI, 15 February 2024, calls him president of FSK). He began supporting the museum in 2023, when his support made possible an exhibition on the gallerist Nadezhda Dobychina. He said he considers it his duty to provide such support, and director Petrova said his role allows the museum to carry out a long-delayed modernisation of the building and equipment.
- Director Petrova said about 40% of the budget comes from tickets, tours, classes, souvenirs and catalogues, and the rest from commercial partnerships and donations (The Art Newspaper Russia, 2024).
- Mints has lived in London for several years. Petrova said he cannot support the museum for legal reasons; Mints said being outside the country prevents him from supporting it adequately, and he still takes part in discussing its development programme and exhibition plans (The Art Newspaper Russia, 15 February 2024).
- OpenSanctions lists Mints as a close associate of a politically exposed person (from Wikidata) and as an oligarch (from the Russian Oligarch/Billionaires Database, which mentions a 2018 CAATSA report on Russian oligarchs). The page says he has not been found on international sanctions lists (OpenSanctions, a non-official database).
- Russia's Ministry of Culture spokesperson said in 2018 that private museums "do not receive any kind of benefits or financing" from the ministry (The Art Newspaper).
- FSK builds multi-apartment residential buildings in Moscow and the Moscow region (RTVI).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is privately funded: Mints founded it, and since February 2024 Vladimir Voronin of the developer group FSK has been its patron-trustee, with about 40% of the budget from the museum's own sales. Director Petrova gave Mints's legal situation after his 2018 move to London as the reason he cannot support it, and OpenSanctions says he has not been found on international sanctions lists.
