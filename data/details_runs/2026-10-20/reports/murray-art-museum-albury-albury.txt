MURRAY ART MUSEUM ALBURY: Albury, Australia
ID: murray-art-museum-albury-albury
Website: https://www.mamalbury.com.au

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum, known as MAMA, at 546 Dean Street, Albury, New South Wales (museum website).
- It grew out of the Albury Regional Art Gallery and opened on 2 October 2015 with "Wiradjuri Ngurambanggu (On Wiradjuri Country)", works by contemporary Indigenous artists (Wikipedia).
- Building: a $10.5 million refurbishment renovated the former gallery and the neighbouring Burrows House, with extensions linking both into QEII Square, designed by NBRS and Partners. Floor space grew from 832 m2 to 2,036 m2 and the building has seven galleries over two levels (Wikipedia).
- It manages Albury City Council's collection of more than 3,000 items, including a photography collection of more than 600 works that grew largely from the National Photography Prize, founded in 1983 (Wikipedia).
- Blair French became chief executive in January 2024, after Bree Pickering, who was director from November 2016 to March 2023 (Wikipedia).
- MAMA Ltd was formed in July 2023 as a company limited by guarantee to take over from the council, following approval by the NSW Minister for Local Government in November 2022. The council continues to operate the museum until the transition is complete and continues to provide financial support (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free entry. Open Monday to Friday 10:00 to 17:00 and Saturday, Sunday and public holidays 10:00 to 16:00; closed Anzac Day, Christmas Day and Good Friday (museum website).

FUNDING
Murray Art Museum Albury: mainly public: AlburyCity Council and Create NSW, with philanthropic benefactors
- AlburyCity Council and Create NSW are listed as funding partners on the museum's website.
- Building (2015): of the $10.5 million cost, $3.5 million came from the federal government, the council's spending rose to a total of at least $6 million, and $1.05 million was a philanthropic target met by the Arts Albury fundraising group; the NSW government did not match the federal money (The Border Mail editorial, "Lofty target but fundraising group came good for MAMA", 29 September 2015).
- The Paul Ramsay Foundation gave $250,000 before opening, and three galleries are named for Paul Ramsay (MAMA, 23 September 2015). Colin and Lorraine Joss gave $200,000, and the 36 benefactors gave between $3,000 and $250,000 (The Border Mail).
- NSW government: $40,000 through the Arts and Cultural Development Program for MAMA to stage "SPEED: faster, larger, stronger, quieter" in 2016 (MAMA, 19 October 2015).
- Major benefactors ($200,000 and over) on the museum's thank-you page: Juan Davila, Colin and Lorraine Joss and Family, Metal Manufacturers, Paul Ramsay Foundation, Russell Mills Foundation, the Estate of the Late Ruth Whyte and two anonymous donors. The page lists further donors by tier, including the Geoff and Helen Handbury Foundation (donor tier, $25,000 to $49,999) and the Mount Pelion Foundation (friend tier, $1,000 to $9,999).
- The independent MAMA Art Foundation is the museum's main fundraising body; it supports the museum and the National Photography Prize (Wikipedia).
- A "MAMA Supporter" campaign from 2014 to 2016 raised over $60,000 (museum thank-you page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind MAMA is public and philanthropic: AlburyCity Council runs and supports the museum and Create NSW is a named partner, while private benefactors including the Paul Ramsay Foundation ($250,000) and the Joss family ($200,000) contributed to the 2015 redevelopment. MAMA Ltd took over governance from the council in 2023.
