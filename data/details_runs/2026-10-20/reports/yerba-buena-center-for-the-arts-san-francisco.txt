YERBA BUENA CENTER FOR THE ARTS: San Francisco, USA
ID: yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-san-francisco
Website: https://ybca.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art, performance and film centre at 701 Mission Street, in the Yerba Buena Gardens area of South of Market, San Francisco.
- Opened in 1993 under a deal Mayor George Moscone made with developers to set aside land and funds for cultural institutions in the redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- Buildings: the Gallery and Forum by Fumihiko Maki, and the Blue Shield of California Theater by James Polshek and Todd Schliemann (stated by the centre).
- Programmes: exhibitions, public art, performances, education, an archive; a 2021 pilot gave $1,000 a month to 130 artists for six months (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday 11am to 8pm and Thursday to Sunday 11am to 5pm; no admission or accessibility details on the about page.

FUNDING
Yerba Buena Center for the Arts: mixed public and private: city arts money, foundations and individual donors
- City money: Proposition E (2018) directs 1.5% of the city's hotel tax to arts and cultural services (Wikipedia). The centre's own city grants were not found.
- Revenue for 2024-25 was $9,315,946 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- The centre's funder line names MacKenzie Scott and Dan Jewett, the California Arts Council and #StartSmall, then further foundations, the San Francisco Arts Commission and YBCA Members (YBCA 100 page).
- Granted AI, an aggregator, lists 17 foundation funders including Hewlett, Koret, Mellon and Doris Duke, plus 2025 federal awards from the Institute of Museum and Library Services and the National Endowment for the Arts. Amounts were not verified.
- No donor roster with amounts was readable; the annual report PDF could not be read as text.

Red flags: boycott and protest by exhibiting artists, aimed at funders and board
- 15 February 2024: eight artists in the Bay Area Now 9 triennial altered their own works at the centre's "Love Letter to SOMA" event; Jewish Voice for Peace Bay Area and two other groups dropped flyers (Hyperallergic, 16 February 2024).
- Demands: end "censorship of artists", a ceasefire in Gaza, and "remove all Zionist board members and funders". Hyperallergic reports the organizers citing the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation and the Koret Foundation.
- 21 February: the centre's statement said: "We will not agree to demands that insist YBCA discriminate on the basis of religion, ethnic background, or national origin." 26 February: the eight artists and 15 current employees published open letters calling the centre's actions censorship, and the artists called for a boycott until leadership met them (KQED).
- Galleries closed for about a month (Wikipedia). Interim CEO Sara Fenske Bahat's resignation letter, dated 3 March 2024, cited "The vitriolic and anti-Semitic backlash directed at me personally" (KQED, 7 March 2024).
- 5 March 2024: Supervisor Hillary Ronen called the centre's actions censorship at a Board of Supervisors meeting and called for a special hearing on the publicly funded centre (KQED).
- Background, December 2023: the centre did not add "Free Palestine" to its marquee for artist Lukaza Branfman-Verissimo; artist Jeffrey Cheung said it blocked Palestinian flag colours in his mural, which the CEO denied (KQED).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the 2024 protest is detailed. Hyperallergic reports both sides; Variable West is advocacy backing the artists.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Artists Alter Their Work, Protesting San Francisco Institution's 'Silence' on Gaza" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/872425/artists-deface-their-work-protesting-san-francisco-yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-silence-on-gaza/
- Reports the action, the artists' claim that the centre kept them from advocating for Palestine, and the demands, and names the Hewlett and Koret Foundations.
- Gives the CEO's reply: Cheung's design arrived after a deadline set to coordinate "city entities, neighborhood partners, and funders".
2. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Yerba Buena Center for the Arts CEO Resigns Over Pro-Palestine Action" (8 March 2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-ceo-resigns-over-pro-palestine-action
- Reports the resignation. The board called the demands "unreasonable" on 21 February; artist Courtney Desiree Morris says the boycott targets institutions, not individuals. Names no funders.
3. Variable West: Ella Ray, "Editor's Note: YBCA's censorship of artists for Palestinian liberation" (10 March 2024)
URL: https://variablewest.com/2024/03/10/editors-note-ybcas-censorship-of-artists-for-palestinian-liberation/
- Argues the centre censored artists, put the eight artists' work in storage and blamed deadlines and paperwork instead of naming its alignment with Israel.
- Unverified: Variable West says the centre has "financial and social connections to individuals and organizations funding said genocide", "namely" the Hewlett and Koret Foundations; it gives no figures. Variable West states it "unequivocally supports" the artists: an advocacy position.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre's money is public and foundation money, and nothing read ties it to a flagged industry. The weak points are thin donor disclosure and the 2024 dispute: censorship claims, a boycott call and a demand that two named foundations be dropped. Watch whether donor lists with amounts are published.
