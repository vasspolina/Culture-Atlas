AMERICAN FOLK ART MUSEUM: New York, United States
ID: american-folk-art-museum
Website: https://folkartmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of folk and self-taught art at 2 Lincoln Square, on Columbus Avenue at 66th Street on Manhattan's Upper West Side, in a building whose landlord is the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (stated by the space).
- Opened in 1961 as the Museum of Early American Folk Arts in rented space at 49 West 53rd Street (Wikipedia).
- From 2001 to 2011 its main home was an eight-level building at 45 to 47 West 53rd Street by Tod Williams and Billie Tsien; the museum sold it to MoMA in 2011 under rising bond payments, and MoMA demolished it in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 7,000 works, formed almost entirely through gifts; the Self-Taught Genius Gallery is funded largely by the Henry Luce Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Partly closed from May 2025 for what the museum calls its most significant renovation in more than 30 years; the West Side Rag said the full closure would last until 26 September, the museum used a temporary entrance in November 2025, and a new facade, entrance and reimagined 66th Street courtyard are due in 2026 (West Side Rag, museum letter).
- Admission is free; usually open Wednesday to Sunday. No accessibility information found on the pages read.

FUNDING
American Folk Art Museum: mainly private: individual donors and foundations, with its landlord subsidising the renovation
- In the year to June 2025 revenue was $6,148,511, of which contributions were $2,966,233 and programme revenue $982,404; expenses were $6,006,629 and net assets $16,884,018 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- Contributions were $7,813,668 in the year to June 2023 (Form 990).
- The landlord, the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, "has generously subsidized" the 2025 to 2026 renovation; no amount given (stated by the space).
- Deborah Davenport and Stewart Stender gave $3 million to establish a curator position (stated by the space, November 2025 letter; the gift year is not given).
- The Henry Luce Foundation gave $1.6 million in 2013 for the touring show "Self-Taught Genius" (stated by the space).
- The museum shared in a $20 million Carnegie Corporation grant made possible by Michael Bloomberg; Wikipedia gives 2007 but cites a 2005 New York Times article, so the year is unconfirmed (Wikipedia).
- Director and CEO Jason T. Busch was paid $331,886 in the year to June 2025 (Form 990).
- No reporting found of corporate or fossil-fuel sponsors.

Red flags: 2024 to 2026 labour dispute
- Workers voted to join UAW Local 2110 in June 2024 and asked for $30 an hour within three years; management offered $21.50 (Hyperallergic, May 2026).
- On 6 May 2026 staff and about three dozen UAW organisers picketed the museum's gala at the Mandarin Oriental; front-line staff then earned $19 an hour, about $39,500 a year (Hyperallergic).
- Union representatives said the museum cut several union posts and outsourced the work during the renovation (Hyperallergic).
- The union ratified its first contract with the museum by 2 October 2026, averting a strike (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns pay and labour, and earlier the loss of the 53rd Street building, which critics aimed mostly at MoMA.
1. Hyperallergic: Aaron Short, "American Folk Art Museum Workers Picket Gala, Calling for Higher Wages" (2026)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/american-folk-art-museum-workers-picket-gala-calling-for-higher-wages/
- Front-line pay was below the MIT living wage for New York while the CEO earned $321,882 in fiscal 2024.
- The museum said it respected its employees' decision to unionize and was "committed to working in good faith".
2. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Three NYC Museum Unions Ratify Contracts, Averting Strikes" (2026)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/three-nyc-museum-unions-ratify-contracts-averting-strikes/
- Not criticism: reports the first contract.
3. Architectural Record: Laura Mirviss and William Hanley, "Architectural Community Speaks Out Against MoMA's Plan to Raze Tod Williams Billie Tsien's Folk Art Museum" (2013)
URL: https://architecturalrecord.com/news/2013/04/130410-moma-will-raze-tod-williams-billie-tsiens-folk-art-museum.asp
- The museum had taken on $32 million of debt to build the 2001 building; an Architectural League letter said MoMA "has not yet offered a compelling justification" for demolishing it.
- Criticises MoMA, not the Folk Art Museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private philanthropy with public accounts and no flagged funder. The weak points were the 2011 debt collapse that cost the museum its building, and a two-year pay dispute that ended with a first union contract in October 2026. Worth watching: the size of the landlord's renovation subsidy, which is not published.
