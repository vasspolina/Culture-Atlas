THE WHITWORTH: Manchester, UK
ID: the-whitworth
Website: https://www.whitworth.manchester.ac.uk

OVERVIEW
- Art gallery in Whitworth Park, Oxford Road, Manchester, part of the University of Manchester (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1889 by Robert Dukinfield Darbishire with a donation from Sir Joseph Whitworth, as "The Whitworth Institute and Park"; it joined the Victoria University of Manchester in 1958 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the 1895 to 1900 red brick and terracotta building is Grade II listed. A 15 million pound redevelopment doubled the public space and opened in February 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Collection: over 60,000 items (Wikipedia). Sook-Kyung Lee has been director since August 2023. Alistair Hudson, director from 2018, announced in July 2022 that he would leave at the end of 2022 for ZKM Karlsruhe (Museums Journal, 22 July 2022).

FUNDING
The Whitworth: University of Manchester gallery funded by Arts Council England, heritage and research funders, trusts and corporate partners
- The gallery's sponsors page lists, without years or amounts, current corporate supporters Pentagon Play, The Little Greene Paint Company, Manchester Accommodation Bid and Hyundai Motor Company.
- Capital funders named on the sponsors page: the National Lottery Heritage Fund, Arts Council England, the Clore Duffield Foundation, Friends of the Whitworth, the Wolfson Foundation, the Garfield Weston Foundation, the Headley Trust, the J Paul Getty Jnr Charitable Trust, the Granada Foundation and the Pilgrim Trust.
- Programme funders named: Arts Council England, Research England, the Friends of the Whitworth, the Baring Foundation, Art Fund, the Rayne Foundation, Frieze x Deutsche Bank, the Contemporary Art Society and the Henry Moore Foundation.

Red flags: university removal of an exhibition statement, 2021
- Forensic Architecture's Cloud Studies at the Whitworth included a statement of solidarity with Palestine. The gallery removed it after a complaint from UK Lawyers for Israel (UKLFI), and reversed the decision when Forensic Architecture threatened to pull the show, which closed for three days. It reopened with the statement beside a second statement from Jewish organisations (Museums Journal, Geraldine Kendall Adams, 22 July 2022). Hyperallergic reports that the university removed it (Valentina Di Liscia, 1 March 2022).
- Forensic Architecture founder Eyal Weizman called the university's action the "last in series of bullying actions" (ArtReview, 23 February 2022). UKLFI called the statement inflammatory and suggested the university take appropriate disciplinary action (ArtReview).
- The University of Manchester says staffing matters are "strictly internal to the university," rejects claims that it suppressed academic and artistic freedom, and says it worked to keep Cloud Studies open for its full planned run (The Art Newspaper, 25 February 2022).

Red flags: artist boycott of British Art Show 9 in Manchester, 2022
- More than 20 artists, 21 named in a joint statement, pledged to withdraw from the Manchester leg of British Art Show 9 unless "meaningful reparative measures" are taken. They condemned the university's "capitulation to continued UKLFI demands." Two more, including James Bridle, had also withdrawn, a total of 23 (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 25 February 2022).
- Their open letter was addressed to Vice-Chancellor Nancy Rothwell (Hyperallergic, 1 March 2022). The artists were responding to reports that the university had asked Hudson to leave. Museums Journal later reported that he stayed in post and that it understood he was not forced to resign (22 July 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism of the university's handling of the 2021 statement and of Hudson's reported ousting, all in 2022.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Artists Withdraw From Manchester Exhibition to Protest Director's Ousting Over Pro-Palestine Message" (1 March 2022)

- L'Internationale called for Hudson's reinstatement.

BOTTOM LINE
The Whitworth is part of the University of Manchester, and its sponsors page names Arts Council England, heritage and research funders, trusts and four current corporate supporters. The documented weak points are the 2021 removal of the Forensic Architecture statement by the university that runs the gallery and the 2022 artist withdrawals from British Art Show 9 that followed.
