SUDAN NATIONAL MUSEUM: Khartoum, Sudan
ID: sudan-national-museum
Website: https://sudannationalmuseum.com (checked 2026-11-20; a 2007 site whose inner pages return 404)

OVERVIEW
- Sudan's largest museum, on El Neel (Nile) Avenue in Khartoum's al-Mugran area, close to the meeting of the White and Blue Nile.
- The building dates from 1955 and it became a national museum in 1971 (Wikipedia).
- Run by the National Corporation for Antiquities and Museums (NCAM).
- Its garden holds temples moved from Nubia during the UNESCO salvage campaign of the 1960s, among them those of Aksha, Buhen, Kumma and Semna.
- The collection ran from the Paleolithic to the Napatan and Meroitic kingdoms and to Christian and Islamic material.
- Visiting: closed. The museum was wrecked and looted in the war: the Rapid Support Forces took it over on 2 June 2023 and were expelled from Khartoum in March 2025 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Sudan National Museum: public and donor-funded: the Sudanese state through NCAM, with Italian money through UNESCO
- A rehabilitation project began in 2018 with support from the Government of Italy through the Italian Agency for Development Cooperation (AICS); UNESCO, the Ministry of Culture, Information, Tourism and Antiquities and NCAM are partners (UN in Sudan, 6 July 2026).
- Work done: a new exhibition design, roof restoration designs, fire protection and IT security systems, conservation of mural paintings, and after April 2023 damage assessments and clearance of rubble and unexploded ordnance.
- Full rehabilitation will proceed in phases "once funding is made available" (UN in Sudan).
- UNESCO had helped renovate the museum in 2019 (AP).

WIDER CONTEXT
- AP reporters found the halls trashed in April 2025; NCAM official Gamal ElDeen Zain al-Abdeen said looters broke into the storerooms and took all the gold artefacts.
- Authorities blame the Rapid Support Forces, which held that district for most of the war. The force neither funds nor controls the museum.
- National authorities have recovered 570 objects stolen from the museum (UN in Sudan).
- The French archaeological unit SFDAS, with the Louvre and Durham University, built a virtual site to show the lost objects (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money on record is the Sudanese state's and Italian aid routed through UNESCO, and no funder is objected to. The museum is closed and stripped, and its rebuilding waits on funding. Worth watching: who pays for the next phase and how many of the looted objects return.
