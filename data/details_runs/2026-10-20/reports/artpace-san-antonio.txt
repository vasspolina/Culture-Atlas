ARTPACE SAN ANTONIO: San Antonio, TX, USA
ID: artpace-san-antonio
Website: https://artpace.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art residency and exhibition space at 445 N Main Ave, downtown San Antonio (Artpace visit page).
- Founded in 1993 by the artist, collector and businesswoman Linda Pace (1945 to 2007) and opened to the public in 1995; Robert Storr, then senior curator at the Museum of Modern Art, chose the inaugural artists (Artpace site). Wikipedia says she launched it through the Pace-Roberts Foundation for Contemporary Art, since renamed the Linda Pace Foundation.
- The building is a former 1920s Hudson automobile dealership with 18,000 square feet, five exhibition spaces, an archives and research library, a workshop and event areas (Wikipedia).
- The International Artist-in-Residence programme invites nine artists a year, chosen by guest curators, to live and work in three furnished apartments; more than 250 artists have taken part since 1995 (Wikipedia). Since 2001 the residencies have used a guest-curator format (Artpace site).
- Other programmes: education, exhibitions, rentals and an archive and resource library (Artpace site).
- Open Monday to Friday 10:00 to 17:00 and Saturday and Sunday 12:00 to 17:00; admission free. The building is ADA-compliant with an elevator and accessible bathrooms; sign-language interpreters, large-print notes and audio guides are available on request (Artpace visit page).

FUNDING
Artpace San Antonio: mainly private: the Linda Pace Foundation, with federal, state, city and foundation grants
- Linda Pace Foundation: on 27 May 2022 it gave $250,000 to Artpace's new Artist Residency Endowment Fund and released the remaining principal of an annuity Linda Pace set up in 2005, a final $1,196,000 for the operations of the residency programme. With the gift, Artpace had raised more than $2.5 million in the first 16 months of its endowment campaign (Artpace, 2022).
- National Endowment for the Arts: $15,000 under Grants for Arts Projects in fiscal year 2022 (San Antonio Current, 12 January 2022).
- Artpace's 2017 residency announcement names as supporters the Kronkosky Charitable Foundation, the National Endowment for the Arts, the Texas Commission on the Arts, the Linda Pace Foundation, the San Antonio Department for Culture & Creative Development, The Brown Foundation, Inc. and its members (Artpace, 2017). Footers on the same page also list The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts and the Parker Foundation among supporters.
- The Artpace support page shows the logos of the Linda Pace Foundation, the National Endowment for the Arts, the Santikos Foundation and the Texas Commission on the Arts, with Mid-America Arts Alliance and Get Creative San Antonio.
- ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer lists Artpace Inc revenue of $1,648,130, expenses of $1,447,940 and contributions of $1,283,714 for the fiscal year ending December 2025.
- Where the foundation's money comes from: Wikipedia says Linda Pace's parents owned Pace Foods; she and her husband Christopher Goldsbury bought the company from her mother in September 1982, she sold him her share after their 1991 divorce, and Pace Foods was sold to Campbell's in 1994.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Artpace is a private foundation set up by the founder, plus federal, state, city and foundation arts grants, with, per Wikipedia, a food-company fortune behind the founder. The weak point is dependence on the Linda Pace Foundation, which gave a final annuity payment of $1,196,000 in 2022; its board president Kathryn Kanjo said Artpace's financial independence lets the foundation focus on its own mission at Ruby City. The endowment campaign is the item to watch.
