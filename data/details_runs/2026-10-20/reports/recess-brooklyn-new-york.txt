RECESS: Brooklyn, USA
ID: recess-brooklyn-new-york
Website: https://www.recessart.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit arts organisation at 46 Washington Avenue, Brooklyn, its address since 2018 (Recess website).
- Allison Freedman Weisberg founded Recess in 2009, opening in SoHo, Manhattan, at 41 Grand Street; it became the registered non-profit Recess Activities Inc. in 2010 (Recess website).
- Session, its artist residency programme, began with its first artist in 2009; Assembly, launched in 2017 at 370 Schermerhorn Street, supports system-impacted youth through art and storytelling; a Critical Writing fellowship began in 2011 (Recess website).
- Recess is led by co-directors in a shared-power model; Shaun Leonardo co-directed from 2021 to 2024, Lindsay C. Harris joined in 2023 and Ladi'Sasha Jones in 2025 (Recess website).
- In 2024 Recess seated its first compensated, consensus-based board (Recess website).
- In September 2025 it hosted the New York Pavilion of the Gaza Biennale, "From Gaza to the World", the Biennale's first North American host site, with 25 of the more than 50 artists in the global exhibition (Hyperallergic, Diba Mohtasham, 12 September 2025). An abbreviated version ran until 20 December 2025.
- Open Thursday to Saturday 12 pm to 5 pm; the public programme space is wheelchair accessible and the restrooms are gender neutral and ADA compliant (Recess website).

FUNDING
Recess: grant-funded non-profit: public arts agencies, foundations and individual donors
- Government: Recess's timeline records its first grants from the National Endowment for the Arts and the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs in 2013 and from the New York State Council on the Arts in 2016 (Recess website).
- Andy Warhol Foundation: a first grant in 2011 (Recess website); the Warhol Foundation's grant archive lists a Multi-year Program Support grant of $100,000 in the Spring 2018 cycle.
- Other foundations whose first grants the timeline records, with year: VIA Art Fund (2015), Donald and Shelley Rubin Foundation (2017), Pinkerton Foundation (Youth Justice, 2017), Art for Justice Fund (2018), ELMA Philanthropies (2019), Mellon Foundation (2021), Ford Foundation (2022), New York Community Trust (2023).
- Individuals and companies: a $2 million donation from MacKenzie Scott in 2021, Alloy Development's support for the opening of Assembly's Schermerhorn Street space in 2017, and the Ruth Arts Artist Choice award in 2025 (Recess website).

BOTTOM LINE
Recess is funded by public arts agencies, named foundations and individual gifts, including a $2 million donation from MacKenzie Scott in 2021 and a $100,000 Warhol Foundation multi-year grant in 2018. Its timeline also records first grants from the Mellon Foundation in 2021 and the Ford Foundation in 2022.
