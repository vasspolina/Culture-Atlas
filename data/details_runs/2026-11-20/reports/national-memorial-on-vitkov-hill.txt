NATIONAL MEMORIAL ON VÍTKOV HILL: Prague, Czechia
ID: national-memorial-on-vitkov-hill
Website: https://www.vhu.cz/en/vitkov-national-memorial-with-the-tomb-of-the-unknown-soldier/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial to Czech statehood on top of Vítkov Hill in the Žižkov district of Prague, above the Army Museum Žižkov at the foot of the hill (Wikipedia, Military History Institute).
- Built to a design by Jan Zázvorka to honour the Czechoslovak legionaries of the First World War; President T. G. Masaryk attended the laying of the foundation on 8 November 1928, the shell was finished in 1933 and the interior in 1938 (Military History Institute, CZ Defence).
- The opening planned for 28 October 1938 did not take place after the Munich Agreement; from November 1942 the Wehrmacht used the building as a store (Military History Institute, Wikipedia).
- Bohumil Kafka's bronze equestrian statue of the Hussite general Jan Žižka, cast after the war, was unveiled on 14 July 1950; it is 9 metres high and weighs 16.5 tonnes (Wikipedia).
- The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier holds the remains of a soldier from the Battle of Dukla, placed there in 1949, and one from the Battle of Zborov, added in 2010 (Wikipedia).
- After February 1948 the Communist Party made the memorial its pantheon; the Klement Gottwald Mausoleum was set up there in 1953, and Gottwald's body was cremated in 1962; the remains of the regime's leaders were removed after 1989 (Military History Institute, Wikipedia).
- The National Museum ran the memorial from 2000, restored it and reopened it on 29 October 2009 (Wikipedia, Military History Institute).
- State ceremonies attended by the highest constitutional officials and parades of military units are held in the Courtyard of Honour (Military History Institute).
- Open to the public: the Ceremonial Hall, the Presidential Lounge, the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, the Chapel of the Fallen, the columbarium and the exhibition "Symbols of Statehood" in the Central Hall, while the former mausoleum is closed (Military History Institute).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10 AM to 6 PM, entry free; the viewpoint above the statue needs a timed ticket from the exhibition entrance; a wheelchair entrance with a lift is on the east side; the viewpoint is not wheelchair accessible, and commercial events are not allowed (Military History Institute).

FUNDING
National Memorial on Vítkov Hill: state-owned: Czech Ministry of Defence
- Since 1 June 2024 the memorial has been the property of the Ministry of Defence, administered by the Military History Institute Prague (Military History Institute).
- The takeover from the National Museum took about two and a half years. In 2024 the access roads and the Courtyard of Honour were still under Prague City Hall, which the ministry was working to take over (VHÚ director Brig. Gen. Aleš Knížek, CZ Defence, 2024).
- The National Museum, which ran the memorial from 2000 to 2024, financed its major reconstruction together with the Ministry of Culture (Knížek, CZ Defence).
- The Ministry of Defence, through the Military History Institute, paid for the reconstruction of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, which the National Museum carried out (Knížek, CZ Defence).
- The ministry awarded the contract to build the new permanent exhibition "Statehood: Symbols of Statehood" to the Prague firm M plus for just under CZK 37 million of state money, after a tender between two firms decided on lowest price (Ekonomický deník, 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the memorial is Czech state money and carries no flag. Worth watching: how the new statehood exhibition presents the country's history under army management.
