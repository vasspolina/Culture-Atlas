MAC MONTRÉAL: Montréal, Canada
ID: mac-montr-al
Website: https://macm.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art founded by the Quebec government in 1964, described as the first institution in Canada devoted exclusively to contemporary art (Wikipedia).
- Its building at Place des Arts opened on 28 May 1992 in the Quartier des spectacles; Jodoin Lamarre Pratte & Associés won the 1983 to 1984 architecture competition (Wikipedia).
- Its earlier homes were Place Ville-Marie (1964 to 1965), Château Dufresne (1965 to 1968) and a gallery at Cité du Havre from 1968 to 1992 (Wikipedia).
- In June 2017 the museum announced a design contest after criticism that it was closed off from its surroundings; Saucier+Perrotte / GLCRM won in April 2018 (Wikipedia).
- During the renovation and extension the museum is installed temporarily at Place Ville Marie, where it showed femmes volcans forêts torrents and Alanis Obomsawin: les enfants doivent entendre une autre histoire in 2024 to 2025 (2024-25 annual report).
- Entry has been free for Indigenous visitors since April 2024, and more than 15,500 people joined 719 activities in 2024 to 2025 (2024-25 annual report).
- Director general Stéphan La Roche signed the 2024-25 report on 9 July 2025; John Zeppetelli left in September 2024; Claudie Imbleau-Chagnon chairs the board (2024-25 annual report).

FUNDING
MAC Montréal: mainly public: Quebec government crown corporation, with private donors through the MAC Foundation
- The museum became a société d'État on 16 May 1984 under the Loi sur les musées nationaux, with a board of directors in charge; before that it reported directly to the Ministry (2024-25 annual report).
- Statements for the year to 31 March 2025 were audited by the Auditor General of Quebec and the report is addressed to the Minister of Culture (2024-25 annual report).
- Revenue in 2024-25: Quebec government grants 13,015,643 dollars (12,535,454 in the operations fund and 480,189 in the capital fund), Canadian government grants 346,027, donated artworks 403,690, and sponsorships and other donations 1,522,705 (2024-25 annual report).
- The MAC Foundation raised 1,760,269 dollars in 2024-25 through its annual campaign, the Bal du MAC, Les Printemps du MAC and the Symposium des collectionneurs Banque Nationale Gestion privée 1859 (2024-25 annual report).
- The Foundation's 20 million dollar campaign "L'art de transformer", launched in April 2024 and co-chaired by Claudine and Stephen Bronfman, had passed half its goal; private gifts complete the public investment in the rebuild (2024-25 annual report).
- The Foundation's donor list includes the Claudine and Stephen Bronfman family foundation, Power Corporation du Canada, Fiera Capital, CDPQ, BMO, RBC Foundation, Banque Nationale, the R. Howard Webster Foundation, the Molson Foundation and individuals (2024-25 annual report).
- The Bal du MAC took place on 14 September 2024 at Royalmount and raised more than 1,000,000 dollars for the Foundation; its governor partners were Air Canada, AtkinsRéalis, Banque Nationale, Banque Scotia, Chanel, Desjardins, Fasken, Fiera Capital, the Bronfman family foundation, Immobilier Carbonleo, KPMG, Québecor Média and RBC Marché des Capitaux (2024-25 annual report).
- The 2024-25 donor list places AtkinsRéalis Canada inc. in the band of 25,000 to 49,999 dollars (2024-25 annual report).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 18 December 2019 SNC-Lavalin's construction division pleaded guilty to one count of fraud over 5,000 dollars over its work in Libya, with a 280 million dollar penalty and three years' probation; the remaining charges, which alleged payments of nearly 48 million dollars to public officials under the Gaddafi regime from 2001 to 2011, were stayed (The Canadian Press, "SNC-Lavalin pleads guilty to fraud, will pay a $280M fine for Libyan work", 18 December 2019). Wikipedia names the convicted company as SNC-Lavalin Construction Inc.
- Connection to the museum: Wikipedia states that SNC-Lavalin is now named AtkinsRéalis. AtkinsRéalis Canada inc. is a donor to the MAC Foundation in the 2024-25 donor list and a governor partner of the Foundation's 2024 gala (2024-25 annual report).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind MAC Montréal is mainly public: Quebec government grants of about 13 million dollars in 2024-25, audited by the Auditor General of Quebec, with private donors giving through the MAC Foundation. AtkinsRéalis, a governor partner of the Foundation's 2024 gala, is the former SNC-Lavalin, whose construction division pleaded guilty to fraud over its Libya work in December 2019.
