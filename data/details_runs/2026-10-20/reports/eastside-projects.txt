EASTSIDE PROJECTS: Birmingham, UK
ID: eastside-projects
Website: https://eastsideprojects.org

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led gallery and commissioning organisation in Digbeth, Birmingham, at 86 Heath Mill Lane, B9 4AR. It describes itself as an "artist-led pluriverse" based in a free public gallery.
- Founded in 2008 by six artists: Simon Bloor, Tom Bloor, Céline Condorelli, Ruth Claxton, James Langdon and Gavin Wade (Wikipedia). It opened on 26 September 2008 with the exhibition "This is the Gallery and the Gallery is Many Things". Current directors are Ruth Claxton and Gavin Wade (Wikipedia).
- Birmingham City University says it set Eastside Projects up in 2008 and works with it in partnership; the gallery says it has worked with the university since 2008.
- Building: the gallery treats its offices and structures as artist commissions. The current staff offices were designed by K4 Architects; an earlier structure, "Pleasure Island", was built by Heather and Ivan Morison (Wikipedia). The gallery closed in 2017 for a refurbishment of about £250,000 and reopened in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Registered as a not-for-profit company limited by guarantee (company number 06402007, incorporated 17 October 2007).
- Open Wednesday to Saturday, 12 to 5pm during exhibitions; admission is free.

FUNDING
Eastside Projects: Arts Council England National Portfolio, Birmingham City University partnership and trust support
- Arts Council England: Eastside Projects describes itself as an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation, in partnership with Birmingham City University (about page). Birmingham City University's news item "Funding gives gallery space to grow" (last updated 8 April 2011) says the gallery secured £375,000 over the next four years, making it a National Portfolio Organisation.
- Birmingham City University: partner since 2008 (about page); Wikipedia says it provides project funding and equipment or facilities, and lists it with Arts Council England, Custard Factory and K4 Architects as supporters of the 2017 refurbishment.
- Paul Hamlyn Foundation: Eastside Projects received a Paul Hamlyn Breakthrough Award (Wikipedia).
- Its about page lists, without dates or amounts, organisations by which it "has been generously supported" or with which it developed projects in partnership. They include the Henry Moore Foundation, Paul Hamlyn Foundation, Jerwood Foundation, Esmée Fairbairn Foundation, William A. Cadbury Charitable Trust, the Elephant Trust, the European Cultural Foundation, the British Council, Heritage Lottery Fund, Canada Council for the Arts, the Mondriaan Foundation, National Express, West Midlands Trains, Chiltern Railways, Genting Casino, McLeman Forklift Services Ltd, Sturge Industries Ltd and the Future Jobs Fund.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Eastside Projects is Arts Council England National Portfolio funding (£375,000 over four years from 2011) and the partnership with Birmingham City University, plus a Paul Hamlyn Breakthrough Award. Its undated supporters list also names trusts such as Esmée Fairbairn, Jerwood and Henry Moore, and Genting Casino.
