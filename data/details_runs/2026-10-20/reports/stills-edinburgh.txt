STILLS: Edinburgh, UK
ID: stills-edinburgh
Website: https://stills.org (checked 2026-10-24). The address https://stills.info/ redirects to a "lander" page with no content, so it is parked.

OVERVIEW
- Centre for photography at 23 Cockburn Street, in Edinburgh's Old Town, with a gallery, production facilities (darkrooms and digital labs), a library, a shop and courses (stated by the space).
- Established in 1977; it grew out of the Scottish Photography Group, which organised "Recent American Still Photography" at the Fruitmarket Gallery in 1976 (stated by the space).
- Its first exhibition, Angus McBean's "A Darker Side of the Moon", was held in 1977 at its original location on the High Street (stated by the space).
- Run as a company (SC063316) and Scottish charity (SC014136); the director is Vivienne Gamble and three board members are named: Jane Martin, Alicia Bruce and Paul Thomson.
- Visiting: gallery open Tuesday to Saturday, 11am to 5pm; the pages read give no admission price.

FUNDING
Stills: public and trust funded, with membership and patron income
- Current funders and supporters named on the website, with no amounts or years: Creative Scotland, City of Edinburgh Council, Project Echo, William Grant Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, MPB, Scottish Library and Information Council's Innovation and Development Fund, Walter Scott Giving Group, McGlashan Trust, Ampersand Foundation, New Park Educational Trust, Dr Guthrie's Association, Bellfield Brewery, Thames & Hudson, Spectrum Photographic, Bloomberg Connects, Hahnemuehle, Stills Friends and Stills Patrons.
- Individual giving: Friends pay 40 pounds a year (25 pounds for students); Patrons give 1,000, 3,000 or 5,000 pounds a year.
- Baillie Gifford does not appear on the current list.
- No reporting found of what Baillie Gifford paid Stills, or in which years.

Red flags: Baillie Gifford funding ended under campaign pressure
- Baillie Gifford is an Edinburgh investment firm that campaigners criticised for investments in arms manufacturers, fossil fuel companies and companies linked to illegal Israeli settlements (Art Basel).
- August 2024: Art Basel reported that "Fruitmarket, Collective, and Stills have all cut ties with the firm" (Tom Jeffreys, Art Basel).
- March 2026: Art Workers for Palestine Scotland, a campaign group, listed Stills among organisations that have refused Baillie Gifford funding, in its call to boycott the Edinburgh International Festival (Bella Caledonia, an openly campaigning outlet).
- No reporting found of a petition or open letter aimed at Stills itself, and Stills' own statement could not be opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Stills is funded by Creative Scotland, Edinburgh's council and charitable trusts, and no reporting found ties it to a fossil fuel, arms or gambling funder at present. It stopped taking Baillie Gifford money in 2024, reported by Art Basel. Worth watching: the amounts, which the space does not publish, and any return of the firm.
