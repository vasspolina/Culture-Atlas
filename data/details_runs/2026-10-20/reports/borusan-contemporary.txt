BORUSAN CONTEMPORARY: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: borusan-contemporary
Website: http://www.borusancontemporary.com/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art, collection and education initiative of the Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation, in the Perili Köşk ("Haunted Mansion"), Baltalimanı Hisar Cad. No:5, Rumelihisarı, Sarıyer, Istanbul.
- The building is also the head office of Borusan Holding; it is open to the public on weekends (Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation).
- Borusan Contemporary opened in September 2011 (Wikipedia); the Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation says it began operating in 2011 as an economic enterprise of the foundation.
- The Perili Köşk was built for the Ottoman ambassador Yusuf Ziya Pasha; construction began in the early 1910s and stopped when the Ottoman Empire entered the First World War. Architect Hakan Kıran led a restoration from 1995 to about 2000, and Borusan Holding began leasing it in 2002 and moved in during 2007 (Wikipedia).
- The foundation was set up in 1992 by Asım Kocabıyık and his family and renamed Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation in 2007 (Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation).
- Programme: the Borusan Contemporary Art Collection, focused on media art, temporary exhibitions (Olivo Barbieri's "Time Space Geometry" and the collection show "Haunted", 19 September 2026 to 15 August 2027), education programmes, a café and a bookstore.
- Visiting: Saturdays and Sundays only, 10:00 to 19:00, last entry 18:00. Adult 350 TL, discounted 100 TL, group 300 TL. Accessibility details were not on the pages read.

FUNDING
Borusan Contemporary: corporate-funded: Borusan Holding, through the Kocabıyık family foundation
- Borusan Holding decided to institutionalise its visual arts activities through Borusan Contemporary (Borusan Kocabıyık Foundation history page). The foundation's pages do not state how much the holding pays or how the foundation is financed.
- The museum sits in the holding's head office; Borusan Holding leased the building in 2002 (Wikipedia).
- Borusan Holding is a Turkish industrial group founded in 1944 by Asım Kocabıyık as İstikbal Ticaret; Ahmet Kocabıyık has chaired it since 2001. Its activities span steel, car distribution, energy and logistics; 2023 revenue was about $7.7 billion (Wikipedia).
- Energy: Borusan EnBW Enerji, owned jointly by Borusan Holding and Germany's EnBW AG, has 725 MW of installed capacity in 12 renewable power plants. On 19 April 2023 the EBRD announced a US$200 million loan to it within a US$600 million package with six banks (EBRD).
- Steel: Borçelik, a joint venture with ArcelorMittal, is described as the biggest galvanized steel manufacturer in Turkey (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts or a funder list for the museum itself. The foundation's 2024 report could not be opened (file too large).
- No reporting found of a sanction, conviction or documented objection against Borusan Holding's funding of the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a single corporate group's: Borusan Holding, owned by the Kocabıyık family, funds it through its family foundation. The weak points are that the museum is run by a corporate group with no published accounts, funder list or amounts, and that the foundation's pages do not say how much the holding pays. Worth watching are the foundation's annual reports.
