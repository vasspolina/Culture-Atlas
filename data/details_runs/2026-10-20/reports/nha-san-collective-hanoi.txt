NHA SAN COLLECTIVE: Hanoi, Vietnam
ID: nha-san-collective-hanoi
Website: https://nhasan.org (checked 2026-10-25; nhasan.org did not resolve on the first attempt and later loaded with only the heading "to be continued" and a menu; the copy at https://mail.nhasan.org/introduces loaded with the text)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit, artist-run group for experimental and contemporary art in Hanoi; its own page gives no street address, only an email and a Facebook page.
- Formed in 2013 by younger artists from Nhà Sàn Studio; Wikipedia credits Nguyen Phuong Linh and Tuan Mami as co-founders.
- Takes its name from Nhà Sàn Studio, founded in 1998 by Nguyễn Mạnh Đức and Trần Lương in Nguyễn's family home, a stilt house. The collective's site says the house was taken in 2020 and that the artists hope to rebuild a space in the Ngọc Thụy area by the Red River.
- Premises have changed often: the Zone 9 art complex, National Medicine Factory No. 2 at 9 Tran Thanh Tong Street (Wikipedia), then 24 Ly Quoc Su Street, where the group show "The Clouds Will Tell" opened in September 2014 (Viet Nam News, 11 September 2014), then Hanoi Creative City (Wikipedia). Art Asia Pacific (2018) wrote of "the closure of the temporary physical space" in December 2017 and gave no reason.
- Programme: exhibitions, open studios and talks; Viet Nam News (2014) describes it as a working studio where artists create, collaborate and receive critiques, not a museum or gallery.
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening days, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
Nha San Collective: volunteer-run: private donations and cultural institutions in Vietnam and abroad (stated by the collective)
- The collective's site says it relies on volunteer work, private donations and support from cultural institutions in Vietnam and abroad, and names none of them (stated by the collective).
- Wikipedia lists Hanoi Creative City, the Goethe-Institut, Manzi Art Space and the Japan Foundation as local partners; it does not say that any of them pays the collective.
- Yale Globalist (Josh Feng, 6 December 2014) quotes Thanh Nguyen, a Nha San member: "contemporary art is simply not funded by the government." It says artists who find funding often rely on sources abroad, naming the Danish Cultural Development and Exchange Fund, the Prince Claus Award and the Goethe Institute, and does not say they fund Nha San.
- Prachatai (Julia Behrens, February 2015): the Old Quarter space is "completely run by volunteers," most of them in their twenties.
- No reporting found of any corporate sponsor, named donor, annual report or accounts.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Art Asia Pacific (1 November 2018, unsigned, "Hanoi") says Nha San Studio was shut in 2011 following state pressure tied to a nude performance. The Yale Globalist (Josh Feng, "Bound to Shackles," 2014) puts a closure in 2010 after a displayed work depicted nudity. The sources differ on the year.
- Prachatai (Julia Behrens, "Free of art or freedom of art: Nha San collective's struggle for space in Vietnam," 2015) says the group has been shut down many times, avoids registering as a formal institution, and still needs permission for public activities.
- Residency Unlimited, describing a 13 June 2019 talk on Vietnam's contemporary art scene, says the field "still receives little support from the government" and increasingly relies on private and corporate money.
- Connection to the collective: it is the successor to the studio that was closed, and it is the group these pieces describe. No source shows the Vietnamese state funding or controlling the collective.

BOTTOM LINE
Nothing published points to a doubtful funder: the collective says it lives on volunteer work and donations and names no sponsor. The weak point is the opposite one, that no funder, account or board is public, so the claim cannot be checked. Worth watching: the main website loaded only with a placeholder heading, and no confirmed physical space is on record.
