ID: miho-museum

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 16 April 2001 Miho Museum administrators signed an agreement with Chinese cultural officials returning ownership of a sixth-century stone bodhisattva, stolen from Shandong Province in 1994, to the Chinese Government without payment; the museum announced the return on 17 April 2001 (International Enforcement Law Reporter, Bruce Zagaris, June 2001; paywalled; only excerpts were read).
- Unverified: unnamed Italian prosecutors suspected that up to 100 ancient Roman treasures were looted and that about 50 of them, including a sculpture and a fresco painting, were at the Miho Museum, from the Yomiuri Shimbun as reported by The Guardian (Justin McCurry, 11 January 2007).
- Japanese officials said they had not been contacted by the Italian authorities (The Guardian, 11 January 2007).
- Hiroaki Katayama, head of the museum's cultural department, said the museum did not hold as many Roman antiques as the report claimed and told the Associated Press: "We believe that our collection does not include anything that was dug up illegally" (The Guardian, 11 January 2007).
