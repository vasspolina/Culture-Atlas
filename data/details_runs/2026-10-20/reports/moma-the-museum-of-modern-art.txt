MOMA (THE MUSEUM OF MODERN ART): New York, USA
ID: moma-the-museum-of-modern-art
Website: https://www.moma.org (checked 2026-10-24; the site's support and visit pages refused automated access, so visiting and sponsor details come from other sources)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum on West 53rd Street in Midtown Manhattan (Wikipedia).
- Conceived by Abby Aldrich Rockefeller, Lillie P. Bliss and Mary Quinn Sullivan; opened on 7 November 1929 at 730 Fifth Avenue, with A. Conger Goodyear as president and Alfred H. Barr Jr. as first director (Wikipedia).
- The museum moved to its current building, designed by Philip L. Goodwin and Edward Durell Stone, in 1939; Diller Scofidio + Renfro with Gensler added 47,000 square feet of galleries in a project that reopened on 21 October 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Glenn Lowry directed it from 1995 and announced in September 2024 that he would step down in 2025 (Hyperallergic, 10 September 2024). Wikipedia lists Christophe Cherix as director.
- Standard adult admission is USD 30; New York State residents enter free on Friday evenings, 17:30 to 20:30, through the Uniqlo Friday Nights programme (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
MoMA: private: admissions, trustees' gifts and endowment, with corporate partners
- MoMA is a private nonprofit that does not accept government funding; revenue was about USD 145 million with an endowment of about USD 650 million at the time of the Wikipedia entry (undated figures).
- David Rockefeller gave USD 77 million in cash toward the 2002 to 2004 expansion and pledged USD 100 million to the endowment in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- The Friday-night free admission runs through the Uniqlo Friday Nights programme (Wikipedia). No page read lists other corporate sponsors with amounts; the museum's support pages were not readable.

Red flags: Leon Black, Epstein payments
- Leon Black chaired the board from 2018 to 2021, leaving the chair citing health reasons after scrutiny over his Epstein links; he remains a trustee (Hyperallergic, 5 February 2026, which says new allegations against Black appear in released files and that he denies them).
- Senator Ron Wyden's findings: Black paid Epstein at least USD 158 million between 2012 and 2017, while CBS News reports USD 170 million; Wyden wrote that Black "has not provided a credible explanation" (The Art Newspaper, 19 August 2025; CBS News, updated 4 June 2026).
- Black's lawyers state that a Dechert review found he paid Epstein only for estate planning and tax advice (CBS News). MoMA did not respond to The Art Newspaper.

Red flags: Glenn Dubin, Epstein links
- Epstein was paid USD 15 million for introducing Dubin to a JPMorgan executive during the 2004 sale of Highbridge Capital and also invested in Dubin's fund; the US Virgin Islands Attorney General moved to subpoena Dubin in September 2020 (Hyperallergic, 10 September 2020). A representative said the Dubins "categorically reject" the allegations.
- Unverified: Virginia Giuffre testified that Ghislaine Maxwell told her to give Dubin a "massage"; Dubin has denied it (Hyperallergic, 4 January 2024).

Red flags: protests aimed at trustees and the museum
- January 2020: 37 artists in the MoMA PS1 show "Theater of Operations" wrote to demand MoMA divest from trustees whose money profits from others' suffering, citing Larry Fink's BlackRock as the second-largest investor in GEO Group and CoreCivic and Black's Apollo owning Constellis, formerly Blackwater (Hyperallergic, 14 January 2020).
- April to June 2021: Strike MoMA, a ten-week coalition action, targeted trustees Black, Steven Tananbaum, Dubin, Steven Cohen and Fink (The Art Newspaper, 24 March 2021).
- 10 February 2024: more than 500 protesters occupied the atrium and MoMA closed its galleries; protesters named Black, Fink, Paula Crown, Marie-Josee Kravis and Ronald Lauder and demanded divestment from trustees they say fund genocide (Hyperallergic, 10 February 2024). Unverified: those investment claims come from the protesters.

BOTTOM LINE
MoMA's operating money is private and large, but the people on its board are the weak point: Black paid Epstein at least USD 158 million and remains a trustee, and Dubin had documented business links to Epstein. Worth watching: whether MoMA acts on Black's trusteeship, and whether it publishes sponsor amounts.
