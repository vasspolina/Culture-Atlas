ALBERTINA: Vienna, Austria
ID: albertina
Website: https://www.albertina.at (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Federal art museum at Albertinaplatz 1 in Vienna's Innere Stadt, with two further sites: Albertina Modern at Karlsplatz 5 and Albertina Klosterneuburg, which opened in April 2024.
- The collection was founded in 1776 by Duke Albert of Saxony-Teschen and Archduchess Marie Christine (stated by the museum). Building and collection passed into state ownership in April 1919 and the house was renamed the Albertina in 1921 (Wikipedia). Since 1 January 2000 it has been a scientific institution under public law and a federal museum (German Wikipedia).
- Directors: Klaus Albrecht Schröder led it from 1999 to 2024; Ralph Gleis has been director general since 1 January 2025 (German Wikipedia).
- Building: a former palace on a site that was part of Vienna's fortifications, extended by Louis Montoyer, heavily damaged by bombing in March 1945 and refurbished from 1998 to 2003; the "Soravia Wing" extension of 2003 drew mostly critical media reaction (Wikipedia, English and German). Albertina Modern occupies the former Künstlerhaus; the Hans Hollein roof over the entrance dates from 2008.
- Collection: one of the world's largest print rooms, with about 65,000 drawings and roughly 1 million old master prints, plus photography and architectural drawings; the Batliner collection has been on long-term loan since 2007 and the Essl Collection of 1,323 works was acquired in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Nazi-era history: works stolen from Jewish collectors entered the collection after the 1938 Anschluss; after the war the museum contested restitution claims for works by Klimt and Schiele, and in 2015 its director called for a time limit on claims (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the main site is open daily 10am to 6pm, until 9pm on Wednesday and Friday; Albertina Klosterneuburg is open Thursday to Sunday 10am to 5pm. The museum had 1,321,385 visits in 2024 (German Wikipedia). Admission prices were not on the pages read; the museum publishes an accessibility statement and an easy-read page.

FUNDING
Albertina: federal museum with corporate partners: the Republic of Austria, Bank Austria, Verbund, BMW and Do&Co
- State: the Republic of Austria owns the building and collection (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the current federal base allocation (Basisabgeltung) in the pages read.
- Annual partners on the museum's sponsorship page: Bank Austria (member of UniCredit) and Verbund. Partners: BMW and Do&Co | DEMEL. The page says partners commit for at least three years and that the partner circle is limited to three companies, yet lists four names. It gives no years or amounts.
- Verbund: the Republic of Austria holds 51% of its shares; about 90% of its output is hydropower, and it ran two gas-fired plants at Mellach, with coal firing there ended in spring 2020 (German Wikipedia).
- Own earnings: no reporting found of the share of the museum's budget it earns itself.
- No reporting found on the Kuratorium's members, the business reports or the 2024 audited accounts, because those pages could not be read.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The only critical reaction found concerned the 2003 Soravia Wing and the museum's handling of restitution claims, and came from Wikipedia summaries rather than from named critics.

BOTTOM LINE
The Albertina is a federal museum with a short list of corporate partners; none of the four named is in a flagged industry on the sources read, and Verbund is mostly hydropower. The weak points are undisclosed sponsor amounts, an unconfirmed share of own earnings, and the museum's past contest of restitution claims.
