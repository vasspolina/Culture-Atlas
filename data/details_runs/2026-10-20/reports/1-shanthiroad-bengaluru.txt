1 SHANTHIROAD STUDIO GALLERY: Bengaluru, India
ID: 1-shanthiroad-bengaluru
Website: https://1shanthiroad.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Art space with a gallery and artist residencies at 1 Shanthi Road, Shanthinagar, in central Bengaluru.
- Founded by Suresh Jayaram, a professor of art history and later dean at Karnataka Chitrakala Parishad from 1993 to 2007 (Temporary Art Review, 2015). The space's own site gives 2002 as the founding year, and the 2015 Temporary Art Review interview says it formally opened in 2002.
- Run by the not-for-profit Visual Art Collective (VAC) Trust.
- Building: the ground floor was Jayaram's childhood home, built in 1985. The space was developed in three phases designed by architect Meeta Jain: Jayaram's residence and the gallery in 2002, a first residency room in 2005 and a second in 2014 (Temporary Art Review). Jayaram still lives on site.
- On site: public exhibition spaces, the Jayaram family living room and living space for resident artists, around a courtyard with a badam tree (The South First). Programmes include residencies (about 150 artists hosted by 2023, per The South First), the NEXT-STEP residency, and talks, movie screenings and workshops (The South First). The space's highlights page lists the "Re-Look" lecture series, begun in 2010, and the Sethusamudram Project, a two-nation project with Theertha of Colombo, Sri Lanka, from 2010.
- Visiting: opening hours, admission and accessibility are not stated on the site pages read; The South First points readers to the space's Instagram account.

FUNDING
1 Shanthiroad Studio Gallery: mainly self-funded by the founder, with project grants for particular residencies and programmes
- Jayaram told Temporary Art Review (2015) that he mortgaged his mother's property and took a bank loan, repaid in monthly instalments from his salaried job. He described private donations as rare and said "polishing the begging bowl has never stopped."
- Project funding: a 2012 curatorial residency at the space was part of the Curatorship Programme, run by Khoj and conceptualised by the India Foundation for the Arts; the Khoj call says "The Curatorship Programme is funded by the Jamsetji Tata Trust".
- The South First (2023) names Khoj, the Goethe-Institut and Fulbright scholars as partners, without saying who pays for what.
- The site names Khoj as a partner for shows of South Asian artists. No funders are named on the site, and no annual report or accounts were found.
- No reporting found of an arts council grant, an endowment or an educational trust paying the space's running costs.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is mainly the founder's own, backed by a mortgage and loan, with project funding from the Jamsetji Tata Trust through Khoj and the India Foundation for the Arts for at least one residency. No fossil fuel, tobacco, arms or Gulf state funder was found. The weak point is the absence of published accounts or a funder list; worth watching is who pays for the NEXT-STEP residency, which the sources read do not say.
