LOUVRE: Paris, France
ID: louvre-paris
Website: https://www.louvre.fr/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum in the former royal palace in the centre of Paris; Philip II began the fortress in the late 12th century (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 10 August 1793 as the Muséum central des Arts de la République, with 537 paintings and 184 objects, mostly from royal collections and confiscated church property (Wikipedia).
- I. M. Pei proposed the Grand Louvre plan in 1984; its glass pyramid has been the main entrance since 1993 (Wikipedia).
- On 19 October 2025 thieves entered through a window of the Galerie d'Apollon using a construction platform and stole jewellery; the museum closed for the day (Wikipedia).
- In January 2025 President Emmanuel Macron announced a renovation and expansion, including a room for the Mona Lisa, with work due to begin in September 2026 (Wikipedia).
- The Louvre had 9.0 million visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia). It lent its name to the Louvre Abu Dhabi, opened in 2017.
- No reporting found of current opening days or prices in the pages opened.

FUNDING
Louvre: mixed: ticket sales, state subsidy and corporate and private patrons
- Ticket sales brought in 125 million euros in 2024, 60% of the museum's own resources, and the Cour des comptes said the museum has "trésorerie extrêmement solide" (Public Sénat, 6 November 2025).
- The 2025 patron list names, for exhibitions, Deloitte, Visa, the Ford Foundation and the Terra Foundation for American Art; for the Tuileries redevelopment, Christian Dior Couture, Colas and Moët Hennessy; for the Olympic cauldron, EDF. Technology supporters include Accenture, EY France and Nintendo. No amounts are posted (stated by the museum).
- Cercle Louvre Entreprises: Deloitte and DS Automobiles are founding members; Bloomberg and Fraîcheur de Paris are associate members. Stellantis Auto, Louis Vuitton, Mastercard and Uniqlo are among the sponsors of restorations (stated by the museum).
- The Al Waleed bin Talal Foundation helped finance the 2012 Islamic art section (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the state subsidy in the sources opened.

Red flags: Opioids, Sackler name
- The Louvre's Sackler Wing of Oriental Antiquities was inaugurated in 1997. On 1 July 2019 the group PAIN, led by Nan Goldin, protested outside the Louvre and demanded the wing be renamed (Apollo, 17 July 2019).
- The name was removed from the galleries; photographs PAIN gave The Art Newspaper showed it on plaques a few weeks earlier. President Jean-Luc Martinez said on radio that the removal followed a 20-year limit on donor naming rights (Apollo, citing The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Total sponsorship
- The campaign Libérons le Louvre, with 350.org, called in 2017 for an end to "two decades of sponsorship" by Total; about 40 activists staged an oil-spill action on 5 March 2017, and a petition had about 10,000 signatures (The Art Newspaper, 7 March 2017).
- Martinez defended the sponsorship in a January 2017 letter, citing support for International Women's Day events and the renovation of the Islamic Arts displays (The Art Newspaper).
- Greenpeace says TotalEnergies has funded Louvre exhibitions for over 20 years and filed in December 2021 at the Paris administrative court for the papers. The Louvre says it has had no direct partnership with the Fondation TotalEnergies since 2018 and that its donor list is public (L'Info Durable, 7 December 2021). No ruling is reported there.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Cour des comptes report of 6 November 2025 (covering 2018 to 2024) said the Louvre favoured "les opérations visibles et attractives" over upkeep and security: 87 million euros on maintenance versus 169 million on redesign and acquisitions, and 3 million euros spent of an 83 million euro security plan (Public Sénat, Romain David).
- It put the Nouvelle Renaissance project at 1.15 billion euros against 700 to 800 million according to the Élysée, and called its patronage assumptions fragile.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly the museum's own ticket sales plus corporate and foundation patrons. The weak points are the Sackler Wing, renamed in 2019 after a documented protest, and Total, which campaigners and the Louvre describe differently since 2018. Watch the patron list for fossil-fuel names, and the funding of the Nouvelle Renaissance project.
