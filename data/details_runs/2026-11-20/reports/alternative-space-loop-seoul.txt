ALTERNATIVE SPACE LOOP: Seoul, South Korea
ID: alternative-space-loop-seoul
Website: http://altspaceloop.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art space on the 3rd floor of 172-1 Cheonggyecheon-ro, Jung District, Seoul, beside the Cheonggyecheon stream, where it reopened on 16 December 2025 (LOOP news post of 8 December 2025; e-flux directory).
- Opened in 1999 in the Hongdae area of Seoul, in Mapo District, as what it calls Korea's first alternative space (e-flux directory; LOOP support page; K-ARTNOW).
- Re-established in May 2019 as a non-profit incorporated association run by a board and "LOOP's Friends", a group of donors (LOOP support page).
- e-flux lists nine board members and eighty general members.
- The support page names Mi Jin Kim and Suh Soo Hyun as chairs and Jeon Joonho as auditor.
- Director Ji Yoon Yang joined as a curator in 2006 and became director in autumn 2017 (Whitewall, Katy Donoghue, 20 November 2024); Sun Mi Lee is curator (LOOP site).
- Programme: shows chosen through an annual international open call, the Feminist Art School festival of May 2026 (organised by Sound Art Korea, curated by Yang and Baruch Gottlieb) and Sound Effects Seoul 2026 Euljiro (20 to 28 November 2026), which LOOP organises (LOOP site).
- 2026 shows include Yunjoo Kwak, "The Chronicle of Boost Barracks" (27 February to 31 March) and Minja Gu, "Middle Ground: (Im)possible Kitchen" (16 October to 14 November) (e-flux; LOOP site).
- Its mission statement for 2026 to 2027 sets out practices "grounded in eco-socialism and feminism" and objects to art treated as a financial asset (LOOP).
- Visiting: Monday to Sunday, 10 am to 7 pm; activities are free and open to everyone (e-flux directory).

FUNDING
Alternative Space LOOP: member-funded non-profit, with public grants for single projects
- Member donations: LOOP's Friends give by monthly automatic transfer; individual and corporate members are accepted and gifts are tax-deductible (LOOP support page; 2025 donation report). The 2025 list names 35 Friends. Yang told Whitewall (2024) that board members and other members have sponsored LOOP monthly since 2019, which she said made it "more stable".
- 2025 accounts: in its annual donation statement to the National Tax Service, dated 24 April 2026, LOOP reported 23,059,420 won in donations received and 20,198,144 won spent. Staff wages took 10,886,140 won, social insurance 2,374,840 won and tax accountant fees 2,116,590 won; a building leak repair cost 605,500 won and the move 484,000 won. The filing gives 12 April 2019 as the date it was designated a public-interest corporation.
- Arts Council Korea: its visual arts creators programme is credited as sponsor of the reopening show "Painting-like" (16 December 2025 to 31 January 2026) and of the Yunjoo Kwak show (LOOP exhibition pages).
- Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture and CBK Rotterdam are also credited on the Yunjoo Kwak show (LOOP exhibition page).
- Sound Effects Seoul 2026 Euljiro is supported by the Seoul Metropolitan Government's private festival support programme, the Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture and Arts Council Korea (LOOP site).

Red flags: December 2025 questionnaire over the move to Euljiro
- On 15 December 2025 the Cheonggyecheon-Euljiro Preservation Alliance, Listen to the City and the Feminist Designer Social Club posted an open questionnaire on Instagram addressed to LOOP and asked it to answer publicly (K-ARTNOW, 23 December 2025).
- The groups say Sanlim-dong, where LOOP moved, is one of the few metal manufacturing clusters left in Euljiro, exposed to both full redevelopment and gentrification, and an area activists and residents have worked to protect.
- They warned that gentrification set off by cultural spaces could drive out existing merchants faster and more harshly than full redevelopment, and asked LOOP to answer publicly on its position on gentrification.
- In its mission statement LOOP pledges: "We will not commodify Euljiro's manufacturing ecologies, questions of migration, labor, and housing, or the tensions and conflicts produced by redevelopment and gentrification as raw material for cultural products" (stated by the space).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One report carries a public challenge to the space over its move; the other piece is an interview with the director, not criticism.
1. K-ARTNOW: A Team, "Korea's First-Generation Alternative Space 'Alternative Space LOOP' Moves to Euljiro" (2025)
URL: https://k-artnow.com/ko/posts.php?co_id=1766074549
- Reports the questionnaire alongside LOOP's mission statement; it gives the questioners' arguments, not its own.
- Title translated from Korean.
2. Whitewall: Katy Donoghue, "Ji Yoon Yang Continues the Evolution of Alternative Space LOOP as its Director" (2024)
URL: https://whitewall.art/art/ji-yoon-yang-continues-the-evolution-of-alternative-space-loop-as-its-director/
- Not criticism: Yang describes how the 2008 financial crisis forced LOOP to "reshape everything just to survive", and how the pandemic closed it.

BOTTOM LINE
LOOP's money is clean: members' gifts and public arts grants for single projects. Its weak point is the public challenge over its move into Euljiro. Worth watching: whether LOOP answers the questionnaire and how its Euljiro programme treats the trades around it.
