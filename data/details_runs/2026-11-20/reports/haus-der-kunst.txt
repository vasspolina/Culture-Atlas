HAUS DER KUNST: Munich, Germany
ID: haus-der-kunst
Website: https://www.hausderkunst.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting exhibition house for contemporary art at Prinzregentenstraße 1, on the edge of the Englischer Garten (hausderkunst.de, Abendzeitung).
- It opened on 18 July 1937 as the "Haus der Deutschen Kunst", one of the Nazi regime's first prestige building projects and a central venue for its art policy and propaganda (hausderkunst.de). Paul Ludwig Troost designed it, and it hosted the Great German Art Exhibition each year from 1937 to 1944 (The Art Newspaper).
- Andrea Lissoni and Bianca Knall are its managing directors. It counted nearly 440,000 visits in 2024, its highest ever; "In anderen Räumen", on women artists' environments 1956 to 1976, drew over 150,000 (Haus der Kunst press release).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Monday 10 to 20, closed Tuesday; free entry on the last Friday of each month from 16 to 22 (hausderkunst.de).

FUNDING
Haus der Kunst: mainly public: the Free State of Bavaria, with Munich private foundations
- It is run by the Stiftung Haus der Kunst München, gemeinnützige Betriebsgesellschaft mbH. A ministerial official of the Bavarian ministry for science and the arts, Angelika Kaus, chairs its supervisory board (Haus der Kunst press release).
- Its shareholders are the Free State of Bavaria, through that ministry, and the Gesellschaft der Freunde der Stiftung Haus der Kunst München e.V. (hausderkunst.de).
- Bavaria's subsidy was EUR 4.4 million in 2018, against EUR 3.2 million in 2017 (The Art Newspaper).
- The Alexander Tutsek-Stiftung is its major supporter, and has been its main supporter since 2016 (hausderkunst.de, Alexander Tutsek-Stiftung). The foundation was set up in December 2000 in Munich by the entrepreneur Alexander Tutsek and Eva-Maria Fahrner-Tutsek (Alexander Tutsek-Stiftung).
- Further supporters are the Ulli und Uwe Kai-Stiftung and the Beisheim Stiftung (hausderkunst.de).
- For the renovation, Bavaria pledged EUR 58 million in 2012, and in November 2015 the Bundestag budget committee added EUR 20 million, for EUR 78 million in public money (Haus der Kunst press release, 2015).
- In late 2018 a Markus Lüpertz show needed a sponsor; Walter Smerling, chairman of the Bonn Stiftung für Kunst und Kultur, offered to ask his members to pay for it (Artforum).

Red flags: 2017 dismissal of a Scientologist personnel manager and the 2019 court case
- In March 2017 director Okwui Enwezor dismissed an external personnel manager, who had worked there since 1995, after staff complained that he put "great psychological pressure" on them and discriminated against non-Scientologists in hiring; Bavaria's intelligence agency examined whether Scientologists had infiltrated the institution (Artforum).
- In 2019 a Munich labour court sided with him on religious discrimination; the museum settled with a severance of USD 123,000 and a full pension (Artforum).

Red flags: 2018 deficit and cancelled exhibitions
- In 2018 the museum, which predecessor Chris Dercon had left with EUR 1.5 million in capital, was EUR 500,000 in the red; its 2016 accounts were delivered late, so the supervisory board was slow to see the shortfall (The Art Newspaper).
- It cancelled Joan Jonas and Adrian Piper retrospectives citing "management errors of the past"; Enwezor, who had left in June 2018, rejected the blame (The Art Newspaper, Artforum).

Red flags: Delays and rising cost of the renovation planned since 2012
- The renovation by David Chipperfield was put at about EUR 80 million in 2017, and at EUR 150 million in 2018 (The Art Newspaper).
- In January 2020 the Bavarian parliament postponed it again; a firm time and cost plan was moved to 2021 (Abendzeitung).

Red flags: Beisheim Stiftung, after a 2005 dispute over Otto Beisheim's SS past
- In late 2005 a Bavarian school asked for an investigation before taking Beisheim's gift; Bavaria's Culture Ministry then said Beisheim, a co-owner of Metro AG, had been a low-ranking SS member, with no indication that he took part in war crimes (JTA, 2006).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on management, programming and how the renovation treats the Nazi building, not on its funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Should Munich contemporary art museum reveal or obscure its Nazi history?" (2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/03/01/should-munich-contemporary-art-museum-reveal-or-obscure-its-nazi-history
- Critics, among them Green lawmaker Sepp Dürr and architectural historian Winfried Nerdinger, argued that rebuilding the steps and clearing the trees glorified Nazi architecture.
- Chipperfield replied that the building no longer deserves "punishment" for its past.
2. Artforum: news report, "Munich's Haus der Kunst Under Scrutiny" (2019)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/news/munichs-haus-der-kunst-under-scrutiny-241788/
- Relays Jörg Heiser's Süddeutsche Zeitung critique: commercialisation, dependence on private art consultants, and a return to showing established German men.
- Quotes Enwezor calling the decisions "illiberal and non-transparent".

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly Bavarian state money with Munich private foundations, and no flagged industry funder. The weak points are governance, a stalled public renovation and the Beisheim name among its supporters.
