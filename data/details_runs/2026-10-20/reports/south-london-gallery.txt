SOUTH LONDON GALLERY: London, UK
ID: south-london-gallery
Website: https://www.southlondongallery.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery on Peckham Road, south London, with a main site at 65 to 67 Peckham Road (galleries, bookshop, gardens, the café South London Louie) and the Fire Station galleries at 82 Peckham Road (South London Gallery).
- Its origins lie in the South London Working Men's College, set up by the philanthropist William Rossiter in 1868 to "bring art to the people of South London" (South London Gallery). Wikipedia says the South London Fine Art Gallery opened on Peckham Road on 4 May 1891.
- Wikipedia says the London Borough of Southwark took over responsibility for the gallery in 1965 under independent trustees, that David Thorp was appointed director in 1992 and that Margot Heller became director in 2001.
- Building: the former Peckham Road Fire Station, built in 1867 in Victorian domestic Gothic style, houses the archive gallery at 82 Peckham Road (South London Gallery); The Art Newspaper dates the Fire Station opening to September 2018.
- Programmes: exhibitions, Art Block, a space on the Sceaux Gardens estate directly behind the gallery, and two permanent gardens, the Orozco Garden and the Fox Garden (South London Gallery; Galleries Now, 2025).
- Visiting: galleries open Wednesday 12pm to 9pm and Thursday to Sunday 12pm to 6pm, closed Monday and Tuesday; admission is free (South London Gallery).

FUNDING
South London Gallery: mixed public and private: Arts Council England and Southwark Council alongside foundations, Bloomberg Philanthropies support and patrons
- Ongoing gallery support named on its supporters page: Arts Council England, Southwark Council, The Bukhman Foundation, Bloomberg Philanthropies and Garfield Weston Foundation.
- Corporate partners: arcarta, Articheck, Artsy, Bloomberg, Crozier, El Rayo Tequila, Hallett Independent, Hollybush Gardens, Stockdale and Wedlake Bell.
- Exhibition support: Art Fund, Gerald Fox and Edwin Fox Foundation, Hauser & Wirth, Institut für Auslandsbeziehungen, kurimanzutto, Marian Goodman Gallery, Simon Nixon, SP Lohia Foundation, The Bukhman Foundation, The Foundation Foundation, The Henry Moore Foundation, The Paul and Louise Cooke Endowment and The Woven Foundation.
- Communities and learning support: Art Fund, BBC Children in Need, Cathy Wills Charitable Trust, Hauser & Wirth, Newcomen Collett Educational Foundation, four Southwark Council funds, The Clore Duffield Foundation, The Headley Trust, The Mildred Fund, The Austin Hope and Pilkington Trust, The Garcia Family Foundation and United St Saviour's Charity.
- Sustainability support: Foyle Foundation, Matthew Slotover and Emily King, and Wolfson Foundation.
- Bukhman Foundation: in March 2025 a two-year grant to secure Art Block, covering staff costs, artists' fees, materials, activities and events; the foundation was established by Anastasia and Igor Bukhman (Galleries Now, 4 March 2025).
- Fire Station campaign: lead funders were the National Lottery Heritage Fund, the Mayor of London and Arts Council England; principal supporters included Art Happens (with Art Fund), Christie's, City Bridge Trust, Garfield Weston Foundation, Pilgrim Trust, Wolfson Foundation and Viridor Credits; founding gallery donors included Gagosian, Hauser & Wirth, Lisson Gallery, White Cube and David Zwirner.
- Sackler gift returned: in 2016 the Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation offered £125,000 for an education centre in the Fire Station. The trustees, chaired by Matthew Slotover, voted by majority in 2018 to return the grant after it had been banked, because of potential reputational damage; the article notes that much of the Sackler money comes from OxyContin proceeds, and other donors then funded the education centre (The Art Newspaper, Martin Bailey, 22 March 2019).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 more than 260 UK artists published an open letter asking five London theatres, the National Theatre, the Royal Shakespeare Company, the Young Vic, the Royal Court and the Almeida, to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies over its links to Israeli settlements (The New Arab).
- Connection: Bloomberg Philanthropies is listed under "Ongoing Gallery Support" on the South London Gallery's current supporters page. The letter named no gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the South London Gallery is mixed: public funding from Arts Council England, Southwark Council, the Mayor of London and the Lottery, with foundations, galleries and patrons. The trustees returned a Sackler grant in 2018, and Bloomberg Philanthropies, an ongoing supporter, was the target of a May 2026 open letter to five London theatres that did not name the gallery.
