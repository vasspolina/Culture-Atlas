DESTE FOUNDATION FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Athens, Greece
ID: deste-foundation-for-contemporary-art
Website: https://deste.gr

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art foundation with its exhibition space in Nea Ionia, Athens, at Filellinon 11 and Em. Pappa Street (stated by the space); "DESTE" is the Greek word for "look".
- Founded in 1983 by the collector Dakis Joannou, in Geneva according to the foundation's own site.
- In 1998 it moved into a former paper factory in Neo Psychiko redesigned by Christian Hubert; from 1983 to 1996 its Athens shows were held at the House of Cyprus (Wikipedia).
- Since 2006 its main space has been a former sock factory built in 1931 and renovated by Christos Papoulias; it includes a library for research and education on art and design (stated by the space).
- Programmes: exhibitions from the Dakis Joannou Collection, the DESTE Prize for a young Greek artist awarded every two years, the destefashioncollection, and collaborations with the Museum of Cycladic Art and the Benaki Museum.
- Hydra: in 2008 the Municipality of the Island of Hydra granted DESTE the island's old slaughterhouse, which has been the DESTE Project Space Slaughterhouse since 2009 and hosts summer exhibitions, open June to October and closed on Tuesdays (stated by the space).

FUNDING
DESTE Foundation for Contemporary Art: private foundation of the collector Dakis Joannou, a construction and hotel businessman
- Joannou established the foundation in 1983 (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia describes Joannou's core business as construction, focused on the Middle East, North Africa, Greece and Southeastern Europe, and lists his other holdings as hotels, Coca-Cola bottling, shipping, aviation and real estate.
- The Municipality of Hydra granted the old slaughterhouse building in 2008 (stated by the space).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Kara L. Rooney (The Brooklyn Rail, ArtSeen, "Skin Fruit: Selections from the Dakis Joannou Collection", April 2010) criticises the New Museum show curated by Jeff Koons: showmanship rather than a refined focus on craft, large works dominating while smaller pieces get lost, and a lesson in art market politics that favours male artists and "bad-boy" works. She concludes the show succeeds at sparking discussion about collecting, the art market and the museum's dual role as commercial venue and educational institution.
- Rooney calls Joannou "founder of the DESTE Foundation in Greece", says he "funded the fourth floor of the museum's gallery spaces" and notes he sits on the museum's board of trustees. The review does not criticise DESTE.
- Connection: the show was a 2010 New Museum exhibition of Joannou's collection, guest-curated by Koons, which Wikipedia lists among DESTE's activities.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind DESTE is its founder Dakis Joannou, whose businesses are construction and hotels; the Hydra slaughterhouse was granted by the island's municipality in 2008. A 2010 Brooklyn Rail review criticised the New Museum show of his collection, where he was a trustee and funded the fourth floor.
