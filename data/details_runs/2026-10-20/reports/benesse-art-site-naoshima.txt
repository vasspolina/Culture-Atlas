BENESSE ART SITE NAOSHIMA: Naoshima, Japan
ID: benesse-art-site-naoshima
Website: https://benesse-artsite.jp (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- The collective name for all art activities run by Benesse Corporation and the Fukutake Foundation on Naoshima and Teshima in Kagawa Prefecture and Inujima in Okayama Prefecture (stated by Benesse Art Site).
- Benesse Corporation began on Naoshima in 1989 with a campsite, opened Benesse House, a hotel and art museum complex, in 1992, started the Art House Project in the Honmura district in 1998, and opened the Chichu Art Museum in 2004 (Mecenat, a Japanese corporate philanthropy association).
- Soichiro Fukutake, Honorary Chairman of the Fukutake Foundation and General Producer of the Setouchi Triennale, began visiting Naoshima to continue his father's project of building a children's campsite there (stated by Benesse Art Site).
- Buildings: the Chichu Art Museum and Benesse House were designed by Tadao Ando (Wikipedia, Naoshima). Chichu holds site-specific works by James Turrell and Walter De Maria and paintings by Claude Monet.
- Museums listed on the site: Naoshima New Museum of Art, Chichu, Benesse House Museum, Teshima Art Museum and Inujima Seirensho Art Museum, plus the Lee Ufan Museum, the Art House Project and the Hiroshi Sugimoto Gallery.
- Visiting: online tickets are required for all art facilities on the three islands since 1 October 2024; prices, opening days and accessibility are on separate pages not read.

FUNDING
Benesse Art Site Naoshima: corporate-funded: Benesse Corporation and the Fukutake family, now with private equity ownership
- The activities are conducted by Benesse Corporation and the Fukutake Foundation (Benesse Art Site). The site's pages do not explain how the project is funded.
- Fukutake describes a concept he calls "public interest capitalism": corporations set up foundations that become major shareholders, and the dividends fund the foundations' work (Benesse Art Site).
- Benesse Corporation, an education and publishing company in Okayama, was founded in 1955 as Fukutake Publishing by Tetsuhiko Fukutake. Soichiro Fukutake succeeded his father as president in 1986 (Wikipedia, Benesse).
- On 10 November 2023 BPEA EQT, an EQT fund, announced a tender offer to take Benesse Holdings private in partnership with the founding family; Benesse's board recommended it (EQT press release). Wikipedia states that on 3 March 2024 it was announced that EFU Investments Ltd. and a fund managed by BPEA EQT had completed the purchase of a 70.1% stake; one reference on that page gives 70.21%. The EQT release itself gives no ownership figure and does not mention EFU.
- No reporting found of the Fukutake Foundation's income or donors, or of any change to the art site's funding after the 2024 sale. The Fukutake Education and Culture Foundation in Okayama (fukutake.or.jp) funds education and culture grants and lists the art site among linked organisations; its pages show no funding of the museums.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, arms, gambling or tobacco funder.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In July 2014 Benesse Corporation announced a leak of customer personal data, with a maximum of about 20.7 million records; police began an investigation on 7 July (Gigazine, 9 July 2014). The Gigazine article does not mention Naoshima or the art site.
- Connection: the leak concerns the company that funds the art site; no link to the museums was found.

BOTTOM LINE
The art is paid for by a private education company and its founding family, with no flagged funder found; since March 2024 EFU Investments and a BPEA EQT fund hold a 70.1% stake in the company, according to Wikipedia. The weak points are the lack of any published donor list or accounts for the Fukutake Foundation, and the unexplained effect of the buyout on who pays for the museums. Worth watching is the foundation's reports for any change after the sale.
