MUSEO ARCHEOLOGICO NAZIONALE DI NAPOLI: Naples, Italy
ID: museo-archeologico-nazionale-di-napoli
Website: https://www.museoarcheologiconapoli.it/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- National archaeological museum at Piazza Museo 18/19, Naples, known as the MANN (museum website).
- It grew from Charles of Bourbon's plan, after he took the throne of Naples in 1734, to excavate the Vesuvian towns and bring part of his Farnese inheritance to Naples; it opened officially as the Real Museo Borbonico in 1816 and was renamed National Museum after Italian unification in 1860 (museum website).
- Building: a late 16th-century royal cavalry barracks that housed the University of Naples from 1616 to 1777, then enlarged by the architects Fuga and Schiantarelli; the paintings left for Capodimonte in 1957, leaving a purely archaeological museum (museum website).
- What is there: the Farnese collection and gems, Egyptian, epigraphic and Magna Graecia collections, Pompeii frescoes, furnishings and a scale model, the Villa of the Papyri finds and the Secret Room, plus a library and a photographic archive (museum website).
- The numismatic section reopened on 25 May 2026 with more than 6,000 coins, medals and dies (Finestre sull'Arte).
- The Alexander Mosaic from the Casa del Fauno is off display for restoration, watched through two viewing points; the second phase began on 3 July 2024 (museum website; Finestre sull'Arte). Since 5 February 2025 the entrance has moved to the west door for building works (museum website).
- Director general: Francesco Sirano, formerly director of the Herculaneum archaeological park, named in July 2025 (Artribune). Paolo Giulierini had been reconfirmed in July 2019 (Finestre sull'Arte).

FUNDING
Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli: public: the Italian Ministry of Culture
- Status: one of the ministry's autonomous museums (Finestre sull'Arte, 2019) and one of five first-tier museums whose director the Culture Minister appoints after a public call (Finestre sull'Arte, 2025).
- Governance: a board of trustees chaired by Sirano, with members appointed by the Culture Minister in agreement with the Economy Minister and by the Consiglio superiore per i beni culturali e paesaggistici, sets its programme under ministry directives (museum website).
- Ministry money: the numismatic rebuild was paid for by extraordinary Ministry of Culture funding under the PON Cultura e Sviluppo 2014-2020 programme, topped up with ordinary funds for 2024 and 2025 (Finestre sull'Arte, May 2026).
- Sponsor: the airline Volotea paid 50,000 euros for the museum's Green Bike Area, a 650-square-metre planted area with e-bike charging opened in May 2023 (Il Mondo di Suk).
- Donations: taxpayers can assign it their 5x1000 share of income tax (museum website). At the July 2019 annual report the ministry's director general of museums, Antonio Lampis, spoke of a "boom di incassi" (boom in takings) (Finestre sull'Arte).

Red flags: 2025 director appointment over a disputed shortlist
- In July 2025 Culture Minister Alessandro Giuli announced the directors of five first-tier museums overnight, Sirano among them (Artribune; Finestre sull'Arte, 17 July 2025).
- In an interview with Corriere della Sera that was not published but that he shared on social media, Giuli had said he was not satisfied with the shortlists from the selection commission and was ready to reopen the call; he made the appointments anyway, after the museums had gone months under interim directors (Finestre sull'Arte, 17 July 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum in art media; the one dispute found concerned a rumoured loan of the Alexander Mosaic.
1. Finestre sull'Arte: editorial staff, "Napoli, il mosaico di Alessandro va in mostra in Giappone? Il MANN: 'nessun accordo'" (2021)
URL: https://www.finestresullarte.info/attualita/polemica-mosaico-alessandro-mostra-giappone-mann
- Reports social media criticism, started by Senator Margherita Corrado, that the restoration was being done to send the mosaic to the Tokyo National Museum and the Hermitage.
- The MANN said it had expressed a wish to take the mosaic but that no agreements existed.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Italian state money with small sponsorships. The weak point is ministerial control of who runs it, shown in the 2025 appointment.
