NATIONAL GALLERY: London, UK
ID: national-gallery-london
Website: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National collection of European painting on the north side of Trafalgar Square, London.
- Founded in 1824, when the British government bought 38 paintings from the heirs of John Julius Angerstein for 57,000 pounds; the first home was 100 Pall Mall (Wikipedia).
- The Trafalgar Square building was designed by William Wilkins, built from 1832 and opened in 1838 on the former Royal Mews site. The Sainsbury Wing (1991) is by Robert Venturi and Denise Scott Brown and was paid for by a gift of almost 50 million pounds from Lord Sainsbury and his brothers Simon and Tim (Wikipedia).
- An exempt charity and a non-departmental public body of the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (Wikipedia).
- Open daily 10:00 to 18:00, with Friday lates until 21:00. Admission to the collection is free. The Sainsbury Wing has step-free access and wheelchairs can be booked.

FUNDING
National Gallery: public body with heavy private support: government grant, individual donors, corporate partners and members
- Gallery Partners (current corporate page): Bloomberg Philanthropies, LG and Bank of America.
- Corporate Supporters include Iberdrola Scottish Power, BBVA, Rothschild & Co and Sotheby's. Corporate Members include American Express, HSBC, JP Morgan, The Citadel and Christie's. No amounts are given.
- Project Domani, announced in September 2025: a 750 million pound programme with a new wing designed by Kengo Kuma. Of 375 million pounds secured, Crankstart (the foundation of Sir Michael Moritz and Harriet Heyman) gave 150 million, the Julia Rausing Trust gave 150 million, and 75 million came from Chair of Trustees John Booth, the National Gallery Trust and anonymous donors (Gallery).
- A 50 million pound endowment from Sir Paul Getty in 1985 funded purchases (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the Gallery's annual grant-in-aid figure on the pages read.

Red flags: Shell sponsorship (dropped 2018)
- Shell was a corporate partner for 12 years and sponsored the 2014-15 exhibition "Rembrandt: The Late Works" (ArtReview, Culture Unstained).
- Campaigners acted for years: a Greenpeace banner on the portico in 2012 during a Shell reception, theatre and choir protests in 2014, and a Reclaim the Power action in 2015 (Culture Unstained, an openly campaigning group).
- Freedom of Information papers showed the partnership ended in January 2018 when Shell told the Gallery it would not renew; ArtReview, citing The Guardian, reported it was worth about 20,000 to 35,000 pounds. Shell said it wanted to focus on training engineers.

Red flags: Finmeccanica (arms) sponsorship ended 2012
- The Italian arms maker Finmeccanica sponsored the Gallery from 2006 on a deal due to run to 1 October 2013; it was worth 30,000 pounds in its last year (Campaign Against Arms Trade, 10 October 2012, an advocacy source).
- The Gallery hosted receptions for the DSEI arms fair in September 2011 and for Farnborough International from 2006 to 2010. After the "Disarm the Gallery" campaign the deal ended about a year early; the Gallery said Finmeccanica "exercised their right to terminate".

Red flags: HSBC, a Benefactor Member
- On 11 December 2012 HSBC Holdings and HSBC Bank USA admitted anti-money laundering and sanctions violations and agreed to forfeit 1.256 billion dollars under a deferred prosecution agreement with the US Department of Justice.
- No reporting found of a campaign against Citadel or Bloomberg at the National Gallery.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little critical commentary on the funders in the art press; the Shell and arms objections come from campaign groups and a composer in the Art Not Oil coalition, with ArtReview reporting the outcome.
1. Apollo: Michael Prodger, "A new dawn for the National Gallery?" (2025)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/national-gallery-collection-after-1900-contemporary-art-tate-dispute/
- Welcomes Project Domani but says the 375 million pounds raised will not go far against building costs.
- Warns the Gallery will compete with Tate for donors. Does not criticise any named funder.
2. ArtReview: staff, "News 19 October 2018: Shell's partnership with London's National Gallery ends" (2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-19-october-2018-shells-partnership-with-londons-national-gallery-ends
- Reports the end of Shell's sponsorship and notes BP still partnered with the British Museum and the National Portrait Gallery.
- A news report, not a critique.
3. openDemocracy: Chris Garrard, "Unexpected partnerships in protest at National Gallery" (2014)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/unexpected-partnerships-in-protest-at-national-gallery/
- Argues Shell uses sponsorship as cover for its brand and that oil sponsorship and the Gallery's plan to privatise up to two-thirds of its workforce belong to the same corporate influence.
- Names Shell and Finmeccanica. Garrard belongs to Art Not Oil; openDemocracy is not an art-press outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ethical objections to oil and arms sponsorship were aimed at several UK institutions in the same years; ArtReview notes Tate ended its BP partnership in 2016 after campaigner protests.
- The Gallery is one of those institutions and held both the Shell and Finmeccanica deals.

BOTTOM LINE
The Gallery's operating money is public and its large current gifts come from named philanthropic foundations. The weak points are its past Shell and Finmeccanica deals, which ended only after campaigns, and HSBC's 2012 deferred prosecution agreement while the bank is a current member. Watch who sponsors the Project Domani wing and how the Gallery handles corporate offers.
