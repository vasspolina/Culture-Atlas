FRUITMARKET GALLERY: Edinburgh, UK
ID: fruitmarket-gallery
Website: https://www.fruitmarket.co.uk

OVERVIEW
- Free public contemporary art gallery at 45 Market Street in the centre of Edinburgh, with a café and bookshop on site (Fruitmarket).
- Established in 1974 (Wikipedia).
- The building was a fruit and vegetable market built in 1938, renovated by Richard Murphy Architects in 1994 (Wikipedia).
- A refurbishment designed by Reiach and Hall added a second exhibition space in a former fruit and vegetable warehouse, later the Electric Circus nightclub, and the gallery reopened in July 2021 with a Karla Black exhibition (Wikipedia).
- Fiona Bradley is director; the board has eight members (Fruitmarket).
- Registered in Scotland as a company limited by guarantee (number 87888) and a Scottish charity (SC005576) (Fruitmarket).
- Open seven days, galleries 11:00 to 18:00, café and bookshop 9:00 to 18:00; free admission (Fruitmarket).

FUNDING
Fruitmarket Gallery: supported by trusts, foundations, galleries and individual donors, with Creative Scotland and the Scottish Government named as public funders
- Creative Scotland and the Scottish Government are named as public funders on the gallery's thanks page for its building project (stated by the space).
- Programme supporters named on the current supporters page: Art Fund, Barry Fish, Bloomberg, Edinburgh World Heritage, EVOC, Eva Hesse Charitable Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, Hinrichsen Foundation, Young Start delivered by The National Lottery Community Fund, Paul Hamlyn Foundation, "Jane Hamlyn, Frith Street Gallery", Queensberry House Trust, RS Macdonald Charitable Trust, Thomas Dane Gallery, William Grant Foundation and White Cube. The page also thanks the Fruitmarket Council, Programme Patrons and Patrons without naming them (stated by the space).
- Building project: major donors were the Binks Trust, the Garfield Weston Foundation and the Wolfson Foundation; champions included Johnny Bute and Stichting West Coast Foundation, Martin Creed and Hauser & Wirth, the Foyle Foundation, and Charles and Ruth Plowden. Gallery patrons were Frith Street Gallery, kurimanzutto, Luhring Augustine and The Modern Institute (stated by the space).
- Baillie Gifford sponsored the gallery until 2024 (see Red flags).

Red flags: Sponsor dropped under pressure, Baillie Gifford
- Snack magazine (Zoë White) reports that by summer 2024 the Fruitmarket, Collective Gallery, Stills and Imaginate had announced they would drop Baillie Gifford as a sponsor, and credits the campaign group Art Workers for Palestine Scotland (AW4PS) with those decisions.
- Demand: AW4PS urged cultural institutions to press Baillie Gifford to divest from Israel's war or sever their ties with the firm (Snack, Zoë White).
- Campaign: on 5 August 2024 AW4PS wrote on Bella Caledonia (partisan) that local institutions like the Fruitmarket and Stills had suspended their partnerships with Baillie Gifford. The group criticises the firm's investments in companies associated with the Israeli occupation of Palestine, arms sales and fossil fuels.
- Morning Star (Matt Kerr, 27 August 2025) reports that the Edinburgh International Book Festival and the Fruitmarket have cut ties with Baillie Gifford.
- Baillie Gifford's side: it has noted that only 2 percent of client money is invested in companies with links to fossil fuels, against a market average of 11 percent (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 2 July 2024, about the National Galleries of Scotland, which kept its sponsorship; the article does not mention the Fruitmarket).

BOTTOM LINE
The Fruitmarket is a free gallery supported by trusts, foundations, galleries and individual donors, with Creative Scotland and the Scottish Government named as public funders on its own pages. Its sponsor Baillie Gifford ended in 2024; Snack magazine credits a campaign by Art Workers for Palestine Scotland over the firm's investments.
