WIDER CONTEXT
- In September 2016 the St. Louis artist Damon Davis called for a boycott and the removal of Kelley Walker's exhibition "Direct Drive" at the museum, which included photographs of Black men and women smeared with chocolate and toothpaste; of the artist talk Davis wrote: "When confronted with an actual black person, Walker became flustered and angry and had no actual answer for why he was using these images" (St. Louis Magazine, Stefene Russell, 22 September 2016; Hyperallergic, Claire Voon, 22 September 2016).
- Three of the museum's Black employees called for the removal of four works and for chief curator Jeffrey Uslip to resign, on the grounds that the exhibition demeaned Black people (St. Louis Public Radio, Nancy Fowler, 26 September 2016).
- Director Lisa Melandri kept the exhibition up, saying removal would be censorship; she acknowledged the initial presentation was hurtful to members of the community, built a wall to obscure the work, put up lobby text identifying it as potentially painful, and said of Uslip: "I stand behind all my staff" (St. Louis Public Radio, 26 September 2016).
- Uslip apologised for ending the question-and-answer session with Walker: "I unintentionally ended the conversation too soon, and for this I sincerely apologize" (St. Louis Public Radio, updated 29 September 2016).
- The museum cancelled its 2016 Art:314 auction after many artists "expressed struggle and doubt" about taking part (St. Louis Magazine, updated 29 September 2016).
