VICTORY MUSEUM (CENTRAL MUSEUM OF THE GREAT PATRIOTIC WAR): Moscow, Russia
ID: victory-museum-central-museum-of-the-great-patriotic-war
Website: https://victorymuseum.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Federal museum of the Second World War on Poklonnaya Hill in Moscow (victorymuseum.ru; Novaya Gazeta, 2025).
- Building work began in 1985, a USSR Culture Ministry order founded the Central Museum of the Great Patriotic War in 1986, and the museum opened officially on 9 May 1995 (victorymuseum.ru).
- Zurab Tsereteli was artistic director of the building project and V. M. Budaev its chief architect (victorymuseum.ru).
- The museum describes itself as one of the leading institutions countering attempts to falsify history and as a centre of patriotic education (victorymuseum.ru).
- The main building holds the Halls of Glory, Commanders, Historical Truth, Faces of Victory, and Memory and Sorrow; guided displays include "Feat of the People" and "Battle for Moscow", and an open-air display of military equipment (victorymuseum.ru; hall names translated by the atlas).
- Its branches include the "Main Weapon Relics of the Army", a Krasnogorsk branch and the G. K. Zhukov Museum (victorymuseum.ru; names translated by the atlas).
- It renamed itself "The Victory MuZeum", with a Latin Z, in March 2022 and sold souvenirs with the Z and V invasion symbols (Meduza, 2022).
- On 19 April 2022 it opened "Ordinary Nazism", which its own description says shows an "inextricable link" between the wartime Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists and present-day Ukrainian groups; its press service reported 150,000 visitors by 18 May (Meduza; The Art Newspaper).
- Its current programme includes a project on soldiers of the war in Ukraine, "SVO. Zashchitniki Otechestva", spelt with a Latin Z (victorymuseum.ru; title romanised by the atlas).
- Visiting: concessions for pensioners, students, Russian citizens aged 14 to 17 and conscripts; disabled visitors of groups 1 and 2 bring one companion; entry is free on the last Thursday of the month for listed groups (victorymuseum.ru).

FUNDING
Victory Museum: public: Russian federal budget
- A federal state budgetary institution of culture under Russia's Ministry of Culture (victorymuseum.ru).
- It takes online donations from the public through a "Support the museum" page (victorymuseum.ru).
- Director Alexander Shkolnik chairs the presidium of the Foundation for Strategic Initiatives of the Victory Museum (UK Sanctions List; Australian sanctions instrument).

Red flags: UK and Australian sanctions on director Alexander Shkolnik, 2022
- The UK designated Shkolnik on 4 May 2022 for using the museum "to spread disinformation", including the claim that the invasion of Ukraine is "de-Nazification" (UK Sanctions List).
- Australia listed him, as the museum's director, in the Autonomous Sanctions Amendment (No. 17) Instrument 2022, made on 17 May 2022 (Federal Register of Legislation, F2022L00708).
- He told the state news agency RIA Novosti he was "proud" to be on the UK list (The Art Newspaper, 2022).
- Putin honoured him in 2021 for his "contribution to the patriotic education of the younger generation" (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage since 2022 centres on the director's sanctions and on the museum's exhibitions against Ukraine.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Director of Moscow's Second World War museum says he is 'proud' to be sanctioned over war in Ukraine" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/06/01/director-of-moscows-second-world-war-museum-says-he-is-proud-to-be-sanctioned
- Reports that Shkolnik was the first Russian museum director targeted by sanctions, and that "Ordinary Nazism" was directed against Ukraine.
- Describes the museum's atmosphere as "a cross between totalitarian chic and luxury funeral home".
2. Meduza: "'Ordinary Nazism'" (2022)
URL: https://meduza.io/en/feature/2022/06/07/ordinary-nazism
- An independent news outlet's visit found a "bizarre mishmash" of internet material, Nazi German memorabilia and TikTok clips without context, with no curators named.
- Traces a list of children's names on the central installation to pro-Russian websites of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic, unconfirmed by Ukraine or Russia.
- Reports that children walked past an "18+" sign into the hall.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The UK designated Russia's Minister of Culture, Olga Lyubimova, on 17 July 2023 for supporting policies that undermine Ukraine's sovereignty (UK Sanctions List).
- After director Vladimir Zabarovsky died in spring 2017, Shkolnik, then an adviser to Culture Minister Vladimir Medinsky, became director on Medinsky's initiative; the deputy post went to Kristina Trubinova, 26, a former assistant of Medinsky, who drew a scandal over a social media photo of herself in front of Third Reich banners (Novaya Gazeta, 2025).
- Both points concern the ministry that oversees the museum and the way it chose the museum's leaders.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on Russian federal money. Its weak point is its leadership: a director under Western sanctions and exhibitions that serve the state's case for the invasion of Ukraine.
