MUSEO NACIONAL DE BELLAS ARTES DE LA HABANA: Havana, Cuba
ID: museo-nacional-de-bellas-artes-de-la-habana-havana
Website: http://www.bellasartes.co.cu (checked 2026-10-25; down, the domain does not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Cuba's national fine arts museum, split between two buildings in central Havana: the Palacio de Bellas Artes for Cuban art and the Palacio del Centro Asturiano for universal art.
- Decreed on 23 February 1913 by President José Miguel Gómez and opened on 28 April 1913 as the Museo Nacional de la República (Spanish Wikipedia). The director is Jorge Fernández Torres.
- Palacio de Bellas Artes: designed by Alfonso Rodríguez Pichardo and opened on 18 June 1954 on the former site of the Mercado de Colón. Palacio del Centro Asturiano: designed by Manuel del Busto, opened in 1927 as the Centro Asturiano's seat; the universal collections have been there since 2001.
- Collection: about 47,600 pieces, of which 45,000 are part of the artistic heritage, including Cuban art, European painting and sculpture, and Egyptian, Greek and Roman antiquities (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Spanish Wikipedia lists 09:00 to 17:00 Wednesday to Saturday and 09:00 to 14:00 Sunday. The museum's site could not be opened, so no admission or accessibility information was confirmed.

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes de La Habana: mainly public: the Cuban state, under the Ministry of Culture
- Spanish Wikipedia describes the museum as public and under the Ministry of Culture. It gives no operating budget.
- The same article records a 14.5 million dollar renovation from 1996 (payer not stated) and a 2010 donation of more than 120 works by the Brownstone Foundation.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, accounts or a board.

Red flags: artists' protest delivered to the museum, 2021
- 24 May 2021: Cuban artists aligned with the activist group 27N asked the museum's director, Jorge Fernández, to cover their works on view and to take their works not on display off the museum's web page. They set three conditions: Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara returned home, his wellbeing verified by friends and family, and the police siege against him since November 2020 ended (The Art Newspaper, which says "Museum of Fine Arts" and names no artists).
- 25 May 2021: OnCuba, which names the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, listed seven signatories (Tania Bruguera, Tomás Sánchez, Marco Castillo, Jorge Luis Marrero, Sandra Ceballos, Celia-Yunior, Reynier Leyva Novo) and said they delivered the letter in person to Fernández Torres.
- The protest concerned the treatment of Otero Alcántara. Neither The Art Newspaper nor OnCuba reports any response from the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself in art media. The main case is the 2021 artists' withdrawal request; one other piece criticises state cultural policy around the museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Daniel Cassady, "Cuban artists ask Museum of Fine Arts to remove their work from display while Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara remains in government custody" (2021)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/cuban-artists-ask-national-museum-director-to-censor-their-work-while-luis-manuel-otero-alcantara-remains-in-government-custody
- Reports the request of artists aligned with 27N to the director, tied to Otero Alcántara's treatment after his hunger strike against government restrictions on artistic freedom; the government's official response, as 27N's letter reports it, is that he "is neither detained nor prosecuted".
- Refers to the "Museum of Fine Arts" in Havana and its director Jorge Fernández; it does not use the museum's full name.
2. The New York Review of Books: Esther Allen, "The Art of the Possible at Havana's Bienal" (2019)
URL: https://www.nybooks.com/daily/2019/06/15/the-art-of-the-possible-at-havanas-bienal/
- Describes the museum as host of the two central exhibitions of the 13th Bienal; one was curated by Fernández Torres.
- Reports that Bruguera declined to attend, saying the Ministry of Culture was diverting resources to the Bienal to "whitewash its international image" after Decree 349.
- Criticises the Bienal and the decree more than the museum.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Decree 349, signed in April 2018 by Miguel Díaz-Canel, requires artists to obtain government approval before exhibiting; Esther Allen (NYRB, 2019) calls it the legal basis for the arrest of Otero Alcántara and other artists.
- An open letter published by e-flux on 15 October 2021 asked the art community to boycott the 14th Havana Biennial, stating that Cuban artists have been in prison for months and dozens of cultural workers are under house arrest. It does not name the museum.
- The museum is connected through the 2019 Bienal: it hosted the central exhibitions, one curated by its own director.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Cuban state money, with no private funder named. The weak points are the artists' 2021 protest to its director, which drew no reported answer, and its role as venue for state-promoted Bienal shows under a censorship decree. Worth watching is whether its website returns with reports or accounts.
