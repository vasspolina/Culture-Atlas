NANJING MASSACRE MEMORIAL HALL: Nanjing, China
ID: nanjing-massacre-memorial-hall
Website: https://www.19371213.com.cn (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The Memorial Hall of the Victims in Nanjing Massacre by Japanese Invaders stands on the Jiangdongmen mass killing site in Nanjing, where the Japanese army buried victims in 1937 (museum website; Xinhua, state media).
- It opened on 15 August 1985 on the 40th anniversary of the end of the Second World War; Contested Histories says the decision followed Japan's 1982 textbook approvals and that Deng Xiaoping coined its name.
- Architect Qi Kang designed the first phases around an excavated mass grave; a second phase opened on 10 December 1997 and a third, not designed by Qi Kang, in 2007 (Emily Matson, Wilson Center).
- The Victory Exhibition Hall, devoted to Japan's surrender, opened on 7 December 2015; a branch at the Liji Lane former "comfort station", a protected national site since 2019, opened on 1 December 2015 (museum website).
- In 1997 the Propaganda Department of the Party Central Committee listed the hall among its first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- Since 2014 the hall has hosted the state ceremony on 13 December, the national day of mourning set by the National People's Congress; Xi Jinping spoke at the first ceremony (museum website, Contested Histories).
- At its 40th anniversary in 2025 the hall reported a site grown from 25,000 to 103,000 square metres, over 106,000 objects and documents, more than 6 million visits a year and 150 million in total; Zhou Feng is director (Xinhua, state media).
- Its education services head said in 2026 that the hall's account of history will not change with Sino-Japanese relations, and that it plans exhibitions in Hong Kong (The Standard).
- Visiting: free, with real-name booking 1 to 7 days ahead through the official WeChat account, each of the three halls booked separately; open 8:30 to 17:30, last entry 16:30, closed Mondays except public holidays (museum website, Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall: state-funded: the Nanjing municipal government
- The Nanjing municipal government prepared the construction from late 1983 and founded the hall (Contested Histories, Wikipedia).
- The Jiangsu provincial government set up a research institute on Nanjing Massacre history and international peace at the hall on 1 March 2016 (Wikipedia; date confirmed by the museum website).
- Members of a Japanese-Chinese friendship group donated many historical items and a garden in the grounds (Wikipedia).
- Since 13 December 2018 Nanjing's regulations on safeguarding the national commemoration govern the site, and visitors must follow them (museum website).

Red flags: Japanese government objections to the exhibition, 2007
- Contested Histories reports that Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, under pressure from the Japanese parliament, repeatedly asked for measures against the exhibition.
- In autumn 2007 Japanese government representatives, including the ambassador, met Chinese officials 13 times about the memorial, while the Japanese consul in Shanghai criticised its contents (Contested Histories).
- The outcome was a "friendship corner" on postwar relations and Japanese aid, and the removal of the three photographs Japanese groups had called "improper"; the main narrative stayed the same (Contested Histories).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from policy and history writing, not art press, and concerns the hall's use in state narrative, not its funding.
1. Wilson Center: Emily Matson, "From Victimhood to Victory: The Evolution of the Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall" (2024)
URL: https://www.wilsoncenter.org/article/victimhood-victory-evolution-nanjing-massacre-memorial-hall
- Argues that each building phase mirrors a shift in the Communist Party's legitimising narrative, from "victor" to "victim" and, under Xi Jinping, to "rejuvenation".
- Says the Victory Exhibition Hall is designed to inspire pride in victory and to support the Party's claim to have been the "mainstay" of the war.
- Stresses that this does not diminish the horrors of the massacre.
2. Contested Histories: case study #32, "Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall" (undated)
URL: https://contestedhistories.org/wp-content/uploads/China_-Nanjing-Massacre-Memorial-Hall-in-Nanjing.pdf
- Sets out the dispute between China and Japan over the death toll and the exhibition's depiction of Japanese troops.
- Notes arguments that the hall serves a Chinese nationalist agenda, while also reflecting genuine remembrance.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the hall is Chinese public money and carries no funder flag. Its weak points are its role in state narrative and the recurring dispute with Japan over its contents.
