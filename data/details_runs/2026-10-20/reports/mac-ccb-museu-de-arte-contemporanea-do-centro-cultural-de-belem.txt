MAC/CCB MUSEU DE ARTE CONTEMPORÂNEA DO CENTRO CULTURAL DE BELÉM: Lisbon, Portugal
ID: mac-ccb-museu-de-arte-contemporanea-do-centro-cultural-de-belem
Website: https://www.ccb.pt/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the Exhibition Centre of the Centro Cultural de Belém (CCB), Praça do Império, Lisbon (CCB ticket page).
- The CCB was commissioned by the Portuguese State to host the 1992 EU Presidency; Vittorio Gregotti and Atelier Risco, led by Manuel Salgado, were its architects, and its public venues opened in 1993. It is owned by the Fundação Centro Cultural de Belém (Wikipedia).
- The Exhibition Centre housed the Museu Coleção Berardo from 2007 to 2022; MAC/CCB has occupied it since October 2023 (Wikipedia).
- The new museum's artistic direction, Núria Enguita and Mariana Pestana, was chosen through an international competition after the State's 15-year agreement with collector José Berardo ended (Sábado, Lusa).
- Collections: the State Contemporary Art Collection, the Ellipse Collection and the Teixeira de Freitas Collection; the Berardo collection remains at the CCB under a court decision (Sábado, Lusa).
- Visitors in 2024: 588,643, up 22.5% on 2023 (Sábado, Lusa, 8 January 2025). A later Lusa report on the CCB's 2026 strategic plan gives 179,890 MAC/CCB visitors for 2024 (Observador); the two reports use different counts.
- No reporting found of current admission or opening hours in the pages opened.

FUNDING
MAC/CCB: mainly public: the State Budget through Fundação CCB, with a small private patron base
- Between 60% and 70% of the CCB's annual budget of about 18.7 million euros depends on the State Budget, which provides about 10.5 million euros (Observador, Lusa, 15 April 2026).
- The CCB says it starts "almost from zero" with major patrons and will create a patronage team for the first time (Observador, Lusa).
- A hotel, tourist apartments and shops built by the Alves Ribeiro group on planned module sites will pay annual rent of 350,000 euros to 1.2 million euros over 65 years (Observador, Lusa).
- Partners named on the CCB site: Epal, Fundação Millennium BCP, El Corte Inglês, Sony, Super Bock Group, Samsung, Nintendo and others. No amounts are posted (stated by the CCB).
- The Berardo collection was pledged as collateral for nearly 1 billion euros in loans; banks CGD, Novo Banco and BCP sued from 2017 to seize 75% of it, and the museum stayed open (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 2 August 2019).

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, dismissal of the CCB president
- In November 2024 Culture Minister Dalila Rodrigues removed CCB president Francisca Carneiro Fernandes a few days before her first year in office ended, which meant no severance. The order cited aligning the CCB with objectives set by higher authority; Fernandes called that "totalmente falso" (Observador, 20 July 2026).
- A petition against her replacement by Nuno Vassallo e Silva gathered more than 2,000 signatures, European cultural bodies issued statements, and the issue went to Parliament (Observador).
- In a settlement of 11 May 2026 the State acknowledged no grounds for the dismissal and will pay her lost salary and about 30,000 euros in court costs (Observador, Joana Moreira).
- Unverified: bullying and harassment under earlier CCB management, alleged by the Workers' Commission and anonymous staff (Observador, 10 December 2024).
- The CCB Workers' Commission later resigned en bloc, citing political interference and the removal of artistic director Aida Tavares (RTP).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum found in art media. The one art-press item concerns the Berardo collection's creditors.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Berardo museum stays open in Lisbon despite uncertainty over future of Portuguese Saatchi's collection" (2019)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/berardo-museum-stays-open-in-lisbon-despite-uncertainty-over-future-of-portuguese-saatchi-s-collection
- Reports the banks' suit over almost 1 billion euros of debt and the pledge of about 900 works.
- Criticises neither the CCB nor the State; the general manager said visitors would be unaffected.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from the State Budget runs the space, with little private support yet. The weak points are the political removal of its president in 2024, which the State later admitted had no grounds, and the legal cloud over the Berardo works that hang in the same halls. Watch the new patronage team and the long-term rents from the Alves Ribeiro development.
