BAK, BASIS VOOR ACTUELE KUNST: Utrecht, Netherlands
ID: bak-utrecht
Website: https://www.bakonline.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art and research institution at Pauwstraat 13a in central Utrecht, run by Stichting BAK, basis voor actuele kunst (KVK 41183440).
- Led for 25 years by its founding director Maria Hlavajova, who stepped down on 30 June 2025; it now runs as BAK Basecamp for Tactical Imaginaries, a collective, artist-led programme under artistic director Jeanne van Heeswijk.
- Known for long research projects: Former West (2008 to 2016), New World Academy (2013 to 2016) and Propositions for Non-Fascist Living (from 2017).
- Basecamp, launched in January 2025 as a six-month period of thinking and planning, is a space for gatherings, events and presentations with a network of community partners.
- Open Tuesday to Saturday, by invitation, on registration or for public events; no admission fees are stated. No accessibility details found on the site.

FUNDING
BAK: private funds since 2025, after losing all multi-year state and city subsidy
- BAK "lost all structural subsidy as of 2025" (stated by the space, ANBI page).
- Utrecht: BAK had received over EUR 620,000 a year and asked for EUR 700,000 a year; the city's culture committee advised giving nothing for the coming four years, citing doubts about whether BAK's plans could be carried out and low visitor numbers (DUIC, June 2024).
- State: the Council for Culture advised against funding BAK because its application described its core task "so briefly" that it "in fact cannot be assessed" (DUIC, July 2024).
- Utrecht named BAK Basecamp on 15 October 2026 among 17 recipients of its two-year culture subsidy for 2027 to 2028; the city's notice gives no amount per recipient (Gemeente Utrecht).
- Current funders: ELJA Foundation, Fonds 21, Het Cultuurfonds, K.F. Hein Fonds, the Municipality of Utrecht, Stichting DOEN / VriendenLoterij Fonds and VSBfonds (stated by the space, no amounts).
- Annual reports for 2021 to 2025 are posted; the 2025 report could not be read as text, so its figures are not checked here.

Red flags: defunded by the state and the city
- Both the national Council for Culture and Utrecht's advisory committee recommended ending BAK's multi-year funding in 2024, and both governments followed (DUIC; BAK).
- BAK called the city advice "a devastating blow" and said it was "incomprehensible" that Utrecht would tear down part of its own cultural infrastructure (DUIC, translated from Dutch).
- BAK said it received "an overwhelming amount of messages of support" (stated by the space).
- No reporting found that either decision cited BAK's politics or programme content; the stated reasons were the weak application (state) and doubts about the plans and low visitor numbers (city), in a round where the other 100 applications asked for over EUR 5.2 million more than was available (DUIC).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
BAK's money was mainly public until 2024; since 2025 it runs on private Dutch funds and a new city grant, one of them the VriendenLoterij lottery fund. The weak point is survival, not the source of the money. Worth watching: the amount of the 2027 to 2028 Utrecht grant and whether the state funding loss is ever shown to be political.
