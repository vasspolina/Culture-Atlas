MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART YINCHUAN: Yinchuan, China
ID: museum-of-contemporary-art-yinchuan-yinchuan
Website: https://www.moca-yinchuan.com/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum inside a 2.5-hectare wetlands park in Yinchuan, capital of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region (The Art Newspaper, 2015); Dezeen places it on the eastern outskirts of the city, and e-flux lists the address as No. 12, HeLe Road, Xingqing District.
- Initiated in 2012 by Minsheng Real Estate (ArtZip). The Art Newspaper calls the owner and operator Ningxia Minsheng Group, a regional property developer, and reports that the museum opened on 8 August 2015 after three years in the making. It calls it the first contemporary art institution in north-west China and the first museum in the country to focus on Sino-Islamic art.
- Building by the Beijing firm We Architech Anonymous (WAA) for MinSheng Real Estate Development: 15,000 square metres, three storeys above ground and a basement, with an undulating facade of more than 1,600 custom glass-fibre-reinforced concrete panels that mimics sediment layers left by the shifting Yellow River (Dezeen, 2015).
- Spaces: a central atrium gallery, side galleries, a lecture theatre, a restaurant and education spaces. The collection includes hundreds of late Qing dynasty Chinese oil paintings (Dezeen) and antique maps (ArtZip).
- Hosted the first Yinchuan Biennale in 2016, curated by Bose Krishnamachari with 73 artists (The Art Newspaper, 2016).

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary Art Yinchuan: corporate-owned: a local property developer with a provincial government partnership
- Construction: about 300m yuan (£30m), according to The Art Newspaper in 2015. ArtZip (2016) gives the cost as "nearly 300 million USD".
- Model: developed under a build-operate-transfer (BOT) public-private partnership (The Art Newspaper). ArtZip reports that Minsheng started working with the provincial government to initiate the museum, and quotes Lu Peng saying the government sought political achievement "without directly providing funds".
- Operations: a budget of 35m yuan (£3.5m) a year, covering 130 staff and a 10m yuan acquisitions budget. The state provides several million yuan a year, which Hsieh Suchen, whom the article calls the museum's art supervisor, described as "barely our power bill" (The Art Newspaper, 2015).

Red flags: Ai Weiwei dropped from the 2016 biennale
- On 21 August 2016 the museum's board decided to exclude Ai Weiwei's work, about two weeks before the first Yinchuan Biennale opened. Artistic director Hsieh Suchen said displeasure in domestic media about his participation worried her, and that "The government doesn't have any involvement at all" (The Art Newspaper, Lisa Movius, 26 August 2016).
- Ai Weiwei said on Twitter on 24 August 2016 that he received a vague letter from Hsieh stating that the decision was made by "higher officials", and cited his "political" status (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: that officials ordered the removal. Ai Weiwei makes this claim; the museum denies it. The Art Newspaper links the show to the national One Belt, One Road initiative and central-government scrutiny. ArtZip (1 December 2016) reports that, amid censorship, the curator had to remove Ai Weiwei's name from the biennale invitation list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One essay questions the museum's model and collection. One news report covers the Ai Weiwei exclusion.
1. ART.ZIP: Gong Zhiyun, "The Problematic Contemporaneity of the Museum of Contemporary Art, Yinchuan" (1 December 2016)
URL: https://www.artzip.org/problematic-contemporaneity-museum-contemporary-art-yinchuan
- Examines the museum as an example of the questionable identity of private museums in China, and questions its funding and a permanent collection of Western-influenced Qing dynasty paintings and antique maps that the essay presents as unrelated to contemporary art.
- Quotes Fei Dawei, who called the funding astonishing for a relatively poor area and said that without research influence the museum would be a waste, and Cheng Meixin, who noted that Yinchuan has only 2 million citizens and argued that Minsheng's maintenance demands would be too enormous to afford.
- Calls the museum a unique and fine example of the current situation of private museums in China.
2. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "Ai Weiwei dropped from Yinchuan Biennale in China" (26 August 2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/08/26/ai-weiwei-dropped-from-yinchuan-biennale-in-china
- Reports the exclusion of Ai Weiwei and the dispute over who decided it: the museum's account is the board and domestic media displeasure, Ai Weiwei's is higher officials.
- News report, not a critique of the museum's programme.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a property developer's project built under a BOT partnership with the provincial government, which pays several million yuan a year toward a 35m yuan operating budget. The documented controversy is the board's removal of Ai Weiwei from the 2016 biennale, which the museum attributes to domestic media displeasure and Ai Weiwei attributes to higher officials.
