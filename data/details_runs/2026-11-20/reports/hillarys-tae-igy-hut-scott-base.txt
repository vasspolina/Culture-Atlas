HILLARY'S HUT (TAE/IGY HUT A), SCOTT BASE: Ross Island, Antarctica
ID: hillarys-tae-igy-hut-scott-base
Website: https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/hillarys-hut-scott-base/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The first building of New Zealand's Scott Base at Pram Point, at the southern tip of Ross Island, with Mount Erebus behind; it stands on the south-western edge of the base's green, flat-roofed buildings (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Built by Sir Edmund Hillary's team in January 1957 during the Commonwealth Trans-Antarctic Expedition; it was the first New Zealand base in the Ross Dependency (New Zealand Government, 26 Nov 2016).
- Scott Base was set up in the summer of 1956 to 1957 with New Zealand government support for the expedition and the International Geophysical Year; 23 men under Hillary, five of them scientists, wintered there, laying supply depots towards the South Pole for Vivian Fuchs (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Ministry of Works architect Frank Ponder designed the original base of six main buildings and three small science huts (Wikipedia). Hut A has since been moved 40 metres closer to the shore, keeping its original orientation (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- It is the only surviving Trans-Antarctic Expedition building in Antarctica and is listed as Historic Site or Monument 75 under the Antarctic Treaty (Wikipedia). It still serves as a refuge for the base (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Visiting: the hut sits inside a working government base, so the Trust made a virtual reality tour with Auckland University of Technology and Staples VR, free at selected New Zealand institutions and online (Antarctic Heritage Trust).

FUNDING
Hillary's Hut: charity-run conservation: the Antarctic Heritage Trust, with New Zealand government grants and donations
- Conservation: the Trust launched its conservation plan at Parliament on 17 March 2015, at a function attended by Prime Minister John Key, and restored the hut over the 2016 to 2017 summer (Antarctic Heritage Trust; New Zealand Government).
- 2016 appeal: the Trust raised $784,000, including a tractor journey from Piha to Aoraki/Mount Cook, and was $180,000 short; Arts, Culture and Heritage Minister Maggie Barry announced a $180,000 government grant in November 2016 to finish the work, which covered asbestos removal and the conservation and digitisation of 350 objects (New Zealand Government; RNZ).
- The Trust's accounts for the year to 30 June 2025 show gross income of NZ$3,433,987, including NZ$1,536,000 in government service grants and contracts, NZ$928,581 in donations and NZ$304,973 in interest; spending was NZ$3,335,082 and net assets NZ$10,866,037 (New Zealand Charities Register, CC24071).
- The Trust names the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and the Ministry for Culture and Heritage as its core funders; MetService as a key programme partner; and Christchurch Airport, the New Zealand Defence Force, The Shackleton Whisky and the Tait Foundation as key supporters (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Antarctica New Zealand provided logistics for the virtual reality project (Antarctic Heritage Trust).

WIDER CONTEXT
- New Zealand claims the Ross Dependency; only Australia, the United Kingdom, France and Norway accept the claim, and the Antarctic Treaty holds all claims in abeyance (Wikipedia).
- The Trust describes the hut's virtual tour as celebrating "New Zealand's first leadership presence in Antarctica" (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- The government committed NZ$498 million in June 2019 to redevelop Scott Base; the work was paused in 2023 when the cost exceeded approved funding, and Cabinet approved a reset programme in May 2026 (Wikipedia).
- The hut stands inside the base under redevelopment and inside the territory New Zealand claims.

BOTTOM LINE
A heritage hut kept by a New Zealand charity on public grants and donations, with the government closing a $180,000 gap in 2016. Its named backers are two government ministries, the Defence Force, local companies and a foundation. Worth watching: how the Scott Base redevelopment treats the hut.
