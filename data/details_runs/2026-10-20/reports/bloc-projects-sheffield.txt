BLOC PROJECTS: Sheffield, United Kingdom
ID: bloc-projects-sheffield
Website: https://www.blocprojects.co.uk

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led contemporary art space at 71 Eyre Lane, Sheffield S1 4RB, supporting the professional development of artists. It describes its programme as "radical, ambitious, socially relevant and critically engaged art practices" (Bloc Projects website).
- Founded in 2002 by a group of artists at Bloc Studios (Bloc Projects website). Richard Bartle founded Bloc Studios on Sidney Street; the gallery began as an informal, volunteer-run project space inside the studios and became a formal gallery in 2007 (Now Then, 2015 interview).
- Corridor8 marked its tenth anniversary in 2012, describing it as open to artists "at a point of change or risk in their career" (Robert Clark).
- Building: after a major refurbishment and expansion, designed with Sheffield architecture social enterprise Studio Polpo, the gallery was set to reopen on 23 October 2015 (Now Then).
- Programme: exhibitions, the Bloc Billboards, screenings, fundraisers and community partnerships (Now Then); the 2015 interview says it planned to take part in the Art Sheffield festival in 2016.
- Director is Dr Yet Chor Sunshine Wong; the board of trustees is co-chaired by Adrian Friedli and Jeanine Griffin (Bloc Projects website).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday until 5pm; current events are free and open to all.

FUNDING
Bloc Projects: small artist-led registered charity: government grants and a National Lottery Heritage Fund award
- Registered charity number 1188511, with the purpose "to advance education in the field of contemporary visual art" (Charity Commission, Bloc Projects website).
- Income was £105,892 and expenditure £118,885 in the year to 31 March 2025; income was £145,200 and expenditure £145,470 in the year to 31 March 2024 (Charity Commission). Income was £66,010 in the year to 31 December 2020, £83,150 in 2021 and £106,020 in 2022.
- Government grants made up £10,900 of income in the year to March 2025, £1,000 in the year to March 2024 and £1,000 in 2022; they were £71,650 in 2021 (Charity Commission).
- The National Lottery Heritage Fund awarded Bloc Projects £8,600 on 15 March 2017 under its Sharing Heritage programme for "Forging Culture and Industry in Sheffield's changing centre".
- The 2015 renovation and expansion was paid for by a grant, and the decision to expand came during a financially difficult period (Now Then).

BOTTOM LINE
Bloc Projects is a small artist-led charity with income of £105,892 in the year to March 2025. Government grants supplied £10,900 of it, and the National Lottery Heritage Fund awarded £8,600 in 2017. A grant paid for the 2015 renovation and expansion (Now Then).
