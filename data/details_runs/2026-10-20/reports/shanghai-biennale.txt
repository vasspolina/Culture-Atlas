SHANGHAI BIENNALE: Shanghai, China
ID: shanghai-biennale
Website: https://www.powerstationofart.com (checked 2026-10-24; the page returned only the title "Power Station of Art"; the e-flux announcement of the 15th edition gives this address. The powerstationofart.org domain serves gambling-promotion spam and is not the museum's site)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale organised by the Power Station of Art, a museum on the Huangpu River at 678 Miaojiang Road, Huangpu District, Shanghai (e-flux, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1996 by Fang Zengxian, then director of the Shanghai Art Museum, as China's first international contemporary art biennale; the first edition was approved by the Ministry of Culture and the Shanghai municipal administration (Wikipedia).
- The Shanghai Art Museum hosted the first eight editions; since the 9th, "Reactivation" (2012), the Power Station of Art has hosted it (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Power Station of Art is the converted Nanshi Power Plant, which served as the Pavilion of Future at Expo 2010 and opened as a museum on 1 October 2012 (Wikipedia).
- The 15th edition, "Does the flower hear the bee?", ran from November 2025 to 31 March 2026 under Kitty Scott, with 67 artists and collectives and over 250 works (e-flux).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. (e-flux, 15th edition).

FUNDING
Shanghai Biennale: state-run: Shanghai city government, with brand partners; total cost undisclosed
- The Power Station of Art is run by the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism. The renovation cost $64 million, paid by the municipal government (Wikipedia).
- The Biennale is funded mainly from city government funds with some sponsors; its total cost is undisclosed (Il Sole 24 Ore, 29 November 2025).
- 15th edition partners: Aesop, DENZA, The Langham, Shanghai, Xintiandi and Brilliant by Langham, and FLOS (Il Sole 24 Ore).
- No reporting found of a list of sponsors by year or of amounts.

Red flags: state censorship at the 9th Biennale (2012)
- Yuri Albert: on 3 September 2012 he said on Facebook that "a couple of weeks ago" Chinese officials gave him an ultimatum to remove the first two questions of his work "Moscow Poll" for the Moscow Pavilion. He refused and withdrew. He said the curators tried and failed to change the officials' decision (ArtLeaks, translated).
- Stella Art Foundation (curator of the Moscow Pavilion), via director of development Nikolay Molok, said the censorship came from government officials, not the Biennale curators, and that other artists were also censored; he gave no details (ArtLeaks, 5 September 2012).
- Barbad Golshiri: in an open letter of October 2012 he says a cultural committee twice rejected his proposed public work for Zhongshan Park, a skinned cow shaped like a whirling dervish; no dates are given. He says the Chinese Embassy in Iran told him a Biennial invitation was not enough and that he needed one from China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Unverified: Golshiri says the work was judged anti-religious, which he denies, and he speculates that Chinese officials acted with Iranian officials' views in mind; no response from the committee is on record (ArtLeaks, an art-activist platform).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found comes from artists' accounts of 2012 and a curator's description of working under censorship; no art-press review makes a full case against the Biennale.
1. ArtLeaks: unsigned post, translated by Corina L. Apostol, "Yuri Albert: I am not going to Shanghai because of censorship" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/09/05/yuri-albert-i-am-not-going-to-shanghai-because-of-censorship/
- Albert says Chinese officials demanded the cuts; he has "no complaints" about the curators Qiu Zhijie, Boris Groys, Jens Hoffmann and Johnson Chan.
- Names the officials only as "Chinese officials".
2. ArtLeaks: Barbad Golshiri, "Shanghai Biennial 2012 open letter" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/11/02/barbad-golshiri-shanghai-biennial-2012-open-letter/
- Says a proposal was rejected and that he was kept out through the invitation and visa process.
- Says Boris Groys told him by email on 22 October 2012 that city pavilions were financed and curated under city authorities.
- Says he protests Chinese or Iranian censorship, not the Biennale team.
3. Americas Quarterly (not art press): Benjamin Russell, "AQ Top 5 China Success Stories: Cuauhtémoc Medina" (2019)
URL: https://americasquarterly.org/article/aq-top-5-china-success-stories-cuauhtemoc-medina/
- Medina, curator of the 2018 edition, says Chinese authorities set four limits: no depictions of Mao, no nudity, no violence, no Tibet. He calls censorship: "a negotiation, a ping pong of proposals and counterproposals", and "a challenge, not an absolute impediment".
- Calls his relationship with the Power Station of Art "harmonious, complicit".

BOTTOM LINE
The money is state money: the Shanghai city government pays for the museum and for most of the Biennale, and the total is not disclosed. The weak points are the 2012 interventions by Chinese officials, reported by the artists and by Stella Art Foundation, and the lack of any published budget or sponsor schedule. Watch for the budget.
