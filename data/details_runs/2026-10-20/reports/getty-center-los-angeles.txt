GETTY CENTER: Los Angeles, USA
ID: getty-center-los-angeles
Website: https://www.getty.edu/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and research campus at 1200 Getty Center Drive in Brentwood, Los Angeles, on a hilltop in the Santa Monica Mountains above Interstate 405 (Wikipedia, Getty).
- Built by the J. Paul Getty Trust, which J. Paul Getty set up in 1953; the Center opened to the public on 16 December 1997 (Getty, Wikipedia).
- Named for J. Paul Getty (1892 to 1976), the oilman and collector whose bequest created the Trust (Getty).
- Richard Meier, chosen in 1984, designed the campus in concrete and steel clad in travertine or aluminium, with about 1.2 million square feet of Italian travertine; the cost was later put at about $1.3 billion (Wikipedia, Getty).
- On a 24-acre campus within a 110-acre site: the Getty Museum, the Getty Research Institute and its library, the Getty Conservation Institute, the Getty Foundation and Robert Irwin's 134,000-square-foot Central Garden (Wikipedia, Getty).
- Open Tuesday to Friday and Sunday 10am to 6:30pm, Saturday 10am to 9pm, closed Monday; admission is free with a timed reservation, parking is $25, $15 after 3pm and $10 after 6pm; the site is ADA accessible with a wheelchair-accessible tram and free wheelchair loans (stated by the space).
- The North and South Pavilions are closed for renovation, and the whole Center closes on 15 March 2027 for a modernisation, reopening in spring 2028 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Getty Center: endowment-funded: the J. Paul Getty Trust's investments, which began as Getty Oil stock
- Getty's will left the Trust nearly $700 million (stated by the space), which Wikipedia describes as nearly $660 million in Getty Oil stock, received in full in 1982.
- In January 1984 the Getty Museum, which held 11.8% of Getty Oil, agreed to sell its shares to Texaco at $125 a share (case brief, Texaco v. Pennzoil). No reporting found of a Getty Oil holding since.
- The annual budget is 5% of the 36-month average of the endowment (stated by the space).
- Audited statements for the year to 30 June 2025: investments of $9.32 billion, net investment income of $864.7 million, expenses of $442.7 million (museum $223.8 million), donor contributions of $11.1 million plus $5.4 million restricted, and $10.4 million in donated art (KPMG audit).
- In 2025 the Trust issued $500 million in taxable bonds to pay for the Center's capital plan (Getty annual report). Press puts the renovation at $600 to $800 million (Blooloop, citing the Los Angeles Times).
- No reporting found of named corporate sponsors or of current fossil fuel holdings in the endowment.

Red flags: 2006 Attorney General findings and the antiquities scandal
- In October 2006 California Attorney General Bill Lockyer reported that president Barry Munitz overspent on travel and meals, had staff run personal errands, and that the Trust spent over $21,500 on artwork gifts for trustees (Chronicle of Philanthropy).
- Munitz resigned in 2006, repaid $250,000 and gave up over $2 million in benefits; an independent monitor oversaw the Trust until 7 May 2008 (Chronicle of Philanthropy, Wikipedia).
- Antiquities curator Marion True was indicted in Italy on 1 April 2005 for conspiracy to traffic in illicit antiquities and resigned from the Getty on 1 October 2005; an Italian court dismissed the remaining charges in October 2010 because the statute of limitations had expired (Wikipedia).
- On 1 August 2007 the Getty agreed with the Italian government to return 40 objects Italy said were looted, keeping a statue of Aphrodite until 2010 (NPR).

Not related: the Climate Emergency Fund, co-founded in 2019 by Aileen Getty, J. Paul Getty's granddaughter, which funds climate protest groups including Just Stop Oil. It is a separate nonprofit; the report on it does not mention the Getty Trust.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little current criticism found. The main critical writing dates from the 2005 to 2007 crisis over spending and antiquities.
1. The New York Review of Books: Martin Filler, "The Getty: For Better and Worse" (2006)
URL: https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2006/11/16/the-getty-for-better-and-worse/
- The founder's bequest made the Trust "the world's richest art institution", and that wealth fed its troubles, which were self-inflicted.
- Criticises spending on buildings over art, above all the Getty Center, "the commission of the century".
- Argues True was made a scapegoat for the Trust's acquisition practices and that its "severely compromised ethics" weakened its defence.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Getty Center is the Getty Trust's own endowment, which began as Getty Oil stock but has not been tied to Getty Oil since the 1984 Texaco sale; outside gifts are small. The weak points are the 2006 Attorney General findings and the antiquities returns agreed in 2007. Worth watching: the $500 million bond issue and the 2027 to 2028 closure.
