MUSEO DE ARTE DE PONCE: Ponce, Puerto Rico
ID: museo-de-arte-de-ponce-ponce
Website: https://museoarteponce.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 2325 Avenida Las Américas (renamed Luis A. Ferré Boulevard), Ponce, Puerto Rico, owned by Fundación Luis A. Ferré, Inc. (Wikipedia).
- Founded by industrialist Luis A. Ferré; it opened on 3 January 1959 in a house at 70 Cristina Street with 72 works, and moved to its current building on Avenida Las Américas, which opened on 28 December 1965 (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by Edward Durell Stone, with hexagonal galleries that let daylight in through their corners (Wikipedia). A 2010 annex added an education space, library, archives, conservation laboratory, storage area, shop and restaurant (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds about 4,500 works (2010, Wikipedia): European and Pre-Raphaelite art, Spanish Golden Age, Latin American and Puerto Rican art, including "Flaming June".
- The Stone building was damaged by the January 2020 earthquake and has been closed since 2020; the museum operates from its Annex Building and targets reopening the main building in 2028 (museum restoration page). From March 2023 the museum opened to the public only on Fridays, 10:00 to 15:00, because of repairs to the main building (El Nuevo Día, 13 March 2023).
- Reconstruction of the main galleries has begun, a $30 million investment led by PRAR Architecture (museum announcement).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte de Ponce: private foundation museum, funded by founding families, individual patrons and corporate donors
- Its patrons page names three founding partners: Antonio Luis Ferré and Luisa Juliana Rangel, the Fonalledas family and Fundación Ángel Ramos. It also lists more than 700 individual partners, Gran Mecenas María Luisa Ferré Rangel and José Enrique Fernández Bjerg, and Mecenas including Antonio Luis Ferré Rangel, CMA Arquitectos and McConnell Valdés.
- The 2019 gala "Somos Isla" was led by Banco Popular de Puerto Rico and El Nuevo Día. Platinum sponsors were Condado Vanderbilt Hotel, Event Atelier and the Paulson Family Foundation. Puma Energy appears among the silver sponsors, with Merrill Lynch and Triple S, and New Fortress Energy and UBS Financial Services among the collaborators.
- The Wikipedia article says most revenue comes from donations by Puerto Rican individuals and businesses, with a small admission fee, and that the Samuel H. Kress Foundation gave 15 paintings in 1962.
- Mellon Foundation: $500,000 to the Luis A. Ferré Foundation on 30 June 2022 for conservation capacity at the museum (Mellon Foundation grant page).
- The foundation's tax filing for the year to June 2023 shows revenue of $4,898,282, of which $4,165,650 was grants and contributions; in 2022 revenue was $6,290,339, with $2,309,792 from grants and contributions (CauseIQ, summarising the filing).
- Reconstruction: the museum announcement says individual donations, corporate contributions, and local and federal funds pay for the $30 million project.
- María Luisa Ferré is president of the Luis A. Ferré Foundation, whose Board of Trustees governs the museum (museum announcement, 16 September 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a private foundation whose museum Luis A. Ferré founded, supported by founding families, individual patrons, corporate donors and a Mellon Foundation conservation grant. The sponsors page lists two energy companies, Puma Energy and New Fortress Energy, only for the 2019 gala, in the silver and collaborator tiers. The $30 million reconstruction draws on private donations and local and federal funds.
