PEGGY GUGGENHEIM COLLECTION: Venice, Italy
ID: peggy-guggenheim-collection-venice
Website: https://www.guggenheim-venice.it (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern European and American art in Palazzo Venier dei Leoni, in the Dorsoduro district of Venice, on the Grand Canal (Wikipedia).
- Peggy Guggenheim, an American heiress, lived in the palazzo for about thirty years, having bought it in 1949, and began opening her collection to the public seasonally in 1951 (Wikipedia).
- She gave the palazzo and about 300 works to the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation in 1976 and died in 1979; the Foundation took control that year and reopened the collection in April 1980 as the Peggy Guggenheim Collection (Wikipedia). It is named for her.
- Building and site: an 18th-century palazzo with a low Grand Canal facade; later additions include a garden annex (1993), the Nasher Sculpture Garden (1995) and a new entrance (2003) (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Philip Rylands (2000 to 2017) and, since 2017, Karole Vail, Peggy Guggenheim's granddaughter (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 10:00 to 18:00 except Tuesdays and 25 December; the ticket office closes at 17:00. Free entry on Thursdays 14:00 to 18:00 for residents and people born in Venice and for students at Venetian universities. The page lists no prices and says the museum "guarantees accessibility" (museum website).

FUNDING
Peggy Guggenheim Collection: parent foundation plus corporate members: the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation and companies in the Guggenheim Intrapresæ programme
- Parent: the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation controls the museum (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the Venice museum's own accounts, budget or board in the pages read.
- Corporate programme: Guggenheim Intrapresæ, started in 1992 as the first museum corporate membership programme in Italy. Listed as "Corporate sponsors": Allegrini, Apice, Eurofood, Florim, Hangar Design Group, Mapei, Pettenon Cosmetics, Roberto Coin, Rubelli, Swatch and Villa Sandi (museum website). The page gives no levels, fees or years and does not say whether the list is complete.
- The museum's corporate page shows logos labelled "Institutional patron" and "Inclusivity Supporter" without naming them in its text.
- Nasher Sculpture Garden: named for a patron's donation (Artlyst, 2014); no amount found.
- Sackler: no reporting found of a Sackler gift to, or the Sackler name at, the Venice museum. The parent foundation's policy applies only to the New York museum it runs: Wikipedia (Sackler family) says that later in 2019 the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum in New York announced it would not accept future donations from any Sacklers involved in Purdue Pharma, after a February 2019 protest by P.A.I.N. at that museum against the Sacklers' backing of it (Wikipedia, P.A.I.N.). Neither page mentions Venice.
- No reporting found of ticket revenue or state grants.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is a family lawsuit against the Foundation, reported in 2014; it concerns how the museum is run, not its funders.
1. ArtsJournal: Matthew Westphal, "Peggy Guggenheim's Descendants Sue Foundation Over What They've Done To Her Venice Palazzo" (8 May 2014)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=173767
- The suit says the Foundation disregarded her wish that the collection be displayed in the palazzo "in its entirety and without additions," and objects to the "expansionist, corporate character" of its activities.
- Names the Foundation and the Venice palazzo. The page gives no reply from the Foundation or court outcome. Criticises how the museum is run, not its funders.
2. Artlyst: no byline ("Via News Desk"), "Peggy Guggenheim Relatives Sue Foundation Over Venice Grave Desecration" (20 May 2014)
URL: https://artlyst.com/?p=168749
- Sandro Rumney and Nicolas Hélion, her grandchildren, say receptions in the garden holding her ashes desecrated it, that 181 works outside the permanent collection were shown, and they want the Nasher donation revoked and the garden name removed.
- The Foundation called the claims "baseless allegations" and said they are "not her heirs and are not mentioned in her will." The article reports no ruling.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Guggenheim Foundation's and a published list of eleven corporate sponsors, with no amounts. No Sackler link to the Venice museum was found. The weak points are the unnamed patrons and the 2014 family complaint that the museum has grown beyond Peggy Guggenheim's wishes. Watch the corporate sponsors and the unnamed institutional patron.
