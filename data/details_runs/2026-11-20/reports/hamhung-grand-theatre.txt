HAMHUNG GRAND THEATRE: Hamhung, North Korea
ID: hamhung-grand-theatre
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Theatre in Hamhung, South Hamgyong Province, which Koryo Tours calls North Korea's second-largest city, fronted by a square that Koryo Tours says is used for leisure and for rehearsing mass events.
- Opened in April 1984, according to Korean Wikipedia, which cites a 2011 study by the Korea Culture and Tourism Institute; Koryo Tours, Lonely Planet and English Wikipedia call it the largest theatre in the country.
- The site covers 110,000 square metres and the building has about 58,000 square metres of floor space, with a main hall of about 2,500 seats and a small hall of about 700 (Korean Wikipedia; North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification).
- The building has seven storeys at the front and nine at the back, 14 columns 26 metres high across the front, outer walls with sculptures of the heroes of the "revolutionary operas", and revolving, sliding and lifting stages (Korean Wikipedia).
- Korean Wikipedia, citing a 2009 Seoul National University conference volume, names the theatre as a representative case of North Korea's turn in the 1970s to modernising traditional building elements, which the Kyonggi University architecture professor Ahn Chang-mo describes as building without tiled roofs. Koryo Tours describes a brutalist concrete exterior that towers over the square.
- Inside, Koryo Tours describes a grey marble entrance hall, spiral staircases, dozens of practice rooms and a large mural of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il among cheering crowds. English Wikipedia says the foyer mural shows Kim Jong Un and Kim Jong Il.
- English Wikipedia says the revolutionary opera troupe uses it for major productions. Koryo Tours adds performances by local bands, including the Samjiyon Band, and says a visiting Russian opera troupe staged Eugene Onegin there in 2010.
- State and party use: a report meeting for the 60th anniversary of Kim Il Sung's guidance visit to South Hamgyong was held there on 19 March 2017, attended by Kim Song Il, chairman of the provincial party committee, and in January 2019 state media reported that markers of Kim Il Sung's and Kim Jong Il's on-site guidance had been put up at the theatre (North Korea Information Portal).
- Visiting: Koryo Tours says anyone passing through the city can see the exterior, and that the interior can be entered only on rare occasions when a performance is on. Lonely Planet says the same.

FUNDING
Hamhung Grand Theatre (Hamhung): state-run: a state theatre used by state art troupes
- The performers state media name there are state companies: KCNA reported that the South Hamgyong Provincial Art Troupe and national art troupes performed at the theatre on 6 March 2010 to celebrate the completion of a vinalon factory, before senior party, state and army officials.
- The state announces its budget only as yearly percentage changes. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The theatre is built, run and used by the North Korean state and its art troupes, so its money is the state's. The weak point is the US blocking order on that state. Worth watching: foreign troupes booked to perform there.
