STOREFRONT FOR ART AND ARCHITECTURE: New York, USA
ID: storefront-art-architecture-new-york
Website: https://storefront.nyc/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit gallery for art and architecture at 97 Kenmare Street in SoHo, Manhattan (Wikipedia, storefront.nyc).
- Founded in 1982 by Kyong Park, R.L. Seltman and Arleen Schloss in a small storefront at 51 Prince Street. It moved to Kenmare Street in 1986 (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Kyong Park (executive director 1982 to 1998), Sarah Herda, Joseph Grima, Eva Franch i Gilabert (2010 to 2018) and José Esparza Chong Cuy (2018 to the present) (Wikipedia). Esparza Chong Cuy signs as Executive Director and Chief Curator (year-end letter, December 2024).
- Building: a triangular ground-level gallery nearly 100 feet long, tapering from 20 feet to 3 feet. In 1993 Steven Holl and Vito Acconci redesigned the facade with rotating panels meant to blur the line between gallery and street; it was meant to last two years and is still there (Wikipedia).
- What is there: exhibitions, talks and public projects. The 2024 programme included the Kyong Park Prize for Art and Architecture, a research convening in the Yucatán Peninsula and a new facade mural by Guadalupe Rosales with Lokey Calderon (year-end letter). The 2025 theme was Homelands (storefront.nyc).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday, 12 to 6 pm (storefront.nyc, checked 2026-10-25). Exhibitions and programmes are free (stated by the space). No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
Storefront for Art and Architecture: mainly foundations and public arts funds, plus individual donors and members
- Mellon Foundation: $1,500,000 awarded 22 February 2024 over 34 months, for capacity building and programming (Mellon Foundation grant page). A separate Mellon grant supports the Kyong Park Prize (year-end letter).
- Andy Warhol Foundation: $80,000 multi-year program support, Fall 2025 cycle (Warhol Foundation grants archive).
- Supporters named by the space: Mellon Foundation, Rockefeller Brothers Fund, Ruth Foundation for the Arts, Teiger Foundation and Graham Foundation; the National Endowment for the Arts, New York State Council on the Arts and New York City Department of Cultural Affairs; the Collaborative Arts Network New York; the board, the Storefront Circle, members and individual donors (stated by the space, storefront.nyc/support). Amounts for these were not found.
- The Rockefeller Brothers Fund received $58 million from John D. Rockefeller Jr. in 1951 and announced it would divest from fossil fuels on 30 September 2014 (Wikipedia). No reporting found of a current fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder behind Storefront.
- The space says it relies on donations from individuals and that contributions are tax deductible (storefront.nyc).
- No reporting found of an annual report, a donor list with amounts or the board members' names in the pages read.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little direct criticism of Storefront. Press coverage is mostly appointment news and anniversary retrospectives, and the one piece with a critical note blames the changed city, not the institution.
1. Architectural Record: Patrick McGraw, "Lost & Found: New York's Storefront for Art and Architecture's 40th Birthday" (2022)
URL: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/15858-lost-and-found-new-yorks-storefront-for-art-and-architectures-40th-birthday
- Argues Storefront mattered to New York's urban history but that its role has diminished as the city changed; "Today, it would be nearly impossible to pull together such a community as Storefront once did."
- Notes SoHo's shift from a bohemian area to boutiques and luxury apartments, and that Storefront depends on community donations and non-profit grants.
- Does not fault Storefront itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Storefront is mainstream philanthropy and public arts funding, led by a $1.5 million Mellon grant, and no flagged funder was found. The weak points are transparency (no published donor amounts or board list found) and the Rockefeller Brothers Fund's family origins, which the fund itself addressed by divesting in 2014. Worth watching is whether it publishes its supporters with amounts.
