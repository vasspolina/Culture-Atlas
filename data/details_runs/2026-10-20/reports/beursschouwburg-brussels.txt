BEURSSCHOUWBURG: Brussels, Belgium
ID: beursschouwburg-brussels
Website: https://beursschouwburg.be (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Multidisciplinary arts centre in central Brussels, in the Auguste Ortsstraat building, with theatre, performance, film, concerts, nightlife, exhibitions, debates and lectures; Dutch Wikipedia describes it as known for giving a platform to young, experimental artists.
- The building opened in 1885 as a shop, café and party hall called "Brasserie flamande"; the hall became a theatre in 1947. The owner, Walckiers, transferred the building to the Flemish Community in 1983, and it was converted into an arts centre by 2004 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Dutch Wikipedia states that the centre is managed by the Flemish Community Commission (VGC) and names Melat Gebeyaw Nigussie as general director and artistic leader. The venue's site states that it embraces shared leadership.
- Programme in autumn 2026: the queer film festival Pink Screens (from 2 November, marking 25 years), the young audiences' film festival Filem'on (3 to 6 November), the music festival Fifty Lab (11 to 13 November) and Basel Zaraa's installation "What Will We Do Without Exile" (3 to 6 December). Some events are free; the rest are ticketed (stated by the venue).
- Renovation: VRT News (15 June 2025) reported that the venue would leave the building in September 2026, with work starting in early 2027 and taking two years, and that the Flemish Department of Culture plans the renovation. The venue's homepage now states that it will temporarily close its doors in September 2027 for a major renovation. The Bovenbouw Architectuur design team was commissioned by the Flemish government's Department of Culture, Youth and Media, Team Vlaams Bouwmeester, Team Brusselse Bouwmeester and the Facilitair Bedrijf of the Flemish government (Beursschouwburg press page).
- Cultural boycott: on 3 October 2025 the venue published a statement, "FREE PALESTINE", that reads "With immediate effect, and in response to persistent calls from Palestinian artists and Palestinian society, we are imposing a cultural boycott on the Israeli state and all Israeli institutions and companies." The venue adds that it acts together with 875 cultural workers and 300 organisations from the Dutch and Belgian cultural sector, and calls on the sports sector, academia, the economic sector and politicians to sever ties (stated by the venue). The boycott is the venue's own action, not a protest aimed at the venue.

FUNDING
Beursschouwburg: mainly public: Government of Flanders, City of Brussels, Flemish Community Commission (VGC) and Brussels Capital Region
- The venue's "financial support and sponsors" list names the Government of Flanders, the City of Brussels, the Flemish Community Commission and the Brussels Capital Region, followed by Maes and Bruzz (stated by the venue).
- The Flemish Community has owned the building since 1983 (Dutch Wikipedia), and the Flemish Department of Culture, Youth and Media is among the bodies that commissioned the renovation design team (Beursschouwburg press page).
- Dutch Wikipedia states that the Flemish Community Commission (VGC) manages the venue.
- Stated by the venue: it and the cultural sector express concern about a "potential 10% cut to the culture budget".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Beursschouwburg is public: the Government of Flanders, the City of Brussels, the Flemish Community Commission and the Brussels Capital Region are listed by the venue as financial support, and the Flemish Community owns the building while the VGC manages it according to Dutch Wikipedia. The venue's own cultural boycott of Israeli institutions is a position it took itself, not a funder issue. The point worth watching is the "potential 10% cut" to the culture budget that the venue raises.
