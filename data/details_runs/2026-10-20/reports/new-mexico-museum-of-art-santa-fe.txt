NEW MEXICO MUSEUM OF ART: Santa Fe, USA
ID: new-mexico-museum-of-art-santa-fe
Website: https://www.nmartmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in two buildings: the Plaza Building at 107 West Palace Avenue, one block from the Santa Fe Plaza, and Vladem Contemporary at 404 Montezuma Avenue in the Railyard Arts District (museum website; Wikipedia).
- Established in 1917 as the Museum of New Mexico Art Gallery; earlier known as The Museum of Fine Arts, and renamed in 2007 (Wikipedia).
- Architect Isaac Rapp designed the Plaza Building, completed in 1917, in Pueblo Revival style with a facade modelled on several Pueblo mission churches (Wikipedia).
- Vladem Contemporary opened in September 2023 and is named for Bob and Ellen Vladem (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 20,000 works, including Georgia O'Keeffe, Ansel Adams and the Taos Society of Artists (Wikipedia).
- The state of New Mexico runs it as one of four state museums in Santa Fe that make up the Museum of New Mexico (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 10am to 5pm, closed Mondays from November to April, and until 7pm on Fridays from May to October. Adults $12, New Mexico residents with ID $7, children 16 and under free, New Mexico residents free on the first Sunday of each month; one ticket covers both buildings (museum website). No reporting found of accessibility details on the pages read.

FUNDING
New Mexico Museum of Art: mainly public: state of New Mexico, with private gifts through the Museum of New Mexico Foundation
- The state runs the museum through the Department of Cultural Affairs (Wikipedia). No reporting found of a published budget for the museum alone.
- The Museum of New Mexico Foundation had revenue of $16,217,538 in the year to June 2025, of which contributions were $6,764,083, and expenses of $7,683,390; chair Michael Knight, President and CEO Jamie Clements (ProPublica).
- Bob and Ellen Vladem gave $4 million to the Foundation in 2018 in exchange for naming rights to Vladem Contemporary (Hyperallergic, Alicia Inez Guzmán; Santa Fe New Mexican, Phill Casaus). The couple say they have given about $15 million in Santa Fe overall, mostly to the Santa Fe Opera (Casaus).
- Foundation business members as of 10 September 2026 include Century Bank, Nusenda Credit Union and Edward Jones (Foundation website).
- No reporting found of Isleta Resort & Casino as a Foundation donor: it is not on the Foundation's business-members page as of 10 September 2026, and no donor list for 2023 was found.

Red flags: removal of the Multi-Cultural mural for Vladem Contemporary
- The mural "Multi-Cultural" was painted in 1980 by Gilberto Guzmán with other artists and students from the Institute of American Indian Arts on the Halpin Records Center in the Railyard (Hyperallergic).
- A 2019 Department of Cultural Affairs press release said no treatment could fully preserve the mural. Guzmán says a 2018 conservator's report offered options including conservation and relocation, and the conservator told the author the department misrepresented her findings (Hyperallergic). Unverified: the claim about the conservator's report, from a piece by a member of the coalition opposing the removal.
- Guzmán sued in US District Court in Albuquerque on 8 March 2021, claiming breach of contract and violation of the Visual Artists Rights Act. The department said the mural would be "retired as part of the renovation" and acknowledged in a display inside the new museum (Santa Fe New Mexican, Phaedra Haywood).
- A protest in front of the mural on 6 February 2021 drew a large police presence, said organiser Carrie Wood (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: naming rights for Vladem Contemporary
- Residents objected to the $4 million naming deal and tied it to the loss of the mural; Bob Vladem called the criticism a "baseball bat to the forehead" and said he did not feel part of the community the gift was meant to serve (Santa Fe New Mexican, Phill Casaus, 8 October 2022). The article quotes no Foundation statement.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the mural removal and the Vladem naming deal, written mostly by local and activist voices.
1. Hyperallergic: Alicia Inez Guzmán, "A Historic Chicano Mural Is Being Destroyed for an Art Museum" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/621539/multi-cultural-mural-santa-fe-vladem-contemporary/
- Argues the museum and the Department of Cultural Affairs are removing a community mural for a new building while ignoring community concerns and being opaque about the decision.
- Quotes Bob Vladem that the mural was not discussed when he made the gift and that "the decision was already made".
- Opinion piece by a member of the Keep Santa Fe Multicultural coalition, which opposes the removal.
2. The Santa Fe New Mexican: Phill Casaus, "Naming rights controversy gives Vladems a lesson in Santa Fe" (2022)
URL: https://www.yahoo.com/news/phill-casaus-naming-rights-controversy-150400443.html
- Column on the backlash over the $4 million gift and the Vladems' reaction; local newspaper, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state institution, so its core money is public, and the identified private money is a $4 million naming gift to the Foundation. The weak points are the Vladem naming deal, the removal of the Guzmán mural over community objections and a lawsuit, and the Foundation's thin public donor disclosure. Watch the Foundation's donor reporting and any later settlement of the mural case.
