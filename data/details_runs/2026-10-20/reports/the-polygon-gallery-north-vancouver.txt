THE POLYGON GALLERY: North Vancouver, Canada
ID: the-polygon-gallery-north-vancouver
Website: https://thepolygon.ca/about/

OVERVIEW
- Photography and lens-based art gallery at 101 Carrie Cates Court, at the Shipyards on Lower Lonsdale, North Vancouver. It describes itself as "grounded in photography" and operates on the unceded territories of the Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh and Musqueam Nations.
- Continues Presentation House Gallery, which operated from 1981; the Polygon opened in its new building on 18 November 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Patkau Architects on the former grounds of the BC Rail North Vancouver station, with 25,000 square feet of exhibition space on two levels, a cafe and a gift shop. Construction began in early 2016 and cost $18 million (Wikipedia).
- Programme: 12 exhibitions featuring 90 artists in 2024, the Kids First family art sessions, a seniors' programme and a 10-year public art commissioning programme with the Burrard Arts Foundation (2024 annual report).
- Visiting: open Wednesday 10am to 5pm, Thursday 10am to 9pm, Friday to Sunday 10am to 5pm. Admission is by donation, all day, every day. Tours are offered in English, Farsi, French, Mandarin and Spanish.

FUNDING
The Polygon Gallery: mixed: public arts councils and local government, corporate sponsors, foundations and individual donors
- 2024 operating revenue $5,411,381 (up 7.6%), expenses $5,408,624, surplus $2,757. Revenue came from earned income (gift shop, bookstore, venue rentals), fundraising (memberships, donations, sponsorships) and an endowment return of $181,783. Gifts of $1,419,458 were added to the endowment in 2024 (2024 annual report).
- Government supporters in 2024: Canada Council for the Arts, Government of Canada, BC Arts Council, Province of British Columbia, the City and District of North Vancouver through the North Vancouver Recreation and Culture Commission, and the District of West Vancouver Community Grants Program.
- Corporate and foundation support in 2024: BMO Financial Group $150,000+; TD Bank Group $100,000+ (presenting sponsor of Tau Lewis's Coloratura); Vancouver's North Shore Tourism Association $50,000+; Canada Gives (Taylor Taliesin Foundation), the Djavad Mowafaghian Foundation, Polygon Homes, RBC Foundation and Rogers Communications $25,000+; Neptune Terminals, the Port of Vancouver, Quay North Urban Development and Seaspan ULC $15,000+; and smaller gifts from the Polar Foundation, PARC Retirement Living, the Consulate General of France, the Deux Mille Foundation, the Houssian Foundation and the Institut für Auslandsbeziehungen at $5,000+ to $10,000+.
- Endowment donors: the Audain Foundation and the Lind Family at $500,000+. The Audain Foundation also made an investment in support of the Audain Chief Curator. The endowment qualifies for matching funds from the federal Department of Canadian Heritage through its Endowment Incentive program.
- Mayor's Gala 2024: Polygon Homes and Quay North Urban Development were presenting sponsors; Neptune Terminals, Seaspan ULC, Royal Bank of Canada, TD Bank Group, G3 Terminal Vancouver and Lyft Commodity Trading were among the Gold Sponsors.
- Neptune Terminals, the Port of Vancouver, Seaspan ULC and the North Shore Tourism Association sponsored the Kids First programme in 2024. Neptune unloads trains and loads ships at its North Vancouver terminal, exporting steelmaking coal from British Columbia and Alberta and potash from Saskatchewan; Canpotex is one of its owners (Neptune Terminals, 1 August 2019).

Red flags: TD Bank Group, 2024 money-laundering guilty plea
- TD Bank N.A. pleaded guilty on 10 October 2024 to conspiring to operate without a Bank Secrecy Act compliant anti-money laundering program, to file inaccurate Currency Transaction Reports and to launder monetary instruments, and agreed to pay $1.8 billion in penalties (US Department of Justice, Criminal Division).
- The Polygon's 2024 annual report lists TD Bank Group at $100,000+, as presenting sponsor of Tau Lewis's Coloratura and as a Mayor's Gala Gold Sponsor.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Polygon is a mix of arts council and local government grants, bank, developer and corporate sponsors, foundations and individual donors, with an endowment led by the Audain Foundation and the Lind family. Neptune Terminals, a port terminal that exports steelmaking coal and potash, gave $15,000+ in 2024 and was a Gold Sponsor of the Mayor's Gala. TD Bank Group, which gave $100,000+ in 2024, pleaded guilty to money-laundering conspiracy in October 2024.
