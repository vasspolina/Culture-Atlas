NATIONAL ARTS FESTIVAL: Makhanda, South Africa
ID: national-arts-festival
Website: https://nationalartsfestival.co.za (checked 2026-10-25; loads, but its About, Sponsors and Board pages were placeholders or did not open)

OVERVIEW
- Annual multi-arts festival in Makhanda (formerly Grahamstown), Eastern Cape, a university city home to Rhodes University (Wikipedia). The 2026 edition ran from 25 June to 5 July (Grocott's Mail).
- First held in the 1970s: Wikipedia lists 64 Main programme events in 1974 and gives a founding date of 13 July 1973.
- Organised by the National Arts Festival Office, a non-profit Section 21 company (Wikipedia). Grocott's Mail reports that the 2026 national grant went to the Grahamstown Foundation in partnership with the festival.
- Programme: a Main programme and the Fringe, which had more than 200 productions in 2026 (Grocott's Mail). Standard Bank sponsors the Young Artist Awards (festival site).
- CEO Monica Newton hands over to Palesa Kadi after the 2026 edition (Mail & Guardian, 10 July 2026).
- Visiting: no admission prices or accessibility information found on the pages that opened.

FUNDING
National Arts Festival: mixed: provincial and local government, a national lottery body, one bank sponsor and ticket sales, with national government money cut since 2025
- National government: the Department of Sport, Arts and Culture paid R5.5 million under a three-year contract for 2022 to 2024 and nothing for 2025. Newton says that was about 10% of the festival's budget (Grocott's Mail, 24 September 2025).
- For 2025 the festival was left out of the new Mzansi Golden Economy funding model because its project timing fell outside the department's requirements (Grocott's Mail).
- For 2026 the festival secured a Mzansi Golden Economy grant to support the Fringe, artist development and the Ovation Awards. The amount is not stated (Grocott's Mail, 12 June 2026).
- The 2026 festival also had support from the National Lottery Commission, the Eastern Cape provincial government and the Sarah Baartman District Municipality (Mail & Guardian). Newton said the province "continued to support the Festival generously" and the district municipality increased its 2025 support (Grocott's Mail).
- Corporate: Standard Bank is a long-standing partner (Mail & Guardian); the Eastern Cape Development Corporation also stayed a partner in 2025 (Grocott's Mail). Newton says the festival "definitely needs more corporate sponsors" (Mail & Guardian). No amounts found.
- No reporting found of a current annual report or accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Older pieces say the festival is an elite event in a poor town; recent pieces criticise the national minister's funding decisions, not the festival. No piece found criticises its sponsors.
1. Mahala, republished by Amandla!: Daniel Krähmer, "A Festival of Resistance" (2012)
URL: https://www.amandla.org.za/a-festival-of-resistance/
- Argues that high prices, distant venues and a largely white audience keep most locals out, and that public money pledged to a festival in a town where many lack running water will "reinforce the inequality" unless the festival addresses it.
- A Rhodes University student's essay; not an art-press outlet.
2. This Is Africa: Brent Meersman, "Rethinking the National Arts Festival" (2015)
URL: https://thisisafrica.me/arts-and-culture/rethinking-the-national-arts-festival/
- Answers the elitism charge, calling it "a misplaced, almost meaningless word", and reports R250,000 in free tickets and a 40% black audience.
3. ArtThrob: ArtThrob Editors, "In Solidarity with Gabrielle Goliath: The Arts Community Issues a Statement and Petition" (2026)
URL: https://artthrob.co.za/2026/01/14/in-solidarity-with-gabrielle-goliath-the-arts-community-issues-a-statement-and-petition/
- A collective of artists says Minister Gayton McKenzie's decisions amount to "stealth forms of censorship"; 302 people had signed by 14 January 2026.
- Names the festival as one whose funding the minister declined to renew. It criticises the minister, not the festival.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2012: the Unemployed People's Movement and the Cape Town collective SOS (Sounds of the South) organised the Rhini Festival of Resistance, an alternative to the official festival. Daniel Krähmer (Mahala, republished by Amandla!, 13 July 2012) says it "petered out and failed to fully contest" the official festival's perceived elitism. The article calls it an alternative festival, not a protest, petition or boycott. No reporting found of a later protest, boycott or open letter aimed at the festival, its sponsors or its management.
- McKenzie said festivals were not defunded but had to apply through the open call, and that the festival was unsuccessful for 2025 (Daily Maverick, 6 November 2025).
- The petition above lists the festival's funding refusal among his decisions, including, in the petition's account, his cancelling Gabrielle Goliath's "Elegy" as South Africa's Venice Biennale entry.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the festival is mixed public money, a national lottery body and Standard Bank, with no flagged funder named in the sources read. The weak points are the loss of national government funding since 2025 and the absence of published accounts. Worth watching are whether the national grant continues beyond the 2026 Fringe grant and whether accounts appear.
