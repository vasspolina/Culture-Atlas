BLUECOAT: Liverpool, UK
ID: bluecoat-liverpool
Website: https://www.thebluecoat.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre in Bluecoat Chambers, School Lane, Liverpool L1 3BX, a Grade I listed building and the oldest surviving building in central Liverpool (Wikipedia).
- Built in 1716 to 1725 for a charity school founded in 1708 by Robert Styth and the sea captain Bryan Blundell; the Sandon Studios Society rented it from 1907, and the Bluecoat Society of Arts, formed in 1927, laid claim to being the oldest arts centre in Great Britain (Wikipedia).
- Blundell profited from trade with Africa and the colonies, "central to which was the transatlantic slave trade"; the Blundell family made 111 known voyages over six decades, and inventories record 29,679 captured Africans, 5,634 of whom died (stated by the space, Colonial Legacies project, 2021 to 2022).
- A 2,250 square metre wing by BIQ Architecten reopened in March 2008, Liverpool's year as European Capital of Culture, with four galleries and a performance space (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, 22 artist studios, a café and bistro, independent shops, a garden, a print studio, a library, and Blue Room, an arts programme for learning disabled adults (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- Visiting: the gallery is free, open Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Monday except bank holidays. The new wing and garden are step-free with lifts; the older listed parts have limited access; induction loops at the desk, café, Performance Space and Sandon Room (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Bluecoat: mainly public: Arts Council England and Liverpool City Council, with lottery and trust grants
- Run by Bluecoat Arts Centre Limited, charity 700862: income £2,346,014 and spending £2,512,865 in the year to 31 March 2025, with 90 staff; spending exceeded income in each year from 2023 to 2025 (Charity Commission data via OpenCharities).
- Arts Council England renewed Bluecoat as a National Portfolio Organisation for three years from March 2023; no amount stated (stated by the space, 2022).
- Arts Council England gave £680,000 in May 2024 from its government-funded Capital Investment Programme to turn the first-floor bistro into an events and performance space (stated by the space).
- Liverpool City Council and Culture Liverpool are listed funders, with the National Lottery Heritage Fund and Bloomberg Philanthropies (stated by the space).
- The National Lottery Heritage Fund paid for the new shop and the "A Cultural Heritage for Liverpool" project; the Health Lottery Foundation funds Young Blue Room (stated by the space, 2025 review).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies chose Bluecoat in January 2025 for its Digital Accelerator Programme, support for technology and management; no amount stated (stated by the space).
- Business membership starts at £500 a year; the page names no business members (stated by the space).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Bluecoat's money is mainly public and lottery money with a few trusts and a Bloomberg programme; no flagged funder was found. The weak points are running deficits from 2023 to 2025 and the building's founding ties to the slave trade, which Bluecoat itself has researched and published. Worth watching: whether business members are ever named and whether the deficits continue.
