AUCKLAND ART GALLERY TOI O TĀMAKI – Auckland, New Zealand
Website: https://www.aucklandartgallery.com (indexed in search results; direct fetch not permitted in this run, 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- New Zealand's largest public art gallery, run by Tātaki Auckland Unlimited (TAU, Auckland Council's arts and events agency). Building reopened after redevelopment in 2011.
- Director Kirsten Lacy. Advisory Board chair Christopher Swasbrook (from March 2024). TAU chair Vicki Salmon, CEO Nick Hill (2023-24 annual review).
- 507,507 visitors in 2023-24 (stated by the space). International visitors have paid a NZ$20 fee since January 2018 (ArtsHub, 2018).

FUNDING
Auckland Art Gallery: mainly public (Auckland Council via TAU), with corporate partners and private trusts.
- Auckland Council funds operations through TAU and funded the Heritage Restoration Project, with NZ Lottery Grants Board support (stated by the space, 2023-24 review).
- In 2018 the operating budget was about NZ$6.9M, down 25% from NZ$9.2M in 2012, after the 2011 council merger (ArtsHub, March 2018). No later reporting retrieved.
- Partners Circle (2023-24): BMW, Van Cleef & Arpels, HSBC, Anthony Harper, Baker Tilly, Beca, Cordis, Omnigraphics, Trinity Hill.
- HSBC and the Auckland Art Gallery Foundation supported "A Century of Modern Art" (7 June to 28 September 2025), lent by the Toledo Museum of Art (TAU press release).
- Auckland Art Gallery Foundation endowment about NZ$4.5M (up 41%), advised by JBWere.
- Trusts: Chartwell Collection Trust, Mackelvie Trust (existing record), Friedlander Foundation, Sue Fisher Art Trust, Stout Trust, Auckland Contemporary Art Trust, M A Serra Trust, Lyndsay Garland Trust and others. Julian and Josie Robertson gave 15 modern works (Robertson Gift).
- Government: Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and Asia New Zealand Foundation supported the Guo Pei exhibition.

Red flags: Partner bank's money-laundering record
- HSBC agreed in December 2012 to pay a record US$1.92 billion to US authorities under a deferred prosecution agreement; prosecutors said it let Mexican cartels move at least $881M and processed about $660M of sanctions-prohibited transactions (CNN Money, 2012).

Not related: Suter Art Gallery, Nelson — a different gallery that removed Diane Prince's "Flagging the Future" in May 2025; Auckland Art Gallery had earlier removed the original 1995 work (1News).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is a council-run public institution whose partners and trusts are named in its reviews. The only funder issue found is HSBC's 2012 US money-laundering settlement; HSBC sponsored a 2025 exhibition.
