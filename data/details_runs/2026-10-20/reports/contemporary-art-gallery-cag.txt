CONTEMPORARY ART GALLERY (CAG): Vancouver, Canada
ID: contemporary-art-gallery-cag
Website: https://www.contemporaryartgallery.ca (checked 2026-10-24; redirects to https://cagvancouver.org)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting public art gallery at 555 Nelson Street, at the edge of Yaletown, on the ground floor and mezzanine of a residential condominium (Wikipedia).
- Opened as the Greater Vancouver Artist's Gallery, set up in its first years through federal employment programmes for artists; the gallery says 1972 and Wikipedia says 1971. Incorporated as a charitable society in 1976, renamed Contemporary Art Gallery in 1983, an artist-run centre from 1984 and a non-collecting public gallery from 1996 (CAG; CanadaHelps).
- Moved in 2001 into a purpose-built space under the City of Vancouver's Amenity Bonus programme in cooperation with Bosa Ventures Inc.; it won an AIBC Architectural Award of Excellence in 2002. Wikipedia names Martin Lewis and Noel Best as designers.
- On site: two galleries, the B.C. Binning Gallery and the Alvin Balkind Gallery (Wikipedia). Matthew Hyland is Executive Director. From 2018 the gallery curated art at the Canada Gallery in London under a five-year contract, and in 2019 it put art on Vancouver TransLink buses (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is always free. As of 2026-10-24 the gallery was closed for installation until 13 November 2026; regular opening hours and accessibility details are not given on the pages read.

FUNDING
Contemporary Art Gallery: mainly public: Canada Council, BC Arts Council, BC Gaming Policy and Enforcement Branch and City of Vancouver, plus donors
- A registered charity supported by the Canada Council for the Arts, the BC Arts Council, the BC Gaming Policy and Enforcement Branch and the City of Vancouver, along with individual, corporate and foundation donors (stated by the gallery).
- The BC Gaming Policy and Enforcement Branch is a named funder; no amount or year is published.
- Corporate support: the gallery offers corporate membership, sponsorship and venue rental, and recognises sponsors on an annual donor wall; the sponsorship page names none (stated by the gallery).
- The staff page lists a "TD Assistant Curator of Programming"; the page does not say who pays for the post.
- Board: Denis Walz (President), Justine A. Chambers (Vice President), Emily Wheeler (Treasurer), Farzina Coladon, Irshad Khan, Ron Regan, Michelle Sound, Nada Vuksic and Ann Webb; no affiliations are given (stated by the gallery).
- No reporting found of Connor, Clark and Lunn Investment Management as a funder on any page that could be opened, nor of annual reports, financial statements or a donor list.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is federal, provincial and city arts funding, including the BC Gaming branch, plus donors; no private funder with a documented controversy was confirmed. The weak points are that no accounts, amounts or donor names are published, and that the staff page lists a post titled "TD Assistant Curator of Programming" without saying who pays for it. Watch the corporate sponsor list.
