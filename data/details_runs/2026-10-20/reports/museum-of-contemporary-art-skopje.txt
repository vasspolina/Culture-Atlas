MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART SKOPJE: Skopje, North Macedonia
ID: museum-of-contemporary-art-skopje
Website: https://msu.mk (checked 2026-10-25; loads, lists exhibitions running to 30 October 2026)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of modern and contemporary art at Samoilova 17, Skopje, near the city's medieval fortress (Wikipedia).
- Created by an act of the Skopje Town Assembly on 11 February 1964, after the earthquake of 26 July 1963 destroyed much of the city. In October 1963 the International Association of Plastic Arts asked artists worldwide to give works for reconstruction; AICA and ICOM also contributed (museum website).
- Wikipedia says 35 countries and the United Nations donated artworks under the slogan "Solidarity with Skopje".
- Building: completed in 1970. The Polish government donated the design, chosen through a competition and drawn by the Polish architects J. Mokrzynski, E. Wierzbicki and W. Klyzewski. It has about 5,500 square metres, over 3,000 of them exhibition space, with a cinema, library and storage (museum website).
- On site: galleries, a 120-seat cinema, a library and archives, a shop and a café (Wikipedia). The museum says it holds the only archives on Macedonian art in the country.
- Collection: modern art from many countries, mostly 1950s to 1970s, including Picasso, Calder and Vasarely (Wikipedia). Its Solidarity Collection toured to Kunsthalle Wien in 2023 and 2024 (e-flux).
- Visiting: the site lists Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 17:00 and Sunday 09:00 to 13:00; the ticket costs 100 denars. No discounts or accessibility information on the page.

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary Art Skopje: mainly public: the state and city, with a small past company sponsorship
- The Skopje Town Assembly founded the museum in 1964, and the Polish government gave the building design (museum website).
- The museum's notice of its director Mira Gaćina's resignation (undated) expresses the hope that its work "will find even greater support from the Ministry of Culture and long-term partners and supporters". The page gives no figures. Gaćina had been director since mid-2017 and resigned for personal reasons after six years.
- In April 2017 Eurolink Insurance paid 335,000 denars (about 5,447 euros) for the museum library's interior and said it would give a further 1,100,000 denars (about 17,886 euros) as general sponsor for one year (Giving Balkans, 26 April 2017).
- Zlatko Teodosievski, senior curator at the National Art Gallery, told Lossi 36 (2022) that museums are limited to whatever funds the government gives and that the cultural budget was set to fall by 30 percent that year.
- No reporting found of a current corporate sponsor, annual accounts or a board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found in art media. The one piece found criticises state neglect of museums, not the museum's own management; the Vienna exhibition of its collection was presented in exhibition listings and no independent review was found.
1. Lossi 36: Xandie Kuenning, "In Skopje, Museums Languish While Pseudo-Historical Monuments Thrive" (2022)
URL: https://lossi36.com/2022/09/14/in-skopje-museums-languish-while-pseudo-historical-monuments-thrive/
- Describes the museum as nearly deserted on a June visit, with the cafeteria closed and one or two rooms open.
- Argues that the state spent over 650 million euros on the Skopje 2014 monuments, leaving little for museums.
- Unverified: funding is often given only to museums whose directors belong to the Minister of Culture's party, from the author, Xandie Kuenning, in this independent volunteer-run outlet, with no source named.
- Quotes Zlatko Teodosievski of the National Art Gallery, not museum staff. A general-interest outlet, not an art-press title.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public Macedonian money plus a 2017 insurance sponsorship, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no accounts, board or funder list are published on the pages read, and that its budget is not reported. Worth watching are the state of the building and programme under reduced cultural budgets, and the director's post after Gaćina's resignation.
