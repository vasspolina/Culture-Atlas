KUINDZHI ART MUSEUM: Mariupol, Ukraine
ID: kuindzhi-art-museum
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the painter Arkhip Kuindzhi, who was born in Mariupol, a couple of blocks from the city's Drama Theatre, where hundreds were killed in a Russian airstrike on 16 March 2022 (Bellingcat).
- A Kuindzhi museum was first proposed in 1914; premises were donated in 1997, renovation began in 2008, and it opened on 30 October 2010 as a branch of the Mariupol Museum of Local Lore (Wikipedia).
- It occupied a turn-of-the-century Art Nouveau mansion built by a city official and philanthropist as a wedding gift for his daughter, with halls of Kuindzhi's letters, photographs and documents and life-size copies of his paintings (The Art Newspaper). Wikipedia dates the building to 1902.
- Collection: 650 paintings, 960 graphic works, 150 sculptures and more than 300 decorative objects; the three Kuindzhi originals, the sketch Red Sunset and the studies Elbrus and Autumn. Crimea, came from the State Russian Museum in the 1960s (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- On 21 March 2022 Konstantin Chernyavsky, head of Ukraine's artists' union, said a Russian airstrike had destroyed the museum; the originals were not in the building (The Art Newspaper; Bellingcat). Planet imagery of 24 April showed the building damaged but not razed, and Bellingcat could not determine the weapon.
- Wikipedia lists the museum as dissolved in 2022. The occupation authorities later set up an open-air display of digital replicas of its removed paintings in a central Mariupol park (Justice Info, Shahida Tulaganova, 29 July 2025).

FUNDING
Kuindzhi Art Museum: state-run: a city museum branch until 2022, since then claimed by the occupation's culture ministry
- Until 2022 it was a branch of the city's Museum of Local Lore (Wikipedia).
- After Mariupol fell, a handwritten sign at the front door declared the museum under the "Ministry of Culture" of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic (DNR) (Bellingcat).

Red flags: Sanctions on the Donetsk People's Republic, which claims the museum
- The DNR was added to the US Treasury's Specially Designated Nationals list on 16 July 2014 (OFAC).
- The EU listed it under Council Regulation 269/2014 and the UK under its Russia sanctions regulations, both from 25 July 2014 (Black Sea News sanctions database).

Red flags: 2022 removal of the paintings to Donetsk
- On 27 April 2022 Petro Andryushchenko, adviser to Mariupol's mayor, said Nataliya Kapustnikova, director of the Museum of Local Lore, had handed the three Kuindzhi paintings to the occupiers; she told the pro-Kremlin outlet Izvestiya the paintings were safe (Bellingcat).
- Footage released by pro-Kremlin media showed DNR culture minister Zheltyakov, the DNR television anchor Alexander Mozgovoi and men in balaclavas packing paintings into a minivan; Mozgovoi said the works went to Donetsk for safekeeping and "Nothing has been stolen" (The Moscow Times, Yanina Sorokina, 2022).
- Lina Garmash of the Donetsk History Museum said 25 paintings from Mariupol, including three Kuindzhis, were in storage there (The Moscow Times).
- More than 2,000 artefacts were taken from the museum to temporary storage in Donetsk (Justice Info).
- In June 2023 the EU Council sanctioned Kapustnikova, saying she "gave those works willingly over to the Russian authorities" without the knowledge or consent of the Ukrainian authorities or the Kuindzhi museum's director (Justice Info).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In June 2023 the EU also sanctioned other museum directors in occupied Ukraine over the transfer of collections, among them Tatyana Bratchenko and Natalya Desyatova of Kherson and Andrei Malgin of the Central Museum of Tavrida in Crimea (Justice Info).
- The Kuindzhi works are among the transfers that this sanctions round addressed.

BOTTOM LINE
There is no private money to grade: a Ukrainian city museum until 2022, its damaged building has been claimed since 2022 by a sanctioned occupation body. Worth watching is whether its paintings return to Ukraine.
