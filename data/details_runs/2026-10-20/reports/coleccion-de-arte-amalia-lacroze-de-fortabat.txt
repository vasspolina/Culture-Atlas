COLECCIÓN DE ARTE AMALIA LACROZE DE FORTABAT: Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: coleccion-de-arte-amalia-lacroze-de-fortabat
Website: https://coleccionamalita.org.ar

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum, branded Colección AMALITA, at Olga Cossettini 141 in Puerto Madero, Buenos Aires, with the building overlooking the northernmost dock of the district (museum website for the address, Wikipedia).
- Founded by María Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat (1921 to 2012), longtime chairperson and chief stockholder of the cement maker Loma Negra, who set aside part of her own art collection for it; opened on 22 October 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder; it is run by the Fundación Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, which hired the Uruguayan-born architect Rafael Viñoly for the building (Wikipedia).
- The two-storey concrete, steel and glass building has about 6,000 square metres indoors, with aluminium awnings on the roof that move with the sun; Fortabat said "I've always wanted to look at pictures and the stars at the same time" (Wikipedia).
- The building holds two exhibition halls, a library, an auditorium, offices and a café-restaurant (Wikipedia).
- At opening the collection had 230 works, including a Turner oil, "Juliet and her Nurse", and an Andy Warhol portrait made for Fortabat; the museum website lists Argentine artists such as Antonio Berni, Raúl Soldi, Xul Solar and Carlos Alonso, international works by Rodin, Dalí and Warhol, and Egyptian, Greek and Byzantine antiquities (Wikipedia, museum website).
- The artistic director is Germán Barraza (museum website).

FUNDING
Colección de Arte Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat: private: founder's foundation, money from the Loma Negra cement fortune
- The museum is run by the Fundación Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, which Fortabat founded (Wikipedia); the foundation's stated focus is projects that widen access to education and health in Argentina (museum website).
- Fortabat's wealth came from Loma Negra, founded in 1926 by her husband Alfredo Fortabat; she became its near-sole owner after he died in 1976 and sold her 80% stake to Camargo Corrêa of Brazil in May 2005 for just over US$1 billion (Wikipedia).
- At the 2008 opening the foundation's board included Ramón Carlos Leiguarda and Pablo Germán Louge; asked the cost of the project, Fortabat said "no tengo idea", I have no idea (Alicia de Arteaga, "El Museo Fortabat abrió sus puertas", La Nación, 22 October 2008; translation mine).
- Wikipedia states that the foundation has given more than US$40 million to Argentine charities since 1976.

WIDER CONTEXT
- History, concerning Loma Negra under the dictatorship that began in 1976, before the 2005 sale: Horacio Verbitsky, "Microeconomía del terror" (Página/12, 25 May 2014; subtitle in my translation: "Loma Negra and the tangible reasons for its support of the 1976 coup"), reports a preliminary study by the Procuración General de la Nación's economic investigation office (OFINEC), based on 118 Loma Negra account books for 1975 to 1983. Verbitsky describes the company as then owned by Fortabat. The study found that labour costs fell from 21% to 14% of the company's costs and expenses between 1976 and 1977, that taxes fell from 8% of sales in 1975/76 to 3% in 1982/83, and that profits peaked in the year that closed on 31 March 1981.
- History: the same article says the labour lawyer Carlos Alberto Moreno, who defended Loma Negra's mine workers and won lawsuits against the company over occupational illness, was murdered in May 1977. It reports that in 2012 the Tribunal Oral Federal de Mar del Plata, sitting in Tandil, sentenced the retired colonels Julio Tommasi and Roque Pappalardo and the non-commissioned officer José Luis Ojeda to life imprisonment, and that the ruling included an order to investigate the board of Loma Negra.
- History: the Boston Globe obituary, "Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, Argentine patroness of art; at 90" (2012), says the Loma Negra cement businesses flourished through government contracts with the military junta.
- Connection: Fortabat controlled Loma Negra until she sold it in 2005, and she created the foundation that runs the museum, which opened in 2008. These points concern the company in the 1970s, not the foundation's funding today.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private: the Fundación Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, built on the founder's Loma Negra cement fortune, which she sold for just over US$1 billion in 2005. A 2014 Página/12 article by Horacio Verbitsky reports a Procuración General study of Loma Negra's books under the 1976 dictatorship, when Fortabat owned the company.
