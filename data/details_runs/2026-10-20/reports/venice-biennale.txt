VENICE BIENNALE: Venice, Italy
ID: venice-biennale
Website: https://www.labiennale.org/en (checked 2026-10-24; the donors page for the 2026 art exhibition loads; the organisation page returned 404)

OVERVIEW
- International arts organisation whose main exhibition is held at the Giardini and the Arsenale in Castello, Venice (Wikipedia).
- The Venice City Council set up the biennial in 1893 and the first exhibition opened in 1895. It became a foundation in 2004; Pietrangelo Buttafuoco has been president since 2024 (Wikipedia).
- The 61st Art Exhibition, "In Minor Keys", curated by Koyo Kouoh, runs 9 May to 22 November 2026 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: not stated on the pages read.

FUNDING
Venice Biennale: mixed: Italian public funds, tickets and commercial income, plus sponsors and donors
- 2024 budget forecast: public contributions of €33.095 million, and €31.427 million from tickets, publishing and food and beverage, including sponsorship and donations estimated at €7.78 million (Biennale budget report 2024, forecast not final accounts).
- The 61st exhibition's donors page lists 46 donors under "Main Donor", including Teiger Foundation, Getty, Ford Foundation, Ammodo, LUMA Foundation, Christian Dior Couture, Samsung Foundation of Culture, Batia and Idan Ofer and The Helis Foundation. It gives no amounts or individual years.
- Sponsors (Milano Luxury Life, 10 May 2026): Bvlgari, exclusive partner for 2026, 2028 and 2030; illycaffè, main sponsor; Zegna; American Express; Bloomberg Philanthropies since 2022. No amounts.
- European Commission: a €2 million grant for 2025 to 2028. On 12 July 2026 Commissioner Henna Virkkunen announced the Commission's endorsement of its withdrawal, a recommendation to the executive agency that has the final say (Euronews).

Red flags: Helis Foundation, oil family money
- The Helis Foundation, listed on the 2026 donors page with no amount, was "established and funded by the William Helis Family in 1955"; its page does not mention Helis Oil & Gas (foundation website).
- Unverified: Healthy Gulf, an advocacy group, wrote on 17 September 2019 that it is "the philanthropic entity of Helis Oil & Gas and the William G. Helis estate" and that Helis is "directly dredging and drilling over 300 acres of land in coastal Louisiana".

Red flags: boycott and protest at the 2026 edition
- Russia's pavilion: the Biennale announced Russia's return in March 2026. On 11 March ArtReview reported the Commission's warning, after a letter from 22 European culture ministers. An open letter demanded a revision, culture minister Alessandro Giuli asked for the Biennale's correspondence with Russia to check sanctions compliance, and the Commission threatened to freeze its grant (Hyperallergic).
- Jury: on 23 April the awards jury said it would omit countries whose leaders face ICC charges. After Israeli pavilion artist Belu-Simion Fainaru filed a legal warning and the Biennale's legal department warned of personal liability, the jury resigned on 30 April (Hyperallergic, 3 June 2026).
- Strike: on 8 May 2026 at least 27 of 100 national pavilions were partly or fully closed in a 24-hour strike demanding Israel's exclusion (Hyperallergic, ArtAsiaPacific, 11 May 2026). The Arsenale closed entirely by late afternoon (ArtAsiaPacific).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly Italian public funds, ticket income and a long list of private donors, one of whom, the Helis Foundation, was established and funded by the William Helis family; only an advocacy group ties it to Helis Oil & Gas. The documented weak points are the 2026 Russia decision, which led the Commission to endorse withdrawing an EU grant, the jury's resignation, and the strike. Worth watching: the final accounts and whether sponsors react to the protests.
