LISTASAVN FØROYA (NATIONAL GALLERY OF THE FAROE ISLANDS): Tórshavn, Faroe Islands
ID: listasavn-f-roya-national-gallery-of-the-faroe-islands-t-rshavn
Website: https://www.art.fo (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- The Faroe Islands' main museum for Faroese art, in the northern part of Viðarlundin park, a ten-minute walk from the centre of Tórshavn, at Gundadalsvegur 9 (stated by the space).
- Grew from the Faroe Islands Art Association, founded in 1941 by Faroese students in Copenhagen, and the General Art Museum that Parliament set up after 1948. The two collections merged into Listasavn Føroya in 1989, when the National Gallery was officially established (stated by the space).
- Buildings: the Listaskálin exhibition hall of 1970, built from sketches by J.P. Gregoriussen, and a 1993 extension by Niels F. Truelsen with Gregoriussen that houses the permanent collection. The extension has pavilions with saddle and pyramid roofs and a black-stained exterior that recalls Faroese boathouses (stated by the space).
- Four bronze sculptures by Hans Pauli Olsen stand around the complex (stated by the space).
- On site: temporary and permanent galleries, a shop selling books, posters and postcards, a café, and the offices of Listafelag Føroya, the artists' association (stated by the space, Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 2,500 works from the 1830s to today, including the largest collection of works by S. Joensen-Mikines (stated by the space).
- Visiting: from 1 May to 31 October open daily 11:00 to 17:00; in winter Monday and Thursday to Sunday 11:00 to 16:00. Adults 110 DKK, seniors 90, students 30, under-20s free. The accessibility page gives no details.

FUNDING
Listasavn Føroya: public bodies and private foundations, the foundations paying for building projects
- Governance: a board of four, one member each from the state government, the artists' association, the artists' union and the Tórshavn city council (Wikipedia).
- 1993 extension: paid for by Føroya Landsstýri (the Faroese government), Tórshavnar Kommuna, Listafelag Føroya, Fossbankin, Bókhandlarin Hans og frú Kate Niclasen - Dronning Margrethe og Prins Henriks Fond (listed on one line), Augustinus Fonden, Beckett-Fonden, Knud Højgaards Fond and Velux Fonden. No amounts are posted (stated by the space).
- Expansion and renovation: on 19 March 2026 the gallery signed a building contract for an expansion due in spring 2028. Funders named are the Augustinus Foundation, the Louis-Hansen Foundation, the Villum Foundation and the Municipality of Tórshavn; the Løgting passed an act in support unanimously. No amounts or total cost are posted (stated by the space).
- Karina Lykke Grand left the director's post on 1 April 2024; board chairman Niels Christian Nolsøe said she had secured funding for the modernisation of the museum (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of annual operating grant figures or a donor list.

Red flags: Tobacco, Augustinus Fonden
- Augustinus Fonden funded the 1993 extension and is named again as a funder of the 2026 expansion (stated by the space).
- The foundation owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, its investment company (Wikipedia). That company reported holdings above 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group as of 1 May 2025 (Scandinavian Tobacco Group's shareholder page).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The Faroese government and Tórshavn are among the builders of the museum, and its four-member board includes state and city seats. The weak point is Augustinus Fonden, a tobacco-linked foundation that paid for the 1993 extension and is a named funder of the 2026 expansion, so the tobacco link is current. Watch the amounts behind the expansion, which are not posted.
