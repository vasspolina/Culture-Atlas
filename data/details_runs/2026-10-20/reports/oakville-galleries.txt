OAKVILLE GALLERIES: Oakville, Canada
ID: oakville-galleries
Website: https://www.oakvillegalleries.com

OVERVIEW
- A not-for-profit contemporary art museum (HIP directory, updated 14 July 2026): one institution with two buildings and an 11-acre garden fronting Lake Ontario, at Centennial Square, 120 Navy Street, and Gairloch Gardens, 1306 Lakeshore Road East. It focuses on the presentation, collection, preservation and interpretation of recent work in the visual and media arts (Visit Oakville).
- Gairloch Gardens: Colonel W.G. MacKendrick had the house built in 1923 to 1924. Toronto investment banker James Gairdner bought the estate, named it Gairloch, and on his death in 1972 left it to the Town of Oakville for use as a park and gallery. The town designated the property under the Ontario Heritage Act in 1986 (Canada's Historic Places).
- A registered charity, number 119064418RR0001 (gallery website).
- Visiting: both sites are open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 17:00, Monday by appointment, closed Sundays and statutory holidays; admission is free. All public areas at Gairloch Gardens are wheelchair accessible, but there is no accessible ground-floor restroom and no automatic door opener; Centennial Square is fully accessible with an elevator (gallery website).

FUNDING
Oakville Galleries: mixed: public arts funders and the Town of Oakville, with corporate sponsors, foundations and individual donors
- The HIP directory (updated 14 July 2026) lists as funding sources the city or town, the federal Department of Canadian Heritage, grants from the Canada Council for the Arts and the Ontario Arts Council, fundraising, donations, fees and a foundation.
- The Funding & Partners page shows logos linked to canadacouncil.ca, arts.on.ca, ontario.ca, oakville.ca, visitoakville.com and mondriaanfonds.nl.
- Sponsor tiers on that page, shown as logos linked to each sponsor's site: Platinum, Hatch and Taglieri Group; Gold, Chatsworth Fine Homes and Siemens; Entertainment, Henderson Partners; Silver, Baylis Medical and Engelbrecht Associates; Media, Best Side Magazine.
- Foundations: Kucey Family Foundation for Children, Vital Community Fund, Larry & Gerry Wilson Family Fund, Alma Fund and Haslett-Theall Family Foundation. Individual donors, benefactors and patrons are named on the page, and donors give through the Give Oakville Campaign.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Siemens: in January 2020, after protests, the company said it would carry out its contract to supply rail signalling for Adani's Carmichael coal mine in Queensland. Chief executive Joe Kaeser wrote in an open letter that there was no legally and economically responsible way to unwind the contract (Mining Technology, Matthew Hall, "Siemens to pursue Adani mine contract despite pressure from protesters", 13 January 2020). Wikipedia gives the contract value as $20 million and adds that Greta Thunberg called on Siemens to stop delivering equipment.
- Connection: the gallery's Funding & Partners page shows Siemens under Gold Sponsor. The protests targeted Siemens, not Oakville Galleries.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Oakville Galleries comes from the Canada Council for the Arts, the Ontario Arts Council and the municipality of Oakville according to the HIP directory, with named corporate sponsors, foundations and individual donors. Siemens, shown under Gold Sponsor, said in January 2020 it would keep its Carmichael coal-mine rail contract after public protests.
