HAMMER MUSEUM: Los Angeles, USA
ID: hammer-museum
Website: https://hammer.ucla.edu (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), at 10899 Wilshire Boulevard, on the corner of Westwood and Wilshire boulevards.
- Founded by Armand Hammer, chief executive of Occidental Petroleum, to house his collection; it opened on 28 November 1990 with a Kazimir Malevich exhibition, and Hammer died less than a month later.
- Named for Armand Hammer; the expanded building, the Lynda and Stewart Resnick Cultural Center, opened in 2023 and is named for the donors of a $30 million gift.
- Edward Larrabee Barnes designed the building on the site of a Westwood parking garage next to Occidental's headquarters; Michael Maltzan designed the Billy Wilder Theater (2006), the John V. Tunney Bridge (2015) and the expansion.
- The collection includes about 7,500 Daumier works.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11 am to 6 pm, closed Monday; admission is free with no reservation, parking is $9 for three hours with validation, with accessible parking on level P1 and a ramped drop-off entrance on Lindbrook Drive.

FUNDING
Hammer Museum: university-run with private donors: UCLA, a 2018 to 2025 capital campaign led by the Resnicks, and Occidental founding money
- The museum is funded, operated and managed by UCLA (Hyperallergic); the University of California Regents signed a 99-year operating agreement with the Armand Hammer Foundation in 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Occidental Petroleum paid to build the museum. In 1990 Newsweek reported the building had cost about $80 million, that shareholders protested the use of company funds, and that a proposed settlement capped Occidental's funding at $60 million.
- Free admission began in February 2014; two gifts from Erika J. Glazer and Brenda R. Potter paid for its first four years (stated by the space).
- Capital campaign launched February 2018 with a $180 million goal, about $90 million for the building and $90 million for the endowment and programmes; Lynda and Stewart Resnick gave $30 million and Marcy Carsey $20 million; the museum says it has raised "over $150 million" (stated by the space; Apollo for the Resnick gift).
- Named campaign donors include the Annenberg Foundation, The Ahmanson Foundation, the Anthony Pritzker Family Foundation and Jamie and Robert Soros (stated by the space).
- The 2024 Gala in the Garden cleared $2.5 million net (The Art Newspaper). The board is chaired by Eric Esrailian.
- No reporting found of current Occidental Petroleum funding.

Red flags: Occidental Petroleum built the museum
- Occidental paid for the building and some of the art, including the Leonardo manuscript then called the "Codex Hammer", bought for $5.28 million in 1980 (Newsweek, 1990).
- Shareholders objected and a settlement capped the company's funding at $60 million; a deputy state attorney general called the museum "a so-called charity whose purpose it is to memorialize Armand Hammer" (Newsweek, 1990).

Red flags: Gaza protest aimed at the museum and its board
- On 4 May 2024 UCLA faculty protested outside the museum's gala, calling for amnesty for students arrested at the pro-Palestinian encampment and for the removal of Chancellor Gene Block (The Art Newspaper).
- In June 2024 the group No Art Under Apartheid rallied outside the museum; its open letter, signed by over 65 people, asked the museum to call for a ceasefire, divest from companies tied to Israel's military and remove Block from its board (Hyperallergic).
- No museum response was reported; Block is not on the board list checked on 2026-10-20.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media: reports of the 2024 Gaza protests and one 2012 attack on Made in L.A.
1. Hyperallergic: Angella d'Avignon, "Artists Rally at Hammer Museum After Arrests at UCLA" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/926339/artists-rally-at-hammer-museum-after-arrests-at-ucla/
- Reports the call to boycott UCLA and the open letter's three demands to the museum; no response from the museum was included.
2. The Art Newspaper: Jori Finkel, "Hammer Museum gala draws artists, celebrities and a faculty protest" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/06/hammer-museum-gala-ann-philbin-faculty-protest
- Mostly admiring report of the gala, with the faculty protest outside. Director Ann Philbin answered by calling the museum a place for "dialogue around some of the most fractious and difficult subjects of our time".
3. Whitehot Magazine: Eve Wood, "Op-Ed: Unmade In LA" (2012)
URL: https://whitehotmagazine.com/articles/op-ed-unmade-in-la/2601
- The museum's public vote for a $100,000 prize in Made in L.A. was commercial and insulting to serious artists.
- The curators chose from personal networks, including recent CalArts graduates, and passed over artists outside them.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum now runs on UCLA support and individual and foundation donors, with no current Occidental money found. The weak points are its origin as an Occidental-funded monument to Armand Hammer, contested by shareholders in 1990, and the 2024 Gaza protests tying it to UCLA's leadership.
