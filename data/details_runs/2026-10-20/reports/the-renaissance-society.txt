THE RENAISSANCE SOCIETY: Chicago, USA
ID: the-renaissance-society
Website: https://renaissancesociety.org (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Free, independent, non-collecting contemporary art museum on the University of Chicago campus, in Cobb Hall, 4th floor, 5811 South Ellis Avenue (Wikipedia; museum site). It presents about four exhibitions a year, plus concerts, lectures and screenings.
- Founded in 1915, in the wake of the 1913 Armory Show; ten University of Chicago professors convened the first exploratory meeting on 20 April 1915. Its constitution provides that all acquisitions except money become the property of the University of Chicago (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Susanne Ghez led it from 1974 to 2013 (Wikipedia; the museum site gives 1973), Solveig Øvstebø from 2013, and Myriam Ben Salah has been executive director and chief curator since 2020.
- Building and site: about 3,000 square feet of exhibition space in a Neo-Gothic lecture hall, which it has occupied since 1979 (The Art Newspaper, 2016). The university cut off its financial support in 1974 after deficits, forgave the debt and let the society stay rent-free (Wikipedia).
- On site: the gallery, with a publications programme supported by the Mansueto Foundation (museum site).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Friday 12pm to 6pm and Saturday and Sunday 10am to 6pm; admission is free. The gallery is reached by elevator; the southeast corner door has a sidewalk-level platform lift; live captioning is offered at public discursive events; large-print gallery texts and portable seating are available on request.

FUNDING
The Renaissance Society: foundation-funded: Mellon, Teiger and Warhol foundations and Chicago family foundations, with the University of Chicago about 5 percent
- The museum's own About page says the Mellon Foundation provides major annual support, that programmes are supported by the Teiger Foundation and the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, and that general operations are supported by grants from the Illinois Arts Council and the University of Chicago Provost's Discretionary Fund.
- University of Chicago: its financial grants and in-kind contributions "constitute roughly 5% of our annual budget", with the rest raised each year (stated by the museum).
- Next Century Fund, a $5 million campaign launched for the 2015 centennial: lead support from the Edlis Neeson Foundation, the Pritzker Traubert Family Foundation and the Zell Family Foundation, which pledged $500,000 each, announced on 20 May 2016. The Art Newspaper (Victoria Stapley-Brown, 2016) reported the campaign at 65% of its goal then. Major supporters named on the About page: Jorge Cauz and Ugo Alfano Casati; Marilyn and Larry Fields; Conor O'Neil and the Chauncey and Marion Deering McCormick Family Foundation; Neil Ross and Lynn Hauser; Richard Wright and Valerie Carberry.
- In February 2019 the Mansueto Foundation gave $1 million to secure the society's publishing for 10 years (Wikipedia).
- Finances (ProPublica, Form 990 data for EIN 36-6109822): fiscal year to June 2025, revenue $2,130,939, expenses $1,893,499, contributions $1,877,959. Fiscal 2024: revenue $1,102,093, expenses $1,615,993.
- Governance: officers in the 2025 filing are Nancy Lerner (president), Raven Thomas Abdul-Aleem (vice president), Anne Van Wart (treasurer) and Jill Ingrassia-Zingales (secretary). The About page lists 25 board members.
- No reporting found of a flagged-industry funder behind the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is foundation and individual giving, with the university providing about 5 percent of its budget; no flagged-industry funder was found. The weak points are the reliance on a handful of large foundation grants, shown by contributions of $905,828 in fiscal 2024 and $1,877,959 in fiscal 2025, and that the full donor list in the annual report could not be read. Worth watching: whether Mellon support continues.
