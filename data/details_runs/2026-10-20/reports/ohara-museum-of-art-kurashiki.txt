OHARA MUSEUM OF ART: Kurashiki, Japan
ID: ohara-museum-of-art-kurashiki
Website: https://www.ohara.or.jp/official/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum in Kurashiki, Okayama prefecture; the museum describes itself as Japan's first privately run museum centred on Western art (museum website, in Japanese; translation mine).
- Founded in 1930 by the businessman Magosaburo Ohara in memory of the painter Kojima Torajiro, who had died the year before; the building was completed on 5 November 1930 and Takeuchi Kiyomi was its first director (museum website).
- The Kojima Torajiro Memorial Hall honours the painter and is covered by the same admission (museum website).
- Kojima travelled to Europe three times at Ohara's urging and chose many of the core works, including pieces by El Greco, Gauguin, Monet and Matisse (museum website). The museum's pages name no architect for the 1930 building.
- On site: the main gallery, a Craft and Oriental museum and the Kojima Torajiro Memorial Hall; the annex is closed for an extended period (museum website).
- Visiting: closed Mondays (open if Monday is a public holiday, no closures in August). March to November 9:00 to 17:00, December to February 9:00 to 15:00, last entry 30 minutes before closing. Adults 2,000 yen, under 18 500 yen, preschoolers free, Kurashiki residents and workers 1,900 yen. People holding disability certificates enter free, and one companion with some grades. An accessibility page exists on a separate site and was not read.

FUNDING
Ohara Museum of Art: private public-interest foundation: admission income, donations and local corporate partners
- Operator: the Ohara Art Foundation, formed on 1 April 2024 by merging the Ohara Museum of Art and the Kurashiki Archaeological Museum. The museum had become a public interest foundation in 2011 (foundation website, museum website). Representative director: Ohara Akane (foundation website).
- Admission: in October 2020 Bijutsu Techo reported that admission fees cover about 80% of operating costs (Bijutsu Techo, 26 October 2020). That year the museum closed for 136 days because of COVID-19 and ran a crowdfunding appeal with a target of 10 million yen.
- Corporate partners: 220 companies were sponsoring as of June 2026, mainly local firms (Bijutsu Techo, 26 June 2026). The museum's partner page (as of 1 April 2026) lists the partners in three unlabelled groups with no amounts or tiers. The first group includes Kurabo, Kuraray, Chugoku Bank, Sanyo Shimbun, Tomato Bank and Mitsubishi Motors. The second includes Okayama Gas, Yokoyama Sekiyu (Yokoyama Petroleum, my translation), Nippon Life and Kanto Denka Kogyo's Mizushima plant. The third includes ENEOS Mizushima refinery, Japan Tobacco Inc., JFE Steel, Asahi Kasei, Mitsubishi Chemical, Mitsubishi Gas Chemical, Nippon Zeon's Mizushima plant, Tokio Marine & Nichido, West Japan Railway and Nomura Securities (names transliterated by me from the Japanese list).
- Individuals: the Supporters Association takes annual members at 10,000 yen or 30,000 yen. It pays for conservation, equipment and facilities (museum website). No member count is given.
- Origin of the founding money: Magosaburo Ohara was the second president of Kurabo (1906 to 1939), founded Kurashiki Kenshoku (now Kuraray) in 1926 and Chugoku Bank in 1930, and founded Kurashiki Dento (now Chugoku Electric Power) in 1909 (Kurabo biography). Kurabo, Kuraray and Chugoku Bank are listed partners today.
- No reporting found of the foundation's total annual income, donation totals or the size of any single partner's payment. The foundation's site links financial statements as PDFs that were not read.

Red flags: fossil fuels
- ENEOS Mizushima refinery, Okayama Gas and Yokoyama Sekiyu are official partners as of 1 April 2026 (museum website). The page gives no amounts.

Red flags: tobacco
- Japan Tobacco Inc. (日本たばこ産業) is an official partner as of 1 April 2026 (museum website). The page gives no amount.
