PALAZZO GRASSI AND PUNTA DELLA DOGANA: Venice, Italy
ID: palazzo-grassi-and-punta-della-dogana
Website: https://www.pinaultcollection.com/palazzograssi/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Two exhibition venues for François Pinault's collection in Venice: Palazzo Grassi, and Punta della Dogana, a former customs house (Wikipedia; museum website).
- Palazzo Grassi was rebuilt between 1748 and 1772 to a design by Giorgio Massari. The Fiat Group bought it in 1983 under Gianni Agnelli, and Gae Aulenti oversaw a restoration. Pinault bought it in May 2005 for 29 million euros (Wikipedia).
- Tadao Ando remodelled Palazzo Grassi, which reopened in April 2006, and restored Punta della Dogana from January 2008 to March 2009; it opened in June 2009. Ando redesigned the Teatrino as a 225-seat auditorium in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- The City of Venice owns the Punta della Dogana building (Wikipedia).
- Current shows include "Lorna Simpson. Third Person" at Punta della Dogana (29 March to 22 November 2026) and "Michael Armitage. The Promise of Change" at Palazzo Grassi (29 March 2026 to 10 January 2027) (museum website).
- Visiting: during exhibition periods open daily except Tuesdays, 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:00, closed 25 December. An accessible guide and an Italian Sign Language presentation are offered; the page lists no prices.

FUNDING
Palazzo Grassi and Punta della Dogana: self-funded: François Pinault's private money
- The Pinault Collection is "a private company held by Pinault's Groupe Artémis"; the Venetian sites are held and managed by Palazzo Grassi S.p.A., in which the City of Venice holds a non-controlling stake and sits on the board (Wikipedia).
- Punta della Dogana: Pinault won the city's competition against the Guggenheim Foundation (Wikipedia). The lease length differs between sources. The Art Newspaper (Georgina Adam, 31 May 2007) reported a 30-year lease at no rent, about 25 million euros of restoration paid by Pinault, and Jean-Jacques Aillagon estimating about 2 million euros a year to run and about 85 million euros over 30 years; The Art Newspaper (Gareth Harris, 30 April 2007) also reported a 30-year lease. Wikipedia says Pinault "signed a 33-year agreement with the city" and gives a renovation cost of 20 million euros. These are projections and reports, not accounts.
- Exhibition sponsors: the Lorna Simpson show is "exclusively supported by Bottega Veneta" (museum website). Bottega Veneta is part of Kering, whose main shareholder is Groupe Artémis, holding 42.34% of shares and 59.34% of votes (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual accounts, ticket revenue, the board or other sponsors.

Red flags: Kering tax settlement (Bottega Veneta's parent)
- Settlement: on 9 May 2019 Kering announced a settlement with the Italian tax authorities of 1.25 billion euros in total, including 897 million euros in additional taxes plus penalties and interest (swissinfo, 9 May 2019). The article names Italian tax authorities and prosecutors, no agency or court.
- Allegation: the authorities accused Gucci of evading taxes on more than 1 billion euros in revenues from 2011 to 2017, booked through the Swiss subsidiary Luxury Goods International. Kering denied avoiding tax and acknowledged that prosecutors had claimed Gucci had a permanent establishment in Italy in that period (swissinfo).
- Bottega Veneta is the sole supporter of the 2026 Lorna Simpson show.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Critical coverage is old and concerns the city's deal and Pinault's programme, not the money behind them.
1. The Art Newspaper: Enrico Tantucci and Gareth Harris, "François Pinault poaches leading Paris fair director" (2010)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2010/06/01/francois-pinault-poaches-leading-paris-fair-director
- Pinault dropped the temporary exhibition programme at both venues, and the decision drew local objections.
- Franco Miracco, a supporter of the rival Guggenheim bid, pointed to Pinault's ownership of Christie's and said: "But these buildings are not auction houses and this is not in the city's interests." Vittorio Sgarbi criticised the city for favouring Pinault's collection over the Terruzzi collection. The outgoing mayor, Massimo Cacciari, called the recent criticism "insane" and praised Pinault's restorations.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Pinault trounces Guggenheim in battle to control customs house" (2007)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2007/05/01/pinault-trounces-guggenheim-in-battle-to-control-customs-house
- Reports (30 April 2007) that an independent committee could not choose between the bids, a second panel awarded the 30-year lease to Pinault, and the city council proposed a joint occupation that Pinault's artistic director refused.
- Not criticism by a critic: it records the dispute over the award.

BOTTOM LINE
The venues are paid for by Pinault's own company, with the City of Venice holding a minority stake in the operating company and owning the Punta della Dogana building. The weak points are the terms of the Punta della Dogana lease, reported as 30 years by The Art Newspaper and 33 years by Wikipedia, and Kering's 2019 tax settlement, as Bottega Veneta sponsors the current Lorna Simpson show. No annual accounts or board list were found.
