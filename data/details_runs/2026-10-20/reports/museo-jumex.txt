MUSEO JUMEX: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-jumex
Website: https://www.fundacionjumex.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum at Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra 303, Colonia Granada, Mexico City; the nearest Line 7 stations are San Joaquín and Polanco (museum site).
- Founded by collector Eugenio López Alonso. The Fundación Jumex Arte Contemporáneo was formally founded on 3 March 2001, when it presented the first Colección Jumex show at Galería Jumex in Ecatepec (museum site).
- The museum opened in November 2013 in a building by David Chipperfield Architects with about 1,600 m² of exhibition space, in Plaza Carso opposite Carlos Slim's Museo Soumaya (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds around 2,800 works by artists such as Damien Hirst, Andy Warhol and Gabriel Orozco (Wikipedia). Chief curator since 2020 is Kit Hammonds (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Friday and Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Saturday 10:00 to 19:00, closed Monday. Admission is free with no reservation; free guided tours in Spanish are offered, and English tours are paid and need 10 days' notice. All entrances and public areas are wheelchair accessible (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo Jumex: self-funded: profits of the Grupo Jumex juice company, through a foundation with no board
- The Jumex Foundation runs the museum, has no board and is supported by company profits (The Art Newspaper, 2020). Wikipedia states the museum has no board of directors and its curators report directly to López.
- Grupo Jumex is a private company headquartered in Ecatepec de Morelos, founded in 1961 as Empacadora de Frutas y Jugos; it has over 4,000 workers (Wikipedia). López Alonso is the formal heir and chairs the boards of Jumex and the Jumex Foundation (Bloomberg Línea, 2022).
- The foundation's "Patrocinios" page belongs to its Fomento programme, which supports other people and institutions working in contemporary art; it names no sponsors of the museum.
- No reporting found of foundation accounts, a funder list or reports beyond the company's support.

Red flags: Protest over the Hermann Nitsch exhibition
- Cancellation: on the eve of the Zona MACO fair the museum cancelled its Hermann Nitsch show, due to open on 27 February 2015 and his first in Mexico. The museum cited only an "administrative situation". An online petition against the show, raising animal-rights concerns, had over 5,000 signatures, and a counter-petition had over 1,500 (Hyperallergic, 9 February 2015). Hyperallergic reports that Jumex staff denied the petition caused the cancellation.
- Aftermath: critics accused the museum of censorship, and former director and curator Patrick Charpenel resigned (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- No reporting found of a state body ordering the cancellation; the pressure reported was the animal-rights petition.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the opening was largely admiring of the building. The sharper criticism concerns the founder's control of programming and the unexplained cancellations and departures.
1. The Art Newspaper: Natalie Schachar, "Critics demand explanation after quiet departures of Museo Jumex's curator and director" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/03/05/critics-demand-explanation-after-quiet-departures-of-museo-jumexs-curator-and-director
- Reports the exit of curator Julieta González in October 2019, adjunct director Rosario Nadal and three other women.
- Critics ask for transparency, a clear decision-making structure and a public explanation. MUAC chief curator Cuauhtémoc Medina is quoted: "The collector or his family have interfered, cancelled and imposed programmes."
- Museum spokeswoman Ruth Ovseyevitz said López "has always led the museum with vision" and that the museum had no comment on staff changes. The article links González's departure to a disagreement with López over showing a Naty Abascal haute couture exhibit beside a James Turrell survey.
2. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "Museo Jumex Abruptly Cancels Hermann Nitsch Exhibition" (2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/museo-jumex-abruptly-cancels-hermann-nitsch-exhibition
- Reports the cancellation, the petitions and the museum's reason of an "administrative situation"; Medina called it "a very sad day for art in Mexico".
- Reports Austrian Ambassador Eva Hager's statement backing the artist.
3. e-flux Criticism: Kevin McGarry, "Dispatch: Opening of Museo Jumex" (2013)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/235252/dispatch-opening-of-museo-jumex
- Admires the building and the Latin American works; argues the inaugural show uses the collection to support the status quo and is dominated by white European and American men.
- Notes the foundation's link to the juice industry but does not examine its funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a family company's profits, with no board and no outside accounts, so the museum answers to one owner. The weak points are that concentration of control, the 2015 Nitsch cancellation and the 2019 to 2020 staff departures, which the museum did not explain. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes governance or financial information.
