ARS AEVI MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina
ID: ars-aevi-sarajevo
Website: https://arsaevi.ba (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Collection and museum project for contemporary art in Sarajevo. The collection is exhibited in a building that used to belong to the Winter Olympics complex in Skenderija, central Sarajevo, where the architect Amir Vuk designed a wooden box meant to suggest temporary storage (Balkan Diskurs, 2015). A purpose-built museum is under construction in Quadrant C of Marijin Dvor (Italian development agency AICS; City of Sarajevo), next to the History Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina (open letter, 2026).
- Enver Hadžiomerspahić conceived the museum on the night of 27 April 1992, during the shelling of the Museum of the Olympic Games in Sarajevo (Balkan Diskurs).
- Named Ars Aevi, a partial anagram of "Sarajevo" meaning "art of the epoch" in Latin (AICS).
- Building: Renzo Piano designed the new museum, offering the first design to the City of Sarajevo, and donated materials for a pedestrian bridge over the Miljacka River (AICS).
- Collection: about 150 donated works, including pieces by Michelangelo Pistoletto, Joseph Kosuth, Joseph Beuys and Jannis Kounellis (AICS). Part of the collection of more than 150 works is kept in a storage room in City Hall, once the National Library (ANSA, 22 Sep 2025).

FUNDING
Ars Aevi: mainly public: EU, Italy, France, Canton of Sarajevo and City of Sarajevo
- Construction: the EU gives 4.1 million euros, with Italy and France co-financing under the "Team Europe" approach, and the City of Sarajevo and the Government of the Canton of Sarajevo as partners (EU delegation to Bosnia and Herzegovina, 23 Sep 2025).
- ANSA (22 Sep 2025) reports that the project is funded by the EU with contributions from the Government of Italy, the Canton of Sarajevo, the City of Sarajevo and other institutional partners, and that a contract between the EU Delegation and UNESCO was signed at Sarajevo City Hall by Ambassador Luigi Soreca and Siniša Šešum.
- The EU delegation says the EU allocated a total of 8.5 million euros for five cultural facilities in Sarajevo, Banja Luka and Mostar under EUzaKulturu.
- Italy: AICS funds the executive design based on Piano's concept; steps have been taken with UNESCO in Bosnia and Herzegovina to establish a multi-donor fund for public and private contributions (AICS, 21 Sep 2022).
- November 2021: the City of Sarajevo, the Canton and the canton's construction institute signed an agreement to build the museum (AICS). On 6 December 2018 the city council adopted the mayor's proposal to lift the construction ban on the land (City of Sarajevo).
- The collection was donated by artists such as Marina Abramovic, Nan Goldin, Joseph Beuys and Anish Kapoor, and is estimated at more than 20 million euros (Balkan Diskurs, 2015).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism comes from the founder and from a civil-society letter about the construction process.
1. Balkan Diskurs (not art press): Marta Vidal, "Ars Aevi: A Museum for Peace" (23 December 2015)
URL: https://balkandiskurs.com/en/2015/12/23/ars-aevi-a-museum-for-peace/
- Reports that construction of the planned building, designed by Renzo Piano, had been postponed several times.
- Quotes Hadžiomerspahić, the founder: "Nobody thinks of the public interest, that is why the project remains marginalized." This is criticism by the space's own founder of public engagement.
2. Open letter in support of the History Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina and its call for the immediate and sustainable protection of its building (24 July 2026), 589 signatures on the page, initial signatories including the Collective of Gallery Manifesto and the CRVENA Association for Culture and Art
URL: https://openletter.earth/open-letter-in-support-of-the-history-museum-of-bosnia-and-herzegovina-and-its-call-for-the-immediate-and-sustainable-protection-of-its-building-a21a3ce9
- Welcomes Ars Aevi as an important project for Sarajevo but states that "the realization of one museum must not come at the expense of another".
- Says construction of Ars Aevi began next to the History Museum without a communicated risk assessment of its effect on the protected monument, and that about a week after it began two facade panels fell from the History Museum's damaged cube into an atrium with a permanent exhibition.
- Asks UNESCO, the EU Delegation and the embassies of Italy, France and Spain, with the local authorities, to guarantee safe conditions for the History Museum during construction with continuous monitoring, and asks stakeholders to fund its restoration alongside Ars Aevi's construction.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the project is public and international: the EU, Italy, France, the Canton and the City of Sarajevo, with UNESCO as the EU's contract partner, and the collection donated by artists. None of these funders is flagged. The weak point is the July 2026 open letter, which objects to construction beginning beside the History Museum without a communicated risk assessment.
