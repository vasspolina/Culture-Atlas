THE MUSE MULTI STUDIOS: Khartoum, Sudan
ID: the-muse-multi-studios
Website: https://musesd.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Describes itself as an "integrative regional artistic organisation" founded in Khartoum; it lists Bait Alnisa, Muse Gallery and the Sudan Art Archive as its studios (Muse website).
- Founded in 2019 by the Sudanese artist and curator Reem Aljeally, who is founder and director. Muse Gallery, its first space, opened in Khartoum in September 2022 (Muse website). OkayAfrica (Elizabeth Okwach, 6 July 2022) describes it as "an enterprise that works towards building a platform for the local art community".
- Building and site: the gallery page gives no address or building description. It says the gallery is "temporarily closed due to the tragic war events" in Sudan since April 2023.
- What it does: exhibitions, residencies, publications, curation and consultation, and a print and online magazine (Muse website; Wikipedia says the first issue of The Muse Magazine came out in September 2023). OkayAfrica reported in 2022 that it had trained 90 artists in painting, drawing and illustration and worked with almost 40 artists on solo or group shows.
- Visiting: no hours or admission; the gallery is closed. Contact is by email and a phone number with Egypt's +20 code on the site.

FUNDING
The Muse Multi Studios: small-grant funded: Culture Resource's Abbara Program (2022) and an Australian-supported fellowship
- Culture Resource's Abbara Program: the Muse's site says it was awarded the Abbara Program by Culture Resource in its 7th round in 2022. A review hosted on the Muse's site (Mahasin Ismail) also names a fund from AFAC, the Arab Fund for Arts and Culture. No reporting found of the amounts.
- Qubit partnership: the Muse and Qubit Incubator began a collaboration in November 2025, "supported by the Australian Government and Creative Australia". A 12-month programme for eight artists from Australia and Sudan starts in April 2026, with an exhibition in Darwin, Australia in 2027. No amounts are given (Muse website).
- Partners named by OkayAfrica in 2022: Rift Digital Lab, the Spanish Embassy and Education without Borders. The article describes them as giving guidance and help with projects and sourcing artists; it does not mention funding.
- The Muse's website has a "Supported by" section that shows only a logo and names no funders.
- No reporting found of accounts, a standing funder list or a board. Only five staff are named on the site: Reem Aljeally, Marwan Mohamed, Ghassan Jaafar, Mawadda Tarig and Hassan AlNasser.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money visible behind the Muse is the Abbara grant from Culture Resource (2022), an AFAC fund and an Australian-supported programme (2025 to 2027), with no amounts found. The weak points are that no accounts, standing funder list or board were found, and the gallery in Khartoum has been closed since April 2023. Worth watching: how the Qubit programme is funded and whether the gallery reopens.
