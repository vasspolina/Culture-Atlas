SHACKLETON'S HUT, CAPE ROYDS (NIMROD HUT): Cape Royds, Ross Island, Antarctica
ID: shackletons-nimrod-hut-cape-royds
Website: https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/shackletons-hut/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Hut on an ice-free patch of rock beside Pony Lake at Cape Royds, Ross Island (Antarctic Heritage Trust), twenty-three miles north of Hut Point (Wikipedia).
- Base of Ernest Shackleton's British Antarctic (Nimrod) Expedition of 1907-09, the third British expedition to the Ross Sea in ten years. The Nimrod carried a shore party of 14 men, 15 Manchurian ponies, nine dogs and an Arrol-Johnston motorcar (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- From here a party of five made the first ascent of Mount Erebus in March 1908, and Shackleton's southern party turned back less than 97 nautical miles from the Pole (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- The timber hut was prefabricated in 1907 by Humphreys Limited of Knightsbridge, London, assembled there first and marked for re-erection (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Historic Site or Monument 15 under the Antarctic Treaty (Wikipedia). The Trust has conserved more than 6,000 artefacts from it (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Sources differ on when the whisky was found. The Trust says its conservators found five crates frozen under the hut in January 2010, three of Mackinlay's whisky and two of brandy, and thawed one whisky crate in a public gallery at Canterbury Museum, revealing eleven bottles (Antarctic Heritage Trust). Wikipedia, citing the BBC, dates the find of the crates to 2006.
- The then owner of Whyte & Mackay flew three bottles to Scotland on his private jet, under a New Zealand Government permit, for analysis; master blender Richard Paterson recreated the whisky in April 2011. Prime Minister John Key returned the three bottles to the Trust's staff in Antarctica in January 2013, and the original crates were returned to the base (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- 2025-26 season: conservators used a camera probe to see inside the walls and plan future work on the roof and walls (Antarctic Heritage Trust, March 2026).
- The Trust sells the hut's conservation plan by email request (Antarctic Heritage Trust).

FUNDING
Shackleton's Hut: charity-run, close to half from the New Zealand government, with whisky sales and a whisky brand among its backers
- Operator: the Antarctic Heritage Trust (New Zealand charity CC24071). Its income in the year to 30 June 2025 was NZ$3,433,987, NZ$1,536,000 of it government grants and contracts and NZ$928,581 donations (Charities Register annual return).
- The Trust names the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and Antarctica New Zealand as its government partners and the Ministry for Culture and Heritage as its core funder (Antarctic Heritage Trust, September 2026).
- Both editions of the replica whisky, Mackinlay's Rare Old Highland Malt and "The Journey" of late 2012, produced "a substantial donation" to the Trust's conservation work (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- The Shackleton Whisky brand is listed as a key supporter of the Trust (Antarctic Heritage Trust partners page).
- The World Monuments Fund put the hut on its World Monuments Watch in 2004, 2006 and 2008 at the Trust's nomination, and in 2004 American Express funded conservation through the Fund (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the hut is clean. The one commercial tie worth knowing is to the Scotch whisky trade, which has profited from the hut's story and pays toward its care. Worth watching: how the planned roof and wall work is funded.
