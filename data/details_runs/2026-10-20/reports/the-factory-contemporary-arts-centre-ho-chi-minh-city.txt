THE FACTORY CONTEMPORARY ARTS CENTRE: Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
ID: the-factory-contemporary-arts-centre-ho-chi-minh-city
Website: https://factoryartscentre.com (checked 2026-10-25; the page returned no content)

OVERVIEW
- Independent contemporary art centre at 15 Nguyen U Di, Thao Dien ward, District 2, Ho Chi Minh City (e-flux directory; Viet Nam News).
- Founded in 2016 by the artist Ti-A (Viet Nam News; Artribune). Zoe Butt is named artistic director in Art & Market (2018) and Artribune (2019); Artribune adds that she was previously executive director and curator at Sàn Art from 2009 to 2016.
- It describes itself as the first purpose-built independent space for contemporary art in Vietnam (e-flux directory).
- Building: HTAP Architects, with Hoanh Tran and Archie Pizzini as architects in charge, designed the 880 m² centre, completed in 2017 (ArchDaily). Viet Nam News describes a street front of coloured containers.
- On site: exhibition spaces, an arts library, workshops, a co-working space, a restaurant and a bar (Viet Nam News, September 2017). Art & Market names The Glass Box, a private exhibition programme for collectors, a Reading Room, and the Pollination residency programme.
- Visiting: the e-flux directory lists Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 7pm. Viet Nam News in 2017 gave entry as VND10,000 to VND100,000 depending on the activity. No accessibility information was found, and no current hours could be checked because the site returned no content.

FUNDING
The Factory Contemporary Arts Centre: self-funded: a social enterprise paying for programmes from art sales and on-site businesses
- The centre says all profit from the sale of art and from the businesses on the property supports the running costs of its art programmes (Art & Market, 2018; Viet Nam News, 2017). No financial figures are given in either piece.
- Artribune (2019) says the centre is not a commercial gallery, though it has sold works.
- No reporting found of any state, corporate or foundation funder behind the centre itself.
- No reporting found of accounts, an annual report or a named board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage is descriptive and positive, and the one piece that criticises anything criticises the setting in Vietnam, not the centre.
1. Artribune: Redazione (editorial staff), "Ho Chi Minh City. The Factory Contemporary Arts Centre" (27 March 2019)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/dal-mondo/2019/03/ho-chi-minh-city-factory-contemporary-arts-centre/
- A first-person account by curatorial assistant Le Thien Bao (Lê Thiên Bảo). The criticism is of Vietnam's system: official education centres on socialist realism, the Minister of Culture gives consent to exhibit works judged of artistic value, every exhibition needs a licence to exhibit, and a work judged unsuitable cannot be shown but can go in a catalogue (translated from the Italian).
- Criticises the context, not the institution. It reports no exhibition of the centre being refused or closed.
2. Art & Market: Ian Tee, "Social Enterprise Meets Art Infrastructure at The Factory" (2018)
URL: https://artandmarket.net/profile/2018/10/28/social-enterprise-meets-art-infrastructure-at-the-factory
- Describes the social-enterprise model and the programmes. Names the limited arts infrastructure and financial support for artistic production in Vietnam as wider challenges.
- Not criticism of the centre.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Artribune (2019), in the words of the centre's curatorial assistant: in Vietnam every exhibition needs a licence to exhibit, the Minister of Culture gives consent, and a work judged unsuitable cannot be shown. This describes the licensing system for all exhibitions in Vietnam, not an act against the centre.
- The centre works inside that system. No reporting found of a specific exhibition the centre was ordered to close or cancel.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre's stated money is the founder's enterprise: art sales and an on-site restaurant and bar, with no named donor, state body or corporate sponsor. The weak points are that no accounts, board or funder list are published and the website returned no content on the date checked. Worth watching: whether the centre publishes a funder list and whether it still operates.
