INDEX - THE SWEDISH CONTEMPORARY ART FOUNDATION: Stockholm, Sweden
ID: index-foundation-stockholm
Website: https://indexfoundation.se (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art foundation at Kungsbro Strand 19 on Kungsholmen, Stockholm; the exhibition hall is a converted laundry room of 140 square metres (Wikipedia).
- It grew out of the Fotograficentrum artists' association, founded in Stockholm in 1974, and became a foundation on 2 December 1998 (Index; Wikipedia).
- Earlier names: its gallery was Galleri Index under Fotograficentrum, and its magazine Bildtidningen was renamed "Index, Contemporary Scandinavian Images" in 1991 and merged with Siksi in the late 1990s to become NU, The Nordic Art Review (Index; Wikipedia).
- The premises moved to Kungsbrostrand in 2005 and opened on 7 June 2006 (Index).
- Programme: exhibitions, talks and events, learning, research and publications; since 2019 the programme has taken a gender perspective on the institution's history (Index).
- Directors: Mats Stjernstedt 2001 to 2011, Diana Baldon 2011 to 2014, Axel Wieder 2014 to 2018, Marti Manen 2018 to 2026; Övül Ö. Durmuşoğlu has been director since 2026 (Index).
- Visiting: Thursday and Friday 12:00 to 18:00, Saturday and Sunday 12:00 to 16:00, closed on several public holidays (Index). No reporting found of an admission price or accessibility statement on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Index Foundation: mainly public: Swedish Arts Council, City of Stockholm and Region Stockholm
- Index states it is supported by Kulturrådet (the Swedish Arts Council), Stockholms stad and Region Stockholm (Index).
- The 2025 director vacancy notice repeats the same three funders, says the board holds ultimate financial responsibility, and asks the director to raise funds from both private and public sources (e-flux, 20 November 2025).
- No reporting found of grant amounts or years, of a named private donor or of a corporate sponsor on the pages opened.
- The board is listed on the site: Malin Hüber (chair), Koshik Zaman (vice chair), Johanna Billing, Olga Krzeszowiec Malmsten, Hanna Ljungh, Masha Taavoniku and Didem Yıldırım (Index).
