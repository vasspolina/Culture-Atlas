PHOENIX ART MUSEUM: Phoenix, USA
ID: phoenix-art-museum-phoenix
Website: https://phxart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1625 North Central Avenue in the Central Corridor of Phoenix, Arizona, with a collection of more than 20,000 objects according to its own site.
- Opened on 18 November 1959; its predecessors were the Phoenix Fine Arts Association (formed 1925) and the Phoenix Art Center (created 1936 under the Works Progress Administration). Forest M. Hinkhouse was the first director (Wikipedia).
- Built on a 6.5-acre site donated by the heirs of Adolphus Clay Bartlett (Wikipedia).
- The board hired Alden Dow in the early 1950s to design the original complex; Tod Williams Billie Tsien Architects designed the 1996 and 2006 expansions, and the 40,000 sq ft Dorrance Sculpture Garden opened in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, a 300-seat theatre, the Lemon Art Research Library (free during public hours) and a sculpture garden.
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, closed Monday and Tuesday. Adults $23, seniors 65+ $20, students $18, youth 6 to 17 $13, children 5 and under and members free; pay-what-you-wish Wednesday 3 to 8 pm. All entrances are wheelchair accessible and free manual wheelchairs can be borrowed.

FUNDING
Phoenix Art Museum: mainly private and corporate: contributions, memberships, ticket sales and a corporate partner programme
- Contributions were $10.2 million of $14.1 million revenue in the year ended June 2025; expenses were $15.8 million, a loss of $1.8 million (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990).
- Corporate partners named on the museum's site include All State, APS, Avnet, Bank of America, BlueCross Blue Shield, CSAA Insurance Group, Heritage Auctions, JPMorgan Chase, National Bank of Arizona, Shamrock Foods Foundation, Snell & Wilmer, Southwest Gas and SRP. The English page shows logos only, with no tier, amount or year. The Spanish page lists PNC Bank at the Presenting level and APS, JPMorgan Chase and SRP at the Partner level; it gives no amount per company. Partnership levels run from $1,500 to $10,000 and above.
- Website sponsors: the Virginia G. Piper Charitable Trust and The Steele Foundation.
- Board: 46 trustees; Andrew Cooper, Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer of Pinnacle West Capital Corporation and Arizona Public Service Company (APS), is board chair (museum release, 26 May 2026).
- No reporting found of Desert Diamond Casino on the museum's English or Spanish corporate partner pages.
- No reporting found of City of Phoenix funding in the sources opened; ProPublica lists no government grants for the 2025 year.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, APS and Pinnacle West
- APS is a current corporate partner, listed at the Partner level on the museum's Spanish corporate page, and the chief financial officer of its parent Pinnacle West chairs the museum's board (museum pages above).
- APS burned coal at its Cholla plant in Arizona until March 2025, when the last two units stopped; it plans to convert them to burn natural gas, with construction from 2028 and the new units online the next year, pending state approval (POWER Magazine, 7 July 2026).
- In January 2020 APS said it would close the Four Corners coal plant in New Mexico in 2031 (E&E News). In August 2025 an APS spokesperson, Jill Hanks, said APS now expects to exit coal "no later than 2038" (Albuquerque Journal).
- No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at the museum over the APS partnership.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded mostly by private donors and local companies, and no source opened names a government or foundation scandal behind its money. The weak points are the board chair's role at APS, a current partner and a utility that burns coal at Four Corners and now expects to exit coal by 2038, and the casino partnership in the record: the museum's partner pages do not list Desert Diamond Casino. The deficit in the 2025 accounts is worth watching.
