ZEUGMA MOSAIC MUSEUM: Gaziantep, Turkey
ID: zeugma-mosaic-museum
Website: https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?SectionId=GZN01&DistId=GZN (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Mosaic museum on Hacı Sani Konukoğlu Boulevard in the Şehitkamil district of Gaziantep, built on the site of a former tobacco factory (Turkish Museums, Ministry of Culture and Tourism; Daily Sabah).
- It opened on 9 September 2011 (Daily Sabah; Wikipedia).
- Holds finds from Zeugma on the Euphrates, where rescue excavations began in 1998 and revealed Roman villas with mosaics (Daily Sabah).
- Site: 30,000 square metres, 25,000 of them indoors, in three blocks: Block A for Zeugma's mosaics, Block B for floor mosaics from Eastern Roman churches around Gaziantep, Block C for offices, conference rooms and foyer (Turkish Museums).
- Collection: about 3,000 square metres of mosaic, 140 square metres of frescoes, four Roman fountains, 20 columns, limestone statues, a bronze Mars, grave steles and sarcophagi (Turkish Museums). Its best-known piece is the "Gypsy Girl" mosaic from the dining room of the Mainad Villa (Turkish Museums).
- Twelve border pieces of the "Gypsy Girl", dug up illegally and smuggled out in the 1960s, were brought back by the ministry in 2018 from Bowling Green State University in the United States (Turkish Museums).
- After the earthquakes of 6 February 2023 the museum closed as a precaution for 74 days; Gaziantep museum director Özgür Çomak said it had no cracks and "we didn't lose a single one" of the mosaics (Hürriyet Daily News, April 2023).
- Visitors: about 3.54 million since opening, more than 385,000 in the first eight months of 2025, and a record 8,167 on 17 May 2025 (Daily Sabah, September 2025).
- Visiting: open every day 8:30am to 5pm, box office closes 4:30pm (Turkish Museums).

FUNDING
Zeugma Mosaic Museum: state: Turkey's Ministry of Culture and Tourism
- Building: the Ministry of Culture and Tourism built the museum (Daily Sabah, September 2025).
- Operator: the ministry, through the Gaziantep museum directorate; the ministry decided the 2023 reopening (Hürriyet Daily News, April 2023).
- Excavations behind the collection: the 2000 rescue campaign before the Birecik dam flooded the site ran under the Ministry of Culture with the Prime Ministry, the South Anatolian Project (GAP) administration, the Birecik dam operating company and the US-based Packard Humanities Institute, which undertook to finance the work up to 5 million US dollars; the Gaziantep provincial administration or the Birecik Dam Consortium also contributed to earlier studies (Ministry of Culture and Tourism).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: a Turkish state museum with no flagged funder, past or present.
