KUINDZHI ART MUSEUM: Mariupol, Ukraine
ID: kuindzhi-art-museum
Website: No site of the museum found (checked 2026-10-24; the atlas lists none, and searches returned only Wikipedia, Bellingcat, Hyperallergic, Moscow Times and Justice Info pages)

OVERVIEW
- Museum dedicated to the painter Arkhip Kuindzhi, born in Mariupol; Bellingcat places it a few blocks from the Drama Theatre, destroyed in an airstrike on 16 March 2022.
- Opened on 30 October 2010 as a branch of the Mariupol Museum of Local Lore; the idea was first proposed in 1914, the premises were donated in 1997 and renovation began in 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Collection: about 650 paintings, 960 graphic works, 150 sculptures and over 300 decorative objects, with works by Aivazovsky, Vereshchagin and Shishkin (Wikipedia).
- Damage: on 23 March 2022 Mariupol's city council said a Russian bomb had destroyed the museum; Bellingcat's satellite imagery of 24 April shows the building damaged, not razed, and Bellingcat could not identify the weapon or establish who was responsible. Wikipedia says the exhibition had been moved to the basement and lists the museum as dissolved in 2022.
- No reporting found of any visiting information since 2022.

FUNDING
Kuindzhi Art Museum: state-run: a municipal branch museum until 2022, then, according to a sign at its door, under the culture ministry of the Donetsk People's Republic
- Until 2022 it was a branch of the city's Museum of Local Lore (Wikipedia). No reporting found of its budget or donors.
- Under occupation, Bellingcat reports only the sign naming the DNR "Ministry of Culture"; no reporting found of a budget, accounts or board.

Red flags: sanctions on the occupying body, DNR
- The Donetsk People's Republic (DNR) was added to the US Treasury's SDN list on 16 July 2014 (OFAC). It is also listed by the EU under Council Regulation 269/2014 and by the UK, both from 25 July 2014 (Black Sea News sanctions database).
- The only source for the DNR running the museum is the handwritten sign at the door that Bellingcat saw in 2022, which placed it under the DNR "Ministry of Culture". No source opened shows that ministry on a list of its own, or gives the museum's budget.

Red flags: removal of the paintings to Donetsk
- Lina Garmash of the Donetsk History Museum said 25 paintings from Mariupol, including three Kuindzhis, are in storage there (The Moscow Times).
- Alexander Mozgovoi, a DNR television anchor, said the works were taken to Donetsk for safekeeping and "Nothing has been stolen" (The Moscow Times).
- On 27 April 2022 Petro Andriushchenko, an adviser to Mariupol's mayor, said on Telegram that Natalia Kapustnikova, director of the Museum of Local Lore, had handed the paintings to the occupiers; she told Izvestiya the paintings were safe (Bellingcat). In June 2023 the EU Council sanctioned her, saying she "gave those works willingly over to the Russian authorities" without the consent of Ukrainian authorities or the Kuindzhi museum's director (Justice Info).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Justice Info (Shahida Tulaganova, 2025) reports that over 2,000 artefacts were removed from the Kuindzhi museum to temporary storage in Donetsk, and that the EU sanctioned four museum directors in June 2023 over transfers of Ukrainian collections.
- The Kuindzhi museum is the museum whose works the EU Council's 2023 statement on Kapustnikova names, which connects it to these sanctions.

BOTTOM LINE
There is no private funder to assess: the museum was a city branch until 2022 and now stands, damaged, under a body the US, EU and UK sanction, on the evidence of one handwritten sign. The weak points are the removal of its Kuindzhi paintings to Donetsk and the lack of any published budget or governance. Worth watching: whether the paintings return to Ukraine.
