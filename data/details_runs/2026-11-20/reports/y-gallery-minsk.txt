Ў GALLERY OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Minsk, Belarus
ID: y-gallery-minsk
Website: https://ygallery.by (checked 2026-11-20; shows only a default nginx server page)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery in Minsk, last at vulica Kastryčnickaja 19A, with an exhibition space, the Ў wine bar, a design shop and the Literary House Logvinov (Wikipedia). It began in a one-storey building in a courtyard near Victory Square (Russian Wikipedia).
- Opened in October 2009, scaled up from the smaller gallery Podzemka run by Valiancina Kisialiova and Hanna Čystasierdava (CIMAM). Wikipedia lists Kisialiova, as Valentina Kiseleva, as director and curator and Ilona Dzyarkach as chairperson.
- Named for the Belarusian letter Ў, which sets the Belarusian alphabet apart from other East Slavic alphabets (Wikipedia).
- Programme: in 2010 it launched START, solo exhibitions and public discussions for young Belarusian artists. It held more than 120 exhibitions in its building, mostly by Belarusian artists, and 14 abroad, in Vilnius, Poznań, Lublin, Moscow and Stockholm, and was named "Best Foreign Gallery" at ArtVilnius in 2011 and 2016 (Wikipedia).
- 2020: programmes were cancelled from late March because of the pandemic, and for more than three months the space was a warehouse and headquarters distributing medical supplies for ByCovid-19. Its last exhibition, "Touchable Distance", opened in late October 2020 with 28 artists from 11 countries (MoMA post, Inga Lāce with Aleksei Borisionok and Anna Chistoserdova, 10 February 2021).
- Closure: Wikipedia dates the dissolution to 29 October 2020; CIMAM and the MoMA post say the gallery closed in November 2020. New owners later ran the space as Vieršy, "poems" (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Ў Gallery: privately owned: a co-owner's business money, with art sales, a wine bar and a design shop
- Owner: Wikipedia names Aliaksandr Vasilievič as co-owner and creator of the gallery. CIMAM calls him a "businessman and philanthropist" and one of its founders (Anna Karpenko, "Nothing is There", 30 November 2021).
- Where his money came from: he owned the Vondel/Hepta agency and co-founded the online outlets kyky.org and The Village Belarus (Wikipedia, Aliaksandr Vasilevich).
- Earned income: visitors could buy paintings and sculptures by Belarusian artists, and the complex had a wine bar and a design shop (Wikipedia). It opened with an exhibition hall, a bookshop, a café and an art library under one roof (Russian Wikipedia).
- Fragility: the MoMA post describes an independent space that the 2020 cancellations brought to the brink of collapse.
- Sale: in 2020 Vasilievič and his partners sold their stakes in the gallery and the bar but kept the Ў gallery and Ў bar brands (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Vasilievič was detained on 28 July 2020 at the KGB building in Minsk after trying to file a petition for bail for the presidential nominee Viktar Babaryka, and given 14 days of administrative detention. On 28 August 2020 officers of the Department for Financial Investigations detained him again after searching his apartment, office and companies (Wikipedia, citing Radio Svaboda and Viasna).
- Human rights organisations declared him a political prisoner on 7 September 2020. On 4 February 2022 a court sentenced him to three years in a corrective labour colony and released him in the courtroom because the prosecution period had expired; he left Belarus about five months later (Wikipedia, citing Dev.by and Zerkalo).
- The MoMA post, co-written by the gallery's Anna Chistoserdova, says the gallery office and "all of our homes" were searched in late August 2020. CIMAM says the art directors left Belarus for security reasons and planned to continue the gallery's work in Poland and Germany.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery was private and clean of state or flagged funders. Its weak point was dependence on one co-owner, and the gallery did not survive the state's prosecution of him. Watch whether the art directors revive the Ў name abroad.
