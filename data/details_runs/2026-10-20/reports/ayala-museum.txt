AYALA MUSEUM: Makati, Philippines
ID: ayala-museum
Website: https://www.ayalamuseum.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of Philippine history, art and culture at Makati Avenue corner De La Rosa Street, Greenbelt Park, Makati City.
- Painter Fernando Zóbel envisioned it in the 1950s as a museum of Philippine history and iconography. It opened in April 1967 under the Filipinas Foundation, now the Ayala Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Ayala group, the family business whose foundation runs it. Zóbel belonged to the Zóbel de Ayala family and worked for Ayala Corporation until 1961 (Wikipedia).
- Building: it stayed at the Insular Life Building until 1974, then moved to the old Makati Stock Exchange Building designed by Leandro Locsin. That building was demolished, which "met some criticism from heritage conservationists" (Wikipedia). The current six-storey granite, steel and glass building was designed by Leandro V. Locsin Partners, led by Leandro Y. Locsin Jr., and was dedicated on 28 September 2004 on the original site.
- On site: Gold of Ancestors (over 1,000 pre-colonial gold objects), The Diorama Experience (sixty dioramas of Philippine history to 1946), A Millennium of Contact (over 500 ceramics), a Digital Gallery and rotating exhibitions.
- It closed on 1 June 2019 for renovation and reopened on 4 December 2021. It had more than 65,000 visitors in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening hours, admission prices or accessibility on the pages opened; the site's visitor pages did not show them.

FUNDING
Ayala Museum: corporate-sponsored: run privately by the Ayala Foundation, with Ayala group co-presenters and partners
- Run privately by the Ayala Foundation (Wikipedia). The foundation's page names "Ayala group of companies" as partners; it gives no budget and no breakdown of sources.
- 2024 Zóbel show "Zóbel: The Future of the Past" (14 September 2024 to 26 January 2025): organised by Ayala Foundation and Ayala Museum, co-presented by Ayala Corporation, supported by the Embassy of Spain and the Embassy of the United States, with BPI, Cathay Pacific and Makati Shangri-La as partners (Daily Tribune).
- The foundation's page also names AIA Philippines, CANVAS and Avida as partners in news items, with no amounts or years.
- Ayala Corporation, the co-presenter, is a diversified group with real estate, banking, telecoms, power and other units (Wikipedia). Its full-year 2025 core net income was P48.3 billion, and the Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI) was its largest earnings contributor, ahead of Ayala Land and Globe; ACEN, its listed energy platform, contributed P6.3 billion of core net income (Daily Tribune, 13 March 2026). No page opened shows energy as the group's largest or majority revenue line.
- ACEN agreed in July 2022 to sell SLTEC, its last coal asset, a 244 MW plant in Calaca, Batangas, for P3.7 billion; the plant is to retire by 2040 (Business Mirror, 26 July 2022, Lenie Lectura).
- In November 2025 ACEN's subsidiary Enex Energy cancelled a 1,100 MW gas power project in Batangas. The Philippine Movement for Climate Justice and Batangas City communities had urged Ayala Corp. to withdraw from it (The Philippine Star, Brix Lelis, 17 November 2025). No reporting found that campaigners targeted the Ayala Museum itself.
- A 2024 Ayala Foundation integrated report exists on its site but was too large to open. No reporting found of amounts from Ayala Corporation or its other companies.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The only criticism located is the heritage conservationists' objection to demolishing the old building, reported in Wikipedia without an outlet or author.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a private arm of the Ayala group, so its money is the group's corporate and family money. No flagged funder was found: banking, not energy, is the group's largest earnings line. The weak points are that no budget, donor list or accounts could be read, and that the group's energy arm has been tied to a coal plant (sold in 2022) and a gas project (cancelled in 2025). Worth watching: the foundation's 2024 report for how much the group pays.
