BELARUS FREE THEATRE: London, United Kingdom
ID: belarus-free-theatre-london
Website: https://belarusfreetheatre.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Exile-run theatre company based in London, with an office at 66 The Cut, SE1 8LZ (stated by the company).
- Founded in March 2005 in Minsk by Nikolai Khalezin, a playwright and journalist, and Natalia Koliada, a theatre producer; the director Vladimir Shcherban joined in May 2005. Its first production, Sarah Kane's "4.48 Psychosis", premiered on 28 May 2005 in a Minsk club (Wikipedia).
- In Belarus the company had no registration or premises and performed in private apartments, cafés and forests (Wikipedia).
- On 22 August 2007 police stormed a performance of Edward Bond's "Eleven Vests" in Minsk and arrested actors, directors and audience members (Wikipedia).
- Khalezin and Koliada have lived in exile since 2011; the remaining members left Belarus in October 2020 to join them in London (Wikipedia).
- Registered in England as Belarus Free Theatre (company 07897118); Companies House lists nine current directors: Michael Attenborough, Mark Ball, Baroness Helena Kennedy, David Lan, Jude Law, Dame Maria Miller, Alison Stanley, Laura Wade and Samuel West. Khalezin, Kaliada and Shcherban are among the former directors.
- 2026: organiser of "Official. Unofficial. Belarus.", a collateral event of the 61st Venice Biennale at the Church of San Giovanni Evangelista, 9 May to 22 November 2026, curated by Daniella Kaliada with Natalia Kaliada MBE (e-flux).

FUNDING
Belarus Free Theatre: philanthropy and grants: private trusts, UK government and National Lottery money, and donations
- The company states: "We work with no regular support from any government and raise our funds wholly through philanthropy" (page at moc.media).
- 360Giving records seven grants from 14 May 2014 to 6 June 2023, totalling 502,228 pounds, by funder: Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, three grants totalling 272,500 pounds; Backstage Trust, 200,000 pounds; Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation UK Branch, 15,000 pounds; National Lottery Community Fund, 9,728 pounds; Esmée Fairbairn Foundation, 5,000 pounds.
- The Sigrid Rausing Trust lists a grant to support the four company members who sought asylum in the UK, including Kaliada, Khalezin and Shcherban, and records support in 2012 to 2014 and previous grants of 50,000 pounds in total.
- Early support: the 2008 educational project Studio Fortinbras received financial support from the British Embassy in Belarus, and a 2011 crowdfunding campaign on Sponsume helped fund the London office (Wikipedia).
- In March 2012 ArtsJournal quoted the company's founder warning that it faced closure in three months without significant funding and needed about 200,000 pounds a year to pay staff and cover costs.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the company is philanthropic: UK trusts and foundations, three Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport grants and a National Lottery grant, plus individual donors, with Sigrid Rausing Trust support for its exiled members. The documented grants total about 502,000 pounds between 2014 and 2023.
