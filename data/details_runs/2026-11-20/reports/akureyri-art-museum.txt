AKUREYRI ART MUSEUM (LISTASAFNIÐ Á AKUREYRI): Akureyri, Iceland
ID: akureyri-art-museum
Website: https://www.listak.is (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum at Kaupvangsstræti 8 on Listagil, Akureyri's Art Street, which also holds artist-run spaces, residencies, an art school and design studios (museum website; 2019 programme).
- Founded in 1993; 2026 is its 33rd exhibition year (2019 and 2026 programmes).
- The building first housed the KEA co-operative dairy, Mjólkursamlag KEA (Hönnunarmiðstöð, 2018).
- Kurt og Pí Architects led a large renovation that added a new exhibition hall, a new entrance, an enclosed walkway to Ketilhús, a new main staircase, a café and a shop (Hönnunarmiðstöð). The museum reopened with 12 galleries on 25 August 2018, during the town festival Akureyrarvaka, and more than 3,000 people came that day (2019 programme).
- The town began buying art around 1930 and owns about 700 works; the museum looks after them and hangs most of them in municipal buildings (2019 programme).
- The annual A! Performance Festival is run with Leikfélag Akureyrar, Menningarhúsið Hof, the Gil Society, the VMA art course and the Akureyri art society; the museum also runs an artist residency (2026 programme; museum website).
- Sigríður Örvarsdóttir is director (2026 programme); Hlynur Hallsson held the post in 2019 (2019 programme).
- Visiting: daily 12.00 to 17.00 from September to May and 10.00 to 17.00 from June to August, closed 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January; 2,400 krónur, 1,200 for over-67s and students, free for under-18s, disabled visitors and members of FÍSOS, SÍM, ICOM and the Gil Society; annual pass 5,700 krónur (museum website; 2026 programme).

FUNDING
Akureyri Art Museum: public: the town of Akureyri, with state money under a cultural agreement
- Owner and operator: the town of Akureyri runs the museum and draws up its annual budget and work plan (state agreement). The town council acts as its executive board, chaired in 2026 by Heimir Árnason, with a separate advisory board (2026 programme).
- State money: under the agreement for 2021 to 2023 between the Ministry of Education and Culture and Akureyri, signed on 12 May 2021, the state paid 230 million krónur a year, 690 million in all, for the town's professional theatre, the North Iceland Symphony Orchestra, the Hof cultural centre and the museum (state agreement).
- Matching: the town's own contributions to the agreement's projects must at least equal the state's each year, and the town reports its accounts to the ministry and the National Audit Office (state agreement).
- Grant: the state Visual Arts Fund gave the museum 700,000 krónur in its spring 2026 round to prepare the A! Performance Festival (Government of Iceland, 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public money from a town and the state. Worth watching is the size and renewal of the state cultural agreement that underpins its running costs.
