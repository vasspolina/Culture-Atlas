INVERLEITH HOUSE (ROYAL BOTANIC GARDEN): Edinburgh, UK
ID: inverleith-house-royal-botanic-garden
Website: https://www.rbge.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery in an 18th-century house in the middle of the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh, which is at 20A Inverleith Row, about one mile from the city centre. Entry to the garden is free (Wikipedia, Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh).
- The house was designed in 1773 by David Henderson and built in 1774 for James Rocheid. The city bought it in 1877 (Wikipedia, Inverleith House).
- From 1960 to 1984 it held the Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art, which then moved to Belford Road. Since 1986 the Royal Botanic Garden has used it for exhibitions of botanical and contemporary art (Wikipedia).
- The gallery is curated by the garden itself (Wikipedia, Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh).
- In 2020 it reopened with the exhibition "Florilegium: A gathering of flowers", the first of a biennial programme, and was set to be rebranded Climate House (Bella Caledonia, Neil Cooper, 15 October 2020).

FUNDING
Inverleith House: run by the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh, which receives Scottish Government grant in aid, plus an Outset Contemporary Art Fund grant
- Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh (RBGE): a registered charity and an executive non-departmental public body of the Scottish Government (Wikipedia). In 2024/25 it received £11,696,000 revenue grant in aid and a £17,000,000 capital grant, £28,696,000 in total from RESAS, the Scottish Government's Rural and Environment Science and Analytical Services Division (RBGE Annual Report and Accounts 2024/25).
- The gallery cost £160,000 a year to run, plus variable exhibition costs, and drew about 2% of the garden's estimated 900,000 visitors (The Art Newspaper, 2017). RBGE cited "inevitable financial risks attached to running a high-profile gallery" in announcing the closure (The Art Newspaper).
- Outset Contemporary Art Fund: RBGE and the Serpentine Galleries were jointly awarded a three-year Transformational Grant of £150,000 for their collaborative work on ecology, funding the General Ecology Network (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, "Inverleith House reinvents as climate crisis venue in partnership with Serpentine Galleries", 23 May 2020).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, 2016 closure
- RBGE's Board of Trustees decided on 5 October 2016 to end the year-round contemporary art programme, and RBGE made the public statement on 18 October 2016 (Bella Caledonia, Neil Cooper, 15 October 2020). The columnist Joyce McMillan started an online petition against the closure that drew more than 10,000 signatures; more than 200 artists and cultural figures signed an open letter, and several hundred protesters gathered outside on the final day of the 2016 exhibition, organised by the Scottish Contemporary Art Network (Bella Caledonia).
- Art-world figures including Douglas Gordon and Martin Boyce wrote to RBGE calling the house "a national treasure and an international beacon" (The Art Newspaper, 30 August 2017).
- The gallery reopened after a working group set up following the outcry recommended keeping Inverleith House as a core venue for key exhibitions; its first show under the new remit, Plant Scenery of the World, opened during the Edinburgh Art Festival (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The two pieces cover the 2016 closure decision and its reversal; Bella Caledonia criticises RBGE's management.
1. The Art Newspaper: Tim Cornwell, "Scotland's pioneering Inverleith House gallery saved from closure" (30 August 2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/08/30/scotlands-pioneering-inverleith-house-gallery-saved-from-closure
- Reports the petition of more than 10,000 signatures and the letter from art-world figures, and the working group's recommendation of an integrated programme of science, horticulture and the arts.
- Names RBGE's reason for closing: the financial risks of running a high-profile gallery.
2. Bella Caledonia: Neil Cooper, "A Change of Climate - How Inverleith House Was Saved From Permanent Lockdown" (15 October 2020)
URL: https://bellacaledonia.org.uk/?p=84012
- Argues RBGE tried to close one of Scotland's great public art spaces "without any notice or apparent consultation", and calls the attempt "cultural vandalism, plain and simple".
- Reports Regius Keeper Simon Milne saying the house could not "wash its face" financially, and the 2017 Arts Working Group report describing RBGE as in "a position of strength compared to many other arts organisations". Says a prolonged refusal by those in office at RBGE to be called to account for their conduct is not forgotten.

BOTTOM LINE
Inverleith House is paid for through the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh, which received £28,696,000 in Scottish Government grants in 2024/25, with a three-year £150,000 Outset grant shared with the Serpentine Galleries for the climate programme from 2020. The 2016 decision to close the gallery drew a petition of more than 10,000 signatures, and the gallery reopened after a working group reported in 2017.
