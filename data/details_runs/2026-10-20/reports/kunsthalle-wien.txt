KUNSTHALLE WIEN: Vienna, Austria
ID: kunsthalle-wien
Website: https://kunsthallewien.at (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- The City of Vienna's main institution for contemporary art, with two sites: the MuseumsQuartier at Museumsplatz 1 and Karlsplatz at Treitlstraße 2 (stated by the Kunsthalle). It has no collection and stages changing shows (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1992 as a temporary container-shaped structure at Karlsplatz by Adolf Krischanitz; in May 2001 it moved its headquarters into the MuseumsQuartier, in a building by Ortner & Ortner that extends the historic Winterreithalle, and the Karlsplatz container was replaced by a glass pavilion (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Gerald Matt (1996 to 2012), Nicolaus Schafhausen (2012 to 2019), the collective WHW (2019 to 2024) and Michelle Cotton since summer 2024 (Wikipedia).
- ORF Wien reported in 2011 that the Kunsthalle was to become a non-profit GmbH with an eight-member supervisory board, owned by the City of Vienna.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday to 20:00, closed Monday. Admission €14, reduced €11, free for under-19s, pay what you can on Thursday evenings from 17:00, and free on the last Sunday of each month, provided by the Dorotheum.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Wien: mainly public: the City of Vienna, with corporate sponsors and foreign cultural funds for single shows
- Core funding comes from the City of Vienna's culture department, MA 7 (stated by the Kunsthalle). No amount was found.
- 2026 programme support: Institut français, the Italian Direzione Generale Creatività Contemporanea and the Mondriaan Fund; Kunsttrans and smc Steirer Mika & Comp. for the Kunsthalle Wien Preis 2026.
- Sponsors named: Dorotheum, Winzerhof Familie Dockner, Ottakringer, Das Teppich Werk, Ton & Bild and Vöslauer.
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts or a board list on the pages opened.

Red flags: protest over the City of Vienna's rejection of all director applicants, including WHW (December 2022 to January 2023)
- December 2022: a six-person panel acting for Vienna's government rejected all 20 applicants for the director post, including WHW, the collective in charge since 2019 (Hyperallergic, 23 January 2023). ArtReview reported it on 23 December 2022, citing Der Standard.
- Boris Marte, CEO of the ERSTE Foundation and a Kunsthalle supervisory board member, resigned in protest, saying the deselection "violates my concept of cultural-political ethics" (Hyperallergic).
- 10 January 2023: the board of the L'Internationale confederation, signed by seven museum directors, sent a public letter to Vienna's executive city councillor for culture, Veronica Kaup-Hasler, asking her to reconsider or state the real reasons (L'Internationale; advocacy source).
- City response: Kaup-Hasler said the decision was "not a rejection of specific curatorial theming" and that the jury found none of the submitted concepts convincing enough for a further term (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The substantial criticism is aimed at the City of Vienna's 2022 decision on the directorship, not at the money. A 2011 dispute over the former director's expenses appeared in general press.
1. Hyperallergic: Avedis Hadjian, "Art Collective Ousted From Viennese Kunsthalle Speaks Out" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/794850/art-collective-ousted-from-viennese-museum-speaks-out/
- WHW said it never received an explanation and saw "some kind of repositioning by the city administration" of the Kunsthalle; it also said ticket sales and visitor numbers were under pressure and COVID-19 limits cut attendance.
- Reports Marte's resignation and the councillor's reply.
2. L'Internationale: Board of Directors, "Public letter on WHW termination as directors of Kunsthalle Wien" (2023)
URL: https://internationaleonline.org/contributions/public-letter-on-whw-termination-as-directors-of-kunsthalle-wien/
- Argues the termination reverses progress toward collective directorship and public accountability, that the pandemic should be weighed and that the city's explanation is inadequate.
- Asks whether the dismissal targeted WHW as a collective, women and outsiders. An advocacy letter by museum directors, not a critic's essay.
3. ArtReview: news item on WHW (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/curatorial-collective-what-how-for-whom-forced-out-of-kunsthalle-wien/
- Reports, citing Der Standard, that WHW was removed and Marte resigned; no statement from the city or WHW. News report, not a critique.
4. ORF Wien: "Kunsthalle sucht nach 'Leichen im Keller'" (2011)
URL: https://wien.orf.at/v2/news/stories/2513356/
- Reports allegations that then-director Gerald Matt used museum staff for private services and billed the costs to the Kunsthalle, plus high phone and travel bills. Matt was released from duty for three months from 1 January 2012.
- General news outlet. Falter reported in June 2013 that the justice system examined the allegations and discontinued all proceedings.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Kunsthalle Wien is the City of Vienna's culture budget, with named Austrian sponsors and foreign cultural funds for single shows, and no flagged industry. The weak points are the city's 2022 rejection of all director applicants and the protest that followed, and the lack of published accounts and amounts on the pages read. The expenses dispute over the previous director, 2011 to 2013, ended without proceedings.
