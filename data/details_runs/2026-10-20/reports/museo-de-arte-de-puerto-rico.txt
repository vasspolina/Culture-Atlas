MUSEO DE ARTE DE PUERTO RICO: San Juan, Puerto Rico
ID: museo-de-arte-de-puerto-rico
Website: https://mapr.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 299 Avenida de Diego in Santurce, San Juan, showing Puerto Rican art from the 18th century to the present (museum site).
- Established on 1 July 2000. The idea dates from 1995, when the Puerto Rico Tourism Company proposed it, and the Government Development Bank for Puerto Rico provided funding (Wikipedia).
- Housed in the former San Juan Municipal Hospital, designed by architect William H. Shimmelphening and completed in 1920. It was a hospital until 1966, then offices of the Department of Transportation and Public Works until 1975; planners built the new museum around the old structure (Wikipedia).
- Permanent collection includes José Campeche's "Virgen de la Soledad de la Victoria" (1782 to 1789) (Wikipedia). The museum also has a sculpture garden and the activARTE Gallery (Fundación Ángel Ramos) (museum site).
- Open Wednesday, Friday and Saturday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday 10:00 to 20:00 with free admission from 17:00 to 20:00, Sunday 11:00 to 17:00; closed Monday, Tuesday and holidays (museum site). No reporting found of ticket prices or accessibility details on the museum's page.

FUNDING
Museo de Arte de Puerto Rico: private non-profit: foundations, donors and a government tourism agency
- The museum's site credits current exhibitions to the Mellon Foundation and the Flamboyan Fund for the Arts ("Laberinto: Conciencia espiritual", via the Tiznando el País programme), and the Flamboyan Foundation, the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation and the Puerto Rico Tourism Company ("El lugar de la morada") (stated by the museum). No amounts or years are given.
- Public filings: the museum files as Museo De Arte De Puerto Rico Inc (EIN 66-0537284). Revenue was $7,308,690 in fiscal 2025 and $12,360,346 in fiscal 2024; contributions were 50.2 percent of revenue in 2025 and 76.2 percent in 2024. Net assets were $18,599,436 in 2025 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Gloria Benítez, a financial contributor, is secretary of the Junta de Síndicos (board of trustees), according to journalist Sandra Rodríguez Cotto. José Carrión III, also named as a donor, is a former president of the island's Fiscal Oversight Board (Amsterdam News).
- No reporting found of a full donor list or a corporate sponsor with an amount.

Red flags: Protest over the Cecilia Orta Allende gallery
- Naming: in May 2022 the museum mounted a wooden plaque with the name and likeness of artist-activist Cecilia Orta Allende in a first-floor gallery. By late August 2023 the plaque was gone (Amsterdam News).
- Donor family: the Amsterdam News reports that the gallery now bears the name of Valeria Antonia Carrión Benítez, only daughter of donors Gloria Benítez and José Carrión III. The museum says it kept Orta Allende's name on the space, now the Galería-Taller Cecilia Orta Allende, and that galleries are named for sponsors, not artists (Rodríguez Cotto). It says the space grew from 731 to 1,157.5 square feet.
- Protest: on 19 November 2023 Afro Puerto Rican activists picketed the museum over the plaque (Amsterdam News). Led by Edwin Velázquez Collazo and others, they called the decision "un acto discriminatorio, racista y elitista" (Rodríguez Cotto, my translation: a discriminatory, racist and elitist act).
- Museum response: executive director María Cristina Gaztambide said the plaque was moved to the Center for Educational Innovation, not eliminated (Amsterdam News).
- Estate: José Antonio Orta, representing the Orta Allende estate, said attempts to meet the board and director failed (Rodríguez Cotto).

Not related: Museo De Arte Contemporaneo De Puerto Rico Inc (EIN 66-0420190). It files separately from this museum in the ProPublica listing.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The dispute was covered by a New York Black newspaper and a Puerto Rican journalist's Substack.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Carrión was appointed to the federal board overseeing Puerto Rico's finances in 2016. University of Puerto Rico professor Emilio Pantojas raised a conflict of interest because Carrión is the brother-in-law of Resident Commissioner Pedro Pierluisi, and Senator Bernie Sanders said the board included "architects of the crisis" (Bond Buyer, 2016).
- Connection: Carrión and Gloria Benítez are named as museum donors, and Benítez is secretary of its board of trustees (Rodríguez Cotto, Amsterdam News).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's income is donations, foundation grants and a government tourism agency, with filed accounts but no published donor list. The weak point is the 2022 to 2023 plaque removal and gallery renaming tied to a trustee family, which drew a picket in November 2023 and gave two accounts (renamed, or name kept). Worth watching: whether the museum publishes who funds each named gallery.
