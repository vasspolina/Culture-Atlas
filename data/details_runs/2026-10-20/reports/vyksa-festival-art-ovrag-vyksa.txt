VYKSA FESTIVAL (ART-OVRAG): Vyksa, Russia
ID: vyksa-festival-art-ovrag-vyksa
Website: https://vyksafest.com/about (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Annual summer festival of street art, public art, theatre, music, performance and architecture in Vyksa, an industrial town with a metallurgical plant about 200 km from Nizhny Novgorod (festival website).
- Launched in 2011 as Art-Ovrag, named after the ravines of Vyksa's central park; renamed in 2020. Irina Sedykh created it and chairs the board of trustees of the OMK-Uchastie charity foundation, which runs it (festival website).
- Irina Sedykh is the wife of Anatoly Sedykh, owner of the plant's parent, the United Metallurgical Company (OMK) (Art Focus Now, The Moscow Times).
- Site: Vyksa is a single-industry town of about 50,000 people whose main employer is the Vyksa Metallurgical Plant, which makes railway wheels and electric-welded pipes (The Moscow Times).
- Venues: Shukhov Park, centred on the restored Shukhov tower, the central park and ravines, the Poseyka pine park, the Industrial Street-Art Park on the plant's walls, the Pavilion of the Future and the plant museum (festival website).
- On offer: about 100 street-art and public-art works, an artist residency since 2017 and educational programmes. The 2024 festival, 12 to 14 July, drew more than 35,000 people to over 30 events (Snob).
- Visiting: the festival is held each summer; its schedule is published on the festival site. No admission information was found.

FUNDING
Vyksa Festival: corporate-funded: the OMK-Uchastie foundation and the United Metallurgical Company (OMK), plus regional support
- The festival is organised by the OMK-Uchastie foundation with support from OMK and the Vyksa city administration (festival website).
- The 2024 edition also received support from the national project "Tourism and Hospitality Industry" and the Government of the Nizhny Novgorod Region. No budget is disclosed (Snob).
- Art Focus Now says the plant was "behind its creation"; no amounts or arrangement are given. The Moscow Times says the festival helped attract 100 million rubles in 2019 for rebuilding the city park.
- Partners named for 2024: DIDI gallery, Kaskad school, Persimfans ensemble, Vyksadrom, Smena creative centre and Ad Marginem publisher (Snob).
- No reporting found of a sponsor that dropped out under pressure.

Red flags: owner's and OMK's UK sanctions
- OMK: the UK Sanctions List (report date 16 October 2026) shows JSC United Metallurgical Company designated on 18 May 2023, with an asset freeze and trust services sanctions, on the ground that it obtains a benefit from or supports the Government of Russia by doing business in the energy and transport sectors. A Director Disqualification Sanction followed on 9 April 2025.
- Anatoly Sedykh, chairman of OMK's board of directors: designated by the UK on 22 February 2024 with an asset freeze, a travel ban and transport sanctions, on the ground that he directs, owns or controls OMK (UK Sanctions List).
- Oil and gas link: OMK makes steel pipes for pipelines, and lists Gazprom and Lukoil among its strategic partners (offshore-technology.com, undated). That is supply to the oil and gas industry, not production or sale of fuel.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is mostly favourable. Two pieces report local resistance to the murals; none criticises the funder.
1. Art Focus Now: Ekaterina Wagner, feature on the Art-Ovrag festival (2019)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/discoveries/the-art-ovrag-festival-bringing-art-to-the-woods/
- Residents resisted early on, complaining about unauthorised murals; some works were vandalised or painted over, and only one of three works by the street artist Pasha183 survives.
- Calls the first idea of painting philosophical murals on dilapidated housing "at the very least naive, if not downright offensive". Notes locals were more excited by a rap concert and a skate park than the art.
2. The Moscow Times (Russian edition): Anna Shilova, "Chugunnoe proshloe, iskusstvennoe nastoyashchee" (2020)
URL: https://ru.themoscowtimes.com/2020/10/21/chugunnoe-proshloe-iskusstvennoe-nastoyaschee-a101051
- Reports one work burned in the festival's second year and some residents saying facades should be insulated instead of covered with murals (translation); Irina Sedykh says facades do need insulating but without an "either-or" choice.
- Does not question OMK's ownership or the festival's funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival is paid for by a charity foundation and a steel group that the UK has sanctioned, as it has the group's board chairman. The money trail is clear at the level of OMK-Uchastie and OMK, but no budget or board is published on the pages read. Worth watching: OMK's UK sanctions and the regional government support.
