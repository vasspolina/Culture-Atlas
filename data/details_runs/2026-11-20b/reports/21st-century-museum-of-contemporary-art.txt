21ST CENTURY MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Kanazawa, Japan
ID: 21st-century-museum-of-contemporary-art
Website: https://www.kanazawa21.jp/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- City museum of contemporary art in central Kanazawa, built on the former site of the Kanazawa University attached junior high, primary school and kindergarten; it opened on 9 October 2004 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Building: a circular, glass-walled structure 112.5 metres across, two storeys above ground and two below, with four entrances, designed by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa of SANAA and built from April 2002 to August 2004 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Collection: mainly works made since 1980, earlier works since 1900 that explain them, and works tied to Kanazawa; more than 2,900 works by the designer Awazu Kiyoshi came by bequest in 2006 (museum website).
- Long-term installations include Leandro Erlich's "The Swimming Pool" (Japanese Wikipedia); there are also an art library, Theater 21, a museum shop and a cafe-restaurant (museum website; Annual Report; museum.or.jp).
- Director: Washida Meruro since April 2025, after Hasegawa Yuko (2021 to 2025) (museum website; Japanese Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions in 2025: "Layers of Accumulated Time" (29 April to 28 September 2025) drew 216,837 visitors; an Otobong Nkanga project and a show for the Noto Peninsula after the 2024 earthquake also ran (Annual Report 2025 to 2026).
- Closure: the city plans to close the museum from May 2027 to about March 2028 to replace air conditioning and electrical systems and repair ceilings damaged in the 2024 Noto earthquake (Japanese Wikipedia, citing NHK and Nikkei).
- Visiting: entering the building is free; exhibition tickets vary, for example 1,200 yen for adults in 2025. The exhibition zone opens 10:00 to 18:00, to 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays, and closes on Mondays and over the New Year (museum website; Annual Report).

FUNDING
21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art: mainly public: the city of Kanazawa, with corporate members and show sponsors
- Owner and operator: the city of Kanazawa owns the museum and the public interest foundation Kanazawa Art Promotion and Development Foundation runs it (Japanese Wikipedia; Annual Report).
- Sustain membership: annual dues per unit are 500,000 yen (Platinum), 300,000 yen (Gold) and 50,000 yen (Standard) for companies, and 200,000 or 50,000 yen for individuals; they count as tax-deductible donations to the foundation. The page does not say how many units each member holds or give a date for the list (museum website).
- Members: Platinum, My Book Service; Gold, Kanazawa Energy, Impress Art Business Division, Fukumitsuya and Dimple; Standard, about 100 companies, bodies and individuals, among them Hokkoku Bank, the Kanazawa Chamber of Commerce, Nikkiso, Yokogawa Electric's Kanazawa office, Takenaka Corporation's Hokuriku office, Iwatani Central Hokuriku and the SANAA office (museum website).
- Show backers in 2025: grants from the Japan Arts Fund; sponsorship from OMO5 Kanazawa Katamachi by Hoshino Resorts; support from the Hokkoku Shimbun; co-organisation by the Japan Arts Council and the Agency for Cultural Affairs; support from Hermès Japan and ESTEC Holdings for the Nkanga project; a grant from the Hong Kong Arts Development Council (Annual Report 2025 to 2026).

Red flags: gas utility and LP gas retailer among sustaining members
- Kanazawa Energy is a Gold member at 300,000 yen a year per unit (museum website). It supplies city gas, runs five hydropower plants taken over from the city and sells electricity; it was founded on 13 May 2021 and began gas and hydropower operations on 1 April 2022 (company website).
- Its shareholders are Hokuriku Electric Power 48.0 percent, Toho Gas 43.0 percent, the city of Kanazawa 3.0 percent, Hokkoku Bank and the Hokkoku Shimbun 2.0 percent each, and Matsumura Bussan and Komatsu Gas 1.0 percent each (company profile, April 2025).
- Iwatani Central Hokuriku, a Standard member, sells LP gas in Ishikawa, Toyama and Fukui prefectures (Be Connect company directory; enepi.jp, citing the 2022 LP gas yearbook).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism; the one critical piece dates from the opening year and concerns the building and the museum's idea of itself.
1. Icon: Kieran Long, "21st Century Museum" (issue 020, February 2005)
URL: https://www.iconeye.com/back-issues/21st-century-museum-icon-020-february-2005
- "The biggest criticism" is that Sejima and Nishizawa "ask us to believe in the building's architectural conceits, rather than making us feel their effects"; with its monochrome colour scheme and long corridors it is like a "highly-appointed hospital".
- Calls the museum's description of itself, appealing to "an undefined and vague common consciousness", "banal".
- Doubts that a collection shaped by consensus will be of "enduring quality" and calls the museum "a curiosity shop"; the same piece calls the architecture "quietly radical".

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly clean city money. The weak point is a city-gas utility owned by Hokuriku Electric and Toho Gas among the top-tier members, with an LP gas retailer further down; the amounts are not published.
