MUSEO DE ARTE DE LIMA (MALI): Lima, Peru
ID: museo-de-arte-de-lima
Website: https://mali.pe (checked 2026-10-24; loads). The education site https://www.arteporexcelencias.com returned a 503 error on 2026-10-24.

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in the Palacio de la Exposición, Parque de la Exposición, Av. 9 de Diciembre 125, at the entrance to Lima's historic centre (museum site, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1954 by Peruvian intellectuals and businessmen, led by the Prado family; the municipality gave a subsidy and the building. In 1960 the Prado family donated nearly 6,000 works (Matthew Bird, ReVista). Wikipedia gives 1961 as the inauguration.
- The palace was built for the 1872 international exposition: Antonio Leonardi designed the Italianate façade and Gustave Eiffel's atelier designed the cast-iron structure (Wikipedia).
- Collections span almost 3,000 years, from pre-Columbian to contemporary art; the museum also holds the Manuel Solari Swayne Library and the Peruvian Art Archive (Wikipedia). It runs art courses, which supplied about half its income in 2023 (Gestión).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, Saturdays to 17:00 (museum site). No reporting found of current admission prices or accessibility information on the museum's pages.

FUNDING
Museo de Arte de Lima: private non-profit: courses, patron members, events and corporate sponsors
- Income in 2023 was about 50 percent from courses, workshops and extension programmes, about 25 percent from the Patronato de las Artes, and about 25 percent from events, the gala dinner, auctions and box office (Gestión, August 2023). About 23,000 enrolments were recorded between July 2022 and July 2023.
- The municipality gave a subsidy and the building at the founding; early support came mainly from businesses, with later money from art classes, the Amigos del Museo membership and an annual art auction (ReVista, 2014).
- Camisea: in August 2022 the museum and the Consorcio Camisea, operated by Pluspetrol, agreed that entry would be free every Thursday under "Jueves de Camisea", from 25 August 2022 until March 2023 (Proactivo, 24 August 2022). In August 2024 the programme was announced to continue until March 2025, and more than 5,200 people had taken part (Diario Correo, 31 August 2024). Minería & Energía (21 August 2024) says the consortium funds the initiative with the museum; general manager Gustavo Zapata tied it to Camisea's 20th anniversary (Diario Correo, Proactivo). On 22 August 2024 Camisea also brought Yine and Matsigenka artisans to the museum for a sale and demonstrations (Proactivo). No reporting found of an amount or of the programme after March 2025.
- The museum posts financial statements for 2003 to 2025 with independent auditors' reports, and names Alberto Rebaza T. as board president (stated by the museum). Its donations page names no donors.
- No reporting found of a named list of Patronato members or corporate donors on the museum's own pages.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Consorcio Camisea, operated by Pluspetrol, funds the museum's free Thursdays, announced for 25 August 2022 to March 2023 and extended until March 2025 (Proactivo, Minería & Energía, Diario Correo). No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at this agreement.
- Wikipedia lists the consortium's partners under the December 2000 contracts as Pluspetrol (27.2 percent), Hunt Oil (25.2 percent), SK Corporation (17.6 percent), Repsol, Tecpetrol and Sonatrach (10 percent each).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum found in art media. The one critical account found is a university magazine essay on its governance.
1. ReVista (Harvard): Matthew Bird, "Institutionalizing the Lima Museum of Art" (2014)
URL: https://revista.drclas.harvard.edu/institutionalizing-the-lima-museum-of-art-revista/page/2
- Describes financial crises that left staff unpaid and utilities in arrears, and seven managing directors in a single year.
- Quotes chief curator Natalia Majluf saying in 2002 that the museum was losing its original vision, and later calling it "crippled" because the permanent collection stayed in storage for years amid public-sector red tape.
- Largely favourable to the museum; a university magazine, not art press. Does not mention Camisea.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia reports five Camisea pipeline spills as of March 2006, the latest on 4 March 2006 with three injuries.
- Wikipedia cites a UN call for "immediate suspension" of expansion plans over risks to uncontacted peoples in the Nahua-Nanti Reserve, and notes protests organised by Survival International in April 2013. It also records a January 2016 finding by a US Ex-Im Bank panel that the project met high environmental and social standards.
- Connection: Camisea funds the museum's free Thursdays; no reporting found of criticism aimed at that deal.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is mostly its own: courses, patron members and events. The weak point is the Camisea deal, which put a gas consortium's name on free admission until March 2025 with no published amount. Worth watching: whether Camisea or another extractive sponsor returns, and what the 2025 accounts show about donations.
