REGGIA DI CASERTA: Caserta, Italy
ID: reggia-di-caserta
Website: https://reggiadicaserta.cultura.gov.it/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Royal palace complex at Piazza Carlo di Borbone, Caserta: the Palazzo Reale, the Parco Reale, the Giardino Inglese, the Bosco di San Silvestro and the Acquedotto Carolino (museum website).
- Carlo di Borbone bought the Caserta fief in 1750 for 489,348.13 ducats; the architect was Luigi Vanvitelli and the king laid the first stone on 20 January 1752 (museum website).
- The Reggia, the Acquedotto Carolino and the San Leucio complex have been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1997 (museum website).
- What is there: royal apartments, the Teatro di Corte, the Cappella Palatina, the Gran Galleria and 123 hectares of park (museum website).
- Archive: set up by Ferdinando IV in 1784, it holds more than 10,000 units from the 15th to the mid 20th century (museum website).
- Status: an autonomous state museum of national interest under prime ministerial decree 171 of 2014 (museum website).
- Direction: Tiziana Maffei led the Reggia for seven years; Antonio Tarasco, the Culture Ministry's director general for archives, took over as interim director in June 2026 (Finestre sull'Arte, 30 June 2026).
- Visitors: 996,345 entries in 2024, 3.11 percent fewer than in 2023 (2024 accounts); over a million in 2025 (Cronache della Campania, January 2026).
- Visiting: closed on Tuesdays, 1 January and 25 December; in November the palace opens 8:30 to 19:15, last entry 18:15. A ticket for palace and park costs 18 euros, the park and English garden 9 euros; entry is free for disabled visitors and a companion and for EU school groups (museum website).

FUNDING
Reggia di Caserta: public: a Culture Ministry museum, mostly self-financed from tickets and concessions
- 2024 current income: 8.49 million euros accrued, of which 5.89 million from sales of goods and services, 0.83 million from property income and 1.62 million in current transfers from the state, against 2.40 million in 2023 (2024 accounts, revenue statement).
- Tickets: gross takings of 5,362,930 euros in 2024, against 5,935,788.64 euros in 2023, with prices raised from 1 March 2024 (2024 accounts, report).
- Concessions: 818,967.49 euros in 2024 from fees and royalties of service concessionaires and the hire of spaces for events (2024 accounts, report).
- Capital: 2.52 million euros of state investment transfers and 77,600 euros from the region accrued in 2024 (2024 accounts). The Reggia holds 25 million euros of PNRR money for the Fizzo springs and Acquedotto Carolino, the royal gardens and the Bosco di San Silvestro (2024 accounts, report).
- Restoration: about 60 million euros were allocated for works after the 2012 collapses, due to finish in 2020 (Artribune, August 2018).
- Sponsors in kind in 2024, 63,418 euros in all: Moroso (furniture for the "Visioni" show, 31,817 euros plus VAT), Angelino, Daman Ristorazioni with the buffalo mozzarella consortium, the Education Ministry and Royal Garden (2024 accounts, report).
- In November 2026 the Reggia sought sponsors for lighting the palace and upkeep of Piazza Carlo di Borbone (museum website).
- Friends: the Associazione Amici della Reggia di Caserta, founded in 2014; membership includes a free annual pass (museum website).

Red flags: 2025 protest over felling 750 holm oaks in the park
- Local committees and environmental groups called the replacement of the 750 holm oaks lining Vanvitelli's central avenue too fast and drastic and named it "progetto Attila"; 645 trees are to be replaced and 105 moved (Finestre sull'Arte, 8 November 2025).
- Director Tiziana Maffei said the trees were beyond repair or diseased; the work is paid for by PNRR money for historic parks and gardens, whose deadlines press the timetable (Finestre sull'Arte).

Red flags: 2026 dispute over a party official's seat on the board
- The new five-year board named in February 2026 joined director Maffei with Paolo Santonastaso, Fratelli d'Italia's Caserta city coordinator since March 2025, and Nicolina Virgilio, both designated by Culture Minister Alessandro Giuli (Il Fatto Quotidiano, 6 February 2026).
- Democratic deputies Stefano Graziano and Piero De Luca filed a parliamentary question, and other opposition parties announced more; Santonastaso said the post is unpaid (Il Fatto Quotidiano).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In March 2016 the unions UILPA, UGL-Intesa, USB and the RSU wrote to the ministry about how the new director Mauro Felicori was organising offices and visitor services; Finestre sull'Arte called the press story that he "worked too much" a fake case built on the letter (Finestre sull'Arte, 6 March 2016; paywalled, only excerpts were read).
- Felicori left on 31 October 2018 on reaching the retirement age, a year before his term ended; Artribune listed the Cavallerizze given to the buffalo mozzarella consortium and a wedding for 250 guests rented for 30,000 euros among the rows of his tenure, and said a 2017 appeal against his appointment was rejected by the regional administrative court (Artribune, August 2018).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean state money, with tickets as the main income and small in-kind sponsors. The weak points are the oak felling under PNRR deadlines and the political appointment to the board.
