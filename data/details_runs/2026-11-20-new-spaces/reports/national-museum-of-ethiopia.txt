NATIONAL MUSEUM OF ETHIOPIA: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia
ID: national-museum-of-ethiopia
Website: none on record (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Ethiopia's national museum in Addis Ababa, near the graduate school of Addis Ababa University.
- It grew from the Institute of Archaeology, founded in 1958, and first showed finds from French-led excavations in northern Ethiopia (Wikipedia).
- The artists Afewerk Tekle and Mamo Tessema helped found it, and Tessema served as curator.
- The concrete building was completed in 1976 (Capital).
- The basement shows early hominid fossils, among them Lucy, an Australopithecus afarensis about 3.2 million years old found in the Afar region, and Selam.
- Upper floors hold ancient and medieval objects, imperial regalia, Ethiopian art and an ethnographic display.

FUNDING
National Museum of Ethiopia: unconfirmed: government money is on record only for a replacement museum
- The Ministry of Finance allocated 50 million birr for feasibility studies and designs of a new National Museum and a Human Origins Museum in Addis Ababa (The Reporter, 2024).
- Abebaw Ayalew, director-general of the Ethiopian Heritage Authority, presented the plan and said the authority would build both museums.
- The gallery of historical archaeology was renovated under a Franco-Ethiopian agreement on heritage and inaugurated on 29 September 2022 by the Ethiopian Heritage Authority, the museum, the French Embassy and the French Center for Ethiopian Studies (Capital).
- The Ethiopian Heritage Authority said in 2025 that the renovated museum would open to the public during its heritage research conference (ENA).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

BOTTOM LINE
The record points to the Ethiopian state and a French heritage partnership, but who runs and pays for the present museum is unconfirmed, so the atlas leaves its grade open. Worth watching: the budget for the two new museums and who is asked to pay for them.
