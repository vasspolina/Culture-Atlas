NATIONAL PALACE MUSEUM: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: national-palace-museum
Website: https://www.npm.gov.tw (checked 2026-11-20; answers automated reads with a challenge page)

OVERVIEW
- National museum in Shilin, Taipei, with a Southern Branch in Taibao, Chiayi County (Wikipedia).
- Founded in Beijing in 1925; the first crates of objects reached Taiwan in 1949 and the Taipei branch opened to the public in 1965 (Executive Yuan). The main building was designed by Huang Baoyu (Wikipedia).
- The collection numbered 699,119 objects in December 2025 (Wikipedia).
- The Southern Branch held its soft opening on 28 December 2015 (Focus Taiwan); its main hall closed in mid-April 2016 to repair leaks in the glass wall and reopened on 23 August 2016 (Focus Taiwan, 21 August 2016).
- Director Hsiao Tsung-huang said in December 2025 that the museum had met its visitor targets for the year: 2.4 million at the Northern Branch and 1.1 million at the Southern Branch (TVBS, 26 December 2025). The Northern Branch had 3.8 million in 2019 (Taipei Times).

FUNDING
National Palace Museum: public: a state agency under the Executive Yuan, paid from the central budget
- One of 31 agencies placed directly under the Executive Yuan, the highest administrative organ, by the Organizational Act as amended on 24 January 2025 (Office of the President).
- Total budget for 2025: NT$3.2 billion (Taipei Times, 15 May 2025).
- Capital works run through the New National Palace Museum Plan (新故宮計畫): NT$10.747 billion for expanding the Northern Branch, a conservation and display hall for national treasures, and a tourism network linking museums, Premier Cho Jung-tai said (Executive Yuan, 27 December 2024).
- Ticket and licensing income: visits and ticket sales to August 2026 fell below the same period of 2025, and forecast royalty income for 2027 is still below pre-pandemic levels (Legislative Yuan Budget Center, October 2026).
- A separate fund, the Cultural Relics and Art Development Fund (故宮文物藝術發展基金, name translated by the atlas), has its own budget, reviewed each year by the same Budget Center (Legislative Yuan).

Red flags: 2023 Control Yuan censure over damaged objects
- In a corrective measure of 16 November 2023 the Control Yuan found cluttered storerooms with poor protection: on 19 May 2022 a worker turned over a box with objects still inside and a Qing Qianlong blue-and-white plate with flowers (清 乾隆 青花花卉盤) fell and broke. Two bowls, a Ming Hongzhi-mark yellow bowl with green twin dragons (明 弘治款 嬌黃綠彩雙龍小碗) and a Qing Kangxi-mark small yellow bowl with hidden dragons (清 康熙款 暗龍白裏小黃瓷碗), were found broken in 2021 and 2022 with no record of who had handled them (Control Yuan; names translated by the atlas).
- In 2012 a section chief had the silk mounting of the painting "Yellow River Lanzhou Pontoon Bridge" (黃河蘭州浮橋圖, title translated by the atlas) cut to fit a case; the museum found out in April 2013 and had still not held anyone to account (Control Yuan).

Red flags: 2022 leak of museum images, censured in 2023
- More than 30,000 high-resolution images leaked from a storage device open to public queries; the museum reported the breach more than nine months after a member of the public raised it, which the Control Yuan found breached the Cyber Security Management Act.

Red flags: Budget freeze and delays in the New National Palace Museum Plan, 2024 to 2026
- In December 2024 legislators froze the NT$350 million budget for the New National Palace Museum Plan until the museum reported on it (Taipei Times, 26 December 2024).
- By May 2025 the frozen total was NT$646.81 million, over 20 percent of the budget, against a 2.5 percent freeze three years earlier; the Education and Culture Committee then released it. KMT legislator Ko Chih-en still doubted a planned glass pavilion (Taipei Times, 15 May 2025).
- A guest column in Storm Media by 邱子安 (13 May 2025) said the museum's report for the release answered none of the 21 legislators' motions folded into the freeze (Storm Media, opinion).
- In October 2026 the Budget Center found that the Northern Branch expansion had not cleared heritage review, that the treasures hall was behind schedule and in its fourth revision, and that the tourism network should be re-evaluated (Legislative Yuan).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage treats the museum as a political object in the China and Taiwan dispute rather than criticising its programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Louise Benson, "China is waging a restitution campaign against Taiwan's Forbidden City treasures" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/06/05/china-is-waging-a-restitution-campaign-against-taiwans-forbidden-city-treasures
- China wants the collection back; the museum refuses to return objects or lend them to countries without seizure protection.
- The Chinese government used the breakages in a state newspaper to argue only reunification could protect the treasures; leaked images were sold on Taobao.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Global Taiwan Institute (2023) wrote that under the KMT the museum served to assert the party's claim to be the sole legitimate Chinese government and to teach Taiwanese their Chinese heritage.
- Connection: this was the museum's own founding role.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money with no private sponsor: clean. The weak points are how the museum looks after its collection and how it runs its capital plan.
