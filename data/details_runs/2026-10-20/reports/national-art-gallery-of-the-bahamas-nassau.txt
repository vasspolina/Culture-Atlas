NATIONAL ART GALLERY OF THE BAHAMAS: Nassau, Bahamas
ID: national-art-gallery-of-the-bahamas-nassau
Website: https://nagb.org.bs

OVERVIEW
- National art museum for Bahamian art at West and West Hill Streets in downtown Nassau, in the colonial-era Villa Doyle.
- Announced in 1996 by Prime Minister Hubert Ingraham as part of a national museum network; it opened on 7 July 2003 with three exhibitions, including the Inaugural National Exhibition curated by Erica James (the gallery's history page). It was established by an Act of Parliament as a non-profit, quasi-governmental private corporation run by a board (the gallery's governance page).
- Historian Gail Saunders was founding chair and led the committee through the building's restoration. Director since January 2025: Maelynn Ford (Wikipedia).
- Villa Doyle was built in the 1860s by Sir William Doyle, a Chief Justice; Sir Walter Moore bought it in 1924 and added a two-storey southern extension with a ballroom. The government bought it in 1995 from Keith Aranha, who had owned it since 1978, and the restoration took nearly seven years, overseen by architect Anthony Jervis and civil engineer George Cox (the gallery's history page).
- Programmes include a biennial National Exhibition, the Inter-Island Travelling Exhibition, workshops and family programmes (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Saturday and Sunday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed Monday and public holidays. Admission $10, residents $7, seniors and students $5, children under 12 free; Bahamians and residents enter free every Sunday. The gallery's page notes its elevator was undergoing maintenance, with restrooms on the first floor.

FUNDING
National Art Gallery of The Bahamas: a government subvention plus public-private partnerships, donations, memberships and admissions
- The gallery says it is supported by a government subvention and public-private partnerships, along with admissions, memberships and donations (history page); it receives "a government stipend" (financial reports page).
- The Royal Gazette (Charles Zuill, "Reaching out to the community through art", 9 July 2009) reported that the government underwrote the purchase and restoration of the building and provided a "substantial annual budget for the ongoing running costs", and that the community also plays a substantial part in funding the gallery.
- Audited financial statements for 2014 to 2024 are published, audited by Baker Tilly, with a fiscal year from 1 July to 30 June.
- Board: John Cox (chair), Vaughn Roberts (vice chair), Patrice Williams (secretary), Maelynn Ford (executive director), Ivoine Ingraham, Kishan Munroe and Royann Dean. Ex officio members include the Director of Culture, the Director General of Tourism and the director of the Antiquities, Monuments and Museums Corporation.
- Atlantis gave $55,000, accepted on 3 March 2024, to acquire new artworks for the National Collection, through the celebration and fundraising for the 25th anniversary of the Royal at Atlantis; four other organisations received the same amount (gallery news item, "NAGB Receives $55K Donation to Grow the National Collection", 13 March 2024).
- Templeton Religion Trust granted $50,000 for general operating costs from 1 February to 30 June 2020 (the trust's grants page).

Red flags: casino resort donor
- Atlantis gave the gallery $55,000 in March 2024 (gallery news item, 13 March 2024). Wikipedia calls Atlantis Paradise Island "an ocean-themed casino resort" with a 60,000 square foot casino, owned and operated by Brookfield Asset Management as of 2023.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Tribune (Rashad Rolle, "Art prompts row as British body backs out", 2 July 2018) reported that, according to the gallery, the British Council removed its logo and texts from the catalogue of a show it had commissioned, weeks before publication (the article cites The Guardian for the timing). The gallery says it received little explanation beyond that the material was "too political"; the main concern was a section of an essay by Ian Bethel-Bennett (spelling as in the article) on Chinese influence in the Caribbean.
- Gallery director Amanda Coulson wrote to the council's board that "A general feeling of being silenced and exploited is the consensus". The council's head of exhibitions, Gemma Hollington, apologised: "With hindsight, we could have handled this differently."
- Connection to the gallery: the British Council commissioned the show; the gallery's director protested to the council, and the gallery acted to ensure the catalogue and all the essays were published.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is a non-profit established by an Act of Parliament; the government bought its building in 1995 and pays a yearly subvention, with income from admissions, memberships and gifts. The documented gifts are $55,000 from Atlantis (a casino resort) in 2024 and $50,000 from the Templeton Religion Trust in 2020.
