NATIONAL PALACE MUSEUM: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: national-palace-museum
Website: https://www.npm.gov.tw (checked 2026-11-20; answers automated reads with a challenge page)

OVERVIEW
- National museum in Shilin, Taipei, with a Southern Branch in Taibao, Chiayi County (Wikipedia).
- Founded in Beijing in 1925 and re-established in Taipei, where its main building, designed by Huang Baoyu, was built from March 1964 to August 1965 and opened as the "Chung-Shan Museum" on the centenary of Sun Yat-sen's birth (Wikipedia).
- The collection, 699,119 objects as of December 2025, came mostly from the Beijing Palace Museum and other mainland institutions, shipped to Taiwan between December 1948 and February 1949 (Wikipedia).
- The Southern Branch, by Kris Yao's firm Artech, cost NT$7.9 billion; it opened on 28 December 2015, closed in April 2016 over water leaks and reopened on 23 August 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Visitors in 2025: 2,426,842 at the Northern Branch and 1,166,291 at the Southern Branch (Wikipedia, citing the museum's tables). The Northern Branch had 3.8 million in 2019 (Taipei Times).
- Director: Hsiao Tsung-huang (Taipei Times).

FUNDING
National Palace Museum: public: a state agency under the Executive Yuan, paid from the central budget
- One of 31 agencies placed directly under the Executive Yuan, the country's highest administrative organ, by the Organizational Act as amended on 24 January 2025 (Office of the President).
- Total budget for 2025: NT$3.2 billion (Taipei Times).
- Capital works run through the New National Palace Museum Plan: NT$10.747 billion for expanding the Northern Branch, a conservation and display hall for national treasures, and a tourism network linking museums (Executive Yuan, 27 December 2024).
- Ticket and licensing income: the Legislative Yuan's Budget Center reported in October 2026 that visits and ticket sales to August 2026 were below the same period of 2025, and that royalty income forecast for 2027 had not returned to pre-pandemic levels.
- A separate fund, the 故宮文物藝術發展基金 (my translation: Cultural Relics and Art Development Fund), has its own budget, reviewed each year by the same Budget Center (Legislative Yuan).
- Origin of the building: in autumn 1960 the museum office received a NT$32 million grant from AID, and the government added more than NT$30 million (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2023 Control Yuan censure over broken porcelain and leaked images
- In a corrective measure of 16 November 2023 the Control Yuan found cluttered storerooms with poor protection: on 19 May 2022 a worker turned over a box with objects still inside and a Qianlong blue-and-white plate fell and broke. Two other bowls, Ming Hongzhi and Qing Kangxi pieces, were found broken in 2021 and 2022 with no record of who had handled them (Control Yuan; object names my translations).
- A section chief had the silk mounting of the painting "Yellow River Lanzhou Pontoon Bridge" cut to fit a case; the museum noticed more than half a year later (Control Yuan; title my translation).
- More than 30,000 high-resolution images leaked from a storage device open to public queries; the museum reported the breach more than nine months after a member of the public raised it, breaching the Cyber Security Management Act (Control Yuan).

Red flags: Cost and delay of the New National Palace Museum Plan, 2024 to 2026
- In December 2024 the Legislative Yuan's Education and Culture Committee froze NT$350 million over repairs, counterfeit souvenirs sold online under the museum's name and centennial planning (Taipei Times).
- By May 2025 the frozen total was NT$646.81 million, over 20 percent of the budget, against a 2.5 percent freeze three years earlier; the committee then released it. KMT legislator Ko Chih-en still doubted a planned glass pavilion (Taipei Times).
- A guest column in Storm Media by 邱子安 said the museum's report for the release answered none of the 23 motions legislators had filed (Storm Media, opinion).
- In October 2026 the Budget Center found that the Northern Branch expansion had not cleared heritage review, that the treasures hall was behind schedule and in its fourth revision, and that the tourism network should be re-evaluated (Legislative Yuan).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage treats the museum as a political object in the China and Taiwan dispute rather than criticising its programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Louise Benson, "China is waging a restitution campaign against Taiwan's Forbidden City treasures" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/06/05/china-is-waging-a-restitution-campaign-against-taiwans-forbidden-city-treasures
- China wants the collection back; the museum refuses to return objects or lend them to countries without seizure protection.
- The Chinese government used the breakages revealed in 2022, in a state newspaper, to argue only reunification could protect the treasures, and leaked images were sold on Taobao.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Global Taiwan Institute (2023) wrote that under the KMT the museum served to assert the party's claim to be the sole legitimate Chinese government and to teach Taiwanese their Chinese heritage.
- Connection: this was the museum's own founding role.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and clean of private sponsors. The weak points are care of the collection and a capital plan that keeps slipping. Watch whether the expansion clears heritage review and whether visits recover.
