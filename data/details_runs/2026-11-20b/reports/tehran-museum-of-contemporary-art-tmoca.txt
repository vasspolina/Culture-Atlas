TEHRAN MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART (TMOCA): Tehran, Iran
ID: tehran-museum-of-contemporary-art-tmoca
Website: https://www.tmoca.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art on North Kargar Street beside Laleh Park, Tehran (museum website), formerly Farah Park (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 14 October 1977 under Empress Farah Pahlavi; designed by her cousin Kamran Diba with Nader Ardalan, drawing on the architecture of desert towns such as Yazd and Kashan (Wikipedia; Etemad Online).
- It holds more than 3,000 items, Western and Iranian (Wikipedia); The Art Newspaper puts its value at about $3bn, with around 60 works by Picasso.
- After 1979 the Western works stayed in the vault until a first show in 1999 (Wikipedia). In 1994 De Kooning's "Woman III" was exchanged for folios of the Shah Tahmasp Shahnameh (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- Closed for renovation in spring 2018; on 27 January 2021 Tehran Times reported it would reopen the next day (Etemad Online; Tehran Times).
- Directors: Ehsan Aghaei was dismissed on 14 March 2022, after an aerial performer was swung into Noriyuki Haraguchi's oil-pool work "Matter and Mind"; Ebadorreza Eslami-Kulai replaced him (Artforum). Reza Dabirinezhad has led it since April-May 2025 (Persian Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- War: closed during the US-Israeli strikes from 28 February 2026, it drained about 80% of the Haraguchi pool and after the 8 April ceasefire reopened with a weekly "Art and War" programme (The Art Newspaper, 14 May 2026). It is marking its 50th year with a talk series (museum website).
- Open daily 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:00 (museum website); tickets for the 2024 "Eye to Eye" show cost about 14 US cents (Associated Press).

FUNDING
Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art: state-owned and state-funded: Iran's Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance
- Owner: the museum operates under the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance; its site names the ministry, its arts deputy and the Institute for the Development of Visual Arts (museum website).
- Founding money: the art was bought under the empress's office with funds from the National Iranian Oil Company (Wikipedia) and the Plan and Budget Organization (Persian Wikipedia).
- Renovation: the ministry allocated the money in 1396 (2017-18) (Etemad Online, January 2021).
- Acquisitions: the biennial's secretary Gholam Reza Nami said the government-funded Institute for the Promotion of Contemporary Visual Arts paid for 2003 biennial purchases, partly with Ministry of Culture money (The Art Newspaper, 2019).

Red flags: 2012 US sanctions listing of the owning ministry
- OFAC added the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance, the museum's owner, to the Specially Designated Nationals list on 8 November 2012 as an entity under the IRAN-TRA programme (OFAC); the Federal Register notice of 16 November 2012 ties the designation to Executive Order 13628.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the handling of the collection and the museum's upkeep.
1. The Art Newspaper: "Artist accuses Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art of selling off works at a premium" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/03/05/artist-accuses-tehran-museum-of-contemporary-art-of-selling-off-works-at-a-premium
- Unverified: Rokni Haerizadeh says the museum bought his painting cheaply before it was auctioned for $86,680; Barbad Golshiri and Behnam Kamrani fear their works left the collection.
- Former director Sami Azar says a separate state institute bought and later sold the works; director Ehsan Aghaei declined to comment.
2. Hyperallergic: Pamela Karimi, "Why on Earth Did Tehran's Contemporary Art Museum Plant Vegetables in Its Yard?" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/why-on-earth-did-tehran-contemporary-art-museum-plant-vegetables-in-its-yard/
- Standfirst: for critics, the vegetable garden planted in May 2023, since removed, symbolised the museum's "decades-long decline".
- Behind a registration wall; only the headline, standfirst and photo caption were read.
3. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Berlin cancels show of Tehran's Modern art" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/12/28/berlin-cancels-show-of-tehrans-modern-art
- Iranian authorities missed the deadline for export permits for works by Picasso, Pollock, Rothko and Bacon; Hermann Parzinger said further delays "couldn't be justified".

WIDER CONTEXT
- Renovation overrun, 2018 to 2021: officials promised a six-month closure; on 26 October 2018 the ministry's resources deputy, Alireza Karandish, said 5 billion toman had been spent and 3 billion more would finish it; in January 2021, still shut, it had cost 16.5 billion toman (Etemad Online).
- On 27 December 2016 the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation ended its agreement for a Berlin show of 61 works from the collection, due to open on 4 December (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin; The Art Newspaper).
- In February 2017 Culture Minister Reza Salehi-Amiri said he had proposed cancelling the Berlin and Rome show pending review over "legal ambiguity"; the works went on view in Tehran in March 2017 (Tehran Times).
- The minister who halted the loan heads the ministry that owns the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from a sanctioned Iranian ministry. The weak points are state control of the collection and loose stewardship of it.
