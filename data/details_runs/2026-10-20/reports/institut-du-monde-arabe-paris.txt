INSTITUT DU MONDE ARABE: Paris, France
ID: institut-du-monde-arabe-paris
Website: https://www.imarabe.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural institution, museum and library at 1 Rue des Fossés Saint-Bernard, 75005 Paris, by the Jussieu (line 7) and Cardinal Lemoine (line 10) metro stops (stated by the space).
- Created under a 1980 international agreement between France and the Arab states that founded it, 22 countries according to the Cour des comptes.
- Its stated mission is to enable "the study, knowledge and understanding of the Arab world, its language, its civilisation" (our translation, stated by the space).
- The pages checked do not name the architect of the building.
- On site: a museum, a library, a shop, exhibitions and Arabic-language courses, including an internationally recognised Arabic proficiency certificate (Cour des comptes).
- Closed Monday; museum Tuesday to Friday 10 am to 6 pm, weekends and holidays 10 am to 7 pm; library Tuesday to Sunday 1 pm to 7 pm (stated by the space). Museum admission is €10 for over-26s and the library is free (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Institut du monde arabe: mainly public: the French foreign ministry, with corporate patrons
- The French Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs provides more than half of the Institute's revenue as a subsidy (Cour des comptes, December 2024); Brut gave the ministry's share as half of the budget, €12.3 million a year (Brut, February 2026).
- The founding Arab states have stopped paying their share of operating costs as the original agreement defined it; the Institute has used up half of its own funds since its creation; the accounts balanced in the last two years reviewed (Cour des comptes).
- The Cour des comptes recommended that the ministry work with Arab countries to redefine their involvement, particularly their funding, and pursue closer ties with AFALULA.
- TotalEnergies Corporate Foundation is major sponsor in a 20-year partnership, backing "Parfums d'Orient" (26 September 2023 to 17 March 2024) and "ARABOFUTURS" (23 April 2024 to 15 January 2025) (stated by the TotalEnergies Foundation).
- The patrons and sponsors page thanks Honor Frost Foundation, Antoine Maamari, Myriam et Georges Antaki, Ishtar Kettaneh Méjanès, Alain et Chantal Gouverneyre, Fondation Michel Issa, Fondation Aimée et Charles Kettaneh and ALAM Suisse in text, and shows logos for AFALULA, AFD, Arab Bank Switzerland, CMA CGM, Design Space AlUla and Fondation Jean-Luc Lagardère (stated by the space). No amounts are given.

Red flags: Jack Lang, president from 2013 to 2026, and Jeffrey Epstein
- Lang's name appears 673 times in the documents US authorities published on 30 January 2026 (Brut).
- Emails cited by AFP show Étienne Binant, an Institute patron, writing to Epstein in 2017 that Lang "a personnellement insisté" (insisted personally) that Epstein come to his birthday; other messages attributed to Lang tell Epstein "votre générosité est infinie" (our translation: your generosity is endless) and ask for a car ride to an event (Brut).
- The files document exchanges between the two men from 2012 to 2019, while Lang was president from 2013 (The Citizen, Euronews).
- Euronews: the files show Lang's daughter Caroline partnered with Epstein in 2016 in a company buying works by emerging French artists and was named a €5 million beneficiary in his will; she denies receiving any funds.
- France's financial prosecutor (PNF) opened a preliminary investigation on 6 February 2026 into Lang and his daughter Caroline for aggravated laundering of tax fraud; police searched the Institute on 16 February (The Citizen). Nobody had been charged by 9 February (Brut). Lang cited "naïveté" about his past ties (Brut) and called the accusations baseless (Euronews).
- Euronews reported on 9 February 2026 that Lang had resigned as president after the revelations; Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot asked the board to meet within seven days to appoint an interim president.
- No reporting found of Epstein money reaching the Institute.

Red flags: TotalEnergies sponsorship
- TotalEnergies Foundation sponsored Institute exhibitions in 2023 to 2025 (TotalEnergies Foundation).
- Greenpeace France named the Institute among TotalEnergies' cultural partners, with the Louvre, quai Branly and Pompidou, calling the sponsorship a "soft power" strategy (L'Info Durable, 6 October 2021). The page quotes no response from TotalEnergies.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media; the coverage is of Lang and the investigation.

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute is run on French state money, with its founding Arab states no longer paying their share and TotalEnergies as a major sponsor. The weak points are the Epstein files naming Lang, who led it for 13 years, and the fossil fuel sponsorship. Worth watching: the PNF investigation and the Institute's choice of a new president.
