PICA PERTH: Perth, Australia
ID: pica-perth
Website: https://pica.org.au

OVERVIEW
- Perth Institute of Contemporary Arts, a contemporary visual and performance arts venue at 51 James Street in the Perth Cultural Centre, Western Australia.
- PICA began operating from the James Street building in 1988, and the building was officially opened as PICA on 8 November 1991 (Wikipedia).
- The building dates from 1896 and was the Perth Boys' and Girls' School, later a technical college annexe; it is a Federation Free Classical building on the State Register of Heritage Places (Wikipedia). Donaldson and Warn designed the conversion (Wikipedia).
- In 2024 PICA turned its 100-seat theatre into a sunlit foyer and moved its public entrance to the west, ahead of the State Government's Perth Cultural Centre Rejuvenation project (PICA Annual Report 2024).
- Its annual Hatched: National Graduate Show has run since 1992 (Wikipedia); in 2024 Hatched showed 22 artists in its 33rd year (PICA Annual Report 2024).
- Hannah Mathews is Director and CEO, and Mark Clapham chairs the board; the 2024 board also listed Melvin Yeo, Sarkis Akopov, Minali Gamage, Tarryn Gill, Jeremy Smith and Paula Taylor (PICA Annual Report 2024).
- Galleries are open Tuesday to Sunday, 12 to 5 pm, and exhibitions are always free. Accessible parking is in nearby City of Perth car parks, and a lift gives wheelchair access from the Cultural Centre car park.

FUNDING
PICA Perth: mainly public with private donors: Western Australian and federal governments, Lotterywest, City of Perth, Minderoo Foundation and individual donors
- Revenue was A$2,840,093 in 2024 against A$2,568,193 in 2023, with a deficit of A$67,550 (PICA Annual Report 2024, financial statements). 169,720 people engaged with the in-person program in 2024, of whom 153,592 visited exhibitions (annual report).
- The annual report says PICA's ongoing programs are primarily supported by the State of Western Australia through the Department of Local Government, Sport and Cultural Industries in association with Lotterywest, and by the Australian Government through Creative Australia. It also names the Visual Arts and Craft Strategy, an initiative of the Australian, State and Territory Governments.
- City of Perth supports After Dark Opening events, Weekends at PICA, Open Studio events, PICA Hub activities and creative learning programs (annual report).
- Minderoo Foundation supports Hatched and the Hatched Artist-in-Residence, and is funding two roles, the Hatched Curatorial Associate and the Learning and Access Associate, to build organisational capacity (annual report).
- 2024 donors named in the annual report: the Ungar Family Foundation (Visionary Gifts); the Estate of Griselda Hitchcock and the estate of artist Shelagh Wakely (bequests); Art Commissioners at A$10,500 and over, including Mark Clapham, Marco D'Orsogna and Terry Scott, Theo and Sia Kailis, and The Mack Family; Director's Circle at A$5,500 and over, including Wesfarmers Arts.
- Trusts and foundations listed for 2024: A&M Fini Foundation, Feilman Foundation, Ian Potter Foundation, Minderoo Foundation, Project Eleven and Ungar Family Foundation.
- Other partners named by the chair: The University of Western Australia, Schwartz Media, JCDecaux, Inspiration Paint, Grace Fine Art, Alex Hotel, Cherubino Wines, Rocky Ridge Brewing Co and Picabar.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia says the Forrests gave A$5 billion of Fortescue shares to Minderoo Foundation in 2023.
- On 12 May 2026 the Federal Court ordered Fortescue to pay the Yindjibarndi Ngurra Aboriginal Corporation A$150 million for cultural loss and A$100,000 for economic loss; the Yindjibarndi had sought A$1.8 billion (The Canberra Times, Aaron Bunch, 12 May 2026). ArtAsiaPacific (Kalani Ko, 15 May 2026) reports that Justice Stephen Burley found Fortescue's Solomon Hub operations proceeded without the consent of traditional owners.
- Connection to PICA: Wikipedia describes Minderoo Foundation as founded by Andrew and Nicola Forrest, and The Canberra Times calls Fortescue "Andrew Forrest's Fortescue Metals Group". Minderoo supports PICA's Hatched program and two staff roles (PICA Annual Report 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
PICA is paid for mainly by the Western Australian and federal governments, with Lotterywest, City of Perth, Minderoo Foundation and named private donors, all listed in its 2024 annual report. The one point of context is Minderoo, which Wikipedia says received Fortescue shares worth A$5 billion from the Forrests in 2023, and Fortescue's May 2026 Federal Court compensation order.
