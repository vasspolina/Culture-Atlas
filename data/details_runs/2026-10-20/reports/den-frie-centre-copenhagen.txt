DEN FRIE CENTRE OF CONTEMPORARY ART (DEN FRIE UDSTILLINGSBYGNING): Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: den-frie-centre-copenhagen
Website: https://denfrie.dk

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition building and arts centre at Oslo Plads 1, 2100 Copenhagen, home of the artists' association Den Frie Udstilling.
- The association was founded in 1891 as an alternative to the censored exhibitions at Charlottenborg; it calls itself Denmark's oldest artists' association. Its founders included J.F. Willumsen, Anne Marie Carl-Nielsen, Vilhelm Hammershøi and Harald and Agnes Slott-Møller (stated by the space).
- The exhibition building, designed by the artist J.F. Willumsen, was completed in 1893 and moved to its current site in 1898; a gilded, winged Pegasus hangs above the entrance (stated by the space).
- The basement houses OSLO, an exhibition space for solo projects with free admission (stated by the space); Copenhagen's committee papers of 20 February 2014 describe a 600 square metre basement funded by a foundation donation.
- A separate building board of a chair and four members serves four-year terms and is appointed in rotation by Den Frie, Akademirådet, AICA and BKF/UKK; Milena Bonifacini, a visual artist appointed by Den Frie Udstilling, is chair (stated by the space).
- Visiting: closed Monday and Tuesday; Wednesday to Friday 12:00 to 18:00 (Thursday to 20:00); Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00. Tickets and annual passes are sold at billet.denfrie.dk. Admission to the Carl Nielsen & Anne Marie Carl-Nielsen Legat 2026 exhibition was free for its run, 20 June to 30 August 2026 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Den Frie Centre of Contemporary Art: artist-owned non-profit financed by state operating grants (2014 figures) and private foundations
- Copenhagen's Culture and Leisure Committee papers of 20 February 2014 list operating grants of 1.2 million kroner a year from the Ministry of Culture and 1.2 million kroner from the Danish Agency for Culture from 2014. They put annual operating costs at 5.5 million kroner at that time.
- The same papers record that the A.P. Møller and Chastine Mc-Kinney Møller Foundation gave funds for restoring the building and for the underground extension and garden; no amount is given. The committee referred the centre's request for a city operating grant for the new level, 1.4 million kroner a year, to the 2015 budget negotiations.
- Villum Fonden gave 1,094,825 kroner in 2024 towards a new forecourt facing Oslo Plads and a renovated foyer, after the six-year project outgrew its budget.
- Scan Magazine (issue 97, February 2017, no byline) said the centre is "owned by 53 artists".
- The centre posts annual reports for 2017 to 2025 on its site.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Den Frie is state operating grants and Danish foundations, with the building governed through artists and artist bodies. The documented sources are the Ministry of Culture and the Danish Agency for Culture (1.2 million kroner each in 2014), the A.P. Møller and Chastine Mc-Kinney Møller Foundation for the building restoration and basement, and Villum Fonden (1,094,825 kroner in 2024) for the forecourt and foyer.
