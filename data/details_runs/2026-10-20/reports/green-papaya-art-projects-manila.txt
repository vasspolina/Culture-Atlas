GREEN PAPAYA ART PROJECTS: Quezon City / Manila, Philippines
ID: green-papaya-art-projects-manila
Website: https://greenpapaya.art

OVERVIEW
- Independent artist-run non-profit initiative in Metro Manila; the 17th Istanbul Biennial page describes it as the "longest-running independently run multidisciplinary initiative in the Philippines".
- Founded in 2000 by artists Norberto "Peewee" Roldan and Donna Miranda; the group's own essay gives the founding date as 1 May 2000 (4A Papers).
- It began in a condominium building near the University of the Philippines in Quezon City, then moved to a converted garage and a two-level apartment (Wikipedia). In August 2008 it moved into a two-storey building in Kamuning, Quezon City, and from around October 2015 the bar Catch272 occupied the ground floor (4A Papers).
- The name is a metaphor for freshness, a space "always in a state of becoming" (Wikipedia).
- Programme: exhibitions, residencies, talks and archival work; it published one issue of the magazine Papaya in 2008 (Wikipedia). It served as artistic director and curator of the 2018 VIVA ExCon (Istanbul Biennial page; Wikipedia places it in Capiz) and showed at the 17th Istanbul Biennial at Barın Han.
- A fire on 3 June 2020 started in a neighbouring space, severely damaged the interior, destroyed much of the archives and collapsed the ground-floor ceiling; fire-service investigators attributed it to an electrical fault (ArtAsiaPacific, HG Masters, 4 June 2020).
- The group had planned to close in 2021 after 20 years; in October 2021 it wrote "Papaya won't close in 2021, largely thanks to our community's support" (4A Papers).
- The Lopez Museum and Library offered its restoration laboratory and temporary archival facility after the fire; materials were moved there in June 2020 and returned in October 2020. Green Papaya began archiving with Asia Art Archive in Hong Kong in 2017, and phase 1 of the archive became accessible on the Asia Art Archive website in February 2021 (4A Papers).

FUNDING
Green Papaya Art Projects: independent: small grants from cultural institutes, donations and shop sales
- Japan Foundation, Manila approved a project proposal in early August 2020, and the group launched "Right People, Wrong Timing" on 14 August 2020 (4A Papers, by the group itself).
- The Goethe-Institut approved an application to its International 2020 Relief Fund for Organisations in Culture and Education around mid-September 2020; the group says the money helped re-establish its office and archive facility and continue digitising archival materials (4A Papers, by the group itself).
- It raised money through the Papaya Shop, put on hold in June 2021 because of insufficient manpower, a live selling fundraiser on 3 February 2021 and donations, which it continues to accept (4A Papers, by the group itself).
- Its collection included more than 300 works donated by artists (ArtAsiaPacific).

BOTTOM LINE
Green Papaya is an artist-run non-profit kept going by small grants from the Japan Foundation, Manila and the Goethe-Institut, donations and shop sales, as stated by the group itself.
