MILITARY MUSEUM OF THE CHINESE PEOPLE'S REVOLUTION: Beijing, China
ID: military-museum-of-the-chinese-people-s-revolution
Website: http://www.jb.mil.cn (checked 2026-11-20; returned a server error)

OVERVIEW
- China's national military museum at No. 9 Fuxing Road in Haidian district, on the western extension of Chang'an Avenue south of Yuyuantan (China Daily, Visit Beijing).
- Planned in 1958 as one of the Ten Great Buildings for the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic; the Central Military Commission approved its name on 12 March 1959, Mao Zedong wrote the name board, and it opened on 1 August 1960 (Visit Beijing, China Daily).
- The main building is 94.7 metres high and carries a six-metre gilded PLA emblem; its 4.9 metre bronze doors were cast from shell casings sent by units on the Fujian front (Visit Beijing).
- The Propaganda Department of the Party Central Committee listed it in July 1997 in its first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- It was rebuilt from September 2012 to July 2017 and now covers 159,000 square metres with 43 exhibition halls (China Daily, state media).
- The collection holds over 180,000 relics, 1,793 of them rated first class, with more than 80,000 historical photographs (Beijing municipal government, 2023); fighter aircraft, cannons, warships and missiles are on display (China Daily).
- On 21 July 2017 Xi Jinping and the Politburo Standing Committee visited the exhibition for the PLA's 90th anniversary there, where he called for building the army into a "world-class military" (Xinhua via gov.cn, state media; own translation).
- Visiting: 9:00 to 17:00, tickets issued until 16:00, closed Mondays except national holidays (Beijing municipal government).

FUNDING
Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution: state-run: a museum of the People's Liberation Army
- An undated Visit Beijing page, which describes the old 22-hall display from before the rebuild, says the museum was a deputy army-level unit placed under the PLA General Political Department, with that department's directly affiliated work department as administrative supervisor and its propaganda department as professional supervisor.
- The same page gives its tasks as collecting and displaying the history of the armed struggle led by the Communist Party and staging exhibitions around "the central work of the Party, the state and the army" (own translation).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism on record is a passing remark in an academic book review.
1. MCLC Resource Center: Joseph R. Allen, review of Kirk A. Denton, "Exhibiting the Past" (2015)
URL: https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/allen
- Describes the museum, as treated in Denton's book, as "conservative", a place "where the cult of Mao lingers, and not in an ironic sense".
- A brief mention in a review of a wider study of Chinese state museums.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state institution run by the army whose history it presents, and carries no funder flag. The weak point is purpose: its exhibitions serve the work of the Party and the army. Worth watching: which body supervises it today.
