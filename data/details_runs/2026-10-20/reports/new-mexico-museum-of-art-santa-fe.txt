NEW MEXICO MUSEUM OF ART: Santa Fe, USA
ID: new-mexico-museum-of-art-santa-fe
Website: https://www.nmartmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in two buildings: the Plaza Building at 107 West Palace Avenue, one block from the Santa Fe Plaza, and Vladem Contemporary (opened September 2023, named for Bob and Ellen Vladem) at 404 Montezuma Avenue in the Railyard Arts District (museum website; Wikipedia).
- Established in 1917 as the Museum of New Mexico Art Gallery, later the Museum of Fine Arts; renamed in 2007 (Wikipedia).
- Architect Isaac Rapp designed the Plaza Building in Pueblo Revival style, with a facade based on several New Mexico mission churches (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 20,000 works, including Georgia O'Keeffe, Ansel Adams and the Taos Society of Artists (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 10am to 5pm, closed Mondays from November to April, open until 7pm on Fridays from May to October. General admission $12, New Mexico residents with ID $7, children 16 and under free, New Mexico residents free on the first Sunday of each month; one ticket covers both buildings (museum website). No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
New Mexico Museum of Art: mainly public: state of New Mexico, with private gifts through the Museum of New Mexico Foundation
- The museum is state-governed, one of four state-run museums in Santa Fe (Wikipedia); the Department of Cultural Affairs is the state agency named in the mural dispute (Hyperallergic). No reporting found of a published budget for the museum alone.
- The Museum of New Mexico Foundation had revenue of $16,217,538 and expenses of $7,683,390 in the year to June 2025 (ProPublica).
- Naming rights to Vladem Contemporary were offered to Bob and Ellen Vladem in early 2018 in exchange for a $4 million gift (Hyperallergic, Alicia Inez Guzmán, 11 February 2021). The gift went to the Foundation (Santa Fe New Mexican, Phill Casaus, 8 October 2022).
- No reporting found of Isleta Resort & Casino as a Foundation donor: it is not on the Foundation's business-members page (listing as of 10 September 2026), and no 2023 donor list was found.

Red flags: removal of the Multi-Cultural mural for Vladem Contemporary
- The mural "Multi-Cultural" was painted in 1980 by Gilberto Guzmán with other artists and Institute of American Indian Arts students on the Halpin Records Center. Construction for Vladem Contemporary was due to start in early February 2021 and would destroy it (Hyperallergic, 11 February 2021).
- A 2019 Department of Cultural Affairs press release said "no treatment is currently possible that can fully address the integrity" of the original image. Unverified: the Hyperallergic author, a member of the coalition opposing the removal, wrote that a 2018 report by conservator Cynthia Lawrence offered options including relocation, and that Lawrence told her the department misrepresented it.
- Guzmán sued in US District Court in Albuquerque on 8 March 2021, claiming breach of contract and violation of the Visual Artists Rights Act. The department said the mural would be "retired as part of the renovation" and acknowledged in a display inside the new museum (Santa Fe New Mexican, Phaedra Haywood, 8 March 2021).
- At a protest by the mural on 6 February 2021, more than a dozen officers stood guard (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: naming rights for Vladem Contemporary
- By 8 October 2022 the $4 million naming deal had drawn criticism. Bob Vladem called it a "baseball bat to the forehead" and said "we don't feel, I don't feel, a part of the community". The column does not say the objections were about the mural and quotes no Foundation statement (Phill Casaus, Santa Fe New Mexican, 8 October 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the mural removal and the Vladem naming deal.
1. Hyperallergic: Alicia Inez Guzmán, "A Historic Chicano Mural Is Being Destroyed for an Art Museum" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/621539/multi-cultural-mural-santa-fe-vladem-contemporary/
- Argues the museum and the Department of Cultural Affairs are removing a community mural for a new building while ignoring community concerns.
- Quotes Bob Vladem that the mural "was not even anything talked about" when he made the gift and that "the decision was already made".
- Opinion piece by a member of the Keep Santa Fe Multicultural coalition, which opposes the removal.
2. The Santa Fe New Mexican: Phill Casaus, "Naming rights controversy gives Vladems a lesson in Santa Fe" (2022)
URL: https://www.yahoo.com/news/phill-casaus-naming-rights-controversy-150400443.html
- Column on the criticism of the $4 million gift and the Vladems' reaction; local newspaper, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-governed, so its core money is public; the private money identified is a $4 million naming gift to the Foundation. The weak points are the Vladem naming deal, the removal of the Guzmán mural over community objections and a lawsuit, and the Foundation's thin donor disclosure.
