BRONX MUSEUM OF THE ARTS: Bronx, USA
ID: bronx-museum-of-the-arts-bronx
Website: https://bronxmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 1040 Grand Concourse in the Bronx, New York.
- Opened on 11 May 1971 in the Bronx County Courthouse rotunda, in a partnership between the Bronx Council on the Arts and the Metropolitan Museum of Art; Wikipedia credits Irma Fleck with founding it.
- Moved in 1983 into a former synagogue the city bought in 1982; a $19 million Arquitectonica expansion opened in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- A renovation of the south wing, which the museum puts at $42.9 million and The Art Newspaper at $43 million, broke ground in 2024 (Wikipedia gives July 2024); the museum stays open and The Art Newspaper (21 September 2026) reports a full reopening in autumn 2027.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm; admission is free for everyone and has been since 2012 (Wikipedia). Public areas are wheelchair accessible, free wheelchairs are available in the lobby, and materials are in English and Spanish.

FUNDING
Bronx Museum of the Arts: mixed public, foundation and corporate funding: New York City and State arts agencies, federal grants, and named foundations and companies
- Public: the museum's supporters page lists the City of New York, the NYC Department of Cultural Affairs, the New York State Council on the Arts, the Dormitory Authority of the State of New York, the Institute of Museum and Library Services and the National Endowment for the Arts, without years or amounts. It names the Department of Cultural Affairs as sustaining support.
- The Institute of Museum and Library Services awarded the museum $231,000 in federal funds in fiscal year 2022 (IMLS).
- The museum says the City of New York primarily funds the south wing renovation. Wikipedia reports that the mayor's office provided $7 million when a renovation and expansion plan was announced in 2016.
- Corporate and foundation supporters, listed without years or amounts, include Bank of America, Con Edison, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Deutsche Bank Americas Foundation, the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Ford Foundation, Henry Luce Foundation and Rockefeller Brothers Fund.
- Filings on ProPublica: for the year to June 2025, revenue was $3,536,705, expenses $4,341,518 and contributions $2,897,503; net assets were $3,551,509, down from $5,565,537 in 2023.
- Wikipedia, citing a 2013 source, states the museum now relies mainly on corporations, foundations and private donors.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Three news items from August and September 2016 report the resignation of six board members over governance and the museum's Cuba projects.
1. Apollo: "News" byline, "Resignations at the Bronx Museum" (29 August 2016)
URL: https://www.apollo-magazine.com/resignations-at-the-bronx-museum/
- Reports that two governing executives and four trustees resigned, "claiming that the institution has lost sight of its local remit"; the preceding sentence mentions initiatives to "build attendance and revenue."
- Names the museum's Cuba exchange, including the delayed "Wild Noise" loan with the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes in Havana, as a particular concern. Cites the New York Times.
2. The Art Newspaper: David D'Arcy, "US museums' Cuban dreams deferred" (30 September 2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/09/30/us-museums-cuban-dreams-deferred
- The chairwoman and five other trustees left in August 2016, citing the cost of "Wild Noise" and a $2.5 million commission for a José Martí statue that director Holly Block hoped to donate to Cuba.
- Departing chair Laura Blanco said these projects "are symptoms of a much deeper and more serious problem facing the museum. It boils down to a lack of governance."
- A museum publicist said the budget was balanced and the statue had a dedicated fund; Block said the museum "remains committed to Wild Noise."
3. DNAinfo: Eddie Small, "Bronx Arts Museum Loses 6 Board Members in a Week" (29 August 2016)
URL: https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20160829/concourse/bronx-arts-museum-loses-6-board-members-week
- Chair Laura Blanco and vice chair Mary Beth Mandanas stepped down the week before; Jonathan Plotkin, Jeanna Hussey, Isabella Hutchinson and Jason Silverman also resigned.
- Blanco's memo objected to the Cuba exchange and to a $2.5 million plan to give Havana a copy of the Martí statue, and questioned the $3.2 million yearly budget.
- Mandanas told the New York Times she was mainly concerned with corporate governance and finances. Museum spokeswoman Sara Griffin said the museum had "minimal financial exposure" on the statue project and did not believe the other four left over those concerns.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public arts funding from the city, state and federal governments plus named foundations and corporations. In August 2016 six board members resigned over governance and the Cuba projects, as Apollo, The Art Newspaper and DNAinfo reported. Annual spending exceeded revenue in each of the last three filings, and net assets fell to $3.55 million in 2025.
