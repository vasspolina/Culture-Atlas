MUSEU DE ARTE MODERNA DE SÃO PAULO (MAM SÃO PAULO): São Paulo, Brazil
ID: museu-de-arte-moderna-de-são-paulo-são-paulo
Website: https://mam.org.br (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum under the marquise of Ibirapuera Park, Av. Pedro Álvares Cabral, Vila Mariana, São Paulo (MAM website).
- Founded in 1948 by the industrialist Francisco Matarazzo Sobrinho ("Ciccillo") and his wife Yolanda Penteado, modelled on New York's Museum of Modern Art (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- In 1963 the founder moved to dissolve the museum and donate its collection to the University of São Paulo; a new MAM formed by Paulo Mendes de Almeida and Mário Pedrosa settled in the Ibirapuera marquise in 1968 (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- The marquise complex was designed by Oscar Niemeyer and adapted by Lina Bo Bardi (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- On site: a sculpture garden of 30 sculptures on 6,000 m², designed by Roberto Burle Marx and opened in 1993 (MAM website); a library of more than 60,000 volumes; the Panorama da Arte Brasileira exhibition, held since 1969 (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:30; the site says it will be closed on Sunday 25 October for the election run-off. The site gives no ticket prices and no accessibility information.

FUNDING
Museu de Arte Moderna de São Paulo: corporate-sponsored: Itaú, Grupo Ultra and others, with tax-incentive and city funds
- The museum's support page ranks sponsors by value: strategic partners above R$2,000,000, master sponsors R$500,000 to R$2,000,000, sponsors up to R$500,000.
- Strategic partners: Itaú and Pro matre Paulista. Master sponsors: Grupo Ultra, Goodstorage, Unipar and Tiffany.
- Sponsors: Alupar, Biolab, BMA Advogados, BV, Fundo Museu Escola, Leo Madeiras, Leo Social, Pinheiro Neto Advogados, TozziniFreire, Vivo and PwC.
- Programme sponsors: Vivo (visitor programme), BV (MAM na Marquise), Tozzini (Ateliê Aberto), and Ultra (MAM nas Escolas).
- Incentive laws used: the federal Lei Rouanet, state ProAC ICMS and municipal ProMAC; the museum also works with the city government through agreements such as subsidies and contributions.
- No reporting found of the sums each sponsor paid in a given year.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Grupo Ultra, master sponsor)
- Grupo Ultra is a master sponsor (R$500,000 to R$2,000,000 tier) and sponsors the MAM nas Escolas programme (MAM website).
- Ultra Group (Ultrapar Participações) owns Ipiranga, which Wikipedia calls the second-largest fuel distributor in Brazil with more than 6,500 service stations, plus the bottled-gas distributor Ultragaz and the bulk-liquid storage firm Ultracargo (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against Ultra's money at the museum.

Red flags: Protest over a 2017 performance
- On 26 September 2017, at the opening of the exhibition "Brasil em Multiplicação", the choreographer Wagner Schwartz performed "La Bête", in which visitors could handle his naked body. A video of a child and her mother touching his hands and feet went viral (Global Voices, translating Fabíola Hauch).
- Demonstrations followed outside the museum on 29 and 30 September, with attacks on employees and visitors. Websites and Facebook pages linked to conservative and religious movements, in particular the Movimento Brasil Livre, led the campaign, and mayor João Dória called the performance "libidinous" and supported a boycott (Global Voices).
- The museum said the performance took place in a room labelled as artistic nudity, had no erotic content and reinterpreted Lygia Clark's "Bicho", and it did not cancel the exhibition (Global Voices). The protest targeted the artwork, not the museum's funders.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media of the museum's funders or governance. The criticism found is of the 2017 attacks on the museum, which the one piece found defends it against.
1. Global Voices: Fabíola Hauch (translated by Liam Anderson), "Art Exhibitions Fall Into the Crosshairs of Brazilian Conservatives" (2017)
URL: https://globalvoices.org/2017/10/09/art-exhibitions-fall-into-the-crosshairs-of-brazilian-conservatives/
- Reports that conservative groups, led by the Movimento Brasil Livre, used a viral video of the performance to campaign against the museum, and that the museum blamed disinformation.
- Quotes the University of São Paulo professor Pablo Ortellado, writing in Folha de S. Paulo: "the right took advantage of the great disconnect of the public from fine art" to recast the performance as an apology for paedophilia.
- Not an art outlet, and it defends the museum rather than criticising it.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a non-profit run on corporate sponsorship and tax-incentive laws, with a published tier list. The weak point is Grupo Ultra, a master sponsor whose group owns the fuel distributor Ipiranga. Worth watching: whether sponsor amounts and dates are published, which the support page does not do.
