WEST BUND MUSEUM: Shanghai, China
ID: west-bund-museum
Website: https://wbmshanghai.com/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 2600 Longteng Avenue, Xuhui District, on the Huangpu river waterfront, about eight kilometres south of central Shanghai (London Review of Books); the area was once docks and an airfield.
- Opened in November 2019 as the Centre Pompidou x West Bund Museum Project, a five-year partnership between the Centre Pompidou and the Shanghai West Bund Development Group; a renewal for five more years was signed on 24 November 2023 (Centre Pompidou).
- Named for the West Bund waterfront district.
- Designed by David Chipperfield Architects: 22,000 square metres, three 17-metre gallery volumes clad in translucent recycled glass, arranged in a pin-wheel around a double-height lobby, with a café pavilion at the river's edge. Construction began in 2016 (David Chipperfield Architects).
- What is there: changing displays from the Centre Pompidou collection, a multipurpose hall, an art studio and education spaces. The current show, "Reinventing Landscape", is listed as 29 April 2025 to 18 October 2026.
- Visiting: the museum site lists Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays; the Centre Pompidou's venue page lists 10:00 to 17:00. Ticket prices are not given on the pages read, and no accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
West Bund Museum: state-owned developer: the Shanghai-based West Bund Group pays for the museum and the Pompidou partnership
- West Bund Group is a government-owned property developer; its president, Li Zhonghui, also directs the museum. West Bund is the operator and pays all operating costs (The Art Newspaper, 2019).
- The Art Newspaper reports West Bund pays the Pompidou 2.7 million euros a year; Pompidou president Serge Lasvignes said the fee varies by year.
- Sixth Tone (2020) describes West Bund Group as a state-owned enterprise better known for land development and infrastructure than for art.
- Since 2019 the two institutions have held nearly 30 exhibitions with over 2 million visitors and over 1,200 works from the Pompidou collection (Shanghai government site, 2024).
- No reporting found of a private donor or corporate sponsor.

Red flags: removal of works under official request
- 2019 opening show: The New York Times reported that local officials asked for fewer than five works to be removed from "The Shape of Time". Lasvignes declined to name the works (The Art Newspaper, 6 November 2019).
- Lasvignes ruled out self-censorship and said the museum stays "absolutely free in our programme" (The Art Newspaper).
- The museum's director is also president of the state-owned developer that funds it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism dates from the 2019 and 2020 opening and concerns censorship, dependence on a state developer and the lasting value of the partnership. No later critical pieces found.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris and Lisa Movius, "'We are the opposite of Starbucks': Pompidou chief on the challenges of setting up a Shanghai satellite" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/centre-pompidou-chief-outlines-challenges-of-setting-up-a-satellite-in-shanghai-including-censorship-threats-and-settling-the-project-costs
- Reports the fewer-than-five removed works, the 2.7 million euro annual fee, and that a state-owned developer pays and its president runs the museum.
- An interview, not an argued critique; Lasvignes answers the censorship question himself.
2. London Review of Books: John-Paul Stonard, "In Shanghai: The West Bund Museum" (2020)
URL: https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v42/n04/john-paul-stonard/in-shanghai
- Calls the museum a project of "soft diplomacy" that spreads a Western account of modern art.
- Argues the exchange runs both ways, since modernist works take on new meanings in China.
- Does not discuss censorship or the removed works in the text read.
3. Sixth Tone: Xie Siman, "What West Bund's Pompidou Partnership Means for China's Art Scene" (2020)
URL: https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1005103/what-west-bund%E2%80%99s-pompidou-partnership-means-for-china%E2%80%99s-art-scene
- Argues the partnership is "not a cure-all": the two sides did not agree on permanent French curators, and a lack of experienced local staff could undermine the project.
- Asks whether West Bund will keep paying for the Pompidou brand after five years.
- Mentions "controversy over content regulation" without naming an incident.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is state money: a government-owned developer pays for the building, the operation and the Pompidou fee, and no private funder is named. The weak point is the documented 2019 removal of works at officials' request alongside a director who also leads the funder. Worth watching: later shows, since no source read here reports on removals after 2019.
