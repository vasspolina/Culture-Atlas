TALBOT RICE GALLERY: Edinburgh, United Kingdom
ID: talbot-rice-gallery-edinburgh
Website: https://www.trg.ed.ac.uk

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery of the University of Edinburgh, in the Old College, South Bridge, Edinburgh EH8 9YL (Talbot Rice website).
- The gallery opened in 1975; an arts centre had opened in the Quad in 1970. It is named for David Talbot Rice, the university's Watson Gordon Professor of Fine Art from 1934 to 1972 (Wikipedia).
- Old College was designed by Robert Adam and completed by William Henry Playfair (Wikipedia).
- Three exhibition spaces, including a contemporary white cube and a neoclassical room that was a 19th-century natural history museum (Wikipedia; Talbot Rice website).
- Tessa Giblin has been director since November 2016, succeeding Pat Fisher (Wikipedia).
- Solo shows give international artists access to university research and collections; group shows address political and social issues. The gallery works with students at Edinburgh College of Art and runs projects with schools, community groups and local prisons (Talbot Rice website).
- Admission is free; British Sign Language tours and quiet hours for people with sensory needs are offered (Talbot Rice website). "The Rupture: No condition is permanent" runs from 24 October 2026 to 13 February 2027 (Talbot Rice website).

FUNDING
Talbot Rice Gallery: public university gallery: the University of Edinburgh, with Creative Scotland, trusts and donors for exhibitions
- Gallery's own account: it relies on individual donations, trusts and foundations. The TRG Supporters' Programme has tiers from £250 a year (Supporter) to £5,000 (Director's Circle), and the TRG Commissioning Fund was set up for the gallery's 50th anniversary (Talbot Rice support page).
- Wael Shawky exhibition (28 June to 28 September 2025): supported by Edinburgh College of Art, Creative Scotland and the Henry Moore Foundation, with the Alwaleed Centre at the University of Edinburgh also listed as a supporter. It was presented in association with the Edinburgh International Festival (Talbot Rice website).
- "The Rupture: No condition is permanent" (from 24 October 2026): Creative Scotland, Edinburgh College of Art and the British Council. Jasmina Cibic's artwork is supported by the Cultural Fund, which promotes Slovenian culture internationally and is administered by the Slovenian Embassy in London (Talbot Rice website).
- Wikipedia lists support from Creative Scotland, the Mondriaan Fonds, Culture Ireland and the Freelands Foundation, and a first Arts and Humanities Research Council grant in 2019.
- Alwaleed Centre: Arab News ("New center to promote cultural ties with West", 28 March 2010) reports the Prince Alwaleed bin Talal Foundation pledged 8 million pounds in 2008 to endow the University of Edinburgh's Prince Alwaleed Bin Talal Centre for the Study of Islam in the Contemporary World. This is a private foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is part of the University of Edinburgh and shows exhibitions backed by Edinburgh College of Art, Creative Scotland, the Henry Moore Foundation and the British Council. The Alwaleed Centre at the University of Edinburgh is a named supporter of the 2025 Wael Shawky exhibition; the Prince Alwaleed bin Talal Foundation pledged 8 million pounds in 2008 to endow the university's Alwaleed centre.
