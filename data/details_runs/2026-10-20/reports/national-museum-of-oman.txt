NATIONAL MUSEUM OF OMAN: Muscat, Oman
ID: national-museum-of-oman
Website: https://www.nm.gov.om/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Oman's national museum in central Muscat, in the Hillat an-Ni'am area on As-Sa'idyyah School Street, facing Qasr al-Alam Palace across the ceremonial boulevard.
- Established by Royal Decree 62/2013 of 20 November 2013 and opened to the public on 30 July 2016, after a partial opening the previous December (gallery site, The Art Newspaper). It replaced the Museum of Omani Heritage, founded in 1974 (Wikipedia).
- A purpose-built building of 13,700 square metres, 4,000 of them permanent galleries and 400 temporary exhibition space. The architect is not named in the sources read.
- 14 permanent galleries, including Maritime History, Arms and Armour, Aflaj and Land of Frankincense, more than 7,000 objects, a learning centre, cafe and shop (gallery site).
- Open Saturday to Thursday 10:00 to 17:00 and Friday 14:00 to 18:00. Admission is 1 rial for Omani and GCC nationals, 2 for residents and 5 for foreigners; students, people with disabilities, certified guides and low-income families enter free. It is listed as wheelchair accessible with Arabic Braille (Experience Oman).

FUNDING
National Museum of Oman: state-funded: Government of Oman under the Minister of Heritage and Tourism
- The museum is a legal entity with financial and administrative independence, set up by royal decree (gallery site).
- The government was planned to cover 90 percent of the budget at opening; Oman's 2016 austerity budget raised concerns, and acting director general Jamal al Moosawi said it "poses challenges" (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- Its Board of Trustees is chaired by the Minister of Heritage and Tourism, Salim bin Mohammed al-Mahrouqi, as of March and September 2025 (Times of Oman, gallery news release).
- The museum signed a cooperation agreement with Russia's State Hermitage Museum in Dhofar in September 2025 (gallery news release).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, donors, an annual report or accounts.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The museum is a body of the Omani state, chaired by the Minister of Heritage and Tourism and established by the Sultan's decree (gallery site, The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: sanctioned board member Mikhail Piotrovsky
- The six-member Board of Trustees includes Professor Mikhail Piotrovsky, Director General of the State Hermitage (board page); the gallery's September 2025 news release calls him a member.
- On 13 September 2025 Piotrovsky signed with the museum's Secretary-General the agreement to open a "Hermitage - Sultanate of Oman Centre" in Dhofar, inside the museum's structure (gallery news release).
- Canada sanctioned Piotrovsky on 31 August 2023. The EU listed him on 23 April 2026, calling him a "close associate" of Putin and citing his backing of Hermitage excavations in occupied Crimea and of a law allowing Ukrainian museum items into Russia's State Museum Fund (The Art Newspaper).
- The Hermitage told The Art Newspaper it had no comment on the sanctions.
- The board page also still names Sayyid Ibrahim bin Saeed Al Busaidi as Minister, so its list is not current.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Omani state's, and no private sponsor or accounts are published. The clear weak point is Mikhail Piotrovsky, who sits on its Board of Trustees and signed the 2025 Dhofar agreement while under Canadian (2023) and EU (2026) sanctions. Watch whether the board changes and whether the Dhofar centre opens.
