SERRALVES FOUNDATION: Porto, Portugal
ID: serralves-foundation-porto
Website: https://www.serralves.pt/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Álvaro Siza Vieira's contemporary art museum, opened on 6 June 1999, with a 1925 to 1944 Art Deco villa and an 18 hectare park in Porto (English Wikipedia).
- The State bought the estate in December 1986, and Decree-Law 240-A/89 created the Foundation in 1989 as a partnership of the State and about 51 public and private bodies (English Wikipedia).
- Philippe Vergne directs the museum. Visitors numbered 902,024 in 2025, against 1,254,193 in 2024 (Annual Report 2025).
- Visiting: 24 euros for all spaces, 15 euros for the park; free for children under 12 and on the first Sunday of each month (Serralves website).

FUNDING
Serralves Foundation: mixed: State subsidy, corporate founders and sponsors, and earned income
- 2025 income was 17.82 million euros: 6.4 million from the Ministry of Culture (36.3 per cent), 5.54 million in patronage and 5.03 million in sales and services. In 2024 income was 16.90 million, with the same State subsidy and 4.05 million in patronage (Annual Report 2025, note 15).
- Founders number 181 (English Wikipedia). Patron Founders pay into an Annual Fund for running costs and heritage investment (Annual Report 2025); the website names them without amounts, Galp Energia from 2021 to 2024 and Tabaqueira in 2021 and 2022. The 2025 list of about 60 includes Caixa Geral de Depósitos, Sonae, Amorim, Mota-Engil and REN Portgás Distribuição, and not Galp (Annual Report 2025).
- 2025 sponsors include BPI and Fundação "la Caixa" (the museum), Ascendi Norte (the park), Fundação EDP, Super Bock Group, novobanco and the City of Porto (the Joan Miró collection); Lizzy and Ramtin Naimi and Hauser & Wirth backed the Cattelan show (Annual Report 2025).
- Serralves put forward 18 projects, more than any other body, under Portugal's scheme granting residence permits for cultural donations of at least 250,000 euros (Diário de Notícias, 26 March 2025, citing the Ministry of Culture).
- In March 2025 it paid 5,954,160 euros of extra VAT assessed for 2011 to 2019 while contesting it, and in April 2025 it took most of those years to court (Annual Report 2025, note 13).

Red flags: Galp Energia, Patron Founder 2021 to 2024
- Galp paid into the Annual Fund in each year from 2021 to 2024 (Serralves website). Its companies work in hydrocarbon exploration and production, refining and gas distribution (English Wikipedia).

Red flags: REN Portgás, a natural gas distributor, Patron Founder in 2025
- Portgás, owned by REN, distributes natural gas in 29 municipalities of northern Portugal (REN).

Red flags: Tabaqueira, Patron Founder 2021 and 2022
- Tabaqueira is absent from the 2023 and 2024 lists (Serralves website). English Wikipedia describes it as a Philip Morris International subsidiary that made the Português cigarette brand.

Red flags: 2025 resignation of board president Isabel Pires de Lima
- Pires de Lima, a former culture minister, resigned on 26 August 2025 after eight months as president, citing a lack of "conditions of trust and institutional solidarity" on the board (Observador).
- She told Público that her predecessor Ana Pinho, now president of the Founders' Council, was pressuring the board and demanded "something like vassalage" (ECO, quoting Público; translation mine). The board replied that it "is not subject, and never was, to pressure" and rejected the claims (ECO; translation mine). The board later elected Manuel Ferreira da Silva president unanimously (RTP).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2018 Mapplethorpe exhibition.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris and José da Silva, "Wolfgang Tillmans and Tania Bruguera pledge support for censored Serralves museum director" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/09/24/wolfgang-tillmans-and-tania-bruguera-pledge-support-for-censored-serralves-museum-director
- Artistic director João Ribas resigned after 20 works were removed and two rooms were restricted to adults.
- More than 150 artists and arts professionals wrote to Ana Pinho that invoking Portugal's law on "pornographic" material was "disingenuous and insulting".
- The Foundation said the curator alone left out the 20 works and that this was not censorship.
2. ArtReview: news desk, "Serralves Museum removes Mapplethorpe works, artistic director resigns" (2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-24-sep-2018-serralves-museum-removes-mapplethorpe-works-artistic-director-resigns
- Reports that 159 of the 179 works Ribas chose were shown, and protests outside.
3. Arte al Día: unsigned, "Censorship and controversy at the Serralves Contemporary Art Museum of Porto" (2018)
URL: https://www.artealdia.com/News/CENSORSHIP-AND-CONTROVERSY-AT-THE-SERRALVES-CONTEMPORARY-ART-MUSEUM-OF-PORTO
- Links the case to the US culture wars of the 1980s and 1990s.
- Reports that collector Luiz Teixeira de Freitas asked for the return of 700 drawings he had lent, in solidarity with Ribas.
4. Observador (news, not art press): Rita Cipriano and Gonçalo Correia, "João Ribas: demiti-me porque não podia tolerar a censura em Serralves" (2018)
URL: https://observador.pt/2018/10/16/joao-ribas-demiti-me-porque-nao-podia-tolerar-a-censura-em-serralves/
- Ribas told parliament's culture committee that the board had two works removed an hour before the press conference.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 25 September 2018 Porto mayor Rui Moreira asked for an urgent meeting of the Founders' Council, on which the city sits, to assess the Mapplethorpe show that led to the museum director's resignation (Correio da Manhã).

BOTTOM LINE
Serralves runs on State, corporate and earned money in roughly equal parts. Its weak points are recent fossil fuel and tobacco founders and the 2025 split at the top of its board.
