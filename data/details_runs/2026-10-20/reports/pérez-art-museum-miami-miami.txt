PÉREZ ART MUSEUM MIAMI: Miami, USA
ID: pérez-art-museum-miami-miami
Website: https://www.pamm.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of 20th- and 21st-century art in Museum Park on Biscayne Bay, at 1103 Biscayne Boulevard, Miami (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1984 as the Center for the Fine Arts, a partnership between Metropolitan Dade County and a private nonprofit; renamed the Miami Art Museum in 1994 (PAMM) or 1996 (Wikipedia); took its present name, Jorge M. Pérez Art Museum of Miami-Dade County, when the new building opened in December 2013.
- Named for the developer and collector Jorge M. Pérez after his leadership gift (PAMM).
- Herzog & de Meuron designed the three-storey building of about 200,000 square feet, with hanging vertical gardens by Patrick Blanc; the site was a derelict waterfront parcel turned into Museum Park (Wikipedia; PAMM).
- Franklin Sirmans is director. The museum has an education programme, guided tours and affiliate groups including Ambassadors for Black Art.
- Visiting: open Monday and Thursday to Sunday, 11 am to 6 pm (Thursday to 9 pm), closed Tuesday and Wednesday. Adults $18, seniors, students and youth $14, children 6 and under and members free; free Thursdays after 5 pm and for military, first responders, Florida educators and visitors identifying as disabled with a caregiver.

FUNDING
Pérez Art Museum Miami: mixed: public capital bonds, donors and earned income
- Miami-Dade County provided the capital funds for the building through its Building Better Communities programme, and the City of Miami provided land and funding for Museum Park (PAMM). Wikipedia puts the voter-approved county bond share at $100 million of a $220 million cost, with $120 million from private donors; it also gives a lower cost of $131 million.
- Jorge M. Pérez gave $35 million (Chronicle of Philanthropy; Wikipedia says $35 million over ten years in one place and $40 million in another).
- Kenneth C. Griffin, founder of the hedge fund Citadel, gave $10 million announced on 9 November 2024; the central gallery is named the Kenneth C. Griffin Gallery (The Art Newspaper; WLRN). A Griffin Collection Basquiat exhibition runs 25 June 2026 to 6 June 2027 (WLRN).
- Mellon Foundation gave $1 million in 2019 and Miami-Dade County provided $2.5 million of the 2013-14 budget (Wikipedia).
- 2024 accounts: revenue $38.4 million, of which contributions $30.0 million; expenses $22.5 million; net assets $193.8 million (ProPublica, Form 990). The 2024 director's pay was $719,251.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors by name on the museum pages opened.

Red flags: Campaigned name, the 2011 renaming
- December 2011: the Miami Art Museum announced it would be renamed for Pérez, and four trustees resigned (Chronicle of Philanthropy).
- Former board president Mary E. Frank took out a full-page newspaper advertisement against the renaming; trustee Rubén A. Rodríguez warned that his firm, Carnival, could withhold the $3.5 million still owed on a $5 million endowment pledge (Chronicle of Philanthropy).
- Collectors Rosa and Carlos de la Cruz said the move could harm future donations; critics argued that a publicly funded institution should carry the community's name (Chronicle of Philanthropy).
- Trustee Craig Robins replied that opponents should match Pérez's gift if they wanted to keep the name (Chronicle of Philanthropy).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Protesters in a Gaza march at the Whitney Museum in New York on 22 November 2023 shouted against Ken Griffin and demanded his removal from the Whitney's board; Griffin had left that board in July 2023 (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara).
- Connection to the space: Griffin gave PAMM $10 million in 2024 and the 2026 Basquiat exhibition is drawn from his collection. No reporting found of a protest, petition or boycott aimed at Griffin at PAMM.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is private gifts plus county bond capital, with no flagged industry behind its named donors in the sources opened. The weak point is the founding name deal: a $35 million gift in exchange for the donor's name on a building largely funded by public bonds, which cost it four trustees in 2011. Worth watching: the Griffin Gallery and Basquiat exhibition, and whether any campaign forms around Griffin at PAMM.
