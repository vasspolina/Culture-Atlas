TARRAWARRA MUSEUM OF ART: Yarra Valley, Australia
ID: tarrawarra-museum-of-art
Website: https://www.twma.com.au (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 313 Healesville-Yarra Glen Road, Healesville, in the Yarra Valley, about 45 km north-east of Melbourne (twma.com.au, Wikipedia).
- Founded by the philanthropists Eva Besen AO and Marc Besen AC, who gave the museum and about 600 works of Australian art from their collection. It was registered in 2000, launched by Prime Minister John Howard on 24 April 2002 in a temporary North Melbourne space, and the purpose-built museum opened in 2003 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia calls it the first art museum in Australia supported by a significant private endowment.
- Building: the gallery was designed by Alan Powell of Powell & Glenn (Wikipedia). The Eva and Marc Besen Centre, with visible art storage and learning and performance spaces, was designed by Kerstin Thompson Architects and opened on 8 March 2025 (twma.com.au).
- On site: sculptures in the grounds, including Callum Morton's "Valhalla", and the TarraWarra Biennial, founded by Maudie Palmer in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Run by an independent board chaired by Daniel Besen AM; Victoria Lynn is director (twma.com.au).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 5pm. Children under 12, concession card holders and local residents enter free, as do students on Wednesdays; the grounds and Besen Centre are free. The galleries are described as fully accessible, with free wheelchair hire (twma.com.au).

FUNDING
TarraWarra Museum of Art: private: the Besen family's endowment and foundation, with corporate sponsors and some state and local government education money
- The Besens founded it with a private endowment and run it as a not-for-profit charity with an independent board (Wikipedia). Marc Besen's wealth came from the Sussan clothing group and shopping-centre interests; the family sold its Highpoint stake for $621 million (50 percent, 2006) and $680 million (25 percent, 2017) (Wikipedia).
- The Besen Family Foundation, set up in 1978, lists TarraWarra as an "Inaugural Foundation Supporter" under its arts programme; no amounts are given. The museum names the TWMA Foundation as its supporter (stated by the space).
- Sponsors named by the space, undated and without amounts: ABL, Chubb, Aon and Pitch Partners; event sponsors Tarrawarra Estate, Four Pillars Gin and Soda Smith; media partners including The Guardian Australia and The Saturday Paper.
- Government: the Victorian Department of Education and Training and Yarra Ranges council are listed as supporters of the learning and engagement programme (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of an annual report, accounts or board-by-board donor list in the pages read; the charity register page did not load.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Reviews of the biennial exist and criticise exhibitions, not the museum's money. No art-press criticism of its funders found.
1. The Conversation: Anita Pisch, "Tarrawarra Biennial underwhelms rather than energises" (2018)
URL: https://theconversation.com/tarrawarra-biennial-underwhelms-rather-than-energises-101096
- Argues "From Will to Form" does not deliver the charged atmosphere its curatorial text promises, and that video works behind curtains and the work "Whistle White" were hard to experience.
- Criticises the art and curation, not the institution; it praises the setting.
2. e-flux Criticism: Tessa Laird, "Endless Circulation," TarraWarra Biennial 2016 (2016)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/239125/endless-circulation-tarrawarra-biennial-2016
- Reads the biennial generously as support for local careers, and notes none of the Indigenous artists were women.
- One institutional point: the museum "sits far outside the circulatory system of the city", so the show is "inaccessible to many Melbournians".
- Does not examine patronage or funding.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Matt Chun and Muhib Nabulsi (Matt Chun's Newsletter on Substack, 15 December 2023, an advocacy piece) argue that arts organisations receiving Besen money should divest and that the foundation "must be comprehensively rejected". Unverified: its claims that the family funded settlement expansion in the Naqab and worked with the Jewish National Fund, from that partisan source, which carries no response from the family.
- It names NGV, Collingwood Yards, Polyglot Theatre, ACMI, Rising Festival and others as places where "Besen money can also be found", without amounts; its only line on the museum calls it "Besen Family owned and controlled".
- No campaign, boycott or open letter aimed at TarraWarra itself was found.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded by a family endowment and foundation whose money came from clothing and shopping centres, with law, insurance and other corporate sponsors, and no flagged industry was found. The weak points are that sponsor amounts and accounts are not published in the pages read, and that the founding family faces advocacy criticism aimed at its foundation in general. Worth watching is whether that campaign extends to TarraWarra.
