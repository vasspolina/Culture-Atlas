REYKJAVÍK ART MUSEUM: Reykjavík, Iceland
ID: reykjavík-art-museum-reykjavík
Website: https://listasafnreykjavikur.is

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum (Listasafn Reykjavíkur) with three sites in central Reykjavík: Hafnarhús at Tryggvagata 17, Kjarvalsstaðir at Flókagata 24 and Ásmundarsafn at Sigtún 105 (museum website).
- Founded in 1973 (Wikipedia). Kjarvalsstaðir opened in 1973 beside Klambratún garden, named for the painter Jóhannes S. Kjarval, and was the first building in Iceland designed for visual art exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Hafnarhús is a harbour warehouse built in the 1930s; Studio Grandi architects renovated it in 1998 to 2000 to house the museum (Visit Reykjavík); Wikipedia gives April 2000 for its reopening.
- Ásmundarsafn opened in 1983 in Laugardalur, in the former home and studio of the sculptor Ásmundur Sveinsson, which he largely built himself between 1942 and 1950 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 17,000 items, mostly by 20th and 21st century Icelandic artists, with holdings by Erró, Kjarval and Sveinsson; about fifteen exhibitions open each year and the museum also manages public art in the city (museum website).
- The museum runs around 200 events a year, including tours for people with disabilities, and takes part in Iceland Airwaves, Reykjavík Museum Night, Reykjavík Arts Festival, DesignMarch and Reykjavík Cultural Night (museum website).
- Hafnarhús is open 10:00 to 17:00 with late opening to 22:00 on Thursdays, Kjarvalsstaðir 10:00 to 17:00 and Ásmundarsafn 13:00 to 17:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Reykjavík Art Museum: municipal: financed and managed by the City of Reykjavík
- Wikipedia states that the museum manages the city's art collection and that Reykjavík city is responsible for the museum's management and financing.
- Its contact address is on the city's reykjavik.is domain (museum website).

Not related: the National Gallery of Iceland (Listasafn Íslands), whose website is listasafn.is. It is a different institution at Laufásvegur 12, with a collection owned by the Icelandic nation and its own sites (the main gallery, Safnahúsið and the Einar Jónsson Museum), not part of the Reykjavík city museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Reykjavík Art Museum is a municipal museum financed and managed by the City of Reykjavík (Wikipedia). Its collection of more than 17,000 items is shown across three sites, and the museum is responsible for public art in the city (museum website).
