MUSEU DO AMANHÃ (MUSEUM OF TOMORROW): Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
ID: museu-do-amanha
Website: https://museudoamanha.org.br (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Science museum on Pier Mauá, at Praça Mauá 1 in the port area of central Rio de Janeiro, which describes itself as "um museu da Prefeitura do Rio", a museum of the city government.
- Conceived by the Fundação Roberto Marinho and the city of Rio, managed by the Instituto de Desenvolvimento e Gestão (IDG), with the Ministry of Culture under "realização" (museum site). It opened on 17 December 2015 with President Dilma Rousseff attending (Wikipedia).
- Designed by the Spanish architect Santiago Calatrava at a cost of about 230 million reais (Wikipedia).
- The main exhibition has five areas: Cosmos, Earth, Anthropocene, Tomorrow and Us (Wikipedia). In December 2025 a new exhibition, "Oceano, o Mundo É um Arquipélago", opened on 17 December (Folha do Leste).
- More than 8 million visitors came in its first decade (Folha do Leste).
- Open Thursday to Tuesday, 10:00 to 18:00 (last entry 17:00), closed Wednesdays. The site gives no ticket prices on the page read; entry was free for everyone on 17 December 2025.

FUNDING
Museu do Amanhã: mixed: run by a city-linked management body with corporate sponsors led by Itaú, Shell, Vale and Motiva, plus tax-incentive money
- Strategic sponsor: Itaú. Maintainers (mantenedores): Shell, Instituto Cultural Vale and Motiva. Sponsors: IBM and Tag. Strategic partner: Grupo Globo. Co-sponsors: Águas do Rio and Saint-Gobain. Supporters: Bloomberg, Engie, B3, White Martins, Granado, Mattos Filho and Porto. Municipal-incentive sponsors: Accenture, Fitch Ratings and the Prefeitura do Rio de Janeiro (ISS tax). (museum's supporters page, undated).
- Folha do Leste (December 2025) names Banco Santander Brasil, Shell, Vale, Motiva, IBM and TAG as sponsors, with Shell, Vale and Motiva supporting the Oceano exhibition.
- Laws used: the federal Lei de Incentivo à Cultura and the municipal incentive law (supporters page).
- Construction: RioOnWatch (2015, advocacy source) reports the Fundação Roberto Marinho, linked to Globo, as builder in a public-private partnership, with costs rising from R$70 million to R$213 million, and R$24 million received from a state environmental fund in 2011, a transfer the Public Ministry investigated.
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts or audited accounts for the museum.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Shell, maintainer)
- Shell is a named maintainer on the supporters page (undated; footer 2026). Folha do Leste (12 December 2025) names Shell among the Oceano supporters, and ecycle (2 December 2021) names Shell Brasil as a maintainer.
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter aimed at Shell's sponsorship of this museum.

Red flags: Mining disaster (Instituto Cultural Vale, maintainer)
- The Instituto Cultural Vale, launched in September 2020 (Correio Braziliense), is a maintainer on the supporters page. It presented the exhibition "Fruturos" at the museum in 2021 (ecycle); Folha do Leste names Vale among the Oceano supporters.
- Vale owned the Córrego do Feijão tailings dam at Brumadinho, which collapsed on 25 January 2019 and killed 270 people. In January 2020 prosecutors announced that Vale, TÜV Süd and 16 individuals would be charged with intentional homicide and environmental crimes. Vale said the mine met the latest standards (Wikipedia).
- On 5 November 2015 the Fundão tailings dam at Samarco's mine near Mariana failed, killing 19 people. Vale and BHP each held 50% of Samarco and in October 2024 signed a settlement of nearly US$30 billion with the Brazilian government (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a protest aimed at Vale's sponsorship of this museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The one substantial critique is from a favela-focused advocacy site and was written before the opening; it criticises the Porto Maravilha redevelopment and the builder's financing more than the sponsors.
1. RioOnWatch: Marta Ill-Raga, "A Critique of Rio's Museum of Tomorrow, Set to Open This Weekend" (17 December 2015)
URL: https://rioonwatch.org/?p=25724
- Calls the museum a flagship of the Porto Maravilha port redevelopment and the successor to a Guggenheim project on the same site that resident opposition ended.
- Argues the city's heritage approach overlooks the port area's history as the largest slave port in world history; the anthropologist Rolf Malungo de Souza says the city "only wants to preserve the festive part of black heritage".
- Advocacy outlet, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is partly a city project and partly sponsor-funded, with two named maintainers: Shell, a fossil fuel company, and the Instituto Cultural Vale, linked to the miner behind two dam disasters. The weak points are those maintainer roles and the undisclosed sponsor amounts. Worth watching: the Oceano exhibition, which Shell, Vale and Motiva support.
