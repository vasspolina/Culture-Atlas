SCOTT'S HUT, CAPE EVANS (TERRA NOVA HUT): Cape Evans, Ross Island, Antarctica
ID: scotts-terra-nova-hut-cape-evans
Website: https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/scotts-hut-cape-evans/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Hut on the north shore of Cape Evans, Ross Island, 20 km north of Scott's earlier hut at Hut Point (Wikipedia).
- Base of Robert Falcon Scott's British Antarctic (Terra Nova) Expedition of 1910-13. Scott and his men set out for the South Pole from here on 1 November 1911; the polar party died on the return (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Prefabricated in London and trial-erected in Lyttelton, New Zealand, it took nine days to build and housed 25 men of the shore party; at 50 ft by 25 ft it is the largest of the Ross Sea huts (Antarctic Heritage Trust). Its walls were insulated with seaweed sewn into quilts, and a stable for nineteen ponies was added on the north wall (Wikipedia).
- From 1915 to 1917 ten stranded men of Shackleton's Ross Sea Party lived in it. Left untouched after 1917, it was dug out of snow and ice by US expeditioners in 1956 (Wikipedia).
- The Trust has conserved its 11,500 artefacts (Antarctic Heritage Trust); The Art Newspaper reported the hut's conservation complete in April 2016 (Emily Sharpe).
- 2025-26 season: archaeologists excavated the shoreline, where material is increasingly at risk of being washed away, recovering a hand-quilted pony blanket and photographic developers used by the expedition photographer Herbert Ponting. Archaeologist Emma St Pierre found a ski pole carved with the initials of Petty Officer Patrick Keohane, one of the men who set out for the Pole with Scott (Antarctic Heritage Trust, March 2026).
- The Trust launched second-edition conservation management plans for the Ross Sea huts at Government House in September 2026; the full plans for the four Ross Island huts are due in November 2026 (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Visiting: Heritage Expeditions' 28-day Ross Sea voyage of 7 January to 3 February 2025 offered passengers visits inside the huts, with the Trust's former programme manager Al Fastier among the experts on board (Cruise Industry News).

FUNDING
Scott's Hut, Cape Evans: charity-run, close to half from the New Zealand government, with a British government grant toward its restoration
- Operator: the Antarctic Heritage Trust (New Zealand charity CC24071). Its income in the year to 30 June 2025 was NZ$3,433,987, NZ$1,536,000 of it government grants and contracts (Charities Register annual return).
- The Trust names the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and Antarctica New Zealand as its government partners and the Ministry for Culture and Heritage as its core funder (Antarctic Heritage Trust, September 2026).
- In February 2007 the British Government pledged £250,000 ($703,000) toward restoring Scott's and Shackleton's huts, passed through the Trust's British sister trust. The Trust's chair, former Cabinet minister Paul East, said it would let work start on Scott's hut, for which the Trust needed about $9 million, and thanked Prime Minister Helen Clark for pressing the case with her British counterpart (NZPA in the New Zealand Herald).
- The Getty Foundation made significant funding available for the Ross Sea Heritage Restoration Project around its 2002 launch (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Heritage Expeditions, the Trust's Inspiring Explorers expedition partner, gives a portion of each fare on its Ross Sea partnership voyage to the Trust's conservation work (Heritage Expeditions).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the hut is clean, and mostly public. The weak point is the Trust's tie to a cruise operator that sells trips to the huts. Worth watching: how the new conservation plan is paid for.
