THE WAR MEMORIAL OF KOREA (전쟁기념관): Seoul, South Korea
ID: the-war-memorial-of-korea
Website: https://www.warmemo.or.kr/ (checked 2026-11-20; the server's certificate chain could not be verified, so the pages were not read)

OVERVIEW
- War museum in Yongsan-dong, Yongsan-gu, on the former site of the army headquarters (Wikipedia).
- Building finished in December 1993; opened on 10 June 1994 to exhibit and memorialise Korea's military history, with the stated aims of preventing war through the lessons of the Korean War and of peaceful reunification (Wikipedia).
- The main building has three floors above ground and two below; galleries flanking the facade carry black marble walls with the names of Korean soldiers and police killed in the Korean War, the Vietnam War and later clashes, and of UN soldiers killed in Korea; the Statue of Brothers stands in the plaza (Wikipedia).
- Collection: more than 6,000 items, with large weapons, tanks and aircraft outdoors and a war history room, a history learning room and a large-weapons hall built for the 30th anniversary indoors (Seoul Metropolitan Government media hub, 2026).
- Free; open every day except Monday; guided tours can be booked online (Seoul media hub).

FUNDING
War Memorial of Korea: public: run by the Korea War Memorial Organization, a body under the Ministry of National Defense
- Operator: the Korea War Memorial Organization (전쟁기념사업회), a corporation set up by the War Memorial Organization Act to collect war records and build and run the memorial (the Act; Korea Times).
- The state and local governments may subsidise its costs within their budgets; individuals, companies and groups may donate; the state may lend it state property free of charge; it may charge admission and fees (the Act, articles 12 to 14).
- The Defense Minister approves its annual plan and budget, receives quarterly reports and audited accounts, and supervises it; the President appoints the chairman on the minister's recommendation, and the chairman, who also chairs the board, appoints the other directors except the vice-chairmen, including one nominee each from the defence, veterans and culture ministries (the Act; CBS Nocut News).
- Debt rose from 5.858 billion won in 2020 to 7.394 billion won in 2023, and the chairman's base salary rose 10 per cent to 128.64 million won in 2024 without a board vote, according to Democratic Party lawmaker Bu Seung-chan citing board minutes (The Fact, 4 October 2024). The organisation said its chairman was paid less than heads of similar bodies.

Red flags: 2026 audit finding against chairman Baek Seung-joo
- The Board of Audit and Inspection found that in July 2023 chairman Baek Seung-joo asked the controlling shareholder of a tenant in the memorial building to help set up a supporters' association, and the shareholder paid its entire 50 million won endowment in August 2023; public bodies may not raise donations directly (Kyunghyang Shinmun, 8 January 2026).
- It also found 25 private trips in his official car between June 2023 and January 2025, including to golf courses, and eight occasions when he had the driver take him to the airport, a child's wedding or outside lectures. The board told the Defense Minister to take "appropriate measures" (Kyunghyang Shinmun).
- A Defense Ministry official said on 19 January 2026 that the ministry had decided to discipline him and was weighing how (CBS Nocut News).

Red flags: 2026 use of China's account of the Korean War in education programmes
- On 9 June 2026 the organisation removed a poster for a children's programme that set South Korea's term for the war beside China's "War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea"; an official said the aim was critical analysis (The Korea Times; Munhwa Ilbo).
- It also dropped a memorial hall in Dandong, China, from a teacher-training itinerary after public complaints. On 15 June 2026 Defense Ministry spokesperson Chung Binna called the itinerary "a grave error", and Defense Minister Ahn Gyu-back ordered an inspection (Korea JoongAng Daily). No result has been published.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money under direct Defense Ministry control, with no private sponsor named. The weak point is governance: audit findings against its chairman, in a body whose board offers little check on him.
