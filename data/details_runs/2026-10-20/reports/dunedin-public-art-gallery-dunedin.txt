DUNEDIN PUBLIC ART GALLERY: Dunedin, New Zealand
ID: dunedin-public-art-gallery-dunedin
Website: https://dunedin.art.museum/supporters

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery in The Octagon in central Dunedin, close to the public library, Dunedin Town Hall and the Regent Theatre (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1884 by W. M. Hodgkins; Wikipedia calls it the first public art gallery in New Zealand. It was moved between several buildings, into Queen's Gardens in 1907 and a Logan Park building in 1927 designed by Edmund Anscombe, which Sir Percy and Lady Sargood bought and gave to the city as a memorial to their son, killed at Gallipoli (Wikipedia).
- The gallery moved to the refitted D.I.C. building in the Octagon in 1996 (Wikipedia).
- The Dunedin Public Art Gallery Society, the gallery's founding organisation, gave the gallery to Dunedin City Council in 1924, and the Council took over management in 1989 (Otago Daily Times, Rebecca Fox, 7 September 2022).
- Collection: New Zealand works from 1860 to the present, historical European art, Japanese prints and decorative arts, including what the gallery calls the only Monet in a New Zealand collection (gallery website). Cam McCracken has been director since 2012 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 10am to 5pm daily, closed Christmas Day (gallery website); admission is free and wheelchair access is listed (Kōtuia).

FUNDING
Dunedin Public Art Gallery: council-run: Dunedin City Council pays the running costs, with Creative New Zealand and private partners
- When the Society gave the gallery to Dunedin City Council in 1924, the Council provided funds for running costs while the Society kept administering the gallery and buying works; the Council took over running it in 1989 (Otago Daily Times, Rebecca Fox, "Achievement over controversy", 7 September 2022). The gallery's contact email is on the dcc.govt.nz domain.
- The Society raises money to buy works for the collection and supports a number of important acquisitions each year; it has received financial bequests and gifts of artworks through the Society (DPAG Society page).
- The supporters page lists three Gallery Partners: Creative New Zealand, Quartz Reef and Craigs Investment Partners.
- The same page lists, without a date, Founding Partners: the New Zealand Lotteries Board, Donaghys Ltd, Dunedin Electricity, Fletcher Challenge, Mobil Oil New Zealand Ltd, Otago Daily Times, Port Otago Ltd, the Alexander McMillan Trust, the Sargood Bequest, Trust Bank Otago Community Trust, Bank of New Zealand, Skeggs Group, Wilsons Distillers, Forsyth Barr Ltd, Fulton Hogan Ltd, Craigs Investment Partners, Palmer & Son Ltd, Telecom New Zealand, Waipori Power Generation Ltd and Wenita Forest Products Ltd.
- Collection Benefactors named on the page include Charles Brasch, the de Beer family, the Sargood family, F. H. D. Smythe and the Gordon Walters Estate.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public: Dunedin City Council has provided running-cost funds since the 1924 handover and has managed the gallery since 1989 (Otago Daily Times), Creative New Zealand is a Gallery Partner, and the Society and benefactors fund the collection. Mobil Oil New Zealand Ltd, the New Zealand Lotteries Board and other firms are listed as Founding Partners on the supporters page, which gives no date for them; the Gallery Partners are Creative New Zealand, Quartz Reef and Craigs Investment Partners.
