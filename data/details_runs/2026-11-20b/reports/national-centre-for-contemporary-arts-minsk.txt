NATIONAL CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ARTS: Minsk, Belarus
ID: national-centre-for-contemporary-arts-minsk
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- State art centre in Minsk with two venues: a Stalinist Empire style building at Prospekt Nezavisimosti 47 and a building at Nekrasova Street 3, designed by Iosif Zborovsky and built for artists in the early 1980s, with 940.7 square metres of exhibition halls in all (Russian Wikipedia).
- Created by a Council of Ministers resolution of 1 October 2015 as a "state cultural institution of mixed type" by merging the Museum of Modern Fine Arts (1997 to 2015) and the Creative Workshops "Centre of Contemporary Arts" (2011 to 2015) (Russian Wikipedia).
- The Nekrasova Street workshops were founded and led by the painter Mikhail Savitsky as three-year residencies for academy graduates; more than 120 artists trained there, and the workshops were wound up in 2011, after his death in 2010 (Russian Wikipedia).
- The Creative Workshops team received the President's special award for figures of art and culture in 2014 (Russian Wikipedia).
- Collection of 4,936 works by 892 Belarusian and foreign artists of the 20th and early 21st centuries, in painting, graphics, sculpture, decorative art, photography and posters (Russian Wikipedia).
- Directors: Natalya Sharangovich (2015 to 2017) and Siarhei Kryshtapovich (2018 to 2025) (Russian Wikipedia).
- It ran special projects at the Venice Biennale in 2015 and 2017, the National Prize in Fine Arts contest (2017, 2023), the Triennial of Young Artists (2022, 2025) and the "TekStilny Buket" textile festival from 2019 to 2025 (Russian Wikipedia). It also supported experimental projects in visual art, theatre and music and worked with young artists and curators (Nasha Niva).
- Belarus's 2019 Venice pavilion, "Exit" by Konstantin Selikhanov, curated by Olga Rybchinskaya at Spazio Liquido, had Kryshtapovich as commissioner (La Biennale di Venezia).
- Closure: the centre announced that exhibitions at Nekrasova Street 3 would stop from 22 June 2026 (Nasha Niva, 4 May 2026), and asked artists to collect their works by 24 July (Pozirk). Russian Wikipedia gives 29 October 2026 as its closing date.

FUNDING
National Centre for Contemporary Arts: state-funded: the Belarus state budget through the Ministry of Culture
- The centre was created by the Council of Ministers and answered to the Ministry of Culture (Russian Wikipedia).
- Council of Ministers Resolution No. 391 of 3 August 2026, published on the national legal portal on 5 August, ordered its liquidation and told the Ministry of Culture to finish the remaining steps by 30 November 2026 (Reform.news; Pozirk).
- The same resolution moved the budget money allocated to the centre: 45,771 roubles to the National Art Museum, and 44,316 roubles to an academy that Reform.news names as the Belarusian State Academy of Music and Pozirk as the Belarusian State Academy of Arts. Pozirk puts the second sum at about 15,150 US dollars (Reform.news; Pozirk).
- Reform.news reported in April 2026 that the collections and the Yakub Kolas Street venue would pass to the National Art Museum and the Nekrasova Street premises to the Belarusian State Academy of Arts; Pozirk notes the resolution itself does not say how the real estate will be used or who initiated the decision.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In May 2022 about 40 works were removed from the Art-Minsk festival at the Palace of Arts in Minsk; artists, among them Kirill Khlopov and Aliaksandr Belski, told Nasha Niva the works were taken down at the demand of Ministry of Culture officials (Nasha Niva; Pozirk).
- In 2026 independent Belarusian artists showed "Official. Unofficial. Belarus" at the Venice Biennale as a collateral event, because a national pavilion can only be entered through state bodies (Nasha Niva, 19 May 2026).
- The centre answered to the same Ministry of Culture and had commissioned the state's 2019 Venice pavilion.

BOTTOM LINE
A Belarusian state body funded through the Ministry of Culture, now wound up by government order with its leftover budget and collections passed to other state institutions. No private or foreign funder is on record.
