STÄDEL MUSEUM: Frankfurt am Main, Germany
ID: stadel-museum
Website: https://www.staedelmuseum.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Schaumainkai 63 on the Sachsenhausen bank of the Main, with a collection the museum describes as covering 700 years of art.
- Established in 1815 as a civic foundation under the will of the banker and spice merchant Johann Friedrich Städel, with a foundation deed dated 15 March 1815.
- Named for its founder, and carried by the non-profit foundation Städelsches Kunstinstitut.
- The main building of 1878 is by Oskar Sommer, with corner wings by Johannes Krahn (reopened 1963), a wing by Gustav Peichl (completed 1990) and the Gartenhallen by schneider+schumacher (2012), which added about 3,000 square metres for contemporary art.
- A five-member Board of Trustees chaired by Wolfgang Kirsch represents the foundation and appoints the director, Philipp Demandt.
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday to Sunday 10 am to 6 pm, Thursday 10 am to 9 pm, closed Monday. Admission is EUR 19, reduced EUR 17, families EUR 30, children under 12 free, and EUR 10 on Tuesdays from 3 to 6 pm.

FUNDING
Städel Museum: mainly self-funded private foundation: ticket income, patrons, foundations and corporate partners, with city and state grants on top
- The museum states that 85 per cent of its total budget comes from its own resources: visitors, private patrons, foundations, corporate partners and the Städelscher Museums-Verein (stated by the space, March 2026).
- The City of Frankfurt and the State of Hesse add public funds.
- Corporate partners listed in November 2026: ADAC Hessen-Thüringen, Allianz Global Investors, Bloomberg L.P., BMW AG, Brunswick Group, Deutsche Bank AG, Deutsche Börse AG, d-fine, dm-drogerie markt, DZ Bank, EY Deutschland, Fraport AG, Fresenius, KPMG AG, PPI AG and The Florentin.
- Project funders listed include the federal culture commissioner (BKM), Kulturstiftung der Länder, Hessische Kulturstiftung, Kulturfonds Frankfurt RheinMain, Ernst von Siemens Kunststiftung, Art Mentor Foundation Lucerne, Bank of America Art Conservation Project, Commerzbank-Stiftung, Dagmar-Westberg-Stiftung, Stiftung Polytechnische Gesellschaft and the Hermann Reemtsma Stiftung.
- The Hermann Reemtsma Stiftung is tobacco-family money: Hermann-Hinrich Reemtsma (1935 to 2020) set it up in 1988 in memory of his father, Hermann F. Reemtsma (the foundation's own page). He worked in the management of the family's cigarette business, was a shareholder and sat on the supervisory board of the Reemtsma group from 1996 until 2002 (German Wikipedia).
- On 7 March 2026 the museum launched a campaign to raise the endowment of the Städelsches Kunstinstitut to at least EUR 100 million. It reported over EUR 10 million in contributions and pledges since 2023 from private individuals, Frankfurt foundations and the Städelscher Museums-Verein.
- The 2012 Gartenhallen extension cost EUR 52 million, half from private contributions and half from public sources (stated by the space).

Red flags: corporate partner Deutsche Bank and its 2020 penalty over Jeffrey Epstein's accounts
- Deutsche Bank AG is a corporate partner and a project funder of the museum.
- On 7 July 2020 the New York State Department of Financial Services imposed a USD 150 million penalty on the bank for compliance failures in its handling of accounts linked to Jeffrey Epstein and in its correspondent banking for Danske Bank Estonia and FBME Bank.

BOTTOM LINE
The Städel earns or raises most of its own money, and the public share comes from its own city and state. The weak point is a corporate partner, a bank penalised in 2020 over a convicted sex offender's accounts, and a cigarette family's foundation sits among the project funders. Worth watching: who gives the large sums to the endowment campaign.
