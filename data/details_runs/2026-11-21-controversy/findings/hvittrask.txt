Red flags: 2024 planned closure under state budget cuts
- On 24 October 2024 the Finnish Heritage Agency (Museovirasto) announced that, after cooperation negotiations seeking 3.2 million euros of cuts to its 2025 operating costs, the National Museum of Finland would close Seurasaari, Hvitträsk, Louhisaari and Langinkoski "toistaiseksi" ("until further notice", translated) and furlough about 150 staff (Museovirasto press release via STT).
- Kulttuurivihkot (editorial by Elias Krohn, 27 December 2024) wrote that the plan to close the four sites raised the largest public outcry ("Isoin julkinen kohu") of the cuts, that culture minister Sari Multala questioned whether the agency had followed the principle of least harm, and that "Voimakkaan julkisen paineen seurauksena" ("as a result of strong public pressure", translated) she proposed 2.1 million euros of extra money for the agency.
- On 21 November 2024 the government proposed, in a supplementary budget, 1 million euros of extra operating money for the agency, taken from arts and culture promotion grants, so that Seurasaari and some other museums would not close (MTV Uutiset, Jari Peltola). The supplementary budget carried the 1 million euros, which the agency said would prevent the closures for 2025 (Kulttuurivihkot).
