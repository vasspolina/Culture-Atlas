CENTRO CULTURAL TIJUANA: Tijuana, Mexico
ID: centro-cultural-tijuana-tijuana
Website: https://www.cecut.gob.mx (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Federal cultural centre, known as CECUT, at Paseo de los Héroes 9350 in Tijuana's Zona Urbana Río.
- Construction began on 25 June 1980 and it opened on 20 October 1982 as the Centro Cultural FONAPAS (Wikipedia, Spanish).
- Designed by Pedro Ramírez Vázquez, with Manuel Rosen Morrison executing the project and Ramírez directing construction (Wikipedia, Spanish); its IMAX dome cinema, "La Bola", opened in 1988 with 307 seats.
- On site: the Museo de las Californias (2000), the El Cubo gallery, an aquarium (2012) with more than 500 species, the 270-seat Sala Carlos Monsiváis (2011) and a 6,400 square metre botanical garden. It had about 1.7 million visitors in 2015.
- Run by the Compañía Operadora del Centro Cultural y Turístico de Tijuana, S.A. de C.V. (its budget reports), and administered by the federal Secretaría de Cultura (Wikipedia). The federal Secretaría de Cultura announced Miriam Gabriela García Aguirre as director on 14 March 2025 (Zeta).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 7pm; tickets are sold through taquillacecut.com.mx. No admission prices or accessibility information were found on the site.

FUNDING
Centro Cultural Tijuana: mainly public: Mexico's federal government
- The federal subsidy was 100,003,368 pesos in 2025; the 2026 budget bill proposed 102,802,450 pesos, about the same as the 100,466,164 pesos approved in 2017 (Zeta, September 2025).
- Federal money approved was paid out at lower amounts from 2019 to 2023; in 2020, 100,947,175 pesos were approved and 76,799,007 paid (Zeta, September 2024).
- Own income from tickets and rentals was 32,008,146 pesos in 2018, fell to 6,947,883 in 2020 and recovered to 34,877,567 in 2023 (Zeta).
- A private board, the Patronato del CECUT, supports the centre; its board named in 2018 was chaired by Víctor A. Vilaplana (Zeta, April 2018). No reporting found of what the Patronato pays for or how much.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors.

Red flags: artists' boycott, about 2008 to 2011
- More than 250 Tijuana artists and writers boycotted CECUT for about three years and demanded that director Virgilio Muñoz resign (KJZZ, July 2011).
- Muñoz had been accused of corruption as head of the state immigration agency in 1994 and was arrested in 1995 and released the same week (KJZZ).
- Under Muñoz the centre fired seven curators and administrators and showed less work by Tijuana artists (KJZZ).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in the art press. Criticism comes from Tijuana's weekly Zeta and public radio, and concerns appointments, budgets and programming.
1. KJZZ (Fronteras Desk): Ruxandra Guidi, "Tijuana's Artists Boycott Museum" (2011)
URL: https://www.kjzz.org/content/8269/tijuanas-artists-boycott-museum
- Reports the boycott by more than 250 artists and writers and their demand for Muñoz's resignation.
- Names the firing of seven curators and administrators and the fall in shows by Tijuana artists.
2. Zeta: Redacción, "El dedazo de Vianka R. Santana en el CECUT" (2019)
URL: https://zetatijuana.com/2019/01/el-dedazo-de-vianka-r-santana-en-el-cecut/
- Argues the federal culture secretary Alejandra Frausto appointed Galia Vianka Robles Santana without any debate of proposals.
- The shortlist sent by Baja California collectives and artists was ignored; Robles had taken part in the 2018 presidential campaign.
3. Zeta: Enrique Mendoza, "CECUT 2018-2024: el retroceso" (2024)
URL: https://zetatijuana.com/2024/09/cecut-2018-2024-el-retroceso/
- Federal cuts of about 48.9 million pesos from 2019 to 2023 and a fall in own income.
- El Cubo held no internationally significant exhibitions; regional artists lost a dedicated space and the book fair lost its earmarked budget of about 3 million pesos a year.
- Poet Eduardo Hurtado said official Jaime Cháidez had a conflict of interest; the piece also covers the July 2024 death of an engineer working at the centre, under investigation by the state prosecutor.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is federal and plain: a subsidy of about 100 million pesos a year plus ticket and rental income, with a private Patronato whose giving is not published. The weak points are governance: a 2019 appointment made without debate of proposals, according to Zeta, a three-year artists' boycott reported in 2011, and programme cuts reported for 2018 to 2024.
