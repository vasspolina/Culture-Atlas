CHILLIDA LEKU: Hernani, Spain
ID: chillida-leku-hernani
Website: https://www.museochillidaleku.com

OVERVIEW
- Private museum and sculpture park devoted to the Basque sculptor Eduardo Chillida (1924 to 2002), around the Zabalaga farmhouse in Hernani, about 10 km from San Sebastián (Arquitectura Viva; The Art Newspaper places the museum near Hernani).
- Chillida and his wife Pilar Belzunce founded it in 2000 in a converted 16th-century farmhouse (The Art Newspaper). Onda Vasca states that the land of the Zabalaga farmhouse was acquired in 1983.
- "Leku" is the Basque word for "place" (Elephant, Joe Lloyd, 26 May 2020).
- The Zabalaga caserio was built in 1594 and belonged to the Churruca family; Chillida emptied it and tore down its interior walls to reveal its "true space" (Arquitectura Viva).
- The 11-hectare site holds around 40 monumental sculptures. For the 2019 reopening the architect Luis Laplace led a refurbishment that added lighting, insulation, a café and better access for visitors with reduced mobility, and the garden designer Piet Oudolf redesigned the landscaping (The Art Newspaper).
- The museum website lists an archive and library and a restaurant.
- It has been open only for guided tours by appointment since 2011; in February 2019 it announced a full reopening on 17 April 2019 under the director Mireia Massagué (Hannah McGivern, "Eduardo Chillida's Basque museum and sculpture park announces full reopening", The Art Newspaper, 21 February 2019).
- Visiting: open Monday and Thursday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Tuesday and Wednesday; €14 standard, €10 reduced, free for children under 8; an annual pass is sold (museum website).

FUNDING
Chillida Leku: private: the Chillida family with financial support from the gallery Hauser & Wirth
- The museum has been open only for guided tours by appointment since 2011, and cited a "recurring deficit" during the economic crisis (The Art Newspaper).
- In 2016 talks with the provincial government of Gipuzkoa ended; Markel Olano of the provincial government said the cost of reopening was too high for Gipuzkoa. ETB reported that the provincial council and the Basque government both declined to buy the museum (Orain, ETB, 30 November 2017).
- In November 2017 Hauser & Wirth announced it would represent Chillida's estate and "develop a new and sustainable model for Chillida Leku", and The Art Newspaper states that it has given financial support to the estate. Elephant states that the gallery "funded the Leku's restoration and reopening".
- Luis Chillida, president of the Fundación Chillida-Belzunce, said without Hauser & Wirth the museum "would be a mausoleum" (translation, Onda Vasca, 19 August 2022).
- The museum website's copyright line reads "Sucesión Eduardo Chillida" and it offers support options for individuals and companies, including corporate sponsorship (stated by the museum).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Chillida Leku is private: the Chillida family's estate, supported financially by the Hauser & Wirth gallery, which announced in 2017 that it would represent the estate. Gipuzkoa's provincial council and the Basque government declined to buy it, according to ETB.
