ROVANIEMI ART MUSEUM (KORUNDI HOUSE OF CULTURE): Rovaniemi, Finland
ID: rovaniemi-art-museum-korundi
Website: https://korundi.fi (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum of the City of Rovaniemi, housed with the Lapland Chamber Orchestra in Korundi House of Culture at Lapinkävijäntie 4, within walking distance of the city centre and next to the Revontuli shopping centre (korundi.fi).
- The building is a red-brick mail truck depot from 1933, one of the few buildings in Rovaniemi that survived the Second World War; after the war it was extended with bricks gathered from the ruins (korundi.fi, Yle).
- The museum opened in the depot in 1986, converted by the architect Juhani Pallasmaa; his office also designed the 2009 to 2010 extension with a concert hall, and Korundi opened in 2011 (Yle).
- Its core is the Jenny and Antti Wihuri Foundation Collection of nearly 4,000 works of Finnish contemporary art from the 1940s on; the city's own collections add about 2,000 works, centred on artists born in or living in Lapland (korundi.fi).
- Since 2020 it is the museum with regional responsibility for 12 Lapland municipalities, and it documents Lappish and Sámi art with the University of Lapland (korundi.fi).
- Riitta Kuusikko is director. The museum counted 99,671 visits in 2025 (Finnish Heritage Agency); its Reidar Särestöniemi centenary show (24 January to 1 June 2025) drew over 25,000 visitors (korundi.fi).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 11 to 18; tickets EUR 13, 10 or 7; free entry on Thursdays 16 to 18. Lifts serve both exhibition floors, assistants enter free and there is an accessible toilet (korundi.fi).

FUNDING
Rovaniemi Art Museum: mainly public: the City of Rovaniemi, with a state subsidy and a private foundation's collection
- The museum is owned by the municipality. In 2025 it spent EUR 1,763,684, including EUR 772,097 on salaries and EUR 682,298 on premises; recorded income was EUR 872,796 (Finnish Heritage Agency museum statistics).
- State money in 2025: EUR 310,218 statutory state share under the Museums Act and EUR 18,268 in state grants; it also received EUR 206,269 in EU funding and EUR 49,184 in other operating grants (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- Its own earnings in 2025 were EUR 288,857, of which admissions gave EUR 209,252 (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- The Jenny and Antti Wihuri Foundation decided on 15 August 1983 to give its collection, then about 500 works, to the city (korundi.fi). The foundation still buys about 60 works a year for it (Wihuri Foundation).
- The Wihuri Foundation paid for the architecture design of the 2009 to 2010 extension, and in March 2024 gave the city EUR 150,000 to design the renovation of the 1986 museum wing (Yle).
- The foundation was set up in 1942 by Antti Wihuri, who founded a shipping company in 1916, and his wife Jenny. It pays grants from investment returns on about EUR 560 million (end of 2024) and owns 22.4% of Wihuri Packaging Oy (Wihuri Foundation).
- The building is rented from the city company Rovaniemen Kaupunkikiinteistöt. In May 2025 the city's welfare board took up a needs assessment for renovating the 1986 wing at over EUR 5 million, a sum that needs a city council decision; the cost falls on the city through rent or direct investment, and work was planned for May 2026 to summer 2027 (Yle).
- The Rovaniemi Art Museum Friends association, founded in 2005 with nearly a hundred members, raises money for the museum's outreach work (korundi.fi).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: a city museum on public funds, with a private Finnish foundation behind its main collection and its building design, and no flagged funder on record. Worth watching: the renovation bill.
