NATIONAL MUSEUM OF DAMASCUS (المتحف الوطني بدمشق): Damascus, Syria
ID: national-museum-of-damascus
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- National archaeology museum on Shukri al-Quwatli Street, west of the city centre, between Damascus University and the Sulaymaniyya Takiyya (English Wikipedia). Arabic name: al-Mathaf al-Watani bi-Dimashq (المتحف الوطني بدمشق; romanisation ours).
- Founded in 1919, during King Faisal's Arab Kingdom of Syria, under the Ministry of Education at the Al-Adiliyah Madrasa; Wikipedia calls it the oldest cultural heritage institution in Syria.
- Building: the present building went up in 1936 beside the Sulaymaniyya Takiyya, with a facade made from the front walls of the Umayyad desert palace Qasr al-Hayr al-Gharbi; the official opening was in 1950, and a three-storey wing for Islamic-period and contemporary Syrian art was added in 1953 (English Wikipedia).
- Collection: over 300,000 items, among them the Dura-Europos synagogue of 245 CE, moved to Damascus in the 1930s (English Wikipedia).
- War closures: the museum closed in 2012, when staff hid over 300,000 artefacts, and reopened four of its five wings on 29 October 2018. It closed again on 7 December 2024, when the Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums asked Hayat Tahrir al-Sham for guards, and reopened on 8 January 2025 (English Wikipedia).
- Theft: on the night of 9 November 2025 thieves broke in through a door of the classical department; an official told the Associated Press six marble statues were taken, and the Damascus police chief, Brig. Gen. Osama Atkeh, told SANA that guards and others were being questioned (Associated Press, 11 November 2025). A Culture Ministry statement of 14 November listed six small Roman-era statues of Venus in marble, alabaster and plaster (Arab News, 8 May 2026).
- Reopening: on 1 December 2025 the Directorate-General reopened the museum after cleaning and refurbishment, with a new system of high-definition cameras and alarms linked to a round-the-clock control room (SANA, 1 December 2025, in Arabic; translation ours).

FUNDING
National Museum of Damascus: public: Syria's Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums, with a UNESCO emergency project from 2025
- Operator: the Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums runs the museum and carried out the 2025 refurbishment and new security system (SANA, 1 December 2025; English Wikipedia).
- UNESCO: in June 2025 UNESCO resumed work in Syria after 14 years with an emergency project for the museum, with an initial budget of $175,000 (North Press, 11 June 2025). The project covers cultural first aid, training and digitisation of heritage (Finestre sull'Arte, 14 June 2025).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Syrian state money with a small UNESCO grant. The weak point is security.
