PALAZZO DELLE ESPOSIZIONI: Rome, Italy
ID: palazzo-delle-esposizioni
Website: https://www.palazzoesposizioniroma.it (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition palace on Via Nazionale with a nineteenth-century Salon design by Pio Piacentini, run today as Palazzo Esposizioni Roma (Palaexpo service charter, 2026).
- In 1930 the architects Del Debbio and Aschieri restructured it for the national Quadriennale art exhibitions, the first of which opened there on 3 January 1931 (museum website).
- From 28 October 1932 to 28 October 1934 it housed the National Fascist Party's Exhibition of the Fascist Revolution, directed by Dino Alfieri and opened by Mussolini, behind a facade by Adalberto Libera and Mario De Renzi; it drew 3,854,927 visitors (Wikipedia).
- Palaexpo closed it in July 2002 for restoration and reopened it in September 2007 (Palaexpo service charter).
- Programme: large exhibitions on the main floor, monographic and themed shows upstairs, photography, and a lower-level space for exhibitions with performance, dance and music (museum website).
- Leadership: president Marco Delogu, vice president Ivana Della Portella, director general Fabio Merosi (Palaexpo service charter).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10.00 to 20.00, last entry an hour before closing; 15 euros, 12 reduced for under-27s, over-65s and teachers, 7 for ages 7 to 18, free for under-6s and disabled visitors with a companion; free for under-30s on the first Wednesday afternoon of the month (Palaexpo service charter).

FUNDING
Palazzo delle Esposizioni: public: Roma Capitale, through its Azienda Speciale Palaexpo
- Owner and operator: Azienda Speciale Palaexpo, an instrumental body owned by Roma Capitale with legal personality and its own budget, set up in 1998 to run the Palazzo (museum website; Palaexpo service charter). A three-year service contract with the city covers the Palazzo, the MACRO museum, the Museo delle Periferie and, for 2026, the programme of La Pelanda and pavilions 9a and 9b at the Mattatoio (Palaexpo service charter).
- Scope over time: Palaexpo also ran the Scuderie del Quirinale until 2016, when the culture ministry's in-house company Ales took it over, and the Casa del Jazz until 2018, when it passed to Fondazione Musica per Roma (Palaexpo service charter).
- Cost to the city: about 10 million euros a year in 2011 (Artribune).
- Staff and board: 62 employees at 31 December 2025; the city's executive set board pay at 59,835.90 euros a year for the president and 10,800 euros for each of four members (Palaexpo anti-corruption plan 2026 to 2028).
- Facade lighting: Areti, the Acea group's electricity distributor in Rome, carried out a 40,000 euro LED lighting of the facade with Palaexpo and Roma Capitale, switched on in May 2025 (Acea).
- Palaexpo says it has one of the highest rates of self-financing in Italy and Europe (stated by the space).

Red flags: 2021 Court of Auditors finding and planned merger into Zètema
- The Corte dei conti, in deliberation 47/2021/GEST, pointed to activities that partly overlap with other city culture bodies; the city assembly then committed, in 2021 and 2022, to study a merger with its in-house company Zètema Progetto Cultura (Giunta Capitolina deliberation 81, 2026).
- In December 2024 the assembly set the aim of a "total merger by incorporation" of Palaexpo into Zètema, and in December 2025 it confirmed that course (Giunta Capitolina memorandum 116, 2025; deliberation 81, 2026).

Red flags: 2011 workers' letter against handing the Azienda to a private body
- Palaexpo workers wrote to mayor Gianni Alemanno against a plan to "rationalise" the Azienda that could hand its core cultural production to a private party, and disputed a 25 July statement in which Fondazione Roma praised its own efficiency in running it; they asked for any change to go through an international, transparent public tender (Artribune, 2011).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean municipal money, with a small utility-company contribution to the building. The weak points are governance: auditors' doubts about overlapping city culture bodies and an unfinished merger that would end Palaexpo as a separate body.
