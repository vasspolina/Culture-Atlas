SURSOCK MUSEUM: Beirut, Lebanon
ID: sursock-museum
Website: https://sursock.museum (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in the Ashrafieh district of Beirut, forty metres from the top of the Saint Nicolas stairs, a few steps from Gemmayzeh Street (stated by the museum).
- Housed in the villa Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock built in 1912; on his death in 1952 he left it as an art museum to the citizens of Lebanon (stated by the museum); Wikipedia says to the city of Beirut.
- Nine years passed before the will was carried out: President Camille Chamoun decreed the villa a guesthouse for visiting heads of state. The museum opened on 18 November 1961 with the Salon d'Automne (stated by the museum).
- Closed for an expansion by Jean-Michel Wilmotte and Jacques Abou Khaled that raised the floor area from 1,500 to 8,500 square metres; reopened on 8 October 2015 (stated by the museum, Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, a library and archives, a store and a restaurant.
- The 4 August 2020 port explosion, 800 metres away, severely damaged it; it reopened on 26 May 2023, and again on 20 December 2024 after closing in October 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm; admission is free; all galleries and facilities are wheelchair accessible (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Sursock Museum: donor-funded: Lebanese patrons, Friends memberships, foreign government and heritage grants
- Patrons in 2026: the Robert A. Matta Foundation and Kamal F. Tabet. Donors in 2026: Nicolas and Michele Garzouzi, the Alexis and Anne-Marie Habib Foundation, Philippe and Zaza Jabre, Omar and Ghalia Al Qattan. Friends of the Museum are tiered Platinum, Gold and Silver; the page gives no amounts (stated by the museum).
- 2026 operational sponsors: Commercial Insurance, Tinol, Mitsulift. Energy sponsors: Energy Assistance, Fondation CMA CGM, Pierre Dammous & Partners and Fondation Engie. 2025 grants: the Italian development agency and the Institut francais du Liban.
- After the 2020 explosion the museum raised 2,376,751 US dollars made up of 2,086,031 from grants, 58,231 from institutions, 152,591 from fundraisers, 40,000 from corporations and 39,897 from individuals (stated by the museum).
- Grants named: ALIPH 500,000 dollars; French Ministry of Culture 500,000 euros; Government of Italy and UNESCO 965,000 dollars; Goethe-Institut 10,000 dollars; Arab Fund for Arts and Culture and Culture Resource 40,000 dollars. Corporate donor: DHL.
- No reporting found of a Lebanese state or municipal operating grant, or of published financial statements.

Red flags: Fossil fuel sponsor
- Fondation Engie is listed as an energy sponsor in the 2026 section of the museum's supporters page, with no amount or start year (stated by the museum).
- Engie is a French utility with Europe's largest gas distribution network and the world's fifth largest LNG portfolio; natural gas was 55 percent of its installed capacity in 2018 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The 2020 to 2022 section of the supporters page says Shaikha Mai bint Mohammed Al-Khalifa, President of the Bahrain Authority for Culture and Antiquities, raised 98,231 US dollars for the museum on behalf of the Arab Regional Centre for World Heritage (stated by the museum). The page gives no exact date and does not say the Authority itself paid.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Sursock is mostly Lebanese private patrons and foreign heritage grants, with no state operating funder named. The weak points are Fondation Engie, the foundation of a gas utility, listed in 2026, and the 98,231 dollars raised through the president of the Bahrain Authority for Culture and Antiquities in 2020 to 2022. Worth watching: the missing financial statements and the size of the energy sponsorships.
