ARTSPACE SYDNEY: Sydney, Australia
ID: artspace-sydney
Website: https://www.artspace.org.au

OVERVIEW
- Independent, not-for-profit contemporary art space at The Gunnery, 43 to 51 Cowper Wharf Roadway, Woolloomooloo (Artspace site).
- Established in 1983 as an artist-run gallery for emerging and early-career artists, in its original home at 11 Randle Street, Surry Hills, a hat factory built in 1912 (Wikipedia). It moved to The Gunnery in 1992 (Artspace site, Wikipedia).
- Building: erected around 1900 as a bulk store for the Sydney Morning Herald, used by the Commonwealth for defence until 1948 and squatted by artists in the 1970s and early 1980s. In 1989 the NSW Government proposed selling it; artists challenged the sale in the Land & Environment Court, and in 1991 the government proposed a visual arts centre there (Wikipedia).
- A redevelopment led by Dunn and Hillam Architects moved the entrance from Cowper Wharf Road to Forbes Street Plaza, restored all three floors and added galleries, studios and universal access; Artspace reopened in December 2023 (ArtAsiaPacific, Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions, a rent-free Studio Program of ten studios for Sydney-based artists on a two-year model, the Ideas Platform, a Banner Series on the building, commissions, publications and an archive (Artspace annual report 2025).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 17:00; exhibitions are unticketed (Artspace site, annual report 2025). Victor Wang has been Executive Director since July 2025 (annual report 2025).

FUNDING
Artspace Sydney: government-supported with a benefactor programme: Create NSW and Creative Australia, plus private benefactors
- The organisation states that it receives government support from the Federal Government through Creative Australia and from the NSW Government through Create NSW, supplemented by partners and benefactors (Artspace site).
- The 2025 annual report records a $350,000 increase in Create NSW funding from 2026, continued Creative Australia investment, a new partnership with TWT Property Group and a major grant from the Fidelity Asia Pacific Foundation.
- The NSW Government committed over AUD 5 million to upgrade The Gunnery (ArtAsiaPacific, 30 November 2020); the chair's foreword to the 2025 report credits the redevelopment to "the generous support of the NSW Government". The 1992 renovation cost AUD 1.5 million, supported by the state government and the industrialist and arts patron Franco Belgiorno-Nettis (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Benefactors listed on the Artspace site (current as of 13 August 2026): Luminaries are Daniel Boyd, Andrew Cameron AM and Cathy Cameron, TWT Property Group and one anonymous donor. Vision-Makers are Anita and Luca Belgiorno-Nettis, Michelle and Guido Belgiorno-Nettis, and The Medich Foundation. Catalysts are the Neilson Foundation and The Keir Foundation. The Turnbull Foundation, Orange & Sardines Foundation and Wheen Family Foundation are also named.
- The 2025 annual report thanks the Anita and Luca Belgiorno-Nettis Foundation and Michelle and Guido Belgiorno-Nettis for supporting the Franco Belgiorno-Nettis Archive, which Artspace houses.
- Self-generated revenue was 28% of annual turnover in 2025, 32% in 2024 and 40% in 2023; the reserves ratio was 20%, 42% and 32% (annual report 2025).
- Board in 2025: chair Lisa Chung AM, treasurer Leisa Sadler, Daniel Boyd, Julie Rrap, Andrew Martin and Alenka Tindale, with Ramesh Mario Nithiyendran, Zoe Paulsen and Victor Wang (annual report 2025). Chung, Tindale and Boyd also appear on the benefactor list.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2014 the Sydney academic Matt Kiem began a call to boycott the 19th Biennale of Sydney with an open letter of 4 February; 28 of the 90 participating artists then wrote to the Biennale board asking it to end its sponsorship arrangement with Transfield, and the board replied that it would not sever ties (Hyperallergic, Jillian Steinhauer, 21 February 2014).
- Transfield Services held a contract worth AUD 1.2 billion to provide and administer offshore detention facilities on Nauru and Manus Island (Apollo, Tim Walsh, 14 March 2014). Luca Belgiorno-Nettis, executive director of Transfield Holdings, resigned as chairman of the Biennale, and the organisers said they would sever ties with Transfield (Apollo, Maggie Gray, 8 March 2014).
- Walsh's Apollo article describes the debate over arts sponsorship in Australia as "riddled with difficult questions and double standards".
- Connection to Artspace: Artspace was one of the venues of the 2014 Biennale (Apollo, Walsh). The campaign was aimed at the Biennale and its sponsor.

BOTTOM LINE
Artspace is supported by the NSW and federal governments through Create NSW and Creative Australia, with private benefactors and a property-group partnership on top. TWT Property Group is a new multi-year partner and a Luminary benefactor. The 2014 Biennale of Sydney boycott over Transfield concerned the Biennale, of which Artspace was a venue.
