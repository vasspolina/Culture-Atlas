DEUTSCHES HISTORISCHES MUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: deutsches-historisches-museum-berlin
Website: https://www.dhm.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Germany's national history museum at Unter den Linden 2, Berlin (stated by the space).
- Founded by the Federal Republic and the Land of Berlin for Berlin's 750th anniversary; Chancellor Helmut Kohl and Governing Mayor Eberhard Diepgen signed the founding document on 28 October 1987 (stated by the space).
- In 1990 the buildings and collection of the GDR's Museum für Deutsche Geschichte passed to it, including the Zeughaus, begun in 1695 (stated by the space).
- Buildings: the Zeughaus and I. M. Pei's exhibition hall next to it, opened in 2003 (stated by the space).
- The Zeughaus has been closed since 2021, when damage to its air conditioning turned a repair into a full renovation of climate systems, IT and energy use, with a second entrance and additional toilets (Berliner Zeitung, 23 January 2026).
- President: Raphael Gross, appointed by the Board of Trustees on 24 November 2016, in office since 15 April 2017. The collection holds some 1,000,000 objects (stated by the space).
- Current exhibitions include "Controversial Kinship. Colonial and National Socialist Violence" (16 October 2026 to 11 April 2027) and "Objects. History. Stories" (8 May 2026 to 31 October 2027) (stated by the space).
- Visiting: Pei building daily 10 am to 6 pm; 7 euros, reduced 3.50 euros, free up to 18; a Bloomberg Connects guide app offers voice-overs, subtitles and font size adjustment (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Deutsches Historisches Museum: public: the German federal government, through the Commissioner for Culture and the Media
- The museum's site states it is "Funded by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media" (stated by the space).
- A federal law of 21 December 2008 made the museum a federal foundation under public law, taking over the assets of the former Deutsches Historisches Museum GmbH; the foundation also carries the Stiftung Flucht, Vertreibung, Versöhnung (DHM law, gesetze-im-internet.de).
- Its Board of Trustees has five members from the federal government, the Bundestag and the Länder, chaired by Dr. Konrad Schmidt-Werthern (stated by the space).
- Federal budget: the 2027 federal culture budget of 2.58 billion euros gives the museum an extra 1.5 million euros for its exhibition and education work (Federal Government press release, 13 November 2026).
- Building: the federal property agency BImA owns the Zeughaus and the Federal Office for Building and Regional Planning runs the renovation (Tagesspiegel, 6 May 2026). Gross said the money for an interim display of the new permanent exhibition was not yet settled (Berliner Zeitung, 23 January 2026). The Humboldt Forum is under discussion as an interim site (Tagesspiegel).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: A 2017 exhibition review and 2026 coverage of the delayed Zeughaus renovation.
1. Contemporary And (C&): Jessica Aimufua, "Where Are You Going, White Man?" (10 April 2017)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/where-are-you-going-white-man
- Review of "German Colonialism: Fragments Past and Present": calls it "an undeniably German and therefore Eurocentric perspective on colonial rule"; the Namibian and Tanzanian curators "were only called in" when the concept was largely complete.
- Calls the exhibition's approach to colonial figures dangerous because it equates satirical exaggeration with racist stereotyping, and its accompanying information "largely superficial".
- About 500 exhibits on 1,000 square metres crowd the space, and the installation on the Black movement in Germany is "almost hidden in the far corner of the exhibition hall".
2. Berliner Zeitung: Kai Kleinwächter, "Sanierung außer Kontrolle: Zeughaus bleibt noch für Jahre zu" (23 January 2026)
URL: https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/article/sanierung-ausser-kontrolle-zeughaus-bleibt-noch-fuer-jahre-zu-10015732
- Citing rbb24, it reports Gross does not expect the Zeughaus to reopen before 2031 and calls 2031 a positive scenario.
- The delay renews debate over escalating Berlin building projects.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 13 June 2025 the museum's board gave the last approval needed for the settlement between the public authorities and the House of Hohenzollern; all objects whose ownership was disputed pass to a new non-profit Stiftung Hohenzollernscher Kunstbesitz and stay in the collections of the DHM, the SPK and the SPSG (press release of 13 June 2025, published by the DHM).
- The new foundation's council has nine members: six from federal, Berlin and Brandenburg authorities and three from the House of Hohenzollern, including Georg Friedrich Prinz von Preußen; the family had withdrawn all pending property and press-law suits before the talks began (same press release).
- The DHM's president sits on the new foundation's three-member executive board.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: the museum runs on German federal money. Worth watching are the open-ended Zeughaus renovation and the Hohenzollern family's say over objects in the collection.
