KUNSTMUSEUM DEN HAAG: The Hague, Netherlands
ID: kunstmuseum-den-haag
Website: https://www.kunstmuseum.nl

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum in The Hague, home to the largest Mondriaan collection in the world (City of The Hague), and, according to Wikipedia, Mondrian's last work, Victory Boogie-Woogie.
- Founded on 29 May 1866 by a group of artists and collectors as the Museum voor Moderne Kunst. It was known as the Haags Gemeentemuseum until 1998 and as Gemeentemuseum Den Haag until September 2019 (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by H.P. Berlage and built from 1931 to 1935. Berlage died in 1934 and his son-in-law E.E. Strasser finished the work (Wikipedia).
- The City of The Hague owns the building, a national monument, and the art collection; the museum manages the collection on the city's behalf (City of The Hague, 30 September 2026).
- The collection holds about 165,000 works, including Delftware, Persian ceramics, prints, fashion and musical instruments (Wikipedia). The museum draws about 450,000 visitors a year, against the 85,000 the building was designed for (City of The Hague).
- Visiting: open daily 10:00 to 17:00 except Monday, with time slots to reserve in advance. Full price is 22 euros; ages 19 to 25 and students pay 12 euros; under 19 and Museumkaart holders enter free. The museum states that wheelchair users are welcome and has two free disabled parking spaces.

FUNDING
Kunstmuseum Den Haag: the City of The Hague, with a lottery, public funds and private funds
- Gemeente Den Haag makes a substantial contribution; on average half of its Kunstenplan contribution goes to the upkeep of the building and collection (museum subsidy page).
- VriendenLoterij pays a fixed 500,000 euros a year (museum subsidy page).
- Mondriaan Fonds gives a structural contribution and support for projects; in 2022 it helped fund the purchase of Paula Rego's triptych "De Kussenman" (museum subsidy page).
- Vereniging Rembrandt contributes to several acquisitions (museum subsidy page, no year given), and Aronson Antiquairs has supported the Delftware collection since 2017 (museum subsidy page).
- Other funds named: Fonds Kunstmuseum, Fonds 21, Fonds 1818, Blockbuster Fonds, De Versterking, Lang Leve Kunst, the Debman Foundation, the Turing Foundation and the Mondriaan Business Club.
- Friends pay 50 euros a year, and patrons of the Fonds Gemeentemuseum give from 500 euros a year for at least five years (museum support page).
- On 30 September 2026 the city executive asked the council for a 2 million euro preparation credit for renovating and making the building more sustainable, and a separate 0.5 million euros for scenarios for a new depot (City of The Hague).

BOTTOM LINE
The City of The Hague owns the building and the collection and is one of the museum's main partners through its Kunstenplan contribution, with VriendenLoterij paying 500,000 euros a year and the Mondriaan Fonds, Vereniging Rembrandt and several private funds adding to it. In September 2026 the city asked its council for 2.5 million euros in preparation credits for the building and depot.
