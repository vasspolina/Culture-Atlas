HONG KONG MUSEUM OF THE WAR OF RESISTANCE AND COASTAL DEFENCE: Hong Kong, China
ID: hong-kong-museum-of-the-war-of-resistance-and-coastal-defence
Website: https://hk.waranddefence.museum (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Government museum at 175 Tung Hei Road, Shau Kei Wan, in the restored Lyemun (Lei Yue Mun) Fort on a headland 64 metres above sea level, about 15 minutes' walk from Shau Kei Wan MTR station; the site covers 34,200 square metres (museum website).
- The British built the fort in 1887 to guard the eastern approach to Victoria Harbour; it fell to Japanese forces on 19 December 1941 and was vacated by British forces in 1987 (Wikipedia).
- The Urban Council decided in 1993 to convert it, and the Hong Kong Museum of Coastal Defence opened on 25 July 2000; it closed in September 2018 after Typhoon Mangkhut damaged it and reopened on 24 November 2022 (Wikipedia).
- In his October 2023 Policy Address, Chief Executive John Lee said the museum would be converted to focus on the War of Resistance against Japan, to build "a stronger sense of national esteem and patriotism" (The Standard, 25 October 2023).
- Lee unveiled the new name on 3 September 2024, which China marks as Victory Day, and said the government "will spare no effort to promote patriotic education"; the government calls it "one of the important cultivation bases for promoting patriotism" (news.gov.hk).
- Galleries: 11 in the permanent exhibition, four of them War of Resistance galleries, and a 24-stop historical trail (museum website). The opening show, "Brothers in Arms", on the East River Column guerrillas, was presented jointly with Shenzhen's culture bureau and two Shenzhen memorial halls (info.gov.hk, August 2024).
- Visiting: open 10.00 to 18.00 from March to September and 10.00 to 17.00 from October to February, closed Thursdays except public holidays (museum website). 174,700 visitors in 2025/26 (museum financial figures).

FUNDING
Hong Kong Museum of the War of Resistance and Coastal Defence: state-funded: the Hong Kong SAR Government's Leisure and Cultural Services Department
- Owner and operator: the Leisure and Cultural Services Department (LCSD) (museum website).
- Spending in 2025/26: HK$33,452,000, of which HK$6,880,000 on salaries, HK$8,429,000 on operating costs, HK$15,508,000 on electricity, cleaning and security, HK$2,518,000 on artefacts and exhibitions and HK$117,000 on publicity (museum financial figures).
- Own income in 2025/26: HK$103,000, HK$100,000 of it from admission and hiring (museum financial figures).
- Exhibition partners: the Culture, Media, Tourism and Sports Bureau of Shenzhen Municipality co-presented "Brothers in Arms"; the LCSD's Chinese Culture Promotion Office collaborated and the Hong Kong Chronicles Institute supported it; a veterans' society of the Dongjiang Column and private collectors lent or gave objects (info.gov.hk).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One international newspaper report names the museum and treats its conversion as part of a wider rewriting of Hong Kong history; there is no art press coverage.
1. The New York Times: Tiffany May, "A History Museum Shows How China Wants to Remake Hong Kong" (2024)
URL: https://u.osu.edu/mclc/?p=49615
- About a new national security gallery at the Hong Kong Museum of History that frames the 2019 protests as a Western plot and leaves out police tear gas and the Yuen Long mob attack.
- Names this museum: reports that the Museum of Coastal Defence, which centred on British fortifications, was being turned into a memorial to China's war with Japan.
- Links both to a campaign to bind Hong Kong's history to the national narrative, alongside school curriculum changes and study tours to mainland China.
- Read as reposted by the MCLC Resource Center (Ohio State University); the original is paywalled.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from the Hong Kong government, with no private funder. The issue is not the money but the mandate: the museum was repurposed by government order to serve patriotic education.
