EL MUSEO DEL BARRIO: New York, USA
ID: el-museo-del-barrio-new-york
Website: https://www.elmuseo.org/

OVERVIEW
- Museum specializing in Latin American and Caribbean art, with an emphasis on Puerto Rico, at 1230 Fifth Avenue in East Harlem, New York (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1969 by the artist and educator Raphael Montañez Ortiz, after Harlem parents, educators and activists demanded education that acknowledged their cultural heritages; it began in a public school classroom, used storefronts until 1976, and in 1977 moved into the Heckscher Building (Wikipedia).
- Patrick Charpenel became executive director in 2017 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
El Museo del Barrio: philanthropy-led: Ford Foundation and MacKenzie Scott, with public support through New York City's Cultural Institutions Group
- Ford Foundation: a $4 million grant paid over four years, announced on 22 October 2020 as part of the $156 million America's Cultural Treasures initiative. Arte al Día calls it the "single largest award" to the museum since its founding. It supports curatorial and education programmes and general operations (Arte al Día).
- MacKenzie Scott: $8 million, reported on 16 June 2021 (The Art Newspaper, Wallace Ludel).
- Public money: the museum joined New York City's Cultural Institutions Group in 1977, which helped it increase and sustain its funding (Wikipedia).

Red flags: protest at the museum's 50th anniversary
- In June 2019, at the museum's 50th anniversary celebration, protesters wore shirts reading "El Museo fue del Barrio", read the Mirror Manifesto aloud and handed out over 400 copies. Debbie Quiñones was a lead organiser. The protest asked the board to respond to community needs and was aimed at the museum's leadership and direction (Latino Rebels, Amanda Alcántara, 13 June 2019).
- The Mirror Manifesto, dated 28 March 2019 and signed by dozens of academics, students, activists, curators and artists, asked the museum to serve El Barrio and other Latinx communities in New York and the tri-state area (Latino Rebels). By 21 May 2019 it had 544 signatories, including former director Marta Moreno Vega (Hyperallergic).
- Its five demands include a decolonizing commission to review the collection and a board that reflects the community. In late March 2019 the museum announced a new curator post for Latinx art, the only demand met at that point (Hyperallergic).
- A separate group, PRIDA (Puerto Rican Institute for the Development of the Arts), wrote to Charpenel on 19 May 2019 that Puerto Rican art had lost its central place (Hyperallergic).
- Charpenel said El Museo "remains faithful to its roots" (Hyperallergic). The museum said it had "no further comment regarding the Manifesto" (Latino Rebels, update of 14 June 2019).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns the museum's mission, leadership and programming choices.
1. Hyperallergic: Seph Rodney, "Has El Museo del Barrio Betrayed Its Mission?" (21 May 2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/500266/el-museo-mission/
- Rodney suggests the conflict is generational, a shift from a Puerto Rican-rooted identity toward a broader Latinidad.
- Names executive director Patrick Charpenel, whose May 2017 selection frustrated protesters, and chief curator Rodrigo Moura.
2. Latino Rebels: Amanda Alcántara, "Community Activists Stage Protest Action at El Museo del Barrio's 50th Anniversary" (13 June 2019)
URL: https://www.latinorebels.com/2019/06/13/barrioanniversary/
- Reports the protest and the Mirror Manifesto; the museum said it welcomed nearly 3,000 visitors.
3. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "El Museo del Barrio Accused of Removing Work With Palestinian Flag" (23 October 2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/852278/el-museo-del-barrio-accused-of-removing-work-with-palestinian-flag/
- The artists Odalys Burgoa and Roy Baizan made a Día de los Muertos altar, "Recordar y Unificar", for the museum café and its Afterlife Fiesta fundraiser. After they added a Palestinian flag, museum staff raised concerns; the artists say the museum worried it could leave people "alienated" and "uncomfortable". The altar remains in the museum, out of public view.
- The artists said: "This isn't neutrality and it's clear where they stand."
4. Hyperallergic: Anna Lee, "El Museo del Barrio Apologizes for Renting Space for 9/11 Conspiracy Event" (24 September 2026)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/el-museo-del-barrio-apologizes-for-renting-space-for-9-11-conspiracy-event
- The International Center for 9/11 Justice rented part of the museum's building for "Turning the Tide: 9/11 25 Years Later", held 10 to 13 September 2026, with speakers including Roger Waters.
- The museum said it "deeply regrets this event" and that "At the time of contracting, the Museum was not aware of the full program, content and speaker list for this event."

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is philanthropic and public: grants from the Ford Foundation ($4 million over four years from 2020) and MacKenzie Scott ($8 million, 2021), and membership of New York City's Cultural Institutions Group since 1977. The documented disputes are the June 2019 protest and Mirror Manifesto, the 2023 altar removal, and the September 2026 venue rental that the museum apologised for.
