INTERNATIONAL CENTER OF PHOTOGRAPHY: New York, USA
ID: international-center-of-photography-new-york
Website: https://www.icp.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum and school of photography on the Lower East Side at Essex Crossing; the main entrance is at 84 Ludlow Street and the legal address is 79 Essex Street (ICP website).
- Founded in 1974 by Cornell Capa to champion "concerned photography". It opened in the Willard Straight House on Fifth Avenue (ICP website, Wikipedia).
- Moved to Midtown in 1985 and to 250 Bowery in 2016. It sold the Bowery building in 2019 and bought its current home; the school and museum were reunited under one roof in 2020 (ICP website, Wikipedia).
- Bob Jeffrey became CEO in 2024, the year of ICP's 50th anniversary (ICP website).
- On site: the museum, the School (one-year programmes, open education, youth programmes), a shop, a café and a library of more than 22,000 volumes (ICP website). The library is open to the public at the monthly Photobook Club, held the last Saturday of each month (ICP visit page).
- Visiting: open Monday to Wednesday and Friday to Sunday, 10:30 AM to 6:30 PM, Thursday until 8 PM. Adults pay $18, seniors and military $14, visitors with disabilities $14 with caregivers free, students $12, and SNAP/EBT cardholders $3. Members, ICP students and visitors 14 and under enter free, and Thursday 5 to 8 PM is $5 (ICP visit page).

FUNDING
International Center of Photography: contributions, galas and corporate partners: Fondation d'entreprise Hermès, Hearst and the Mellon Foundation
- Finances: in the year to June 2023 ICP reported $7,963,364 in revenue, $15,019,573 in expenses and $3,743,386 in contributions. Net assets were $44,069,399 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 data).
- In earlier years contributions were larger: $12,123,258 in the year to June 2021 and $7,972,662 in the year to June 2022 (ProPublica).
- Fondation d'entreprise Hermès: established the Latitudes programme in 2024 with ICP and the Fondation Henri Cartier-Bresson in Paris. Each year the three partners select a laureate who receives a grant to make a photographic series, shown in Paris, at ICP and in the laureate's home country. The ICP winter exhibitions that opened on 29 January 2026 included Latitudes: Nuits Balnéaires and François-Xavier Gbré, presented in partnership with the Fondation d'entreprise Hermès (ICP website).
- Hearst: sponsor of the 40th Annual Infinity Awards, held on 10 April 2024 at The Shed (ICP news release, 4 December 2023). The 2025 gala, held at Tisch Skylights at The Shed, raised over $1 million (PalmerPB, 2 April 2025).
- The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation: in 2016 ICP established the Center for Visual Culture with a grant from the foundation (ICP website, About).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, open letter of 2020
- Noah Morrison, a founding member of ICP Center Blackness Now, wrote a letter with colleagues, supported by almost 150 community members, addressed to ICP over students' protest photographs that showed protesters' distinguishing features (Hyperallergic, Valentina Di Liscia, 23 June 2020).
- The demands were that ICP require students to remove such photos online and discipline those who did not, publish an institutional ethical framework for photojournalism, make Critical Race Studies a learning requirement and commit to Black people making up at least 30% of students, faculty and staff within two years (Hyperallergic).
- ICP said it had not required students to "censor their work" and cited its commitment to "free expression and documentary practice." Its 9 June statement had pledged equity training, a DEI initiative and online conversations about documenting protest. Its Chief Experience Officer invited the group to "restorative dialogue," which Morrison declined, saying ICP's measures were superficial (Hyperallergic).

BOTTOM LINE
ICP is a nonprofit funded by contributions, events and corporate partners; its Form 990 shows $7,963,364 in revenue and $15,019,573 in expenses for the year to June 2023. Named partners are the Fondation d'entreprise Hermès, Hearst and the Mellon Foundation. The one documented dispute is the 2020 open letter over protest photography.
