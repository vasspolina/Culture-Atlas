OCEANIA CENTRE FOR ARTS, CULTURE AND PACIFIC STUDIES (USP): Suva, Fiji

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre of the University of the South Pacific (USP) on its Laucala campus in Suva (USP page, archived September 2018).
- Founded in 1997 as a special project of the USP University Council, with Professor Epeli Hau'ofa as its first director (USP page, archived 2018).
- Originally the Oceania Centre for Arts and Culture. In 2008 Pacific Studies joined it, forming the present name (USP page, archived 2018).
- Hau'ofa described its purpose as creating spaces to experiment with and develop new forms and styles, rooted in Pacific identity (Wikipedia).
- The Pacific Heritage Hub began in 2012 as a UNESCO project and became a department of the centre in 2018 (USP page, archived 2018).
- Hosts the Oceania Pacific Arts Residency, a six-week pilot from 1 October to 12 November 2026 on the theme of climate change, with one artist each from New Zealand, Australia and Fiji (Creative New Zealand).

FUNDING
Oceania Centre for Arts, Culture and Pacific Studies: a unit of the University of the South Pacific, with Creative New Zealand and Creative Australia funding its 2026 residency
- The centre was founded in 1997 as a special project of the USP University Council (USP page, archived 2018).
- The 2026 residency is a tri-nations partnership of Creative New Zealand, Creative Australia and the Oceania Centre. Creative New Zealand and Creative Australia contribute toward travel and living costs: an artist fee of NZD 9,000 (about AUD 7,373), NZD 4,000 (about AUD 3,273) for project costs, and return travel to Suva, accommodation and per diems (Creative Australia, Creative New Zealand).
- The Oceania Centre hosts the residency, sits on the assessment panel and is consulted on the final selection (Creative Australia).

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is a unit of the University of the South Pacific, founded in 1997 as a special project of the University Council. Its 2026 residency is funded by two national arts agencies, Creative New Zealand and Creative Australia, with the centre as host.
