FACT: Liverpool, UK
ID: fact-liverpool
Website: https://www.fact.co.uk

OVERVIEW
- Gallery and cinema centre for art and new technology at 88 Wood Street, Liverpool L1 4DQ (FACT).
- FACT began as Merseyside Moviola, which screened artists' film and video in a disused chapel; Moviola founded the Video Positive festival in 1989 and in 1993 began a 10-year fundraising effort for a dedicated centre (FACT). Eddie Berg says he founded the Foundation for Art and Creative Technology in 1988 (Sculpture magazine, Robert Preece, 1 April 2004). Wikipedia names Graeme Phillips as one of the people behind it.
- The building opened in 2003 as Liverpool's first new arts building in over 60 years; Wikipedia says Isaac Julien's "Baltimore" was commissioned for the opening.
- The building was designed by Austin-Smith:Lord (Wikipedia). Sculpture magazine described it in 2004 as having two galleries, three cinemas, an online research facility, a media lab, a cafe and a bar.
- Programmes: contemporary art galleries on technology and digital culture, a cinema programmed and run with Picturehouse, the Studio/Lab skills programme and a learning programme for young people and intergenerational groups (FACT).
- Galleries open Wednesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 18:00, with free entry and no booking. Cinema tickets run from £3 for children to £15 for adults at peak times (FACT).
- Accessibility: step-free access from Wood Street and a ramp at the rear on Fleet Street; a lift serves every floor; accessible toilets on the ground, first and second floors; guide dogs and trained support dogs are welcome (FACT).

FUNDING
FACT: mainly public with charitable trusts: Arts Council England and Liverpool City Council, plus Paul Hamlyn, Jerwood, John Ellerman and Granada
- FACT's supporters page lists as statutory funders Arts Council England (Lottery) and Liverpool City Council, and says FACT is part of Arts Council England's National Portfolio. It also names Liverpool City Council's Culture & Arts Investment Programme.
- Its funders and supporters block shows the logos of Culture Liverpool, Creative Europe, Paul Hamlyn Foundation, Jerwood Arts, Bloomberg Philanthropies, John Ellerman Foundation, The Fenton Arts Trust, Idlewild Trust, Eleanor Rathbone Charitable Trust, Granada Foundation, Medicash Foundation, Stowe Family Law, Marjory Boddy Charitable Trust and Hays Travel Foundation. Partner logos include the universities of Liverpool, Salford and Nottingham, Liverpool John Moores University, Liverpool Hope University, Picturehouse, Arriva and Merseyrail.
- 360Giving data (GrantNav) records 24 grants to FACT from seven funders between 26 October 2006 and 31 March 2025, totalling £6,080,518: the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport £5,408,178 (8 grants), Paul Hamlyn Foundation £354,000 (4), John Ellerman Foundation £117,000 (1), Foyle Foundation £88,000 (2), National Lottery Heritage Fund £76,800 (3), National Lottery Community Fund £26,540 (4) and Fenton Arts Trust £10,000 (2).
- Early support in the 1980s and 1990s came from the Arts Council, the city council, sponsors, trusts and foundations (Sculpture magazine, 2004).

BOTTOM LINE
FACT's core money is public: Arts Council England and Liverpool City Council, with the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport the largest funder in the 360Giving record (£5,408,178 of £6,080,518 granted between 2006 and 2025). Charitable trusts are also listed as supporters.
