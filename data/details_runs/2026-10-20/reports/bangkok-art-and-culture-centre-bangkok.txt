BANGKOK ART AND CULTURE CENTRE: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: bangkok-art-and-culture-centre-bangkok
Website: https://www.bacc.or.th/ (checked 2026-10-20; returns HTTP 403 to automated requests, so supporters and reports could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre at 939 Rama 1 Road, Pathum Wan, in the Siam area, linked to National Stadium BTS station by an elevated walkway.
- Started by Governor Bhichit Rattakul in 1995, suspended by Governor Samak Sundaravej in 2001 in favour of retail space, revived by Governor Apirak Kosayodhin in 2004 after cultural groups objected, and opened on 29 July 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Built by the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration (BMA) and run under contract by the BACC Foundation; the first contract ran from 2011 to 2021, and in March 2021 the governor announced another 10 years (Time Out Bangkok).
- Designed by Robert G. Boughey and Associates around a central atrium; built for 509 million baht (Wikipedia).
- Nine storeys of exhibition rooms, a cinema, a library, a 280 square metre multi-function room, shops and cafés, with a spiral walkway from the sixth floor up.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 8pm, closed Monday; entry free (Time Out Bangkok). No accessibility details found.

FUNDING
Bangkok Art and Culture Centre: mixed: Bangkok city money, private sponsors and space rentals
- The BMA's contract set 40 million baht a year, against an operating budget of 75 to 90 million baht; donors and corporate sponsors cover the rest (Wikipedia).
- BMA money was more than half the budget until it stopped in 2018, the city citing legal complications; the 2019 budget was 81 million baht (Time Out Bangkok; ArtsEquator via Bangkok Post).
- The BMA failed to fund the centre in 2009, 2010 and 2017 (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the current BMA grant under the 2021 contract.
- No reporting found that could be checked naming current corporate sponsors; the centre's own site blocked access.

Red flags: 2025 censorship under Chinese embassy pressure
- On 27 July 2025, three days after "Constellation of Complicity" opened, BMA representatives, the centre's main funder, visited with Chinese embassy officials who flagged works as "problematic" (ArtReview).
- The centre blacked out the names of Clara Cheung, Gum Cheng Yee Man, Tenzin Mingyur Paldron and Mukaddas Mijit, covered flags and removed a Liz Hee graphic; officials then demanded removal of Paldron's videos and any image of Xi Jinping (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Staff told artists the changes were to avoid "diplomatic tensions between Thailand and China" (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Co-curator Sai left for the UK after gallery directors told him Thai police were asking for his contact details (ArtReview, citing the New York Times).

Red flags: 2018 city takeover attempt and defunding
- In May 2018 Governor Aswin Kwanmuang announced the BMA would take over the centre, lease the ground floor and make it a learning centre, and the city cut its annual 40 million baht (The Nation).
- Artists including national artist Suchart Sawassri and Vasan Sitthiket objected, and Kallaya Kassakul launched a #freebacc petition on Change.org with more than 3,200 supporters in a day (The Nation).
- The BMA deferred any change of management until the contract ended in 2021, and the foundation kept the centre (Wikipedia; Time Out Bangkok).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage centres on the 2025 censorship, in news pieces; the artists quoted mostly do not blame the centre itself.
1. ArtReview: unsigned, "Bangkok Art & Culture Centre censors works following visit from Chinese officials" (2025)
URL: https://artreview.com/bangkok-art-culture-centre-removes-works-following-visit-from-chinese-officials
- Sai: "It is tragically ironic that an exhibition on authoritarian cooperation has been censored under authoritarian pressure."
- Calls the episode "a chilling signal to all exiled artists and activists in the region." No response from the centre.
2. Hyperallergic: Isa Farfan, "Thai Art Center Censors Exhibition After 'Pressure' From China" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/thai-art-center-censors-exhibition-after-pressure-from-china
- Frames the changes as censorship by a foreign government, with the city as main supporter.
- Paldron did not blame the centre but said museums should serve the public, not "dictators of any ideology."
3. ArtAsiaPacific: unsigned, "Bangkok Art Center Censored Works After Chinese Embassy Pressure" (2025)
URL: https://www.artasiapacific.com/news/bangkok-art-center-censored-works-under-chinese-embassy-pressure
- Paldron: "They must let the powers that be know this is unacceptable."
- Quotes the centre's aim "to de-escalate the situation."

BOTTOM LINE
The money is clean but fragile: city funds that come and go, plus unnamed private sponsors. The weak point is that the city funder, together with a foreign embassy, could force changes to a show in 2025. Worth watching: the BMA grant under the 2021 contract and whether sponsors are ever named.
