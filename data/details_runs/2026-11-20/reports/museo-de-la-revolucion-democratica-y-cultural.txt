MUSEO DE LA REVOLUCIÓN DEMOCRÁTICA Y CULTURAL: Orinoca, Bolivia
ID: museo-de-la-revolucion-democratica-y-cultural
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum in Orinoca, Evo Morales's birthplace, in the municipality of Santiago de Andamarca, Sud Carangas province, Oruro department (Los Tiempos, 2017).
- Construction began in December 2012; Morales inaugurated it on 2 February 2017 (Los Tiempos, 2 and 3 February 2017).
- Three blocks named Inchura, Sullca and Collana on a 12,000 square metre site, with an open-air theatre, a ceremonial courtyard, an Andean temple, a viewing deck and a condor area (Los Tiempos, 2017).
- Shows Bolivia's indigenous history, Morales's life and gifts he received: ponchos, football shirts, a golden trumpet from his years as a folk band musician, the sweater he wore when elected in 2005 and a full-length statue of him with the presidential sash (Los Tiempos, 2017; EFE, 2019). His adobe childhood home stands next to it (EFE).
- Minister of Government Carlos Romero said in 2017 that Orinoca had "un valor teológico, casi divino" (a theological, almost divine value) for the Andean indigenous world, and that the museum "no es para ensalzar la figura de Evo Morales" (is not meant to exalt Evo Morales) (Los Tiempos; translations ours).
- The Vice Ministry of Tourism counted about 17,000 visitors in the first year; damp walls and leaks appeared in early 2019 (EFE, 2019).
- Closed: in December 2023 the culture minister said it had not operated for more than three years (eju.tv, citing ANF). Indigenous authorities and residents of Orinoca asked the Ministry of Cultures to reopen it (eju.tv).

FUNDING
Museo de la Revolución Democrática y Cultural: state-built: public money under Evo Morales's government
- Cost: almost 50 million bolivianos of state investment (Los Tiempos, 2017), about 7.1 million US dollars according to the authorities of the time (EFE, 2019). In April 2026 the Vice Minister of Transparency put it at over 47 million bolivianos for the building and about 2.5 million for equipment (eju.tv, citing ERBOL).
- Administration: the national investment fund, the Fondo Nacional de Inversión Productiva y Social (FPS), and the Andamarca municipality still held it in June 2023. Culture Minister Sabina Orellana said the museum had "problemas legales" (legal problems) and had not been transferred to her ministry, which could pay for its running and repair only after a transfer (Los Tiempos).
- The Ministry of Cultures oversees the heritage inside and pays for security every two to three months (eju.tv, December 2023).
- Own income: 17,000 bolivianos collected since opening (Los Tiempos, 2023).
- Repairs: in August 2022 a former Oruro assembly member, Limbert Maceiros, put the restoration needed at over 2 million bolivianos; neither the Oruro governorship nor the municipality would pay it (Los Tiempos, 2023).

Red flags: 2026 investigation into the direct award of the building contract
- On 7 April 2026 eju.tv, citing ERBOL, reported that the government's Vice Ministry of Transparency had opened an investigation into how the project was justified before investment and why it was contracted directly instead of by public tender.
- Unverified: Vice Minister of Transparency Yamil García said the officials who approved the project, set its cost and authorised direct contracting will have to answer for it (eju.tv, citing ERBOL).

Red flags: Decay and closure of the building, 2019 to 2023
- In December 2019 interim culture minister Martha Yujra said the building "no tiene ningún beneficio" (brings no benefit) and lost a great deal of money, and proposed a referendum on its future (EFE).
- In June 2023 Sabina Orellana said her ministry would close the museum and audit it once it was transferred (Los Tiempos).
- In December 2023 she said the structure "no está bien" (is not sound) and could endanger visitors (eju.tv, citing ANF).
- Unverified: Orellana said those who built it will have to answer for it (eju.tv, citing ANF).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from the national press, not the art press, and targets the museum as a personality cult and a waste of public money.
1. Los Tiempos: Rocío Estremadoiro Rioja, "El museo al ego" (2017)
URL: https://www.lostiempos.com/node/139008
- An opinion column by a sociologist: 47 million bolivianos of public money went on a building that shows the "megalomanía y borrachera de poder" of officials.
- Argues the museum reduces indigenous history to "el culto a la personalidad de un individuo" (the personality cult of one individual), while Orinoca lacks adequate schools and health care.
- Supports a museum of local ancestral cultures, but not this one.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money with no outside funder, but poorly accounted for: the weak points are how it was contracted and spent, and a purpose closer to a monument to its patron than a museum. Worth watching: the outcome of the 2026 inquiry.
