APRIL 25 HOUSE OF CULTURE: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: april-25-house-of-culture
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Theatre and assembly hall on Pipha Street in Moranbong District, Pyongyang, near Ryomyong Street, the Metro Museum and the Chinese Embassy; visitors usually pass it on the way in from the airport (Koryo Tours; Wikipedia).
- Construction began in April 1974 on a cleared 12.4-hectare site, and it opened on 7 October 1975 as the February 8 House of Culture, a venue for military education (Wikipedia; Koryo Tours).
- Renamed for 25 April, the date North Korea gives for the founding of the anti-Japanese guerrilla army that the Korean People's Army treats as its predecessor; 8 February 1948 was the army's official founding (Koryo Tours; Wikipedia).
- A colonnaded building 105 metres wide, 176 metres deep and almost 50 metres high, with theatres of 6,000 and 1,100 seats, a 600-seat cinema and about 600 other rooms in 80,000 m2 of floor space. Portraits of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il were added to the facade in 2012 and 2013 (Koryo Tours; Wikipedia).
- Home of the April 25 Culture and Art Composition Office, which organises the army's major cultural events, including international conferences and state funerals, and a stage for the Korean People's Army Ensemble and the Moranbong Band. Kim Jong Il met South Korean president Roh Moo-hyun here in 2007 (Koryo Tours).
- Visiting: not generally open to tourists, mainly because it is a military site; tourists could attend the closing ceremony of the 2006 Pyongyang International Film Festival here (Koryo Tours).

FUNDING
April 25 House of Culture: state-run: a military and party venue of the North Korean state
- Koryo Tours calls the house a military site, built for military education, whose resident office runs the army's cultural events.
- North Korea announces its budget only as yearly percentage changes. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with the cultural sector's funds at 103.1 percent of the year before and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence (KCNA, copied by GlobalSecurity).

Red flags: a venue of the sanctioned party, state and army (US listings of 2016 and 2017)
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the house.
- The Workers' Party has held four congresses here: the 6th on 10 to 14 October 1980, the 7th on 6 to 9 May 2016, the 8th on 6 to 12 January 2021 and the 9th on 19 to 25 February 2026 (Wikipedia).
- OFAC added the Korean People's Army, whose cultural office the house holds, to its sanctions list on 1 June 2017 (OFAC); the notice names the army, not the house.

Not related: the April 25 Film Studio, a separate studio of the Korean People's Army (Koryo Tours; Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art criticism; two news reports citing anonymous sources describe incidents at the building, a fire in 2014 and graffiti in 2018.
1. Radio Free Asia: Kim Jun-ho, "Big fire breaks out at Pyongyang's April 25 House of Culture" (2014; byline romanised and title translated from Korean, 평양 4.25 문화회관에 큰 화재 발생)
URL: https://www.rfa.org/korean/in_focus/fire-04252014095421.html
- Unverified: RFA reported on 25 April 2014, citing an anonymous Pyongyang resident, that a fire started in the building's ceiling a few days earlier; the resident declined to describe the damage and blamed a South Korean intelligence agent.
- RFA notes the army anniversary meetings of 2012 and 2013 were held at the house, while the 24 April 2014 meeting was held elsewhere.
2. Daily NK: Lee Sang Yong, "Graffiti directly criticising Kim Jong Un at Pyongyang's April 25 House of Culture" (2018; byline romanised and title translated from Korean, 평양 4.25문화회관에 '김정은 직접 비난' 낙서)
URL: https://www.dailynk.com/%ED%8F%89%EC%96%91-4-25%EB%AC%B8%ED%99%94%ED%9A%8C%EA%B4%80%EC%97%90-%EA%B9%80%EC%A0%95%EC%9D%80-%EC%A7%81%EC%A0%91-%EB%B9%84%EB%82%9C-%EB%82%99%EC%84%9C/
- Unverified: Daily NK reported on 19 March 2018, citing an anonymous Pyongyang source, that graffiti directly criticising Kim Jong Un was found on the side of the building at about 4 a.m. on 1 March.
- The source said the authorities tightened checks on people moving about and began handwriting investigations.

BOTTOM LINE
The house is paid for by the North Korean state through the army and the party, all three under US sanctions. It remains the stage for the party's congresses and the army's ceremonies.
