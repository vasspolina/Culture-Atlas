PEJMAN FOUNDATION (ARGO FACTORY): Tehran, Iran
ID: pejman-foundation-argo-factory
Website: https://pejman.foundation/argofactory

OVERVIEW
- Private non-profit contemporary art foundation in Tehran, with Argo Factory, a museum and cultural centre in a former drink factory, as its headquarters.
- The Pejman Foundation was established in 2015 and is led by its founder and director Hamidreza Pejman; the foundation also runs Kandovan, a non-profit project space (Wikipedia, MERIP).
- Argo Factory was built in the early 1920s and stood derelict for almost four decades before the foundation acquired and reconstructed it in 2016 (Argo Factory website, MERIP); Wikipedia says it closed in the 1970s.
- Architects were Ahmadreza Schricker Architecture North of New York, working with a structural engineer; the project kept the original brick walls and added five concrete roofs, and the floor area grew from about 480 to 1,890 square metres (Wikipedia). It won a 2022 Aga Khan Award for Architecture and Dezeen's 2022 Architecture Project of the Year in the cultural buildings category (Wikipedia).
- On site: six galleries, a library, event spaces, a shop, an artist residency studio and a bar serving non-alcoholic beer (Dezeen, 8 May 2022).
- The museum opened to the public in January 2020 with a solo show by Nazgol Ansarinia, and closed soon after because of Covid-19 (The Art Newspaper, Carolyn Tso, 14 May 2020).
- The war began on 28 February 2026, according to Pejman, and Argo Factory's website notice reads "closed until further notice"; works and equipment were moved to safer places (MERIP interview, 20 August 2026).
- In the same interview Pejman says that during the April 2026 ceasefire an unnamed institution shut the third screening of the Argo film series, citing permits; stated by the founder.

FUNDING
Pejman Foundation (Argo Factory): privately funded: the foundation's own money, with three company sponsors for the building work
- Al-Monitor reports that there is no government funding to support restoration projects like this one, and that Pejman cited years of crippling sanctions (Ramin Khanizadeh and Menna Zaki, 6 March 2025).
- Wikipedia says the foundation decided to acquire the factory from the state.
- Argo Factory's sponsors page names three companies: Brutal Beton, which provided the concrete roofs, walls and some decorative elements; Barad, a brand of Pars Diba Chemical Industries, which provided the paint for all the spaces; and Castle Noush, one of Kalle's food industry subsidiaries, whose sponsorship the page says made the opening possible.
- In the 2026 interview sponsors withdrew and the marketing budget was cut, and paying salaries has become difficult (MERIP, Hamidreza Pejman and Parham Ghalamdar, 20 August 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is private: the foundation bought and rebuilt Argo Factory, and Al-Monitor reports no government funding for such restoration. Its sponsors page names three companies: Brutal Beton, Barad (a brand of Pars Diba Chemical Industries) and Castle Noush. Sponsors have withdrawn since the war began in February 2026, according to Pejman in MERIP.
