KUNSTRAUM KREUZBERG/BETHANIEN: Berlin, Germany
ID: kunstraum-kreuzberg-bethanien
Website: https://www.kunstraumkreuzberg.de

OVERVIEW
- Municipal gallery for group and thematic exhibitions on contemporary social and cultural issues, at Mariannenplatz 2 in Kreuzberg, Berlin. The nearest U-Bahn station is Kottbusser Tor, with bus 140 to Mariannenplatz.
- Founded in 1973 in the former operating rooms of the Bethanien deaconess hospital, which closed in 1970 and was slated for demolition; the fight to save it became a starting point of Kreuzberg's alternative culture and squatter scene (taz interview with Stéphane Bauer, 27 September 2023). It was called Kunstamt Kreuzberg/Bethanien until 2001 (Wikipedia).
- The building is the former Diakonissen-Krankenhaus, built in 1847 to designs by Theodor von Stein. Since summer 2010 it has been called Kunstquartier Bethanien (stated by the space).
- Stéphane Bauer has led the gallery since 2002 and, since 2016, also heads the district's Fachbereich Kultur und Geschichte (stated by the space).
- The main exhibition area is about 450 square metres, with a project room of about 220 square metres and Studio1 of about 660 square metres. It shows five to six exhibitions a year and holds about 450 works in its own collection (stated by the space). Bauer told taz that it draws 50,000 to 60,000 visitors a year.
- In January 2012 the gallery agreed to collect donated copies of Thilo Sarrazin's book "Deutschland schafft sich ab" for Martin Zet's campaign in the 7th Berlin Biennale. Bauer's statement says it drew "fierce and polarizing criticism and protest" as well as support, and that the gallery kept collecting (Berlin Biennale, 17 January 2012).
- Visiting: open daily 10:00 to 20:00 with free admission. The gallery entrance is step free, with an accessible toilet, seating, a quiet room and touchable works marked with pictograms; the main door of the building opens by hand only and is 98 cm wide.

FUNDING
Kunstraum Kreuzberg/Bethanien: the Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg district, with Berlin Senate exhibition funding and project grants
- The gallery is an institution of the Bezirksamt Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg and has basic funding through the district (stated by the space).
- Since 2014 it has received support from the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Europe's Ausstellungsfonds Kommunale Galerien (stated by the space).
- Bauer told taz (Tilman Baumgärtel, 27 September 2023) that the programme budget was 50,000 euros, higher than before, and that larger shows depend on grants from funding pools. Partners he named are the Goethe-Institut, IfA, Goldrausch and Transmediale.
- The Senate department also funds a scholarship for artists living in Istanbul, held in Raum 139 at Bethanien in cooperation with nGbK and DEPO Istanbul (stated by the space).
- The building is managed by the non-profit GSE (Gesellschaft für StadtEntwicklung gemeinnützige GmbH) under a hereditary lease, the Erbpachtvertrag, since May 2009 (stated by the space; taz also dates it to 2009).
- In September 2024 the Bundestag budget committee approved 5 million euros from the KulturInvest 2024 programme for the renovation of the Kunstquartier Bethanien, which houses the gallery (Bezirksamt Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg press release, 26 September 2024).

Not related: the Künstlerhaus Bethanien GmbH. It is a separate company that was based in the Bethanien building until June 2010 and then moved to Kottbusser Straße 10 (stated by the space).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is an institution of the Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg district, which provides its basic funding; the Berlin Senate's exhibition fund has supported it since 2014, and grant pools fund larger shows. The 5 million euro federal grant of 2024 is for the renovation of the building.
