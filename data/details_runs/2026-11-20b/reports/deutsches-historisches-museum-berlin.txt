DEUTSCHES HISTORISCHES MUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: deutsches-historisches-museum-berlin
Website: https://www.dhm.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Germany's national history museum at Unter den Linden 2, Berlin, run by the Deutsches Historisches Museum Foundation (stated by the space).
- Founded on 28 October 1987 by the Federal Republic and the Land of Berlin for Berlin's 750th anniversary, with strong backing from Chancellor Helmut Kohl; it was inaugurated in the Reichstag building in West Berlin (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- On 3 October 1990 the federal government transferred to it the collection and premises of the GDR's Museum für Deutsche Geschichte, including the Zeughaus of 1695 (Wikipedia).
- Buildings: the Zeughaus and I. M. Pei's exhibition hall of 2,700 m² on four floors, opened in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- The Zeughaus has been closed since 2021, when damage to its air conditioning turned a repair into a full renovation of climate systems, IT and energy use, with a second entrance and additional toilets (Berliner Zeitung, 23 January 2026).
- President: the Swiss historian Raphael Gross, appointed by the Board of Trustees on 24 November 2016, in office since 15 April 2017. The collection holds some 1,000,000 objects (stated by the space).
- Current exhibitions in the Pei building include "Controversial Kinship. Colonial and National Socialist Violence" (16 October 2026 to 11 April 2027) and "Objects. History. Stories" (8 May 2026 to 31 October 2027) (stated by the space).
- Visiting: Pei building daily 10 am to 6 pm; 7 euros, reduced 3.50 euros, free up to 18; a Bloomberg Connects guide app offers voice-over, subtitles and adjustable text (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Deutsches Historisches Museum: public: the German federal government, through the Commissioner for Culture and the Media
- The museum's site states it is "Funded by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media" (stated by the space).
- Since 30 December 2008 the museum has been a public-law foundation of the federal government; it also hosts the Stiftung Flucht, Vertreibung, Versöhnung, founded in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Its Board of Trustees has five members from the federal government, the Bundestag and the Länder, chaired by Dr. Konrad Schmidt-Werthern (stated by the space).
- Federal budget: the 2027 federal culture budget of 2.58 billion euros gives the museum an extra 1.5 million euros for its exhibition and education work (Federal Government press release, 13 November 2026).
- Building: the federal property agency BImA owns the Zeughaus and the Federal Office for Building and Regional Planning runs the renovation; Gross said funding for an interim display of the new permanent exhibition was still unsettled (Tagesspiegel, 6 May 2026; Berliner Zeitung, 23 January 2026).
- Hohenzollern settlement: the museum's board gave the last approval on 13 June 2025 for the settlement with the House of Hohenzollern; objects whose ownership was disputed pass to a new non-profit Stiftung Hohenzollernscher Kunstbesitz and stay in the collections of the DHM, SPK and SPSG. The public side holds six of nine council seats and the Hohenzollern family three; the family had withdrawn its pending property and press-law suits before talks began (Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media, press release, 13 June 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: A 2017 review of a colonial history exhibition and 2026 press coverage of the delayed Zeughaus renovation.
1. Contemporary And (C&): Jessica Aimufua, "Where Are You Going, White Man?" (2017)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/where-are-you-going-white-man
- Review of "German Colonialism: Fragments Past and Present": calls it "an undeniably German and therefore Eurocentric perspective on colonial rule"; the Namibian and Tanzanian curators "were only called in" when the concept was largely complete.
- Calls the exhibition's approach to colonial figures dangerous because it equates satirical exaggeration with racist stereotyping, and its accompanying information "largely superficial".
- About 500 exhibits on 1,000 square metres crowd the space, and the installation on the Black movement in Germany is "almost hidden in the far corner of the exhibition hall".
2. Berliner Zeitung: Kai Kleinwächter, "Sanierung außer Kontrolle: Zeughaus bleibt noch für Jahre zu" (2026)
URL: https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/article/sanierung-ausser-kontrolle-zeughaus-bleibt-noch-fuer-jahre-zu-10015732
- Citing rbb24, it reports Gross does not expect the Zeughaus to reopen before 2031 and calls 2031 a positive scenario.
- The delay renews debate over costly Berlin building projects and calls for interim displays of the new permanent exhibition.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: the museum runs on German federal money. Worth watching are the open-ended Zeughaus renovation and the Hohenzollern family's seats on the foundation that now holds formerly disputed objects.
