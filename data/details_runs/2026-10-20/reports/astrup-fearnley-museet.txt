ASTRUP FEARNLEY MUSEET: Oslo, Norway
ID: astrup-fearnley-museet
Website: https://www.afmuseet.no (checked 2026-10-20; the English about page and the Norwegian sponsor page in the record return 404, the current pages are at /en/about-us/)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of contemporary art at Strandpromenaden 2 on Tjuvholmen, on Oslo's waterfront.
- Opened in 1993 at Dronningens gate 4 in Kvadraturen, in a building by LPO Arkitekter; founded by the Thomas Fearnley Foundation (1939) and the Heddy and Nils Astrup Foundation, which merged in 1995.
- Named for the Fearnley shipping family and its Astrup descendants; the collector Hans Rasmus Astrup (1939 to 2021), who took over the shipbroker Fearnley & Eger after his father died in 1972, founded it (Wikipedia).
- Since 29 September 2012 it has occupied two buildings by Renzo Piano, his first in Scandinavia, with a double-arched glass roof over the main exhibition hall, with a sculpture park beside it, also designed by Piano (Visit Oslo); the project was put at NOK 650 million (Attractions Management).
- Holds the Astrup Fearnley Collection, strong in 1980s and 1990s American art; it bought Jeff Koons's Michael Jackson and Bubbles in 2002 for US$5.1 million.
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 12:00 to 17:00, Thursday 12:00 to 19:00, weekends 11:00 to 17:00, closed Monday except in July and August (12:00 to 17:00); adults NOK 180, students and seniors NOK 100, under-20s free. No accessibility details on the visit page.

FUNDING
Astrup Fearnley Museet: private: foundations built on Fearnley shipbroking money, plus a state grant and a bank foundation
- The Hans Rasmus Astrup Foundation owns the museum and, since the end of 2013, 97 percent of Astrup Fearnley AS, a group mainly in shipbroking that also provides services to the oil and offshore industry (Store norske leksikon).
- The Foundation Thomas Fearnley, Heddy and Nils Astrup also funds it; Astrup's fortune was estimated at NOK 5 billion in 2011 (Wikipedia).
- The Norwegian Ministry of Culture and Equality and the Savings Bank Foundation DNB are named funders; no reporting found of the amounts.
- Sponsors are EGD Holding, wholly owned by Espen Galtung Døsvig, which holds stakes in Egd Shipholding, Egd Property and Egd Capital (Proff), and The Thief (stated by the space).
- The board is chaired by Anna Catharina Astrup; Solveig Øvstebø has been director since 2020, after Gunnar B. Kvaran.

Red flags: Lundin Norway, main sponsor 2012 to 2019
- Lundin Norway, the Norwegian arm of Lundin Petroleum, was main sponsor from autumn 2012 until the deal lapsed in December 2019 (Kunstkritikk).
- Kunstkritikk's editor Jonas Ekeberg called the 2012 deal "an effort to launder the image of a company that has exploited resources in the Third World since the 1970s."
- Kunstkritikk's Swedish editor Kim West cancelled a lecture at the museum over the partnership.
- Former Lundin executives Ian Lundin and Alex Schneiter stand trial in Stockholm for complicity in war crimes in Sudan during its second civil war; prosecutors seek 10 and 6 years, both deny the charges, and a ruling is expected in late December 2026 (Arab News, AFP).
- Then director Kvaran said "there were no grounds for criticising the conduct of the Norwegian branch of Lundin"; the chair said there was "no special reason" it was not renewed and that it "came to a natural end."

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: A modest amount, mostly in Kunstkritikk: the Lundin deal, the private, Pop-heavy collection and the 2012 opening hang.
1. Kunstkritikk: Mariann Enge, "Controversial Sponsorship Agreement Ends" (2020)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.no/controversial-sponsorship-agreement-ends/
- Recounts the 2012 criticism of the Lundin deal and West's cancelled lecture.
- Notes the museum did not end the deal because of the criticism.
2. Kunstkritikk: Line Ulekleiv, "Breathless at the museum" (2012)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/breathless-at-the-museum/
- The building "is presented as the main masterpiece with everything else taking up subordinate positions"; the artists Hirst and Koons "have played out their parts," and works "consistently kill each other."
- Tjuvholmen "has little to offer to observers who appreciate a multi-faceted urban experience," though the review calls the museum a "cultural lynchpin" for the area.
- Criticises the art and building more than the institution.
3. Kunstkritikk: Andreas Breivik, "When an Exhibition Actually Matters" (2020)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.no/when-an-exhibition-actually-matters/
- Reports Ina Blom's 2018 criticism in Morgenbladet of the focus on American Pop Art and of a very private museum that "only follows suit."
- Øvstebø answers that the programme must stay independent "no matter where the money comes from."

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on foundation money from the Fearnley shipbroking fortune, whose group also serves the offshore oil industry. The clearest weak point was the 2012 to 2019 Lundin sponsorship, now tied to a war crimes trial. Worth watching: the Lundin verdict due in December 2026 and the size of the state grant.
