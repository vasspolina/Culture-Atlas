TAIPEI CONTEMPORARY ART CENTER: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: taipei-contemporary-art-center-taipei
Website: https://tcac.tw

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run contemporary art space in Taipei. In February 2021 it announced the closure of its space and the dissolution of its association by member vote (Videotage).
- Grew out of Jun Yang's research project "A Contemporary Art Centre, Taipei (A Proposal)" for the 2008 Taipei Biennial; after a weekend retreat with about 50 Taiwanese art figures, a core group formed in August 2009 to plan a real space (ART iT).
- Opened in late February 2010 in two buildings in central Taipei as a two-year pilot. About 30 people founded it as an association, which had around 70 members and used open board elections to avoid a single director (ART iT).
- Meiya Cheng was director and curator by 2013; the association was modelled on the European Kunsthaus and had over 60 members (Asia Art Archive in America, 4 May 2013).
- Until 2013 it used about 800 square feet in two buildings, with an office on the first floor behind a transparent façade; from February 2013 it had a space of roughly 150 square metres in downtown Taipei, and it ran a branch at 3331 Arts Chiyoda in Tokyo (Asia Art Archive).
- Programme: over 130 programmes, exhibitions and talks in three years, a performance or panel discussion each Friday, and exhibitions including "Mirage: Disused Public Property in Taiwan" (2011) and "Trading Futures" (4 to 31 March 2012, curated by Meiya Cheng and Pauline J. Yao) (Asia Art Archive; Charwei Tsai's project page).
- Its premises on Baoan Street were where it was located longest; before disbanding it ran a Minecraft-based virtual residency on that space (Videotage).

FUNDING
Taipei Contemporary Art Center: artist-run: sales of donated artworks, a small government subsidy and a rent-free building from a developer
- Sales of donated art: around 40 artists donated works that TCAC sold on commission to fund itself (ART iT). In 2013 Cheng said most of the funding came from sales of artwork donated by artists (Asia Art Archive).
- Government: Yang said TCAC received no government funding for now (ART iT). In 2013 Cheng said a government subsidy covered about 15 percent of the budget (Asia Art Archive).
- The real estate developer JUT Land Development sponsored the 2009 to 2012 venue for two years under its Urban Core Art District program (Asia Art Archive). ART iT describes a real estate company providing the building for two years without rent, with TCAC paying for the renovation from its own budget.
- Budget: USD 280,000 for the two years before the 2013 talk, half on staff, facilities and logistics and half on programming (Asia Art Archive).
- Jun Yang paid for most of the 2008 Biennial project himself (ART iT).
- Yang said the long-term goal was to persuade the city, the government and private companies to support TCAC without controlling it (ART iT).

BOTTOM LINE
TCAC was funded by sales of artworks donated by artists, a government subsidy of about 15 percent of its budget in 2013, and a rent-free building from JUT Land Development from 2009 to 2012. In February 2021 it announced the closure of its space and the dissolution of its association by member vote (Videotage).
