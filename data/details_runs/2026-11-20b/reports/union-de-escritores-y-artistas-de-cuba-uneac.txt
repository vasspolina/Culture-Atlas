UNIÓN DE ESCRITORES Y ARTISTAS DE CUBA (UNEAC): Havana, Cuba
ID: union-de-escritores-y-artistas-de-cuba-uneac
Website: http://www.uneac.org.cu (checked 2026-11-20; serves a default server page; the archive copy of 15 February 2026 was read)

OVERVIEW
- Membership union of Cuban writers and artists, based in Havana, with 14 provincial committees, whose members join by selection on their artistic record (UNEAC site).
- Founded on 22 August 1961 by the poet Nicolás Guillén; Alejo Carpentier, José Lezama Lima and René Portocarrero served in its leadership (UNEAC site).
- Its 2020 statutes recognise the Communist Party as the superior leading force of society and the state (article 2) and set the objective of rejecting and combating any activity contrary to the principles of the Revolution (article 8).
- On 11 January 2024 the musicologist Marta Bonet replaced Luis Morlote as president, on a proposal from the Communist Party's Ideological Department presented by its head, Rogelio Polanco, and Morlote joined the Party Central Committee's ideological staff (OnCuba). Prensa Latina reported her speaking as president at the 10th congress on 2 November 2024.

FUNDING
UNEAC: membership union: dues, a state contribution and donations
- Article 81 of the 2020 statutes lists its assets: members' dues; the state contribution, donations, bequests and other financial sources; property it owns; artworks held in its buildings; and other income allowed to social organisations, including proceeds of its own art-market activity at home and abroad.
- Articles 83 to 85 put its economic management under national rules, review it at each ordinary meeting of the governing bodies and let its national bodies order audits.

Red flags: 2024 expulsion of the historian Alina Bárbara López
- On 12 September 2024 UNEAC expelled López at a meeting of its Matanzas provincial committee, citing article 40 of its regulation; the grounds included criticising the "alta dirigencia de la Revolución" (the Revolution's top leadership), solidarity with the 11 July movement, and offending Julio César Pérez, a Communist Party official (14ymedio).
- López says she received no written decision and was told it could not be appealed (14ymedio); article 79 of the statutes says all sanctions can be appealed.
- Unverified: López says State Security agents rather than writers and artists dictated the decision (14ymedio, an independent outlet critical of the government).
- On 14 September 2024 the writer Jorge Fernández Era resigned in protest, writing that the union had done nothing about the harassment he and López had faced for two years (14ymedio).
- 14ymedio writes that the union has used expulsion as a political tool since 1961, naming Guillermo Cabrera Infante in 1967, Rafael Vilches and Pedro Armando Junco.

Red flags: 2025 protest resignation of Laideliz Herrera Laza
- In August 2025 the writer Laideliz Herrera Laza, Fernández Era's wife, announced her resignation in a Facebook post, saying the union gave only evasive answers when he asked for legal advice after police beat him in July (Cubanet, 18 August 2025).
- In the post and to Martí Noticias she accused UNEAC of circulating collective letters against critics and of serving "una política de Estado que censura" (a state policy that censors, my translation) (Cubanet).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: News reports on the 2024 expulsion from one outlet critical of the government.
1. 14ymedio: "Expulsan a Alina Bárbara López de la Uneac por criticar a la 'alta dirigencia de la Revolución'" (2024)
URL: https://www.14ymedio.com/cuba/expulsan-alina-barbara-lopez-uneac_1_1106246.html
- Reports López's reply: she exercises rights to free expression and peaceful protest under article 56 of the Constitution, and the decision did not follow the law.
- Names Luis Morlote as president, which OnCuba and Prensa Latina contradict.
2. 14ymedio: "Jorge Fernández Era renuncia a la Uneac en solidaridad con Alina Bárbara López" (2024)
URL: https://www.14ymedio.com/cuba/jorge-fernandez-renuncia-uneac-solidaridad_1_1106327.html
- Reports his letter to the UNEAC presidency, which says the union "se limpió las manos" (washed its hands, my translation) of the complaints he and López filed.

WIDER CONTEXT
- PEN International, an advocacy group, records that police detained López for several hours in April 2024 and for 11 hours on 18 June 2024, and charged her under article 182 of the Penal Code with attempted assault on police officers, which carries three to eight years; PEN holds that the charge aims to limit her critical activity.
- The police, not UNEAC, detained her.

BOTTOM LINE
The union's money is members' and state money, with no private sponsor of note. The weak points are the Party's say over its leadership and the union's own punishment of critics.
