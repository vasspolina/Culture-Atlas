SLOUGHT: Philadelphia, USA
ID: slought-philadelphia
Website: https://slought.org

OVERVIEW
- Experimental cultural organisation and 501(c)(3) nonprofit at 4017 Walnut Street in University City, between 40th and 41st streets, next to the Daily Pennsylvanian's offices; it is affiliated with the University of Pennsylvania (Slought; 34th Street Magazine).
- Founded in 2002 by Aaron Levy, English professor Al Filreis and Tom Lussenhop of Penn's real estate office, as the first gallery of the Slought Networks programme; the gallery was set to open on 18 October 2002 (Daily Pennsylvanian, 2002). Levy was executive director and chief curator from 2002 to 2022 (Slought).
- Private institution separate from Penn, which leased it the space for six years from 2002 (Daily Pennsylvanian, 2002).
- Building: a historically designated building whose status limits outdoor art and signs (34th Street Magazine, 2021).
- Programmes included Into the Open (2008), Mixplace Studio (2009 to 2012), Architecture on Display (2010) and Ai Weiwei's Fairytale Project (2011) (Slought; 34th Street Magazine). Into the Open represented the US at the 2008 Venice Architecture Biennale (34th Street Magazine).
- The website describes the organisation as a twenty-year history, and says its spirit continues as Public Trust.

FUNDING
Slought: independent nonprofit: a Pew project grant, a university lease and programme partnerships with Penn
- Pew Center for Arts & Heritage: $25,000 Discovery Grant in 2011 for Mixplace Studio, which paired West Philadelphia youth with Penn architecture and design students (Pew Center for Arts & Heritage).
- University of Pennsylvania: six-year lease on the Walnut Street space from 2002 and programme partner (Daily Pennsylvanian; Slought).
- Into the Open (2008 to 2009), the US Pavilion at the Venice Architecture Biennale, was organised on behalf of the US Department of State and the National Endowment for the Arts; Levy was one of its co-curators (Slought).
- Levy said the lack of institutional money pushed Slought toward collaborative, process-based work; the organisation operates independently with little oversight from Penn's administration (34th Street Magazine, 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
Slought ran as an independent nonprofit with a $25,000 Pew grant in 2011 for Mixplace Studio and a six-year University of Pennsylvania lease from 2002. Its director co-curated the 2008 US Pavilion at Venice, which was organised on behalf of the US Department of State and the National Endowment for the Arts.
