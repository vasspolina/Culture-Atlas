MUSEO DE LA SOLIDARIDAD SALVADOR ALLENDE: Santiago, Chile
ID: museo-de-la-solidaridad-salvador-allende
Website: https://www.mssa.cl (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art at Av. República 475, Santiago, near República and Toesca Metro stations (museum site).
- Art critic José María Moreno Galván proposed it in 1971 and President Salvador Allende approved it; it opened in May 1972 without its own building. About 650 works arrived from abroad, including pieces by Joan Miró and Roberto Matta (Wikipedia).
- It closed after the 1973 coup, was continued abroad by exiled organisers from 1975, and reopened in 1991 at the National Museum of Fine Arts. Since 2005 the Arte y Solidaridad Foundation has managed it (Wikipedia).
- It occupies the Palacio Heiremans, built in 1925 for a Belgian entrepreneur. Under the Pinochet dictatorship it served as a detention centre and a DINA listening station (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 18:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00 (museum site). No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museo de la Solidaridad Salvador Allende: mainly public: Chilean state transfers through the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural, plus project grants
- 2025: the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural gave the Fundación Arte y Solidaridad, which runs the museum, CLP 660,197,000 of basal funding, 75.1 percent of it for staff (2025 funding report, museum PDF). Ex-Ante (T13, 11 April 2025) adds that pay cannot exceed public-sector pay, unspent funds go back at year's end, and the transfers date from 2005.
- Other 2025 money, in pesos: 21,785,143 from the Fondo Nacional de Desarrollo Cultural y las Artes (a Chile-France publication); 15,500,000 from the Senate under a loan agreement for the collection; 1,111,203 from the University of Pennsylvania; 7,616,486 from the Henry Moore Foundation (2025 funding report).
- 2024: the state subsidy was CLP 592,136,000, 79.9 percent spent on staff. Other funds: US$30,000 from the FfAI Foundation for Arts Initiatives and US$6,000 from the Italian Cultural Institute (2024 annual report). The Mellon Foundation, through the University of Pennsylvania, and Chile's Ministry of Cultures supported Bruguera's 2023 visit (El Mostrador, citing the museum's site).
- The foundation's board has five members: one each from the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio and the Ministry of Education, two from the Salvador Allende Foundation and one by common agreement (2024 annual report). No named board members or private donors were read.

Red flags: Protest over the Tania Bruguera exhibition
- Campaign: Allende's grandson Pablo Sepúlveda Allende wrote to the museum asking for the cancellation of Bruguera's "Magnitud 11.9" and posted comments on X (The Art Newspaper). In August 2023 he led a declaration against it with over 600 signatures, and said museum director Claudia Zaldívar is not "a la altura del cargo" (El Mostrador, my translation: not up to the post).
- Outcome: the show was delayed and opened on 8 October 2023 with changes (CIMAM). The museum says one of its central subjects was suppressed (The Art Newspaper).
- Museum response: Zaldívar said the attempt came from a small group and that the Salvador Allende Foundation approved the show (The Art Newspaper).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Salvador Allende Foundation, a separate body, received about $98 million from the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio in February 2025 after the failed purchase of Allende's house on Guardia Vieja; board member Genaro Cuadros is a defendant in the criminal case (Ex-Ante, T13).
- Connection: the museum's funding comes from the same state service, and two of its five board members come from the Salvador Allende Foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly Chilean state funding, with conditions on staff pay and returned surplus, plus named grants for individual projects. The weak points are the 2023 campaign to cancel a show, which the museum modified, and the political attention on state transfers to Allende-linked foundations. Worth watching: whether the museum names private donors.
