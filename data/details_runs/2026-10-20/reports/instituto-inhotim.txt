INSTITUTO INHOTIM: Brumadinho, Brazil
ID: instituto-inhotim
Website: https://www.inhotim.org.br

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum and botanical garden in Brumadinho, Minas Gerais, about 60 km from Belo Horizonte (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 2004 by Bernardo Paz, described by Wikipedia as a former mining magnate, to house his art collection; opened to the public in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- The landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx designed the botanical garden (Wikipedia).
- In June 2022 Paz donated 326 works from his collection and an area of 140 hectares to the institute (Carta Capital, Ana Paula Sousa, 22 February 2024); Wikipedia gives the donation as 2021 to 2022, with the grounds, galleries, pavilions and 330 works.
- On site: galleries named for artists, including Adriana Varejão, Cildo Meireles and Tunga, and themed gardens such as the Garden of All the Senses, the Desert Garden and the Orchid Square (Inhotim website).
- Visiting: a full ticket cost R$ 60 and a half-price ticket R$ 30 in April 2025; entry is free on Wednesdays and on the last Sunday of each month (Portal Agita, 11 April 2025). Brumadinho residents registered in the "Nosso Inhotim" programme and children aged 0 to 5 enter free every day (Itatiaia, 26 June 2023).

FUNDING
Instituto Inhotim: corporate-sponsored and tax-incentive funded: Vale, Shell, Itaú, Santander and the Rouanet law
- Vale: has sponsored Inhotim for more than a decade. In 2023 it committed up to R$ 400 million over ten years, to maintain active collecting, expand artistic and cultural programming and expand socio-educational projects (Itatiaia, Bruna Truocchio, 26 June 2023). Carta Capital reports that half is incentivised money and half is Vale's own investment (Ana Paula Sousa, "No meio do minério, a arte", 22 February 2024).
- The Inhotim website lists the Instituto Cultural Vale and Descentra Cultura as master maintainers, Shell and Itaú as master sponsors and Santander as gold sponsor. It names the Culture Incentive Law (Rouanet Law) and, as realisation, the Minas Gerais culture and tourism secretariat and the federal Ministry of Culture.
- Stella Artois: four-year sponsorship, reported on 4 September 2026 (Meio & Mensagem, Ana Serra).
- Shell: sponsored free admission to Inhotim on Sunday 13 April 2025, marking its 112 years in Brazil (Portal Agita, 11 April 2025).
- Founder: Paz funded about 70% of the operating budget before the 2021 to 2022 donation (Wikipedia). In 2017 a federal court in Belo Horizonte convicted Paz of money laundering; he was acquitted in 2020 (Wikipedia).
- The institute's council has 28 members, from large businesspeople and bankers to artists (Carta Capital).

Red flags: Mining disaster
- Vale committed in 2023 to up to R$ 400 million over ten years for the institute (Itatiaia, 26 June 2023), and the Instituto Cultural Vale is listed as a master maintainer on the Inhotim website.
- At the start of 2019 the Córrego do Feijão mine of Vale collapsed in Brumadinho, leaving 270 dead. Inhotim is about 20 km from the mine, was not physically hit by the mud and closed for 15 days; about 80% of its staff live in the town (Carta Capital, 22 February 2024).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Shell is listed on Inhotim's own website as a master sponsor, and sponsored the free-entry day of 13 April 2025 (Inhotim website, Portal Agita, 11 April 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One art-press piece concerns the institute's treatment of Black artists.
1. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Artist Maxwell Alexandre Goes to War With Brazil's Inhotim Institute" (8 December 2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/artist-maxwell-alexandre-goes-to-war-with-brazils-inhotim-institute
- Alexandre asked Inhotim to remove his work from the "Quilombo" exhibition, called the institute a "white man's theater" in Instagram posts and criticised its lack of staff diversity.
- Inhotim removed the work but kept the rest of the exhibition, scheduled to run through July 2023. A spokesperson said the 2021 purchase of his work carried no display conditions. The institute said it was working towards a more significant presence of Black artists in future programmes and broader diversity in decision-making roles.
- Criticises the institution, not its funders.

BOTTOM LINE
Inhotim is run by a nonprofit institute funded by corporate sponsorship and Rouanet tax incentives. Vale, whose Córrego do Feijão mine collapsed at the start of 2019 and left 270 dead, committed in 2023 to up to R$ 400 million over ten years, and Shell is listed as a master sponsor and paid for a free-entry day in April 2025. Wikipedia reports that the founder paid about 70% of the operating budget before his 2021 to 2022 donation.
