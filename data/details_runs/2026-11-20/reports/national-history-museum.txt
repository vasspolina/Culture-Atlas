NATIONAL HISTORY MUSEUM: Tirana, Albania
ID: national-history-museum
Website: https://tirana.al/en/points-of-interest/museums/national-historical-museum (checked 2026-11-20; city of Tirana visitor page)

OVERVIEW
- The largest museum in Albania, on Skanderbeg Square in central Tirana, opened on 28 October 1981 (Tirana municipality; New Eastern Europe). It stands on the site of a neo-Renaissance town hall built during the Second World War (New Eastern Europe, 2023).
- Built under the communist regime of Enver Hoxha between September 1978 and October 1981 (Shqiptarja). Architects included Sokrat Mosko, Enver Faja, Nina Shehu and Petraq Kolevica (Tirana municipality).
- The building covers 27,000 square metres, 18,000 of them for exhibitions, and is listed as a second-category cultural monument (Tirana municipality; Shqiptarja).
- Over the entrance is a 565 square metre socialist realist mosaic of 1981 by Vilson Kilica, Josif Droboniku, Agim Nebiu, Anastas Kostandini and Aleksandër Filipi (UNOPS). After a 1993 government decision ordering the removal of communist symbols, Nebiu retouched out the worker's red book and two stars (Shqiptarja, 23 May 2023).
- More than 6,200 objects from the fourth millennium BC to the late 20th century in eight pavilions: Antiquity, Middle Ages, National Renaissance, Independence, Second World War and Resistance, Communist Terror, Iconography and Mother Teresa (Tirana municipality). The Communist Terror pavilion opened on 20 February 2012 (Top Channel).
- The building also holds part of the Albanian archives and laboratories (Tirana municipality) and has stored the National Gallery of Arts collection during that gallery's reconstruction (Shqiptarja, March 2024).
- 99,796 visitors in 2023 (Shqiptarja, citing the former Ministry of Culture).
- Closed on 15 March 2024 for restoration, reconstruction and a new display; reopening is planned for 15 March 2028 (Shqiptarja; Balkanweb). In 2024 the ministry reopened the competition for a director; Entela Çipa, who also runs the National Gallery of Arts, had held the post as acting director (Shqiptarja).

FUNDING
National History Museum: public: Albanian state, with EU money for the reconstruction
- Owner: an Albanian state museum; in March 2024 the Ministry of Economy, Culture and Innovation ran its director competition (Shqiptarja). Culture Minister Blendi Gonxhja announced the completion of the facade restoration (Balkanweb).
- State budget: the domestically financed Public Investment plan for 2024 to 2026 set aside 606,093,000 lek for the museum, 121,219,000 lek of it in 2024 (Shqiptarja, citing the plan).
- European Union: the museum is one of 24 earthquake-damaged heritage sites in EU4Culture, a 40 million euro EU programme run by UNOPS with the Ministry of Culture (UNOPS). EU4Culture restored the facade mosaic, inaugurated in May 2023 (UNOPS). UNOPS opened the tender for the building's restoration, reconstruction and new display on 5 January 2024 (Shqiptarja).
- A 350,000 euro European Investment Bank grant paid for advisory services on the restoration (Balkanweb).
- At a December 2023 presentation the ministry said the works would also draw on the annual budget and that the Japanese government would support the museum's digitisation (Shqiptarja). The display concept is by Casanova + Hernandez architects; the structural work was presented by Gent Agolli of the studio I RI (Shqiptarja).

Red flags: cost and transparency of the 2024 to 2028 reconstruction
- Estimates of the cost differ. Shqiptarja (31 Oct 2018), citing the agency ATA, reported that Arta Dollani, director of the Institute of Cultural Monuments, put a preliminary value of up to 13 million euros on the project in a television interview. On Report TV in March 2024 the former museum director Melsi Labi said the budget would be at least 26 million euros, a figure he said had appeared in the media (Shqiptarja, 11 Mar 2024).
- Labi called the lack of information "worrying" and asked who the restorers were, how many researchers were involved and why there was no clear project, saying the money belongs to taxpayers (Shqiptarja, 11 Mar 2024).
- The four-year closure is a year longer than the original construction took; the museum deleted its closure notice from social media and gave no details of where the collection would go (Shqiptarja, March 2024).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Dark Tourism writes that under Hoxha the partisan war against fascism "had to be especially celebrated", and that the section on the Hoxha years was gone by the author's 2011 visit; the Communist Terror display focuses on repression and its victims and gives Hoxha a marginal role.
- Kinga Anna Gajda in New Eastern Europe (2023) calls the museum "a tool of memory appropriation and amnesia"; in 1991 its exhibition on building socialism and its monuments to Stalin and Hoxha were removed.
- The museum was built by that regime and opened as its national history display, so its building and mosaic carry this history.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money: the Albanian state and European public lenders and donors. The weak point is how openly that money is accounted for during the reconstruction. Worth watching: the final cost and whether it reopens on time.
