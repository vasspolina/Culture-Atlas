CHÂTEAU DE VERSAILLES: Versailles, France
ID: ch-teau-de-versailles
Website: https://www.chateauversailles.fr/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Former royal palace and national museum in Versailles, Yvelines, about 16 km southwest of Paris; the park covers about 815 hectares (Wikipedia).
- Louis XIII built a hunting lodge on the site in 1623; Louis XIV enlarged it into a palace after 1661, and Louis-Philippe opened the Museum of the History of France there on 30 June 1837 (Wikipedia).
- Built by Louis Le Vau and Jules Hardouin-Mansart, who added the Hall of Mirrors, with gardens by André Le Nôtre; a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979.
- Owned by the French state and run since 1995 as a public administrative body under the Ministry of Culture and the budget ministry (COPEPS, Wikipedia); Christophe Leribault became its president on 4 March 2024 (Ministry of Culture) and was named president of the Louvre on 25 February 2026 (Artdependence). No reporting found of his successor at Versailles.
- The Salon de Diane in the King's State Apartment reopened in June 2026 after about 18 months of work on its ceiling, walls and Bernini's bust of Louis XIV (stated by the space).
- It had 8.4 million visitors in 2024, 83 percent from abroad. Since 14 January 2026 visitors from outside the European Economic Area pay 35 euros in high season instead of 32 euros (AFP via Noovo). No opening hours or accessibility details were found in a source opened.

FUNDING
Château de Versailles: mainly self-funded public body: tickets and commerce, with a state subsidy and private patrons
- Its consolidated budget is about 170 million euros a year; ticket sales and commercial activity cover most running costs, and an annual state subsidy pays part of investment (COPEPS).
- About 75 percent of the budget comes from its own revenue: tickets, shops, events and private hire; maintenance costs more than 60 million euros a year (L'Essentiel de l'Éco, 2025).
- The higher non-EEA ticket price is projected to bring about 9.3 million euros a year (AFP via Noovo).
- Dior and The American Friends of Versailles paid for the Salon de Diane restoration, with help from the Société des Amis de Versailles (stated by the space). An American Friends of Versailles gala with Becca Cason Thrash as honorary chair raised 3.5 million dollars for the restoration (PaperCity, July 2026).
- Leribault said state funding cuts make basic repairs hard to fund (Apollo, 2025).
- No reporting found of the amount Dior gave.

Overall: Little criticism of the institution in art media; coverage of the restoration and patronage is largely admiring.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Jeffrey Epstein and the 2019 Pritzker Architecture Prize: in a September 2018 email to Jack Lang, also sent to Caroline Lang, Epstein offered to suggest a venue for the prize ceremony and noted that the Hall of Mirrors had been used as the venue before. The email was among files the US government made public on 30 January 2026 (The Architects' Journal, 23 February 2026).
- This was a venue use, not funding. The 2019 ceremony was held at the Palace of Versailles; The Architects' Journal reports no payment or funding from Epstein to Versailles and says it is unclear whether Epstein acted on his own or for Tom Pritzker. The Hyatt Foundation sponsors the prize.
- Tom Pritzker retired as Hyatt executive chairman on 17 February 2026 and said he regretted his association with Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell (PBS NewsHour).
- On 15 November 2018 the anticorruption association Fricc filed a complaint in Paris alleging fraud and tax fraud over LVMH's tax deductions for the Fondation Louis Vuitton; LVMH said the complaint was written to damage its reputation. The Cour des comptes found that tax reductions for LVMH companies made up about 8.1 percent of the state's total tax spending on corporate patronage over the period (La Libre).
- Connection to Versailles: the palace was the venue for the 2019 Pritzker ceremony, which is its only link to the Epstein emails, and Christian Dior Couture, a patron of the Salon de Diane, is a subsidiary of LVMH (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
Versailles runs mostly on its own ticket and commercial income, with a state subsidy for investment and private patrons for restorations; none of that money is from a flagged industry. The Epstein material is a venue use at the 2019 Pritzker ceremony, not funding, and no source shows money or leadership ties. Worth watching: the undisclosed size of Dior's gift and LVMH's use of patronage tax relief.
