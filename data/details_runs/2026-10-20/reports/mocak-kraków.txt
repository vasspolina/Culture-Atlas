MOCAK: Kraków, Poland
ID: mocak-kraków
Website: https://www.mocak.pl

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Contemporary Art on Lipowa Street in Kraków, about 3 km from the city centre, on a demolished part of Oskar Schindler's former factory site (Wikipedia).
- Established on 1 February 2010 and opened on 19 May 2011; Maria Anna (Masza) Potocka was its first director (Wikipedia). The museum's site describes it as an institution of culture of the City of Kraków.
- Building designed by Claudio Nardi, with 4,000 square metres of exhibition space in a 10,000 square metre complex (Wikipedia).
- On site: permanent and temporary exhibitions, a library, a bookshop, a café and a contemporary art conservation laboratory (Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Wikipedia says Potocka led the museum from 2010 until 2024 and was dismissed over workplace bullying allegations that a court confirmed. Her successor Adam Budak was dismissed in May 2026 and Grzegorz Kuźma has been acting director since 12 May 2026 (ArtReview). On 19 October 2026 the museum's site announced that, by order of the President of the City of Kraków, the competition for a new director was cancelled.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 19:00, at ul. Lipowa 4; the site links to an accessibility page and online ticket sales (museum website).

FUNDING
MOCAK: public: an institution of culture of the City of Kraków, with Ministry of Culture grants and EU funds for the building
- The museum's site names it "instytucja kultury miasta Krakowa", an institution of culture of the City of Kraków. The city bought the buildings and land of the former Schindler factory in 2004, and the Ministry of Culture granted 4 million PLN to the project at the mayor's application (MOCAK website, history page).
- Building: total cost over 70 million PLN, of which 35 million PLN was co-financed from EU funds (MOCAK website, history page).
- The museum's page of Ministry of Culture and National Heritage grants includes 200,000 PLN in 2026 for the collection (Narodowa Kolekcja Sztuki Współczesnej programme), 470,000 PLN in 2023 for the collection, 269,999 PLN for 2026 and 2027 for a Kiki Smith retrospective, 312,978 PLN for 2026 and 2027 for an Eleanor Antin retrospective, and 882,000 PLN for 2025 to 2027 for ventilation and air-conditioning modernisation (MOCAK website).
- The partners page, which carries no year, lists STRABAG under "Sponsor Muzeum" (a logo linking to strabag.pl), Tribe Hotels as hotel partner, RE-Bau as patron of the Re Gallery, Electro-System and REMONDIS as sponsors of the installation "Gniazdo Katatzyny" by Katarzyna Krakowiak-Bałka, and the Ministry of Culture (MKiDN) among partner institutions (MOCAK website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The press coverage concerns the management of the museum and the dismissal of its directors, not its funders or its programme.
1. ArtReview: no named author, "Adam Budak dismissed as director of MOCAK amid controversy" (18 May 2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/adam-budak-dismissed-as-director-of-mocak-amid-controversy/
- The city of Kraków announced the mayor's decision to dismiss Budak in a statement of 16 May 2026, citing "the finding of improper performance of duties related to work organization and team management"; his employment ends with his notice period on 30 June.
- According to Onet, cited by ArtReview, the investigation followed a misconduct complaint signed by 37 employees and submitted to City Hall.
- Budak said the dismissal "has no legal basis and was made under time pressure" and that he was considering an appeal.
- Monika Drożyńska, Wilhelm Sasnal, Katarzyna Krakowiak-Bałka and Gabrielle Goliath withdrew from MOCAK's 2026 to 2027 programme in solidarity with him; Paulina Ołowska started a petition for reconsideration.
- Concerns workplace management, not funding or the art.

BOTTOM LINE
MOCAK is an institution of culture of the City of Kraków. The museum's site lists grants from the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage, and 35 million PLN of the building's cost of over 70 million PLN came from EU funds. The documented problems are two director dismissals over workplace management, in 2024 (Wikipedia) and 2026 (ArtReview).
