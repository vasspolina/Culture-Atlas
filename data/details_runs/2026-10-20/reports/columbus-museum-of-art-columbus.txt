COLUMBUS MUSEUM OF ART: Columbus, USA
ID: columbus-museum-of-art-columbus
Website: https://www.columbusmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 480 East Broad Street in downtown Columbus, Ohio, with a second site, CMA at The Pizzuti, at 632 Park Street in the Short North Arts District.
- Founded in 1878 as the Columbus Gallery of Fine Arts, the first art museum in Ohio to receive a state charter; renamed the Columbus Museum of Art in 1978 (Wikipedia). Brooke A. Minto is Executive Director and CEO.
- Merged with the Columbus Art School in 1887; the school later became the Columbus College of Art & Design, and the two separated in 1987.
- Building: the 1931 building was designed by Richards, McCarty and Bulford (Wikipedia). The 50,000 sq ft Margaret M. Walter Wing, designed by Michael Bongiorno of DesignGroup, opened in October 2015 (Wikipedia).
- On site: collections of late 19th- and early 20th-century American and European modern art, with holdings of Aminah Robinson, Elijah Pierce and George Bellows; the Center for Creativity, an 18,000 sq ft learning space that opened in 2011 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: main museum open Tuesday to Sunday (Thursday until 9 pm), closed Monday. Adults 26 and over $22, seniors 60 and over $18, students 26 and over with ID $16, visitors 25 and under free from 1 July 2026, members free, Sundays free. Restrooms are ADA accessible; free stroller rentals and sensory resources are available, and a multi-use comfort room is described as suitable for low-stimulation breaks.

FUNDING
Columbus Museum of Art: donor-funded: Columbus Foundation funds, Walter Foundation, Art Bridges Foundation, Battelle and JPMorgan Chase
- Its About page states that the Henry D. and Carol B. Clark, Paul-Henri Bourguignon and Erika Bourguignon Fund for Visual Arts, and Fred R. Place funds of The Columbus Foundation provide ongoing support. The museum's October 2023 Art Bridges release says the same of the Greater Columbus Arts Council, Nationwide Foundation and Ohio Arts Council.
- Walter Foundation: a $4 million lead gift for Access 150, a five-year programme to June 2031 that pays for free daily admission for visitors 25 and under, youth and teen programming, two new staff posts and a redesign of the Wonder Room gallery (museum release, 9 June 2026). The foundation was founded by Bob and Peggy Walter; the museum's 2015 wing carries the Walter name.
- Art Bridges Foundation: a $1.2 million grant from its Access for All program for Free Sundays and quarterly Community Days, announced on 12 October 2023; the museum says Free Sundays continue through 2026 (museum release and tickets page).
- Battelle: a $1.2 million gift to establish the three-year Fund for Learning, announced on 20 September 2023 (museum release).
- JPMorgan Chase: a $1 million donation toward the museum's renovation and expansion, honouring the late Bank One patriarch John G. McCoy (JPMorgan Chase press release, 2015); the museum's learning space is the JPMorgan Chase Center for Creativity (museum release, 2023).
- Pizzuti family: Ann and Ron Pizzuti, whose wealth came from the Pizzuti Companies real estate firm, gave the Pizzuti Collection building and part of its collection to the museum in September 2018 (Wikipedia; museum).
- Total revenue for the fiscal year to June 2025 was $13,954,674, of which contributions were $9,354,076 (Form 990 data, ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).

BOTTOM LINE
Contributions were $9.35 million of $13.95 million revenue in the fiscal year to June 2025. Named gifts are the Walter Foundation's $4 million for Access 150 (2026), Art Bridges' $1.2 million and Battelle's $1.2 million (2023), JPMorgan Chase's $1 million (2015) and the Pizzuti family's building gift (2018), alongside funds of The Columbus Foundation.
