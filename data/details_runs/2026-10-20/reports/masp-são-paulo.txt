MASP: São Paulo, Brazil
ID: masp-são-paulo
Website: https://masp.org.br/ (checked 2026-10-24; redirects to https://masp.com.br/, live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art museum at Avenida Paulista 1578, São Paulo, reached by Metro Line 2 (Trianon-Masp station). It holds more than 8,000 pieces (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the media owner Assis Chateaubriand of Diários Associados with the Italian critic Pietro Maria Bardi; opened 2 October 1947 on the upper floors of the Diários Associados headquarters (Wikipedia).
- Present building designed by Lina Bo Bardi, a concrete-and-glass block suspended on four pillars over a 74-metre free span; built from the late 1950s and opened on 7 November 1968, inaugurated by Queen Elizabeth II (Wikipedia).
- A 14-storey annex, the Pietro Maria Bardi Building, opened in March 2025, designed by Metro Arquitetos Associados; it adds 7,821 square metres and raises exhibition space by 66% (Poder360; The Art Newspaper).
- Collection strongest in European painting, with Brazilian and Latin American art, and African, Asian and ancient works (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10 am to 6 pm, Thursday until 8 pm, closed Monday. Adults R$75, students and over-60s R$37; free for all visitors on Tuesdays and on Friday evenings 6 to 10 pm, and free for people with disabilities and one companion (museum website).

FUNDING
MASP: corporate-sponsored and private donors, with state development bank BNDES as a supporter
- Partner tiers on the museum's page: Strategic Partner Itaú; Master Sponsors Nubank, AkzoNobel, B3, BASF, Bradesco, Citi, Google, Levy e Salomão, Havaianas, Instituto Votorantim, McKinsey & Company, Klabin, QI Tech, Vitru Educação, Uber, Vivo; Financial Supporter BNDES; Sponsors Tintas Coral, Goodyear, Bloomberg, Levisky, Ultra, Unilever, Unipar. The page does not say what each partner pays for or how much.
- Vitru Educação joined as master sponsor in June 2025: R$2 million direct, up to R$7 million a year with incentive-law funds, for the public-programmes and mediation programme (Times Brasil).
- The annex cost about R$250 million (Poder360) or $43 million over six years (The Art Newspaper), funded entirely by private donors: collector Cleusa Garfinkel, Denise Aguiar Alvarez of Fundação Bradesco, André and Lilian Esteves, and Ronaldo Cezar Coelho. Individual amounts are not stated. Poder360 counts donations from more than 20 families.
- No reporting found of MASP's total revenue in the pages read for this report; annual reports and financial statements are posted on the museum's site.

Red flags: fossil fuels
- Ultra is a named Sponsor on the museum's current partner page. Ultrapar, known as the Ultra Group, owns the fuel distributor Ipiranga, which has more than 6,500 service stations, and the gas distributor Ultragaz (Wikipedia). The page does not say how much Ultra pays.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is mostly admiring of the building and programme. The criticism found concerns curatorial decisions, not funders.
1. ArtReview: unsigned, "Censorship row engulfs Museum of Art São Paulo" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/censorship-row-engulfs-museum-of-art-sao-paulo/
- MASP removed photographs of the Landless Workers Movement (MST) by João Zinclar, André Vilaron and Edgar Kanaykõ from its 2022 survey Histórias Brasileiras; curator Hélio Menezes called it censorship, and co-curators Sandra Benites and Clarissa Diniz said they felt "wronged and disrespected".
- Notes the photos were pulled the same week a book launch by activist Guilherme Boulos was cancelled.
- The museum says the photos arrived after the deadline and that the Boulos event was a political demonstration; the co-curators say they were never told of a deadline.
2. The Art Newspaper: Ela Bittencourt, "Museu de Arte de São Paulo traces its own history and evolution across exhibitions in its new tower" (2025)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/04/02/museo-arte-sao-paulo-annex-opening-exhibitions
- A review of the annex's opening shows. Calls the annex "striking"; notes construction on the linking tunnel would continue until late 2025 and fencing partly blocked the façade.
- Criticises neither funders nor management.

WIDER CONTEXT
- More than 260 UK artists asked five London theatres in May 2026 to end ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies, saying it is "complicit in grave war crimes" through links to Israeli settlements (The New Arab, 29 May 2026).
- Poder360 reports social-media mockery of the annex's black design as "a CPU da avenida Paulista".
- Connection: "Bloomberg" is a named MASP sponsor on the museum's partner page; the campaign names the theatres, and no reporting found of a campaign aimed at MASP.

BOTTOM LINE
MASP's money is mostly private and corporate, with Itaú as strategic partner and a state development bank as supporter. The weak point is Ultra, a fuel-distribution group, on the current sponsor list; Bloomberg's name also sits there while a campaign targets Bloomberg Philanthropies at UK theatres. Worth watching: whether MASP publishes sponsor amounts and how it answers the 2022 censorship row.
