MUSEU DE ARTE POPULAR: Lisbon, Portugal
ID: museu-de-arte-popular
Website: https://museuartepopular.wordpress.com (checked 2026-11-20; the home page returns "page not found", the inner pages work)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of Portuguese folk art on Avenida de Brasília in Belém, Lisbon (Time Out; MNE programme 2019).
- The building began as the Folk Life Section pavilion of the 1940 Portuguese World Exhibition, designed by António Reis Camelo and João Simões; Jorge Segurado adapted it as a museum to a programme by the ethnographer Francisco Lage and the artist Tomás de Mello (museum site).
- It opened in 1948, on 15 June by the museum's account and 15 July in Maria de Barthez's history (museum site; Barthez, Universidade Nova de Lisboa). Lage was its first director (Barthez).
- Its murals of the regions are by Tomás de Mello, Carlos Botelho, Eduardo Anahory, Estrela Faria, Manuel Lapa and Paulo Ferreira; they were the grounds for classing the building a Monument of Public Interest in 2012 (museum site).
- The collection holds ceramics, folk jewellery, musical instruments, basketry, textiles, costume, model boats and carts, and farm tools (museum site).
- The first permanent display ran through five rooms by region; visitors numbered about 45,000 a year in the 1960s and 75,000 in the early 1970s (Barthez).
- It was closed from 1974 to 1980, was placed under the Museu Nacional de Etnologia in the late 1980s, regained autonomy in 1997 and closed again in 2003 (Barthez).
- It reopened in December 2016 without its original ethnographic display; since 2016 its work has been tied to the mission of the Museu Nacional de Etnologia (Time Out; museum site).
- Current show: "Produção Artesanal Portuguesa: a atualidade do saber-fazer ancestral", produced by DGARTES for the national Saber Fazer Portugal programme, 22 October 2026 to 30 April 2027 (museum site).
- Time Out listed it in 2020 as open Wednesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, admission €5 (Time Out).

FUNDING
Museu de Arte Popular: public: Portuguese state
- Founded and run from 1948 to 1974 by the Estado Novo's SNI, which managed it directly (Barthez).
- The 2003 closure was for a renovation under the culture operational programme POC 2000 to 2006 (Barthez).
- In 2019 it was one of two sites of the Museu Nacional de Etnologia / Museu de Arte Popular, with Directorate-General for Cultural Heritage (DGPC) addresses; its collection was moved to the ethnology museum in 2006 (MNE programme 2019; museum site).
- Decree-Law 79/2023 created Museus e Monumentos de Portugal E.P.E., which took over DGPC's management of national museums, monuments and palaces (European Heritage Hub).

Red flags: 1948 founding by the Estado Novo's propaganda secretariat
- The museum grew out of António Ferro's "política do espírito" at the SPN/SNI; its ties to propaganda aims and direct dependence on the SNI kept its display unchanged from 1948 to 1974 (Barthez).
- The ethnology museum's own 2019 programme called the 1948 murals "de cunho marcadamente ideológico" (markedly ideological; translation), tied to the Estado Novo's nationalist propaganda (MNE programme 2019).

Red flags: 2006 to 2008 closure plan and protests
- In 2006 culture minister Isabel Pires de Lima announced the museum would become a museum of the Portuguese language; between October 2006 and 2008 its archive and library were moved to the ethnology museum (Barthez; DN).
- ICOM Portugal issued a statement on the risk of dismantling and dispersing the collections (Barthez).
- A citizens' campaign ran a blog and an online petition with more than 3,000 signatures; Alexandre Pomar, Catarina Portas, Rosa Pomar, Raquel Henriques da Silva and Joana Vasconcelos were among its faces (DN, 2010).
- Minister Gabriela Canavilhas later said the museum should stay dedicated to Portuguese folk art; architect Andreia Galvão was asked to lead a reopening (DN, 2010).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from museum studies and opinion pages, and is aimed at the state's treatment of the museum since 1974.
1. Observador: Helena Matos, "O museu que não pode ser" (2018)
URL: https://observador.pt/opiniao/o-museu-que-nao-pode-ser/
- Opinion column: the museum was closed for political reasons, because it was seen as Salazarist propaganda, not for lack of visitors.
- The collection is kept in the ethnology museum's stores but not shown; the language museum announced in 2006 was never built.
- Names Isabel Pires de Lima and the DGPC.
2. Maria de Barthez, "Ascensão, decadência e reinvenção de um museu: o Museu de Arte Popular (1948-2010)"
URL: https://ler.letras.up.pt/uploads/ficheiros/10308.pdf
- Academic article: the museum suffered steady neglect after 1974 and the ethnology museum treated it as secondary, with too few staff.
- Calls the 2006 closure decision a purely political one.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money throughout, with no private funder. The weak point is its origin as a propaganda museum of the dictatorship, and a long record of state neglect that its supporters protested.
