NATIONAL MUSEUM OF NATURAL HISTORY (SMITHSONIAN): Washington, USA
ID: national-museum-of-natural-history-smithsonian
Website: https://naturalhistory.si.edu/ (checked 2026-10-24; refused automated requests with HTTP 403, so its pages were not read)

OVERVIEW
- Smithsonian natural history museum in Washington, holding over 148 million specimens; the building is by the Washington firm Hornblower & Marshall (Wikipedia).
- The United States National Museum was founded in 1846 as part of the Smithsonian; the Natural History Building opened to the public on 17 March 1910 (Wikipedia).
- Its halls include the Sant Ocean Hall (opened 27 September 2008) and the David H. Koch Hall of Human Origins (opened 17 March 2010).
- Admission is free and the museum is open 364 days a year; it recorded about 3.3 million visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
National Museum of Natural History: mainly public: US Congress through the Smithsonian, with named private donors
- The Smithsonian budget is about 1.25 billion dollars a year, about two-thirds from federal appropriations (Wikipedia, Smithsonian Institution).
- The Roger Sant family gave 15 million dollars in 2008 to endow the Ocean Hall (Wikipedia).
- A 5 million dollar American Petroleum Institute offer for the Ocean Hall was withdrawn by the donor before the Board of Regents considered it (Wikipedia).

Red flags: David H. Koch (fossil fuels), campaigned name
- Koch was a member of the museum's advisory board, not a trustee; Hyperallergic (21 January 2016) says he still sat on it. No source read gives the year he joined or left.
- He and his brother Charles became majority owners of Koch Industries in June 1983 (Wikipedia). Salon (24 March 2015) says he spent over 67 million dollars since 1997 funding groups that undermine climate science. He died in 2019.
- His gifts: 15 million dollars toward the Hall of Human Origins, which opened on 17 March 2010, and 35 million dollars in May 2012 toward the dinosaur hall (Wikipedia).
- In March 2015 an open letter signed by 36 scientists went to more than 330 museums asking them to cut ties with fossil fuel funders; fifteen non-profits asked the Smithsonian and the American Museum of Natural History to remove Koch from their boards (Salon).
- In June 2015 protesters delivered a petition of about 430,000 signatures to Smithsonian representatives asking it to remove him from the advisory board; he stayed on it (Hyperallergic).
- A museum spokesperson denied that Koch shaped the Hall of Human Origins (Salon).

Red flags: White House review, 2025
- On 12 August 2025 White House officials Lindsey Halligan, Vince Haley and Russell Vought sent Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch a letter starting a review of eight museums, this one included. It covers exhibition text, social media and the curatorial process, with content corrections to begin within 120 days (NPR, 12 August 2025). The Smithsonian said it was reviewing the letter.

Red flags: Arctic exhibit, 2006
- Unverified: about the exhibit "Arctic: A Friend Acting Strangely" (April to November 2006), Robert Sullivan, the museum's former associate director for exhibitions, who resigned in fall 2006, said in a May 2007 CBS News report that executives including Director Cristian Samper and Undersecretary for Science David Evans had the script rewritten to add uncertainty about human-caused warming. Samper, by then Acting Secretary, denied political pressure (CBS News, 21 May 2007). Sullivan said he knew of no pressure from the Bush administration.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One art-press piece found, on Koch leaving the American Museum's board; the Smithsonian board campaign appears mainly in news and advocacy coverage.
1. Hyperallergic: Benjamin Sutton, on David Koch leaving the American Museum of Natural History board (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/269664/introducing-the-new-koch-zero-billionaire-leaves-american-natural-history-museum-board/
- Points to "the apparent incongruity" of a Koch Industries executive vice president sitting on science museum boards, and repeats activists' claim of over 67 million dollars for climate-denial groups.
- Names the Smithsonian museum: Koch still sat on its advisory board and was the target of the 430,000-signature petition.
2. Salon: Lindsay Abrams, "Scientists to museums: Stop taking money from climate deniers" (2015)
URL: https://www.salon.com/2015/03/24/scientists_to_museums_stop_taking_money_from_climate_deniers/
- Reports the scientists' letter and a 2010 New Yorker report that the Hall of Human Origins never names fossil fuels as a cause of rising carbon dioxide.
- Names Koch and the Smithsonian; a general news site, not an art outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The 2015 scientists' letter went to more than 330 museums, and the board campaign also targeted the American Museum of Natural History, where Koch's term ended on 9 December 2015 (Salon, Hyperallergic). Koch's seat at this museum was the other target.

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the museum's money is public, through the Smithsonian. The weak point is David Koch: his 15 million and 35 million dollar gifts funded two halls, he was still on the advisory board in January 2016, and a 2015 campaign targeted him there. He died in 2019. The 2025 White House review is the other item to watch.
