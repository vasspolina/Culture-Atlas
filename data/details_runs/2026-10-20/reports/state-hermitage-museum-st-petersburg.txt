STATE HERMITAGE MUSEUM: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: state-hermitage-museum-st-petersburg
Website: https://support.hermitagemuseum.org/ru/sponsors (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but its certificate failed verification in one of two fetch tools; the sponsor list was read from the page itself)

OVERVIEW
- Russia's state art and culture museum, in six historic buildings on Palace Embankment including the Winter Palace, former residence of Russian emperors (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1764, when Catherine the Great bought a collection of paintings from the Berlin merchant Johann Ernst Gotzkowsky (Wikipedia).
- Collection of over three million items; only a small part is on permanent display (Wikipedia).
- Mikhail Piotrovsky has been director since July 1992 (Wikipedia).
- A federal state property (Wikipedia). Its branch in Amsterdam, opened in 2009, cut ties in March 2022 and is now the H'ART Museum.
- Visiting: closed on Mondays; foreign visitors pay more than citizens of Russia and Belarus (Wikipedia). Other sources disagree on the free-entry day, so no free day is stated here.

FUNDING
State Hermitage Museum: state-owned, with Russian state-controlled banks, oil companies and oligarch trustees as sponsors
- The museum is federal state property; no reporting found of its budget split between the state and its own income.
- Its 2026 sponsor list names, by circle: Vladimir Potanin and Vladimir Putin among the Jupiter circle; Rosneft, VTB, SBER, Magnit, Rosatom Mashinostroenie and Samsung Electronics Rus in the Athena circle; T-Bank, Gazprom Neft, Rosseti, Rostelecom and the Vladimir Potanin Charitable Foundation in the Nike circle; the Gazprombank "Social Responsibility" charitable foundation, Alfa-Bank, AlfaStrakhovanie and Ingosstrakh among others in the Atlas circle (Hermitage Friends Club). No amounts or years of payment are given.
- Board of trustees: Potanin chairs it; members listed include Anton Siluanov, German Gref, Alexei Kudrin, Leonid Fridlyand, Mikhail Shvydkoi and Piotrovsky, with JTI Russia (LLC) as corporate member (Hermitage Friends Club). Potanin has chaired it since April 2003 and his donations to the Hermitage endowment fund total $5 million (Wikipedia).

Red flags: sanctions on the director and trustees
- Canada sanctioned Piotrovsky in August 2023; the EU listed him on 23 April 2026 as a "close associate" of Putin who "actively supported and justified" the war, citing his backing for Hermitage-linked digs in occupied Crimea (The Art Newspaper). The museum gave no comment.
- Potanin, the board chairman, was sanctioned by Canada on 6 April 2022, by the UK in 2022 and by the US (OFAC) on 15 December 2022 (Wikipedia, Kelley Drye).
- 4 December 2025: Poland detained Hermitage ancient archaeology head Alexander Butyagin on a Ukrainian request over excavations at Myrmekion, Kerch, in Crimea; Ukraine puts the damage at over 200 million hryvnia (Novaya Gazeta Europe).

Red flags: fossil fuel and state bank sponsors
- Rosneft and Gazprom Neft appear on the 2026 sponsor list; the Gazprombank charitable foundation, VTB and Sber also appear (Hermitage Friends Club).
- The same list names President Putin in the Jupiter circle.

Red flags: boycott and resignations over the war
- Hermitage Amsterdam cut ties with St Petersburg on 3 March 2022 (AP, via PBS).
- Dmitry Ozerkov, head of contemporary art for 22 years, left the museum and Russia in October 2022, tying the decision to Piotrovsky's June interview praising the war (RFE/RL).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism of the director's politics and the museum's foreign partnerships from 2022; little on the building or collection.
1. Hyperallergic: Rachel Spence, "How the Hermitage Museum Artwashes Russian Aggression" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/715271/how-the-hermitage-museum-artwashes-russian-aggression/
- Foreign partnerships, especially with the Louis Vuitton Foundation, lend legitimacy to a government that represses artists.
- Piotrovsky describes his loyalty to Putin and led United Russia's St Petersburg ticket in 2021.
- Piotrovsky asked whether the museum's expansion could be called "cultural aggression".
2. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Isolated by Russia boycotts, Hermitage director calls for 'cultural bridges between the nations'" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/04/04/russia-boycott-hermitage-museum
- Piotrovsky had not condemned the war; he answered the Bizot group's suspension with an open letter ending "The circle is closed."
- The museum planned to shift focus to Russian regions and outside Europe.
3. RFE/RL: North.Realities, "Top Official At Hermitage Museum In St. Petersburg Leaves Russia In Protest At The War In Ukraine" (2022)
URL: https://www.rferl.org/a/hermitage-director-ozerkov-leaves-russia-ukraine-invasion/32063557.html
- Ozerkov: "I left because I don't intend to have anything in common with today's Russia."
- Reports Piotrovsky called Hermitage exhibitions abroad another "special operation". News outlet, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Hermitage is Russian state money and state-controlled sponsors, including Rosneft, Gazprom Neft and VTB, under a board chaired by the sanctioned Potanin. The director is sanctioned by Canada and the EU. Worth watching: the Butyagin extradition case and the sponsor list.
