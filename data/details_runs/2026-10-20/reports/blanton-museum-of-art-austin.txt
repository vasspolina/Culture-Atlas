BLANTON MUSEUM OF ART: Austin, USA
ID: blanton-museum-of-art-austin
Website: https://blantonmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of the University of Texas at Austin, at 200 E Martin Luther King Jr Blvd on the campus (UT Austin blog, 2026). The collection has more than 22,000 works, with strengths in Old Master paintings, prints and drawings, modern and contemporary art, Latin American art and Western American art (Wikipedia).
- Opened in late 1963 as the University Art Museum, later renamed the Archer M. Huntington Art Gallery; Donald Goodall was the first director. It became the Blanton in the late 1990s after a $12 million gift from Houston Endowment, Inc. in honour of its then-chairman Jack S. Blanton, which launched the campaign for a new building (Wikipedia).
- Building: Herzog & de Meuron resigned the commission in 1999 over creative differences with the University's Board of Regents; Kallmann McKinnell & Wood took it over and the Michener Gallery Building opened in 2006. Snøhetta redesigned the grounds, unveiled in 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Ellsworth Kelly's "Austin", a 2,715-square-foot stone building with stained glass, opened in February 2018 after a campaign that grew from $15 million to $23 million (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Monday; open 10am to 5pm Tuesday to Friday and Sunday, 10am to 8pm Saturday; free with a UT ID (UT Austin blog, 2026), and free to all every Tuesday (2025 impact report).

FUNDING
Blanton Museum of Art: university museum funded by admissions, endowment income, donor gifts and University funding
- The 2025 to 2026 operating budget is $11.8 million. Income sources: admissions, membership and museum store 27%; community-funded museum endowments (annual income) 27%; community-funded annual operating gifts 23%; University funding 23% (2025 impact report).
- Gifts of $100,000 or more in September 2024 to August 2025, the Visionary Circle: Kelli and Eddy S. Blanton; Leslie and Jack Blanton, Jr.; Suzanne Deal Booth; Aubrey and Bobby Epstein; The Moody Foundation; The Powell Foundation; Scurlock Foundation; Still Water Foundation; Judy and Charles Tate (2025 impact report).
- Chairman's Circle, $25,000 to $99,999, includes Charles Butt, the Ford Foundation with the Austin City Art Fund, and the Carl and Marilynn Thoma Foundation. Benefactor's Circle, $10,000 to $24,999, includes J.P. Morgan Private Bank, the Joan Mitchell Foundation, the Applied Materials Foundation and the Texas Commission on the Arts.
- The Moody Family Free Day Endowment pays for free admission days; the Moody Foundation gave $20 million in 2019 toward a $35 million campus redesign (impact report, Wikipedia).
- Earlier gifts: Archer Huntington's 1927 land gift to the university funded early acquisitions, and Mari Yoriko Sabusawa gave $5 million in 1994 (Wikipedia).
- The museum sold works the Micheners donated through Christie's to create an acquisitions endowment named for the Micheners (2025 impact report).
- Leadership Board, as of 31 December 2025: Aubrey Epstein (chair), Elle Moody (vice-chair), Jack Blanton, Jr., Kelli Blanton, Stacy Hock, Katya Jestin, Donald Moffett, Lora Reynolds and Judy Tate. The Museum Council includes Leslie Blanton and Eddy Blanton.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Scurlock oil money)
- The Scurlock Foundation gave $100,000 or more in 2024 to 2025 (2025 impact report).
- Eddy Scurlock formed Scurlock Oil Company in 1936 and later bought a Houston refinery, renamed Eddy Refining Company. Scurlock Oil was sold to Ashland Oil in 1982; Eddy Refining and the Scurlock Foundation continued operating. Scurlock established and chartered the Scurlock Foundation in April 1954 (Wikipedia).
- The museum's namesake Jack S. Blanton joined Scurlock Oil in 1950, became its president in 1958 and was its CEO and chairman from 1983; Wikipedia's Eddy Scurlock entry says Blanton married Scurlock's daughter Laura (Wikipedia). The Scurlock Foundation also supported a 2015 Blanton exhibition (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded by its parent university (23% of the 2025 to 2026 budget), by admissions and store income, by endowment income and by donor gifts, with Texas family foundations in the top giving tier. The weak point is the Scurlock Foundation, an oil-fortune foundation that still gives at the highest tier, together with the namesake's former role at Scurlock Oil.
