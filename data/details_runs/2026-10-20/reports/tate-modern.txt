TATE MODERN: London, UK
ID: tate-modern
Website: https://www.tate.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Gallery of modern and contemporary art at Bankside, London SE1 9TG, one of Tate's galleries (stated by the space).
- Housed in the former Bankside Power Station, designed by Sir Giles Gilbert Scott and built between 1947 and 1963; it closed in 1981 (Wikipedia).
- Herzog & de Meuron won the conversion competition in January 1995; the Queen opened Tate Modern on 11 May 2000 (Wikipedia).
- The Switch House extension opened on 17 June 2016 and was renamed the Blavatnik Building in May 2017 (Wikipedia). Tate Modern had 4,432,890 visitors in 2024-25 (Tate Annual Report).
- Visiting: open Sunday to Thursday 10am to 6pm and Friday to Saturday 10am to 9pm; entry to the collection is free, exhibitions are ticketed; wheelchairs are free to borrow and there are accessible toilets on every floor (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Tate Modern: mainly public and philanthropic: government grant-in-aid, corporate partners and private donors
- Tate group income in 2024-25 was 167.15 million pounds: grant-in-aid revenue 41.44 million, capital grant-in-aid 12.46 million, fundraising 41.68 million, trading 32.55 million, admissions 9.13 million, fees and other 15.36 million, donated works of art 14.52 million (Tate Annual Report 2024-25).
- Corporate partners in 2024-25 include Anthropic, Bank of America, Bloomberg, Burberry, Chanel, Gucci, Hyundai Motor, LVMH, Sotheby's, Swatch, UNIQLO and Van Cleef & Arpels. Hyundai Motor extended its support, including the Turbine Hall commission, to 2036 (Tate Annual Report).
- At Tate Modern the Huo Family Foundation supported "Expressionists" and Zanele Muholi, and John J. Studzinski supported Yoko Ono (Tate Annual Report).
- The Blavatnik Family Foundation is listed among 2024-25 donors and gave grants to "Sargent and Fashion" at Tate Britain (Tate Annual Report).
- The extension was funded by 50 million pounds from the UK government, 7 million from the London Development Agency, 6 million from John Studzinski and 10 million from Eyal Ofer, plus a large share from Len Blavatnik (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- BP sponsored Tate for 26 years; in March 2016 it announced the deal would end in spring 2017, citing an "extremely challenging business environment". Tate disclosed it received 4 million pounds from BP between 1990 and 2006 (The Art Newspaper).
- Len Blavatnik, whose name is on the Blavatnik Building, built his fortune through Access Industries, whose group bought 40 percent of Tyumen Oil in 1997; BP bought it in 2003 and Rosneft paid 28 billion dollars for the group's 50 percent stake in TNK-BP in 2013. The Blavatnik Family Foundation still gives to Tate (Wikipedia, Tate Annual Report).

Red flags: Opioids
- Tate said in 2019 it would take no further Sackler money. In February 2022 it removed the Sackler name from three places at Tate Modern and two at Tate Britain (The Art Newspaper, Cristina Ruiz).

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- Liberate Tate protested BP from 2010; Culture Unstained, a campaign site, credits the Art Not Oil coalition and arts workers with ending it (Wikipedia, Culture Unstained).
- November 2024: more than 900 artists and art workers asked Tate to cut ties with Zabludowicz Art Trust, Zabludowicz Art Projects and Outset Contemporary Art Fund, citing Israel, ahead of the 3 December Turner Prize ceremony. The letter cites Tate's 2022 divestment from Viktor Vekselberg and Petr Aven as precedent. Tate's response was not reported (Euronews).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism of Tate's funders is documented over a decade, mostly BP, the Sacklers and the 2024 donor letter.
1. The Art Newspaper: Cristina Ruiz, "Tate's U-turn on Sacklers" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/02/08/tates-u-turn-on-sackler-naming
- Tate reversed its 2019 position that it would keep the Sackler name; it called the removal "mutually agreed" and declined to explain the change.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Tate chair reveals story behind Sackler name removal" (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/13/tate-chair-reveals-story-behind-sackler-name-removal
- Chair Roland Rudd said he told the Sacklers protests would be unpleasant; "You can't just wake up one day and say, 'Oh, we took money from these people but now we think they're awful'."
3. The Art Newspaper: Anny Shaw, "BP ends 26-year sponsorship deal with Tate" (2016)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/11/bp-ends-26-year-sponsorship-deal-with-tate
- Liberate Tate's sit-in and seed-bombing protests; Tate lost a three-year freedom of information fight over BP's payments.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Protesters outside Tate Modern in 2024 accused Blavatnik of interfering in Israeli news coverage; his spokesperson rejected it. Ukraine sanctioned him personally in December 2023 (Wikipedia). Critic Ann Marlowe called his donations "influence-buying and whitewashing".

BOTTOM LINE
Tate Modern's core money is public grant-in-aid and a wide set of corporate and private donors. The weak points are past BP and Sackler money, the Blavatnik name on the building with continuing Blavatnik Family Foundation grants, and the 2024 letter over Zabludowicz and Outset links. Worth watching: Tate's response to that letter and its ethics committee decisions.
