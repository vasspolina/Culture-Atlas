TATE MODERN – London, UK
Website: https://www.tate.org.uk (not opened in this run; the Tate annual report PDF on the domain loaded, 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of modern and contemporary art at Bankside, one of four Tate galleries. Run by Tate, a DCMS-sponsored body and exempt charity. 4,432,890 visitors to Tate Modern in 2024-25 (Tate Annual Report 2024-25).
- Opening days, discounts and access were not checked in this run.

FUNDING
Tate Modern: mixed; DCMS grant-in-aid around a third of group income, the rest self-generated (admissions, retail, sponsorship, philanthropy).
- Tate group income 2024-25: £167,150,000; grant-in-aid £41,442,000 revenue plus £12,461,000 capital (Tate Annual Report 2024-25). The record's "£120M (group)" does not match.
- 2022-23: income £146.2m, grant-in-aid £54.2m, deficit of nearly £9m (Civil Society). In March 2025 DCMS and the Tate Foundation assured Tate it would remain a going concern for 12 months while it cut about 7% of staff (Arts Professional).
- Corporate partners named in the 2024-25 report include Bank of America, Hyundai Motor (support extended to 2036), UNIQLO, Bloomberg, LVMH, Chanel, Gucci, Burberry, Bottega Veneta, Van Cleef & Arpels, Sotheby's, La Caixa Foundation, Anthropic, PJT Partners and Arcis Capital; corporate members include Morgan Stanley, State Street, T. Rowe Price, Clifford Chance and Slaughter and May (stated by the space).
- Named donors include the Blavatnik Family Foundation, Julia and Hans Rausing Trust, Huo Family Foundation, Rothschild Foundation, Terra Foundation, John J. Studzinski, AKO Foundation and Dimitris Daskalopoulos (stated by the space).
- The corporate support page was not retrievable in this run.

Red flags: BP sponsorship ended
- BP sponsored Tate for 26 years; it announced on 11 March 2016 that the deal would end in 2017, citing an "extremely challenging business environment", not protests. Liberate Tate had staged repeated protests, including at Tate Modern in December 2015. BP gave £4m between 1990 and 2006, disclosed in January 2015 after a freedom of information case (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Sackler name removed
- In early 2022 Tate removed the Sackler plaque on a Tate Modern escalator and planned to rename the Sackler Octagon at Tate Britain, saying it had "mutually agreed" this with the family, after a campaign led by Nan Goldin's P.A.I.N. since 2017 (Artnet, 7 Feb 2022). The Sacklers own Purdue Pharma, maker of OxyContin.

Red flags: Bloomberg among partners
- Bloomberg is a Tate corporate partner (stated by the space). In May 2026, artists asked five London theatres to drop Bloomberg Philanthropies (Morning Star, left-wing; The New Arab). Tate was not named.

BOTTOM LINE
Tate Modern is a public museum funded partly by government grant and largely by self-generated income, with a long roster of luxury, finance and tech sponsors. It ended its BP and Sackler ties after campaigns. Worth watching: its financial strain, and which corporate partners it keeps.
