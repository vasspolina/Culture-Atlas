KUNSTHALLE BREMEN: Bremen, Germany
ID: kunsthalle-bremen
Website: https://www.kunsthalle-bremen.de

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Am Wall 207 in Bremen (Wikipedia).
- Run by the Kunstverein in Bremen, founded in 1823 by a group of merchants around Senator Hieronymus Klugkist; the Kunstverein remains the sole operator and has about 9,000 members (Wikipedia). The taz report of 8 September 2021 puts the membership at just over 10,000 and the staff at about 70.
- Building: opened on 1 May 1849, designed by Lüder Rutenberg; extended in 1902, 1982 and in 2009 to 2011 (Wikipedia). It has been a listed monument since 1977.
- Directors: Gustav Pauli (1899 to 1914), Emil Waldmann (1914 to 1945), Günter Busch (1945 to 1984), Siegfried Salzmann (1985 to 1993), Wulf Herzogenrath (1994 to 2011) and Christoph Grunenberg (since November 2011). Nicole Lamotte has chaired the Kunstverein since October 2020 (Wikipedia).
- On site: the collection, temporary exhibitions and the Pixelwald Wisera.
- Visiting: until 30 October 2026 Tuesday 10:00 to 21:00, Wednesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; from 31 October Wednesday to Sunday until 18:00. Adults EUR 12, reduced EUR 6; free for under-18s and Kunstverein members. A tactile floor guidance system, elevators, tactile models, a German Sign Language video guide and audio guides in Easy Language are offered.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Bremen: mixed: members of the Kunstverein, public subsidy from the Bremen state and private donors
- Public: a state treaty of 1921 put five members of the Bremen parliament (Bürgerschaft) on the board (Wikipedia). Public funding covers about 40 percent of the annual budget, and the association bears the financial risk (taz, Bettina Maria Brosowsky, "Stolzer Träger eines Museums", 8 September 2021).
- Funders page: the Kunsthalle shows 22 foundations, firms and public bodies as logos with a media partner and a cultural partner.
- The 2011 extension was paid for by the Lürssen family, the Karin und Uwe Hollweg Foundation, the city and the federal government; the Kunstverein covered about EUR 3.5 million in overruns (Wikipedia).
- Hermann Reemtsma Stiftung: history, 2018. German Wikipedia states that the foundation was established in 1988 by Hermann-Hinrich Reemtsma in memory of his father Hermann Fürchtegott Reemtsma. A separate German Wikipedia page on the father (1892 to 1961) states that his firm B. Reemtsma & Söhne was Germany's cigarette market leader with a 40 percent share by the 1950s. The foundation paid for the restoration of over 900 Dutch and Flemish drawings of the Kunsthalle, shown in the 2018 exhibition "Kühles Licht und weite See" (7 March to 1 July 2018; artinwords.de, Alexandra Matzner, 4 February 2018). The foundation's own Wikipedia page states that it funds non-profit institutions in northern Germany in science, culture and social causes.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Mostly descriptive coverage; the one point of criticism concerns the building's extensions.
1. taz: Bettina Maria Brosowsky, "Stolzer Träger eines Museums" (2021)
URL: https://taz.de/!5799087
- Part of a series on Kunstvereine; the article of 8 September 2021 calls the postwar rebuilding and extensions "nicht immer geglückt" (not always successful; translated).
- Reports that the chair Nicole Lamotte says the Kunstverein carries the burden when something goes wrong, and that money for the large exhibitions of the 1990s and 2000s is no longer available.

BOTTOM LINE
Kunsthalle Bremen is funded by its Kunstverein members, about 40 percent public money (taz, 2021) and private foundations and firms. The Hermann Reemtsma Stiftung, founded in 1988 by the son of cigarette maker Hermann Fürchtegott Reemtsma, paid for the 2018 drawings restoration; that is history, not a current funding line.
