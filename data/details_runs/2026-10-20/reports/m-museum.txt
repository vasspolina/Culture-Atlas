M+ MUSEUM: Hong Kong, China
ID: m-museum
Website: https://www.mplus.org.hk

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary visual culture at 38 Museum Drive in the West Kowloon Cultural District, administered by the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority (WKCDA) (Wikipedia, M+).
- Opened on 12 November 2021; Lars Nittve was the inaugural director and Suhanya Raffel has been director since January 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Herzog & de Meuron and Farrells designed the winning entry, announced in June 2013; the building was completed in December 2020 (Wikipedia).
- The founding gift was 1,463 works by 325 artists from the Swiss collector Uli Sigg, "conservatively valued" at HK$1.3 billion (Wikipedia).
- The collection held more than 6,400 works in 2021; the museum has 33 galleries and 183,000 square feet of exhibition space (Associated Press).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, Friday until 22:00; ticket HKD 190, concession HKD 100, children aged 6 and under free; wheelchairs are free to borrow and audio guides come in Cantonese, English and Mandarin (M+).

FUNDING
M+ Museum: public: administered by the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority, a statutory authority, with a corporate lead partner and foundation supporters
- The WKCDA is a statutory authority set up under the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority Ordinance and in action from 11 July 2008; Wikipedia calls the district a government-planned cultural district.
- Wikipedia calls M+ "the HK$5.9 billion institution".
- HSBC became M+'s first Lead Partner on 1 November 2022, sponsoring select special exhibitions, tours for non-governmental organisations and M+ Family Day activities; the value was not disclosed (M+ press release, 5 August 2022).
- Institutional supporters since 2021: the Robert H. N. Ho Family Foundation Hong Kong (youth learning programme, Lars Nittve keynote lectures) and the De Ying Foundation (a curator of Chinese contemporary art) (M+).

Red flags: M+ withheld an Ai Weiwei work under the national security law
- The museum itself acted: in November 2021 M+ chose not to display Ai Weiwei's "Study of Perspective: Tian'anmen (1997)" after pro-Beijing politicians said it was "spreading hatred against China" and could violate the national security law. M+ replaced the image on its website with its logo and said it was only complying with the law (Associated Press, 11 November 2021).
- Henry Tang, chairman of the WKCDA board, said at the opening ceremony that the museum wanted to make clear that "people don't think that we are above the law" (Associated Press). Al Jazeera, calling him the district chief of West Kowloon, reports he said all exhibits would be subject to the National Security Law (Violet Law, 12 November 2021).
- Ai Weiwei said freedom of speech could no longer be exercised in a normal way (Associated Press).

Red flags: HSBC, lead partner, 2012 money laundering settlement
- HSBC has been M+'s first Lead Partner since 1 November 2022 (M+ press release, 5 August 2022; see FUNDING).
- On 11 December 2012 the US Department of Justice announced that HSBC admitted anti-money laundering and sanctions violations and entered a five-year deferred prosecution agreement, forfeiting $1.256 billion. The release says HSBC processed transactions for customers in Cuba, Iran, Libya, Sudan and Burma in breach of US sanctions law, and that at least $881 million in drug proceeds, including from the Sinaloa and Norte del Valle cartels, was laundered through HSBC Bank USA (US Department of Justice, archived release). The Inquirer reported the settlement the same day as $1.9 billion in penalties.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critical coverage concerns the opening period and the national security law, and comes from news outlets rather than art magazines.
1. Al Jazeera: Violet Law, "Hong Kong opens top art museum amid fear of cultural 'dark ages'" (2021)
URL: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/11/12/m-art-hong-kong
- Reports that the museum's statement on the Ai Weiwei work "was widely seen as a climb down by the museum's executive director" in the face of attacks by pro-Beijing politicians.
- Quotes Paul Gladston, University of New South Wales, saying the law "limits what M+ can do", and says that for artists whose works must circulate locally, self-censorship is becoming a strategy for self-preservation.
2. Associated Press: "Hong Kong's M+ museum opens amid censorship controversy" (2021)
URL: https://www.news4jax.com/entertainment/2021/11/11/hong-kongs-m-museum-opens-amid-censorship-controversy/
- Reports the withheld Ai Weiwei work and the museum's statement that it follows the law.
- Quotes Ai Weiwei's criticism of the decision.

BOTTOM LINE
M+ is administered by the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority, a statutory authority, with HSBC as lead partner since 2022. The documented issues are the museum's own 2021 decision not to display an Ai Weiwei work under the national security law and the 2012 US money laundering and sanctions settlement of its lead partner HSBC.
