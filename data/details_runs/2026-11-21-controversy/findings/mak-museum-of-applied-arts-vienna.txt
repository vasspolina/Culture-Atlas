Red flags: 2022 recommendation to return a dragon vase to the heirs of Oscar Bondy
- On 30 March 2022, in its 99th session, the Art Restitution Advisory Board (Kunstrückgabebeirat) recommended returning a dragon vase in the MAK to the legal successors of the persecuted industrialist and art collector Oscar Bondy (ORF.at, 30 March 2022).
- Eight objects from the MAK were restituted to Bondy's widow in 1948 and 1949, while the dragon vase, inventoried in 1952 as a "Geschenk eines Ungenannten" (translated: gift from an unnamed donor), stayed in the MAK (ORF.at, 30 March 2022).
- The board did not recommend the return of three other objects from Bondy's former collection, which the MAK bought after 1945 from the dealer Blasius Fornach, who had bought them from Bondy's widow after they were restituted to her (ORF.at, 30 March 2022).

Red flags: 2024 ruling against returning Wiener Werkstätte cutlery to the heirs of Ernst Bunzl
- On 27 September 2024 the Art Restitution Advisory Board recommended to the federal minister for arts and culture that the "Besteck der Wiener Werkstätte aus dem früheren Eigentum von Lili und Fritz Waerndorfer", inventory number GO2009, in the MAK not be transferred to the heirs of Dr. Ernst Bunzl (Kunstrückgabebeirat decision, 27 September 2024).
- The Commission for Provenance Research studied the cutlery after a suggestion from the heirs' side; the board had recommended the transfer of another MAK object to Bunzl's heirs on 11 January 2019 (Kunstrückgabebeirat decision, 27 September 2024).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In its final report on the MAK, published in March 2013, the Austrian Court of Audit (Rechnungshof) found that the museum had wrongly counted 47.4 percent of the 185,602 visitors it listed for 2010, and stated that the former managing director Peter Noever's average of 79 travel days a year from 2001 to 2010 was "sehr hoch" (translated: very high) against 221 working days a year, as were travel costs of about 81,000 euros a year (wien.ORF.at, 11 March 2013).
- The Court also examined ten birthday parties for Noever's mother held in the MAK between 2000 and 2009, which cost the MAK about 172,000 euros that it did not invoice to Noever (wien.ORF.at, 11 March 2013).
- Noever rejected the findings: "Das war alles immer transparent" (translated: it was all always transparent), also of his business trips. He had resigned as managing director in February 2011, was dismissed by the MAK board of trustees in March 2011 and challenged the dismissal in court (wien.ORF.at, 11 March 2013).
- In November 2012, after the draft audit report, director Christoph Thun-Hohenstein said it was "schlichtweg falsch" (translated: simply wrong) that 6,600 objects had disappeared under Noever, and that objects were missing for reasons far in the past, such as wartime loss, exchange with other museums and restitution (wien.ORF.at, 5 November 2012).
