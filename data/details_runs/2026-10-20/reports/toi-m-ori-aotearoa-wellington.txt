TOI MĀORI AOTEAROA: Wellington, New Zealand
ID: toi-m-ori-aotearoa-wellington
Website: https://www.maoriart.org.nz

OVERVIEW
- Charitable trust and national network of Māori artists, also called Māori Arts New Zealand, with a small Wellington team and office at Level 4, Change House, 150 Featherston Street (Toi Māori website, Annual Report 2024).
- Founded in 1996: the trust deed is dated 14 October 1996 and the trust was set up by the chairs of eight national Māori art form committees, who elect the trustees (Annual Report 2024). It was publicly launched at "Patua: Māori Arts in Action", curated by Sandy Adsett at City Gallery Wellington (Toi Māori website).
- Founding trustees named on its website include Hirini Moko Mead, Emily Rangitiaria Schuster, Tīmoti Kāretu, Dame Georgina Kirby and Trevor Horowaewae Maxwell. Current trustees listed there: Tracey Morgan (Chair), Robert Gabel (Deputy Chair), Ranui Ngarimu, Sir Derek Lardelli, Lady Rose Gould-Lardelli, Rongomaiaia Te Whaiti and Reweti Arapere.
- The group has three entities: the charitable trust, Toi Māori Limited (established 2005) for very large or high risk projects, and an event company (established 2007) that produces the Māori Art Market, a biennial event (Annual Report 2024). Wikipedia lists Art Markets in 2007, 2009, 2011, 2014 and 2016/17.
- The Toi Māori Gallery was established in 2008 and sits in the Wellington office, with exhibitions also staged with partner organisations around New Zealand (Annual Report 2024). The website also runs a Toi Māori Store selling work by featured artists.
- Programmes in the 2024 report include national committees for art forms such as weaving and carving, the Taihonoa internships, and the Māori Arts Internship (MAI) programme, which placed 45 Māori interns with 40 arts organisations over ten years.
- The office is open 9am to 5pm on weekdays (Toi Māori website).

FUNDING
Toi Māori Aotearoa: mainly public: Creative New Zealand, with shop and service income
- Creative New Zealand: investment programme listing for Toi Māori Aotearoa - Māori Arts New Zealand Charitable Trust of $821,100 in 2023, $837,522 in 2024 and $854,272 in 2025 (Creative New Zealand, Toi Tōtara Haemata 2023-25 results). Creative New Zealand is the government arts funding body.
- Creative New Zealand also funded the MAI programme from 2014 to 2024 (Annual Report 2024).
- Group revenue was $1,407,389 in 2024 and $1,311,586 in 2023; expenses were $1,286,127 in 2024. Revenue from providing goods or services made up $1,276,134 of the 2024 revenue, and donations were $470 (Annual Report 2024). The Charities Register shows consolidated income of $1,162,678 and expenditure of $1,107,218 for the year ended 31 December 2025.
- At the end of 2024 the group held $237,900 of unused Creative New Zealand grants and $18,356 of other unused grants (Annual Report 2024).
- Wikipedia says much of the trust's funding comes indirectly from the government, through Creative New Zealand, Te Puni Kōkiri and similar bodies.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Toi Māori Aotearoa is public arts funding from Creative New Zealand, about $0.82 million to $0.85 million a year in 2023 to 2025, plus income from its store, events and services.
