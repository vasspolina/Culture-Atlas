BRITTO ARTS TRUST: Dhaka, Bangladesh
ID: britto-arts-trust-dhaka
Website: https://brittoartstrust.com/

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run non-profit collective at Green Road, Dhaka (33, 33/1 BDDL Green Mart, Space 208 to 210, first floor). It calls itself "the first artists' run non-profit collective in Bangladesh with a global reach" and a "non-commercial contemporary art environment" (Britto Arts Trust website).
- Founded in 2002 by six founding trustees, all artists; the founders say the idea goes back to 2000 and that they started officially in 2002 (On Curating interview with Tayeba Begum Lipi and Mahbubur Rahman, in conversation with Anastasia Baka, Leilani Lynch and Anna Wälli).
- "Britto" means circle in Bangla (Bad at Sports).
- Space: the founders began in a living room and had around seven spots, all in residential areas, over about seven years, with moves forced by property owners. They bought their own space in 2011. They describe Britto Space as in the middle of the city and rather small (On Curating).
- Programme: five international workshops from 2003 to 2012, the public art project "1mile² Dhaka" (2009, 2014), "No Man's Land" at the India-Bangladesh border (2014) and "Counter Enclave" since 2017 (website). Its first national representation of Bangladesh at the Venice Biennale was in 2011, with Bengal Foundation and Gervasuti Foundation.
- In 2022 nineteen Britto artists took part in documenta fifteen in Kassel with a seven-part project on the politics of food, shown at documenta Halle (Showcase, 1 June 2022). Its work "Rasad" (2024) is in the Tate Modern display "Performer and Participant" (Tate).
- The website lists 33 members and a board.

FUNDING
Britto Arts Trust: artist-run non-profit: the trustees' own savings, a six-year grant, donations and project funding from institutions
- The trustees donated their salaries and all remuneration to the house, saved some money from their own artworks and put the savings in a fixed deposit, which grew and paid for the space bought in 2011 (On Curating).
- A grant around 2004 to 2005 ran for six years, and Triangle Network co-founder Robert Loder "also donated some funds". The founders say "Bangladesh does not have any local or public funding at all" (On Curating).
- documenta fifteen: the project was financially supported by documenta und Museum Fridericianum gGmbH, with major supporters Goethe Institut, Arts Collaboratory and Bagri Foundation (Showcase, 1 June 2022).
- Partners named on its website: Triangle Network, the South Asian Network for Art (Khoj, VASL, Theertha), Bengal Foundation, Gervasuti Foundation, British Council, Gasworks, Ford Foundation and Prince Claus Fund.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review, of the documenta fifteen installation, which praises the work with one reservation about the art.
1. Bad at Sports: Keeley Haftner, "Bad at Art Forum #8: Not Retweeting Documenta Fifteen" (28 June 2022)
URL: https://badatsports.com/?p=52016
- Calls Britto's installation at documenta Halle a highlight and praises the bamboo kitchen garden, the palan, as "deftly-constructed".
- Finds the atrium bazaar of ceramic and embroidered parodies of foodstuffs "delectable (albeit on the nose)".
- Criticises the art, not the institution.

WIDER CONTEXT
- documenta fifteen opened on 18 June 2022; days later the mural "People's Justice" (2002) by Taring Padi drew condemnation from the German and Israeli governments over what they called antisemitic imagery, and was covered up and taken down. General director Sabine Schormann resigned on 17 July 2022. Findings released in January 2023 concluded that the exhibition "served as an echo chamber for Israel-related antisemitism and sometimes pure antisemitism" (Wikipedia).
- The mural was by Taring Padi, another documenta fifteen participant. Britto Arts Trust is on Wikipedia's list of the exhibition's participants, and its project was shown at documenta Halle (Showcase).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Britto is the trustees' own savings, an early six-year grant, donations including from Triangle Network co-founder Robert Loder, and project funding from documenta und Museum Fridericianum gGmbH, with the Goethe Institut, Arts Collaboratory and the Bagri Foundation as major supporters. Its documenta fifteen participation places it inside the programme criticised over antisemitism in 2022.
