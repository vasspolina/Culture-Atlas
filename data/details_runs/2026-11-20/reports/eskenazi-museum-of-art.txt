ESKENAZI MUSEUM OF ART: Bloomington, United States
ID: eskenazi-museum-of-art
Website: https://artmuseum.indiana.edu (checked 2026-11-20; the page loads, but its certificate chain failed an automated check)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of Indiana University Bloomington, at 1133 East Seventh Street on the Bloomington campus (museum website).
- Began in 1941, when IU president Herman B Wells and the art department head Henry Radford Hope, its first director, set out to build a collection (museum website); it opened that year in a gallery in Mitchell Hall (Wikipedia).
- Renamed in May 2016 for the Indianapolis philanthropists and IU alumni Sidney and Lois Eskenazi (museum news release; IU Today).
- The I.M. Pei building opened in 1982 (museum website). A renovation designed by Susan T. Rodriguez of Ennead Architects with Browning Day Mullins Dierdorf (museum news release) closed the museum from May 2017 to November 2019 (Wikipedia).
- More than 45,000 works from ancient to contemporary (IU Today).
- Directors: David A. Brenneman from 2015 to 2024 (IU Today); Mindy N. Besaw since her appointment on 17 June 2025 (museum news release).
- Visiting: free; closed Monday, open to 7pm on Thursday. Lifts serve all galleries and a sensory-friendly hour runs on Saturdays (museum website).

FUNDING
Eskenazi Museum of Art: university-run: Indiana University, with private naming and renovation gifts
- Owner: Indiana University, a public university. The museum states that most of its acquisitions came as gifts of art or purchase funds from individual donors (museum website).
- Renovation: Sidney and Lois Eskenazi gave $15 million in 2016, the largest cash gift in the museum's history, as the lead gift, plus nearly 100 works, mostly prints, including 34 by Joan Miró; IU added $20 million through its For All Bicentennial Campaign match (museum news release). The museum puts the renovation at $30 million (museum website; IU Today).
- Source of the naming money: Sidney Eskenazi founded the real estate firm Sandor Development Co. in 1963 and built it to more than 70 properties in 23 states (Eskenazi Health). He died in early 2026, weeks after Lois Eskenazi (WRTV, 2 Feb 2026).
- Mellon Foundation: $500,000 to Indiana University in 2012 "to strengthen student and faculty engagement with the collections" at the museum (Mellon Foundation).

Red flags: 2023 cancellation of the Samia Halaby retrospective
- On 20 December 2023 Indiana University cancelled "Samia Halaby: Centers of Energy", the first American retrospective of the Palestinian artist and IU alumna, due to open in February 2024 after three years of preparation (Indiana Public Media). An IU spokesperson said "academic leaders and campus officials canceled the exhibit due to concerns about guaranteeing the integrity of the exhibit for its duration" (Hyperallergic).
- Unverified: Madison Gordon, who works at Halaby's studio, said the university told Halaby the show was cancelled because of her pro-Palestinian social media posts and security risks (Indiana Daily Student).
- Halaby said: "Clearly the intent is to suppress Palestinian voices at this very time" (The Art Newspaper).
- On 15 November 2023 US Representative Jim Banks wrote to IU asking about antisemitism on campus and warned it could lose federal funding if it condoned antisemitism (Indiana Daily Student).
- In January 2024 Provost Rahul Shrivastav called the show a "potential lightning rod" and said he would "err on the side of caution" when there was a risk of violence (Indiana Daily Student).

Red flags: 2024 protest over the Halaby cancellation
- A petition by Gordon, Halaby's grandniece, passed 13,500 signatures by 18 January 2024. Julie Trébault of PEN America's Artists at Risk Connection called the cancellation "an alarming affront to free expression", and the National Coalition Against Censorship wrote to IU president Pamela Whitten on 11 January 2024 (Indiana Public Media).
- On 7 February 2024, 75 faculty and staff signed a letter criticising IU's lack of transparency; it said three artists had withdrawn from the Eskenazi School's McKinney Visiting Artist Series (The Herald-Times).
- Michigan State University's Broad Art Museum showed its own Halaby retrospective until 15 December 2024 (Michigan State University).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Strong criticism in the art press, all about the 2023 Halaby cancellation; it targets the university's decision more than the museum's staff.
1. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Indiana University Cancels Palestinian Artist Samia Halaby's Exhibition" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/865825/indiana-university-cancels-palestinian-artist-samia-halaby-exhibition/
- Unverified: that the decision was tied to Halaby's pro-Palestine advocacy, which the petition and emails Hyperallergic reviewed suggest.
- Quotes the petition: "the University is canceling the show to distance itself from the cause of Palestinian freedom."
- Paywalled on the live site; read in full from the Internet Archive copy of 15 January 2024.
2. The Art Newspaper: Theo Belci, "US museum under fire for cancelling Palestinian artist's retrospective" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/01/12/indiana-university-cancels-palestinian-artist-samia-halaby-retrospective
- Reports the petition's claim that IU gave "no evidence of threats to the artwork or the campus".
- Notes Halaby asked why the university did not object "during the three long years of preparation".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean: a public university and a real estate couple's naming gift. The weak point is the university's control over what the museum shows, as the Halaby cancellation proved. Worth watching: whether the director appointed in 2025 changes how such decisions are made.
