DJERASSI RESIDENT ARTISTS PROGRAM: Woodside, USA
ID: djerassi-resident-artists-program-woodside
Website: https://djerassi.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Free artist residency on a 583-acre former cattle ranch at 2325 Bear Gulch Road in the Santa Cruz Mountains above Woodside, California.
- Founded in 1979 by the Stanford chemist Carl Djerassi and the author Diane Middlebrook; run by the Djerassi Foundation until it became an independent non-profit in 1999. The first resident, Tamara Rikman of Jerusalem, came in 1979; co-ed groups began in 1983 (history page).
- Created in memory of Carl Djerassi's daughter Pamela, a poet and painter who died in 1978 aged 28 (history page).
- The land was home to the Salson people of the Ohlone and later used for logging and cattle; Carl Djerassi ran it as SMIP Ranch. The ranch manager's house and barn became living and studio space in 1983, and four Middlebrook Studios were added in 2012. A 1999 conservation easement with Peninsula Open Space Trust limits building (history page).
- One-month residencies at no cost for artists in choreography, literature, film, music, visual and media arts, and science and technology; about 40 to 45 new artists are expected for 2027. Sculptures by David Nash and Mauro Staccioli stand on the land.
- The public can join guided Fall Art Hikes (23 September to 16 November) and sound events. No reporting found of access details for disabled visitors.

FUNDING
Djerassi Resident Artists Program: private donations, with small state grants and a shrinking reserve
- In 2023 revenue was $717,420, of which contributions were $521,718 and investment income $22,474; expenses were $1,644,331. Net assets fell from $6,167,660 at the end of 2020 to $3,898,749 at the end of 2023 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- The California Arts Council gave $12,600 in general operating support for 2025-26 (CAC grantee database).
- The program says it relies on donations from individuals, businesses and foundations, but names none; RBC Wealth Management receives its stock gifts (donate page).
- Carl Djerassi gave the land and buildings to the program in 1999 (history page).
- No reporting found of named major donors or their amounts.

Red flags: trustees visited Epstein's island
- Trustees Alexander Maxwell Djerassi, board secretary, and Michael Molesky, on the executive committee, are listed in the Department of Justice Epstein files as guests on an itinerary for the "Mindshift Conference" on the weekend of 7 January 2011, with events at the Ritz Carlton on St. Thomas and on Epstein's island, Little Saint James (Hyperallergic, 2026).
- The visit came two years after Epstein's first conviction; both joined the board in 2017 (Hyperallergic).
- Alexander Maxwell Djerassi is the son of Isabel Maxwell and the producer Dale Djerassi, a founding trustee, and the nephew of Ghislaine Maxwell (Hyperallergic).
- The program said the trustees attended a social lunch before joining the board, that it "had no knowledge of and no involvement in this event", and that neither Epstein nor Ghislaine Maxwell has donated to, visited or contacted it (stated by the space, to Hyperallergic).
- Unverified: staff were discouraged from raising the connection in Maxwell Djerassi's presence, from an anonymous former employee quoted by Hyperallergic.
- The board page, checked 2026-10-20, still lists Alexander Djerassi as secretary and Michael Molesky on the executive committee.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is small and private: individual and foundation gifts, a modest state grant and net assets that fell by more than a third from 2020 to 2023. The weak point is that two sitting trustees, one of them Ghislaine Maxwell's nephew, appear in the Epstein files as guests on his island in 2011; no Epstein money has been reported. Worth watching: whether the board responds beyond its statement.
