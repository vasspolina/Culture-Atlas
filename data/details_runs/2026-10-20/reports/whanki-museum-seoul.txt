WHANKI MUSEUM: Seoul, South Korea
ID: whanki-museum-seoul
Website: http://whankimuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; fails its security certificate check, "self signed certificate", and could not be opened)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum at 210-8 Buam-dong, Jongno-gu, in the hills of north-central Seoul (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Kim Hyang-an (1916 to 2004), the widow of the painter Kim Whanki, who created the Whanki Foundation after his death and established the museum (Munhwa Ilbo, 2016). Construction began in 1990 and the museum opened in November 1992 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Kim Whanki (1913 to 1974), a first-generation Korean abstract painter.
- Designed by the architect Kyu Sung Woo (Wikipedia).
- On site: a main building designed to show Kim's art, Annex 1 for special exhibitions with a café and art shop, and Annex 2 for lectures (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Time Out lists Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm, with admission varying by exhibition. No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Whanki Museum: private foundation: the Whanki Foundation; no funder list or accounts published
- The museum is run by the Whanki Foundation, a foundation corporation (Korea Herald, July 2026). Foundation president Jang Jae-ryong and museum director Park Mee-jung are named in the 2026 complaint (Korea JoongAng Daily).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, donor, state grant or accounts for the museum or the foundation. Neither the Korea Herald nor the Korea JoongAng Daily coverage of the foundation addresses its funding.
- No reporting found of how the museum came to hold Kim Whanki's works. Munhwa Ilbo does not explain this.

Red flags: protest over the poisoned ginkgo tree
- Event: residents of Buam-dong found holes drilled around the roots of a ginkgo tree beside the museum wall, estimated at 100 to 200 years old. Footage showed contractors hired by the museum injecting herbicide on 22 April 2026 (Hankyoreh, Korea Herald).
- Protest: residents held an event on 23 May and, with the Seoul Environmental Alliance, a press conference in front of the museum on 26 May, demanding an apology and restoration (Hankyoreh, Kyunghyang Shinmun).
- Museum's account: the roots were collapsing a retaining wall, and it could not get agreement from the tree's co-owners; it admitted hiring the contractors, and posted an apology around 31 May to 3 June (Korea Herald, Korea JoongAng Daily, Kyunghyang Shinmun). It has not disclosed the chemical or the amount (Korea JoongAng Daily).
- Complaint: on 29 June 2026, 60 residents, groups and individuals filed a criminal complaint against director Park Mee-jung and the Whanki Foundation for property damage and violating the Soil Environment Conservation Act (Korea Herald).
- Status in July 2026: the tree was in critical condition; Jongno-gu's "Beautiful Tree" designation stalled with 16 of 46 landowners consenting (Korea Herald).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Nothing is published about who pays for the museum, so its money cannot be judged either way. The documented weak point is the April 2026 herbicide injection and the residents' protest and criminal complaint that followed. Worth watching: the outcome of the complaint and the tree, and whether the foundation publishes accounts or a donor list.
