PALAZZO GRASSI AND PUNTA DELLA DOGANA: Venice, Italy
ID: palazzo-grassi-and-punta-della-dogana
Website: https://www.pinaultcollection.com/palazzograssi/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Two exhibition venues for François Pinault's collection in Venice: Palazzo Grassi, and Punta della Dogana, a former customs house (Wikipedia; museum website).
- Palazzo Grassi was rebuilt between 1748 and 1772 to a design by Giorgio Massari. The Fiat Group bought it in 1983 under Gianni Agnelli, and Gae Aulenti oversaw a complete restoration. Pinault bought it in May 2005 for 29 million euros (Wikipedia).
- Tadao Ando remodelled Palazzo Grassi, which reopened in April 2006, and restored Punta della Dogana from January 2008 to March 2009; Punta della Dogana opened in June 2009. Ando redesigned the Teatrino into a 225-seat auditorium in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- The City of Venice owns the Punta della Dogana building (Wikipedia). Both venues show temporary exhibitions from the Pinault Collection (Wikipedia).
- Current shows include "Lorna Simpson. Third Person" at Punta della Dogana (29 March to 22 November 2026) and "Michael Armitage. The Promise of Change" at Palazzo Grassi (museum website).
- Visiting: during exhibition periods open daily except Tuesdays, 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:00, closed 25 December. An accessible guide and an Italian Sign Language presentation are offered. The page lists no prices.

FUNDING
Palazzo Grassi and Punta della Dogana: self-funded: François Pinault's private money
- The Pinault Collection is "a private company held by Pinault's Groupe Artémis"; the Venetian sites are held and managed by Palazzo Grassi S.p.A., in which the City of Venice holds a non-controlling stake and sits on the board (Wikipedia).
- Purchase: 29 million euros for Palazzo Grassi in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- Punta della Dogana: Pinault won the city's competition against the Guggenheim Foundation. In 2007 The Art Newspaper reported a 30-year lease at no rent, with about 25 million euros of restoration paid by Pinault, and Jean-Jacques Aillagon estimating about 2 million euros a year to run after ticket and catalogue revenue and a total commitment of about 85 million euros over 30 years (Georgina Adam, 31 May 2007). Wikipedia gives a 33-year agreement and a restoration cost of about 20 million euros. These are projections and reports, not accounts.
- Exhibition sponsors: Bottega Veneta "exclusively supports" the Lorna Simpson show (museum website). Bottega Veneta is part of Kering, whose main shareholder is Groupe Artémis, holding 42.34% of shares and 59.34% of votes (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual accounts, ticket revenue, the board or other sponsors.

Red flags: Kering tax settlement (Bottega Veneta's parent)
- On 9 May 2019 Kering announced a settlement of 1.25 billion euros, including 897 million euros in additional taxes plus penalties and interest, after Italian tax authorities accused Gucci of evading taxes on more than 1 billion euros in revenues from 2011 to 2017 through a Swiss subsidiary, Luxury Goods International (swissinfo). Kering acknowledged the claim that Gucci had a permanent establishment in Italy in that period but has denied avoiding tax.
- Bottega Veneta is the sole supporter of the 2026 Lorna Simpson show.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Critical coverage is old and concerns the city's deal and how Pinault ran the two sites, not the money behind them.
1. The Art Newspaper: Enrico Tantucci and Gareth Harris, "François Pinault poaches leading Paris fair director" (2010)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2010/06/01/francois-pinault-poaches-leading-paris-fair-director
- Venetian criticism of Pinault's management grew after he dropped the temporary exhibition programme at both venues.
- Franco Miracco, a supporter of the rival Guggenheim bid, said the arrangement did not serve the city because Pinault's collection is shown in buildings tied to his auction house: "But these buildings are not auction houses and this is not in the city's interests." Vittorio Sgarbi criticised the city for giving Pinault spaces that could have housed another collection. Mayor Massimo Cacciari dismissed the criticism and praised Pinault's restorations.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Pinault trounces Guggenheim in battle to control customs house" (2007)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2007/05/01/pinault-trounces-guggenheim-in-battle-to-control-customs-house
- Reports that an independent committee could not choose between the bids, a second panel chose Pinault, and the city council proposed a joint occupation that Pinault's side refused.
- Not criticism by a critic: it records the dispute over the award.

BOTTOM LINE
The venues are paid for by Pinault's own company, with the City of Venice holding a minority stake in the operating company and owning the Punta della Dogana building. The weak points are the terms of the Punta della Dogana lease, which are published only through press reports, and Kering's 2019 tax settlement, as Bottega Veneta sponsors the current Lorna Simpson show. No annual accounts or board list were found.
