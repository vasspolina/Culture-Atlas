LEOPOLD MUSEUM: Vienna, Austria
ID: leopold-museum
Website: https://www.leopoldmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of early 20th-century Austrian art in Vienna's MuseumsQuartier, in a white shell limestone building by the architects Laurids and Manfred Ortner set in the courtyard of the former imperial stables (Vienna.at). It opened on 20 September 2001.
- Founded by the collectors Elisabeth and Rudolf Leopold, who gathered more than 5,000 exhibits over five decades; in 1994 the collection was placed in the Leopold Museum Private Foundation with help from the Republic of Austria and the National Bank of Austria (Wikipedia). Wikipedia's Portrait of Wally article says the Austrian government bought 5,400 works for $500 million in 1994 and named Leopold director for life.
- Named for Rudolf Leopold, the collector.
- What is there: key works by Egon Schiele and Gustav Klimt, and the world's largest Schiele collection according to Wikipedia; the museum also shows temporary exhibitions.
- Visiting: open daily except Tuesday, 10:00 to 18:00; closed 24 December 2026 and open 10:00 to 17:00 on 31 December. The museum pages read did not state admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
Leopold Museum: mixed: federal subsidy and corporate sponsors
- Federal ministry: the museum's sponsors page thanks the Bundesministerium für Kunst, Kultur, öffentlichen Dienst und Sport and gives no amount. A motion by Wolfgang Zinggl and colleagues, filed in the National Council on 21 November 2018, states that the Republic subsidises the museum with over €4.3 million a year, appoints half of the foundation board and provides the building. The Culture Committee did not approve the motion on 28 November 2018 (Austrian Parliament committee report).
- Partner: Wiener Städtische and the Wiener Städtische Versicherungsverein (insurer).
- Annual sponsors: Siwacht, Post, PORR and the Arbeiterkammer Wien. Project sponsors: Dorotheum and the auction house im Kinsky (all stated by the museum; no amounts).
- Falter (Matthias Dusini, 2013) wrote that the state provided hundreds of millions for the purchase and the building but only a minimal annual subsidy afterwards, and that the promised acquisition budget never came.
- No reporting found of the foundation board, annual accounts or individual patrons.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism, focused on Nazi-looted art and the museum's reluctance to return it, rather than on its funders.
1. Hyperallergic: Marisa Carroll, "The Painting That Launched a Thousand Lawsuits" (2012)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/andrew-shea-portrait-of-wally
- A review of Andrew Shea's film "Portrait of Wally". Says the film argues that the art world put its own future ahead of justice for victims of Nazi theft.
- Says Rudolf Leopold used dirty tricks to acquire works: in 1954 Lea Bondi asked him to help recover the painting and he bartered with the Belvedere and made it his own.
- Under the 2010 settlement about 50 Bondi heirs received $19 million and a placard on the painting's history stays beside it; the review does not say who paid (Wikipedia says the Leopold Museum agreed to pay).
2. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Jewish cabaret artist's heirs file suit for return of 12 works by Schiele" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/11/09/jewish-cabaret-artists-heirs-file-suit-for-return-of-12-works-by-schiele
- News report (9 November 2023): Fritz Grünbaum's heirs sued the Leopold and Albertina museums and the Republic of Austria in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York to recover 12 Schiele works.
- The Leopold said it was "at present unable to comment on the Causa Grünbaum"; the Austrian museums' provenance reports attribute 1950s sales to Grünbaum's sister-in-law through the Swiss dealer Eberhard W. Kornfeld, which the heirs dispute.
3. Falter (Vienna weekly, not art press): Matthias Dusini, "Zoff im Leopold Museum" (2013)
URL: https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20131106/zoff-im-leopold-museum-wo-liegt-der-hund-begraben
- Describes a "Kabale" at the museum after one director helped found a foundation and another resigned; calls the board a "großer Restitutionsverhinderer" (my translation: major obstructer of restitution).
- Says the ministries sent their own people to the board to limit the founding family's influence, and reports that Elisabeth Leopold wanted nothing to do with the looted-art topic.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wikipedia lists later restitution cases: 2011, a settlement with the heirs of Moriz Eisler over two Romako paintings; 2016, a settlement over five Schiele works with the heirs of Karl Maylaender; 2023, the Grünbaum suit.
- The motion filed by Wolfgang Zinggl and colleagues on 21 November 2018 says the Leopold is not a federal museum, so the Art Restitution Act does not apply to its collection, which has caused serious problems with Nazi-looted art.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a federal subsidy plus named Austrian corporate sponsors; none falls under a flagged industry. The weak points are the museum's private-foundation status, which keeps it outside the Art Restitution Act, and its long record of contested Nazi-era claims. Worth watching: the Grünbaum suit and whether sponsor and accounts information is published.
