MUSEO ARCHEOLOGICO NAZIONALE DI REGGIO CALABRIA (MARRC): Reggio Calabria, Italy
ID: museo-archeologico-nazionale-di-reggio-calabria-marrc
Website: https://museoarcheologicoreggiocalabria.cultura.gov.it (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State archaeological museum in Palazzo Piacentini, Piazza De Nava 26, in the centre of Reggio Calabria, next to Reggio Lido station and facing the Strait of Messina (museum website; Ministry of Culture).
- Marcello Piacentini designed the building in the 1930s as one of the first in Europe made for an archaeological collection; the museum opened in part in 1954 and officially in 1959 (museum website).
- An underwater archaeology section was created in 1981 for the Riace Bronzes, two 5th-century BC Greek warriors found off Riace Marina in August 1972, restored in Reggio and Florence (museum website).
- The museum closed in 2009 for restoration and reopened fully renovated in 2016 under a glass-roofed central court, Piazza Paolo Orsi, with a terrace over the Strait (museum website); Paolo Desideri's ABDR studio defined the restoration design (Panorama, 2012).
- What is there: five levels, A to E, from Magna Graecia cities and sanctuaries to "Dalla Reggio antica ai Bronzi di Riace", a sealed Bronzes room entered through filter spaces, a library and a restoration laboratory (museum website).
- Director: Fabrizio Sudano (museum website). The exhibition "Ispirazioni. Vite italiane dell'arte greca", first shown at the Acropolis Museum in Athens, runs from 22 October to 31 December 2026 (museum website).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 20:00, last entry 19:30; 10 euros, 2 euros for ages 18 to 25, free under 18; annual passes from 5 euros (museum website).

FUNDING
Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Reggio Calabria: public: the Italian Ministry of Culture, with EU recovery funds
- Owner: the Ministry of Culture, which hosts its website on the ministry domain and lists it among its sites (Ministry of Culture).
- 2009 to 2016 renovation: about 18 million euros from the Presidency of the Council of Ministers, 6 million from the CIPE planning committee and 5 million from the Calabria Region for fit-out and systems (Panorama, September 2012).
- EU money: under Italy's recovery plan (PNRR M1C3, Next Generation EU) the museum replaced 1,637 light fittings with LEDs between November 2024 and September 2025, work carried out by City Green Light, and it runs PNRR projects to remove physical and cognitive barriers and to build "Masterpiece Box: Bronzi di Riace a 360°" (museum website).
- Donations and sponsors: it accepts gifts under the Art Bonus scheme, which gives donors a 65% tax credit, and cash or in-kind sponsorships (museum website).
- The 2024 conservation check of the Riace and Porticello bronzes was a collaboration with the Istituto Centrale per il Restauro and the University of Genoa (Ministry of Culture).

Red flags: 2009 to 2016 renovation delays and cost rise
- The Bronzes were due back on display in March 2011 but returned only at the end of December 2013, and the renovation's cost rose from 11 million to 30 million euros (The Art Newspaper, February 2014).
- The museum reopened fully only in 2016, seven years after closing (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself; coverage centres on the delayed renovation and on disputes over moving the Bronzes.
1. The Art Newspaper: "The Riace bronze warriors back on their feet at last" (2014)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2014/02/01/the-riace-bronze-warriors-back-on-their-feet-at-last
- Reports that the project was "plagued by delays", that costs spiralled, and that completion had slipped again from April to June 2014.
2. Finestre sull'Arte: "Valorizzazione Bronzi di Riace, è polemica tra Sgarbi e il Comitato per i Bronzi" (2022)
URL: https://www.finestresullarte.info/attualita/valorizzazione-bronzi-di-riace-polemica-sgarbi-comitato
- Reports a dispute between the critic Vittorio Sgarbi and the Reggio-based committee for the Bronzes over showing them abroad and over a Pietrasanta event that sank replicas in the sea.
- Concerns the Bronzes, not the museum's money. Only the opening paragraphs were readable.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2012 the historian Franco Cardini proposed moving the Bronzes to Florence, backed by Mayor Matteo Renzi and Culture Undersecretary Roberto Cecchi; the Calabria Region called the idea unworkable (Panorama).
- The museum holds the Bronzes that these proposals concerned.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Italian state and EU recovery money. The weak point on record is the overrun renovation of 2009 to 2016.
