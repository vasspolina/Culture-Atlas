LONDON MUSEUM: London, United Kingdom
ID: london-museum
Website: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- City museum of London's history, holding about seven million objects covering 450,000 years. It is open at London Museum Docklands, No. 1 Warehouse, West India Quay, E14, and moves to a new home in the former Smithfield markets on 28 November 2026 (stated by the museum).
- Formed in 1976 by merging the Guildhall Museum, founded by the City of London Corporation in 1826, with the London Museum, founded in 1911; a 1965 Act of Parliament enabled the merger (Wikipedia).
- Called the Museum of London from 1976 until the rename to London Museum in July 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the new site converts Smithfield's General Market and Poultry Market. Stanton Williams and Asif Khan were chosen as architects from a competition of more than 70 firms (Wikipedia). The General Market galleries open in 2026 and the Poultry Market learning centre and exhibition spaces in 2028 (The Art Newspaper).
- The London Wall site closed on 4 December 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Docklands galleries are free and open daily 10:00 to 17:00. Admission prices for the Smithfield site are not posted.

FUNDING
London Museum: mainly public: City of London Corporation and the Mayor of London, with Bloomberg Philanthropies the largest private donor
- The museum is jointly controlled and funded by the City of London Corporation and the Greater London Authority (Wikipedia).
- New museum budget: set at £337m in 2019, risen to £437m by September 2024. The City of London Corporation's total is £222m and the Mayor's £95m, after £25m extra from each in 2024. The museum's fundraising target is £100m, with about £45m raised from private donations, sponsors and philanthropy and green loans to be explored for £20m (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 2024).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies gave £20 million, announced on 18 March 2025: the museum's largest private donation, for the Smithfield transformation and digital access. Bloomberg also gave the museum the Bloomberg Collection of more than 14,000 Roman artefacts, found by MOLA archaeologists in the 2012 to 2014 excavation for Bloomberg's City of London headquarters (London Museum press release).
- The Worshipful Company of Goldsmiths announced £10 million in 2017 for a permanent display of the Cheapside Hoard (Wikipedia).
- The website lists the City of London Corporation, the Mayor of London and Arts Council England as funders (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of a fossil-fuel, tobacco, arms or Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is light and aimed at the rebrand, cost and pay, not at the museum's funders. Art-press coverage of the 2024 identity was mocking; the cost report was neutral.
1. The Art Newspaper: unsigned, "Making a splat: Museum of London reveals new name and bird-themed identity" (2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/07/25/making-a-splat-museum-of-london-reveals-new-name-and-bird-themed-identity
- Mocks the pigeon identity, joking that the "glitter" is the bird's droppings.
- Turns director Sharon Ament's line that a good logo gets people talking into a jab that a poor one does too.
- Criticises the branding, not the funding.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "New London Museum gets £50m cash boost in face of rising costs" (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/09/05/new-london-museum-%C2%A350m-cash-boost-in-face-of-rising-costs
- Reports the budget rise from £337m to £437m and the £50m from the City and the GLA.
- Reports no explicit criticism from any named party.
3. Museums Journal: Geraldine Kendall Adams, "London Museum rejects pigeon and splat plagiarism claim" (5 May 2026)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2026/05/london-museum-rejects-pigeon-and-splat-plagiarism-claim/
- Manchester studio May Wild Studio says the rebrand copies its Coo Pigeon design; Anti Copying in Design backs the claim.
- The museum says Uncommon Creative Studio developed the identity independently: "We are confident it was developed entirely independently and do not accept that the pigeon and splat is a copy."
- Trade press; concerns the logo, not the museum's money.
4. Museums Journal: Ioan Hazell, "Union action secures 3.5% pay rise for London Museum staff" (16 April 2026)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2026/04/union-action-secures-3-5-pay-rise-for-london-museum-staff/
- Prospect members, over 100 of them on a picket line at Docklands in February, struck over a 2025 pay rise they called unacceptable; they accepted 3.5% for 2026/27 with 96% in favour on an 86% turnout.
- Prospect's Noel McClean said investment was going to capital projects instead of people. The museum did not respond to the journal.
- Labour dispute, not criticism of funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean on the record opened: public funding from the City of London Corporation and the Mayor, plus Bloomberg Philanthropies and the Goldsmiths. The weak points are the £100m rise in the Smithfield budget, the 2026 pay dispute and the open plagiarism claim over the logo. Watch the final cost and funding gap when the new site opens.
