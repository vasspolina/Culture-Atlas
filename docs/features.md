# Features

What the atlas does and how each part behaves. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## What it does

- **Chat.** Laid out as a dialogue: set like an interview transcript: a two-letter speaker mark (ME, CA) in the margin wrapped lines hanging 1.6em in, and the first line of every paragraph, first or later, indented one em, your turns in green, one blank line between turns, no bubbles and no italics. Answers from the records first: it names the record and its source. Where the atlas has no record of what was asked, or the record has nothing on the detail asked for, the model fills the gap from its own knowledge, and that part opens with the words 'From general knowledge' so it is never read as the record (user, 9 Nov 2026; the rule is GENERAL KNOWLEDGE in the chat's system prompt). With no model reachable the chat answers from the records alone and says when they hold nothing. An empty chat narrates what the atlas does: six lines are typed out under its title, with the suggested questions under them (never fewer than four, even if the panel then scrolls); with reduced motion it appears complete, and a screen reader gets the whole text at once (`assets/intro-narration.js`, the "INTRO NARRATION" block in `assets/atlas.css`). Where the atlas is thin (a name with no record, a city or country with three records or fewer) or the visitor asks about history, archives or what is missing, the answer closes on one short paragraph: most lives were never written down, a place with few records is not a place without history, imagine the people who lived there, and add what you know with Share information (user, 18 Nov 2026; UNRECORDED LIVES in the chat's system prompt, and the narration's closing lines). The Share information form opens with the same undertone. An optional API key in settings has your own model answer in the browser.
- **Globe and map.** Works like a maps app: nothing opens on hover. Click or tap a space and its record opens in the sheet (the right column on a desktop, the bottom sheet on a phone), with a dropped pin on the place; click a city and its place sheet opens there, with its country and its spaces, each one a link to its record; click empty sea to close it. Zoom carries on from the globe into a city's street map and back out: zooming out of the street map past z8 hands over to the globe at that spot with the open sheet kept, instead of jumping to the whole world. The globe is drawn in hairlines only (coastlines, borders, hollow rings for spaces) and fills its whole pane.
- **Money maps.** Each record has a map of what it states about its funding: the space in the middle, a circle for each funder, flag or documented tie, and lines between them. Circles that name the same funder link to other spaces. Open it from a record's Money map button, or go to `money-maps.html#<record id>`; `money-maps.html` on its own shows the big map of every space and sponsor. Drag to turn the map in 3D, pinch or scroll to fly through it, touch to make the circles sway. See "Money maps" below.
- **Artists’ images.** A layer in the Layers panel (Artists, “Artists’ images”), also switched on by the Images filter, the chip at the end of the row of filters over the map, which shows only the spaces with artists' images (user, 13 Nov 2026: "add filter that has places with images by artists"), over the spaces that the cultural space critique collection (references 1801–2000) names: 31 images of artists’ and designers’ work on cultural institutions, on seven spaces (W139, Raven Row, MoMA, Tate Modern, Rijksakademie, Walker Art Center, Museum Folkwang). SALT and the Taipei Fine Arts Museum are mapped in `SPACES` too, but the one image each had was left out as too small or soft (see below), so they are off the layer until a better file is found. KADIST (Paris) has a record since 14 Nov 2026 and is mapped to Pablo Helguera's 30 Artoons in `SPACES`, but kadist.org refuses automated downloads, so it joins the layer only once those files are added to the image directory. Only references whose own credit, connection note or source page names a space the atlas has a record for are kept; those that name only a commercial gallery, a publisher or a space with no record (Nest, KADIST, Fundación Proa, Sandberg Instituut, Flat Time House, Gallery 400 and others) are left out. With the layer on, each of those spaces has a diamond ring on the globe, and pointing at it shows its images; this is the one hover on the map and works only while the layer is on (user, 13 Nov 2026). On a desktop the images show in the chat window, not over the map (user, 15 Nov 2026): one large picture at the column's width with its title and artist under it, a strip of the space's other images (pointing at one makes it the large one) and Open gallery; the column keeps them while the pointer travels from the map into it, and leaving it or pointing at empty map gives the conversation back. Where the column is not on screen, a card over the map is used instead. A thumbnail or Open gallery opens a gallery that steps through the space’s images with Previous and Next, the arrow keys or a swipe, each with its credit and a link to its source. A click on the space still opens its sheet, and the record carries the same images in an Artists’ images row, which is how a touch screen reaches them. The images were fetched from the source address the collection’s index records for each one and are kept in `assets/critique_gallery/`; `tools/build_critique_gallery.py` builds them and `assets/critique-gallery-data.js` from the collection’s `gallery.html`, and `SPACES` in it says which reference sits on which space and why. The layer is `assets/critique-gallery.js`; its look is the “ARTISTS’ IMAGES, THE IMAGES LINK AND THE LOAD IMAGE” block at the end of `assets/sheet.css`, and nowhere else. `artists-images.html` (linked as Images in the head bar, Artists’ images in the phone menu and under Studies in the Layers panel, and set on `assets/sheet.css` like the atlas) shows the collection By date, from the oldest work to the newest with the decades as its contents line (user, 15 Nov 2026: "rearrange it starting from the oldest criticism"), or By space (the layer's 31, a work on two spaces shown once under both names). The date of each work, its caption and, where pictures of one work differ, a line for the single image are written by hand in `assets/critique-captions.js`, from what the picture shows, its credit and its source page; `window.critiqueCaption` there makes the caption for this page and for the map layer's gallery alike: title, people, what the picture is and where, photographer. The collection's type label ("Graphic design / artist-run exhibition") is printed nowhere, and the page no longer lists what could not be fetched (user, 15 Nov 2026, of both: "slop"). **No low-resolution, enlarged, cropped or blurry image is shown** (user, 15 Nov 2026): `tools/build_critique_gallery.py` leaves out any source under 900px on its long side (`MIN_LONG`) and the files named in `LEFT_OUT`, each with its reason, found by looking at every image at full size; 141 of the 167 fetched remain. Twenty images of thirteen works found after the collection (Mierle Laderman Ukeles, Andrea Fraser, Guerrilla Girls, Hito Steyerl, Maria Eichhorn, Occupy Museums, Decolonize This Place, Forensic Architecture, BP or not BP?; user, 15 Nov 2026: "find more images of critisizm of art spaces by artists and add to the archive") are listed in `data/critique_additions.json`, one entry per image with its source page, credit and date; `tools/add_critique_images.py` writes them to `assets/critique_additions/` and `assets/critique-additions-data.js`, and the page sorts them in by date. Nine more images of seven works were added the same day (Guerrilla Girls' posters on the Whitney and on Europe's museums, three of Louise Lawler's photographs of art where it is kept and sold, Fred Wilson's Grey Area, and Lawler's Adjusted at Museum Ludwig; user, 15 Nov 2026: "add more images to critisizm images archive"). Every work has a Learn more line under its title that opens its background story (user, 15 Nov 2026: "add learn more dropdown explaining whats the background story"): the collection's in `assets/critique-stories.js`, the additions' in the `story` field of `data/critique_additions.json`, each written from the work's source page and established accounts, and short where the source says little. Each was looked at whole and at full size before it went in; files under 1200px stop that tool, and found files that were soft (two Andrea Fraser video stills, Tania Bruguera's Tatlin's Whisper #5), a strip (Hans Haacke's Shapolsky et al. as MACBA reproduces it) or without a page saying which work they show were not added. The five pictures in `assets/visual_critique/` that some records show are illustrations, not photographs of the works, and are not on this page. each opens in a viewer that zooms up to six times its fitted size (scroll, pinch, double-click or double-tap, + and −, 0 to fit) and pans by dragging. For that the full images are kept at up to 2400 pixels on the long side, never enlarged past the source.
- **Load image.** While the atlas loads, one of the artists' images at random (a set of thirteen, `SPLASH` in `tools/build_critique_gallery.py`, saved at 1400px; mostly memes and caricatures, user 15 Nov 2026: ten are cartoons, the ArtLeaks Gazette caricatures and W.A.G.E.'s MISSING poster; `python3 -I tools/build_critique_gallery.py --splash` rebuilds the set alone) fills about 40% of the screen's width on a desktop and 70% on a phone, on the paper with its title and artist under it. It stays for 5 seconds, never longer, and a click or a key takes it away sooner (user, 13 Nov 2026). `assets/load-image.js`, drawn by the same block at the end of `assets/sheet.css`, alone on the paper with no stone bars.
- **Phone menu.** The ⋮ menu's items are fixed 44px rows parted by grey rules, the check mark in its own slot at the right, so nothing moves when an item is pressed or checked (user, 14 Nov 2026); the rules are the phone-menu block at the end of `assets/sheet.css`.
- **Personal accounts.** Each staff or visitor post in a record opens with "User posted on Glassdoor" (or Reddit, kununu, Indeed), then the poster's role and the date, each on a line of its own, with no middle dots (user, 13 Nov 2026).
- **Typography.** Set after Jost Hochuli's *Detail in Typography*: kerning, ligatures and capital-height punctuation on; measure held to about 66 characters with a soft ragged right and no lone last words; leading rises with line length; tracking tightens as size grows; quotes, apostrophes, ellipses, en dashes and year ranges are corrected in the chat and the record by `assets/typeset.js`; acronyms are set a step smaller. Emphasis is set in Regular Italic. There is no bold (the sheet, `assets/sheet.css`): the name of a space is told by size, as the sheet's large line, and keeps the capitals it was written with; every other heading and label is in the one size of text and is told by the space around it. In a record, the info text and the chat on a phone, the first paragraph of a run is flush and each one after it is indented one em, with no blank line; a source list or registry line is a break, so the paragraph after it starts flush. The desktop chat keeps its transcript setting, where every paragraph is indented. The fonts carry no small caps or old-style figures.
- **Street map.** Drawn in hairlines one device pixel wide (50% grey, the same in light and dark; buildings, paths and landcover only from zoom 16, the table is at `CITY_MAP_LINE_STYLE` in `index.html`), with its place names still set in PP Neue Gstaad Regular (the glyph files in assets/glyphs were made from it; the rest of the page is Jokker). The map host serves only its own fonts, so the glyphs for the Latin ranges are generated from the font file and kept in `assets/glyphs/` (regenerate them if the font changes). The Building view button appears only on records with a street address. **Close in (zoom 13 and up) the street map is set like a printed gallery map**, after Fraser Muggeridge studio's Bloomsbury Art Galleries map: the blocks a fine stipple of dots, the streets paper between two edges in the map's line colour, squares and water plain, street and square names in Atlas Serif capitals (glyphs cut from Liberation Serif, Times New Roman's metric twin), stations in a ruled box, each space a solid mark in its funding grade's colour and its tier's shape (circle, square, triangle) with its name in a rounded ink speech bubble set in Atlas Serif whose tail points at it (no dropped pin there) and a scale bar. Further out the hairlines stay as they were. The map layers are the `poster_` layers in `CITY_MAP_LINE_STYLE`; the marks are the "STREET MAP CLOSE IN" block at the end of `assets/atlas.css`, the tags the last block of `assets/sheet.css`.
- **On a phone, like a maps app.** The globe fills the screen. The search and chat box floats over it at the bottom. A question typed there goes to the chat, the Chat button beside it opens the conversation where it was left, and its menu holds Read answers aloud and New chat. A row across the top of the map and the chat holds Back to chat, Dark and Gossip mode (16 Nov 2026); Back to chat shows on the map and on the Share information form, not over the chat itself, and the filters hang under the row. The suggested questions sit over it as a row of chips that scrolls sideways. Layers is a round button at the top right that opens as a sheet from the bottom. While its sheet is up it is the only thing on the map: a scrim behind it, and the filters, the colour key and the Top story strip step aside (9 Nov 2026: it had opened under the filters and the key). The colour key is the first section of that sheet on a phone, not a box on the map: open on the map by default it had covered it on a small phone. A space's record is a sheet with three heights: peek, half and full. Peek holds the title and the description: for a space, its name, grade, place, the first three lines of its description and its actions; for a funder's record, its head (kind, name, place, source). It is set like a Google Maps place card: the name, a rating line (the funding grade as a coloured word, Grade D, with nothing after it, and an i that opens how grades work), the place, a row of pill actions with icons (the first filled), then tabs (Overview, Funding, Record, Sources) with the open one underlined; Overview lists the address, hours, admission and website as rows with an icon each. It follows the finger and snaps to the nearest height; a flick lands on the next height past the finger, in its direction; a flick down from peek closes it, and from higher up a pull lands on peek. Where peek is already about half the screen, half is not a stop: the heights are peek and full. The grabber is a button: a tap, or Enter, goes up a height and from full back to peek (15 Nov 2026; the rules are `sheetStops` and `sheetLanding` in `assets/phone-maps.js`, shared with the reports sheet). The map answers as a maps app does: drag to turn it, pinch to zoom, double-tap to zoom in where you tap, tap with two fingers to zoom out, and panning it lowers a half-open record to its peek. A location button at the bottom right flies to where you are and marks it with a blue dot. A Top story strip over the chips shows the newest dated report and opens a feed of the latest reports on the spaces, laid out after a news app: the top story with a poster block (kind and date, the space's name in capitals, its place and the report's status), the outlet, headline, when and where, what is said, the reply and outcome, a row of the sources and the status, and Share; then Near you (once the location is known) and Worldwide. It is built only from the dated items in each record's `gossip_data.gossip` and `case_notes` and follows `notes/gossip-criteria.md`: outside Gossip mode only what is confirmed or resolved, and the documented cases; in Gossip mode talk and denials too, each marked for what it is. On a phone the chat is part of the same app: a sheet over the map like the record, with a title, the map's ⋮ menu (Read answers aloud, New chat) and one cross, the map's white pill as its composer, questions as rounded bubbles and the suggested ones as chips; the old bar across the top is gone. The chat's head holds two tabs, Chat and Details, as the Claude app sets Chat and Preview over an artifact; Details is there once a space is known and opens the record as a page of its own: full height, it only scrolls and is never dragged, with the same two tabs at its top, Details now the open one, and Chat goes back to the conversation where it was left. The phone's chat composer holds the box and Send, with Share information as a full-width button under it (16 Nov 2026). On a phone the record has one view: the sheet's tabs (Overview, Funding, Record, Sources) hold the whole record, sorted by subject, and the separate Full record page opens the sheet instead. The suggested questions follow the place: the visitor's city once the location button has found them, or the city the map is zoomed into. An alert now is the Top story, on the strip over the chips and at the head of the feed, with any other alerts after it. Alerts mark a protest, strike, dispute, allegation or similar at a space now (reported in the last 60 days and not resolved; outside Gossip mode only confirmed ones) with a red ring on the map and a section in the feed; Alert me sends a browser notification for a new one while the atlas is open. Every report says what kind of source it rests on and when the atlas last opened its links, and says outright when it is user-generated (anonymous posts, user reviews, the Personal accounts). Not every report is the press: the feed also carries the posts by staff and visitors on the records (Glassdoor, Reddit and the like), in a section of their own and marked user-generated and not verified, and the leaked documents on the records (WikiLeaks), with their dates. A staff or visitor post never raises an alert outside Gossip mode, and a document never does. The feed is checked every minute: `tools/build_live_feed.py` writes the records' dated, sourced items to `assets/live.json` and `assets/live-stamp.json` on every deploy and once a day on a schedule (05:17 UTC, `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`), the page polls the stamp, and loads the new items without a reload. New reports are searched for every day: a scheduled Claude session looks through the press for the last three days, adds what passes the rules with `tools/add_reports.py` (which numbers the items, rebuilds the records and the feed and moves the release on) and merges its own pull request; `notes/daily-reports.md` is its instruction and keeps a log of every run. It is as current as the records, never ahead of them: an item reaches the page once the editor has checked it and it is deployed, and nothing without a date, a source address and a check date is shown (`tests/live-feed.test.mjs`). The box at the bottom asks and finds in one: a space's or a city's name on its own goes to the map, anything else to the chat, and with a conversation under way a tap on it goes back to the chat. The code is in `assets/phone-maps.js`, and the look is the "PHONE: MAPS APP" block at the end of `assets/atlas.css`.
- **Tab suggestions in the chat box** (`assets/tab-suggest.js`, 17 Nov 2026). While a question is typed, the rest of a likely question, or of a space's or city's name, shows in grey after the caret. Tab or the right arrow takes it, Escape puts it away, and on a phone a tap on the grey words takes it. Nothing is sent anywhere to make a suggestion; Tab moves focus as usual when none is shown.
- **Offline.** Installable as an app; the service worker caches the page.

Tiers describe how well a record is sourced, not a verdict on the museum: Clean (source cited), Flagged (a recorded reason), Unverified. The map carries a colour key (`#mapColourKey`, top right on a desktop, top left on a phone) that names the three colours, with a round i button by its title that opens the rules behind them, the same control as a record's funding grade; a city takes the tier most of its spaces have. See [What the page may show](data.md#what-the-page-may-show). Funding transparency ratings, A to F, summarize what each record shows about funding; see [Funding transparency ratings](data.md#funding-transparency-ratings).

A row of filters sits over the map, on the desktop beside the search box and on a phone at the top of the screen: **All spaces**, then **Funding connections**, **Epstein connections**, **Propaganda**, **Independent**, **Open books**, **Artist-run** and **Images** (the spaces with artists' images). One is on at a time: pick one and the map shows only the spaces it is about; pick it again, or All spaces, and every space is back. The same seven are the Filters boxes of the Layers panel, which follow the same rule. The panel's **Funding connections** (one box for the lines between spaces that share a funder, flagged and right-wing funders, the funders around a space in focus and their head offices), **Epstein connections** (spaces whose funders or people appear in the files, and those people's offices), **Propaganda**, **Independent** and **Artist-run**. The records carry no field for the last two, so `assets/layers.js` reads each record's own words (summary, watch note, funding note, focus, name): "independent art space", "remains independent", "artist-run", "founded by artists" and the like. "Independently verified" or "independent audit" does not count.

## Using the chat

Open the chat, or press `Cmd+K` / `Ctrl+K`, and ask about a space, a city or a funder, for example "Which spaces in Tokyo are Clean?" or "What does the record for Te Papa say about funding?". An optional API key can be added under settings.

The chat is called Clio or Iris, picked again on every load (user, 17 Nov 2026): the tab reads "Chat with Clio", the heading "Ask Clio". The pick is the first script in `index.html` (`window.ATLAS_CHAT_NAME`, the CSS variable `--chat-name`, and `data-chat-name` in the markup), and the model is told the same name.

## Visit counting

`assets/analytics.js` counts visits without cookies and without a third-party script, and is off until `window.ATLAS_ANALYTICS_URL` in `assets/config.js` holds the count address of a GoatCounter site (`https://<code>.goatcounter.com/count`; the account is free for non-commercial use). To turn it on: make the site at goatcounter.com, put its address in `config.js`, bump the `?v=` on the `config.js` tags.

One small request per count carries the page path (never the query string or hash), the title, the referring site without its query, and the screen width. Three events are counted by name: `record/<id>` when a record opens, `chat/question` when a question is asked, `gossip/on` when Gossip mode is switched on. The words of a question, the text and files of a note, settings and API keys are never sent, and nothing is stored in the browser. Nothing is sent when the browser sets Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, on localhost, or from a file. `window.atlasCount(name)` adds a count from anywhere. Tests: `node --test tests/analytics.test.mjs`.

The page itself is kept here so the atlas is the one place it is published. Both
are linked from Map layers & legend, under Studies. Rebuild after each Sponsor Atlas import:

## Influence mode

**Influence mode is the money map** (user, 17 Nov 2026: "Influence mode is now money map. Combine the research. Make money map go into influence mode"), renamed **Influence map** the same day ("rename money map into influence map"). The switch is called **Influence map** in the head bar and the phone's top row, and `?money` opens the page with it on (`?influence` still does). The map draws every private funder with a head office on record, not only the ledger's: the funders of the funding maps (`assets/money-maps.json` and the offices of `assets/hq.json`, through `window.atlasHq`) join the 39 ledger funders, about 400 dots in all; public bodies stay out, as they do in the ledger. A ledger funder's spaces are its ledger's plus those the funding maps name it on; a funder from the maps alone has no ledger, and its record is its office's, which lists the spaces it funds. Only the 60 widest-reaching map-only funders try for a name at world zoom, so placing names stays quick; the rest show theirs when pointed at, picked, or tied to the space in focus. A record's **Money map** button opens the mode on that space: a line from each of its funders' offices, their names placed first, and the chat lists them. The **Exit money map** pill and Escape leave the mode. The code is `mergeMoney`, `mapSpaces` and `atlasInfluenceFocusSpace` in `assets/influence-mode.js`, and `hq.moneyFunders`, `hq.spacesOfKey` and `atlasShowMoney` in `assets/layers.js`; the old money map layer is no longer reached from the page.

The atlas from the funders' side (user, 17 Nov 2026: "switch the perspective a little from the receivers to the senders ... and see what kind of influence they're buying"). Influence mode is a switch beside Gossip mode, in the desktop head bar and in the phone's row of top buttons, and `?influence` opens the page with it on. While it is on, the map shows the funders instead of the spaces (user, 17 Nov 2026: "Influence mode needs to switch the map from culture spaces to cultural funders"): each pilot funder at its head office as a dot, larger the more spaces it reaches (user, 17 Nov 2026: "just do dots dont do different figure"; it was a hexagon for a company and a triangle for a family), its name in plain type beside it (a name with no free place waits, as a city's does, until the map is zoomed in or the mark is pointed at); city marks and the alert marks over spaces are put away. Picking a funder, on the map or in the chat, opens its ledger and brings its spaces onto the map, each joined to its office by a great-circle line; picking it again puts them away. Head offices come from `assets/hq.json`, and for the funders it lacks, from `HQ_EXTRA` in `tools/build_influence.py` (city level, the listed head office, or for a family the company its money came from); the chat says what the mode is and lists the funders, each with a tally of what it got; a space's record leads with **What funders got here**; and a funder's record (the Details tab, `assets/hq-detail.js`) leads with its ledger, kind by kind and space by space. Switched off, the page is as it was. The code is `assets/influence-mode.js`; the spaces are put away through `window.atlasModeKeep`, which `atlasLayerKeep` in `assets/layers.js` reads, and the globe in `index.html` calls `atlasInfluenceDraw` and `atlasInfluencePointer` for the funders' marks; the look is the "INFLUENCE MODE" block at the end of `assets/sheet.css`.

**The sender is the subject** (user, 17 Nov 2026: "If the money doesn't go to culture it doesn't disappear so who is at fault?"). A space that turns the money down ends one tie, not the money, and a ledger that counts only what spaces took makes the receivers the ones at fault. So a funder's record also says, before what it got:

- **Where the money comes from**: one sentence on the business, then any harm a source documents (a settlement, a fine, a disaster).
- **Beside culture**: the size of the business for one stated year, what it spends on lobbying, and its political giving, each a figure exactly as its source gives it.
- **When a space said no**: the spaces that ended a tie, then every tie the records date in or after the first refusal and do not say ended, at a space that did not end it later. The refusal's year is the latest year the entry names, since some entries are dated at the gift and give the end in the summary. Last come what the sources say happened next, where one does. The section is drawn only when there is something after the refusal to show, a tie dated after it or what a source says happened next (user, 17 Nov 2026: "only add section if there is an info"); a refusal alone is already in the ledger, marked Ended, and the chat's line under the funder then says only how many spaces ended it.

On the map, a picked funder's ended ties are dashed lines and its running ones solid; the chat's list of funders says under each how many spaces ended it and how many are still dated after. The facts beside culture are hand-sourced in `data/influence_senders.json`, keyed by funder, each with a kind (`harm`, `scale`, `lobbying`, `politics`, `after`), a date and its own source, read on that page. The first batch (17 Nov 2026) covers all 16 funders: EU lobbying from LobbyFacts, climate-lobbying grades from InfluenceMap, profits from the companies' results, settlements and political giving from news and court sources. OpenSecrets would not load, so there were no US federal lobbying totals in that batch. A second pass (17 Nov 2026) covers all 25 funders under the same rules, sourced, dated and checked: EU lobbying from the EU Transparency Register, each figure read on its LobbyFacts datacard and checked against the register's own export of 16 November 2026; InfluenceMap grades re-read on each company page; and US federal lobbying for 2025 from the Senate Lobbying Disclosure Act reports on lda.gov, the filings OpenSecrets compiles its totals from, since OpenSecrets itself still refuses an automated reader. A company's own in-house reports for the year are summed (the in-house figure already includes what it paid outside firms); where it files none, the fees its outside firms reported are given instead. Each fact was checked against the atlas: it names the entity the atlas ties name, so Philip Morris International's facts were removed because the atlas ties under Philip Morris are Altria's, Bloomberg L.P. and Bloomberg Philanthropies are named apart, and the Estée Lauder Companies' lobbying is not given to the Lauder family. Absences are stated in the data, not shown as facts: BMO, the Royal Bank of Canada itself, Koch, Purdue Pharma and Access Industries are not on the EU register; RBC and Access Industries filed no 2025 federal report; InfluenceMap shows no score for UBS or J.P. Morgan and has no page for RBC, Bloomberg or Chanel. The kinds of influence an entry can name:

| Kind | Counts |
|---|---|
| Name | its name on a prize, a room, a building or a programme |
| Seat | a place on the board, or its people on it |
| Programme | a show, a festival, research or education it paid for |
| Building | a building, a refurbishment or an endowment |
| Access | galas, corporate memberships, events and hospitality |
| Silence | terms kept from the public, or work withdrawn |
| Licence to operate | culture tied to its own operations or reputation |
| Tax | a gift routed through a tax incentive |
| Money only | money is stated, and nothing about what came with it |

An entry also says whether the tie ended and whether it met protest; a protest or resignation with nothing bought stated is listed as pushback.

**Where the entries come from.** `tools/build_influence.py` reads `institutions.json` and writes `data/influence_candidates.json`: every sentence of a record's own text (funding, watch, flags, details, confirmed reports, documented cases) that names one of the pilot funders in `FUNDERS`, with the field it is in, that field's sources and any date. The kinds it guesses from keywords are a reading aid only. Each candidate is then read by hand, and `data/influence_review.json` keeps the verdict: kept, with its kinds, a one-line summary, and whether it ended or met protest; or dropped, with the reason (BP that is the French *budget primitif*, a different Koch or Griffin, a shell mound, a law firm that acts for an oil company). Only kept entries reach `assets/influence.json`, which the page reads. Nothing is fetched and no record is changed.

```bash
python3 -I tools/build_influence.py   # candidates from the records, then assets/influence.json from the review
node --test tests/influence.test.mjs  # every entry has a summary, known kinds, a real space and a source; no stale review
```

**When the records are reworded.** A verdict is keyed by a hash of the record id, the funder key and the sentence, so a reworded sentence loses its verdict and the build warns that reviewed candidates are no longer in the records. Before the build, while `data/influence_candidates.json` still holds the old sentences, run the re-key tool:

```bash
python3 -I tools/rekey_influence_review.py           # report only
python3 -I tools/rekey_influence_review.py --write   # move the verdicts, park the rest
python3 -I tools/build_influence.py
python3 -I tools/build_art_periods.py                # its ties are keyed by cid too
```

It moves a verdict only to a sentence in the same record and field that names the same funder, where each is the other's closest match and either the rewrite logs (`data/slop_runs/*/*.json`) show the one became the other, or the two are at least 85% alike with the same numbers and no rival. The verdict's kinds, summary and checks are carried untouched. Anything else is parked in `data/influence_review_unmatched.json` with the old sentence and the closest current one; a parked kept entry stays in `assets/influence.json` with its sentence as reviewed and `unmatched: true` until the editor settles it, by setting `move_to` (the cid of a current candidate) or `remove: true` on it and running the tool with `--write` again.

## Gossip mode

Gossip mode shows what people are actually saying about a space on the internet: in the arts press, on Reddit, on accounts that collect staff complaints, in open letters, union statements and court filings, and in the comments under them. The rules for what counts, what never goes in, which sources are trusted and how an item is written are in `notes/gossip-criteria.md`; read them before adding anything.

The items live in `data/gossip_items.json`, one object each (schema in the rules, §4). `tools/attach_gossip.py` checks every item against the mechanical rules, marks `talk` items older than 36 months `stale`, drops rumours older than a year that no outlet picked up, and writes the rest onto the records as `gossip_data.gossip`, newest first; then `clean_data.py` and `embed_data.py` as usual. Re-running replaces the field. Items are reopened at least once a year; `retrieved_on` is the proof.

In Gossip mode the record page leads with the items (desktop: the Gossip row; phone: the cards at the top of the record), the globe marks a space with at least one item that is not stale, and the chat answers from the items first, naming who says each one and ending any item about a named person with the response line. The model gets them as the GOSSIP section of the grounding (`assets/grounding.js`) with the same instruction. Outside Gossip mode the items are not volunteered; asked directly, the chat answers from them with the same rules. Every gossip answer carries the line "Talk collected from the internet, with its sources. Unverified unless marked confirmed or resolved."

Switching Gossip mode on tells one story and asks before telling another. The story is about the place in view (user, 21 Nov 2026: "make gossip mode to be specific to selected place"): the open space's own newest item, or the reported line of its record when it has no dated item; then its city, its country, and the rest of the records, in that order (`gossipScope`, `gossipTiers`, `pickGossip` in `index.html`). With a city or a country on the map and no space open, the stories start there. A story from further out opens by saying where it is ("Elsewhere in New York, at ...") and never says what the place in view lacks. "Tell me another" follows whatever is in view when it is asked, the chips under the story stay in the same place, and opening a space on the map while the mode is on tells that space's own story if it has one.

Gossip mode speaks in one voice (user, 21 Nov 2026: "make gossip mode talk like yandex alisa, it needs to sound like investigative journalist"): an investigative journalist who talks the way a voice assistant with a character does. First person, straight to the visitor, quick and dry, a little ironic, never sweet; and a reporter's habits: who says it, where and when, what is confirmed and what is only said. The irony is for institutions and money, never for a named person. The page's own lines (`GOSSIP_OPENERS`, `GOSSIP_HOOKS`, `GOSSIP_OFF`), the model's greeting (`gossipModelGreeting`) and the gossip answers (VOICE in the `<gossip_mode>` block) are written in it. The voice reports what others published and never claims a source, a document or an interview of its own: a greeting that does is dropped for the page's own sentences, and a gossip answer loses the sentence (`GOSSIP_OWN_SOURCE`, `dropOwnSourceClaims`). Read aloud, a gossip turn, and any turn while the mode is on, uses a woman's English voice from those installed on the device, a little brighter and brisker than the curator's (`selectGossipVoice`); with none installed the curator's voice reads at the same delivery. Alisa's recorded voice is Yandex's own and is not one a web page can use.

Gossip in the chat is set upright and told apart by its colour alone (user, 21 Nov 2026: "do not make it italics").

In Gossip mode the content itself is told in the mode's voice (user, 21 Nov 2026: "it just not changing content to gossip", then "Retell in gossip voice"). An item can carry `told`: its `said` retold as the dry first-person reporter, with the same claims, sources, dates, figures and quotations (schema and rules in `notes/gossip-criteria.md` §4). While the mode is on, the feed's cards (`assets/phone-maps.js`) and the record's gossip items (`gossipSaid` in `index.html`) show `told` in place of `said`; outside the mode, and for an item not yet retold, they show `said`. The chat model is always given `said`. Retellings are written in batches with `tools/tell_gossip.py`, which lists the items still to do, newest first, and refuses a retelling that adds a figure, a quotation or a name its item does not carry; `--count` says how many of the items are retold. In Gossip mode a gossip report in the feed is also set in the gossip colour (user, 21 Nov 2026: "make yellow also").
