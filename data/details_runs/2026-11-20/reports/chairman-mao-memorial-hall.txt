CHAIRMAN MAO MEMORIAL HALL: Beijing, China
ID: chairman-mao-memorial-hall
Website: http://cpc.people.com.cn/GB/143527/143528/index.html (checked 2026-11-20; the hall's page on People's Daily, where it posts notices and bookings)

OVERVIEW
- Mausoleum of Mao Zedong on Tiananmen Square, on a 5.72 hectare site with 33,867 square metres of floor space (People's Daily, 2020).
- Building began on 24 November 1976, the main structure was finished on 24 May 1977 and it opened on 9 September 1977 (People's Daily, 2020).
- A colonnaded cube with 44 square granite columns; Mao lies in a crystal coffin in the Hall of Tribute, draped with the Party flag, and the second floor holds memorial rooms for six leaders and a cinema hall (Our China Story, Academy of Chinese Studies).
- Wikipedia names Zhou Enlai, Liu Shaoqi and Zhu De among those honoured, after a 1983 Central Committee decision to share the hall with other revolutionaries.
- Design was led by Zhao Pengfei with Yuan Jingshi and Shen Bo, drawing on Tsinghua and Tianjin universities; materials came from across China, from Sichuan granite to rock samples from Mount Everest (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia, citing scholars, notes that Mao was the first to sign a 1950s proposal that leaders be cremated, and that no reliable record explains the decision to preserve his body.
- People's Daily counted about 240 million visits by 2020. On 9 September 2026, the 50th anniversary of Mao's death, long queues formed outside (Beijing Daily).
- Visiting: free, by real-name booking one to six days ahead through the hall's WeChat account, WeChat mini-program or People's Daily page; open Tuesday to Sunday 8.00 to 12.00 since reopening on 1 September 2026 (People's Daily notices; Wikipedia for free entry).

FUNDING
Chairman Mao Memorial Hall: state-funded: the Communist Party and the Chinese state
- Founders: built by joint decision of the CCP Central Committee, the National People's Congress Standing Committee, the State Council and the Central Military Commission, taken on 8 October 1976 (People's Daily, 2020).
- Construction: coordinated by the State Council's "Ninth Office" under Vice Premier Gu Mu, with a project command led by Li Ruihuan from 9 November 1976; 700,000 people from across China did "symbolic voluntary labor" (Wikipedia, citing Chinese-language sources).
- Operator: the Chairman Mao Memorial Hall Administration Bureau, which issues its notices and closed the hall from 1 March to 31 August 2017 for repairs to the building and equipment over about 40,744 square metres (Phoenix News, 2017).
- Visitors pay nothing; since 1 January 2025 they can download an electronic commemorative ticket (People's Daily notice).

BOTTOM LINE
State money only, from the Party and government that built it, with no outside funder. The weak point is its purpose: a Party shrine to its founder, not an independent museum.
