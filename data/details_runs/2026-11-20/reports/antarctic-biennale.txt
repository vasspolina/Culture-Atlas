ANTARCTIC BIENNALE: Antarctic Peninsula, Antarctica
ID: antarctic-biennale
Website: https://antarcticbiennale.com (checked 2026-11-20; serves gambling spam)

OVERVIEW
- A shipborne art expedition to the Antarctic Peninsula: the research vessel Akademik Sergey Vavilov sailed from Ushuaia on 17 March 2017 for a 12-day voyage, with talks, performances and screenings on board and works installed at landings (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- Proposed in 2011 by the Russian artist and former Soviet Navy submariner Alexander Ponomarev, its commissioner; in 2014 he set up an Antarctic Pavilion at the Venice Architecture Biennale as its first phase (Biennial Foundation; The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- Organised by Ponomarev with the organiser Anna Shvets and the curator Nadim Samman, who ran a competition for artists under 35; it drew more than 500 applications from 59 countries, and the winners Sho Hasegawa and Gustav Düsing joined the voyage (The Art Newspaper; Biennial Foundation).
- Artists on board included Tomás Saraceno, Julian Charrière, Yto Barrada, Lara Favaretto, Zhang Enli and Abdullah Al Saadi; the advisory board included Hans Ulrich Obrist, Sam Keller and Sheikha Hoor Al-Qasimi (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- Landings included Cuverville Island, where Joaquín Fargas laid reflective sheets over rocks and Düsing tried to freeze a cotton tent (The Art Newspaper, Adrian Dannatt's dispatch, 2017). The 15 competition finalists showed in the Antarctic Pavilion at the 2017 Venice Biennale (The Art Newspaper).
- UNESCO granted the project its patronage on 24 January 2017 (Biennial Foundation).
- Samman wrote in 2015: "Ours is a topsy-turvy biennale, so perhaps we will only go once" (The Art Newspaper, 2016).

FUNDING
Antarctic Biennale: privately sponsored: mainly Eugene Kaspersky, with the Oceanic Project Foundation
- Cost: the voyage cost "well over a million dollars"; most of it came from Eugene Kaspersky, head of the Russian cyber security firm Kaspersky Lab, and the Chinese collector Qiao Zhibing and his partner Lihsin Tsai also paid in (The Art Newspaper, Anna Somers Cocks, 17 Mar 2017).
- Kaspersky Lab wrote in March 2017: "We are sponsoring the Antarctic Biennale"; Eugene Kaspersky sailed with the expedition, and the company said it inspired Fargas's snow-compressing robot, the Glaciator (Kaspersky blog, 23 Mar 2017).
- Main backer: the Oceanic Project Foundation, whose trustees included Marina Abramovic, Sam Keller of the Fondation Beyeler, museum directors and Russian donors, chaired by Anna Somers Cocks, then chief executive of The Art Newspaper (The Art Newspaper, 15 Jun 2016). Somers Cocks declared in her 2017 article that she chaired the biennale's board of trustees.
- BMW Group was partner of the open call; Thomas Girst, head of BMW Cultural Engagement, presented the winners at Art Basel Miami Beach in December 2016 (Biennial Foundation).
- Erik Owen Arneberg, chairman of the Robert Lee Blaffer Foundation, put money into a documentary of the voyage directed by Denis Delestrac (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- The 2014 Antarctic Pavilion in Venice had the support of the AVC Charity Foundation (Biennial Foundation).

Red flags: main sponsor's company sanctioned by the United States, 2024
- On 21 June 2024 the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control designated twelve executives and senior leaders of AO Kaspersky Lab (US Treasury).
- The Department of Commerce put AO Kaspersky Lab, OOO Kaspersky Group and Kaspersky Labs Limited on the Entity List "for their cooperation with Russian military and intelligence authorities", and barred Kaspersky Lab from providing anti-virus and cyber security products in the United States (US Treasury).
- Eugene Kaspersky is not among the twelve people named in the Treasury release; the sanctions came seven years after the voyage.

BOTTOM LINE
A one-off 2017 voyage paid for mostly by Eugene Kaspersky, whose company the United States put on its Entity List in 2024 for cooperating with Russian military and intelligence authorities. The project's main coverage came from The Art Newspaper, whose chief executive chaired its backing foundation. Its website now serves gambling spam.
