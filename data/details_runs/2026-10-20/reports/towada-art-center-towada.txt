TOWADA ART CENTER: Towada, Japan
ID: towada-art-center-towada
Website: https://towadaartcenter.com/ (checked 2026-10-24; opens; admission prices are not on the pages read)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art museum at 10-9 Nishi-Nibancho, Towada, Aomori Prefecture, in a city of about 66,000 people (Japanese Wikipedia); the museum is the hub of the city's Arts Towada project.
- Opened in 2008. The idea was first studied in 2001, the basic concept finished in 2004, and the name decided in 2007 (the museum). Arts Towada reached its grand opening in 2010.
- Towada City is the owner; a designated manager system was introduced in April 2012 (Japanese Wikipedia) and the museum names Nanjo and Associates Group as the current manager.
- Architect Ryue Nishizawa designed it as separate galleries, one per artwork, linked by glass corridors (the museum).
- 38 permanent works by 33 artists or groups, mostly large installations made for the museum, including Yoko Ono, Yayoi Kusama and Yoshitomo Nara (Japanese Wikipedia). Cube Cafe & Shop on site.
- Visiting: open 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 30 minutes before closing, closed Mondays (or the Tuesday after a Monday holiday). Three accessible parking spaces behind the centre. A closure for air-conditioning renovation is planned, tentatively, from 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2028 (the museum's homepage notice of 27 February 2026). Admission prices were not found.

FUNDING
Towada Art Center: mainly public: city of Towada, with local company members; Japanese Wikipedia says power-siting subsidies paid for the building (unconfirmed)
- Construction: Japanese Wikipedia states that ¥2.284 billion, over 90% of the project cost, came from the "three power laws" subsidies (電源三法交付金) drawn from Aomori's Rokkasho nuclear fuel reprocessing and radioactive waste facilities, and that the city acknowledged it could not have built the museum without them. It cites a Kahoku Shimpo series article ("Kakunen saiko: izon, kofukin de kokyo shisetsu / Genshiryoku manee 1", URL dated January 2015). The newspaper article was not opened, so this is Japanese Wikipedia's claim, not a finding.
- Running costs: the city's page on its FY2017 power-siting area grant projects lists the museum's operation and maintenance, with a project cost of ¥7,539,224 and a grant of ¥5,000,000 (Towada City).
- Corporate members (TAPS), on the museum's members page dated 11 August 2026 (names romanised from the Japanese page): S course: Izumi Denki Koji-ten, Kamikita Kensetsu, Konno Kensetsu, Towada Gas, Nakatsuna-gumi and one anonymous company. The A course has nine members, including Towada Taxi and the Towada Oirase Tourism Organization. Annual fees are not published on the page; membership supports the museum's learning programmes.
- No reporting found of a private foundation or national government grant for the museum's operation.

Red flags: Towada Gas, fossil fuels
- Towada Gas is an S course corporate member of the museum's support scheme, per the members page dated 11 August 2026 (https://towadaartcenter.com/learn/taps/).
- Towada Gas offers gas supply, gas appliances and kerosene delivery (company site). The company site does not mention the museum, and no amount of its membership is published.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by the city, with a corporate member list that includes Towada Gas, a gas and kerosene supplier. Japanese Wikipedia, citing Kahoku Shimpo, says power-siting subsidies tied to Aomori's nuclear facilities paid over 90% of construction; that claim is unconfirmed, and the city's FY2017 list shows a ¥5,000,000 grant towards operation. Watch the city's grant list and the member fees, which are not published.
