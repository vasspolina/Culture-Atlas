6TH OF OCTOBER WAR PANORAMA: Cairo, Egypt
ID: 6th-of-october-war-panorama
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- War museum and memorial to the 1973 October War on Salah Salem Street, Cairo (Egypt Independent), on about 31,000 square metres (al-Dostor, 2025).
- Mada Masr writes that Hosni Mubarak got the idea during a visit to North Korea and that it opened on 5 October 1989.
- A cylindrical, fort-like building in an Islamic architectural style, built with partial assistance from North Korean architects (English Wikipedia).
- Inside, a documentary, a moving model of the Suez Canal front and a 360-degree painted mural seen from a rotating platform; tanks, aircraft and guns, Egyptian and Israeli, stand outside (Egypt Independent; English Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 9:00 to 21:00 except Tuesdays in 2010, with tickets of 3 Egyptian pounds for Egyptians, 10 for Arabs and 30 for foreigners (Egypt Independent).

FUNDING
6th of October War Panorama: public: the Egyptian state through its armed forces
- Commissioned by President Mubarak (Egypt Independent) and run by the armed forces: in October 2025 the armed forces' Military Research Authority held its 18th "Memory of October" exhibition there under the supervision of the Military Museums Department and others (Sada El Balad, 7 October 2025).
- Build: Egypt Independent says Koreans built it over five years; Public Delivery lists it among the works of North Korea's Mansudae Overseas Projects.
- On 5 October 2025 Egypt Telegraph reported that the armed forces had opened military museums free until 6 October, including the panorama's outdoor weapons display, for the 52nd anniversary of the war.

Not related: the Tishreen War Panorama, also called the October War Panorama, in Damascus, Syria. It also had North Korean help but is a separate Syrian museum (English Wikipedia; Public Delivery).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces discuss the panorama: a visitor review that mocks its upkeep and a critical essay on how Egypt presents the war. Neither concerns its money.
1. Egypt Independent: Ali Abdel Mohsen, "Egypt's Museums II: The October War Panorama" (2010)
URL: https://egyptindependent.com/egypts-museums-ii-october-war-panorama/
- Calls the panorama marred by "an almost surreal level of incompetence": misspelt tickets, war vehicles repainted in one flat green, a documentary too degraded to follow, and kiosks selling toys and trinkets.
- Calls the rotating mural striking and realistic, and concludes the visit offers amusement and frustration but no education.
2. Mada Masr: Iman Hamam, "Asleep at the wheel" (2014)
URL: https://www.madamasr.com/en/2014/10/06/feature/culture/asleep-at-the-wheel/
- Asks how the 1973 war came to be presented as a victory "even though it wasn't", and traces the army's mark on Cairo through bridges, public art and museum displays.
- Describes the panorama as a peculiar spot for the victory, set back from the road beside the Ministry of Information and a park with children's fairground rides.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Public Delivery reports that Mansudae Overseas Projects is under sanctions and cites an unpublished UN Panel of Experts report connecting statue projects in Africa to it in 2020.
- Its work in Cairo dates from the 1980s, before those sanctions, and it was a builder, not a funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The Egyptian armed forces own and run the panorama, with no private sponsor. Its weak points are the sanctioned North Korean firm tied to its construction and a display that presents the army's version of the war.
