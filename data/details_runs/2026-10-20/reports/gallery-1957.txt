GALLERY 1957: Accra, Ghana
ID: gallery-1957
Website: https://www.gallery1957.com (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Commercial contemporary art gallery in Accra with three spaces: Gallery 1957 I, II and III, at the Kempinski Hotel Gold Coast City and the Galleria Mall in Ridge, plus a London space at 1 Hyde Park Gate (gallery website).
- Founded by Marwan Zakhem; opened on 6 March 2016, Ghana's Independence Day (Wikipedia). The first space was 140 square metres inside the newly built Kempinski hotel (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- Named for 1957, the year Ghana gained independence (The Art Newspaper).
- Zakhem was donating his private collection to the gallery and planned to commission new work; Nana Oforiatta-Ayim is creative director (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- A second Accra space opened across the road in the Galleria Mall in 2017 (Nataal, 2018), and the London space opened in October 2020 (Wikipedia). It also runs the Yaa Asantewaa Art Prize for women artists.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 18:00 during exhibitions; admission free at both locations (gallery website). No accessibility details found.

FUNDING
Gallery 1957: self-funded: its founder, Marwan Zakhem, and a commercial gallery model
- Zakhem, a British, Lebanese-born, London-raised entrepreneur, founded the gallery and was donating his private collection to it (The Art Newspaper, 2016; TrueAfrica, 2016).
- Nataal (Helen Jennings, 2018) says Zakhem presents the gallery as a commercial model, not a philanthropic one, and that it showed at the 1-54 fair in London in 2018.
- Yaa Asantewaa Art Prize: the 2026 edition, the sixth, pays $5,000 cash to each of two winners, one Ghanaian and one Senegalese, with the Institut Français du Sénégal as partner (gallery website). No GHC 50,000 prize figure appears on that page. The jury includes people from Tate Britain, the 1-54 fair, Gasworks, Haus der Kulturen der Welt and Fondation Cartier.
- Zakhem's business: TrueAfrica (Claude Grunitzky, 10 June 2016) says his company "handles large engineering, procurement and construction contracts in the oil and gas sector" and developed and built the Kempinski Hotel Gold Coast City, which opened in 2015 and houses the gallery. Nataal (2018) calls the Zakhem group a family-owned group of construction companies with interests across Africa and says the hotel's art is Zakhem's collection. Neither page names the company or shows oil production by it, and no payment from it to the gallery is itemised.
- No reporting found of a board, accounts, annual report or any state or corporate funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is launch reporting and interviews that are admiring in tone.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is private: founder Marwan Zakhem, whose company does oil and gas engineering, procurement and construction work and built the hotel that houses the gallery. The weak point is that the gallery publishes no board, accounts or funder list. Worth watching is whether the gallery discloses its funding and governance.
