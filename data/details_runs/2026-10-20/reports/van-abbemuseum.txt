VAN ABBEMUSEUM: Eindhoven, Netherlands
ID: van-abbemuseum
Website: https://vanabbemuseum.nl (checked 2026-10-25; live, though some subpages returned "page not found")

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art at Stratumsedijk 2 in Eindhoven (Van Abbemuseum visit page), on the east bank of the Dommel River (Wikipedia); the museum calls itself "one of the first public museums for contemporary art to be established in Europe" (Van Abbemuseum).
- Opened in 1936; founded by the cigar businessman Henri van Abbe, who sold his collection to the Eindhoven city council in 1934 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Henri van Abbe. Called "Stedelijk Van Abbemuseum" in publications until about 1990 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Alexander Kropholler designed the original building; the Van Abbe factory paid for and donated it in exchange for the city buying part of the collection. Abel Cahen designed the extension with a 27-metre tower, inaugurated by Queen Beatrix in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Collection of over 3,600 works including Beuys, Picasso and Marlene Dumas; four themes: Inclusion and Multivocality, Demodernising and Decolonising, Collectivity and Solidarity, Ecology (Van Abbemuseum). Charles Esche directed it from 2004 to 2024; Defne Ayas was announced as director from 15 September 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday, 11 am to 5 pm (Van Abbemuseum visit page).

FUNDING
Van Abbemuseum: public and lottery funded: city and national funds, a charity lottery, foundations and a company circle
- Eindhoven Municipality, the Mondriaan Fund (exhibitions and public programmes) and Creative Europe are listed as supporters; the pages give no amounts (Van Abbemuseum).
- VriendenLoterij: partner since 2007, extended in 2022 for five years; pays 250,000 euros a year, plus extra money from players who choose the museum. It also paid a one-off 798,500 euros in February 2020 for the "Delinking and Relinking" collection presentation (Van Abbemuseum foundations page).
- Ammodo: production partner since 2012 for the Positions exhibition series; no amount given (Van Abbemuseum). Ammodo's own statement says Inphykem Foundation is the source of most of Ammodo's funding and was formerly the sole shareholder of the insurer Optas N.V., sold to Aegon N.V. in 2007 (Ammodo).
- Other funds named: Cultural Participation Fund, Fonds 21, Turing Foundation, Cultuurfonds, Rembrandt Society, VSBfonds and Zabawas, all without amounts (Van Abbemuseum).
- Promotors Van Abbemuseum Foundation: brings together companies and private individuals who give financial support; the pages read name none of them. L'Internationale, a confederation of seven European art institutions, is also listed.
- No reporting found of accounts or annual reports on the pages read, or of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.
- History: the founder was a cigar businessman (Wikipedia); no page shows that the family business funds the museum today.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum found in art media. The pieces found concern political pressure on it and its director's role in another dispute.
1. Kunstkritikk: Mariann Enge, "The Van Abbemuseum under political pressure" (24 October 2011)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/the-van-abbemuseum-under-political-pressure/
- Reports that Labour Party representative Arnold Raaijmakers backed a plan to raise paying visitors from 51,750 to 170,000 and raise self-generated income by 900,000 euros, cutting public funding by the same amount. Cuts of 200,000 euros were already approved.
- Esche disputed the plan and asked residents and supporters abroad to speak up. Criticises the local politicians, not the museum.
2. Hyperallergic: Claire Voon, "Three Resign from International Museum Committee Board, Demand New Leadership" (12 November 2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/252613/three-resign-from-international-museum-committee-board-demand-new-leadership/
- Esche, Vasif Kortun and Abdellah Karroum left the board of CIMAM after its president Bartomeu Marí cancelled a MACBA exhibition over a sculpture by Ines Doujak.
- Does not criticise the Van Abbemuseum; it records Esche's own stand.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The only source is Ammodo's own statement on its website about the programme Zwarte Zwanen (Omroep Max, NPO 2, episode of 23 April 2025). Ammodo writes that it does "not recognise ourselves in this highly one-sided representation of the facts". The statement says Inphykem made a one-off contribution of 500 million euros in 2010 for pension improvements for Optas policyholders. No independent reporting on the programme was found.
- Connection to the museum: Ammodo has been a production partner of the Van Abbemuseum since 2012.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum comes from the Eindhoven municipality, national funds and foundations, and a charity lottery that gives 250,000 euros a year. The weak points are the lack of published accounts and amounts, unnamed Promotors members, and Ammodo's statement about the origin of its capital, which has no independent source. No 2021 climate and ethics manifesto was found on the pages read.
