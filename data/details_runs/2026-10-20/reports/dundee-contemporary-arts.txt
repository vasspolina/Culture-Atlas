DUNDEE CONTEMPORARY ARTS: Dundee, UK
ID: dundee-contemporary-arts
Website: https://www.dca.org.uk/

OVERVIEW
- Registered charity (Scottish Charity No. SC026631) at 152 Nethergate, Dundee, with two gallery spaces, two cinema screens, a print studio and a learning programme.
- Dundee Printmakers Workshop initiated the project, which became a partnership between Dundee City Council, the University of Dundee and a new company, Dundee Contemporary Arts. It opened on 19 March 1999 (Wikipedia).
- The project was hoped to replace the part-time Steps Theatre, Dundee's only arthouse cinema, which closed when DCA opened (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Council bought a semi-derelict garage at 152 Nethergate in March 1995. Richard Murphy Architects won the international design competition in July 1996. The final construction cost was £9 million (Wikipedia).
- Director Beth Bate; Steve Grimmond was appointed Chair during 2024-25 (annual report).
- Art Fund Museum of the Year shortlist in 2024 (annual report 2024-25).
- Centre open Sunday to Thursday 10:00 to 23:00, Friday and Saturday 10:00 to midnight; galleries open Tuesday to Sunday from 10:00.

FUNDING
Dundee Contemporary Arts: core funding from Creative Scotland and Dundee City Council, plus trusts and foundations
- DCA says it is "grateful to receive core funding from Creative Scotland and Dundee City Council" (DCA). The 2024-25 annual report says Creative Scotland gave good news in January 2025: a significant increase in funding from 2025 to 2028.
- Dundee City Council's 2025-26 budget cut its grant to DCA by 9.9 percent (The Third Force News, Niall Christie, "Council cuts grant to charity arts venue by 10%", 28 February 2025). DCA said any cut to its budget has a serious impact and that it will need to make some changes to its programme.
- The annual report thanks Creative Scotland and Scottish Government, Dundee City Council, and the local supporters Northwood Charitable Trust and Tay Charitable Trust.
- Learning activity was supported by the Alexander Moncur Trust, Corra Foundation, Engage on behalf of the Max Reinhardt Charitable Trust, Gordon Fraser Charitable Trust, Leng Charitable Trust, Perinatal and Infant Mental Health Fund and the R J Larg Family Trust.
- The 2024-25 supporters list names Ampersand Foundation, Art Fund, British Council, Freelands Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, Jerwood Foundation, Mondriaan Foundation, Paul Hamlyn Foundation, the Kingdom of the Netherlands, Film Hub Scotland, Inspiring Scotland, Cycling Scotland, Diversity Art Forum, the Mila Charitable Organisation, and the companies 71 Brewing, Fisher & Donaldson and Ninja Kiwi. Fifteen named individual Friends are also listed, and some wish to remain anonymous.
- A commercial subsidiary, 152 Trading, raises income through retail, events and hires, and returns it to DCA.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind DCA is public funding plus charitable support: Creative Scotland and Dundee City Council as core funders, with arts trusts and foundations, Art Fund, three companies (71 Brewing, Fisher & Donaldson, Ninja Kiwi) and 15 named Friends. Creative Scotland announced a significant increase from 2025 while the council cut its grant by 9.9 percent in 2025-26.
