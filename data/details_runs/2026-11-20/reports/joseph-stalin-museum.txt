JOSEPH STALIN MUSEUM: Gori, Georgia
ID: joseph-stalin-museum
Website: https://www.stalinmuseum.ge (checked 2026-11-20; serves gambling spam)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of the life of Joseph Stalin in Gori, the town where he was born in 1878, an hour west of Tbilisi (RBTH); it stands in Stalin Park on Stalin Avenue (Irish Times).
- The house where Stalin was born became a memorial museum in 1937, during his lifetime; the main complex opened in 1957, four years after his death (Civil.ge, Irish Times).
- The main building is by the Georgian architect Archil Kurdiani, in the Stalinist style, with a red-carpeted marble staircase rising to a statue of Stalin (museumstudiesabroad.org, Irish Times).
- The complex holds the birth house under a columned canopy, Stalin's personal railway carriage and six halls covering his life, gifts and personal effects, with his death mask displayed alone in a red marble hall (museumstudiesabroad.org).
- A room on Soviet repression under the main staircase was added in 2010 (VOA).
- About 162,000 people visited in 2018, a figure given by Gori's tourist centre that SBS could not check (SBS, from The New York Times).
- Visiting: labels are in Georgian and Russian, with English for the main exhibits; English-speaking guides cost extra, and the railway carriage needs an extra ticket (museumstudiesabroad.org).

FUNDING
Joseph Stalin Museum: public: the Georgian state, through the Ministry of Culture
- It is a legal entity of public law, the I.B. Stalin State Museum, founded under Government resolution N697 of 25 December 2014 as successor to the body created by presidential decree N937 of 14 November 2005. The culture minister appoints and dismisses its director and the ministry exercises state control over its finances (statute, Legislative Herald of Georgia, 2015).
- The statute lists its funding sources as grants and donations, budget allocations, state orders, contract work and ticket sales (Legislative Herald of Georgia).
- The Government of Georgia approved its 2016 budget, staff list and wage fund (Legislative Herald of Georgia, 2016).
- In January 2025 the museum put the restoration of Stalin's birth house out to tender through the State Procurement Agency at GEL 109,500, about USD 39,000: roof, floor, wiring, doors, windows and memorial plaques, to be done in 11 days (UNN, citing Mtavari TV).

Red flags: Plans since 2012 to refocus the museum on Stalin's crimes, never carried out
- On 9 April 2012 culture minister Nikoloz Rurua said the museum would be renamed the Museum of Stalinism and refocused on Soviet repression, calling it "simply incompatible with our present day" (Civil.ge, The Moscow Times).
- In August 2013, under the next government, Zurab Karumidze of the government office said the museum would become an Anti-Stalinism Museum and the Stalin statue removed from Gori's main square in 2010 would be placed there (DFWatch).
- In 2019 a guided tour still made no reference to the gulag or the Great Terror (SBS, from The New York Times). In 2024 VOA wrote that the museum "appears to have changed little over the past seven decades", and The Irish Times described it as presenting "an airbrushed version" of Stalin's life.

BOTTOM LINE
State money from the Georgian government, with no private funder on record. The concern is content, not money: a state budget keeps a Soviet shrine to Stalin largely as it was built.
