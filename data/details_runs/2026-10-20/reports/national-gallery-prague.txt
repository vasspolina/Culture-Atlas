NATIONAL GALLERY PRAGUE: Prague, Czechia
ID: national-gallery-prague
Website: https://www.ngprague.cz/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Czechia's national art museum, spread over several Prague buildings; the largest is the Trade Fair Palace at Dukelských hrdinů 47, Prague 7, which holds the modern and contemporary collection.
- Founded on 5 February 1796 by the Society of Patriotic Friends of the Arts, a group of Bohemian aristocrats and intellectuals, as the Picture Gallery; it became the central collection of Czechoslovakia in 1918 (Wikipedia).
- The Trade Fair Palace is a functionalist building begun in 1925; other sites are the Sternberg, Schwarzenberg and Salm Palaces, the Convent of St Agnes, the Waldstein Riding School and the Kinsky Palace, which is closed (Wikipedia, gallery site).
- The gallery also runs the Czech and Slovak Pavilion in Venice and chateau sites in Karviná-Fryštát and Telč.
- Most buildings open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00. Admission to permanent exhibitions is 250 CZK basic and 140 CZK discounted at the Trade Fair Palace; visitors under 16 enter free. Komerční banka card holders get 20 percent off.

FUNDING
National Gallery Prague: mainly public: the Czech Ministry of Culture, with a bank as general partner
- The Ministry of Culture, the gallery's founder, contributed 387.9 million CZK to running costs in 2024, 3.5 percent more than in 2023, plus 46 million CZK in subsidies, 30.4 million of it for capital spending (annual report 2024).
- Supporting contributions in 2024 totalled 9,873,382 CZK: 7,358,250 from companies, 2,300,000 from foundations and 76,603 from private donors (annual report 2024).
- Komerční banka has been general partner since 2014 and gave 6,000,000 CZK in 2024 (6,500,000 in 2021). Other 2024 donors: Nadace ČEZ 1,400,000; J&T NOBLESSE OBLIGE 900,000; MediaRey 450,000; Hodinářství Bechyně 200,000; Bohemian Heritage Fund 200,000.
- In December 2024 J&T Banka donated its Magnus Art collection, more than 30 works or series by 28 Jindřich Chalupecký Award winners, valued at 17,860,000 CZK (gallery news release).
- In 2021 NET4GAS, s.r.o. and the Robert H. N. Ho Family Foundation also supported exhibitions (annual report 2021).
- The 2025 annual report was not on the gallery's reports page when checked.

Red flags: public protest against the director, 2024 to 2026
- On 13 June 2024 more than 90 artists, among them Milan Knížák, David Černý and Josef Pleskot, signed a public call demanding an analysis and restructuring of the gallery and criticising director Alicja Knastová (Seznam Zprávy, ČTK).
- According to Deník N, 23 members of the gallery's advisory boards resigned in protest at her actions (Seznam Zprávy; confirmed by ČT24, 15 June 2024).
- Knastová replied that an expert jury, not she, chose the Venice pavilion project from more than 20 proposals, and she called the dispute partly generational (ČT24).
- On 19 March 2026 Culture Minister Oto Klempíř dismissed her, saying the institution "urgently needs a fresh wind" (Seznam Zprávy, my translation). Radio Prague (20 March 2026) reported that no official reason was given. Art historians Marie Klimešová and Ladislav Kesner called the move pretextual or arbitrary, and the architect Josef Pleskot said it shocked him (Seznam Zprávy).

Overall: No criticism in dedicated art media found; the critique is in Czech general news. It targets management, not funding.

2. Archiweb (ČTK, 20 March 2026): Tereza Stejskalová of tranzit.cz, "The problem of NGP is not Knastová, but long-term underfunding" (2026)
URL: https://www.archiweb.cz/en/n/home/problemem-ngp-neni-knastova-ale-dlouhodobe-podfinancovani-uvedla-odbornice
- Argues the problem is underfunding and a contributory-organisation status tied too closely to the Ministry, not personnel.
- Calls it unacceptable that a minister can dismiss a director without a clear vision.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public, with a bank as general partner for ten years and named foundations and companies on top; nothing in the sources read ties a funder to a flagged industry. The weak points are the 2024 revolt of artists and advisers against the director and the political handling of her dismissal in 2026. Watch the director tender and whether the 2025 donor list is published.
