NORTH HAMGYONG PROVINCE E-LIBRARY: Chongjin, North Korea
ID: north-hamgyong-province-e-library
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Two-floor provincial library in the centre of Chongjin, very close to the Grand Monument statues, opened in 2012 (Koryo Tours). Wikipedia gives its Korean name as 함경북도도서관 (North Hamgyong Provincial Library, my translation).
- The ground floor holds rooms of computers loaded with science and technical manuals, language lessons and vocational courses, where lectures run; upstairs are book rooms, reading areas and further lecture rooms (Koryo Tours).
- Koryo Tours says an old index-card catalogue lists books in English, Chinese, Japanese and Russian, and that the library is connected to the national intranet, which lets students contact other educational institutes, study houses and libraries.
- In June 2013 a guide, translating the head librarian, told DW's reporter the library had 301 computers and opened every day at 10 a.m.; the reporter found it half full at 9 a.m. and saw Dell machines running Windows XP and Microsoft Office, with an Internet Explorer icon on the desktop.
- Koryo Tours lists it as a tour stop where visitors can look through the card index and try a computer.

FUNDING
North Hamgyong Province E-Library (Chongjin): state-run: a provincial library of the North Korean state
- Staff: South Korea's Ministry of Unification, in its 2022 directory of North Korean institutions, lists Jo Sung Gwang (조승광) as director of the North Hamgyong Provincial Library and Kim Un Chol (김은철) as deputy director, among the provincial libraries grouped under the Central Library (my romanisations).
- Access: DW's guide said every person in Chongjin can use the computers.
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and for science and technology at 105.3 percent.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the library.

Not related: the North Hamgyong Provincial Revolutionary History Museum, a separate state museum on the other side of the statues (Koryo Tours).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art criticism. One news report covers the library: a 2013 DW account of a guided visit.
1. DW: Benjamin Mack, "Behind the curtain" (2013)
URL: https://www.dw.com/en/a-rare-glimpse-into-north-korea/a-16977796
- Reports the 301-computer figure, the Dell machines and the desktop Internet Explorer icon; the guide said trying the browser was not possible and moved the group on.
- Quotes Amnesty International researcher Rajiv Narayan on North Korean tourism in general: the reasoning is financial and propaganda, and visitors see only what the authorities want. He does not mention the library.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The same DW piece quotes Greg Scarlatoiu of the Committee for Human Rights in North Korea saying he had seen no sign of improving human rights, and cites Human Rights Watch's 2013 World Report, which called arbitrary arrest, detention, lack of due process and the torture and ill-treatment of detainees serious and pervasive problems.
- The library is a facility of the state these assessments concern.

BOTTOM LINE
The library is paid for by the North Korean state. The weak point is the US blocking order on that state; who supplied the Dell computers DW saw in 2013 is worth watching.
