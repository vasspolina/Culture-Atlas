MAMBO: Bologna, Italy
ID: mambo-bologna
Website: https://www.museibologna.it/artemoderna/

OVERVIEW
- Municipal museum of modern and contemporary art at Via Don Minzoni 14, Bologna, part of the city's civic museums (Bologna Welcome).
- Opened on 5 May 2007 in the former municipal bakery, built in 1915 under mayor Francesco Zanardi; a redevelopment designed by Aldo Rossi turned it into the museum (Finestre sull'Arte, 6 May 2020).
- It grew out of Bologna's Galleria d'Arte Moderna, founded in 1925, and became a separate entity in the 1990s; Wikipedia says it was rebranded "MAMbo" and moved into the former bakery.
- Since 2012 it has hosted the Museo Morandi, with more than 250 works by Giorgio Morandi; its performance-art section documents the International Performance Weeks of 1977 to 1982 (Bologna Welcome; Wikipedia).
- Since 1 January 2013 it belongs to the Istituzione Bologna Musei, which merged earlier city museum bodies and runs 13 museums (Artribune, 15 September 2013).
- Lorenzo Balbi became artistic director of MAMbo and head of the Modern and Contemporary Art area of Istituzione Bologna Musei on 3 July 2017; he was reappointed after a public selection announced by the Municipality of Bologna on 22 February 2022, with a contract to 30 September 2026 (Finestre sull'Arte, 13 April 2022). The area also includes Villa delle Rose, Museo Morandi, Casa Morandi, the Museo per la Memoria di Ustica and the Sandra Natali Artist Residence.
- Open Tuesday 14:00 to 18:30, Wednesday and Friday 9:30 to 18:30, Thursday 9:30 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 19:00; closed Monday. Full ticket 6 euros, reduced 4 euros, including the Museo Morandi; free for people with disabilities and companions and for holders of the Bologna Welcome Card and Card Cultura. There are restrooms on every floor, and the permanent collection can be explored by touch with a museum educator booked in advance (Bologna Welcome).

FUNDING
MAMbo: mixed: the City of Bologna's transfer to Istituzione Bologna Musei, slightly under half of its revenue, plus own revenue
- In January 2020 Balbi said slightly under half of the revenue of Istituzione Bologna Musei, excluding staff costs, came from the City of Bologna's annual transfer. The rest came from tickets, space rentals, sponsorships, public and private grants and other own income. He aimed to raise own revenue to 60 percent in 2020 while keeping the municipal transfer unchanged (Artribune, Santa Nastro, 12 January 2020).
- In 2013 Artribune gave the institution's operating costs as about 8 million euros, with 110 staff and 13 museums (Artribune, 15 September 2013).
- The 2020 "Nuovo Forno del Pane" studio project for artists was a collaboration between Istituzione Bologna Musei, the city's culture department and Balbi, with the Accademia di Belle Arti and the Università di Bologna as partners (Finestre sull'Arte, 6 May 2020).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism; the one documented dispute was political and concerned the 2013 reappointment of the director.
1. Artribune: Redazione, "Ecco come è successo che Gianfranco Maraniello è di nuovo direttore del Mambo. Anzi di tutti i Musei Bolognesi: a Vittorio Sgarbi piacendo. Storia travagliata di un rinnovo" (15 September 2013)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/tribnews/2013/09/ecco-come-e-successo-che-gianfranco-maraniello-e-di-nuovo-direttore-del-mambo-anzi-di-tutti-i-musei-bolognesi-a-vittorio-sgarbi-piacendo-storia-travagliata-di-un-rinnovo/
- The city's personnel chief ruled that Maraniello could not be appointed directly and that an open competition was required; the Lega Nord called the process rigged and appointed Vittorio Sgarbi as its expert to sit in on the interviews of the nine shortlisted candidates (35 applied).
- After the interviews Sgarbi told reporters that Maraniello was by far the most qualified candidate.
- Criticises the appointment process, not the museum's programme or funding.

BOTTOM LINE
MAMbo is a city museum. In January 2020 its director said the City of Bologna's annual transfer made up slightly under half of the revenue of Istituzione Bologna Musei, excluding staff costs, and the rest came from tickets, rentals, sponsorships and grants.
