MSU ZAGREB (MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART): Zagreb, Croatia
ID: msu-zagreb
Website: http://www.msu.hr/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art at Avenija Dubrovnik 17 in Novi Zagreb (e-flux directory, Wikipedia).
- It traces its origins to the City Gallery of Contemporary Art, established on 21 December 1954 in the Kulmer palace in Gornji grad, where lack of space meant it never had a permanent display (Croatian Wikipedia).
- Building: a decision to build was made in 1998 and architect Igor Franić won the design competition; the cornerstone was laid in 2003 and the museum opened on 11 December 2009 (Croatian Wikipedia, Wikipedia).
- The building covers about 14,600 m2, with about 3,500 m2 for the permanent display and about 1,500 m2 for temporary exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- On site: a library, multimedia hall, bookshop, café and restaurant. The collection holds more than 12,000 works, including the Vjenceslav Richter collection and photo archives such as Toše Dabac (Croatian Wikipedia).
- Vesna Meštrić is director; she succeeded Zdenka Badovinac, temporarily in late 2023 and officially in 2024 (Wikipedia).
- In 2025 the museum held 28 exhibitions in the Novi Zagreb building and the Richter Collection; its "Invitation" exhibition has been shown at MoMA in New York since March 2025 and runs until February 2028 (Novi list/Hina, 2 January 2026).
- Visiting: the e-flux directory gives Tuesday to Friday 11:00 to 19:00 and Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00.

FUNDING
MSU Zagreb: mainly public: City of Zagreb and Ministry of Culture
- The building cost 450 million kuna, up from a budget of about 200 million, split equally between the Ministry of Culture and the City of Zagreb (Wikipedia). In 2009 the City and the Ministry had financed the museum together (tportal).
- Projected annual running costs were about 10 million kuna; the City and the Ministry were still negotiating how to divide founder rights (tportal, M. Pavliša, 29 December 2009).
- The museum takes part in L'Internationale, an alliance of European institutions supported by the EU's Creative Europe programme (Novi list/Hina, 2 January 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum. Coverage of the 2009 opening criticised how the opening was handled.
1. tportal: M. Pavliša, "Dok je infrastrukture, bit će i kulture" (29 December 2009)
URL: https://www.tportal.hr/kultura/clanak/dok-je-infrastrukture-bit-ce-i-kulture-20091228
- The museum opened after years of disputes between the City and the Ministry over the display, the choice of curator, delays and responsibility.
- The opening focused on ceremony, and critics and commentators largely did not review the exhibition; artists who did not receive invitations complained, and citizens waited for hours in the rain to be among the first visitors.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: the City of Zagreb and the Ministry of Culture built it together for 450 million kuna, and the museum takes part in a Creative Europe supported alliance, L'Internationale.
