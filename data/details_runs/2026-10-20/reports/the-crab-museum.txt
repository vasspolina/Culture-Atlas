THE CRAB MUSEUM: Margate, UK
ID: the-crab-museum
Website: https://www.crabmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Small museum on the first floor above the Pie Factory Gallery at 9 Broad Street, Margate Old Town, near Turner Contemporary and Dreamland (museum site; Museums Association).
- Founded by Chase Coley and brothers Bertie and Ned Suesat-Williams, none with a crab or museum background (Wikipedia); Wikipedia gives 2021 as the opening year and the Museums Association gives October 2022 for the first full season. The three are the directors of Crab Museum CIC, incorporated on 26 September 2019 (Companies House).
- Named for its subject: it bills itself as "Europe's first and only museum dedicated to the world of the decapod" (museum site).
- Building: a first-floor space in a former pie factory (Museums Association).
- On site: absurdist displays and cartoons by the artist Ed Stockham, including a diorama of three crab species in costumes from the 1926 general strike (Wikipedia). The museum links crabs to capitalism, colonialism and climate change (New Statesman).
- Visiting: open Friday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, last entry 4:30pm; free admission, and the museum says it will never charge an entry fee. Accessibility details are on a separate page of the museum site, which was not read.

FUNDING
The Crab Museum: self-funded and donation-funded: the directors' savings, visitor donations and shop sales
- Legal form: a community interest company limited by guarantee, without share capital; status active, latest accounts made up to 30 September 2025 (Companies House). The company page does not call it a cooperative. The directors are Chase John Coley, Rachada Edmund Suesat-Williams and Satakalet Bertie Suesat-Williams, all appointed on 26 September 2019.
- Start-up money came from the three directors' savings, now depleted; each spent a year earning money to put in (Museums Association, 2023).
- Running money: visitor donations and gift-shop sales (Museums Association). The museum site says it relies on donations and "will never sell our souls to big corporations".
- Wikipedia says funding also came from crowdfunding and an initial loan; the pages read give no amounts or dates for either.
- Awards: a 2023 Digital Culture Award from Arts Council England for its social media (Wikipedia). No reporting found of any Arts Council England grant or other public grant.
- No reporting found of published accounts figures beyond the Companies House filing dates.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

1. New Statesman: Hugh Smiley, "'Crabs taught me to be anti-capitalist': how a Margate museum is on a mission to change politics" (2023)
URL: https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/nature/2023/01/crab-museum-anti-capitalist-margate
- A sympathetic profile, not a critical review: it calls the museum "possibly the most political, anti-capitalist and pro-crab space in the country".
- Offers no outside rebuttal; the one critical note is the founders' own account that a local Conservative MP spent "about 45 seconds" inside, which the piece does not verify.
- Does not discuss funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the directors' savings, visitor donations and shop sales, and no flagged funder was found; the museum states it will not sell its "soul" to big corporations. The weak points are that no accounts figures were found, the loan and crowdfunding behind the start-up are unquantified, and the legal form is a community interest company, not a cooperative. Worth watching: the roof-garden expansion that Wikipedia says was planned for 2025, and how it is paid for.
