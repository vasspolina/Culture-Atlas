MAC MONTRÉAL – Montréal, Canada
Website: https://macm.org (ok, checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Musée d'art contemporain de Montréal, a Quebec government museum at Place des Arts. Its building is closed for an architectural transformation targeted for 2028; the site lists Place Ville Marie as its temporary address, with a banner saying no exhibitions are currently presented there (stated by the space, January 2024 release).

FUNDING
MAC Montréal: mainly public, with a private foundation campaign.
- Operating: existing record cites C$12.5M in Quebec grants for 2024-25 from the annual report. The 2024-25 annual report PDF could not be opened in this run, so that figure was not re-checked.
- Transformation project: federal government C$50M (including C$18.85M in 2016) and Quebec C$55M (including C$30M in 2016); the MAC contributes C$11.5M (Government of Canada, 16 June 2023).
- Fondation du MAC campaign "L'art de transformer", launched April 2024, goal C$20M, co-chaired by Claudine and Stephen Bronfman. Donors of C$350,000-500,000 each announced 26 August 2025: BMO Financial Group, Carole Gagnon and Michel Ringuet, the Morris and Rosalind Goodman Family Foundation with the Stephen and Lillian Vineberg Family Foundation, RBC Foundation, and Sanimax Inc. (stated by the space).
- Other donors in the existing record (Banque Nationale as lead, Power Corporation, AtkinsRéalis, Scotiabank, Desjardins) come from the 2024-25 report; not re-checked this run.

Red flags: Donor with corruption conviction
- On 18 December 2019 SNC-Lavalin Construction (now AtkinsRéalis) pleaded guilty to one count of fraud over its Libya dealings and was fined C$280M over five years with three years' probation; prosecutors had alleged about C$47.7M in bribes to Libyan officials, 2001-2011 (RCI; reported by an independent outlet). AtkinsRéalis is named as a donor in the existing record.

BOTTOM LINE
The MAC is a publicly funded museum with documented government capital and named private donors. The one item worth watching is AtkinsRéalis, a donor whose subsidiary pleaded guilty to fraud in 2019; its listing could not be re-checked against the 2024-25 report in this run.
