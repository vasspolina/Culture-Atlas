MAXXI: Rome, Italy
ID: maxxi
Website: https://www.maxxi.art (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of contemporary art and architecture in Rome's Flaminio neighbourhood, at Via Guido Reni 4A; it has two parts, MAXXI Art and MAXXI Architecture (Wikipedia, museum website).
- Created by the Italian Ministry of Culture as a foundation. Zaha Hadid won the international design competition in 1998; the building stands on the site of the former Caserma Montello barracks and incorporates parts of it (Wikipedia).
- Construction took more than ten years; it opened in 2010 and won that year's Stirling Prize (Wikipedia). A second site, MAXXI L'Aquila in the eighteenth-century Palazzo Ardinghelli, opened on 30 October 2020 (Wikipedia).
- Collection: more than 400 artworks and a photography collection of about 2,400 works begun in 2003 (museum website).
- Maria Emanuela Bruni became president on 28 March 2025, after Alessandro Giuli left to become Minister of Culture (Fondazione MAXXI accounts 2025).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 11 am to 7 pm, closed Monday. Full ticket €15 at the desk, €14 online; under-18s free; free entry for disability card holders and companions (museum website).

FUNDING
MAXXI: mainly public: the Ministry of Culture, with a regional grant, a utility company and corporate sponsors
- Total production value in 2025 was €21,823,228 and the net result €3,267 (Fondazione MAXXI accounts 2025).
- Operating grants in 2025 totalled €15,141,122: Ministry of Culture €14,003,860 across four budget lines (€7,028,195, €3,228,396, €1,947,269 and €1,800,000 for L'Aquila); Enel €600,000; Regione Lazio €350,000; ALES €187,262 (accounts 2025).
- Enel is a founding promoter member and Regione Lazio a promoter member; each designated a board member in 2026 (accounts 2025).
- Sponsorship exceeded €1 million in 2025 (€1,166,639 in 2024). KME backs the Gallery 1 architecture shows from 2024 to 2028, and Bulgari continues a partnership to 2028 (accounts 2025).
- Ticket income at the Rome site was €1,250,401 from 148,133 tickets in 2025, against €1,807,255 from 204,548 in 2024 (accounts 2025).
- Eni: the accounts list "Progetto ENI" at €48,750 in 2025, for cataloguing Eni's archives through the museum's architecture archive centre under a multi-year agreement begun after the 2012 exhibition Energy. A November 2025 event at Eni's Gazometro in Rome was a MAXXI and Eni collaboration.
- No Leonardo or Med-Or entry was found in the 2025 accounts text.

Red flags: fossil fuels
- Eni, an oil and gas company, pays €48,750 for archive work and collaborated on the November 2025 Gazometro event (accounts 2025).

Red flags: boycott over Eni, Leonardo and Gaza
- 27 October 2025: artists Tania Bruguera, Phil Collins, Siniša Mitrović, Alessandra Saviotti, Gemma Medina and Dora Garcia withdrew from "1+1: The Relational Years" and published a letter. They demand that MAXXI publicly denounce Israel's actions in Gaza as genocide and end ties with companies they call complicit, naming Eni and Leonardo S.p.A. (Hyperallergic, Nero Editions). Curator Nicolas Bourriaud said only Collins, Garcia and Bruguera were in his show.
- The letter says MAXXI has a "history of partnerships" with Leonardo and also names Med-Or, e-GEOS and Telespazio without specifying the collaborations (Hyperallergic).
- 17 May 2025: protests followed the opening of "Mediterranea", produced by the Fondazione MAXXI and the Med-Or Italian Foundation, described as a Leonardo offshoot (Artribune). In May 2024 pro-Palestinian activists occupied the Rome building (Artribune).
- September 2025: artist Sara Leghissa staged "MAXXI Boycott" outside MAXXI L'Aquila during a festival it had invited her to (Artribune).
- No MAXXI response is reported in Hyperallergic, Nero Editions or Artribune.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from activists and artists and is reported rather than argued by critics.
1. Hyperallergic: Isa Farfan, "Artists Call to Boycott Maxxi Museum Over Alleged Israel Ties" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/artists-call-to-boycott-maxxi-museum-over-alleged-israel-ties
- Reports the boycott letter and its demands; notes MAXXI's Eni collaboration and that Israel awarded gas licences to Eni and others (per Reuters).
- Names Bourriaud; MAXXI had not responded when published. Reports that it is unclear whether the artists' works were removed.
2. Artribune: Valentina Muzi, on Sara Leghissa's "MAXXI Boycott" (2025)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/?p=1182113
- Reports the poster text accusing MAXXI and cultural institutions of complicity in genocide; recounts the Mediterranea protests and the 2024 occupation. Does not mention Eni.
3. Nero Editions: unsigned, "Artists Boycott MAXXI" (2025)
URL: https://www.neroeditions.com/artists-pull-out/
- The artists' own statement, not independent reporting: says MAXXI accepted funding from and worked with firms complicit in Gaza; "neutrality is complicity".

BOTTOM LINE
MAXXI's money is overwhelmingly Italian public money, with a small oil-and-gas link: Eni at €48,750 and a joint event. The weak points are that link and the 2025 boycott, whose Leonardo claim is not backed by the accounts read. Worth watching: Eni and Med-Or collaborations and any MAXXI reply to the boycott.
