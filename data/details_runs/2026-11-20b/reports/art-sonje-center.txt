ART SONJE CENTER (아트선재센터): Seoul, South Korea
ID: art-sonje-center
Website: https://artsonje.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre at 87 Yulgok-ro 3-gil, Jongno-gu, east of Gwanghwamun Gate, near Anguk station (centre website). Wikipedia places it in Samcheong-dong.
- Opened in 1998, after the "Sprout" (싹) exhibitions of 1995 in a hanok and a Western-style house on the site. Founder and chair Heeja K. Chung (정희자) set it up to remember her late son, Kim Son-je, after founding the Sonje Museum of Art in Gyeongju in 1991 (centre website).
- Building: three floors and a basement by the architect Kim Jong-sung (김종성), on a corner once occupied by a hanok; galleries of 219, 456 and 466 square metres, a 238-seat basement Art Hall, an inner garden and a rooftop garden (centre website).
- Kim Sunjung (김선정) is artistic director; she and curator Lee Yongwoo (이용우) curated "Spectrosynthesis Seoul" (20 March to 28 June 2026), a queer art show with about 70 artists (Newsis, 2 January 2026).
- Four shows opened on 27 October 2026, including Meiro Koizumi's "The Fire, an Archive" and Haneyl Choi's (최하늘) twelve sculptures (Financial News).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00; closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok. All-exhibition ticket 10,000 won; free for children 8 and under, visitors with disabilities and ICOM, CIMAM and SCM cardholders (centre website).

FUNDING
Art Sonje Center: private non-profit, founded by the Chung family as an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's arts work, with corporate partners and co-organisers
- The centre states that it was founded as an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's "social sharing practices in the arts" (centre website).
- The Daewoo Foundation's page on the centre's website says its projects "have been primarily funded by the late Daewoo Group chairperson Kim Woo Choong since 1978". Chung married Kim (김우중) in 1964; he died in 2019 (centre website).
- Chung's own business was hotels: she became president of the Seoul Hilton in 1984 and set up the Seoul-Gyeongju Hilton and the Daewoo hotels in Yanbian and Hanoi (stated by the centre).
- The centre invites corporate partnerships, sponsorships with terms set for each company, for exhibitions, research and learning (centre website).
- The Sunpride Foundation co-organised "Spectrosynthesis Seoul" in 2026 (Newsis; Art & Market).

Not related: the Wooyang Museum of Art in Gyeongju. It was the Art Sonje Museum, run by Daewoo Development, a company Kim controlled; Wooyang Industrial Development bought Daewoo Development at auction in 2012 and renamed the museum (USA Art News). Prosecutors had seized the company, then called Best Lead, as Kim's property held under other names, and sold it through KAMCO's public auction system for 92.3 billion won (Jaekyung Ilbo). On 10 July 2026 the Seoul Central District Court ordered Wooyang to return three of the 188 works Chung claimed, two Nam June Paik works from the "My Faust" series and a Sigmar Polke; Wooyang appealed on 14 July (Newsis, 18 July 2026; USA Art News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review praises a show at the centre, with one reservation.
1. Art & Market: Ian Tee, "Editor's Notes: Exhibitions in Seoul" (2026)
URL: https://www.artandmarket.net/exhibition/2026/04/23/editors-notes-exhibitions-in-seoul
- Calls "Spectrosynthesis Seoul" "unapologetically queer" and credits the curators for including many Korean artists.
- Wanted "more breathing room" for the nuances among works to come through.
- Criticises the show, not the institution.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 30 May 2006 the Seoul Central District Court sentenced Kim Woo-choong, Daewoo's founder and former chairman, to 10 years in prison, forfeiture of more than 21 trillion won and a 10 million won fine, for over 20 trillion won of accounting fraud, 9.8 trillion won of illegal financing, 19 trillion won diverted abroad and $100 million embezzled (AP via NBC News).
- Daewoo collapsed under its debts after the 1997 to 1998 Asian financial crisis (AP via NBC News).
- Kim was the main funder of the Daewoo Foundation, whose arts work the centre says it extends, and was married to its founder.

BOTTOM LINE
Private family money resting on the Daewoo fortune, with no flagged sponsor and no Red flag.
