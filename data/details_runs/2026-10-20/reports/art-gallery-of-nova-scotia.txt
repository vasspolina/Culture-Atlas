ART GALLERY OF NOVA SCOTIA: Halifax, Canada
ID: art-gallery-of-nova-scotia
Website: https://agns.ca (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Provincial art museum at 1723 Hollis Street, Halifax, described by the gallery as the largest art museum in Atlantic Canada and an agency of the Province of Nova Scotia (AGNS website).
- Origins date to 1908 as the Nova Scotia Museum of Fine Arts; renamed the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia in 1975 and opened its downtown Halifax home in 1988. In 1998 two floors of the neighbouring Provincial Building opened (AGNS website).
- Building: the former Dominion Building, opened in 1867, combined with two floors of the Provincial Building (Wikipedia).
- Collection: over 19,000 works (AGNS website). Wikipedia states that as of June 2017 the gallery held 55 works by Maud Lewis, which it calls the world's largest public collection of her art, and that it acquired 2,070 Annie Leibovitz images in June 2013. A Yarmouth branch opened in 2006, closed in January 2020 after smoke damage and reopened in July 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Building plans: a 2008 report called the downtown building "seriously inadequate"; a KPMB-led design was chosen in November 2020, and in 2022 the province put the project on hold because of rising costs (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open 10am to 5pm Monday to Wednesday and Friday to Sunday, 10am to 9pm Thursday. Admission is 15 dollars general, 13 dollars for seniors and youth 7 to 17, with an annual pass at 28 dollars; free for Nova Scotia residents under 25, children under 7 and Indigenous Peoples, and free from 5 to 9pm on Thursdays through BMO Free Access Thursday Nights. The gallery states it is wheelchair accessible, with temporary wheelchairs at the Welcome Desk (AGNS website).

FUNDING
Art Gallery of Nova Scotia: mixed public and private: provincial and federal grants, with donors and partners raising over half the operating budget
- The gallery states that it raises "more than 55% of its operating funds" through individual, corporate and foundation donors, other private sector partners, and admissions and memberships. It names the Nova Scotia Department of Communities, Culture, Tourism & Heritage, the Department of Canadian Heritage and the Canada Council for the Arts as further funders (AGNS Reports and Plans page).
- Corporate supporters: BMO Financial Group supports Free Access Thursday Nights (AGNS plan-your-visit page); the business-support page lists TD under "Current Supporters" for the TD Assistant Curatorial Fellowship and shows further supporter logos, including Scotia Investments and CKF.

Red flags: TD Bank, supporter of the curatorial fellowship, pleaded guilty to money laundering
- On 10 October 2024 TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering. Penalties totalled about 3 billion dollars: 1.8 billion to the Department of Justice, 1.3 billion to FinCEN and over 124 million from the Federal Reserve Board (NBC News, Dan Mangan of CNBC, 10 Oct 2024).
- The bank admitted failing to monitor 18.3 trillion dollars in customer activity over six years ending October 2023, and three money laundering networks moved more than 670 million dollars through its accounts (NBC News).
- TD is listed under "Current Supporters" for the TD Assistant Curatorial Fellowship on the gallery's business-support page (checked 25 October 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is of one acquisition dispute, not of the gallery's funders.
1. ArtsJournal: post by ArtsJournal2, "Once Again, A Canadian Panel Finds That An Annie Leibovitz Collection Is Not Fully 'Significant'" (30 July 2017)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=127358
- Reports that a Canadian panel ruled again that the Leibovitz collection, donated to the gallery in 2013, is not fully significant, with major tax implications for the donor.
- The gallery says it disagrees with the decision and calls the collection culturally significant to its province, its country and internationally. The post is a short report, not a critique of the gallery's management.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is a provincial agency with federal grants, and it says it raises more than 55% of its operating funds from donors, private partners, admissions and memberships, including bank sponsors. The main weak point is TD Bank, listed as a current supporter of its curatorial fellowship, which pleaded guilty to money laundering in October 2024. Worth watching are the Leibovitz acquisition dispute and the building project on hold since 2022.
