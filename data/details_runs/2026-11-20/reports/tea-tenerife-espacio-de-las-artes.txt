TEA TENERIFE ESPACIO DE LAS ARTES: Santa Cruz de Tenerife, Spain
ID: tea-tenerife-espacio-de-las-artes
Website: https://teatenerife.es/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre of the Cabildo de Tenerife in the old centre of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, on the right bank of the Barranco de Santos between the Nuestra Señora de África market and the church of La Concepción, next to the MUNA natural history and archaeology museum (TEA website).
- Opened on 31 October 2008 in a building of about 20,622 square metres designed by Herzog & de Meuron, with the Canarian architect Virgilio Gutiérrez Herreros directing the work (Spanish Wikipedia).
- A public plaza leads into the building and a public route crosses it diagonally, linking the Serrador bridge with the bank of the Barranco de Santos (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The building also holds the Centro de Fotografía Isla de Tenerife, the city's central public library, an art library and documentation centre, a cinema and the MiniTEA children's space (TEA website; Spanish Wikipedia).
- The collection counts just over 2,000 works, with deposits from the Los Bragales collection, the Ordóñez-Falcón photography collection (COFF) and the Asociación Canaria de Amigos del Arte Contemporáneo; its core is the Óscar Domínguez and Surrealism collection of about fifty pieces (TEA website).
- TEA also runs satellite spaces, Espacio TEA Candelaria and Centro TEA in La Laguna (TEA website).
- Jaume Plensa's 7-metre iron head "Carla", bought by the Cabildo, has stood outside the building since 2023 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Sergio Rubira, a professor at the Complutense University of Madrid, has been artistic director since his appointment by the board on 9 July 2024 (Diario de Avisos, 10 July 2024).
- In 2025 the building received 287,824 visitors and the exhibition rooms 140,133; the 2026 programme has ten exhibitions, a second TEA Contemporary Biennial and a permanent Óscar Domínguez room (Diario de Avisos, citing EFE, 23 January 2026).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 20:00, closed Mondays except holidays; films run Friday to Sunday at 19:00 for 4 euros a session (TEA website; 2026 price resolution).

FUNDING
TEA Tenerife: mainly public: the Cabildo de Tenerife
- Legal form: a public business entity (entidad pública empresarial) created by the Cabildo de Tenerife under article 85.2.A.c of Law 7/1985 to run the island's contemporary culture service directly, with its own legal personality (2019 annual accounts; 2026 price resolution).
- Running costs: the Cabildo gave operating subsidies of 1,882,857.20 euros in 2018 and 2,144,966.38 euros in 2019; since 2010 TEA has booked further Cabildo transfers as partner contributions, 1,400,879.34 euros in 2019 (2019 annual accounts).
- Santa Cruz city council: 150,000 euros a year under an agreement for running the library, counted as income in 2018 and 2019 (2019 annual accounts).
- Capital grants from the Cabildo in 2019: 230,000 euros to pay debt on the purchase of the Fernández-Ordóñez collection, 159,750 euros for a work of art, 77,500 euros for hanging rails and 11,955.60 euros for improvements (2019 annual accounts).
- Gifts: the Ordóñez-Falcón collection was donated by public deed in 2018, valued at 3,789,100 euros, alongside a loan agreement valued at 2,560,000 euros (2019 annual accounts).

Red flags: 2024 dispute over the choice of artistic director
- Diario de Avisos reported that the selection process was questioned by a broad part of the island's art sector from start to finish (10 July 2024).
- It reported Rubira's close ties to two of the five jury members, Tania Pardo of CA2M and Álvaro Rodríguez Fominaya of MUSAC: the jury was published on 22 May 2024, MUSAC opened "Sangre en oro", curated by Rubira, on 8 June, and the jury met on 19 and 20 June.
- Unverified: art-world sources quoted by Diario de Avisos said Rubira runs CA2M's thought programme under Pardo and has worked with her since at least 2002.
- Rubira's project, "100 acciones para TEA", was the top-rated of 11 candidates, and the board approved his appointment on 9 July 2024 (Diario de Avisos).

BOTTOM LINE
TEA is a Cabildo de Tenerife company paid for mainly by the island government, which covered more than two million euros of running costs in 2019 and buys art for it; Santa Cruz city council adds a library grant. The weak point on record is the 2024 director selection, which local press reported as contested over the jury's ties to the winner.
