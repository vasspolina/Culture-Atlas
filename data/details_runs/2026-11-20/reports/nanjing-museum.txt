NANJING MUSEUM: Nanjing, China
ID: nanjing-museum
Website: https://www.njmuseum.com (checked 2026-11-20; the page loads only a script shell, so its text could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Provincial museum of Jiangsu in Nanjing (Sixth Tone).
- Its predecessor was the preparatory office of the National Central Museum, proposed by Cai Yuanpei and others in 1933 (CCTN Design on gooood; Wikipedia).
- Xu Jingzhi won the 1935 design competition and completed the design under Liang Sicheng; the halls were finished only after 1949 (CCTN Design). Part of the collection was moved to southwest China during the Japanese invasion and ended up in the National Palace Museum in Taipei (Wikipedia).
- A second phase was approved in 2006; Cheng Taining's team won from nearly 30 schemes around August 2008, raised the Liao-style main hall by 3 metres and added more than 30,000 square metres underground (CCTN Design).
- The collection holds more than 430,000 cultural relics (China Daily, 23 December 2025).

FUNDING
Nanjing Museum: public: the Jiangsu provincial government
- The Jiangsu Provincial Department of Culture and Tourism and the Jiangsu Provincial Cultural Heritage Bureau oversee the museum as a subordinate unit; the 2026 probe found that they failed to supervise their subordinate units effectively (China Daily, 10 February 2026).

Red flags: Sale of donated Pang family paintings, exposed in 2025
- In 1959 the Pang family donated 137 paintings; 132 remain in the collection (China Daily, 10 February 2026). Sources name the collector differently: Pang Laichen, 1864 to 1949 (China Daily, 23 December 2025; Sixth Tone; ArtReview), or Pang Zenghe, called the donor and Pang Shuling's father (China Daily, 18 December 2025) and dated 1864 to 1949 (China Daily, 10 February 2026).
- After the family sued in 2024, the museum told them in July that five works had been deemed forged and "reallocated"; its lawyer, Zhang Han, argued that ownership passed on donation (China Daily, 20 December 2025).
- "Jiangnan Spring", attributed to Qiu Ying and estimated at 88 million yuan, was due for sale at China Guardian in Beijing in May 2025 and was withdrawn after Pang Shuling, the collector's great-granddaughter, reported it (ArtReview; China Daily).
- The National Cultural Heritage Administration and a Jiangsu party and government team opened investigations on 23 December 2025 (China Daily). The probe covered 12 provinces, more than 1,100 interviews, 65,000 documents and 30,255 works (China Daily, 10 February 2026).
- It found that Xu Huping, then executive vice director and also legal representative and manager of the state-owned Jiangsu Provincial Cultural Relics Store, approved the transfer of donated works to the store for sale despite national bans (China Daily, 10 February 2026). In July 1997 a custodian surnamed Zhang cut the price of "Jiangnan Spring" from 25,000 to 2,500 yuan, bought it through an intermediary for 2,250 yuan and sold it on for 120,000 yuan (Sixth Tone, 10 February 2026).
- Twenty-nine people were implicated, five of them dead, and the province ordered administrative cases against the museum (China Daily; Sixth Tone). Three paintings were recovered, one was found in a museum warehouse and "Pine Breeze and Temple" is still missing (China Daily).
- The museum apologised and pledged new donation rules, stronger internal controls and public supervision (Sixth Tone).
- On 16 September 2026 Jiangsu announced that staff of the former provincial culture department, the heritage bureau, the museum and the store, Zhang among them, had been dismissed, removed, demoted or warned, and began a campaign on the safety of state museum collections (Science and Technology Daily, from CCTV).

Red flags: 2026 bribery conviction of former director Xu Huping
- In results announced by Jiangsu authorities on 16 September 2026, Xu, executive deputy director and later director, was expelled from the Communist Party, stripped of retirement benefits, and sentenced to three years in prison and a 200,000 yuan fine for accepting bribes in return for help with construction contracts and business deals (Science and Technology Daily, from CCTV; Global Times, from Xinhua).

Red flags: 2025 whistleblower claims of relic smuggling
- Unverified: on 21 December 2025 an 80-year-old man who said he was the retired museum employee Guo Lidian, an identity The Standard did not confirm, accused Xu of organising the theft and smuggling of relics moved from the Forbidden City and of passing relics off as forgeries for cheap sale. Xu, who retired in 2008, denied involvement (The Standard, Hong Kong, 22 December 2025).

Not related: the Nanjing Municipal Museum, the city's own museum, a separate institution (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is news reporting of the 2026 probe.
1. ArtReview: unsigned news, "'Mismanagement and corruption' alleged at Nanjing Museum" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/mismanagement-and-corruption-alleged-at-nanjing-museum/
- Reports the government finding of systemic mismanagement and alleged corruption, and that the store's invoice called the painting a "Landscape Scroll Imitating Qiu Ying" without naming the buyer.
- A news report that names Xu Huping.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a Jiangsu state museum with no sponsor or flagged funder on record. Its weak point is governance: the probe and the conviction show its leadership and its supervisors failed to protect donated works. Watch the province-wide collections campaign and the search for the missing painting.
