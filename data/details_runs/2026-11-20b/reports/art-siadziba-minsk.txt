ART-SIADZIBA: Minsk, Belarus
ID: art-siadziba-minsk
Website: https://artsiadziba.by (checked 2026-11-20; down)

OVERVIEW
- Independent cultural platform and non-governmental organisation in Minsk that, through concerts, lectures and entertainment events, brought young people across the country to Belarusian language and culture (Radio Svaboda, Kalektar).
- Founded on 24 December 2011 (Radio Svaboda); Radio Svaboda names Pavel Belavus as a founder and its first director, Kalektar lists Belavus as founder with Zmitser Afanasenka and Franak Viachorka as co-founders, and Wikipedia also names Stas Babaytsau.
- The English Wikipedia entry translates the name as "Art Headquarters"; RFE/RL renders it as "Art Homestead".
- Venues: the Horizont factory building, then 28a Pushkin Avenue, then a two-room flat in central Minsk in 2012, and from 2013 6 Instrumentalny Lane (Kalektar); a new space at 4 Maskvin Lane was lost in December 2012 before it opened (Nasha Niva, 6 December 2012).
- Programme: concerts, book launches, lectures and film screenings, with musicians from the unofficial blacklist such as Lavon Volski and Zmitser Vaitsiushkevich (Nasha Niva, July and December 2012).
- Its coordinator Nikita Brovka devised the festival "Dzen vyshyvanki" («Дзень вышыванкі», my translation: Embroidered Shirt Day), first held in Minsk in October and December 2014 under Art-Siadziba and the Assembly of NGOs (Nasha Niva, 2016).
- It announced on 3 January 2022 that it was ending its activity, citing its liquidation and a coming law on liability for work in liquidated organisations (Radio Svaboda).

FUNDING
Art-Siadziba: independent non-profit in rented premises; the culture ministry declined to work with it
- Premises were rented: the planned Maskvin Lane space belonged to JSC Sukno and was leased by JSC RefOilProdukt, with Art-Siadziba as sub-tenant (Nasha Niva, 6 December 2012).
- In March 2016 Belavus said the culture ministry, through deputy minister Iryna Dryga, had declined Art-Siadziba's offer to run "Dzen vyshyvanki" and other events jointly in the Year of Culture; the government's plan gave the festival to the Belarusian Republican Youth Union and the Minsk city executive committee (Nasha Niva, 18 March 2016).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In July 2012, when the space closed for the first time, the city culture department said it does not handle decisions of this kind (Nasha Niva).
- Unverified: after the third loss of premises in December 2012, Belavus said the landlord had been summoned to the Minsk city executive committee and strongly advised to cancel the lease, and that the order came from its ideology department; Nasha Niva reports this as his account.
- Radio Svaboda reports that Art-Siadziba was put on a list of organisations to be liquidated in late July 2021, after Alexander Lukashenko had announced a purge of non-governmental organisations on 22 July 2021.
- By the end of 2021 the authorities had liquidated at least 275 NGOs; by the end of April 2024 at least 1,622 non-commercial organisations had been liquidated, according to Lawtrend (Radio Svaboda).
- Amnesty International (Aisha Jung, 22 February 2021): "The authorities are methodically destroying Belarus's spirited cultural life and its most creative members." The statement is about the country's cultural scene as a whole.
- Belavus was detained on 15 November 2021 (Nasha Niva), and on 11 May 2023 Judge Zhanna Brysina of the Minsk City Court sentenced him to 13 years for treason, leading an extremist group and other charges; the extremist charge concerned his leadership of the Belarusian Culture Council (RFE/RL).
- He was released on 16 September 2026 in a group of political prisoners freed after a US delegation came to Minsk (Nasha Niva).
- The liquidation and the founder's prosecution came from the Belarusian state, which did not fund the space.

BOTTOM LINE
An independent venue with no flagged funder, closed under the Belarusian state's liquidation of non-governmental organisations.
