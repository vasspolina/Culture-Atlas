SCOTT'S DISCOVERY HUT: Hut Point, Ross Island, Antarctica
ID: scotts-discovery-hut-hut-point
Website: https://nzaht.org/conserve/explorer-bases/scotts-hut-hut-point/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Prefabricated hut at Hut Point on Ross Island, by McMurdo Sound, about 300 m from the US McMurdo Station; New Zealand's Scott Base is on the same peninsula (Wikipedia).
- Built in February 1902 for Robert Falcon Scott's National Antarctic (Discovery) Expedition of 1901-04, a joint venture of the Royal Geographical Society and the Royal Society (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Prefabricated in Sydney of Douglas fir and Scots pine, square, with a verandah on three sides (Antarctic Heritage Trust); designed by Professor Gregory and made by James Moore for £870 14s 5d (Wikipedia).
- Too cold to live in: the expedition's 47 men stayed on the ship and used the hut for science, repairs, storage and plays (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Three later expeditions used it: Shackleton's in 1908, Scott's Terra Nova party in 1911-12 as a staging post, and Shackleton's stranded Ross Sea Party in 1915-16 (Wikipedia). Burning seal blubber on a stove built by Scott's 1910 expedition coated the interior in greasy soot (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Historic Site or Monument 18 under the Antarctic Treaty, proposed by New Zealand and the United Kingdom (Wikipedia).
- The Trust restored the building and just over 500 artefacts between 2013 and 2015 (Antarctic Heritage Trust). That closed a decade-long project with Antarctica New Zealand on the three Ross Island huts: 62 specialists from 11 countries conserved 18,202 artefacts (RNZ, 28 January 2015).
- Visiting: the Trust offers a virtual reality tour of the hut and a Minecraft model; its conservation plan for the hut is sold by email request (Antarctic Heritage Trust).

FUNDING
Scott's Discovery Hut: charity-run, with close to half its operator's income from the New Zealand government
- Operator: the Antarctic Heritage Trust (New Zealand charity CC24071, Christchurch), which runs the Ross Sea Heritage Restoration Project for the five explorer bases of Scott, Shackleton, Borchgrevink and Hillary; the Princess Royal launched the project in 2002 (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- In the year to 30 June 2025 the Trust's gross income was NZ$3,433,987: government service grants and contracts NZ$1,536,000, donations NZ$928,581, New Zealand dividends NZ$304,973, membership NZ$39,300 and commercial revenue NZ$36,254. It spent NZ$3,335,082, held investments of NZ$10.3 million and employed 14 full-time staff (Charities Register annual return).
- The year before, income was NZ$5,027,836, NZ$2,880,138 of it donations (Charities Register).
- At the September 2026 launch of its new conservation plans the Trust named the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade and Antarctica New Zealand as its government partners and the Ministry for Culture and Heritage as its core funder; chair Sir Jerry Mateparae said a tough economy was making fundraising harder (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- Key supporters linked on its partners page: Christchurch Airport, the New Zealand Defence Force, The Shackleton Whisky, Staples VR and the Tait Foundation (Antarctic Heritage Trust).
- All proceeds of a limited-edition "Scott Base" T-shirt showing the hut go to the Trust (Antarctic Heritage Trust).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the hut is clean. The weak point is its operator's reliance on state grants while private giving swings from year to year. Worth watching: whether government support holds.
