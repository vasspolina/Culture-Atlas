GFZK, GALERIE FÜR ZEITGENÖSSISCHE KUNST LEIPZIG: Leipzig, Germany
ID: gfzk-galerie-fur-zeitgenossische-kunst-leipzig
Website: https://gfzk.de/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art at Karl-Tauchnitz-Strasse 9 to 11 in Leipzig's Music Quarter, at the edge of the Johanna Park, on the grounds of the Herfurth Villa (GfZK).
- It grew from an idea of the East German art historian Klaus Werner; in November 1990 Werner and Arend Oetker, a member of the Kulturkreis der deutschen Wirtschaft im BDI, founded the Friends of the GfZK association, which still runs today (GfZK).
- The villa, built from 1892 for Hermann Credner and later bought by Edgar Herfurth, was converted by Peter Kulka from 1996 to 1998 and opened in 1998; a second building by AS-IF opened in November 2004 with more than 1,000 square metres of exhibition space (GfZK).
- The collection's core is part of the Kulturkreis collection, added to by gifts, purchases by the Friends and long-term loans; the villa holds a public library of about 30,000 volumes (German Wikipedia). The collection has over 2,000 objects by over 500 artists (Nadja Sthamer, member of the Bundestag, 2023).
- Directors: Klaus Werner, then Barbara Steiner in the 2000s; Franciska Zólyom has directed it since 2012 (GfZK). The foundation council extended her contract for five years from 1 January 2017 (Saxon ministry release, 8 February 2016).
- Autumn 2026: shows of Sara Sejin Chang (Sara van der Heide), Heidi Specker and "The Most Beautiful German Books 2026" (GfZK).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 2 pm to 7 pm, weekends and holidays from 12 to 6 pm; tickets 7 euros, reduced 5 euros, free on Wednesdays (GfZK).

FUNDING
GfZK: mixed public and private: the City of Leipzig, the Free State of Saxony and the Friends of the GfZK, in equal shares in 2016
- The Foundation Galerie für Zeitgenössische Kunst Leipzig is supported by the Friends of the GfZK, the City of Leipzig and the Free State of Saxony (SMWK), and is partly financed from the budget approved by the Saxon Landtag (GfZK imprint).
- In 2016 the city, the Saxon science and arts ministry and the Friends each paid 220,000 euros a year (Saxon ministry release, 8 February 2016).
- In 2023 the federal "KulturInvest" programme granted 1.25 million euros, half the cost of a new storage depot for the collection (Nadja Sthamer, SPD member of the Bundestag, in Leipziger Zeitung, 30 September 2023).
- Ticket income, and two artist-designed hotel apartments in a former studio building on the site, let at 120 to 150 euros a night (GfZK).
- 2025: the Saxon minority government's draft double budget for 2025/2026 cut culture funding, the GfZK included. Claudia Maicher, culture spokesperson of the Greens in the Saxon Landtag, said the Free State as co-founder had always matched the City of Leipzig's share, and that leaving upkeep to the city and private founders puts the funding model at risk (Leipziger Zeitung, 1 June 2025).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is clean: two public shares and one private share. The weak point is the state share, under pressure since the 2025/2026 budget draft; watch the next budgets of Saxony and Leipzig.
