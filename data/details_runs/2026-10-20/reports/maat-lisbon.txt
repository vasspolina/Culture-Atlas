MAAT: Lisbon, Portugal
ID: maat-lisbon
Website: https://www.maat.pt/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Art, Architecture and Technology on the River Tagus in Belém, Lisbon, with two venues: MAAT Gallery, a new building, and MAAT Central, the Tejo Power Station (Wikipedia, stated by the museum).
- Opened in October 2016. The new building opened to the public on 5 October 2016; the programme had started on 30 June 2016 in the renovated power station (Wikipedia).
- Owned and run by Fundação EDP, the foundation of the energy company EDP (Wikipedia, ECO).
- Building: designed by Amanda Levete's firm AL_A; the Central Tejo power station, built in 1908 according to the museum's presentation text (its buildings page gives the 1910s to 1940s), became MAAT Central (stated by the museum).
- Cost: roughly 20 million euros (ECO).
- On site: exhibitions, the EDP Foundation Art Collection, the Energy Heritage Collection and a riverside garden with sculptures (stated by the museum).
- Open Wednesday to Monday 10:00 to 19:00; closed Tuesdays, 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. Admission prices and accessibility details are not listed on the page opened.

FUNDING
MAAT: corporate: Fundação EDP, the foundation set up and financed by EDP
- Fundação EDP is described as a non-profit set up and financed by the company (Wikipedia, EDP Group).
- EDP's 2023 shareholder meeting proposal kept a transfer of 6.2 million euros to Fundação EDP from the 2022 results (ECO, 14 March 2023).
- EDP's largest shareholder is China Three Gorges with 22.2%, followed by BlackRock with 8.6% (Wikipedia, EDP Group, as of October 2026).
- In November 2021 Fundação EDP and Fundação de Serralves announced a plan to move MAAT's management to Serralves. It was scaled back: MAAT stays under Fundação EDP, which funds one major Serralves exhibition each year (ECO).
- No reporting found of public grants or other donors.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, EDP
- Climáximo, a climate group, painted MAAT's facade and covered its staircase in red paint on 3 December 2023 and accused EDP of being a major importer of fossil fuels for power in Portugal and of using its foundation to "lavar a imagem" (clean up its image, translation) (Diário de Notícias).
- EDP condemned the vandalism and said about 80% of its energy is renewable and it closed its Portuguese coal plant in 2021 (Diário de Notícias, EDP's statement).
- EDP's subsidiaries include EDP Gás and Naturgás (Wikipedia, EDP Group).

Red flags: EDP leadership case
- On 28 October 2024 prosecutors charged António Mexia, former executive chairman of EDP, and João Manso Neto, former president of EDP Renováveis, with active corruption of former Economy Minister Manuel Pinho, over events between 2006 and 2014. The Public Prosecutor said the state lost more than 840 million euros and asked for forfeiture of up to that sum from the defendants and two EDP Group companies (ECO, 28 October 2024; Diário de Notícias).
- ECO reports MAAT was an initiative of Mexia when he chaired EDP (ECO, 14 March 2023). No verdict is reported in the sources opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the programme; the criticism found targets the building, the funder and a planned change of management.
1. Metropolis: Francisco Moura Veiga, "Portugal's MAAT Museum a Monument to Corporate Ego" (29 May 2017)
URL: https://metropolismag.com/viewpoints/portugal-maat-museum-monument-corporate-ego/
- Compares the building to the houses Portuguese emigrants built on their return, a corporate version of that impulse, and calls it "an isolated jewel" that gives little regard to its surroundings.
- Says EDP, not bound by public procurement rules, hired Levete directly and let marketing goals outweigh the project's good intentions.
- Finds the exhibition spaces work well; criticises the building, not the programme.
2. Associação de Artistas Visuais Portugueses, statement on the Serralves and EDP memorandum (10 December 2021; reported by 24noticias)
URL: https://24noticias.sapo.pt/atualidade/artigos/associacao-de-artistas-visuais-desagradada-com-protocolo-entre-serralves-e-fundacao-edp
- Warned of a "preocupante tendência" toward a business-style mentality in cultural policy, called it alarming that Serralves and EDP Foundation spaces such as MAAT would share "a mesma programação homogénea", and feared a "terraplanagem da crítica" (translations: worrying trend, the same homogeneous programming, a levelling of criticism).
- Said questions about the MAAT collection and the EDP prizes remained unanswered after the memorandum of 5 November. Artist-association statement, not an art outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is EDP's: the museum is a corporate foundation project, with no public or outside donors found. The weak points are EDP's gas and fuel business, named in the 2023 Climáximo protest, and the corruption charges against its former chairman, which are not tied to the museum. Watch EDP's transfers to the foundation and the Serralves ties.
