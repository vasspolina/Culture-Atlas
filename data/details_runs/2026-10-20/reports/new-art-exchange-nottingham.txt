NEW ART EXCHANGE: Nottingham, United Kingdom
ID: new-art-exchange-nottingham
Website: https://www.nae.org.uk/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and cultural centre in the Hyson Green neighbourhood of Nottingham, at 39-41 Gregory Boulevard, NG7 6BE (NAE website, Wikipedia).
- Formed in 2003 by the merger of two grassroots groups: APNA Arts, a South Asian arts collective founded by Parbinder Singh, and East Midlands African Caribbean Arts, co-founded with educationalist and historian Len Garrison (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by Hawkins\Brown and officially opened on 5 September 2008, replacing the former Art Exchange building, previously known as The People's Dispensary (Wikipedia).
- Saad Eddine Said was appointed Chief Executive Officer and Artistic Director in 2021; Wikipedia says the gallery set up the "VOICE Assembly" in May 2024 as the first permanent citizen's assembly embedded in the governance and leadership framework of a major cultural institution.
- Programme: changing exhibitions, public programmes, family and youth activities, film screenings, festival days and live performance; it is executive producer of Nottingham Mela, which its site calls "the oldest Mela in the UK" (NAE website).
- Past exhibitors include John Akomfrah, Zanele Muholi and Hassan Hajjaj (NAE website). It is a Living Wage Employer.
- Open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 16:00; closed Sunday, Monday and bank holidays (NAE website).

FUNDING
New Art Exchange: mainly public: Arts Council England core funding
- Income for the year to 31 March 2025 was 1,610,538 pounds, with spending of 1,633,227 pounds and a deficit of 22,689 pounds (Charity Commission accounts, charity 1121755, via OpenCharities).
- Arts Council England: 1,268,853 pounds of unrestricted core revenue funding, about 79% of income. NAE is an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation (accounts, Wikipedia).
- Power to Change: 104,028 pounds of restricted income for Freelands, a three-year programme that the accounts say has now ended. The NAE site also lists Freelands Foundation among its funders.
- Nottingham City Council: 25,541 pounds from two UK Shared Prosperity Fund grants, a Digital Skills Capital Grant (14,312 pounds) and a Cultural, Heritage and Creative Capital Grant (11,229 pounds) (accounts).
- Nottingham Trent University: 20,000 pounds of unrestricted income for "University Reimagined" (accounts).
- Trading and tax relief: 178,604 pounds, including cafe and catering (74,288 pounds) and the HMRC Creative Industries Tax Credit (80,344 pounds) (accounts).
- Unrestricted reserves stood at 3,914,436 pounds; the chair of trustees is Samori Gambrah and the vice chair Professor Christopher O'Brien (accounts).

BOTTOM LINE
New Art Exchange is a charity funded mainly by Arts Council England, which gave 1,268,853 pounds, about 79% of its 1,610,538 pound income in the year to March 2025, with smaller grants from Nottingham City Council, Nottingham Trent University and Power to Change, and trading income.
