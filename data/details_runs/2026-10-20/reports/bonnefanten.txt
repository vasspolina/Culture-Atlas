BONNEFANTEN: Maastricht, Netherlands
ID: bonnefanten
Website: https://www.bonnefanten.nl (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Provincial art museum at Avenue Ceramique 250 in Maastricht, on the former Céramique industrial site; its rocket-shaped cupola overlooks the river Maas (Wikipedia).
- Established in 1884 as a historical and archaeological museum for the province of Limburg; since 1999 it has been exclusively an art museum (Wikipedia).
- Named after the French "bons enfants" ("good children"), the popular name of a former convent that housed it from 1951 to 1978 (Wikipedia).
- Building by the Italian architect Aldo Rossi, occupied since 1995 (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Alexander van Grevenstein from 1986, Stijn Huijts from 2012 (Wikipedia). In 2011 the collectors Jo and Marlies Eyck and the province agreed a deal that brought the Eyck collection of postwar art and the Wijlre castle and grounds into the museum.
- On site: exhibitions of old, modern and contemporary art, a museum shop, an art rental service (Kunstuitleen) and the Museumcafé Ipanema.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Monday (museum.nl listing). Ticket prices and accessibility details were not on the pages read; the museum raised its entrance prices from 1 January 2025 (museum press release).

FUNDING
Bonnefanten: mainly public: the Province of Limburg, with national, municipal, lottery and private funds
- Province of Limburg: "Without structural subsidy from the Province of Limburg there is no Bonnefanten" (2025 annual report). The province's 3 March 2026 grant letter awards an operating subsidy of up to about €6.4 million for 2026 (3.2% indexation; the scan is partly garbled) plus an extra €250,000 to match the national contribution under the national cultural basic infrastructure (BIS).
- The 2023 budget was about €9.9 million, of which €6.5 million came from the province; the museum had 53 permanent staff (Wikipedia).
- In summer 2025 the province granted a one-off €500,000 to cover higher base costs. A 2024 provincial review committee had recognised the museum's financial strain and recommended raising the operating subsidy; the province adopted a new museum vision with structural extra funding on 6 February 2026 (museum press release, 2025 annual report).
- Ministry of Education, Culture and Science: regional BIS subsidy for outreach and context programmes in 2025.
- Mondriaan Fund: contributions to acquisitions and to the Carl Cheng and Mounira Al Solh exhibitions. Ammodo: supported the Małgorzata Mirga-Tas and Al Solh exhibitions. Other 2025 funders named: Fonds 21, Niemeijer Fonds (via Fonds Peggy), Turing Foundation, Gemeente Maastricht, Gemeente Sittard-Geleen, CODE043 and Platform ACCT.
- VriendenLoterij, a lottery whose participants' stakes partly fund the museum, supports it structurally; a new five-year contract started in 2026 (2025 annual report). Participants automatically allot 40% of their stake to a good cause and can choose Bonnefanten; earmarked participants rose from 180 to 527 in 2025.
- Private: 225 Friends; the Mecenaat aan de Maas of businesses (Capra Advocaten, Centrum Art Handling, ERCO Lighting, Eshgro, De Hypotheker, Intergarde, Oostwegel Collection, Vebego) and anonymous individuals; the Museumfonds held €178,698 at the end of 2025.
- Results: about 135,000 visitors in 2025, roughly 10% below budget; operating result €35,621 negative; equity €2,517,170. Top salaries: the director earned €192,605 and the business director €166,098; supervisory board members are unpaid (2025 annual report). Board: Petra Stienen (chair), Lieve Schouterden, Bastiaan Klomp, Britte Sloothaak, Madeleine van Lennep, Maurice Lambriex, Barbara Strating.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mainly the Province of Limburg, backed by national, municipal and foundation money, and it is documented in a published annual report. The weak point is the 2025 funding squeeze (a cut exhibition, a smaller programme and visitor numbers 10% under budget). Worth watching are the new museum vision's structural funding and the director's succession: the supervisory board's 2025 report lists the succession of the director and of the board chair as a point to settle.
