PRINTED MATTER, INC.: New York, USA
ID: printed-matter-new-york
Website: https://www.printedmatter.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art bookstore and publisher of artists' books at 231 Eleventh Avenue in Chelsea, New York, its location since 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1976 in Tribeca by a loose consortium of artists, critics and publishers including Sol LeWitt, Lucy Lippard, Edit DeAk, Pat Steir, Walter Robinson and Carl Andre (Wikipedia). It began as a for-profit art space and had 501(c)(3) non-profit status by 1978 (Wikipedia).
- Ingrid Sischy was the first director in 1976; AA Bronson was executive director from 2004 to 2010 and started the New York Art Book Fair in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- The Los Angeles Art Book Fair started in 2013; Printed Matter now presents both fairs (Wikipedia). The 2023 Los Angeles fair ran on 10 to 13 August at the Geffen Contemporary at MOCA (fair site).
- W Magazine (Jim Lewis, 26 August 2014) described the store as holding more than 12,000 items, mostly at a median price of about $10, and named Phil Aarons as president of its board.
- Staff at the bookstore joined OPEIU Local 153; management recognised the union voluntarily on 18 October 2021 (Publishers Lunch, Katy Hershberger, 25 October 2021).

FUNDING
Printed Matter, Inc.: grant-funded: private foundations and public arts agencies, plus board and supporter giving
- The Los Angeles Art Book Fair 2023 site lists these funders of Printed Matter: Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, Arison Arts Foundation, Cowles Charitable Trust, Frances and Benjamin Benenson Foundation, Horace W. Goldsmith Foundation, John W. and Clara C. Higgins Foundation, Imperfect Family Foundation, JLV Charitable Fund, Milton and Sally Avery Arts Foundation, National Endowment for the Arts, New York City Department of Cultural Affairs, and New York State Council on the Arts.
- The same site names its lead supporters for the 2023 fair: Phil Aarons and Shelley Fox Aarons, Barbara Kruger, the Maurice Marciano Family Foundation and Jonas Wood. Other supporters include Blum & Poe, Regen Projects, Karma, Hyperallergic, Ace Hotel and LAXART.
- Phil Aarons: W Magazine describes him as a real estate developer, co-founder of Millennium Partners and the board's president in 2014; he sits on the MoMA PS1 board, and the 2023 fair site thanks him and Shelley Fox Aarons by name.
- Wikipedia calls Printed Matter a grant-supported non-profit and names Aarons as a board member who helped organise the fairs.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The documented criticism concerns one programming decision in 2024, a window installation on Gaza, and is reported by Hyperallergic, which is also listed as a 2023 fair supporter.
1. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "Printed Matter Window Display Divides Audiences on Israel-Palestine" (25 July 2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/936479/printed-matter-window-display-divides-audiences-on-israel-palestine/
- The window installation by ShadowComms (Shiva Addanki) ran from 13 June to 24 July 2024 and centred on a Reuters photograph of bulldozers breaking through the Gaza-Israel fence on 7 October.
- The curator and writer Rotem Rozental wrote an open letter asking for its removal, which had over 3,100 signatures at the time; some Instagram commenters objected to the photograph and others asked why Printed Matter had not joined the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel.
- Printed Matter did not respond directly to the letter. A website update on 15 July said it "does not censor the artists who take part in our programming," and it declined to comment to Hyperallergic. Faisal Saleh and Jacqueline Béjani defended the work.

WIDER CONTEXT
- DNAinfo (Maya Rajamani, "'We Failed' To Design Park That Benefited Neighbors, High Line Creator Says", 13 February 2017) reported High Line co-founder Robert Hammond telling CityLab "Ultimately, we failed", and that the park has been accused of putting gentrification into "hyperdrive." The article does not name Phil Aarons or Printed Matter.
- Connection: W Magazine (Jim Lewis, 26 August 2014) describes Aarons as one of the first advisers to the High Line and as president of the Printed Matter board, and the Los Angeles Art Book Fair 2023 site lists Phil Aarons and Shelley Fox Aarons among its lead supporters.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Printed Matter is a mix of foundation and public arts grants (Mellon, NEA, New York State and City) and supporter gifts, among them Phil Aarons, a Millennium Partners co-founder and its board president in 2014. The documented controversy is the 2024 Gaza window installation, where the organisation said it does not censor the artists in its programming.
