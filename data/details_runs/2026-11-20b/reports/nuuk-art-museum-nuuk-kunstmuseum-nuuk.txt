NUUK ART MUSEUM (NUUK KUNSTMUSEUM): Nuuk, Greenland
ID: nuuk-art-museum-nuuk-kunstmuseum-nuuk
Website: https://www.nuukkunstmuseum.com/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum of about 600 square metres at Kissarneqqortuunnguaq 5, a few minutes' walk from the centre of Nuuk; its site calls it Greenland's largest art museum (stated by the museum).
- Opened in 2005 as a gift from the businessman Svend Junge and his wife Helene Junge Pedersen to the people of Nuuk; after Svend Junge died in 2007 the municipality took over the building, fixtures and collection (museum website). Wikipedia gives the opening as 22 May 2005 and the inauguration of the exhibition wing as 21 June 2007.
- Building: an Adventist church and clinic built in 1958, designed by the Adventist Jens Johan Frederiksen, beside a minister's house of 1955; the oil company NunaOil bought the complex in the late 1990s and made it offices, and an exhibition room was added in 2007 (museum website).
- Collection: mostly works gathered by the Junges, including European artists who worked in Greenland such as Christine Deichmann, J.E.C. Rasmussen, Harald Moltke and Emanuel A. Petersen, Greenlandic artists such as Hans Lynge, Anne-Birthe Hove and Pia Arke, and carvings in soapstone, bone, wood and ivory (museum website). Wikipedia counts over 150 paintings by Petersen.
- In 2024 the museum bought the whole "Melting Barricades" project of 2004 by Inuk Silis Høegh and Asmund Havsteen-Mikkelsen, intending it for Greenland's planned National Gallery of Art, and showed it from 10 October to 30 December 2024 (museum website); in a Kunstkritikk interview (Louise Steiwer, 2025) Havsteen-Mikkelsen said Christensen first met the project as a 10-year-old pupil.
- Nivi Christensen has led the museum since 2015; from 1 December 2025 she became project director of Greenland's National Gallery of Art, which entered the Greenlandic budget in 2020 (Altinget, 12 November 2025). The museum's staff page still lists her as director.
- Programmes: an audio guide, the Art Walk through Nuuk, teaching materials for schools, a residency, and events on Culture Night, International Museum Day and the National Day (museum website).
- Visiting (winter, 28 September 2026 to 31 May 2027): closed Monday to Wednesday; Thursday 11:00 to 21:00 with free entry; Friday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00. Admission 40 kr.

FUNDING
Nuuk Art Museum: municipal, with a 2024 Augustinus Fonden grant for one major acquisition
- Kommuneqarfik Sermersooq owns and runs the museum (museum website); staff addresses are on the municipality's sermersooq.gl domain.
- Founding gift: the building, fixtures and collection were donated by Svend and Helene Junge (museum website).
- Augustinus Fonden: "a large funding" from the foundation paid for buying all parts of "Melting Barricades", announced in April 2024, and the foundation also supported the exhibition of the work that autumn (stated by the museum).

Red flags: 2024 acquisition paid for with tobacco-company foundation money
- Augustinus Fonden was set up in 1942 by the owners of Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, a predecessor of Scandinavian Tobacco Group; the foundation now wholly owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker as the holding company of its investments (Wikipedia).
- As of 1 May 2025, Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker held more than 25 per cent of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, the only holder above 5 per cent listed (Scandinavian Tobacco Group).
- The grant was made in 2024, within the last five years.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a municipal institution built on a private founding gift. Its one named outside funder is a foundation whose wealth comes from a large stake in a listed tobacco company, and that grant paid for its most important recent acquisition.
