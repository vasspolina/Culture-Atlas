MAWSON'S HUTS: Cape Denison, Commonwealth Bay, Antarctica
ID: mawsons-huts-cape-denison
Website: https://www.mawsons-huts.org.au (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Wooden huts at Cape Denison, Commonwealth Bay, in the far east of the Australian Antarctic Territory, 3,000 km south of Tasmania (Australian Antarctic Division).
- Built in 1912 by the Australasian Antarctic Expedition of 1911-14, led by the geologist Douglas Mawson; the main hut housed 18 men in 1912 and the seven, Mawson among them, who stayed an unplanned second year in 1913 (Mawson's Huts Foundation submission to Treasury).
- Main Hut joins living quarters prefabricated in Sydney and a workshop prefabricated in Melbourne, clad in Baltic pine on an Oregon frame (Treasury submission). The site also holds Magnetograph House, the Absolute Magnetic Hut, the Transit Hut, a memorial cross and a 1931 proclamation pole (2025 heritage management plan).
- On the Commonwealth Heritage List since 2004 and the National Heritage List since 2005; an Antarctic Specially Protected Area (ASPA 162) and Historic Site under the Antarctic Treaty (Australian Antarctic Division). The government calls it the foundation of Australia's claim to 42 per cent of Antarctica (Australian Antarctic Division, 2013).
- Conservation has re-clad the roofs with timber from the same region of Finland as the original, removed about 80 per cent of the ice inside before the 2017-18 season and braced the Transit Hut (Treasury submission; 2025 plan). The 2025 plan commits to an on-site heritage assessment within its first two years. The 2021-22 expedition spent five weeks on site, led by Marty Passingham, and travelled on the French vessel L'Astrolabe (Foundation).
- Visiting: entry needs a permit. About 650 people visited in the twentieth century, two thirds of them tourists, and more than 2,500 tourists since 2000; the iceberg B09B blocked Commonwealth Bay from 2012 to 2017, and no visits were reported in 2023-24 (2025 plan).
- A full-size replica, the Mawson's Huts Replica Museum on the Hobart waterfront, opened on 2 December 2011 (Treasury submission).

FUNDING
Mawson's Huts: government-managed site, conserved by a charity that lives mainly on replica museum and shop income plus federal grants
- The Australian Antarctic Division manages the site for the Australian Government. Since 1996 the Mawson's Huts Foundation, a private charity, has mounted conservation expeditions under its approval (2025 plan). Sources differ on its founding year: the Foundation's own site and its 2021 release say 1996, the Division's 2006 release says 1995, and the Foundation's 2017-18 pre-budget submission and its chair's biography say 1997.
- The Foundation's income in 2023-24 was A$762,114: A$452,902 from goods and services, A$126,166 donations, A$114,900 government revenue and A$68,146 other income. It spent A$630,948 and ran a A$131,166 surplus (ACNC annual information statement). In 2022-23 income was A$643,534, with A$45,194 from government, and it ran a A$156,465 deficit (ACNC).
- Federal grants: A$320,000 in 2005-06 (Australian Antarctic Division, 2006); A$472,725 for conservation and A$350,000 for stage one of the replica, a venture with the Australian Geographic Society, announced by Prime Minister Julia Gillard and Heritage Minister Tony Burke (Australian Antarctic Division, 2013); A$321,000 announced on 26 April 2020 by Environment Minister Sussan Ley from a A$5.3 million national heritage programme (Foundation).
- In its pre-budget submission for 2017-18 the Foundation said it had raised more than A$9 million in cash and in kind, A$2.5 million of it federal special grants. It asked for A$576,500 of a A$951,000 expedition, putting in A$139,500 itself and A$235,000 in kind; sponsors named were Reuters (satellite communications) and Honda (quad bike and generator parts) (Treasury submission).
- The French Antarctic programme provides the main transport to the site (Treasury submission).
- Board: chair Greg Holland, a director since the Foundation's establishment apart from a break in 2020, is a former chief executive of Spirits & Cocktails Australia and former chair of Alcohol Beverages Australia. Director Greg Carter, the Foundation's chief executive in 2021, has joined expedition cruise operator Antarpply Expeditions. The chief executive is Lara Colrain (Foundation).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Mawson's Huts is clean. The weak points are a small charity's dependence on grants for costly expeditions and board ties to the alcohol and cruise trades. Worth watching: whether federal grants keep pace with the work the site needs.
