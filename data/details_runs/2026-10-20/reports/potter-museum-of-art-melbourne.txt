POTTER MUSEUM OF ART: Melbourne, Australia
ID: potter-museum-of-art-melbourne
Website: https://potter-museum.unimelb.edu.au/

OVERVIEW
- University art museum on the University of Melbourne campus in Parkville, at the corner of Swanston Street and Masson Road; it is part of University of Melbourne Museums and Collections (Wikipedia; museum site).
- It traces its origins to the University Gallery, established in 1972, and was renamed the Ian Potter Museum of Art in July 1998 when the current building opened (Wikipedia).
- The 1998 building was designed by Katsalidis Architects and won a commendation for Public Architecture at the 1999 Australian Institute of Architects National Architecture Awards (ArchitectureAU).
- It holds the University of Melbourne's art collections, including university portraits, material from the Grainger Museum and the Norman Lindsay collection (Wikipedia).
- The museum closed for refurbishment in 2018 and reopened on 30 May 2025 after a redevelopment by Wood Marsh Architects: a new campus-side entrance with a vaulted double-height foyer, learning and teaching studios, event spaces and a restaurant (University of Melbourne announcement, 10 October 2024; ArchitectureAU, Lucia Amies, 29 May 2025).
- The reopening exhibition, "65,000 Years: A Short History of Australian Art", ran until 22 November 2025 with more than 400 artworks and seven new commissions by First Nations artists, curated by Marcia Langton, Judith Ryan and Shanysa McConville.
- Charlotte Day is Director of Art Museums (University of Melbourne announcement).
- Visiting: free entry; open Tuesday to Saturday 11:00 to 17:00 (museum site).

FUNDING
Potter Museum of Art: university museum: part of the University of Melbourne, with private donors and arts agencies named for the 2025 reopening
- Parent body: the museum is part of University of Melbourne Museums and Collections (museum site).
- Redevelopment: the Ian Potter Foundation and Lady Primrose Potter AC supported the redevelopment and the new learning spaces (University of Melbourne announcement, 10 October 2024).
- Exhibition: "a significant donation from principal supporters Peter McMullin AM and Ruth McMullin" made the reopening exhibition possible; Naomi Milgrom AC, Andy Zhang and Rainie Zhang are named foundational supporters (University of Melbourne announcement).
- The Gordon Darling Foundation is the publication partner (University of Melbourne announcement).
- John Wardle and Judith and Leon Gorr are named supporters; Creative Australia and Creative Victoria are named supporters of the exhibition (University of Melbourne announcement).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one review read gives five stars and criticises the University of Melbourne's past as a eugenics research centre, shown in the exhibition, not the museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Tim Stone, "The Big Review | 65,000 Years: A Short History of Australian Art at the Potter Museum of Art, Melbourne" (1 September 2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/09/01/the-big-review-%7C-65000-years-a-short-history-of-australian-art-at-the-potter-museum-of-art-in-melbourne
- Five-star review: the exhibition goes further than earlier surveys of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander art in geography, culture and history.
- Says the "Scientific Racism" room exposes the University of Melbourne's past as one of Australia's leading eugenics research centres, which the review calls a "shameful past".
- Praises the exhibition and its works; criticises the university's history, not the Potter. The only mention of the reopening is that the exhibition at the newly reopened Potter is on track to be the most visited in the institution's history.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Green Left (Jacob Andrewartha, "Student encampment forces Uni of Melbourne to disclose weapons ties", 23 May 2024) reported that after a four-week Gaza solidarity encampment and a week-long occupation the University of Melbourne agreed to disclose its funding arrangements with weapons corporations, that Unimelb for Palestine expected disclosure of ties to all weapons manufacturers including Lockheed Martin, Boeing and BAE Systems, and that the university rejected the demand to divest.
- The Melburnian (Georgie Atkins, "University commits to disclose ties to weapons companies amid student protest", 28 May 2024) reported that the university committed to additional disclosure of its research grant arrangements, and named the same three companies.
- Neither article names the Potter; the connection is that the Potter is part of the University of Melbourne (museum site).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is part of the University of Melbourne, with the 2025 reopening backed by the Ian Potter Foundation, Lady Primrose Potter, Peter and Ruth McMullin, Naomi Milgrom, Andy and Rainie Zhang and the Gordon Darling Foundation, with Creative Australia and Creative Victoria named as supporters of the reopening exhibition. The 2024 student campaign over the university's weapons-company ties concerned the university; neither article about it names the Potter.
