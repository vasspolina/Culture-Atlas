OVERVIEW
- Executive non-departmental public body that runs the Scottish National Gallery, the Scottish National Portrait Gallery and the Scottish National Gallery of Modern Art in Edinburgh, and oversees two partner galleries, Duff House in Banff and Paxton House in Berwickshire (Wikipedia).
- The National Gallery of Scotland opened on The Mound in 1859; the Portrait Gallery opened in 1889; the modern art gallery was established in Inverleith House in 1959, moved to the former John Watson's Institution in 1984, and the Dean Gallery (now Modern Two) opened in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Governed under the National Galleries of Scotland Act 1906 as amended in 1985 by a board of trustees appointed by Scottish Government ministers; registered Scottish charity, about 327 staff, Director-General Anne Lyden (Wikipedia).
- New Scottish galleries opened at the National in September 2023; the share of National Gallery visitors who go on to the Scottish collection rose to 63% from 19% (The Art Newspaper, Rachel Ashcroft, 12 January 2024).

FUNDING
National Galleries of Scotland: mainly public: Scottish Government grant-in-aid, plus donations, membership and sponsors including Baillie Gifford
- Scottish Government grant-in-aid; for 2024/25 about 95% of it goes on salaries, leaving 5% for running costs. The budget leaves the organisation "in a deficit position". Energy costs rose 72% in two years (The Art Newspaper, 2024).
- The galleries plan to cover running costs through visitor donations and membership subscriptions (The Art Newspaper). No income totals could be confirmed: the 2023-24 accounts PDF could not be read.
- Baillie Gifford, an Edinburgh asset manager: sponsor of the galleries; the value and length of the sponsorship are not disclosed and Baillie Gifford declined to comment (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 2 July 2024).
- BP: the galleries hosted the BP Portrait Award, a London-based exhibition that toured to Edinburgh; trustees ended it "in its present form" after the 2019 show (Hyperallergic, 11 November 2019).
- No reporting found of a full donor and trust list in the sources opened.

Red flags: Baillie Gifford and fossil fuels, Israel
- 2 July 2024: the galleries said they would keep Baillie Gifford as a sponsor despite protests over its ties to fossil fuels and Israel. Director-General Anne Lyden said the galleries are "confident" the funding meets their ethical standards; an ethics committee reviewed the relationship and leaders consulted the Scottish Government (The Art Newspaper).
- Campaign: Fossil Free Books called on Baillie Gifford to divest from fossil fuels and from companies involved in Israeli occupation; more than 800 signatories backed it, and Baillie Gifford's sponsorship of nine book festivals ended in May 2024 (The Art Newspaper). Baillie Gifford says 2% of client money is in fossil-fuel-linked companies against a market average of 11% (stated by Baillie Gifford).
- 4 August 2026: the campaign group Art Workers for Palestine Scotland said the galleries still receive Baillie Gifford backing and argued that the sector is complicit; the group is an openly partisan advocacy group (Snack, interview by Zoe White).

Red flags: BP sponsorship dropped under pressure
- Scottish branch of BP or Not BP? protested the BP Portrait Award at the Edinburgh gallery in 2016 and 2018; trustees cited the "climate emergency" when ending it in November 2019 (Hyperallergic, Naomi Polonsky).

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the money is Scottish Government grant, which the galleries say is almost wholly absorbed by salaries, so private income matters to them. The weak points are the Baillie Gifford sponsorship, kept in 2024 against protests, and the undisclosed value of that sponsorship. Worth watching: whether Baillie Gifford stays as the campaign continues and whether the accounts disclose its gift.
