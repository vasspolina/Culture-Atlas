CENTRO DE ARTE CONTEMPORÁNEO WIFREDO LAM: Havana, Cuba
ID: centro-de-arte-contemporaneo-wifredo-lam
Website: http://www.cnap.cult.cu/ (checked 2026-11-20; did not load)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at Calle San Ignacio 22, corner of Empedrado, beside the Plaza de la Catedral in Old Havana (EcuRed, state encyclopedia, archive copy of 5 February 2026).
- Created on 28 February 1983 and named for the Cuban painter Wifredo Lam, to study and promote contemporary visual art from Africa, Asia, Latin America and the Caribbean and to study Lam's work (EcuRed; CNAP).
- Building: an 18th-century mansion of the Counts of Peñalver; a restoration was decided in 1984 and the building went to the Centre in 1993. It closed in 2003 and reopened for the Biennials of 2006 and 2009 (EcuRed).
- It holds exhibition rooms, a library and a collection of about 1,250 works (EcuRed).
- The director is Nelson Ramírez de Arellano (EcuRed; Two Coats of Paint).
- The 15th Biennial, "Horizontes Compartidos" (Shared Horizons), ran from 15 November 2024 to 28 February 2025, announced by the Centre, CNAP and the Ministry of Culture (OnCuba).

FUNDING
Wifredo Lam Centre: mainly public: Ministry of Culture through CNAP, with collectors and foreign foundations behind the Biennial
- CNAP, a Ministry of Culture body, lists the Centre among the institutions that make it up and says CNAP and the Centre organise the Biennial (CNAP, archive copy of 8 December 2023).
- Building: the 2005 to 2009 works were paid for by the Centro Inversionista de Obras Priorizadas (CIOP) and carried out by Puerto Carena, a company of the Office of the City Historian (EcuRed).
- Collection: built from artists' donations and from purchases from institutions and artists (EcuRed).
- Biennial, 2003: CNAP's president Rafael Acosta said foundations like Prince Claus and Hivos had provided 70 percent of the external funding for the previous Biennial. After they withdrew, the Cuban government agreed at the last minute to give 156,000 dollars, less than first planned, and foreign artists found their own funding (IPS).
- Biennial, now: six Cuban and Cuban-American arts professionals wrote in 2024 that collectors with sizable holdings of Cuban art are among its key international supporters (e-flux).

Red flags: 2024 protest by the jailed artist Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara aimed at the 15th Biennial
- November 2024: from Guanajay prison, Otero Alcántara invited Biennial visitors to visit him as a work called Proof of Life. Coco Fusco said he took part because of the government's hypocrisy in staging a biennial amid repression and censorship (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism targets the Biennial the Centre runs, as funder withdrawals, boycott calls and essays on state control, not the Centre's own exhibitions.
1. e-flux: Solveig Font, Coco Fusco, Celia Irina González, Hamlet Lavastida, Julio Llópiz Casal and Yanelys Nuñez Leyva, "Can a Biennial Respect Difference in a Country that Represses Dissidence? On the Havana Biennial" (2024)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/627502/can-a-biennial-respect-difference-in-a-country-that-represses-dissidence-on-the-havana-biennial
- Asks what it means for a state entity to stage a biennial of difference and resistance in a country with over one thousand political prisoners.
- Argues the Ministry of Culture uses ties to foreign collectors and gallerists to keep control of Cuban art, and that foreign curators are watched by the Interior Ministry.
2. e-flux: Cuban cultural workers, "Open letter: Why we say NO to the 14th Havana Biennial and ask you to do the same" (2021)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/426144/open-letter-why-we-say-no-to-the-14th-havana-biennial-and-ask-you-to-do-the-same
- Says the organising institutions condoned and took part in violence against cultural workers. Does not name the Centre.
3. Inter Press Service: Dalia Acosta, "Arts Weekly / Cuba: Spectre of Censorship Haunts Biennial" (2003)
URL: https://www.ipsnews.net/2003/11/arts-weekly-cuba-spectre-of-censorship-haunts-biennial/
- Gives the officials' side: Acosta denied that the organisers censored any of the works or artists a Venezuelan newspaper had named.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2003: Hivos said censorship was evident in the 8th Biennial's programme; the Prince Claus Foundation tied its withdrawal to the March 2003 arrest of 75 dissidents. Acosta said more than 200,000 dollars were withdrawn. France's AFAA, a foreign ministry agency, cited bureaucratic difficulties (IPS).
- 2003: the artists Alexander Apóstol and Priscila Moge pulled out; Moge wrote to the Centre objecting to censorship (IPS).
- October 2021: the open letter against the 14th Biennial cited jailed artists and over 1,000 people arrested in the protests of 11 July. Ursula Biemann withdrew (La Jornada); Tania Bruguera said Nicolas Bourriaud and Biemann declined to take part in related events (ARTnews). Ramírez said five participants had cancelled and that the boycott rested on arguments beyond an art space's remit (La Jornada).
- These cases concern the Biennial the Centre organises.

BOTTOM LINE
The Centre's money is state money with no flagged funder. Its weak point is the standing of the Biennial it organises among artists who protest repression in Cuba. Worth watching: which collectors and foundations back the next edition.
