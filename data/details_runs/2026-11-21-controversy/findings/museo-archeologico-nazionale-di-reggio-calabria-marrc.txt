WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2014 the photographer Gerald Bruneau photographed the Riace Bronzes in the museum dressed in boas and leopard-print loincloths; the website Dagospia first reported the pictures (Finestre sull'Arte, 3 August 2014).
- The superintendent Simonetta Bonomi told ANSA that Bruneau had asked to take a shot of a statue with a white tulle behind it and then changed his plans without her knowledge, and that the custodians stopped him, though he had time to take several shots (Finestre sull'Arte, 3 August 2014).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
1. Finestre sull'Arte: Federico Giannini, "Riace bronzes with boas and loincloths: whose responsibility is it?" (2014)
URL: https://www.finestresullarte.info/en/news-focus/riace-bronzes-with-boas-and-loincloths-whose-responsibility-is-it
- The published rules for the Bronzes' hall say they "are to be strictly observed", yet the shoot ran past the 20-minute visit slot.
- Staff let the photographer work in close contact with "very delicate statues" without special care; the custodians should have watched him throughout and stopped him at once.
- The affair "casts several shadows on the work of the superintendence."
