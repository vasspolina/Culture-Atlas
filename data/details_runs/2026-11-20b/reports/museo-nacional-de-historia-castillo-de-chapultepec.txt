MUSEO NACIONAL DE HISTORIA, CASTILLO DE CHAPULTEPEC: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-nacional-de-historia-castillo-de-chapultepec
Website: https://mnh.inah.gob.mx/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Mexico's national history museum, in Chapultepec Castle at the highest point of Chapultepec Forest, run by the federal Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia (INAH) (INAH).
- The castle was begun in 1785 as a country retreat for Viceroy Bernardo de Gálvez; it later became the seat of the Military College, a decision taken in 1833, then Maximilian and Carlota's imperial residence (1864 to 1867) and a presidential residence (museum site; INAH).
- The museum traces back to the first Museo Nacional, ordered in 1825; President Lázaro Cárdenas decreed the castle its seat in February 1939, and it opened on 27 September 1944 after five years of adaptation (museum site).
- Major works: a remodelling in 1964 and a full intervention on the building from 2000 to 2004 (director Salvador Rueda Smithers to La Razón, 2024).
- 12 permanent rooms on Mexican history from the Conquest to the Revolution and 22 rooms in the Alcázar recreating the apartments of Maximilian and Carlota and of Porfirio Díaz, plus murals, archaeology and a library and photo archive in an annexe (museum site).
- The collection holds 106,000 pieces, about 7,000 of them on show; recent acquisitions favour dress and women's history (La Razón, 26 September 2024).
- Directed in 2024 by the historian Salvador Rueda Smithers (La Razón).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 17:00, closed every Monday; general entry 210 pesos, with a 50% discount for Mexicans and residents, free on Sundays for nationals and residents, free for children under 13, over-60s, teachers and students, and people with disabilities; photo shoots and professional equipment are banned (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Historia: federal: INAH budget, with a friends' association, an INAH patronato and one bank partnership
- INAH, a federal institute under the Secretaría de Cultura, runs the museum; the government budget covers security, exhibitions and maintenance, not restoration (Rueda to La Razón, 2024).
- An INAH patronato lets the museum save money for acquisitions, and the Asociación de Amigos contributes funds (La Razón, 2024).
- Bank of America paid 50,000 dollars for the restoration of four works, among them "El Bautizo de Ixtlilxóchitl de Texcoco" and "Batalla del 5 de mayo de 1862" (La Razón, 2024).
- The federal Proyecto Chapultepec: Naturaleza y Cultura paid for repairs to awnings and rooms, a cafeteria, a larger storage depot, a fence and exterior lighting (La Razón, 2024).
- The museum recorded 1,365,042 pesos in fees for use of its spaces from 2022 to 2026, from 394 requests that included corporate events for firms such as Ingredion, Merck, FEMSA and Grupo Coppel; the rate for most outside events in 2026 was 12,245 pesos (Reforma, via Vanguardia). These hirers are clients, not donors.

Red flags: INAH researchers' 2026 complaint over the FIFA gala dinner in the castle
- A group of INAH researchers filed a complaint over misuse of the space after FIFA held a private World Cup gala dinner on 10 June 2026 in the Alcázar and its patio, the esplanade and the Jardín de Pérgolas, for about 1,000 guests (Reforma, via Vanguardia, 4 August 2026).
- The investigation is open: INAH confirmed it holds the authorisation, the technical report and the legal instrument for the dinner but classified them as reserved because of an ongoing investigation, and the dinner is missing from the event records it released under transparency law (Vanguardia).
- The Secretaría de Cultura found no document declaring the dinner a relevant cultural or civic act, as a 1977 presidential agreement requires for such use (Vanguardia).
- INAH said FIFA paid more than one million pesos to rent the castle and cover costs, that the event complied with the rules and protected the building, and that it promoted Mexico's heritage to more than 200 foreign representatives (LatinUS, 20 June 2026; Grupo Animal). The museum's own website says only cultural, academic or scientific events are allowed (LatinUS). FIFA was a paying client, not a funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean: federal funds with private top-ups, none from a flagged funder. The weak point is governance, and the outcome of the open investigation into the FIFA dinner is worth watching.
