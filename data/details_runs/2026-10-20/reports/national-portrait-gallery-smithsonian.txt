OVERVIEW
- Smithsonian portrait museum at Eighth and F Streets NW, Washington, in part of the Old Patent Office Building, built from 1836 to 1867 (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1962; it moved into the building in 1966 and opened to the public on 7 October 1968 (Wikipedia).
- The complex is named the Donald W. Reynolds Center for American Art and Portraiture in appreciation of the Donald W. Reynolds Foundation's gifts (Wikipedia).
- The renovation, which closed the building in 2000 and used Hartman-Cox Architects and a glass canopy designed by Norman Foster, cost 283 million dollars against a first estimate of 42 million; it reopened on 1 July 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Holds the Hall of Presidents, with a portrait of every US president centred on the Lansdowne portrait of George Washington (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of current hours, admission or accessibility on the pages read.

FUNDING
National Portrait Gallery: mainly public: US Congress through the Smithsonian, with private donors
- The Smithsonian's budget is about 1.25 billion dollars a year, about two-thirds from federal appropriations, plus endowment, gifts and earned income (Wikipedia, Smithsonian Institution).
- The Donald W. Reynolds Foundation gave 30 million dollars in 2001 toward the Lansdowne portrait and 45 million in 2005 for the renovation; Robert and Arlene Kogod gave 25 million in 2004 for the courtyard canopy (Wikipedia).
- Presidential portraits cost about 150,000 to 200,000 dollars each; in 2022 a Trump-aligned PAC paid 650,000 dollars for portraits of Donald and Melania Trump (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a current donor list for this gallery on the pages read.

Red flags: White House pressure, 2025 to 2026
- 30 May 2025: Trump said he had fired director Kim Sajet, calling her "a highly partisan person". On 9 June the Smithsonian said personnel decisions were for the Secretary with the Board's oversight. Sajet then resigned; in a 13 June letter to staff Secretary Lonnie Bunch said she "put the needs of the Institution above her own" (Apollo, 15 June 2025).
- July 2025: Amy Sherald cancelled her show "American Sublime", due to open in September, saying the gallery was considering removing her painting "Trans Forming Liberty" (2024) and that Bunch suggested replacing it with a video. A Smithsonian spokesperson said the institution "could not come to an agreement with the artist" (Hyperallergic, 24 July 2025).
- Reported 12 January 2026: the gallery replaced the label beside a new Trump photograph in its presidents gallery; the old label said he was impeached twice "on charges of abuse of power and incitement of insurrection", the new one gives his birth year, term dates and the photographer. The Smithsonian said the gallery had begun a planned update and was exploring "tombstone labels" (OPB, Democracy Now).
- 12 August 2025: White House officials wrote to Secretary Bunch about a review of eight Smithsonian museums, this one included (NPR, via WUSF).

WIDER CONTEXT
- A 2010 "Hide/Seek" exhibition at the gallery drew criticism over a David Wojnarowicz video, which was removed (Wikipedia).
- The 2025 and 2026 events above took place at this gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly Congress through the Smithsonian, with named foundation gifts for the building, and none from a flagged industry in the sources read. The weak points are the 2025 dismissal attempt and resignation of its director, the cancelled Sherald show and the 2026 label change. Watch the White House review and who is appointed director.
