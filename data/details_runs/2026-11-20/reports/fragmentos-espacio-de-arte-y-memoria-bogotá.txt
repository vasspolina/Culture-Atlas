FRAGMENTOS, ESPACIO DE ARTE Y MEMORIA: Bogotá, Colombia
ID: fragmentos-espacio-de-arte-y-memoria-bogotá
Website: https://www.fragmentos.gov.co/Paginas/default.aspx (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial, gallery and counter-monument at Carrera 7 # 6B-30 in La Candelaria, Bogotá, one block from the Palacio de Nariño (the space; La República).
- Created by artist Doris Salcedo with architect Carlos Granada; pre-opened on 30 July 2018 and officially opened on 10 December 2018 (José Ruiz; Radio Nacional).
- The floor is 1,288 tiles cast from about 37 tons of steel from 8,994 FARC weapons. Salcedo worked on the molds with 20 women who survived sexual violence in the conflict (Wikipedia).
- Built among the ruins of an abandoned colonial-era house; the plates cover 800 square metres (Wikipedia; Radio Nacional).
- A yearly call, run from 2019 by the Ministry of Culture and the Museo Nacional, brings projects that answer the work (Radio Nacional). Shows include Francis Alÿs (2020 to 2021), Beatriz González (2022 to 2023), Gabriela Golder (2023), Tania Candiani (2024) and Goya's "Los Desastres de la Guerra" (5 September 2026 to 17 January 2027) (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 9 am to 5 pm, closed Mondays, 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. Free entry (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Fragmentos: public: Colombia's Ministry of Culture, through the Museo Nacional
- The Government of Colombia, through the Ministry of Culture, sponsored and owns the site; the Museo Nacional manages the gallery and its exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Santos government's handover report records 2,364,543,568 pesos paid in the first half of 2018 to the builder, Gerencia Construcción Arquitectónica (José Ruiz).
- Running costs: in May 2021 the ministry said the government provides the budget that keeps Fragmentos open and pays for its calls (El Universal).
- Resolution 2655 of 2018 created a Committee of Artistic Activities that can raise additional money; its seats include Salcedo, the Museo Nacional's director and the Bogotá Chamber of Commerce president (Herrera and Peñuela).
- Yearly award: 160 million pesos in 2019 (Radio Nacional) and again in 2025, to the group Enjambres for "Coral-es"; Salcedo sat on the jury (ministry resolution 1836).

Red flags: Protest over President Duque's use of the space (May 2021)
- On 9 May 2021 President Iván Duque, members of his government and church leaders met in Fragmentos during the national strike (El Universal).
- Works by Francis Alÿs on show there were covered with white paper for the meeting (El Universal). Salcedo told Hyperallergic that neither Alÿs, the museum's board nor she was consulted.
- Salcedo said the work had been "abusively used, breaking all the international norms of conservation and copyright", and of the meeting: "It was theater" (Hyperallergic).
- Mexican curator Cuauhtémoc Medina published "El Museo Ocupado": "Así es como los gobernantes vampirizan la cultura" (El Universal).
- El Universal reported that Alÿs demanded that his show be taken down.
- The Ministry of Culture said "en ningún momento se rompió alguna norma" and the works "no presentan ninguna alteración" (El Universal, 14 May 2021).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: News coverage criticises the state's use of the space in 2021; Colombian critics question how the commission was awarded.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Colombian Government Exploited Doris Salcedo's Art to Denounce Nationwide Protests" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/645108/colombian-government-exploited-doris-salcedo-art-to-denounce-nationwide-protests/
- Reports the meeting as the latest case of "artwashing" by the Ministry of Culture, which did not answer the outlet.
2. El Universal: Sonia Sierra, "'Gobernantes vampirizan la cultura'; cuestionan conversación del gobierno colombiano e iglesias en un museo" (2021)
URL: https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cultura/gobernantes-vampirizan-la-cultura-cuestionan-conversacion-del-gobierno-colombiano-e-iglesias/
- Reports Medina's case that the room should serve reflection on the conflict's memory, not political staging for the media.
3. El Universal: Sonia Sierra, "Se protegió la obra de Francis Alÿs" (2021)
URL: https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cultura/se-protegio-la-obra-de-francis-alys-ministerio-de-cultura-de-colombia/
- Not criticism: the ministry's answer, that the space was chosen for "su importancia simbólica". El Universal notes the ministry did not say who checked the works.
4. Estudios Artísticos: María Mercedes Herrera Buitrago and Jorge Peñuela, "Fragmentos, espacio de arte y memoria: ¿monumento de memoria histórica o galería comercial de arte contemporáneo?" (2022)
URL: https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8234885.pdf
- Argues the commission went to Salcedo without justification, while the United Nations monument went to Mario Opazo through an open call of 28 projects.
- Asks why no delegate of the FARC party or the victims sits on the committee; concludes the space serves some artists' commercial careers.
5. Premio Nacional de Crítica: José Ruiz, "El artista como institución" (2019)
URL: https://premionalcritica.uniandes.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/El-artista-como-institucion_Categoria-1_Reconocimiento-nacional-a-la-critica_Jose-Ruiz.pdf
- Argues that making the memorial an institution pushed the melted weapons into second place, and that its final cost and upkeep are not known with certainty.

WIDER CONTEXT
- During the 2021 national strike the state met protests over poverty, corruption and inequality with militarised riot police; dozens died and at least 87 people were reported missing (Hyperallergic).
- Fragmentos is a Ministry of Culture site, and the government held its own meeting there in the middle of that crackdown.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Colombian state's, through the Ministry of Culture and the Museo Nacional. The weak points are control and openness: the president used the memorial for a political meeting without consulting the artists, and critics say the commission and its full cost went unexplained. Worth watching: how later governments use the space.
