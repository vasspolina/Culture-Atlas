WINNIPEG ART GALLERY-QAUMAJUQ: Winnipeg, Canada
ID: winnipeg-art-gallery-qaumajuq-winnipeg
Website: https://www.wag.ca/

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery in downtown Winnipeg, made up of the 1971 main building and Qaumajuq, a building devoted to Inuit art that opened in March 2021 (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- Its roots go back to the Winnipeg Museum of Fine Arts, which opened on 16 December 1912. The present Winnipeg Art Gallery succeeded earlier bodies in 1963 (Wikipedia).
- Qaumajuq is Inuktitut for roughly "it is bright, it is lit" (Wikipedia, ArtReview).
- Building and site: the main building opened on 25 September 1971, designed by Gustavo da Roza in a late modernist style and clad in Tyndall stone, with sloping walls and a wedge-shaped entrance. Qaumajuq is a four-storey, 40,000 square foot extension by the Los Angeles architect Michael Maltzan with Cibinel Architecture, with a three-storey glass vault that visitors can see into from the street (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- On site: Qaumajuq has an 8,000 square foot exhibition space, a 90-seat theatre, a cafe and art studios. The Art Newspaper says the gallery holds the largest public collection of Inuit art in the world, around 14,000 pieces by more than 2,000 artists. About 8,000 works came on loan from the Government of Nunavut under a 2015 agreement (Wikipedia).
- Its inaugural Qaumajuq exhibition, Inua, was organised by Inuit curators and showed more than 90 contemporary Inuit artists (The Art Newspaper, ArtReview).
- Ferdinand Eckhardt served as a director of the gallery from 1953 to 1974 (Global News, The Canadian Press). In a 19 December 2023 press release the gallery said it had started removing his name from its main entrance hall and its materials, after The Walrus reported his Nazi ties (The Art Newspaper).

FUNDING
Winnipeg Art Gallery-Qaumajuq: public and private: per Wikipedia, three levels of government provided C$35 million of Qaumajuq's estimated C$65 million cost and donations the rest
- Qaumajuq cost an estimated C$65 million. Federal, provincial and municipal governments provided C$35 million and public and private donations the rest (Wikipedia).
- The Government of Nunavut lent about 8,000 works to the collection in 2015 (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two Art Newspaper reports name the gallery: the 2021 opening report, which covers its colonial collecting history and Eckhardt's framing of Inuit art, and the 2024 report on the gallery's response to the reports about its former director Eckhardt.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gabriella Angeleti, "World's biggest Inuit art collection revealed at Winnipeg's new museum within a museum" (25 March 2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/winnipeg-gallery-opens-new-centre-dedicated-to-inuit-art
- Says Eckhardt, who became director in 1953, was later criticised for framing Inuit works as an advanced form of folk art and failing to give the artists the same due as their European counterparts, and that he acquired Inuit soapstone carvings for the gallery from the Hudson's Bay Company in Winnipeg.
- Says some critics argued that the Hudson's Bay Company's entanglement in colonialism was largely ignored in later shows. Organisers say they will keep working with a panel of Nunavut advisors so that Indigenous voices are at the forefront.
2. The Art Newspaper: Elena Goukassian, "Canadian museum removing name of former director and Nazi supporter Ferdinand Eckhardt from its entrance hall" (11 January 2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/01/11/winnipeg-art-gallery-removing-name-nazi-supporter-ferdinand-eckhardt
- Reports that Conrad Sweatman's investigation for The Walrus (November 2023) cited Eckhardt's signed oath of allegiance to Hitler and his polemics in early-1930s far-right journals, and reported that he worked for IG Farben.
- The gallery said in a 19 December 2023 press release that it had started removing his name and would research the provenance of Eckhardt and Eckhardt-Gramatté Foundation donations; Manitoba revoked his 1982 Order of the Buffalo Hunt on 10 January 2024. Director Stephen Borys had earlier told the Winnipeg Free Press there were "no gaps" in the provenance of works Eckhardt acquired.
- Concerns the gallery's former director and the gallery's own response; it does not address current funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The Winnipeg Art Gallery-Qaumajuq is a public gallery; Wikipedia says its Qaumajuq building cost an estimated C$65 million, of which federal, provincial and city governments provided C$35 million and donations the rest. The Art Newspaper reported in 2021 that critics saw later shows as ignoring the Hudson's Bay Company's colonial history, and in 2024 that the gallery was removing the name of former director Ferdinand Eckhardt after The Walrus reported his Nazi ties.
