OVERVIEW
- Free contemporary art gallery, artists' studios and international residency centre at 155 Vauxhall Street, Kennington, in a 19th-century warehouse just behind the Oval cricket ground (The Art Newspaper, 2024; Art Fund).
- Founded in 1994 by Robert Loder, who had earlier set up the beginnings of the Triangle Network with the sculptor Anthony Caro (The Art Newspaper, Louisa Buck, 2024).
- Building: Gasworks raised £2.1 million to buy and redevelop the building, redesigned by Hana Loftus of HAT Projects (The Art Newspaper, Louisa Buck, 2015).
- On site: a gallery that often stages artists' first major UK shows (Art Fund), nine subsidised studios on five-year leases for London artists, and four studios for three-month international residencies (The Art Newspaper, 2024). The Triangle Network stretches from Colombia to Bangladesh and from Kenya to China.
- Visiting: Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00 during exhibitions, or by appointment; free (Art Fund). Accessibility details were not found.

FUNDING
Gasworks: mainly public and trust-funded: Arts Council England, local authority grants and arts foundations
- Charity: Gasworks sits within the Triangle Arts Trust (charity 326411). Income for the year to 31 March 2026 was £999,591 against spending of £891,028; for 2025 it was £903,812 against £898,537 (OpenCharities, from Charity Commission data).
- Arts Council England: credited as "public funding" on all four 2026 exhibitions (e-flux, Gasworks programme announcement). The annual amount was not found, and no page read confirms a figure of £290,000 from Arts Council England. In 2013 the Arts Council pledged £1 million towards buying and renovating the building as the first stage of a £1.8 million capital campaign, with a Lambeth council grant for the development phase (Artlyst, 2013).
- Exhibition funders credited in 2026: Catherine Petitgas, Cockayne Grants for the Arts, the Great Britain Sasakawa Foundation, the Kumagai Masatoshi Culture Foundation and the Yoshino Gypsum Art Foundation (Umi Ishihara show); Prue MacLeod and Fundación Ama Amoedo (Paloma Contreras Lomas residency and show) (e-flux).
- Director: Robert Leckie succeeded Alessio Antoniolli in 2024; nine trustees are listed on OpenCharities, from Charity Commission data, including Alexa Waley-Cohen (appointed March 2025).
- No reporting found of City Bridge Foundation, Lambeth or Henry Moore Foundation amounts; the supporters page that names them could not be opened.
- No reporting found of any corporate funder from a flagged industry.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Gasworks is public and charitable: Arts Council England, grants from trusts and foundations, and individual patrons, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that the supporters page could not be opened for this report and annual amounts per funder are not published in the sources read.
