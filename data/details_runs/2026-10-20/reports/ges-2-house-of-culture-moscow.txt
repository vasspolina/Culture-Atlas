GES-2 HOUSE OF CULTURE: Moscow, Russia
ID: ges-2-house-of-culture-moscow
Website: https://v-a-c.org

OVERVIEW
- Art centre in a former power plant at 15 Bolotnaya Embankment, opposite the Kremlin, spanning 41,000 square metres (ges-2.org; The Art Newspaper, 3 December 2021).
- It is the Moscow venue of the V-A-C Foundation, founded in 2009 by Leonid Mikhelson and Teresa Iarocci Mavica (Wikipedia).
- Renzo Piano Building Workshop carried out the conversion; the centre opened on 4 December 2021 (V-A-C website; The Art Newspaper, 2021).
- Teresa Iarocci Mavica stepped down as GES-2 director weeks after the opening. V-A-C's artistic director Francesco Manacorda resigned in March 2022 citing the war, and Ragnar Kjartansson pulled out of his planned opening project Santa Barbara (The Art Newspaper, Sophia Kishkovsky, 19 October 2022).
- Alisa Prudnikova was appointed programme director of V-A-C and GES-2 in May 2022 (The Art Newspaper, 19 October 2022).
- In January 2023 Mikhelson transferred the GES-2 cultural centre to his daughter Victoria, according to Forbes as cited by Wikipedia.
- Visiting: open daily 11:00 to 22:00; entry is by online registration (ges-2.org; Moscow city website, 4 December 2021).

FUNDING
GES-2 House of Culture: privately funded: the gas billionaire Leonid Mikhelson, through the V-A-C Foundation
- Mikhelson is chairman, CEO and major shareholder of the natural gas producer Novatek (Wikipedia); Forbes lists his source of wealth as "Gas, chemicals".
- Mavica put the cost at around $300 million in 2018, and Mikhelson said in 2019 "the frightening figure in my mind has doubled" (The Art Newspaper, Sophia Kishkovsky, 3 December 2021).
- Novatek subsidiaries partner on GES-2's Kostroma festival (The Art Newspaper, 19 October 2022).
- Putin toured GES-2 in December 2021 and returned in July 2022 for a technology forum (The Art Newspaper, 19 October 2022).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Mikhelson finances GES-2: Forbes says he spent more than $470 million turning the power plant into the art space, and The Art Newspaper (14 July 2025) says GES-2 was "financed by the gas magnate Leonid Mikhelson", who spent at least $300 million on the building.
- Mikhelson is the founder and chairman of the natural gas producer Novatek (Forbes); Wikipedia calls him its CEO and major shareholder.

Red flags: Sanctions
- UK: Mikhelson was added to the UK Sanctions List on 6 April 2022 under the Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019. The UK Financial Sanctions Notice of that date says he supports the Government of Russia as a director of Novatek and Sibur.
- Canada: Mikhelson is item 728 in Part 1 of Schedule 1 to the Special Economic Measures (Russia) Regulations, added by SOR/2022-74, registered 5 April 2022 (Canada Gazette, 27 April 2022).
- 21bis (4 May 2022) also states he is under UK and Canadian sanctions.

Overall: Art press coverage concerns the cost, the funder and the departures after the invasion of Ukraine.

URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/07/14/amid-cracks-down-on-dissent-russias-beleaguered-private-museums-fight-to-survive
- Names Mikhelson as financier of GES-2 and says he and Mavica gave Putin a personal tour of the space.
- Covers private museums under pressure: Garage, the Museum of Russian Impressionism and Erarta alongside GES-2.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In May 2022 the group Global Project, with more than 100 activists, occupied V-A-C's Palazzo Zattere in Venice under banners reading "Let's expropriate Russian oligarchs for peace and climate justice", and criticised the EU for not sanctioning Mikhelson (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris and José da Silva, 19 May 2022). V-A-C suspended all activities at Palazzo Zattere; GES-2 stayed open (21bis, 4 May 2022).
- The occupation targeted Mikhelson at V-A-C's Venice building, not GES-2. The connection: V-A-C is the foundation Mikhelson co-founded in 2009, and GES-2 is its Moscow venue.

BOTTOM LINE
GES-2 is a private centre funded by Leonid Mikhelson, founder and chairman of the gas producer Novatek, who has been on the UK sanctions list since 6 April 2022 and on Canada's Schedule 1 since April 2022. Forbes puts his spending on the building at more than $470 million.
