PALACIO LIBERTAD: Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: palacio-libertad-buenos-aires
Website: https://palaciolibertad.gob.ar/ (checked 2026-11-20; answers automated requests with HTTP 403)

OVERVIEW
- National cultural centre in the former Central Post Office at Sarmiento 151, central Buenos Aires; its full name is Palacio Libertad, Centro Cultural Domingo Faustino Sarmiento (Wikipedia).
- Norbert Maillart was commissioned to design the building in 1889. It opened on 28 September 1928, became a National Historic Monument in 1997, and its last postal bureau closed in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- Néstor Kirchner proposed the conversion in June 2005. A team led by the siblings Enrique, Federico and Nicolás Bares designed it, and it opened in May 2015 as the Centro Cultural Kirchner (Wikipedia).
- Renamed: the Milei government announced a change in March 2024, revealed the name in May 2024 and made it official by decree 897/24 of 10 October 2024 (Wikipedia). In mid-April 2024 Culture Secretary Leonardo Cifelli ordered a plaque naming Cristina Fernández de Kirchner covered, and by 7 May 2024 crews were removing Kirchner-name signs (La Nación, Diego Cabot, 7 May 2024).
- On site: the 1,950-seat Sala Sinfónica with a Klais organ of about 8,000 pipes, where the National Symphony Orchestra is resident, five other auditoriums and 40 gallery rooms, on 88,050 square metres (Wikipedia).
- Valeria Ambrosio is director, and the centre hosts 180 to 300 activities a month (argentina.gob.ar, 19 June 2026). Cifelli counted more than 1.5 million visitors and more than 680 artistic projects in 2025 (argentina.gob.ar, 23 December 2025).
- Visiting: admission was free at the time of Wikipedia's sources.

FUNDING
Palacio Libertad: state: the Argentine federal government through the Secretaría de Cultura
- Operator: the centre is a deconcentrated body under the Subsecretaría de Gestión y Desarrollo Cultural of the Secretaría de Cultura (Ámbito, 17 October 2024).
- Conversion: the works cost 3,811 million pesos, about US$700 million at the 2015 opening, and the construction firm was Esuco (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Friends' association: on 16 December 2025 Cifelli presided over the creation of the Asociación de Amigos del Palacio Libertad, a non-profit chaired by Martín Cabrales with Gabriel Martino as treasurer. It is to support programming, heritage and the building and to seek public and private alliances (argentina.gob.ar).
- Concessions: in November 2024 the cabinet office announced a five-year tender, run by the state property agency AABE, for three food and event spaces in the building, the largest a 705 square metre panoramic restaurant (El Cronista, 7 November 2024).

Red flags: Removal of works from the fileteadores show, September 2025
- Hours before the opening of the 14th Encuentro Anual de Fileteadores, with 160 works by 152 artists, the Asociación de Fileteadores called the show off and denounced censorship. It said Palacio authorities had questioned four works, three supporting Palestine and one defending public education (La Nación, Daniel Gigena, 11 September 2025).
- Curator Ayelén Fagiani said authorities told her the works "herían la susceptibilidad del Gobierno" (translation: hurt the government's sensitivities).
- Secretaría de Cultura sources replied that no national cultural venue would host political expression, and that cancelling was the association's decision, not the Secretaría's or the Palacio's.
- The association said the show would go ahead elsewhere with every work in it (Izquierda Diario, 10 September 2025).

Red flags: Cancellation of the Indigenous art biennial shows, February 2026
- The Secretaría de Cultura took the show "Desde el Corazón del Gran Chaco" off the programme of room 606 two days before installation, after six months of work with the Palacio. Organiser Mercedes Avellaneda de Bocca said Palacio staff cited "órdenes de arriba" (orders from above) and called it "un acto de discriminación y censura" (Tiempo Argentino, Patricio Féminis, 23 February 2026).
- Authorities dropped the Biennial itself close to its date, citing "un cambio de programación" (a change of programming). They had asked that the word "indígena", a curatorial text and the organising network's logos not appear (Perfil, Laura Isola, 22 March 2026).
- The Biennial opened at the Pabellón de las Bellas Artes of the Universidad Católica Argentina and the Chaco show at Vitriol, both until 12 April (Perfil).

Red flags: Dismissal of staff and closure, 2016
- After the Macri government took office, 85% of the workforce was dismissed. In January 2016 about 450 employees were barred from the building and the centre stayed closed until a concert on 4 May 2016; the dismissals met protests (Spanish Wikipedia).

Red flags: Claim of overpriced equipment, 2016
- Unverified: Hernán Lombardi, head of the Macri government's public media system, said the previous government paid 620 million pesos for equipment his team valued at no more than 150 million. He called the results provisional and said he would send them to the Oficina Anticorrupción (El Cronista, 21 May 2016). Lombardi served the government that replaced the one he accused.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Palacio Libertad is the Argentine federal government's, now joined by a friends' association and food concessions opened to private operators. The weak points are two exhibitions that organisers say officials forced out in September 2025 and February 2026, the first disputed by the Secretaría, under the same state body that runs the centre. Watch who gives through the friends' association and whether the Secretaría publishes programming criteria.
