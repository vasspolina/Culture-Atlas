LUSAKA CONTEMPORARY ART CENTRE (LUCAC): Lusaka, Zambia
ID: lusaka-contemporary-art-centre-lucac
Website: none found (no official site turned up in searches on 2026-10-25; www.lucac.org did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Private foundation and art centre in New Chamba Valley, Lusaka, with a gallery, a research library, an artist residency, a social room for talks and recordings, and two private spaces for visiting artists (Art Africa, January 2023; Artdependence, January 2023).
- Founded by Victor Mutelekesha, a Zambian artist based in Oslo; he conceived the idea in December 2002 during a visit in Amsterdam, and the centre opened on 6 January 2023 (Nkwazi Magazine). It describes itself as Zambia's first contemporary art centre of its kind (Kunstkritikk, 2020).
- Named for the city of Lusaka; LuCAC is its abbreviation.
- Building: Mutelekesha bought the Lusaka plot with his own funds in the early 2000s; main building construction began in March 2020 (Kunstkritikk, 2020) and took three years, delayed by COVID-19 (Nkwazi Magazine). Architect not named in the sources read.
- What is there: the gallery, library, residency, a plan for workshops and seminars and an archive of its own activities. The inaugural exhibition "Prospice, Kwacha!", curated by Karen Reini, ran from 6 January to 17 December 2023 with Banji Chona, Sana Ginwalla, Daut Makala and Germain Ngoma (Art Africa, Nkwazi Magazine).
- Visiting: Nkwazi Magazine (2023) gives hours of Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 17:00 and Sunday 12:00 to 16:00; no admission or accessibility information found.

FUNDING
LuCAC: self-funded: the founder's household, with small grants and gifts
- Mutelekesha and his wife, Mercy, fully funded the building's construction (Nkwazi Magazine). Kunstkritikk (October 2020) said the project had been self-financed until then.
- Pro Helvetia, a Swiss cultural fund, helped pay for the shelves and cabinets (Nkwazi Magazine).
- OCA, a Norwegian arts organisation, partly financed the inaugural exhibition (Nkwazi Magazine). OCA now says it has launched a residency in partnership with LuCAC (OCA).
- The late artist Jan Groth made a significant donation (Nkwazi Magazine).
- Kunstkritikk (2020): Mutelekesha formed an international board of fifteen within two weeks on advice from Pietro Rigolo, and was approaching the City of Oslo for support; no reporting found of a grant from the City.
- No reporting found of ongoing funding since 2023, annual accounts, a board list or a donor list.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the founder's own, with small named grants from a Swiss fund and a Norwegian arts body; nothing found ties it to a flagged funder. The weak points are that one household carries the building and that no accounts, current funder list or board list is published. Worth watching: how running costs are met now that the 2023 opening show has ended.
