MUSÉE MOHAMMED VI D'ART MODERNE ET CONTEMPORAIN (MMVI): Rabat, Morocco
ID: mus-e-mohammed-vi-d-art-moderne-et-contemporain-mmvi-rabat

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art at 2 Avenue Moulay Hassan in Rabat, described as the first museum in Morocco devoted entirely to modern and contemporary art (Fondation Nationale des Musées).
- Founded on the initiative of King Mohammed VI; it opened in October 2014 with the exhibition "1914 to 2014: 100 Years of Creation" (Wikipedia, Yabiladi).
- Building: 6,813 m2 over three floors at the corner of avenues Moulay El Hassan and Allal Ben Abdellah, designed by architect Karim Chakor, who drew on the monumental administrative buildings of the French protectorate (Bladi, Fouad Boumnadel, 22 July 2014).
- The museum is run by the Fondation Nationale des Musées (FNM). Le360 (2018) names Mehdi Qotbi as head of the FNM and Abdelaziz Idrissi as the museum's director.
- The collection holds more than 500 paintings and about ten sculptures, built by purchase, donation and long-term loan (FNM). The museum hosts yearly international temporary exhibitions, with past shows on Picasso, Giacometti, Goya and Delacroix (FNM).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 18:00, closed Tuesdays. Admission is 30 DH for Moroccan adults, 15 DH for Moroccans under 18 and 60 DH for foreign visitors (FNM).

FUNDING
Musée Mohammed VI d'Art Moderne et Contemporain: building paid by the Fonds Hassan II and the Ministry of Culture
- Construction cost more than 200 million dirhams, financed by the Fonds Hassan II pour le développement économique et social; the Ministry of Culture, as project owner, contributed 44 million dirhams (Bladi, Fouad Boumnadel, "Ouverture du musée national d'art contemporain à Rabat", 22 July 2014).
- The museum is run by the Fondation Nationale des Musées, which operates fourteen museums of the national network (FNM, Wikipedia).

Red flags: state censorship allegation at the 2014 opening
- Unverified: artist Mounir Fatmi, in a Facebook post reported by Yabiladi (Ghita Ismaili, 21 October 2014), said his installation "Face au Silence", a homage to Mehdi Ben Barka, was not shown at the opening of "100 ans de création" on 7 October 2014. Citing visitors, he said the museum management excluded political works and works about the body or nudity.
- The museum told Yabiladi the work was never censored, had been on display since the public opening on 9 October 2014, with photo evidence, and that the video could not be projected on inauguration day because of a technical problem fixed the next day.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. Moroccan press covered a censorship complaint in 2014 and criticised the programming in 2018.
1. Le360: Qods Chabaa, "Polémique autour d'une exposition bâclée au musée Mohammed VI" (16 October 2018)
URL: https://fr.le360.ma/culture/polemique-autour-dune-exposition-baclee-au-musee-mohammed-vi-176588/
- Reports that Hossein Talal, son of painter Chaïbia Talal, was told the show would be a solo tribute to his mother and learned of the group format from the press.
- The piece questions the show's scientific basis and accuses Mehdi Qotbi, head of the FNM, of prioritising costly international exhibitions over support for Moroccan artists.
- Museum director Abdelaziz Idrissi answered that several people were consulted, no contract was signed, and the group format placed Chaïbia's work in the context of the 1960s.
2. Yabiladi: Ghita Ismaili, "Une oeuvre de Mounir Fatmi censurée ? Le Musée Mohammed VI de Rabat réfute" (21 October 2014)
URL: https://www.yabiladi.com/articles/details/30339/oeuvre-mounir-fatmi-censuree-musee.html
- Reports Fatmi's complaint and the museum's denial, without verifying either side.

BOTTOM LINE
The building was paid for by the Fonds Hassan II (more than 200 million dirhams) and the Ministry of Culture (44 million dirhams), and the FNM runs the museum. The one documented dispute is Mounir Fatmi's 2014 censorship claim, which the museum denied.
