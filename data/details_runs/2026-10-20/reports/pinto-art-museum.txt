PINTO ART MUSEUM: Antipolo, Philippines
ID: pinto-art-museum
Website: https://pintoartmuseum.ph/ (checked 2026-10-25; loaded)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 1 Sierra Madre St., Grand Heights Rd, Antipolo, Rizal, outside Manila; it sits in the Silangan Gardens, a botanical garden of two hectares (pintoartmuseum.ph).
- Founded in 2010 to show the collection of the neurologist and arts patron Dr. Joven Cuanang; Wikipedia says the Pinto Gallery opened in 2000 and became a museum in 2010.
- "Pintô" means "door" in Filipino (pintoartmuseum.ph).
- Building and site: Wikipedia says the complex has six buildings on 12,000 square metres, designed by Tony Leaño (Antonio Leaño), an artist from the Saling Pusa collective, in an organic, eclectic style, with open-air ventilation instead of air conditioning.
- On site: seven galleries, the seventh opened to the public on 2 January 2020 (pintoartmuseum.ph); an adjacent gallery promoting contemporary Filipino artists, an academy for literature, visual and performing arts, a cafe and an arboretum (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00; tickets are pre-registered online (pintoartmuseum.ph). No reporting found of current admission prices or accessibility on the pages read.

FUNDING
Pinto Art Museum: privately owned: founder Joven Cuanang, with ticket and art sales
- Wikipedia says the museum is run by the Silangan Foundation for the Arts, Culture, and Ecology, which Cuanang co-established as the Antipolo Foundation for Arts, Culture, and Ecology after the 1986 People Power Revolution. The museum's own site does not name the foundation.
- The museum's site says proceeds from art sales at its "Sampayan at Pintô" exhibition go to a scholarship programme for arts and sciences (stated by the museum).
- Unverified: the museum takes a 50 percent commission on artworks it sells, according to former curator Riel Jaramillo Hilario, quoted in Lifestyle Asia (9 April 2021). He is a named former curator; the article says the museum had issued no official statement.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, state funder, donor list, annual report or accounts. No reporting found of how Cuanang's wealth is sourced beyond Wikipedia's note that he is a former chief medical officer at St. Luke's Medical Center, Global City.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is one patron's, run through a foundation, and no flagged-industry funder was found. The weak points are that no accounts, donor list or board are published, and that former curator Riel Hilario made unverified accusations in 2021 of poor care of works and an unfair sales commission, with no reply on record. Worth watching whether the museum answers the commission and conservation claims.
