PORTLAND ART MUSEUM: Portland, USA
ID: portland-art-museum-portland
Website: https://portlandartmuseum.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1219 SW Park Avenue in downtown Portland, Oregon, along the South Park Blocks (English Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1892 when seven business and cultural leaders incorporated the Portland Art Association; Henry Corbett's $10,000 gift paid for the first collection, 100 plaster casts of Greek and Roman sculpture (English Wikipedia).
- The 1932 main building, the Hirsch Wing (1939) and the Hoffman Wing (1970) were designed by Pietro Belluschi (English Wikipedia).
- The Rothko Pavilion, a glass-walled structure by Vinci Hamp Architects linking the Main and Mark buildings, opened on 20 November 2025. It is named for the artist, not a donor, and Mark Rothko's children Christopher Rothko and Kate Rothko Prizel agreed to lend paintings to the museum for 20 years (Hyperallergic, Jillian Steinhauer, 6 October 2016; English Wikipedia).
- Brian Ferriso was appointed director in 2006 and left in November 2025; Michael J. Anderson replaced him in 2026 (English Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the museum's site lists open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; adults $27.50, seniors 65 and over $24.50, students $22.50, under 18s and members free; free admission on the first Thursday of each month, with hours to 19:00.

FUNDING
Portland Art Museum: donor-funded: foundations, individual donors and trustees
- Finances: the fiscal year to June 2025 shows revenue of $31.3 million, of which contributions were $22.6 million (72.2 percent) and program services $5.0 million (16.0 percent); expenses were $27.7 million and net assets $229.6 million (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, filing of 4 March 2026).
- Major sponsors named on the museum's corporate-giving page: Blue Balloon Fund, Mary and Don Blair, Mary and Tim Boyle, William G. Gilmore Foundation, Mary and Pete Mark Family Foundation, The Meier Family Fund, The James F. and Marion L. Miller Foundation, and one anonymous sponsor.
- Capital campaign: the museum announced on 16 October 2025 that it had raised $116 million for the campus transformation, 98 percent from private sources, from more than 1,000 individual donors. Lead gifts: $13.5 million from Arlene Schnitzer, $8 million from an anonymous donor, and $5 million each from Mary and Tim Boyle, Trudy and Pat Ritz, Nani S. Warren, Helen Jo and Bill Whitsell and another anonymous donor.
- Endowment: a separate $30 million endowment campaign had reached $28.4 million by October 2025 (museum announcement).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on two decisions, not on the museum's funding: the 2023 end of the volunteer docent council and the 2017 plan for the Rothko Pavilion over a public passageway.
1. ARTnews: Francesca Aton, "Portland Art Museum Disbands Its Docent Program, Sparking Controversy" (6 October 2023)
URL: https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/portland-art-museum-disbands-its-docent-program-sparking-controversy-1234681370/
- On 22 August the museum emailed its 175 docents under the subject line "Future Directions", ending the 40-year-old docent council and recruiting paid learning guides from Portland Community College, Portland State University and Lewis & Clark College.
- Former docent Paulla Dacklin said the message was that "the services of a group of older, retired educators are no longer needed." Ferriso said the council was "evolving to meet the needs of the community."
- Paywalled; only a reposted copy was read.
2. Willamette Week: Rachel Saslow, "The Portland Art Museum Ditches Its Docents, Raising Questions About Race, Class and Especially Age" (4 October 2023, local press)
URL: https://www.wweek.com/news/2023/10/04/the-portland-art-museum-ditches-its-docents-raising-auestions-about-race-class-and-especially-age/
- Reports the same 22 August email to 175 docents, which said the museum had received feedback that the word "docent" was "confusing and unclear."
- Asks whether reflecting the communities the museum serves means reducing the role of senior white women, who made up a large share of the council; Ferriso said "I apologize if people heard it that way."
3. OPB: Aaron Scott, "Portland Art Museum Adapts Expansion Plan To Win Over Critics" (2 December 2017, local press)
URL: https://opb.org/radio/programs/state-of-wonder/article/portland-art-museum-rothko-pavilion-accessibility
- Pedestrian, bicycle and disability rights advocates said the pavilion would block the public passageway between the two buildings; city disability policy coordinator Nickole Cheron said a three block detour would be a hardship for some people.
- The museum revised the plan to keep the pavilion open during the plaza's hours, 7:00 to 23:00; critics said this did not go far enough.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is donor contributions, 72.2 percent of revenue in the year to June 2025 (ProPublica): foundations, the Boyle, Ritz, Whitsell and Jubitz families and anonymous donors, with 98 percent of the $116 million capital campaign from private sources (museum announcement, 16 October 2025). The documented criticism concerns the end of the docent council in 2023 and the 2017 pavilion design.
