ISHARA ART FOUNDATION: Dubai, UAE
ID: ishara-art-foundation-dubai
Website: https://www.ishara.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art foundation for South Asian contemporary art at 3 Alserkal Avenue, Street 17, Al Quoz 1, Dubai, in a two-storey warehouse space (Ishara; The National, 2019).
- Founded by Smita Prabhakar, its chairperson (Ishara); it opened on 18 March 2019 (The National, 18 March 2019). ArtAsiaPacific (18 January 2019) called it the first non-profit institution in the UAE dedicated to art and artists from South Asia.
- No earlier name is given in the sources opened; the name is not explained in them.
- The space is modular and adapts to each show; Prabhakar said "We spent a fortune on lighting" (The National, 2024).
- Programme: an average of two shows a year, from the inaugural "Altered Inheritances: Home Is a Foreign Place" (Shilpa Gupta and Zarina, opened March 2019) to the group show "Sheher, Prakriti, Devi" (The National, 2024). The Ishara site names Sasha Altaf as director.
- Visiting: 10:00 to 19:00, Monday to Saturday (Ishara). No reporting found of an admission price or accessibility statement.

FUNDING
Ishara Art Foundation: self-funded: founder Smita Prabhakar, from her business interests
- The National (Razmig Bedirian, 8 March 2024) calls the foundation a non-profit and quotes Prabhakar saying it is "completely self-funded" and crediting her team with "world-class shows on a limited budget".
- The same article names her as director of the AMS International Group. Khaleej Times (6 May 2017) names her also as president of Lazer Capital, whose management team leads projects in infrastructure, oil and gas, agriculture, technology, power and utility, real estate and construction, and as CEO of Apsons International, a trading company. Neither Khaleej Times nor any source opened links Lazer Capital or Apsons to Ishara.
- Alserkal Avenue hosts the space; Abdelmonem Alserkal, founder of Alserkal Avenue, is honorary chairman of the trustees (Ishara; The National). No reporting found of rent terms or of any grant from Alserkal.
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts or any other named funder. No source opened shows a trustee's employer funding Ishara.
- Trustees (Ishara): Abdelmonem Alserkal (Honorary Chairman), Hoda Barakat, Mustafa Al Hashimi, Ahmed Bin Shabib, Rashid Bin Shabib and Abdulfattah Sharaf. Advisory board: Richard Armstrong (chairman), Princess Alia Al-Senussi, Bharti Kher, Chittrovanu Mazumdar, Shaikha Maisa Al Qassimi, Maya Allison, Sandhini Poddar, Smita Prabhakar, Sasha Altaf and Sabih Ahmed.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is launch reporting and a 2024 profile, based on the founder's and director's own statements.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Trustee Abdulfattah Sharaf: the Ishara board page lists him as "Chairman, HSBC Bank Middle East Limited". Gulf News (page last updated 27 April 2023) reported his appointment as chairman of HSBC Bank Middle East from 1 May.
- On 11 December 2012 HSBC reached a deferred-prosecution agreement with the US Department of Justice in a money-laundering case and agreed to pay $1.92 billion, with an independent monitor under a five-year agreement (Fox Business). Neither article mentions Ishara or ties this agreement to Sharaf. His seat is a board seat; no reporting found that HSBC funds Ishara.
- Trustee Mustafa Al Hashimi: the Ishara board page lists him as "Chief Executive Officer, Wasl Hospitality and Leisure". Wikipedia describes Wasl as a semi-government company established on 25 May 2008 by the Dubai Real Estate Corporation and lists Wasl Hospitality & Leisure among its subsidiaries. No reporting found of money from Wasl or the Dubai government to Ishara.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Ishara is stated by the founder to be her own, and the sources opened show no outside funder. The weak points are that no accounts are published and that her other companies are named but not tied to the foundation. Two trustees hold senior posts at HSBC Middle East and a Dubai government-linked company, as board seats with no funding shown; worth watching is whether any trustee's organisation begins to pay for programmes.
