KARACHI BIENNALE TRUST: Karachi, Pakistan
ID: karachi-biennale-trust-karachi
Website: https://karachibiennale.org.pk/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads; announces that the KB27 edition is cancelled)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit that stages the Karachi Biennale, a free citywide art event held in public venues in Karachi (KB22: eight public venues including heritage sites, free entry, per APP).
- Founded in 2016 as the Karachi Biennale Trust by a group of art professionals and educators; Niilofur Farrukh is listed as co-founder and was CEO through 2025 (the Trust's founders page). The first edition, KB17, was curated by trustee Amin Gulgee.
- Editions on the Trust's site: KB17, KB19 (theme "Ecology and the Environment"), KB22 (art and technology, curator Faisal Anwar, Pakistan's 75th anniversary) and KB24. The site calls KB27 the fifth edition and says the Trust cancelled it, citing "the war conditions prevailing in the region" (stated by the Trust).
- No reporting found of a permanent site or collection; the Trust's site lists only editions.
- Ten trustees are listed, led by Shanaz Ramzi (Managing Trustee) and Atteqa Malik (Chairperson); Niilofur Farrukh and Amin Gulgee are among them.
- No reporting found of an annual report or accounts.

FUNDING
Karachi Biennale Trust: corporate-sponsored: Pakistani companies, the Sindh culture department and foreign cultural institutes
- KB22: Engro Corporation was lead partner and contributed Rs. 75 million (Associated Press of Pakistan, June 2022).
- KB24 sponsors by tier on the Trust's site: Gold, Bank Alfalah, HBL and Neo Color; Bronze, JS Bank, Image, Ali & Associates, Saga Foundation, Jubilee, Munir and Maliha Bhimjee; Energy Partner, K-Electric; and single-partner roles for HUM News, Signify, TCS and others.
- The cancelled KB27 page thanks National Foods, EBM, Bank Alfalah, JS Bank, Khaadi and others, with the Culture Department of the Government of Sindh, British Council, Goethe-Institut and Pro Helvetia as institutional supporters (stated by the Trust).
- No reporting found of a figure for any sponsor other than Engro, or of total budget.

Red flags: fossil fuels, Engro and K-Electric
- Engro: lead partner of KB22 (Rs. 75 million). Wikipedia lists the Thar Engro plant as a 660 MW lignite plant owned by Engro Powergen Thar Ltd, fed by an open-cast mine run by Sindh Engro Coal Mining Company, a joint venture of the Sindh government and Engro Corporation.
- K-Electric: Energy Partner of KB24. Wikipedia lists its plants as steam, combined-cycle and gas-fired units at Bin Qasim and Korangi.
- In 2019 Pakistan's regulator NEPRA held K-Electric responsible for 19 of 35 electrocution deaths during the July and August rains and fined it Rs 50 million (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2019 censorship at KB19
- On 27 October 2019 plainclothes men ordered Adeela Suleman's "Killing Fields of Karachi", 444 tombstone-like sculptures for the victims of killings by former police officer Rao Anwar, closed; part was sealed and the rest vandalised (Global Voices).
- The organisers said the work, "despite the artist's perspective", was not compatible with the ethos of KB19 and said they were against censorship (Dawn, 28 October 2019).
- Unverified: that the men came from a Pakistani intelligence agency, as they said themselves (Global Voices); Dawn named no agency or person.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2019 closure and the organisers' statement. No reporting found of art-press criticism aimed at the sponsors Engro or K-Electric.
1. Global Voices: Quratulain Tejani, "Pakistan shuts art installation depicting extrajudicial killings" (2019)
URL: https://globalvoices.org/2019/10/30/pakistan-shuts-art-installation-depicting-extrajudicial-killings/
- Reports that organisers backed the closure in a statement to Dawn, and records the response: Asad Hashim accused them of endorsing censorship; lawyer Mohammad Jibran Nasir held a press conference at the venue; Brad Adams of Human Rights Watch called the closure an attack on free expression.
- Names the Biennale organisers; not a review, a news report with named critics.
2. Dawn: Nimra Khan, "Discourse: Censorship in Art" (2020)
URL: https://www.dawn.com/news/1563415/discourse-censorship-in-art
- Argues censorship in Pakistani art breeds self-censorship and says art institutions must stand up to oppressive systems.
- Quotes Niilofur Farrukh, as Trust CEO, saying before KB19 that the Biennale's guidelines came from "what the public wants" and that inclusion, not censorship, was the issue. Gives no organiser response to the 2019 closure and dates the destruction to November 2019, which conflicts with Global Voices.
3. Dawn: report on the organisers' statement (2019)
URL: https://www.dawn.com/news/1513412/sealed-exhibit-on-extrajudicial-killings-not-compatible-with-ethos-of-karachi-biennale-say-organisers
- Records the statement and criticism of it from Aiman Rizvi and Reem Khurshid, who called it "craven". A news report.

BOTTOM LINE
The Biennale runs on corporate and institutional sponsorship with no money from a named founder or family found. The weak points are the 2019 censorship at KB19, where the organisers backed the closure, and two fossil-fuel sponsors: Engro (KB22) and K-Electric (KB24). Worth watching: whether the Trust publishes accounts and how it re-launches after cancelling KB27.
