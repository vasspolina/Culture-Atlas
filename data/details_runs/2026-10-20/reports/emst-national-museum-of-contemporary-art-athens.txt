EMST NATIONAL MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Athens, Greece
ID: emst-national-museum-of-contemporary-art-athens
Website: https://www.emst.gr

OVERVIEW
- State contemporary art museum in the former FIX brewery at Kallirrois Avenue and Amvrosiou Frantzi Street, Athens, under the Hellenic Ministry of Culture.
- Established in October 2000 with Anna Kafetsi as founding director; directors since: Katerina Koskina (2014 to 2018), interim directors Dimitris Antonakakis and Syrago Tsiara (2019 to 2021), and Katerina Gregos (from 2021) (Wikipedia).
- Building: a 50-year lease for the former FIX brewery was signed in 2002 (Apollo, William Summerfield, 19 March 2020). 3SK Stylianidis Architects won the international competition to complete its renovation (Wikipedia). Apollo says only a fraction of the space has been operational since 2015, after "a period of funding shortfalls, administrative problems and difficulties in selecting a permanent director".
- The museum reopened at the end of February 2020 with "EMST Open" (Apollo, William Summerfield, 19 March 2020).
- In 2022 it received 140 contemporary art works through the D. Daskalopoulos Collection Gift (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 19:00, Thursday to 22:00, closed Monday. General admission €10, reduced €5; free on the first Thursday of each month from 18:00 (except July and August), and for children 12 and under. All exhibition areas are wheelchair accessible and elevators serve every floor.

FUNDING
EMST National Museum of Contemporary Art: state-funded: the Greek state, with a Stavros Niarchos Foundation grant
- State: Apollo (Fatema Ahmed, 1 June 2026) writes that EMST is entirely state-funded but keeps the income it generates, and that its funding is set annually. The board is appointed by the Ministry of Culture, and the museum operates at arm's length.
- Wikipedia states the Greek government had spent €40 million on the museum by 2020 and that delays in opening cost it a US$3.3 million EU subsidy.
- Stavros Niarchos Foundation (SNF): Apollo (William Summerfield, 19 March 2020) says the reopening came "largely thanks to an additional €3m grant from the Stavros Niarchos Foundation". SNF's grant page records a 2018 grant that supports the purchase of equipment and completion of construction works so that EMST can show its permanent collections in its new building.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critical material concerns the ministry's handling of the museum's directors and the long delay in opening.
1. Apollo: Fatema Ahmed, "At EMST in Athens, all systems are go" (1 June 2026)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=1043910
- Recounts that the culture ministry fired Anna Kafetsi a year before the museum returned to its site in May 2015, and Katerina Koskina in 2018, and that the call for applications for a successor was cancelled.
- Favourable about Gregos's tenure, calling EMST "one of the most interesting and politically engaged museums of contemporary art in Europe".
2. Apollo: William Summerfield, "The brief end to the long wait for the National Museum of Contemporary Art in Athens" (19 March 2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/national-museum-contemporary-art-athens-emst/
- The museum had become "a symbol of the economic uncertainty of modern Greece and the general lack of state support" for contemporary art.
- Says the work of Greek artists is not widely known internationally; the library, café and restaurant were not yet open at the reopening. At the opening press conference the culture minister Lina Mendoni promised to fund new purchases.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind EMST is public: the Greek state funds the museum through the Ministry of Culture, which also appoints its board, and the Stavros Niarchos Foundation gave €3 million toward the 2020 opening. The documented problems are the ministry's dismissals of two directors and the long delay in opening the building.
