CHISENHALE GALLERY: London, UK
ID: chisenhale-gallery
Website: https://chisenhale.org.uk (checked 2026-10-20; down: the connection was refused and timed out on repeated tries)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art gallery on the ground floor of a former veneer factory on Chisenhale Road, Tower Hamlets, near Victoria Park and the Hertford Union Canal in east London (Wikipedia).
- Its roots go to 1980, when artists evicted from Thames-side studios leased the building from Tower Hamlets Council as Arts Place Trust; the gallery was established in December 1986 and became an independent charity in 1993 (Wikipedia, Charity Commission data).
- The building dates from 1942 to 1943; its last factory operator, Morris Cohen's CHN Veneers, made veneer and, in the Second World War, cockpit veneers and propellers for RAF aircraft. The factory closed in 1972 (Wikipedia).
- Chisenhale Art Place Trust holds the lease from Tower Hamlets council until 2031, with a break clause in 2028; the gallery holds a sub-lease renewed in August 2023, and the council has made no commitment to renew (2025 accounts).
- It commissions new work, often an artist's first UK institutional solo show; Rachel Whiteread, Helen Marten, Lubaina Himid and Lawrence Abu Hamdan showed there before winning the Turner Prize (Wikipedia).
- Edward Gillman became director in March 2025, after Zoé Whitley stepped down in February 2025; May Calil chairs the trustees (2025 accounts, Charity Commission data).
- Visiting: exhibitions are free (Wikipedia). No reporting found of current opening days or access details, since the gallery's own site was down when checked.

FUNDING
Chisenhale Gallery: mixed: Arts Council England core grant plus trusts, foundations and individual patrons
- Income was £999,221 in the year to 31 March 2025 and £1,251,438 the year before (Charity Commission data via OpenCharities).
- Arts Council England, its core funder as a National Portfolio Organisation, gave £168,421 in 2023-24 and made up 17% of income in 2024-25 (2024 and 2025 accounts).
- Bloomberg gave £120,000 in 2023-24 for a digital accelerator programme upgrading hardware, software and digital practice, to be spent over three years and concluded by March 2025; £95,000 was spent in 2023-24 and £25,000 carried forward (2024 accounts). CultureSource lists Chisenhale as a UK participant in the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator.
- Other 2023-24 grants: Asymmetry Art Foundation £74,980, Deborah Roberts £56,059, Ford Foundation £40,430, Teiger Foundation £31,656, Lotus L. Kang Foundation £29,166, Freelands Foundation £25,600, Terra Foundation £25,000, A4 Arts Foundation £23,999, Fondation Pernod Ricard £16,745, Mondriaan Fund £12,184 (2024 accounts).
- The Friends and Patrons programme and the Futures Fund raised £174,240 of unrestricted income in 2024-25, against £183,969 the year before (2025 accounts).
- In 2016 The Spectator reported that trustee Shane Akeroyd, a senior staff member of Markit, provided "a good portion" of the rest of the funding for one show; the 2024 accounts record him as resigning on 22 July 2024.
- Bloomberg paid Chisenhale £120,000 in 2023-24 for a digital accelerator programme to be concluded by March 2025 (2024 accounts); CultureSource names Chisenhale in the Bloomberg Philanthropies Digital Accelerator.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little. One 2016 review attacked the gallery's handling of public money and private finance around one show; no open letter or protest aimed at Chisenhale was found.
1. The Spectator: Niru Ratnam, "Chisenhale Gallery's conceptual critique of neoliberalism is being funded by high finance" (2016)
URL: https://spectator.com/article/chisenhale-gallery-s-conceptual-critique-of-neoliberalism-is-being-funded-by-high-finance/
- Maria Eichhorn's "5 Weeks, 25 Days, 175 Hours" closed the gallery so staff could withdraw their labour; Ratnam calls it a conventional conceptual gesture with precedents in Buren and Barry.
- Argues the gallery receives around £165,000 a year of Arts Council money and that Tower Hamlets taxpayers are part-funding the show with the closed gallery; calls the gallery's reasoning about that money "creative accounting at its neoliberal best".
- Names trustee Shane Akeroyd, a senior staff member of Markit, "a firm who specialised in the field of credit default swaps", as the source of "a good portion" of the rest of the show's funding.
- Criticises both the art and the institution.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 28 May 2026, Artists for Palestine UK published an open letter signed by 261 artists asking five British theatres to end Bloomberg partnerships, citing Bloomberg funding for training officials who govern West Bank settlements at Tel Aviv University's Bloomberg-Sagol Centre (Morning Star). The letter does not name Chisenhale. It is listed here, not as a red flag: the campaign targets Bloomberg at other institutions.

BOTTOM LINE
Chisenhale runs on an Arts Council England grant, a long list of art foundations and its patrons. The weak points are the finished Bloomberg digital grant, Bloomberg being the target of a 2026 artists' letter, and an insecure council lease that runs only to 2028 or 2031. Worth watching: whether Bloomberg money returns and whether Tower Hamlets renews the lease.
