MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART CHICAGO (MCA) – Chicago, USA
Website: https://mcachicago.org (ok, checked 2026-10-20; the corporate-giving page could not be fetched in this run)

OVERVIEW
- Founded in 1967 by a group of collectors, dealers, artists, critics and architects who first met in 1964; collecting was added to its mission in 1974. Its current building, by German architect Josef Paul Kleihues, was completed in June 1996 (stated by the space).
- Open Wed–Sun 10am–5pm, Tue 10am–9pm, closed Mon. Suggested admission $19/$22 for adults, pay-what-you-can, free for under-18s, people with disabilities and their carers, veterans and others; Illinois residents free on Tuesday evenings; $3 tickets for EBT card holders (Museums for All). Free wheelchairs, accessible and all-gender restrooms, EnChroma glasses on loan (stated by the space).

FUNDING
MCA Chicago: mainly private philanthropy (individuals and foundations) plus investment income, with some corporate and government money.
- FY2025 (year to 30 June 2025): total revenue $28.8M, expenses $34.9M, net assets $174.1M; special events net $7.1M (2025 Art Auction); government grants $309k; investment gain $18.5M (2025 annual report, stated by the space).
- $100,000+ individual and foundation donors in FY2025 include the Bluhm Family Foundation, Dimitris Daskalopoulos, Kenneth C. Griffin, Ford Foundation, Mellon Foundation, Edlis Neeson Foundation, Cari and Michael Sacks, Karyn and Bill Silverstein and the Zell Family Foundation; MacArthur, Kovler and Terra foundations gave $50k–$99k (annual report).
- $100,000+ corporate donors: Chanel, Google, Hauser & Wirth, NBC Universal, Northern Trust; Max Mara gave $50k–$99k; Christie's, Sotheby's and Gagosian $25k+ (annual report).
- Government: Chicago Park District, Illinois Department of Commerce & Economic Opportunity, Institute of Museum and Library Services (each $100k+), Illinois Arts Council ($50k–$99k) (annual report).
- Earlier large gifts: $10M each from Stefan Edlis and Gael Neeson and from Sam and Helen Zell in 2012 (Hyperallergic); an anonymous $10M gift for performance in March 2025 (The Art Newspaper).
- A tax-filing aggregator lists the Leonard and Ruth Horwich Family Foundation ($22.3M), Fidelity Investments Charitable Gift Fund ($10.7M) and the Eli and Edythe Broad Foundation ($9.7M) as top funders (Impala, figures as given there).
- Board chair: Cari Sacks, succeeded by Bill Silverstein (2025 annual report).
- BMO Harris and Bank of America, named in the existing record, were not confirmed in the parts of the FY2025 donor list reviewed here.

Red flags: Labour and policing disputes (past)
- June 2020: after a campaign by its Teen Creative Agency, MCA agreed to stop contracting Chicago Police Department services (Hyperallergic).
- March 2021: artists withdrew from "The Long Dream" over the January 2021 layoff of more than 40 staff; the artists say 57 withdrew, while the museum counts six artists and one collective (Artnet).

Not related: Art Institute of Chicago — a separate museum. The May 2024 encampment arrests in the record's Block Club source took place at the Art Institute, not at the MCA.

BOTTOM LINE
The MCA is funded mainly by Chicago philanthropists, foundations and its endowment, and it publishes donors by tier in its annual reports. No funding-related red flags were found. Past labour and policing disputes (2020–21) are on record, and the record cites a source about a different museum.
