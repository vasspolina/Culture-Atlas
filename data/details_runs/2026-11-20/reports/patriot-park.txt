PATRIOT PARK: Kubinka, Russia
ID: patriot-park
Website: https://patriotp.ru (checked 2026-11-20; the domain did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Military theme park of Russia's armed forces in Kubinka, Moscow region, west of Moscow; it covers about 5,414 hectares, of which 3,530 are a military base and 1,884 a civilian area (Wikipedia).
- A pet project of defence minister Sergei Shoigu, designed to inspire patriotism in young Russians; critics call it Russia's "military Disneyland" (AP).
- Soft-opened during the Army-2015 forum and officially opened in 2016; it has hosted the "Army" international military-technical forum since 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Exhibits include hangars of Second World War armour, a mini-Reichstag that visitors can "storm", a partisan village of more than twenty dugouts, the Kubinka tank and air base museums and, since 2020, the Main Cathedral of the Russian Armed Forces (Wikipedia).
- Shoigu promised that young people would drive and fly military equipment as well as view it (The Moscow Times, 2015).

FUNDING
Patriot Park: state-funded: Russia's Defence Ministry
- Run as a federal state autonomous institution (Wikipedia).
- The Defence Ministry allocated over 20 billion roubles (about $363.5 million) for the park, including 7 billion roubles for the central exhibition and congress hall, according to tender data cited from Kommersant (The Moscow Times, 2015).
- The 8.3 billion rouble contract for the expo centre went to the state contractor Oboronstroi in a May 2015 tender in which no other firm bid (The Moscow Times, 2015).
- Plans included a hotel, offices and a residence for Shoigu, due in 2017 (The Moscow Times, 2015).

Red flags: embezzlement of park funds (convictions 2025 to 2026)
- On 5 August 2024 the Investigative Committee arrested the park's head, Vyacheslav Akhmedov, and Major General Vladimir Shesterov of the Defence Ministry's innovations department on suspicion of embezzling funds for the park (AP).
- The arrests followed the April 2024 jailing of Shoigu's deputy Timur Ivanov on bribery charges and Shoigu's removal as defence minister in May 2024 (AP).
- On 15 August 2025 the Odintsovsky City Court sentenced Akhmedov to five years for large-scale fraud and forgery over construction papers faked from 2022 to 2024; work worth nearly 26 million roubles on former deputy defence minister Pavel Popov's private property was billed as park work. Shesterov got six years in July 2025 (The Moscow Times).
- On 10 April 2026 the 235th garrison military court sentenced Popov to 19 years and an 85 million rouble fine, mainly for embezzling 25.8 million roubles of park funds; his lawyer said he would appeal (Meduza).

Red flags: 2023 display of captured Western weapons from Ukraine
- At the Army-2023 forum in the park in August 2023, Russia showed an American M777 howitzer, an Australian Bushmaster and a French AMX-10 captured in Ukraine, a drone bomb painted "to Berlin" and "to London", and a photo display on alleged "Ukrainian Nazism", next to stands where children assembled mock firearms (The Moscow Times).

Red flags: Canadian sanctions on the park (2024)
- Canada listed the Military-Patriotic Park of the Russian Armed Forces "Patriot" under its Special Economic Measures Act from 21 February 2024; Ukraine's defence intelligence also lists it among sponsors and accomplices of the war (OpenSanctions).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage comes from news reporting, which treats the park as Defence Ministry propaganda and, since 2024, as a corruption case.

BOTTOM LINE
Entirely Defence Ministry money, spent on war propaganda for the young and partly embezzled by the officials who ran it.
