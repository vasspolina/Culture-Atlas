ALTES MUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: altes-museum
Website: https://www.smb.museum/museen-einrichtungen/altes-museum/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of classical antiquity on the Lustgarten, Museum Island, Berlin; it houses the Antikensammlung of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin and part of the Münzkabinett (museum website).
- Karl Friedrich Schinkel's building went up from 1823 to 1830, with 18 fluted Ionic columns and a rotunda modelled on the Roman Pantheon; the foundation stone was laid on 9 July 1825 and the museum opened on 3 August 1830 as the first public museum in Berlin and Prussia (museum website).
- The inscription over the columns says Friedrich Wilhelm III founded the museum in 1828 for the study of antiquities and the liberal arts (museum website).
- The collection grew from the art holdings of the electors of Brandenburg and the Prussian kings; castle records of 1648 list antiquities still in Berlin, and the "Thesaurus Brandenburgicus" of 1701 documented many works (museum website).
- What is there: Greek, Etruscan and Roman art from the Aegean Bronze Age to the late Roman period, including vases, sculpture, bronzes, terracottas, gems, glass and ivory, with finds from Etruria, Cyprus and the northern Black Sea; the Greek and Roman sculpture moved here from the Pergamonmuseum in 2010 and 2011 (museum website).
- Current exhibition: "Grundstein Antike. Berlins erstes Museum", on the museum's beginnings, runs from 10 July 2025 to 10 January 2027 (museum website).
- Building condition: Marion Ackermann, president of the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz, told dpa in August 2026 that the building has cracks in its walls and needs a general renovation, now planned for the 2030s (Volksstimme, 9 August 2026; entwicklungsstadt, 9 August 2026).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 17:00, weekends 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays; 14 euros, reduced 7 euros; fully wheelchair accessible through the staff entrance on Am Lustgarten (museum website).

FUNDING
Altes Museum: public: the German federal government and the 16 states through the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz
- Operator: the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, part of the Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz (SPK); the Antikensammlung's holdings came through purchases, gifts and divisions of excavation finds with the host countries (museum website).
- Running costs: under the SPK's funding agreement the federal government pays 75 percent and the states 25 percent of the publicly funded operating budget, on a base of 120 million euros; needs above that are split 75 percent federal and 25 percent State of Berlin (SPK).
- New agreement: signed on 12 March 2025 and in force since 1 January 2026, it raises the annual contributions by 12 million euros, 9 million of them federal; the Berlin Senate said reviews by the Wissenschaftsrat since 2018 had found the SPK seriously underfunded (SPK; Berlin Senate press release, 2025).
- Building works: construction and major renovation were first split equally between the federal government and Berlin; since 2003 the federal government alone has paid the SPK's construction budget (SPK).
- Friends: the Freunde der Antike auf der Museumsinsel e.V., with nearly 800 members, supports the Antikensammlung and the Vorderasiatisches Museum; it traces back to a society founded in February 1913 by the collection's director Theodor Wiegand (museum website).
- The current exhibition is supported by the Freunde der Antike and by Museum & Location, the Staatliche Museen's events company (museum website).

Red flags: Red Army seizure of the collection in 1945
- During the Second World War objects were moved to bunkers; all of the Antikensammlung's art objects in Berlin at the end of the war were seized by the Red Army and taken to Moscow or Leningrad (museum website, Antikensammlung page).
- In 1958 the Soviet Union returned one and a half million works of art to the GDR, including the Pergamon Altar frieze panels (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: German federal and state money through the SPK. Worth watching is the cost and timing of the overdue renovation.
