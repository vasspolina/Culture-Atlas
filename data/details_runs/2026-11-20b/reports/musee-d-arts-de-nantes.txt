MUSÉE D'ARTS DE NANTES: Nantes, France
ID: musee-d-arts-de-nantes
Website: https://museedartsdenantes.nantesmetropole.fr/home.html (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Fine arts museum at 10 rue Georges Clemenceau in central Nantes, formerly the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Nantes; a musée de France since 5 January 2002 (Museofile record; Le Voyage à Nantes).
- Founding: the Museofile record dates the consular order creating the museum to 1800; Apollo dates the Chaptal decree to 1801, and Le Voyage à Nantes also gives 1801. The City bought the Cacault collection in 1810, the works went on show in the Halle aux toiles in 1830, and a new palace by the Nantes-born architect Clément-Marie Josso opened in 1900 (Museofile record; Apollo, 26 June 2017).
- The seventeenth-century Chapelle de l'Oratoire was restored and given to the museum in 1988 (Apollo).
- Stanton Williams won the 2009 competition for a renovation and extension; the museum closed for six years and reopened in June 2017 with 30 percent more exhibition space, a new building called the Cube for contemporary art, an underground auditorium, a library and documentation centre, a print room and an archive building (Ministry of Culture; Apollo; The Spaces, 22 June 2017).
- The first tender, in separate lots, came in well over budget on the main lots; soundings in 2011 and 2012 found underground water veins, the second basement was dropped, and Nantes Métropole re-tendered the contract as one general contract in September 2013 (Fédération française du bâtiment, BAM, March 2020).
- Collection: from Italian primitives to contemporary art, with about 900 works on show; during the works about 150 were restored and 185 acquired (Ministry of Culture). About half the collection dates from after 1900 (Apollo). The Spaces gives the whole museum 17,000 square metres. Figures for the Cube differ: 2,000 square metres in the Ministry of Culture, AFP via Weka, Apollo and Le Voyage à Nantes; 4,000 square metres in The Spaces.
- Director: Emmanuelle Delapierre since 1 June 2025, formerly director of the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Caen; she succeeded Sophie Lévy, who became head of Le Voyage à Nantes on 1 January 2025 (Nantes Métropole).
- The museum had 297,520 visitors in 2025, with the exhibitions "Paquebots 1913-1942", "Electric Op" and, from 5 November 2025, "Sous la pluie, peindre, vivre et rêver" (Nantes Métropole, 2026).
- Visiting: daily 11.00 to 19.00 except Tuesday, Thursdays until 21.00; 9 euros, 4 euros reduced; free on the first Sunday of the month outside July and August, on Thursday evenings and for under-18s, disabled visitors and jobseekers (Le Voyage à Nantes).

FUNDING
Musée d'arts de Nantes: public: Nantes Métropole, with 2017 renovation money from the EU, the State, the Region, the Département and the City
- Owner and operator: Nantes Métropole, which was the client for the renovation (BAM).
- Renovation: the project cost 88.5 million euros in total, paid by the European Union, the State, the Pays de la Loire regional council, the Loire-Atlantique departmental council and the City of Nantes with its metropolitan area (AFP, published by Weka, 16 November 2016).
- Building contract: Quille Construction, now Bouygues Bâtiment Grand Ouest, built it for 48.8 million euros before tax, 10 million euros under the City's estimate, and delivered in January 2017 (BAM, March 2020).
- Friends: a Société des amis du musée was founded in 1919 and backed modern art purchases (Museofile record).
- Bequest: in 2023 the Paris collector Jacqueline Boejat left the museum 20 paintings and 14 drawings, valued at more than 800,000 euros, the largest old-master bequest since a donation of 1852 (Nantes Métropole, 25 September 2023).
- State deposits: the national contemporary art fund (FNAC) and the FRAC Pays de la Loire deposit works with the museum (Museofile record).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public money throughout, led by Nantes Métropole. No funder carries a flag.
