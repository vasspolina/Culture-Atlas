BELARUSIAN COUNCIL FOR CULTURE (RADA KULTURY): Warsaw, Poland
ID: belarusian-council-for-culture-rada-kultury-warsaw
Website: https://byculture.org/en/meet-us/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Exile network of Belarusian cultural experts, managers and artists, described by itself as "a community of experts, managers, and artists" that works to rebuild Belarusian cultural life under repression and emigration.
- Registered in Estonia since October 2020 as MTÜ Valgevene Kultuuri Assotsiatsioon (registry code 80587137), with its legal address at Aru tn 21-8, Tallinn (stated by the council).
- Granted human rights organisation status in May 2023 (stated by the council).
- Staff named on its site: Siarhiej Budkin, CEO; Nadzieja Haretskaja, deputy director; Elina Kałnibałockaja, development director.
- Programmes: ArtPower Belarus (grants for independent Belarusian cultural initiatives), Belarusian Magistrates (community funding for cultural projects), Vasminoh (education for Belarusian school students) and joint projects with independent media.
- Its banner states that 79 cultural figures have been convicted in Belarus.

FUNDING
Belarusian Council for Culture: mainly EU-funded: European Union grants through ArtPower Belarus, with the Danish Cultural Institute as partner
- ArtPower Belarus is funded by the European Union and implemented with the Danish Cultural Institute (stated by the council). The council's press release states the EU provides 2 million euros over 28 months, with the Danish Cultural Institute as lead partner. The first edition ran from the end of 2022 to the end of February 2025 and approved 83 creative projects and 57 mobility grants.
- Second stage, 2025 to 2027: the June 2025 call offered Track 1 organisational development grants of 10,000 to 60,000 euros (up to 240,000 euros in the call) and Track 2 creative projects grants of 3,000 to 60,000 euros (up to 400,000 euros in the call); Track 3 offers 1,000 to 10,000 euros for urgent cases such as repression or loss of funding.
- Vasminoh is also EU-supported (stated by the council).
- The Partners section of the site shows the logos of EU-POS, the Danish Cultural Institute, ABBA, RESET!, the National Platform Forum of the Eastern Partnership and NDF.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Belarus declared the council an "extremist formation": the council's site says the Ministry of Internal Affairs did so, and a Minsk court ruled its materials "extremist". Reform.news reported on 24 April 2026 that the State Security Committee (KGB) added the council, the projects Mahistrat, Vasminoh and ArtPower Belarus, and the Foundation of the Belarusian Council for Culture to its list on 9 April 2026, and listed eleven individuals as associated with them, including Siarhiej Budkin and Elina Kałnibałockaja.
- Reform.news reported human rights defenders saying investigators are looking for "foreign funding", with searches, detentions and interrogations of people linked to cultural organisations.
- The council is registered in Estonia and names the European Union as the funder of ArtPower Belarus; the designation comes from Belarusian authorities (stated by the council; Reform.news).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the council is public grant money from the European Union, delivered with the Danish Cultural Institute, which the council re-grants to Belarusian cultural projects; the EU contribution is stated as 2 million euros over 28 months. The Belarusian state's designation of the council as an extremist formation is background, not a funder issue.
