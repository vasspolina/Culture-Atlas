KLING & BANG: Reykjavík, Iceland
ID: kling-og-bang-reykjavik

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit, artist-run gallery in the Marshall House at Grandagarður 20, in Reykjavík's harbour district, a former herring factory built in 1948 partly with US Marshall aid (City of Reykjavík announcement, 12 February 2016).
- Founded in 2003 by ten local artists on Laugavegur in downtown Reykjavík; founding member Erling Klingenberg said there was no platform for young or emerging artists to exhibit (The Art Newspaper, Tom Seymour, 15 November 2023). The article also names Hekla Dögg Jónsdóttir and Guðmundur Oddur Magnússon as founders of Klink and Bank.
- The National Bank of Iceland contacted the gallery in November 2003, and the artists persuaded it to fund the conversion of an unused former fish-net factory nearby into a roughly 5,000 sq. m space called Klink and Bank. About 137 artists, designers, filmmakers and musicians worked there daily; the building was demolished in autumn 2006 (The Art Newspaper).
- Ragnar Kjartansson's "The Visitors" was presented with Kling & Bang in 2013 (TBA21 page).
- On 12 February 2016 the city signed agreements placing it in the Marshall House with the Living Art Museum (Nýló), i8 Gallery and Ólafur Elíasson's studio, Stúdíó Reykjavík (City of Reykjavík announcement).

FUNDING
Kling & Bang: artist-run non-profit: a city-guaranteed lease, TBA21 programme support and early support from the National Bank of Iceland
- Marshall House: the City of Reykjavík leases the 2nd to 4th floors from HB Grandi for 15 years and subleases them to the art organisations and guarantees the lease (City of Reykjavík, 12 February 2016).
- TBA21 (Thyssen-Bornemisza Art Contemporary): its page says it is supporting Kling & Bang's annual curatorial programme; in 2013 TBA21 and Kling & Bang collaborated on bringing Ragnar Kjartansson's "The Visitors" to the gallery (TBA21 page).
- The National Bank of Iceland, which approached the gallery in November 2003, funded the Klink and Bank conversion; founder Erling Klingenberg recalls that the bank's only proviso was that the artists run it responsibly. Hekla Dögg Jónsdóttir says it remained a volunteer-led initiative, and after Iceland's 2008 banking collapse the financial sector stopped supporting the arts (The Art Newspaper, Tom Seymour, 15 November 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
Kling & Bang is an artist-run non-profit founded in 2003 by ten artists. The documented money is a City of Reykjavík lease guarantee for its Marshall House premises, support from TBA21 for its annual curatorial programme, and, in its early years, National Bank of Iceland funding for the Klink and Bank building.
