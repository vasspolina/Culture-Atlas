MUSEO DE ARTE MODERNO DE BUENOS AIRES (MUSEO MODERNO): Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: museo-de-arte-moderno-de-buenos-aires-buenos-aires
Website: https://www.museomoderno.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- City art museum at Av. San Juan 350 in San Telmo, Buenos Aires (museum site).
- Created on 11 April 1956 by decree 3527/56 on the initiative of Rafael Squirru, its first director. For its first four years it had no permanent home; it took the eighth and ninth floors of the Teatro Municipal General San Martín after 1960 (museum history page).
- Moved to its definitive home at Av. San Juan 350, a former tobacco warehouse, with a grand opening on 1 September 1989 (museum history page). Spanish Wikipedia dates the original building to 1918, built for the Piccardo tobacco company.
- The museum closed temporarily in 2005 to expand; the first stage opened in December 2010 as an 11,000 m² building. Emilio Ambasz donated the architectural work for the expansion (museum history page).
- Victoria Noorthoorn has directed it since August 2013 (museum history page). Collection of nearly 11,000 works (Spanish Wikipedia), including 72 León Ferrari works on paper donated in 2014.
- Open Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday 11:00 to 19:00, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 11:00 to 20:00, closed Tuesday. Admission is listed as $16.000 for foreigners and $5.000 for national residents; free on Wednesdays and the last Sunday of each month, and free for people with disabilities and one companion, under-12s, seniors, students and teachers. The page links an "Accessible Visit" section but gives no details (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires: mainly public: City of Buenos Aires, with corporate and private supporters
- The museum is owned by the City of Buenos Aires (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper reported in 2023 that 85 percent of the museum's public budget comes from city coffers, not the federal culture ministry.
- The Asociación Amigos del Museo de Arte Moderno was founded in 1986 and raised funds that helped the move to Av. San Juan 350 (museum history page).
- Mecenazgo: the city programme lets companies that pay gross-revenue tax in Buenos Aires direct part of it to museum projects and deduct up to 80 percent of the contribution; the open project is collection research and digitisation. The page names no contributors or amounts (museum site).
- The museum's supporter list names Asociación Amigos del Moderno, Premio Azcuy, TotalEnergies, Samsung, Plavicon, Fundación Andreani, Catena Zapata and the city mecenazgo programme, shown as logos with no amounts or years (museum site).
- Banco Supervielle gave 21 works in 2022 after lending 83 in December 2021; the bank had earlier helped enlarge the building and create an education room (La Nación, Celina Chatruc, 2022).
- A public-private acquisition committee, with Noorthoorn and invited donors, was presented in September 2019 (Arte al Día).
- No reporting found of what TotalEnergies or Samsung paid, or of published accounts or a board. Samsung also appears as a visitor discount (2x1 for Samsung Members).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- TotalEnergies is listed among the museum's supporters (museum site). No reporting found of the amount, the years or any protest aimed at this sponsorship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. The criticism found concerns budget, donation-based acquisitions and staff dismissals, and comes from newspaper and left-wing outlets.
1. La Nación: Verónica Gómez, "Cuestiones de fondo" (2012, opinion column)
URL: https://www.lanacion.com.ar/cultura/cuestiones-de-fondo-nid1493363/
- Says the dispute raises four questions: a larger museum budget, acquisitions that do not enter only by donation, funding for producing shows, and recognition for artists and museum workers.
- Disagrees with the artists' group Grupo AO's tactic of suspending donations two weeks before an opening, and says the press reduced the dispute to a scandal.
- Names the then director Laura Buccellato. A newspaper column, not art press.
2. La Izquierda Diario: anonymous interview, "Museo Inhumano: despidos y pandemia" (2020)
URL: https://www.laizquierdadiario.com/Museo-Inhumano-despidos-y-pandemia
- Nine women working as educators, attendants and event and production staff say they were told in June 2020 their contracts would end early, after being assured their jobs were safe, including by the director. They also describe unpaid salaries and contracts listing posts they did not hold.
- Authorities told them the City Government was responsible, citing a 30 percent budget cut as funds went to the Ministry of Health.
- Partisan: a left-wing outlet linked to the PTS party.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city institution with sponsor logos and a tax-deduction programme, so the main money is public. The weak points are the TotalEnergies listing with no amount, the thin public financial reporting, and past disputes over donation-led acquisitions and staff dismissals. Worth watching: which companies use the mecenazgo programme and whether the museum publishes who they are.
