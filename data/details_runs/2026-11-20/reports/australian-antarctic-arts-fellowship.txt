AUSTRALIAN ANTARCTIC ARTS FELLOWSHIP: Casey Station, Antarctica
ID: australian-antarctic-arts-fellowship
Website: https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-us/antarctic-arts-fellowship/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A government residency that sends artists and creative professionals in any artform to Antarctica and the sub-Antarctic to develop work shared with Australian audiences on their return (Australian Antarctic Division).
- Run since 1984 as part of the Australian Antarctic Program, administered by the Australian Antarctic Division of the Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (RMIT).
- Fellows travel to one of Australia's research stations: Casey, Davis, Mawson or Macquarie Island (Australian Antarctic Division).
- Selection is competitive: an expert panel shortlists expressions of interest, which must meet two or more of the fellowship's objectives; fellows are invited to give a 20-minute talk on station (Australian Antarctic Division).
- The 2025 fellow, the filmmaker and RMIT lecturer Dr Polly Stanton, spent four weeks at Casey station making a large-scale audiovisual work; the 2023 fellow Leila Jeffreys travelled with her in November 2025 to photograph seabirds for a Thames & Hudson book (RMIT; ANAT).
- Earlier fellows include the sound artist Philip Samartzis (2009 to 2010 and 2015 to 2016), the writer Favel Parrett (Casey in 2011, Macquarie Island in 2024), the botanical illustrator Maura Chamberlain, Janet Laurence, Dr K Verell and David Bridie (RMIT; Australian Antarctic Division).

FUNDING
Australian Antarctic Arts Fellowship: public: the Australian government's Antarctic Division, with a grant from ANAT
- Operator: the Australian Antarctic Division of the Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water runs and supports the fellowship (ANAT; RMIT).
- Travel: the fellowship covers travel between Hobart and Antarctica or the sub-Antarctic, and meals and accommodation while there (Australian Antarctic Division). Recent fellows have sailed on the government icebreaker RSV Nuyina (Australian Antarctic Division, 2024).
- Project grant: the Australian Network for Art and Technology (ANAT) helps select the fellows and gives each $5,000 towards the project (Australian Antarctic Division; RMIT).
- ANAT is itself assisted by the Australian Government through Creative Australia and by the South Australian government through CreateSA (ANAT).
- Partner: RMIT University works with ANAT to broker opportunities for artists with science partners such as the Australian Antarctic Program (RMIT).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The fellowship's stated aims include "demonstrating Australia's continuing connection with the region, established through its historical presence and legacy" (Australian Antarctic Division).
- Australia claims about 42 per cent of Antarctica as the Australian Antarctic Territory; under the Antarctic Treaty all claims are held in abeyance, and only Norway, New Zealand, France and the United Kingdom accept Australia's claim (Wikipedia).
- In April 2023 an independent review of the Antarctic Division by Leigh Russell found intentional exclusion, gender discrimination, bullying and sexual harassment; of 236 people surveyed, 15 per cent said they had been sexually harassed and 34 per cent bullied (The Canberra Times, Miriam Webber). A follow-up review completed in March 2026 found "meaningful progress" (Australian Antarctic Division).
- The Division that faced these findings runs the fellowship and the stations where fellows live.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money: a small government residency with a grant from a publicly assisted arts body. The points to watch are its stated role in promoting Australia's connection to a territorial claim most countries do not accept, and the workplace culture at the Division that hosts the fellows.
