ATLANTA CONTEMPORARY: Atlanta, USA
ID: atlanta-contemporary-atlanta
Website: https://atlantacontemporary.org/

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting contemporary art centre at 535 Means Street NW, West Midtown, Atlanta, near the Georgia Tech campus (Atlanta Contemporary site, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1973 by Atlanta photographers as Nexus, a storefront cooperative gallery supported by member dues and run by volunteers. It became Nexus Contemporary Art Center in 1984, Atlanta Contemporary Art Center in 2000 and Atlanta Contemporary in 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a 35,000-square-foot warehouse complex on Means Street, renovated after a $1.95 million capital campaign finished in 1987, in phases from 1989 to 1994 (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions by local and international artists, commissions, education programmes, a Studio Artist Program with subsidised studio space, and a shop selling artist-made goods and Nexus Press books (Wikipedia). Nexus Press, which produced more than 150 titles, closed in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Admission is free every day, a policy in place since 1 September 2015. Open Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, Thursday 11:00 to 20:00, Friday and Saturday 11:00 to 18:00; closed Monday to Wednesday (Atlanta Contemporary site).

FUNDING
Atlanta Contemporary: private non-profit funded by foundations, city and county arts money, corporate and individual donors
- FY26 donors page (1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026): major support from the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts and Ruth Foundation for the Arts; programme support from the City of Atlanta Mayor's Office of Cultural Affairs and the Fulton County Board of Commissioners; generous support from the Community Foundation for Greater Atlanta, Intuit, the Molly Blank Fund of the Arthur M. Blank Family Foundation, Massey Charitable Trust, LuluMa Foundation and Robert Rauschenberg Foundation. Supporters giving $5,000 or more also include the Bill & Agnes Monk Family Foundation, the Tim & Lauren Schrager Family Foundation and the Georgia Committee of the National Museum of Women in the Arts.
- In-kind business sponsors: AAA Parking, Hotel Indigo, Kaldi's Coffee Roasting Co., Orange Barrel Media, Post Meridiem Spirit Co, Poston Communications and Rough Draft Atlanta.
- Form 990 data (ProPublica, fiscal years ending June): FY2025 revenue $906,793 and expenses $712,289, with contributions of $745,319 (82.2% of revenue); FY2024 revenue $592,865 and expenses $841,621; FY2023 revenue $1,055,402 and expenses $837,529. Net assets were $1,505,670 at the end of FY2025.
- The 1999 selection for the Warhol Initiative funded long-term institutional planning (Wikipedia).
- Culture Type reports that its 2025 to 2028 strategic plan calls for improving fundraising, adding revenue streams and relying less on grants (Victoria L. Valentine, 19 February 2026).
- Leadership: Floyd Hall left as executive director in August 2025 after 18 months; former board chair Everett Long served as interim; Lauren Haynes became Executive Director on 16 March 2026 (Culture Type; Atlanta Contemporary announcement, 9 February 2026). Culture Type, citing the website, reports a staff of five including the interim director.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Atlanta Contemporary is foundation, city, county and individual giving on a budget under $1 million a year. The weak points are the thin finances, with expenses above revenue in FY2024, and the leadership turnover of 2025 to 2026; the fundraising plan under the new director is the item to watch.
