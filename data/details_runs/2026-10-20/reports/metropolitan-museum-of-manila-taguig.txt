METROPOLITAN MUSEUM OF MANILA: Taguig, Philippines
ID: metropolitan-museum-of-manila-taguig
Website: https://www.metmuseum.ph/

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit museum of contemporary art, also called "The M", at the MK Tan Centre, 30th Street, Bonifacio Global City, Taguig (Metropolitan Museum of Manila).
- Wikipedia dates its establishment to 3 October 1976 at the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas complex in Malate, Manila. The Metropolitan Museum of Manila Foundation, Inc. was set up in 1979 by former First Lady Imelda Marcos and businessman Bienvenido Tantoco Sr., and in September 1986 the museum became a non-government organisation (Wikipedia).
- The museum site says it opened its new home in Bonifacio Global City in October 2022. The Urban Roamer blog (20 July 2025) places it next to the JY Campos Centre, the headquarters of NutriAsia.
- The new premises cover 3,000 square metres over three levels and were designed by Carlos Arnaiz (Lifestyle Asia, 22 November 2021).
- Open Tuesday to Friday 11am to 6pm and Saturday and Sunday 10am to 6pm; closed Monday. The museum is moving from free entry to paid electronic ticketing, with reduced rates for seniors, people with disabilities and students.

FUNDING
Metropolitan Museum of Manila: non-government foundation, formerly subsidised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas; chaired by NutriAsia founder Joselito Campos Jr.
- Wikipedia says the museum was formerly subsidised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and that management has since moved to the foundation.
- Joselito Campos Jr. chairs the museum's Board of Trustees (Lifestyle Asia, 22 November 2021). Wikipedia says he founded NutriAsia in 1990, chairs it and is vice chairman of Del Monte Philippines.
- Unverified: the Urban Roamer blog (20 July 2025) says the museum's founding benefactors include companies led by the Campos family, NutriAsia and Del Monte Philippines.
- Three Que family couples said they would give the full proceeds of artworks sold at the auction of 4 December 2021 to the museum's new home (Lifestyle Asia, 22 November 2021).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One episode draws criticism, the 2024 "Banksy Universe" exhibition; the pieces below are from general and lifestyle press, not art press.
1. Asia News Network, from the Philippine Daily Inquirer: Lala Singian, "Let's talk about the Banksy controversy in Manila" (23 May 2024)
URL: https://asianews.network/?p=148061
- Mostly defends the museum, a non-profit foundation that had to start charging entrance fees. It says "The M did not say as explicitly as they could have that the exhibit was unauthorized" and that curation at the soft opening could have been better.
- Reports that the museum's announcement carrying the slogan "Art is not a crime" was taken down by Bonifacio Global City, and pushback over the lack of Filipino artists in its programming.
- Argues unofficial Banksy shows are common worldwide. Names the museum.
2. BluPrint: Chad Rialp, "Banksy Universe Was Never Pre-Approved" (26 April 2024)
URL: https://bluprint-onemega.com/news/banksy-universe-was-never-pre-approved
- Reports that Pest Control, Banksy's licensing company, confirmed the show is not authorised and said the artist "is not involved or associated with" any of his exhibitions.
- Names the museum as organiser and Bonifacio Global City as a partner; says that district's stance against unsanctioned street art drew criticism from the local art community.
3. The Freeman (Philstar): Joseph Gonzales, "Banksy bile" (28 April 2024), column "Looking Askance"
URL: https://philstar.com/opinion/2024/04/28/2351064/banksy-bile
- Not criticism of the museum: a column defending it. Gonzales argues the online outrage was aimed at the wrong target because the museum and Bonifacio Global City are separate bodies, and calls the reporting on Banksy's representatives disowning the show "lazy".

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2018 NutriAsia strike (Wikipedia): workers hired through the contractor B-Mirk, citing contractualisation and unsafe conditions, formed a union at NutriAsia's Marilao plant and its officers were laid off. On 23 February 2018 the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) ordered NutriAsia to give regular employment to 914 workers hired through three contractors as labour-only subcontracting; NutriAsia rejected that reading. On 25 June 2018 DOLE Region 3 reversed the finding.
- On 30 June 2018 police dispersed about 300 people at the end of an ecumenical mass at the picket. At least 30 people were injured and 19 were arrested, 14 workers or supporters and five journalists. DOLE condemned the dispersal (Wikipedia).
- Connection to the museum: the strike and dispersal took place at NutriAsia's plant, not at the museum. NutriAsia's founder Joselito Campos Jr. chairs the museum's board, and the Urban Roamer blog names NutriAsia as a founding benefactor.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a non-government foundation, formerly subsidised by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, chaired by NutriAsia founder Joselito Campos Jr. The Urban Roamer blog names NutriAsia and Del Monte Philippines as founding benefactors. The documented episodes are the 2018 NutriAsia strike dispersal, which happened at the company's plant, and the 2024 Banksy Universe row.
