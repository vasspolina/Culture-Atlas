INTERLOCHEN CENTER FOR THE ARTS: Interlochen, USA
ID: interlochen-center-for-the-arts-interlochen
Website: https://www.interlochen.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Arts summer camp, boarding school and arts centre on 1,200 wooded acres between two lakes in Green Lake Township, Grand Traverse County, Michigan, about 10 miles southwest of Traverse City (Wikipedia).
- Joseph E. Maddy incorporated the National High School Orchestra Camp in 1927 and opened its first season in 1928; it became the National Music Camp in 1932 and Interlochen Arts Camp in 1991 (Wikipedia).
- Interlochen Arts Academy, the year-round boarding school, opened in 1962 (Wikipedia).
- Programmes listed by the space: Interlochen Arts Camp, Interlochen Arts Academy, Interlochen Online, Interlochen Public Radio, Interlochen Presents, Interlochen Hospitality and the Interlochen College of Creative Arts.
- Enrolment: 3,344 summer campers and about 500 Academy students (Wikipedia). Trey Devey has been president since 2017 (NPR).
- Visiting and fees: 2026 camp tuition ran from $2,050 for a one-week intensive to $10,350 for six weeks; the space states that 85 percent of students who applied for aid received it for summer 2025.

FUNDING
Interlochen Center for the Arts: private nonprofit: donor gifts, tuition and an endowment
- Fiscal 2025 (year to 31 May 2025): total revenue $34,248,705, of which contributions were $24,099,852; expenses $28,161,240; net assets $318,252,483 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- Fiscal 2024: revenue $65,975,420, contributions $23,448,760. Fiscal 2023: revenue $91,841,304, contributions $19,460,522 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- Named funds in the 2025 report: trustee Dr. David Wu and Dr. Bernadine Wu (Wu Family Conductor Endowment), trustee Dara Levan and Jarett Levan (mental health fund and scholarship endowment), the Foster Family Foundation (costume and set production fund), and family endowments including Stern, Breibart, Hamann, Lambert and Goldstein. The report gives no amounts.
- President Arthur (Trey) Devey received $620,512 in fiscal 2025 (Form 990).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor or a state budget line.

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein as donor, 1990 to 2003
- Epstein attended the camp in 1967 at 14 as a bassoon player (NPR).
- He gave more than $400,000 between 1990 and 2003, including $200,000 for a lodge on Green Lake (AP, NPR).
- A February 1994 letter from vice president for institutional advancement Tim Ambrose thanked him for the $200,000 and said he could use the lodge up to two weeks a year and still claim a full tax deduction; its rental proceeds were to fund a scholarship in his name (NPR).
- Interlochen admitted him to its President's Club, whose members were expected to help identify prospective students (NPR). Correspondence in the Justice Department's release shows he directed that tuition for at least one student be paid out of his donations (AP).
- "Jane", who testified at Ghislaine Maxwell's 2021 trial, said the pair met her at the camp in summer 1994; NPR puts her age at 13, Deadline Detroit at 14. A second woman told NPR her contact with the pair began at the school when she was 14.
- DOJ documents show Epstein and Maxwell met one student alone at the lodge (Fox 2 Detroit, citing NPR).
- Interlochen removed his name from the Jeffrey E. Epstein Scholarship Lodge in 2008, renamed it Green Lake Lodge, and its board approved demolition in May 2026 (AP).
- Internal reviews in 2008 and 2019 found no complaint involving Epstein; the school's 1990s policy was to destroy paper records after 10 years (NPR).
- Sanghavi Law Office's 97-page report, published in July 2026, recorded two women's accounts of sexual conduct by Epstein, one at the lodge, and 70 alumni accounts against 47 faculty and staff from the 1950s to the 2010s (AP via Fortune).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage of the Epstein link and the abuse report is in news outlets and public radio, not the art press.

BOTTOM LINE
Interlochen's current money is private donor gifts and an endowment of about $318 million in net assets, with named family funds and no flagged corporate sponsor. The weak point is Epstein: a documented donor from 1990 to 2003 whose gift built the lodge where, according to DOJ records and the 2026 report, he met students. Worth watching: what follows from the names the school gave to Grand Traverse County authorities.
