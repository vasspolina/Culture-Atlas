YEKATERINBURG MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS: Yekaterinburg, Russia
ID: yekaterinburg-museum-of-fine-arts-yekaterinburg
Website: https://i-z-o.art/

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum with five sites in Yekaterinburg: the main building at 5 Voevodina Street, the Hermitage-Ural cultural centre at 11 Vainera Street, the Museum of Naive Art, the Stone-Carving History Center named after A. K. Denisov-Uralsky, and a restoration and storage centre at 16A Vainera Street (museum website).
- Founded on 1 April 1936 and registered as a municipal autonomous cultural institution on 3 December 2015; the founder is the Department of Culture of the Yekaterinburg city administration and the director is Nikita Nikolayevich Korytin (museum website).
- Former names: Wikipedia gives the Sverdlovsk Art Gallery as the museum's original gallery, and says it took its current name in 1992 and gained full museum status in 1988.
- Building: the core of the main building is a hospital built in 1730 for the Yekaterinburg Ironworks, altered several times in the 19th century. Most of the ironworks buildings were demolished in the 1970s and the Historical Square was laid out in their place (Wikipedia).
- On site: more than 18,000 items, including Russian painting from the 18th to early 20th centuries, the Russian avant-garde, icons, porcelain, Western European art and the Kasli cast-iron collection, whose pavilion took part in the 1900 Paris World Exhibition (Wikipedia). The museum lists thirteen collections, among them Ural jewellery, naive art and Tobolsk carved bone (museum website).
- Hermitage-Ural: a branch tied to the State Hermitage, planned under a 2014 three-way agreement between the federal, regional and city governments. Atomstroykompleks was named contractor for the reconstruction of 11 Vainera Street in November 2018 (EAN, 28 November 2018).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 19:00, closed Mondays; from 22 October 2026 Hermitage-Ural is open until 18:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Yekaterinburg Museum of Fine Arts: public: Yekaterinburg city administration as founder, with regional and federal money for Hermitage-Ural
- Founder: the Department of Culture of the Yekaterinburg city administration (museum website). The museum publishes financial statements for 2014 to 2024, annual reports for 2014 to 2023 and the supervisory board's membership changes from 2017 to 2026 (museum website).
- Hermitage-Ural budget: the 2014 agreement split costs between the federal government, the region and the city. For the period to 2020 the city was to contribute 91 million rubles, the region 66.2 million and the federal government 309.3 million; construction was estimated at 466.5 million rubles plus about 110 million for equipment (EAN, 14 March 2017). The maximum initial contract price for the 11 Vainera Street building was 217.2 million rubles (EAN, 28 November 2018).
- Federal equipment funding: in April 2020 Korytin told the city Duma committee that the federal Ministry of Culture owed about 200 million rubles for equipment and that the outlook was uncertain, while local authorities had met their obligations (EAN, 22 April 2020).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: it is a municipal institution founded by the Yekaterinburg city administration, and the Hermitage-Ural branch was financed by federal, regional and city budgets under a 2014 agreement. The museum publishes its financial statements and annual reports.
