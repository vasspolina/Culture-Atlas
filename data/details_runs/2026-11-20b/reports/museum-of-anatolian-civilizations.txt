MUSEUM OF ANATOLIAN CIVILIZATIONS: Ankara, Turkey
ID: museum-of-anatolian-civilizations
Website: https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?SectionId=AMM01&DistId=AMM (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Archaeology museum on the south side of Ankara Castle in the Atpazarı area of Ulus, Ankara (Wikipedia; Turkish Museums, Ministry of Culture and Tourism).
- Began in 1921 when the culture director Mübarek Galip set up a museum in the Akkale bastion of the castle, at Atatürk's wish for a Hittite museum (Turkish Museums).
- Housed in two Ottoman buildings, the Mahmut Paşa Bedesteni (covered bazaar) and the Kurşunlu Han, which the Culture and Tourism Minister dates to the reign of Mehmed II; the central hall opened to visitors in 1943, during the conversion (Turkish Museums; DHA, 21 July 2026).
- Collection: Anatolian archaeology from the Palaeolithic onward, arranged by period, with the Classical and "Ankara Through the Ages" sections on the lower floor (Turkish Museums); much of the Phrygian section comes from the royal tumulus at Gordion (Wikipedia).
- Named the first European Museum of the Year on 19 April 1997 (Turkish Museums; Wikipedia).
- Visitors: 325,913 in 2019 (Wikipedia).
- A display renewal begun in May 2026 put 288 objects on view for the first time, among them objects recovered from trafficking or returned from abroad, a Göbeklitepe cult reconstruction and a new setting for the Cybele statue (Hürriyet Daily News, 23 July 2026; DHA).
- Visiting: open every day 8:30am to 5:30pm, box office closes 4:45pm. The Ankara Hall and the Classical Hall are closed from 14 September 2026 to 2 March 2027 for maintenance and repair (Turkish Museums).

FUNDING
Museum of Anatolian Civilizations: state: Turkey's Ministry of Culture and Tourism
- Operator: the Ministry of Culture and Tourism; the minister credited its General Directorate of Cultural Assets and Museums for the 2026 renewal (DHA, 21 July 2026).
- Building: the conversion of the two Ottoman buildings, recommended by culture director Hamit Zübeyir Koşay and Education Minister Saffet Arıkan, ran from 1938 to 1968 (Wikipedia).
- Renovation: the museum was renovated in 2014 (Turkish Museums).
- 2026 renewal: new circulation routes, a group gathering area and a relandscaped courtyard; further phases will update the Ankara and Classical sections and add basement galleries (Hürriyet Daily News; DHA).
- Income: admission at 12 euros (Turkish Museums).

WIDER CONTEXT
- A Court of Accounts (Sayıştay) audit found that the museum's asset codes for its objects did not match its inventory register, so a physical count could not easily locate an object, and that its storerooms lacked suitable size, temperature, light and humidity conditions (Sözcü, 2 October 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Turkish state money only, with no private sponsor named. Worth watching is whether the storage and inventory faults the auditors found have been fixed.
