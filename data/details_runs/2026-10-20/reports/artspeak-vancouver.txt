ARTSPEAK: Vancouver, Canada
ID: artspeak-vancouver
Website: https://artspeak.ca

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit, artist-run gallery and publisher at 233 Carrall Street in Gastown, Vancouver, registered as the I.E. Artspeak Gallery Society (CanadaHelps).
- Founded in 1986 at the Kootenay School of Writing, a writers' collective in Vancouver, by Jeff Derksen, Cate Rimmer and the artist Kay Higgins; Rimmer and Higgins ran it as volunteers at first (Wikipedia). It became independent in 1987 and moved to 311 West Hastings (Wikipedia).
- In 1996 it bought its present building at 233 Carrall Street. Wikipedia says it is one of the few artist-run centres in Vancouver to own its space.
- Mandate: "to exhibit contemporary art and to encourage a dialogue between visual art and writing" (Artspeak director/curator job listing, 2022). Programme: contemporary practices, publications, bookworks, editions, talks and events (CanadaHelps).
- Directors: Lorna Brown was director-curator from 1999 to 2004 (Wikipedia); Canadian Art, 15 March 2017, quotes the curator Tarah Hogue on Bopha Chhay's "recent appointment as director of Artspeak".

FUNDING
Artspeak: public arts grants and donations: BC Arts Council grants, plus fundraisers
- BC Arts Council, 1 April 2023 to 31 March 2024: $41,080 in operating assistance for visual arts and media arts organisations, $14,000 under Arts Circulation and Touring and $3,430 under Arts Infrastructure (BC Arts Council awards listing 2023/24).
- The 2022 director/curator listing says the post includes successful grant applications to municipal, provincial and federal governments, foundations and other private sources, and pays $56,000 a year plus benefits (Akimbo).
- Artspeak's 2024/25 benefit appeal on CanadaHelps says "2024 has witnessed drastic cuts to funding in arts and culture" and sets a goal of $25,000 from key donors.
- In 2004 the gallery held a runway fundraiser, "Fashionista" (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
Artspeak's money is public arts grants, of which the BC Arts Council's are documented, with donations and benefit events; it owns its building, per Wikipedia. The 2024 cuts to arts funding and the $25,000 donor appeal are the points to watch.
