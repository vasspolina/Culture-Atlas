VASL ARTISTS' ASSOCIATION: Karachi, Pakistan
ID: vasl-artists-association
Website: https://vaslart.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit artist-run organisation with a residency, a small gallery and an office at D-53/1, 1st Street, Block 4, Clifton, Karachi (Vasl).
- Established in 2001 in Karachi; Adeela Suleman, now Executive Director, has been a volunteer since 2002 and is listed as a founding member. The site names no other founders (Vasl).
- Affiliated with the UK-based Triangle Network, founded in 1982 (Vasl).
- The name is Vasl; no namesake person is given.
- Premises: a residence with two ensuite bedrooms and a courtyard studio, and a gallery of 189 square feet indoors and 184 outdoors, run with Full Circle Gallery (Vasl).
- Programmes: residencies, exhibitions, workshops and publications; over 700 artists have taken part in residencies and workshops according to the site, while its 2026 disclosure says more than 500 in 25 years.
- Visiting: no opening hours are published; the site asks visitors to contact Vasl first.

FUNDING
Vasl Artists' Association: privately funded: local patrons, art foundations and companies; no accounts published
- Its 2026 corporate disclosure lists sponsors, donors and supporters: COMO Museum of Art, Taimur Hassan, Dr. Furqaan Ahmed and The Furqaan Ahmed Collection, Farhana Mowjee, Ghouse Akbar, Khurram Kasim Art Foundation, Sultan Ali Akbar Allana, Shehnaz Ismail, Maya Ismail, Nishat Siddiqi, Asghar Kazim, Duraid Qureshi, HUM TV, HBL Art Committee, Kadist and AFIELD. No amounts or years are given.
- The 2025 exhibition "Traces: Works from the Vasl Archive" (22 July to 1 August 2025) was supported by KKAF, HumTV, HBL, Sharifa and Sultan Ali Allana, Full Circle Gallery and SAGA (Vasl).
- AFIELD says its 2025 Art Space Grant, a collaboration with Kadist, was awarded to Vasl; no amount is stated.
- In a December 2015 interview Suleman said Vasl was supported by local individuals and received a monthly amount from Getz Pharma Pakistan, which also backed its programmes and provided an apartment. She named ASEF, Arts Collaboratory, Hivos, Stichting Doen and the Ford Foundation as international funders that gave intermittent funding until the previous year (ASEF Culture360, Herman Bashiron Mendolicchio).
- Legal form: a company limited by guarantee licensed under Section 42 of Pakistan's Companies Act 2017, with a tax exemption certificate; the disclosure names three directors (Adeela Suleman, Sohail Osman Ali, Fatima Munir Amjad), two advisers (Khurram Kasim, Maria Hasan) and the auditor Salahuddin and Company. It says audited statements for the year ending 2025 are maintained but does not publish them.
- Vasl's pages name "HBL" (exhibition page, under "Supported By") and "HBL Art Committee" (2026 disclosure) without a full name, an amount or a year of giving beyond the 2025 exhibition. No page opened says that HBL is Habib Bank Limited.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The coverage found is an interview with Suleman and Vasl's own catalogues.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 7 September 2017: New York's Department of Financial Services fined Habib Bank and its New York branch 225 million dollars for failures to comply with New York anti-money-laundering and sanctions-screening rules. The release cites "Lack of evidence for adequate OFAC and sanctions screening" and says the bank handled billions of dollars of transactions with Al Rajhi Bank, which it describes as having "reported links to al Qaeda", without adequate controls. The bank agreed to surrender its New York branch licence (New York DFS). The order is dated history from 2017.
- Connection to Vasl: Vasl's pages name only "HBL" and "HBL Art Committee" as supporters, with no full name. The pages do not show that this is Habib Bank, so the fine is context and is not applied as a flag.

BOTTOM LINE
Vasl is funded by local patrons, foundations and companies, with earlier support from European and US foundations. The weak points are that the disclosure gives no amounts, years or published accounts, and that the supporter "HBL" is not identified; if it is Habib Bank, that bank paid a 2017 New York fine. Worth watching: which supporter pays for what, and whether audited accounts become public.
