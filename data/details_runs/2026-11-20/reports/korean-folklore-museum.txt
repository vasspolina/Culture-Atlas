KOREAN FOLKLORE MUSEUM: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: korean-folklore-museum
Website: none found (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of Korean folk customs in Taedongmun-dong in the Central District of Pyongyang, north of the Korean Central History Museum and next to the Taedong Gate (Wikipedia, Korean Wikipedia, North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification).
- Opened in February 1956 (Wikipedia, korea-dpr.com).
- Wikipedia, citing a 2014 reference book, counts seven rooms of 1,800 square metres with 2,100 exhibits.
- korea-dpr.com, the Korean Friendship Association's site, describes 7 halls with over 2,300 items from the Old Stone Age to the end of the 19th century.
- The North Korea Information Portal lists sections on working customs, food, clothing, housing, family rites, folk games and music.
- Among the displays it names are early farming tools, Koryo celadon and Joseon white porcelain, brassware-making tools, furniture, seven-dish and twelve-dish table settings, costumes by class and trade from Koguryo to the Joseon period, and models showing the development of ondol floor heating.
- The games section shows archery, horse riding and ssirum wrestling on large folding screens, and the music section sets old instruments beside modern reworked ones (North Korea Information Portal).

FUNDING
Korean Folklore Museum: state-funded: the Government of North Korea
- Party oversight: at a meeting for the museum's 40th anniversary in February 1996, party secretary Choe Thae Bok delivered a congratulatory message from the Party Central Committee and the museum's director, Ri Jong Sun, gave the report, which presented the museum as a base for ideological and cultural education (North Korea Information Portal, summarising North Korean media).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: Sanctions on the state
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the North Korean state's, and the Workers' Party treats it as a base for ideological education. The weak point is the sanctions basis under Executive Order 13722. The two published item counts come from different sources, a reference book and a friendship association's site.
