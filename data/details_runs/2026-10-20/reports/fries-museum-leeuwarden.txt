FRIES MUSEUM: Leeuwarden, Netherlands
ID: fries-museum-leeuwarden
Website: https://www.friesmuseum.nl

OVERVIEW
- Museum of art, crafts and history of Fryslân on Wilhelminaplein in Leeuwarden, with a collection of about 1 million objects covering 1200 to 2000 (Wikipedia).
- Founded on 13 April 1881 by a society for the preservation of Frisian culture, and first housed in the former Eysinga house on the Koningstraat (Wikipedia).
- The present building on Wilhelminaplein was designed by Hubert-Jan Henket and opened on 13 September 2013 by Queen Máxima; the Frisian architect Abe Bonnema initiated it and left 18 million euros to the museum when he died in 2001 (Wikipedia).
- Includes a hall dedicated to Mata Hari and the Hindelooper Room (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; the building is described as easy for disabled visitors to reach (Fries Museum).
- Kris Callens led the Fries Museum, the Fries Verzetsmuseum and the Princessehof National Museum of Ceramics from 1 August 2015; on 4 February 2025 the museum announced he was stepping down by mutual agreement with the supervisory board, and cited financial strain (Fries Museum).
- On 21 March 2025 the museum said its supervisory board received reports of possible sexual and boundary-crossing behaviour and acted on them, and that "the seriousness of the situation was obvious, and that hastened the departure of Kris Callens" (VRT NWS, Jani Lambrechts; quotation translated).

FUNDING
Fries Museum: funded by the Province of Fryslân, the municipality of Leeuwarden, Dutch funds and partners including the VriendenLoterij and Windpark Fryslân
- Provinsje Fryslân is the museum's fixed subsidy provider (Fries Museum partners page).
- A briefing titled "Fries Museum 2025-2028: Gevolgen van besparingen voor de gemeente Leeuwarden" (effects of savings for the municipality of Leeuwarden, title translated), dated 16 September 2024 on the Leeuwarden council information site, states that the Province's cut of 331,000 euros comes on top of weak inflation correction for 2008 to 2023, so that the contribution to the new museum is about 1.3 million euros a year lower than agreed. It states that in 2024 the municipality gave 150,000 euros for the exhibition "Ja, ik wil", which costs 400,000 euros, and received 100,000 euros in property tax (OZB) from the museum, which owns its building.
- On 4 February 2025 the museum stated that a 300,000 euro subsidy cut from 2026 adds to its financial strain (Fries Museum).
- Partners named on the museum's page: Abe Bonnema Stichting, VriendenLoterij, Windpark Fryslân, Fonds 21, Mondriaan Fonds, Het Cultuurfonds, Stichting Turing Foundation (the museum won its 2015 award), Stimuleringsfonds, Vereniging Rembrandt, VSBfonds, Wassenbergh-Clarijs Fonteinstichting, Wetterskip Fryslân and Modemuze. Private and business Friends also support the museum (Fries Museum).
- The same briefing states that covering the staff costs of large exhibitions through funds and sponsors, on top of their doubled costs, is impossible.

BOTTOM LINE
The Fries Museum is funded by a fixed subsidy from the Province of Fryslân, by the municipality of Leeuwarden and by named Dutch funds and partners, among them the VriendenLoterij and Windpark Fryslân. The Province cut 331,000 euros in 2024, and the museum's February 2025 statement names a further 300,000 euro cut from 2026. Director Kris Callens left in 2025; the museum said in March 2025 that reports of boundary-crossing behaviour hastened his departure.
