GOLDEN THREAD GALLERY: Belfast, UK
ID: golden-thread-gallery-belfast
Website: https://goldenthreadgallery.co.uk/ (checked 2026-10-20; the record lists a GrantNav page as the website)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary visual art gallery at 23-29 Queen Street in Belfast city centre, reached from the front of Belfast City Hall by about 175 yards along Donegall Square North and 140 yards up Queen Street (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1998 by artist Gail Prentice in a former linen mill in North Belfast, in an interface area between nationalist and unionist neighbourhoods; reconstituted in 2001 as Golden Thread Gallery Ltd, a charity (Wikipedia, annual report).
- Moved from the Crumlin Road to a former electricity switch room on Great Patrick Street on 13 April 2007, and to Queen Street in August 2024 (stated by the space, Wikipedia).
- The Queen Street building dates from 1871 and was the Gas Corporation Showroom and later Craftworld; Dickson Fitzgerald Architects restored it for Leja Properties (Arts Council of Northern Ireland).
- Two floors hold two large galleries, a projection room, a Community Participation and Engagement Hub, and what the gallery calls Northern Ireland's first visual art library and archive (stated by the space).
- Run by Co-Directors Peter Richards, director since 2002, and Sarah McAvera, co-director since 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Friday 11:00 to 17:00 and Saturday 11:00 to 16:00, closed Sunday and Monday; entry is free; step-free street access, a lift to the upper gallery and a wheelchair-accessible ground-floor bathroom (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Golden Thread Gallery: mainly public: Arts Council of Northern Ireland through the National Lottery, and Belfast City Council
- Wikipedia names the Arts Council of Northern Ireland, the National Lottery and Belfast City Council as its principal funders; the Arts Council funds it annually through the National Lottery (Arts Council of Northern Ireland, 2024). No reporting found of the annual amount.
- Income was £494,382 in the year to March 2024 (annual report) and £399,592 in the year to March 2025 (Charity Commission for Northern Ireland data via GrantNav).
- Grants on GrantNav, 2018 to 2025, total £199,270: the National Lottery Heritage Fund (£83,270 in 2025 for a feasibility study on Wilmont House), the Foyle Foundation (£55,000), the Garfield Weston Foundation (£40,000), the Halifax Foundation for Northern Ireland (£16,000) and the Community Foundation for Northern Ireland (£5,000).
- The Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation and Art Fund support the Community Hub; the Ampersand Foundation supports the library and archive; the Fondation Louis Vuitton enabled the 2025 Sophie Calle show (Arts Council of Northern Ireland, 2024).
- The site also shows logos, without amounts or dates, for Future Screens NI, The Ireland Funds and Arts & Business NI (stated by the space).

Red flags: Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation's oil endowment
- The Gulbenkian Foundation was founded in 1956 under the will of Calouste Gulbenkian, whom Wikipedia describes as an oil magnate.
- The Foundation owned the oil and gas company Partex until it sold it to a subsidiary of Thailand's PTTEP; the sale agreement of 17 June 2019 was for up to US$622 million, and the sale closed on 4 November 2019 (Gulbenkian Foundation).
- No reporting found, among pages that could be opened, of the 2016 protest the record cites.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery runs on public money, Arts Council lottery funding and Belfast City Council, plus UK trusts. The only weak point is the Gulbenkian Foundation's oil endowment; the Foundation sold its oil company in 2019. No amounts are published for the Arts Council or Gulbenkian support.
