CLYFFORD STILL MUSEUM: Denver, USA
ID: clyfford-still-museum-denver
Website: https://clyffordstillmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Single-artist museum at 1250 Bannock Street in Denver's Civic Center Cultural Complex, devoted to the Abstract Expressionist painter Clyfford Still (1904 to 1980) (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Still's will left his collection to a city that would build a museum for his work alone; about 20 cities competed, and in 2004 his widow Patricia chose Denver after Mayor John Hickenlooper accepted the terms. It opened on 18 November 2011 (Wikipedia).
- Founding director Dean Sobel led it from 2004 to 2020; Joyce Tsai has been director since 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Building by Allied Works Architecture, led by Brad Cloepfil: 28,500 square feet with nine galleries; ground was broken in December 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Holds 3,125 works, 93% of Still's lifetime output, and his complete archives; the will bars showing other artists' work (Wikipedia, ArtsJournal).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 5pm, closed Mondays; free for children; the museum held 27 pay-what-you-wish days and 4 SCFD free days in 2025 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Clyfford Still Museum: endowment-funded: its own foundation, built from the 2011 sale of four Still paintings, plus the regional arts tax and donors
- In 2025 revenue was $6,530,455: $4,833,104 in grants from a related entity, $853,732 in general contributions, $404,972 from the Scientific and Cultural Facilities District sales tax, and $169,536 from admissions and programmes (2025 annual report).
- The Clyfford Still Museum Foundation, a separate charity, held $168,536,037 in assets at the end of 2025 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- That endowment came from four paintings from Patricia Still's estate that the City and County of Denver sold at Sotheby's on 9 November 2011 for $114 million, netting at least $85 million, with court permission (Denver Post via Saunders, CultureGrrl).
- The board includes a Denver City Council representative and a mayoral appointee (2025 annual report).
- 2025 major supporters include The Anschutz Foundation and Sarah Anschutz, the Jay Pritzker Foundation, Art Bridges Foundation, the Denver Foundation, Bloomberg Connects, the National Endowment for the Arts and the Institute of Museum and Library Services (2025 annual report).

Red flags: fossil fuels (The Anschutz Foundation)
- The Anschutz Foundation is listed among 2025 major supporters of $1,000 or more; no amount is given (2025 annual report).
- Philip Anschutz heads the foundation; his fortune began in his father's oil drilling company, and he sold an interest in the Anschutz Ranch oil field to Mobil for $500 million in 1982 (Wikipedia).
- The Anschutz Corporation still owns Anschutz Exploration Corporation, which drills for oil and gas mainly in Colorado, Utah and Wyoming (Wikipedia).
- The foundation has given to conservative groups including the Heritage Foundation and the Federalist Society (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Bloomberg Connects
- Bloomberg Connects is listed as a 2025 major supporter and a current supporter on the partners page (2025 annual report, stated by the space). The map lists Bloomberg as a campaigned name; no campaign aimed at this museum was found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the museum is mostly admiring. The criticism on record targets the 2011 painting sale and the restrictive terms of Still's will.
1. CultureGrrl (ArtsJournal): Lee Rosenbaum, "Donor Intent Violation: Sotheby's to Auction Denver's Four Clyfford Stills on Nov. 9" (2011)
URL: https://artsjournal.com/culturegrrl/2011/09/sothebys_to_auction_denvers_fo.html
- The sale broke the no-sale terms in the wills of Clyfford and Patricia Still, with a court's permission.
- The museum had said it needed only $25 million for its endowment, so the sale bought "a fatter cushion".
- The few-day window for a museum to buy the works was "mere window-dressing".
2. Adobe Airstream: Leanne Goebel, "Clyfford Still Museum to Open with Fanfare, and Controversy" (2011)
URL: https://leannegoebel.com/2011/11/15/clyfford-still-museum-to-open-with-fanfare-and-controversy-from-adobeairstream-com/
- Reports that Denver Post critic Kyle MacMillan took the museum to task over the sale.
- Goebel defends it: the city owned the works, so it was not a deaccession, and the four paintings were about 2% of Still's output.
3. ArtsJournal: Douglas McLennan, "A Closer Look At Denver's Still Deal" (2004)
URL: https://artsjournal.org/?p=25418
- The deal is restrictive: no work can be sold and no other artist can be shown, which rules out varied temporary exhibitions.
- Doubts Still's name alone will draw a wide audience.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on its own endowment, created by selling four Still paintings in 2011, plus Denver's arts sales tax and local donors. The weak point is the Anschutz Foundation, whose money comes from an oil and gas fortune that is still drilling. Worth watching: the size of the Anschutz gift, which the annual report does not give.
