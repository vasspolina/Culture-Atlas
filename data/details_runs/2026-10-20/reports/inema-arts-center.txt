INEMA ARTS CENTER: Kigali, Rwanda
ID: inema-arts-center
Website: https://inemaartcenter.com

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre with a gallery and a cafe at KG 563 St, Kacyiru, Kigali (Inema website).
- Founded in 2012 by the brothers Innocent Nkurunziza and Emmanuel Nkuranga, both self-taught painters (Inema website). Wikipedia lists six painters, all brothers or siblings, as founders: Nkurunziza, Nkuranga, Kenneth Nkusi, Denis Mpabuka, Timothy Akimanzi and Joseph Ntwali.
- Stated aims: to unlock Rwanda's artistic potential, bring communities together through creative expression and create economic opportunities for underserved artists (stated by the space).
- Building: National Geographic Traveller describes it as housed in a former ambassador's residence, opened 13 years before the article (Zoey Goto, "How Kigali's creativity is helping the Rwandan capital step into the limelight", 29 July 2026).
- On site: work by 10 local artists including co-founder Nkurunziza, and rare Congolese masks, some dating back to the 13th century (National Geographic Traveller). The website also lists creative courses and a School of Art.
- Visiting: Monday to Friday 10am to 8pm, weekends 10am to 2pm, closed on public holidays. No ticket is needed for the galleries, exhibitions or guided tours, some events need free registration, and groups of ten or more should book (Inema website).

FUNDING
Inema Arts Center: privately founded by two painter brothers
- The centre was founded by Nkurunziza and Nkuranga, who are painters themselves (Inema website).
- Entry to the galleries, exhibitions and guided tours is free.
- The website lists creative courses and a cafe.

BOTTOM LINE
Inema was founded in 2012 by the painter brothers Innocent Nkurunziza and Emmanuel Nkuranga, and entry to its galleries and guided tours is free.
