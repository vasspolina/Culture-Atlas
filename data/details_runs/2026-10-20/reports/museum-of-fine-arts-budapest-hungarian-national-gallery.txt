MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS BUDAPEST / HUNGARIAN NATIONAL GALLERY: Budapest, Hungary
ID: museum-of-fine-arts-budapest-hungarian-national-gallery
Website: https://www.szepmuveszeti.hu (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- The Museum of Fine Arts (Szépművészeti Múzeum) stands on Heroes' Square in Budapest and opened in 1906; Albert Schickedanz and Fülöp Herzog designed it, and it was built between 1900 and 1906 (Wikipedia).
- It holds over 100,000 non-Hungarian works in six departments (Wikipedia).
- László Baán has led the museum since 2004 (Wikipedia); USA Art News (2026) calls him director general of it and the National Gallery.
- It was extensively restored under Liget Budapest, a park-renewal plan that also produced the House of Hungarian Music and a new Museum of Ethnography building (USA Art News).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays; last entry 17:00.

FUNDING
Museum of Fine Arts Budapest: mainly public: Hungarian state, with corporate sponsors for exhibitions
- The museum's sponsor page states that the Ministry for Social Relations and Culture is its maintainer (fenntartó), not a sponsor.
- 2026 sponsors named on the page, with what each supports: OTP Bank (main sponsor: the graphic exhibition space and two graphic shows); E.ON Energiamegoldások (main sponsor: the Egyptian exhibition); MBH Bank (main sponsor: the Gauguin exhibition); PPG Trilak (key supporter: "Vasarely 120"); MVM Group (key supporter: "Vasarely 120"); Szerencsejáték Zrt. (key supporter: the Egyptian exhibition); Veolia Energia Magyarország (key supporter: the Gauguin exhibition).
- Partners: KLM and K&H Bank (Dutch and Flemish rooms of the Old Picture Gallery); Laterex Építő (European collection additions); the Dutch embassy has supported restoration of Dutch and Flemish works for nearly ten years. Cooperation partners include Air France-KLM, Danubius Hotels and Porsche Hungária.
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (MVM Group, key supporter)
- MVM Group is 100% owned by the Hungarian state (Wikipedia). Its sponsor-page listing is for 2026. Its member companies include electricity and gas network operators, a gas storage company, retailers and the Paks nuclear power company (MVM website); Wikipedia says it entered the gas industry in 2011. No revenue split by business line found.
- No reporting found of a campaign aimed at MVM's sponsorship of the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself. Most criticism found concerns the Liget Budapest project and its director Baán, and a 2026 decision to drop its final building.
1. USA Art News: Sarah Mitchell, "Hungary Scraps New National Gallery Plans in City Park" (8 September 2026, sourced from The Art Newspaper)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/hungary-scraps-new-national-gallery-plans-in-city-park/
- Reports that Hungary's new government under Prime Minister Péter Magyar scrapped the planned 50,000 sq. m New National Gallery in City Park (about 100 billion forints); a government decree of 18 June halted the remaining work on the Liget project.
- Baán hopes the SANAA design can be built elsewhere. Tamás Don, president of AICA Hungary, asked whether relocation is the only solution.
2. hvg.hu: Erna Sághy, "Kétségek a múzeumi negyed ötletpályázata körül" (no date shown on the page)
URL: https://hvg.hu/kultura/201345_ketsegek_a_muzeumi_negyed_otletpalyazata_ko
- Reports architects were divided over the museum quarter plan; joint competition winner István Berkeczi wrote that the winners built on the organiser's flawed ideas, and critics objected to building on City Park.
- Says Baán, the museum quarter's ministerial commissioner, drives the project and cites a payback of 10 to 15 years that no impact studies support. Total budget 150 billion forints.
- Hungarian news weekly, not art press.

WIDER CONTEXT
- National Cultural Fund (NKA): around 30 April 2026 Baán resigned from the NKA committee, writing to the minister that many grants, some very large, did not match the fund's basic aims. Outgoing culture minister Balázs Hankó said the tenders followed the rules; incoming prime minister Péter Magyar said an investigation was needed (Economx, 5 May 2026).
- Liget Budapest protests: activists occupied part of City Park from 17 March 2016 when workers began clearing trees for the museum quarter; an Ipsos survey cited by Waging Nonviolence (29 June 2016) found 75 percent of Budapest respondents opposed new facilities there. A letter from 13 signatories to UNESCO and ICOMOS (undated; page metadata shows 2017) said Liget Budapest "would cause irreversible damage to the World Heritage Site and its buffer zone" on Andrássy Avenue; it does not name the Museum of Fine Arts.
- Connection: Baán was ministerial commissioner for the museum quarter (hvg.hu), and the museum was restored under Liget Budapest.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-maintained and its 2026 sponsors are named by exhibition, but no amounts are published, and one key supporter is the state-owned MVM Group. The weak points are MVM's role as a gas and electricity group and the director's close tie to the Liget Budapest project, which drew protests. Worth watching: what follows the scrapped New National Gallery.
