TOWNER EASTBOURNE: Eastbourne, UK
ID: towner-eastbourne
Website: https://townereastbourne.org.uk (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery with a cinema in Devonshire Park, College Road, Eastbourne BN21 4JJ, beside the Congress Theatre (Towner website; Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1920 through a bequest from Alderman John Chisholm Towner, who left 22 paintings and £6,000 for a gallery for the people of Eastbourne. It opened in 1923 in the Manor House in Old Town (Wikipedia).
- Named for John Chisholm Towner (Wikipedia). Formerly the Towner Art Gallery.
- Building: the council sold the Manor House in 2005 to 2006. The gallery reopened in 2009 in a purpose-built concrete building designed by Rick Mather Architects at a cost of £8.6 million, funded in part by the Heritage Lottery Fund and the council. German artist Lothar Götz added an exterior mural, "Dance Diagonal", in 2019, and the ground floor and cinema were remodelled in 2021 to 2022 (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, a cinema, a collection of more than 5,000 works including the broadest collection of Eric Ravilious's work, a learning programme, a shop, a cafe and a second-floor restaurant. It also runs a creative programme in Newhaven (Towner website). It hosted the Turner Prize 2023 (Studio International).
- Visiting: the Ravilious Collection is open Tuesday to Sunday and bank holiday Mondays and is free but ticketed; current exhibitions are ticketed from pay-what-you-can at £5 to £14. The page read gives no accessibility information.

FUNDING
Towner Eastbourne: mainly public and grant-funded: Arts Council England and Eastbourne Borough Council, plus trusts, trading and the Newhaven Enterprise Zone
- Total income for the year to 31 March 2025 was £3,554,485 (2024: £3,665,931), including £667,920 of works donated to the collection. Expenditure was £2,641,316 and total reserves £6,220,662; unrestricted free reserves were £1,115,703 (2025 accounts).
- Arts Council England: £537,962 secured in both 2025 and 2024 (2025 accounts). On 4 November 2022 Towner announced that it had been awarded National Portfolio Organisation funding from Arts Council England for 2023 to 2026 (Towner website).
- Eastbourne Borough Council: £314,000 in 2025 and £413,880 in 2024 (2025 accounts). The council had paid £614,000 a year since 2015, then voted on 21 February 2018 to cut it; the gallery's statement called this a "50% disinvestment within four years" (The Art Newspaper, 2018).
- Other income in 2025: Newhaven Creative, funded by the Newhaven Enterprise Zone, £190,513; trading about £623,706; donations and legacies £303,683 (2025 accounts).
- Trusts: the Rick Mather David Sieff Foundation funded environmental sustainability work including solar panels; the Fidelity Foundation funded a CRM system; the Art Fund supported an acquisition and the Reimagine fund; the National Lottery Heritage Fund awarded a development grant for Black Robin Farm. No amounts are stated for these (2025 accounts).
- Governance: the council passed operation to an independent charitable trust in 2014 but kept ownership of the building and collection (Wikipedia). Stephen Welton CBE became chair in November 2024, succeeding David Dimbleby, who became president. The auditor found no material uncertainty on going concern.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of Towner itself. The press found concerns the 2018 council funding cut, which Towner's chair and the Art Fund opposed, and a 2023 review of the Turner Prize that praises the gallery.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Towner Art Gallery on England's south coast at risk as local council confirms funding cut" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/02/22/towner-art-gallery-on-englands-south-coast-at-risk-as-local-council-confirms-funding-cut
- Reports the council vote of 21 February 2018. Council leader David Tutt said continuing to support Towner at about £800k "is no longer a viable proposition".
- Chair David Dimbleby said a cut put at risk "everything that Towner stands for"; Art Fund director Stephen Deuchar said it "could even threaten [Towner's] existence".
- Criticism is of the council, not of Towner.
2. Studio International: Beth Williamson, "Turner Prize 2023" (10 October 2023)
URL: https://studiointernational.com/index.php/turner-prize-2023-review-towner-eastbourne-jesse-darling-ghislaine-leung-rory-pilgrim-barbara-walker
- Opens with a wry comparison of the prize to "a bit like a toddler having a tantrum", and finds Jesse Darling's Towner show weaker than earlier work: it "doesn't match the intelligence, surprise and poignancy" of No Medals No Ribbons.
- Praises the gallery as able to "punch above its weight"; its one reservation is that the success of the wraparound Eastbourne Alive programme "remains to be seen".
- Criticises the prize and the art, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Towner is public funding from Arts Council England and Eastbourne Borough Council, with lottery, trust and trading income, and no flagged-industry funder was found. The weak point is the council grant, which fell from £614,000 a year since 2015 (The Art Newspaper) to £314,000 in 2025. Worth watching: the next council and Arts Council funding rounds.
