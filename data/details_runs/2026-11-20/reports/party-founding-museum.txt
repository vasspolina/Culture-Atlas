PARTY FOUNDING MUSEUM: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: party-founding-museum
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the founding of the Workers' Party of Korea, on the south side of Haebang Hill in the Central District of Pyongyang, a short walk from the Koryo Hotel (Wikipedia, korea-dpr.com, Koryo Tours).
- Housed in a building completed on 30 November 1923 under Japanese rule as the South Pyongan Province commodity exhibition hall, later a department store, and after 1945 the Party's offices, where Kim Il Sung also worked (Korean Wikipedia, Wikipedia).
- Korean Wikipedia says the exterior is modelled on Japan's Diet building in reduced form, and Koryo Tours says it looks much like the National Diet in Tokyo. Korean Wikipedia says the building was destroyed in the Korean War and restored.
- CNN described a pre-war Japanese-style colonial building with photos and artifacts on the ground floor and the upper floor kept as in the Party's early days.
- Turned into the museum in October 1970 (KCNA, Wikipedia). Korean Wikipedia reports that Kim Il Sung ordered this when new party headquarters were built, and that Kim Jong Il changed its Korean name in the 1980s.
- KCNA claimed in 2009 that it holds two offices of Kim Il Sung, a reception room and a meeting hall arranged as they were, plus show rooms on the Party's founding.
- KCNA says his residence and a monument put up for the Party's 30th anniversary stand beyond the main building. Wikipedia counts the house as part of the museum and dates the monument to 10 October 1975.
- Koryo Tours says the guide plays a recording of Kim Il Sung's speech declaring the Party founded, and calls the museum "surprisingly little-visited".
- The state dates the founding to 10 October 1945. Korean Wikipedia says that was the day the North Korean Branch Bureau of the Korean Communist Party was formed.
- Kim Jong Un visited the museum with senior party officials on 8 October 2025, before the Party's 80th anniversary, gave a speech there and called it a "sacred sanctuary" of the Party's tradition (Reuters, citing KCNA). Members of the Party's central leadership body visited again for the 81st anniversary in October 2026 (North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification).

FUNDING
Party Founding Museum: state-funded: the Government of North Korea
- The building was put up and managed by the South Pyongan provincial government under Japanese rule, and its head was a provincial official (Korean Wikipedia).
- Korean Wikipedia calls mistaken a widely repeated story that a wealthy Pyongyang man, Pak Jong Sik, owned the building and gave it to Kim Il Sung as party offices.
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government and the Workers' Party, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).
- The museum presents the founding of the Party the order names, in what CNN calls the Party's original headquarters.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece from an outlet opened discusses the museum. It is reporting from a guided visit, not art criticism.
1. CNN: Tim Schwarz, "North Korea: Celebration reinforces Kim dynasty's iron grip on power" (2015)
URL: https://edition.cnn.com/2015/10/09/asia/north-korea-70th-anniversary/
- Describes a visit at the Party's 70th anniversary: groups taken room by room and lectured in Party history, with what he calls an almost religious reverence from the crowds.
- Notes portraits of Marx, Engels, Lenin and Stalin in the auditorium and no portrait of Mao Zedong.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the North Korean state's, and the Party uses it as a shrine to its own founding. The weak point is that the US blocking order covers both that state and that Party.
