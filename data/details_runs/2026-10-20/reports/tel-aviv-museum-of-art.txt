TEL AVIV MUSEUM OF ART: Tel Aviv, Israel
ID: tel-aviv-museum-of-art
Website: https://www.tamuseum.org.il (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Israel's first art museum for modern and contemporary Israeli and international art, at 27 Shaul HaMelech Boulevard, in the Golda Meir Cultural and Art Center, with the Eyal Ofer Pavilion at 6 Tarsat Boulevard (stated by the museum).
- Founded in 1932 in the house of Tel Aviv's first mayor, Meir Dizengoff, at 16 Rothschild Boulevard, given in memory of his wife Zina; the museum moved to Shaul HaMelech in 1971 (Wikipedia).
- The main building was designed by Dan Eytan and Yitzhak Yashar and is now named the Paulson Family Foundation Building; the Herta and Paul Amir Building by Preston Scott Cohen opened in 2011 (Wikipedia, the museum).
- Collection includes Klimt, Kandinsky and the Peggy Guggenheim Collection, donated in 1950 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Sunday; Monday, Wednesday and Saturday 10am to 6pm; Tuesday and Thursday 10am to 9pm; Friday 10am to 2pm; adults NIS 55; free for children and youth to 18; wheelchairs can be rented free (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Tel Aviv Museum of Art: privately funded in large part by donor families
- Paulson Family Foundation, led by John Paulson: 15 million dollars announced 7 June 2021 for the museum's 90th anniversary, for an upgrade of the main building and an endowment. The building took the foundation's name (stated by the museum).
- Eyal and Marilyn Ofer Family Foundation: 5 million dollars in 2019 to renovate the Helena Rubinstein Pavilion, renamed the Eyal Ofer Building for the Arts (Globes, 14 March 2019). The pavilion reopened in 2023 as the Eyal Ofer Pavilion (Wikipedia).
- Ingrid Flick gave 600,000 euros (about 670,000 dollars) in three instalments in 2013 and 2014 through the German Friends of the Tel Aviv Museum (USA Art News).
- The Zvi and Ofra Meitar Family supports the museum's website (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of city or state operating grants, a sponsor list, or published accounts.

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- June 2021: the #VisualArtsforPalestine campaign, backed by Turner Prize winners including Lawrence Abu Hamdan and Helen Cammock, named the museum among Israeli institutions whose signatories pledge not to exhibit with or sell to, saying it had partnered with agencies of Israel's government and never denounced apartheid (ArtReview, 1 June 2021).
- September 2025: more than 50 artists and performers, many Israeli, signed a letter asking Judy Chicago to withdraw her show "What If Women Ruled the World?" over Gaza. Director Tania Coen-Uzzielli said cancelling "would not be an act of solidarity, but of surrender" (Hyperallergic, ArtReview).
- Nadya Tolokonnikova, co-creator of the quilt at the show's centre, said she agrees with the letter. The museum said the quilt was loaned by the company DMINTI (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: Flick donation
- July 2023: the museum said it was "thoroughly reviewing" the Flick gift after reports that her late husband's family fortune traces to Friedrich Flick, convicted as a war criminal in 1947. Flick said the donation "has nothing to do with the history of my late husband's family". No final decision was reported (USA Art News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns the museum's position on Gaza and the boycott, not its funders; the donor reporting is a news item.
1. Hyperallergic: Isa Farfan, "Israeli Artists Urge Judy Chicago to Withdraw Tel Aviv Exhibition" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1044626/israeli-artists-urge-judy-chicago-to-withdraw-tel-aviv-exhibition/
- Signatories argue showing socially conscious art in a country they say is committing genocide in Gaza lets it appear progressive.
- Guy Ben-Ner: "to save Gaza (and Israel from itself), boycott Israel. Isolate it."
2. ArtReview: staff, "Judy Chicago and Nadya Tolokonnikova face calls to cancel Tel Aviv exhibition" (2025)
URL: https://artreview.com/judy-chicago-and-nadya-tolokonnikova-face-calls-to-cancel-tel-aviv-exhibition
- Letter signatories cite a UN Women estimate of more than 28,000 women and girls killed in Gaza; the director says the museum is "horrified by the devastation and pain in Gaza".
3. ArtReview: staff, "Artists denounce 'Israeli apartheid'; renew calls for boycott" (2021)
URL: https://artreview.com/artists-denounce-israeli-apartheid-renew-calls-for-boycott/
- Campaign critics say the museum partnered with Israeli government agencies and never denounced apartheid.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is large gifts from donor families, with Paulson at 15 million dollars and Ofer at 5 million dollars, and nothing read links these to a flagged industry. The weak points are the boycott pledge naming the museum, the 2025 letter over the Chicago show, and the undecided review of the Flick gift. Worth watching: the outcome of that review and the missing public funding figures.
