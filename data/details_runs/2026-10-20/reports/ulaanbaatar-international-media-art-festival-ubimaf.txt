ULAANBAATAR INTERNATIONAL MEDIA ART FESTIVAL (UBIMAF): Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia
ID: ulaanbaatar-international-media-art-festival-ubimaf
Website: https://artscouncil.mn/art_program_mon/ulaanbaatar-international-media-art-festival (checked 2026-10-25; live, a page of the organiser's site; it describes only the 2023 edition)

OVERVIEW
- Festival of media art in Ulaanbaatar, run by the Arts Council of Mongolia (ACM), a non-governmental organisation. ACM states that it has organised the festival since 2015.
- The first edition ran from 9 June to 9 July 2016 under the theme "Tradition and Modernity", with 18 artists from nine countries (ASEF Culture360).
- ACM was established in 2002 by Mongolian arts, civic and business leaders supported by the Mongolian Foundation for an Open Society (Soros Foundation) (ASEF Culture360, 1 June 2017).
- The 2020 edition was held online from 18 June to 8 July under the theme "Mirage" (ASEF Culture360 review).
- 2022 (7th edition), 16 to 23 June, theme "Divoccinated": venues in Ulaanbaatar included the Zanabazar Fine Art Museum, the Bogd Khan Palace Museum and the White Rock Center, plus an "Eastern Gobi" train trip (ASEF Culture360).
- 2023 (8th edition), 23 to 30 May: works by 23 artists from six countries, shown free of charge at three locations (ACM).
- 2024 (9th edition): 6 or 7 to 19 May (ASEF Culture360 gives 6 May, Hunter College gives 7 May); theme "Seeing is Believing", curated by the Berlin-based curator Inga Seidler (ASEF Culture360); venues the Mongolian National Art Gallery and Red Ger Gallery (Hunter College).
- Visiting: free in 2023 (ACM). No later admission details found.

FUNDING
Ulaanbaatar International Media Art Festival: NGO-run, funded by foreign cultural bodies and embassies; no funder list for later editions
- 2016: Asia-Europe Foundation (ASEF) and Arts Network Asia, through the Creative Encounters programme; Goethe-Institut Mongolia, the Liaison Office of the Academy of Media Arts Cologne and AD Compagnie as supporters; the U.S. Embassy and Xacbank as official sponsors (ASEF Culture360). No amounts are given.
- 2020: the Arts Council organised it with support from TodaysArt Festival of the Netherlands (ASEF Culture360 review).
- 2022: Goethe-Institut Mongolia as partner, Blue Sun contemporary art centre as collaborator, and the Taiwan Foundation for Democracy and the Taipei Trade and Economic Representative Office in Ulaanbaatar as supporters (ASEF Culture360). No amounts are given.
- 2023 and 2024: the pages read name no organiser, partner or funder beyond ACM itself.
- The Arts Council: in 2006 the arts manager Tserenpil Ariunaa said it raised around $120,000 every year, and organisers had recruited a member of parliament, an ambassador and business people to its board; the board later included three ambassadors (Seattle Times, 12 February 2006). ACM's own pages read name no governing council and no accounts.
- The Ulaanbaatar Biennale 2025, a separate ACM project, was initiated by the Capital City Governor's Office, with ACM as main programmer (ACM). No reporting found of a government body funding the festival itself.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, gambling, tobacco, arms or Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The one review found is largely positive.
1. ASEF Culture360: Nominbileg P., "Losing and finding our reality | A review of Ulaanbaatar International Media Art Festival" (6 August 2020)
URL: https://culture360.asef.org/insights/losing-and-finding-our-reality-review-ulaanbaatar-international-media-art-festival
- Reviews the 2020 edition, held virtually; the review is mostly positive and says the festival has run with TodaysArt for three years.
- Reports panellists saying the main challenge for artists in digitisation is finding a way to monetise their work, and calling for government support for the arts.
- Does not criticise the festival or its funders. ASEF is also a named supporter of the 2016 edition.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the festival, as far as editions with named supporters show, comes from foreign cultural institutes, embassies and exchange programmes; no flagged-industry or state funder turned up. The weak points are that no accounts, funder list or governing council are published for the 2023 and 2024 editions, and that the organiser's own page covers only 2023. Worth watching: who pays for later editions, given the organiser's work with the city government on the biennale.
