ESPACIO DE ARTE CONTEMPORÁNEO (EAC): Montevideo, Uruguay

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art space at Arenal Grande 1930, Montevideo, in the former Miguelete prison (Cárcel de Miguelete); Wikipedia calls it the only national public institution devoted solely to contemporary art.
- Opened in July 2010 from a project begun in 2008 by Uruguay's National Directorate of Culture (Dirección Nacional de Cultura) (Wikipedia).
- The prison was built in the late 19th century by the architect Juan Alberto Capurro on Jeremy Bentham's panopticon model, with a central watchtower and four radial three-storey wings, and held prisoners for over a century until it closed in the 1990s (Wikipedia). It is a national historic monument (Artefacto, 2021).
- The EAC occupies the wing on Arenal Grande; the National Museum of Natural History enters from Miguelete street, and the site also holds Sala M and a photo gallery of the Centro de Fotografía de Montevideo. Sala Seis, a 160 square metre gallery, opened in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Programme: open calls for individual, collective and curatorial proposals, residencies, training and research, a public programme of talks, workshops and guided tours, and the LAB EAC; since 2018 it has shown the National Visual Arts Prize, co-produced with the National Institute of Visual Arts (INAV) (Wikipedia).
- Season: the international exhibition "Are You for Real: Cosmotechnics, Counterfictions, and the Art of Worldbuilding" opened at the EAC on 23 April 2026, and the second "1825" residency for ten artists runs in two periods, 7 to 28 October and 5 to 26 November 2026 (Ministry of Education and Culture).
- Galleries open Wednesday to Saturday, 13:00 to 19:00; plazas and photo gallery daily, 9:00 to 19:00; free entry (Ministry of Education and Culture notice, 23 March 2026).

FUNDING
Espacio de Arte Contemporáneo: public: Uruguay's Ministry of Education and Culture
- The ministry lists the EAC among the museums of its National Directorate of Culture (Ministry of Education and Culture).
- The founding project had support from the Spanish Agency for International Development Cooperation (AECID) (Wikipedia).
- In 2022 the National Directorate of Culture sought a special allocation of 20 million pesos for 2023, and a similar sum for 2024, for buildings in critical condition, saying its yearly maintenance budget of about 25 million pesos was not enough; the EAC was among the sites needing work (El Observador, 2022).
- The 2026 exhibition was backed by Germany's ifa (Institut für Auslandsbeziehungen) with the Goethe-Institut, with the National Directorate of Culture through INAV (Ministry of Education and Culture).
- The "1825" residency is run by INAV with the EAC; each place includes lodging at the EAC and travel to Montevideo for artists who live outside the capital department (Ministry of Education and Culture).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money from Uruguay's culture ministry, with foreign cultural agencies backing single projects. The weak point is the upkeep of a 19th-century monument on a stretched maintenance budget.
