FONDAZIONE PRADA: Milan, Italy
ID: fondazione-prada
Website: https://www.fondazioneprada.org (checked 2026-10-20; its pages refused automated reading, so visit details are not confirmed)

OVERVIEW
- Art foundation with its main venue at Largo Isarco 2 in the south of Milan, a second Milan space, the Osservatorio, on the fifth and sixth floors of the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, and a Venice venue, Ca' Corner della Regina on the Grand Canal (Wikipedia; Fondazione Prada institutional report).
- Its programme began in 1993; Miuccia Prada and Patrizio Bertelli have co-chaired it since 1995. It held 24 solo shows in Milan from 1993 to 2010 and opened Ca' Corner della Regina in 2011 (Wikipedia; Prada Group).
- The Milan venue opened on 9 May 2015 in a former distillery from the 1910s, converted by OMA under Rem Koolhaas: seven old buildings and three new ones, Podium, Cinema and the 60-metre Torre; 19,000 square metres, 11,000 for exhibitions (Prada Group; Wikipedia).
- The "Haunted House" is clad in 24-carat gold leaf; Wes Anderson designed the bar after old Milanese cafés; the Torre holds the permanent "Atlas" display from the Prada collection and a restaurant (Wikipedia; Prada Group).
- Programme: exhibitions, a cinema with screenings and talks, conferences and a children's academy (Prada Group; institutional report).
- Visiting: no current official hours or prices could be read; listings disagree. No reporting found on accessibility.

FUNDING
Fondazione Prada: corporate: a sponsorship of the Prada Group, plus ticket, catalogue and sponsorship income
- The Prada Group lists the foundation as "a Sponsorship of Prada Group" (Prada Group).
- Net revenue was €28,319,392 in 2017, €28,315,462 in 2018 and €25,233,570 in 2019, from tickets, guided tours, catalogues and "considerable" sponsorship contracts; it received no donations in any of the three years (institutional report 2017 to 2019).
- 2019: operating costs €22.0 million; leases and rentals were 28% of operating costs because the foundation "does not own the exhibition venues"; net profit €174,050 (institutional report).
- Board in 2019: Miuccia Prada (chair), Carlo Mazzi, Giò Marconi, Massimo Sterpi, Cristian Valsecchi, Astrid Regina Welter; directors are unpaid (institutional report).
- No reporting found of who owns the Largo Isarco site, of the sponsors' names, or of any accounts after 2019.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found that names Fondazione Prada. The broader critique of luxury-brand foundations in Europe targets Louis Vuitton and Pinault, and coverage of Prada's foundation is mostly admiring.
1. Kunstkritikk: Georges Didi-Huberman, Giorgio Agamben, Pierre Alferi and others, "Is Art a Mere Luxury Good?" (2014)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.no/is-art-a-mere-luxury-good
- Argues luxury patrons are "only speculators" and corporate foundations turn art into branding.
- Demands that public institutions stop serving "the interests of big private groups".
- Does not name Fondazione Prada; aimed at Fondation Louis Vuitton.
2. Apollo: Jane Morris, "Is the art world sitting in the lap of luxury?" (2024)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/art-fairs-auction-houses-luxury-goods-collaborations-sothebys-paris/
- Describes luxury brands building arts programmes and museums, "including Prada, Cartier and Louis Vuitton".
- Does not name Fondazione Prada or criticise it directly.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In December 2018 Prada withdrew its Pradamalia figurines Otto and Toto after civil rights lawyer Chinyere Ezie called one "racist and denigrating #blackface imagery" and called for a boycott; Prada said they were "not intended to have any reference to the real world and certainly not blackface" (Hypebae).
- The boycott call targeted the Prada brand, the foundation's sponsor, not the foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
Fondazione Prada is the Prada Group's own art programme: the group sponsors it, and in 2017 to 2019 it ran on tickets, catalogues and sponsorship with no donations. No flagged funder is on record. The weak points are that sponsors are not named and no accounts are posted after 2019; worth watching is who owns the Largo Isarco site it leases.
