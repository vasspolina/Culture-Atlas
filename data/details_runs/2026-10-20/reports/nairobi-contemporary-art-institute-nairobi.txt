NAIROBI CONTEMPORARY ART INSTITUTE: Nairobi, Kenya
ID: nairobi-contemporary-art-institute-nairobi
Website: https://www.ncai254.com

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit visual art space in Nairobi focused on contemporary art in East Africa; it describes its mission as "the growth and preservation" of that art (Contemporary And).
- Founded in 2020 by the Kenyan-British artist Michael Armitage; Wikipedia says he and Mukami Kuria, with other artists, saw the need for non-profit art and education spaces in Nairobi. It opened to the public in January 2022 with a survey of paintings by the Kenyan artist Sane Wadu.
- The Gathering, a 2017 event that Wikipedia says drew 52 artists from 12 African countries, contributed to the establishment of NCAI in 2020.
- Wikipedia says it started in a commercial building in the Rosslyn area; Contemporary And reports COVID delayed the opening of the physical space and that the long-term plan is a permanent space.
- Programmes listed on its site: exhibitions, events, education outreach, the TURN2 curatorial residency, offsite projects, The Gathering, artist films and talks, the Ujuzi programme and publications. It is also building an East African art library and archive.
- Exhibitions include 60 Years: Selections from the NCAI Collection (August and September 2024), curated by Don Handa, which Contemporary And reports was free and open to the public. NCAI is included in the 2026 Venice Biennale exhibition In Minor Keys (David Zwirner).

FUNDING
Nairobi Contemporary Art Institute: the founder's own money, benefit sales, gifts from a gallery, individuals and institutions, and a German Federal Cultural Foundation launch for TURN2
- In an interview with Anaël Pigeat published on 7 December 2021 Armitage told The Art Newspaper France that NCAI's funding was for now his own responsibility ("Pour l'instant, cela relève de ma responsabilité !" in his words, translated here).
- In the same interview he said he also received generous support from White Cube gallery; a lithograph he made, Dream and Refuge, raised funds for NCAI and two other organisations, and half the profits of a sweater designed with Stella Jean and the Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo went to NCAI's educational projects (The Art Newspaper France, translated).
- Contemporary And reports that NCAI receives contributions from several individuals, institutions and galleries; its own site says the library and archive are built with book donations and support from art institutions and galleries in Kenya and abroad.
- The TURN2 residency page credits the German Federal Cultural Foundation with launching the programme, in partnership with the Centre for Arts and Urbanistics (ZK/U) and the Triangle Network.
- Business Daily (Margaretta wa Gacheru, "A fortnight of best East African art", 3 November 2022) calls NCAI "one of the few art institutions where art is not for sale" and says Armitage "doesn't seem concerned about gaining government support" for it.
- David Zwirner gallery's news page describes NCAI as the foundation of the artist Michael Armitage.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Contemporary And's review of one show is favourable; its critical notes concern explanatory context in that show and access to art in Nairobi in general.
1. Contemporary And: Mūhunyo Maina, "An Exhibition Aims at Accessibility Against a Backdrop of Protest in Kenya's Capital" (17 October 2024)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/an-exhibition-aims-at-accessibility-against-a-backdrop-of-protest-in-kenyas-capital
- Finds the 60 Years show well curated in an aesthetic sense, with curator Don Handa linking works from across East Africa and the continent by style and theme.
- Says "perhaps more could have been done" to give a general audience historical context on lesser-known artists such as Louis Mwaniki, Kepha Sempanghi and Josephine Alacu.
- Argues that art viewing in Nairobi remains a privilege of the elite, citing suburban gallery locations, high prices, English-language materials and jargon; the point is about the city's art scene, not a charge against NCAI.

BOTTOM LINE
The documented money behind NCAI is the founder's own funds, proceeds from a benefit lithograph and sweater, contributions from White Cube gallery, other galleries, institutions and individuals, and the German Federal Cultural Foundation, which launched its TURN2 residency programme.
