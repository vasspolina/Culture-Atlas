WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2004 the Spoliation Advisory Panel agreed that a Chardin still life from the sale of the stock of A. S. Drey, a Jewish-owned gallery in Munich, "had been subject to a forced sale"; the heirs accepted a £10,000 ex gratia payment from Glasgow City Council and the painting stayed in the collection (Glasgow Life).
- In 2014 the panel concluded that Burrell's acquisition of the tapestry The Visitation, from the collection of Emma Ranette Budge, was the result of a forced sale; Glasgow City Council made an ex gratia payment reflecting its current market value, the estate released its claim and the tapestry stayed in the collection (Glasgow Life).
- Unverified: on 31 March 2022, as the museum reopened, the Campaign Against Antisemitism, an advocacy group, wrote that a book by the Glasgow Museums curator Martin Bellamy, A Collector's Life: William Burrell, maintains that more works than previously acknowledged belonged to Jewish owners who gave them up through "forced sale", and that Glasgow Life accepted such works are on display without identifying which. It quoted the historian Sir Tom Devine, "I find the refusal rather curious", and Ephraim Borowski of the Scottish Council of Jewish Communities calling on public galleries "to acknowledge the dubious history of items in their collection".
