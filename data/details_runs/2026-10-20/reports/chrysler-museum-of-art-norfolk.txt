CHRYSLER MUSEUM OF ART: Norfolk, USA
ID: chrysler-museum-of-art-norfolk
Website: https://chrysler.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at One Memorial Place, on the border between downtown Norfolk and the Ghent district, Virginia, with about 50 galleries and more than 35,000 objects (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Founded in 1933 as the Norfolk Museum of Arts and Sciences; renamed after Walter P. Chrysler Jr. brought his collection of more than 10,000 works to Norfolk in 1971 (stated by the space).
- Named for Walter P. Chrysler Jr., son of the founder of the Chrysler Corporation; he met his wife Jean Outland Chrysler while stationed in Norfolk during the Second World War, and at his death the museum received three-quarters of the Chrysler Family Trust (Wikipedia).
- The buildings belong to the City of Norfolk, and the museum uses them without charge; an expansion and renovation of the main building reopened on 10 May 2014 (2024 audited statements, Wikipedia).
- Also runs the Perry Glass Studio (opened 2011), the Moses Myers House of about 1792 and a research library (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 10am to 5pm and Sunday noon to 5pm, closed Mondays; admission and parking are free; wheelchair accessible; the Myers House has limited access (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Chrysler Museum of Art: city-backed and donor-funded: City of Norfolk, endowment, members and private donors
- The City of Norfolk gave $2,753,955 in operating support in the year to June 2024 and $2,627,396 the year before: 36% and 37.9% of total support and revenue from operating activities (2024 audited statements).
- The city appoints 15 of the 28 trustees and is a related party (2024 audited statements).
- On 14 March 2022 the city awarded $15 million to the museum's capital campaign; $10.5 million of conditional pledges for the museum and Glass Studio expansion remained at June 2024 ($13.5 million a year earlier), the majority conditionally committed by the city (2024 audited statements).
- A $55 million capital campaign, the city and the Commonwealth of Virginia paid for the Glass Studio expansion (Wikipedia).
- Endowment: $74,295,282 donor-restricted and $34,833,448 board-designated at June 2024 (2024 audited statements).
- Corporate partners: Dollar Tree Family Dollar and Sentara at $25,000; KPMG, Towne Bank, McGuireWoods, Newport News Shipbuilding and nine others at $5,000 (stated by the space).

Red flags: arms (Newport News Shipbuilding)
- Newport News Shipbuilding is a current $5,000 corporate partner (stated by the space).
- It is a division of Huntington Ingalls Industries and the sole designer, builder and refueller of US Navy aircraft carriers, and one of two builders of Navy submarines (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter over the sponsorship.

Red flags: the Chrysler Jr. collection and provenance
- In 1962 the Art Dealers Association of America said 90 of 187 paintings from Chrysler's collection shown at the National Gallery of Canada were fakes; the suspect works were removed when the collection moved to Norfolk (Wikipedia).
- The museum held Peter Stephenson's 1850 marble "The Wounded Indian", which entered the collection in 1986; Hyperallergic states Chrysler Jr. bought it from the collector James Ricau, while the Massachusetts Charitable Mechanic Association said it had gone missing from its collection in 1958.
- In August 2023 the museum agreed to return the statue to the association, with no payment (USA Art News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's programme. The press record centres on a provenance dispute over one sculpture, which ended in 2023 when the museum agreed to return it.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Chrysler Museum Asked to Return Allegedly 'Stolen' Statue" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/825788/chrysler-museum-asked-to-return-allegedly-stolen-peter-stephenson-statue/
- The association's attorney, Greg Werkheiser, said Ricau lied about the statue's origins and the museum withheld documents; Neil disputed this. Neil also told the Washington Post that the museum lacks full provenance on all objects in Chrysler Jr.'s collection.
- Director Erik Neil said "there has never been any indication the statue was stolen" and that this was not looting or grave robbing.
2. USA Art News: "Chrysler Museum of Art will return disputed sculpture of Indigenous man" (2023)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/chrysler-museum-of-art-will-return-disputed-sculpture-of-indigenous-man
- An earlier deal with a $200,000 payment fell through; the association's lawyers said the museum backed out and asked the FBI to investigate. The museum said no deal existed.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on City of Norfolk money, a large endowment and local donors and companies, and the city both owns the buildings and appoints most of the board. The weak point is a current corporate partner that builds Navy warships, at a small $5,000 level and with no protest on record. Worth watching: provenance gaps in the Chrysler Jr. collection, which Neil acknowledged during the 2023 statue dispute.
