HE ART MUSEUM: Foshan, China
ID: he-art-museum-foshan
Website: https://hem.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private non-profit art museum (HEM, 和美术馆) in the Shunde district of Foshan, Guangdong, opposite the headquarters of the appliance maker Midea (Al Jazeera; e-flux).
- Founded by the collector He Jianfeng, son of Midea founder He Xiangjian; it opened on 1 October 2020 after Covid-19 delayed a March opening (ArtAsiaPacific; The Art Newspaper).
- The name He (和) means harmony, balance and mixture and takes the founder's family name (Wikipedia).
- Tadao Ando, commissioned in 2015, designed the RMB 220 million (USD 31 million), 16,000 square metre building, with 8,000 square metres of exhibition space and a concrete double-helix staircase (ArtAsiaPacific).
- The He family collection of more than 500 works covers international contemporary, Chinese modern and Chinese contemporary art, with Lingnan School ink painting by Gao Jianfu, Gao Qifeng and Chen Shu-ren (e-flux; ArtAsiaPacific).
- The founding director Shao Shu, formerly of Shanghai's Long Museum, is executive director (ArtAsiaPacific; The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- The museum sold around 400,000 tickets in 2024 and expected about 20% fewer in 2025 (The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- On view from 6 July to 15 November 2026: Qi Lan, In the Wake of Blossoms, the artist's first solo show in the Lingnan region (HEM).
- Visiting: open daily 10am to 6pm, last entry 5.30pm (HEM); admission is 150 yuan, with an average of just under 100 yuan per head after discounts (The Art Newspaper, 2025). The museum calls itself one of the first pet-friendly museums in China (HEM).

FUNDING
He Art Museum: family-funded: the He family's Midea fortune, through a foundation, with tickets and merchandise
- The museum describes itself as family established and private (e-flux) and receives support from a foundation, which The Art Newspaper does not name (The Art Newspaper, Lisa Movius, 6 November 2025).
- Shao Shu said the funding structure is "quite healthy" and would not be easily influenced "if the company made bad investments"; tickets and merchandise cover exhibition costs, with a goal of full independence after a decade (The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- The museum does not run gallery-funded selling shows (The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- The family fortune comes from Midea Group. He Xiangjian set up the He Xiangjian Charity Foundation in December 2013 with 400 million yuan, renamed it He Charity Foundation in 2017, and pledged 100 million Midea shares and 2.4 billion yuan in cash to it (stated by Midea).

Red flags: 2025 report of Midea factories in the Uyghur labour-transfer scheme
- The Bureau of Investigative Journalism reported on 29 May 2025 that factories directly owned by big brands, "like those run by Midea and LG Electronics", took part in the Chinese government programme that sends Uyghur, Kazakh and Kyrgyz workers from Xinjiang to eastern factories (TBIJ, Daniel Murphy).
- A plastics factory filmed by one of the workers has supplied Midea (TBIJ).
- Midea did not respond to several requests for comment (TBIJ).
- The museum's founding money is the He family's Midea fortune.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is admiring and centres on Ando's building, the collection and the museum's finances; no critic has made a case against it.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "How China's private museums are navigating a post-boom era" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/11/06/china-private-museums-post-boom-era
- Not criticism: the curator Li Anqi names HEM among the private museums still operating with sound finances and reasonably paid professional staff.
- Shao Shu says free admission at state museums cuts into private museums' visitor numbers.

BOTTOM LINE
A family museum paid for with a Midea fortune through a foundation. Midea's reported part in the Uyghur labour-transfer programme is the weak point; the name of the paying foundation is worth knowing.
