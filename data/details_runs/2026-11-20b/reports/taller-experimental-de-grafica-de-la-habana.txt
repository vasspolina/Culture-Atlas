TALLER EXPERIMENTAL DE GRÁFICA DE LA HABANA: Havana, Cuba
ID: taller-experimental-de-grafica-de-la-habana
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Printmaking workshop and gallery in a former warehouse at Callejón del Chorro 62, beside the Plaza de la Catedral in Old Havana (OnCuba, 2022).
- Founded on 30 July 1962 in a small space on the Plaza de la Catedral that shared a portico with the Palacio del Conde de Lombillo; the muralist Orlando Suárez was its first director (OnCuba).
- EcuRed names the Chilean painter José Venturelli with Suárez as the main movers behind it, nicknamed the Taller de la Plaza; Prensa Latina (2025) names Suárez and José A. Díaz Peláez as founding artists.
- Its first presses and stones came from the Compañía Litográfica Cubana, a firm that printed labels for the tobacco industry (OnCuba).
- It moved to its current building in the 1990s, which has room for more than ten presses, its own gallery and a print cabinet holding work from its first lithographs on (EcuRed).
- Nine directors since 1962, the latest Yamilys Brito Jorge (OnCuba, 2022); ACN quoted her as director in June 2025.
- A committee chooses new member artists only every four or five years; the Smithsonian reported a March 2016 visit by Michelle, Malia and Sasha Obama (Smithsonian, 2016).
- It teaches the practical engraving course of Cuba's art schools (CNAP, 2013; EcuRed).
- For its 60th anniversary, "Dissonances, marges, fissures", with works by 67 Cuban artists, opened on 24 November 2022 at the University of Western Brittany in Brest, France, before touring northern France (OnCuba).
- For its 63rd anniversary in June 2025 it opened "In memoriam", for six members who died in 2024, in its Sala Rhino, and announced 30 works by the founder Alfredo Sosabravo for the Biblioteca Nacional in July (ACN; Prensa Latina).

FUNDING
Taller Experimental de Gráfica: earned income from print sales, courses and commissions; founded by the Cuban state and listed in 2013 by CNAP, a Ministry of Culture body
- Smithsonian magazine (2016) reports that in 1962 Che Guevara, as minister of industry, signed a mandate to give Cuban lithographers materials, space and machines, and that the workshop was born from it.
- CNAP's 2013 page lists the workshop among its institutions and describes its sales gallery, commissions for decoration, custom cards and reproductions (CNAP, archived 11 May 2013). CNAP's institutions page of December 2023 names its member institutions and the workshop is not among them.
- Sales: OnCuba describes a gallery for members' work and a shop that sells the prints made there; the Smithsonian reported gallery prices of 10 to 20 times the average monthly Cuban salary.
- Courses: classes in woodcut, lithography and etching cost 100 to 500 dollars in 2016; the author paid 300 dollars for six two-colour prints over two days (Smithsonian).
- Partners: Prensa Latina (2025) says the workshop hosts residencies and exhibitions in collaboration with institutions in Europe and the Americas.

BOTTOM LINE
The workshop's money comes from the Cuban state that founded it and from what it sells to visitors: prints, courses and commissions. There is no private sponsor of note. Worth watching: which state body it answers to now, since CNAP's 2023 list leaves it out.
