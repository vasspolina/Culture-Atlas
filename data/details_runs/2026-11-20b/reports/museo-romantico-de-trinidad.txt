MUSEO ROMÁNTICO DE TRINIDAD: Trinidad, Cuba
ID: museo-romantico-de-trinidad
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Museum of decorative arts in the Palacio Brunet, Calle Fernando Hernández Echerry 52 at Simón Bolívar, on the Plaza Mayor of Trinidad, Sancti Spíritus province (EcuRed; Spanish Wikipedia).
- Trinidad's historic centre has been on the UNESCO World Heritage list since 1988 (Prensa Latina; Spanish Wikipedia).
- The ground floor dates from 1740; José Mariano Borrell y Padrón bought the house in 1807 and added the upper floor in 1808 (EcuRed; Spanish Wikipedia).
- His daughter Ángela Borrell y Lemus inherited it in 1830 and married Nicolás Brunet y Muñoz, Count of Casa Brunet, that year (Cubanet, 2023); the family left for Spain in 1857 (EcuRed).
- The Asociación Pro Trinidad bought the house in 1945 and used it as its seat until 1964, when full restoration began (EcuRed).
- Opened as the Museo Romántico on 26 May 1974, the first building in Trinidad to become a museum (EcuRed).
- About 2,000 pieces, some 900 on show, in four sections: decorative arts, painting and drawing, sculpture, and prints; porcelain and glass from Bohemia, Baccarat and Sèvres (Escambray, 2024).
- The museum also holds the Centro de Documentación Manolo Bécquer (Escambray, 2024).
- It won the 2024 Museo del Año prize of ICOM's Cuban committee and the Consejo Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural; Isabel Rueda had directed it for more than 40 years (Escambray, 2024).

FUNDING
Museo Romántico de Trinidad: mainly public: the Ministry of Culture's heritage system, with its own ticket revenue
- The Consejo Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural of the Ministry of Culture works through provincial heritage centres to which museums are attached (council site, archived 24 July 2024); Prensa Latina's 2024 report on the Sancti Spíritus provincial heritage centre's summer programme includes this museum.
- A 2014 repair, part of the programme of the Oficina del Historiador de la Ciudad de Trinidad for the town's 500th anniversary, was paid from a budget that director Isabel Rueda told Radio Sancti Spíritus was not enough for all the building needed (Cubanet, 7 November 2015).
- In November 2015 Cubanet reported that the museum had been closed for a month after part of a ceiling fell and injured an employee; Rueda said there were no materials or money allocated for the repair.
- In 2024 Rueda said the museum had always had political support for its conservation, but, despite the money it takes each year, had no financing to fix the building or buy new pieces (Escambray, my translation of her quoted words).
- The museologist Carlos Enrique Sotolongo Peña named the work needed in 2024: the false ceiling, the murals and two roof beams (Escambray).

Not related: the Museo Romántico of Barranquilla, Colombia, owned by the Fundación Museo Cultural de Barranquilla, which in 2023 put the cost of restoring its house at about 1,850 million Colombian pesos (El Heraldo). It is a separate museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for by the Cuban state's heritage system and earns from tickets, with no private funder. The weak point is upkeep: repairs have been underfunded since at least 2014. Worth watching: whether the ceiling, murals and roof beams are repaired.
