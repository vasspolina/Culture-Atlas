NATIONAL GALLERY OF MODERN ART (NGMA): New Delhi, India
ID: national-gallery-of-modern-art-ngma
Website: https://ngmaindia.gov.in/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the pages read held only menu headings and no visitor details)

OVERVIEW
- India's national museum of modern art, at Jaipur House by India Gate in the Central Hexagon at the end of Rajpath, New Delhi.
- Established on 29 March 1954 by the Government of India; inaugurated by Vice-President S. Radhakrishnan with Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru present. Hermann Goetz, a German art historian, was its first curator (Wikipedia).
- Housed in Jaipur House, a former residence of the Maharaja of Jaipur, a butterfly-shaped building with a central dome built in 1936 and designed by Sir Arthur Blomfield (Wikipedia).
- About 17,000 works by roughly 2,000 artists, mainly Indian, with about 12,000 square metres of exhibition space in Delhi and branches in Mumbai and Bangalore (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6:30pm. Entry is 20 rupees for Indian citizens aged 18 and over and 500 rupees for foreign nationals (Tour My India, a travel listing, not the gallery).

FUNDING
National Gallery of Modern Art: public: Government of India, Ministry of Culture
- Run by the Government of India under the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia); the Ministry told the press in 2019 that the gallery's advisory committees in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Delhi had not been dissolved and that their terms had recently ended (Tribune, PTI).
- No reporting found of any corporate sponsor, donor, foundation or budget figure for the Delhi gallery.

Red flags: ministry control of what is shown
- September 2000: at the exhibition "CombineVoice for the New Century", Culture Ministry Secretary P. V. Vaidynatha Ayyar ordered director Mukta Nidhi Samnotra to remove Surendran Nair's painting of a nude Icarus on the Ashoka Pillar, on the ground that it treated the national emblem irreverently. Samnotra agreed; all 25 artists withdrew their works in protest (The File Room archive, an anti-censorship project; ArtsJournal, citing the BBC, 5 September 2000).
- February 2019: at the Mumbai branch, the actor-director Amol Palekar criticised the Ministry for scrapping the branch advisory committees and was interrupted by some NGMA members at an exhibition opening (Tribune, 11 February 2019). Director General Adwaita Gadanayak said the committees were not dissolved but their terms had ended, and that their earlier recommendations would be honoured (Latestly, 11 February 2019). The Ministry said the same (Tribune).
- May 2023: the Delhi gallery hosted "Jan Shakthi: A Collective Power", curated by Alka Pandey, with themes from Narendra Modi's radio broadcast "Mann Ki Baat"; South First called it "reportedly pro-government" (South First, 13 July 2023).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No full critique of the Delhi gallery's funding found in art media. The objections found are to ministry control of programming, and none comes from a dedicated art outlet.
1. DailyO: Gautam Benegal, "No Podium for Palekar: When art is attacked, it's the people who lose" (2019)
URL: https://dailyo.in/politics/amol-palekar-ngma-mumbai-bjp-rss-censorship-hindutva-29398
- Argues that a policy, in force since October 2018, giving the Ministry of Culture control over NGMA exhibitions threatens artistic freedom; before it, local artists' committees chose the Mumbai and Bangalore shows.
- Names Palekar, Suhas Bahulkar, Jesal Thacker and NGMA Mumbai director Anita Rupavataram; also questions the Delhi NGMA's January 2019 auction of gifts given to Modi.
- A news site, not an art outlet.
2. South First: Sreerag PS, "Artists stand in solidarity with museum employee fired for criticising pro-government art exhibition" (2023)
URL: https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/art-world-stands-in-solidarity-with-museum-employee-fired-for-criticising-artists-who-collaborated-with-pro-modi-show/
- Reports that Sandip K Luis lost his contract at the Kiran Nadar Museum of Art after a 15 May 2023 post criticising the artists behind "Jan Shakthi" at NGMA Delhi.
- Criticises the artists and the Kiran Nadar Museum; does not itself criticise NGMA.
3. ArtsJournal: Douglas McLennan, post citing the BBC (2000)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=171538
- Reports that a painting of a small nude figure was removed from a Delhi exhibition and all 25 artists withdrew.
- Does not name NGMA.

WIDER CONTEXT
- DailyO places the 2019 episode within pressure on Indian artists by Hindutva groups, citing the arrest of the Baroda art student Chandramohan more than 10 years earlier and the 8 December 2016 attack on the Jaipur Art Summit.
- NGMA is the state's own gallery, so the Ministry's decisions on its programme are the ones DailyO criticises.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind NGMA is the Government of India's, with no sponsors found. The weak points are documented ministry orders over what is shown (the 2000 removal) and complaints about a state-friendly programme in 2019 and 2023. Watch for published budgets and for who curates the gallery's shows.
