MUSÉE DE CLUNY: Paris, France
ID: musee-de-cluny
Website: https://www.musee-moyenage.fr (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- France's national museum of the Middle Ages, in the Latin Quarter in the 5th arrondissement of Paris (Ministry of Culture, Wikipedia).
- Created in 1843, after the collector Alexandre Du Sommerard bought the Hôtel de Cluny in December 1832 to show his medieval and Renaissance collection and the French state bought that collection following his death in 1842 (Ministry of Culture, Wikipedia).
- Named for the Abbey of Cluny, whose Paris mansion the 15th-century hôtel was (Wikipedia).
- The site joins the hôtel to Roman baths with a preserved frigidarium, and a reception building by the architect Bernard Desmoulin opened on 14 July 2018 (Ministry of Culture, Wikipedia).
- The renovated museum reopened on 12 May 2022 (Wikipedia).
- The Ministry of Culture runs it as a national service attached to its Direction générale des patrimoines (Carenews).

FUNDING
Musée de Cluny: mainly public: the French Ministry of Culture, with Louvre Abu Dhabi contract money in the renovation
- The Ministry of Culture alone paid for the entrance works of more than EUR 7.6 million, about half of it with money received from the Louvre Abu Dhabi, of which Cluny is a partner (Carenews, 2019).
- The whole renovation cost EUR 23.1 million and was partly financed by the Louvre Abu Dhabi contract (Wikipedia).
- Oppic, the state's building agency for culture, managed the works for the ministry's heritage directorate (Ministry of Culture).

Red flags: Gulf state money in the renovation
- About half of the entrance works was paid with money France received from Abu Dhabi for the Louvre Abu Dhabi (Carenews, 2019).
- The museum put EUR 4 million of its Abu Dhabi fee into a EUR 7.4 million expansion, and its director, Elisabeth Taburet-Delahaye, said the work "would certainly have been slower and more difficult" without Abu Dhabi (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- The Emirati government financed the Louvre Abu Dhabi, and 12 French institutions besides the Louvre agreed to lend works to it for a fee of EUR 265 million over 15 years (The Art Newspaper, 2016).

Red flags: Lafarge sponsorship returned in 2018
- Lafarge paid EUR 200,000 in 2015 for a concrete footbridge over Gallo-Roman remains in the new reception hall, part of the Cluny IV programme (Carenews).
- The museum still named Lafarge as a patron in February 2017 and no longer did by March 2018.
- The ministry's heritage directorate confirmed that the money was reimbursed in 2018 and declined to say more.
- Unverified: the agreement was cancelled under the ministry's patronage charter, from unnamed sources quoted by Bernard Hasquenoph, founder of the museum watchdog site Louvre pour tous.
- Christian Herrault, a former Lafarge deputy chief executive, had been an administrator of the Société des Amis du musée de Cluny since 2005 and its vice-president since 2014, and he and his wife made a contribution to the museum's Cercle des mécènes philanthropes in 2014 (Carenews, 2019).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum lives on its own state's budget, which is not a flag. The weak points are the Abu Dhabi money that paid for part of the new entrance and the quiet return of a cement maker's sponsorship. Worth watching: whether later works again draw on the Abu Dhabi money.
