FOAM FOTOGRAFIEMUSEUM AMSTERDAM: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: foam-fotografiemuseum-amsterdam-amsterdam
Website: https://www.foam.org (checked 2026-10-24; returned a security checkpoint and HTTP 429 and could not be read; facts below come from the Annual Report 2025 PDF and other sources)

OVERVIEW
- Photography museum at Keizersgracht 609 in Amsterdam's canal ring (Wikipedia; DutchCulture).
- The city council gave permission in November 2001 and Foam opened on 13 December 2001 with "Dutch Delight"; after renovation the official opening was on 6 June 2002. Paul Huf and Eva Besnyö are described as its originators (Wikipedia).
- Also called Fotografiemuseum Amsterdam; the operator is Stichting Fotografiemuseum Amsterdam (Annual Report 2025). The Foam Paul Huf Award was once the KLM Paul Huf Award (Wikipedia).
- Building: BenthemCrouwel converted three Keizersgracht buildings; the site goes back to Carel Joseph Fodor, whose will made the warehouse Museum Fodor (open 1863 to 1994), and the Nederlands Vormgevingsinstituut used it from 1994 to 2001 (Wikipedia).
- What is there: about 20 exhibitions a year, Foam Magazine, the Foam Talent programme, the Foam 3h talent space, the in-house gallery Foam Editions, a cafe, and community spaces Galerie Mercatorplein and MAQAM (Annual Report 2025).
- Visiting: the Annual Report says Foam received nearly 200,000 visitors in 2025, about half with a Museumkaart, and that a seven-month renovation in 2026 means fewer exhibitions and visitors. Opening days, admission and accessibility could not be read.

FUNDING
Foam Fotografiemuseum Amsterdam: mixed earned and subsidised: tickets and sales, the Amsterdam arts fund, and lottery, foundation and corporate partners
- Share of income: about 20% of annual turnover comes from municipal subsidy and 80% from ticket sales, fundraising, sponsorship and commercial activity (Annual Report 2025).
- 2025 income of €5,145,422: audience income €1,706,027, sponsorship €234,500, other income €975,404; subsidy from the Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst €1,079,833, other City of Amsterdam subsidies €134,749, private contributions €1,014,909. Costs were €4,962,999 and the surplus was €191,538 (Annual Report 2025).
- Amsterdam: from 2025 the four-year arts plan grant comes from the Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst, replacing the City's operating subsidy (Annual Report 2025).
- VriendenLoterij, a charity lottery, is called one of the main structural partners next to the city subsidy (Annual Report 2025). VandenEnde Foundation backs Foam Talent and added a gift for the first Foam Photographers' Dinner.
- Corporate partners in 2025: GarantiBBVA International (the youth education programme), DLA Piper (Foam 3h talent programme), Mentha (the Paul Huf Award) and Parcom, which donated to the 25th anniversary party in Paris (Annual Report 2025). No amounts are stated per partner.
- Funds and foundations in 2025: Deutsche Börse Photography Foundation (Foam Talent), EgeriaDo, Van Bijleveltstichting, Leeuwensteinstichting, Stadsdeel West, Mondriaan Fonds (artists' fees), Turing Foundation, Cultuurfonds and Vfonds (Annual Report 2025).
- Governance: director Karin van Gilst; supervisory board chair Jeroen van Ingen (Sony Music Entertainment), with Shivan Shazad, Lotte van Zuijlen and Auke van den Hout. Board members receive no fee. Frédérique Demenint left in December 2025 (Annual Report 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is descriptive. Foam's own Annual Report 2025 (supervisory board report dated 20 May 2026) states that the "interne onrust" (internal unrest) of the previous policy period, whose years it does not give, shifted attention to social safety, inclusion on the workfloor, transparency and the work culture. The board adds that the high workload and the need for a safe working climate took time and commitment from management, and that an external confidential adviser was appointed. The staff section records one report to the adviser in 2025 (stated by the space; translations ours).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Foam is mostly clean: ticket and shop income, city arts money, a charity lottery, Dutch and German foundations and named companies. The weak points are corporate backers whose sums are not disclosed (GarantiBBVA International, Parcom, DLA Piper, Mentha), the internal unrest Foam discloses, and reserves that the director's foreword says had to be rebuilt "after a few lean years" (translation). Worth watching are the 2026 building works and whether the corporate partner list changes.
