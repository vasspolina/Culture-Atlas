NATIONAL MUSEUM OF DAMASCUS: Damascus, Syria
ID: national-museum-of-damascus

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Syrian archaeology on Shukri al-Quwatli Street, west of the city centre, between Damascus University and the Sulaymaniyya Takiyya (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1919 under the Syrian Ministry of Education at Al-Adiliyah Madrasa; Wikipedia calls it the oldest cultural heritage institution in Syria.
- The present building was completed in 1936 beside the Sulaymaniyya Takiyya, and its facade uses the front walls of the Umayyad palace Qasr al-Hayr al-Gharbi, moved from the Syrian Desert. The official opening was in 1950, with later additions in 1953, 1963, 1974 and 2004 (Wikipedia).
- Holds more than 300,000 items covering more than 11 millennia, including over 5,000 cuneiform tablets, the Ugaritic alphabet, the Dura-Europos synagogue wall paintings, the Hypogeum of Yarhai and the Lion of al-Lat (Wikipedia).
- It closed in 2012 during the civil war, when staff hid its artefacts, and partly reopened in late October 2018; Wikipedia says four of its five wings reopened on 29 October 2018 (Smithsonian Magazine, Brigit Katz, 30 October 2018; Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia says it closed again on 7 December 2024 after the fall of Damascus and that the Directorate-General requested security from Hayat Tahrir al-Sham. The Associated Press (11 November 2025) reports it reopened on 8 January 2025.
- On the night of 9 November 2025 thieves broke into the classical department. An anonymous official told the Associated Press six marble Roman-era statues were taken; the Directorate-General said "some exhibits" were missing and opened an investigation, and the police chief, Brig. Gen. Osama Atkeh, told the state news agency SANA that guards and other people were being questioned (Associated Press, Gaith Alsayed, 11 November 2025).

FUNDING
National Museum of Damascus: Syria's Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums, with a UNESCO emergency project from 2025
- The Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums is the museum's partner in the UNESCO project and requested security for the museum in December 2024 (Wikipedia).
- In June 2025 UNESCO launched a first-aid project with the Directorate-General for the museum, with an initial budget of $175,000 covering emergency preparedness, restoration of damaged objects, digitisation of documentary heritage and staff training in inventory, museology and heritage education (Finestre sull'Arte, Noemi Capoccia, 14 June 2025; North Press, 11 June 2025). Finestre sull'Arte mentions "new financial commitments" from international partners.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum works with Syria's Directorate-General of Antiquities and Museums, which partnered with UNESCO on an emergency project with an initial budget of $175,000 launched in June 2025. The documented events are the closure and reopening around the fall of the Assad government and the November 2025 theft of six Roman statues.
