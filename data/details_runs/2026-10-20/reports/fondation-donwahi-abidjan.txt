FONDATION DONWAHI: Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire
ID: fondation-donwahi-abidjan
Website: https://www.jeuneafrique.com/mag/529540/culture/cote-divoire-la-fondation-donwahi-un-havre-dart-et-de-paix-a-abidjan/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation in the Cocody district of Abidjan (AFP, 9 April 2013), at Boulevard des Martyrs (CAAC).
- Official name Charles Donwahi Foundation for Contemporary Art, founded in 2008; Illa G. Donwahi is president and artistic director (CAAC; Mondriaan Fonds).
- A group of art lovers, mostly African private-sector figures, set up the foundation, which opened in 2008; over a little more than ten years Illa Donwahi turned her family home into its space (C&, Cédric Vincent, 18 December 2019).
- It closed during the 2010 to 2011 post-election crisis and reopened in 2013 with a large exhibition on the history of African photography (AFP, 9 April 2013).
- Programme: exhibitions, research residencies and masterclasses (Mondriaan Fonds); an exhibition space, a commercial gallery, an artists' residency, an events venue and art education; the curator Simon Njami contributed to the reconstruction after 2011, and Marème Malong is vice president (C&).
- In 2016 it hosted "Lumières d'Afriques", 54 works by 54 African artists, shown from 27 April to 6 June 2016 with the African Development Bank (CAAC).
- In November 2019 it launched the Abidjan Green Arts biennial, with artists working in Banco National Park (C&).
- In 2024 Illa Donwahi was commissioner of the Ivory Coast Pavilion at the 60th Venice Biennale (Mondriaan Fonds).

FUNDING
Fondation Donwahi: private: a group of mostly African private-sector founders, the family home as venue, with project partners
- Founded by a group of art lovers, mostly African private-sector figures (C&, 2019).
- After 2011 the main challenge was raising new funding; Illa Donwahi says the foundation relied on its past work to persuade new patrons (C&, 2019).
- "Lumières d'Afriques" (2016) was organised by African Artists for Development in collaboration with the African Development Bank; CAAC lists Orange, the Schneider Electric Foundation and Tilder as its partners.
- The Abidjan Green Arts biennial (2019) was supported by three Ivorian government ministries, the Office Ivoirien des Parcs et Réserves, GIZ, the Goethe-Institut and the foundation itself (C&, 2019).

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation was founded by a group of mostly African private-sector figures in Illa Donwahi's family home, with project support from the African Development Bank, Orange, the Schneider Electric Foundation, Tilder, GIZ, the Goethe-Institut and Ivorian ministries for individual shows.
