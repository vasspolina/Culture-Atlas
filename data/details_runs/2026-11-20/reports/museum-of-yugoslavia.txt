MUSEUM OF YUGOSLAVIA: Belgrade, Serbia
ID: museum-of-yugoslavia
Website: https://muzej-jugoslavije.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public history museum of Yugoslavia and of Josip Broz Tito at Mihaila Mike Jankovića 6 in the Dedinje district, where Tito and his wife Jovanka are buried in the House of Flowers (muzej-jugoslavije.org; Wikipedia).
- The Government of the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia founded it in 1996 as the Museum of the History of Yugoslavia, replacing the "Josip Broz Tito" Memorial Centre and the Museum of the Revolution of Yugoslav Peoples; it took its present name in 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Its main building, the May 25 Museum by Mihailo Janković, was finished in 1962 as the City of Belgrade's 70th birthday gift to Tito, to show and keep the gifts he received; it was Belgrade's first purpose-built museum (muzej-jugoslavije.org).
- The museum states that its programmes now run in all three buildings of the complex and in a sculpture park, after several years of work on its infrastructure (financial plan 2026).
- Its staff stated in 2026 that it had a record 200,000 visitors a year and that its exhibition "Ježeva kućica" won a Europa Nostra Grand Prix the previous year (Vreme, 2026).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. Entry is free on the first Thursday of the month, on 4 and 25 May, for children under 10 and for disabled visitors; wheelchair users can enter the May 25 Museum, and tours in Serbian sign language can be booked (muzej-jugoslavije.org).

FUNDING
Museum of Yugoslavia: mainly public: Republic of Serbia, plus its own income
- A state institution financed through Serbia's Ministry of Culture, which sets its financial plan (financial plan 2026).
- In 2025 it took in 266.9 million dinars: 162.4 million from the Republic's budget, 103.4 million from its own sales of goods and services and 1.2 million in voluntary transfers from individuals and companies (final accounts 2025).
- For 2026 the ministry allotted 183.7 million dinars from the budget, 113.74 million for regular work and 69.96 million for programmes, and set its own income at 75.324 million dinars (financial plan 2026).
- The museum wrote in that plan that the ministry had not funded staff, running costs, contracted services and maintenance as requested, and that it would cover the gap from its own income (financial plan 2026).

Red flags: 2026 appointment of an acting director without a competition
- In May 2026 the Serbian government replaced director Neda Knežević with an acting director, Gordana Vujović (Vreme).
- The staff said the board had asked the Ministry of Culture in July 2025 for consent to open a competition for the post, as the Law on Culture requires, and that the ministry never replied (Vreme).
- The staff told the ministry and the board that they would not accept the appointment unless the appointee met the legal conditions, and said they had asked repeatedly for her biography without receiving it (Vreme).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Serbian state money and its own ticket and sales income, with negligible private giving. The weak point is political control of its leadership, shown by the 2026 appointment made over the staff's objections.
