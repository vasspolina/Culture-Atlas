ERARTA MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: erarta-museum-of-contemporary-art-st-petersburg
Website: https://www.erarta.com/ru/museum/about/

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum on Vasilievsky Island, at 29th Line, building 2, St Petersburg, with 12,000 square metres of space and about 40 new exhibitions a year (stated by the museum). It calls itself the largest private museum of contemporary art in Russia.
- Opened to the public in September 2010; the name combines "Era" and "Arta", "the era of art" (Wikipedia). Marina Varvarina founded it (The Art Newspaper Russia, Natalia Shkurenok, 3 December 2019).
- Building: a five-storey building constructed in 1951 for a district committee of the Communist Party and later used by the Synthetic Rubber Research Institute; the entrance sculptures "Era" and "Arta" are by Dmitry Zhukov (Wikipedia).
- From 2013 to 2017 it ran Erarta Motion Pictures, a festival of short films about painting (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open every day except Tuesday, 11:00 to 23:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Erarta Museum of Contemporary Art: private: the museum states it takes no state support, grants or sponsors
- Stated by the museum: it neither receives nor has ever received state support or grants and has no sponsors ("не получает и никогда не получал государственной поддержки, каких-либо грантов и не имеет спонсоров", museum website, About page).
- Varvarina told The Art Newspaper Russia on 3 December 2019 that the museum survives financially but the details are a commercial secret ("Выживает, но это коммерческая тайна", translated); she called Erarta an independent organism ("самостоятельный организм").
- Novaya Gazeta Europe (Polina Troitskaya, 24 March 2025) describes Varvarina as the widow of the businessman Dmitry Varvarin, who was killed 25 years earlier in a case that was never solved; within a few years she sold the assets she inherited.
- Unverified: pundits, citing Vedomosti, put the cost of opening the museum at about $30 million, from Novaya Gazeta Europe (24 March 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The press coverage concerns the museum's relations with Russian authorities and its identity as a museum.
1. The Art Newspaper Russia: Natalia Shkurenok, "Marina Varvarina: 'We Go Against the Canons'" (3 December 2019, title translated)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.ru/posts/7605/
- An interview. The interviewer says Erarta is often criticised for doing show business rather than art; Varvarina answers that various "prominent specialists" have voiced disapproval.
- Varvarina says joint projects with large museums have not been discussed and that official-level cooperation does not exist.
2. Novaya Gazeta Europe: Polina Troitskaya, "Erarta against the world" (24 March 2025)
URL: https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2025/03/24/erarta-against-the-world-en
- Reports that Erarta still has far more creative freedom than Russia's publicly funded museums, though scrutiny has grown since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.
- Reports the January 2025 calls for an investigation of its exhibits and the dismissed case over Sergei Grinevich's painting (see Wider context).

WIDER CONTEXT
- July 2024: a protocol was drawn up against the museum's legal entity under Part 1 of Article 20.3 of the Code of Administrative Offenses (propaganda of Nazi symbols) because of the inscription "Жыве Беларусь" under Sergei Grinevich's painting "Festival", which has been in the museum since 2016; the Vasileostrovsky District Court was to review it. Experts judged the phrase a slogan of the 13th Belarusian police battalion and the 30th Waffen-SS grenadier division (Novaya Gazeta, 18 July 2024, title translated). The case was dismissed because the statute of limitations had expired (Novaya Gazeta Europe, Polina Troitskaya, 24 March 2025).
- January 2025: Sergey Mironov, a State Duma member and leader of the party A Just Russia, called for an investigation into "anti-patriotic exhibits". Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova, head of the party's youth wing, posted examples on Telegram and said the party was not seeking closure, only that the St Petersburg governor "restore order". Deputy Ksenia Goryacheva of New People said closing the museum would be a "tragic page" in the city's history, and Vice Governor Boris Piotrovsky acknowledged "bewilderment about some of the exhibits" while affirming the importance of free expression (Novaya Gazeta Europe, 24 March 2025). A party demand is a political demand, not an act of the state.
- Connection: both actions targeted the museum's own displays. The museum states that it takes no state support, so the actions came from outside its funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Erarta is private: the museum states it takes no state money, grants or sponsors, and its founder is Marina Varvarina, widow of the businessman Dmitry Varvarin. The documented pressure on the museum came from an administrative case over one painting, dismissed in 2025, and from a party's call in January 2025 for an investigation of its exhibits, not from a funder.
