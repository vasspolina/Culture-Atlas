HOSPITALFIELD: Arbroath, Scotland, UK
ID: hospitalfield-arbroath
Website: https://hospitalfield.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre, historic house and garden in Arbroath, Angus, run by the Hospitalfield Trust, a registered Scottish charity (SC009987).
- In the 13th century the monks of Arbroath Abbey built a hospital on the site to receive pilgrims (Hospitalfield history pages).
- The painter Patrick Allan married Elizabeth Fraser, the last heir to the estate, in 1843. The couple left their estates and collections in trust to support artists and education in the arts, and when Allan-Fraser died in 1890 the trustees took over management of Hospitalfield (Hospitalfield history pages).
- The residential art college opened in 1902 and became a postgraduate school in the 1920s; from 1922 it accepted students recommended by the four main art colleges in Scotland for three months in the summer (Hospitalfield history pages).
- The Trust appointed the architects Caruso St John in 2013, and Nigel Dunnett was appointed in 2016 to design the walled garden (Hospitalfield Future Plan page).
- On site: the house, a walled garden, a restored Victorian fernery that opened to the public in June 2021 (Hospitalfield visit page), and artists' studios completed in March 2024 (Future Plan page).
- Visiting: the garden shows Eduardo Paolozzi sculptures, open Thursday to Sunday, 10am to 4pm, until 2027, with a garden entry ticket. There is no public access to the house outside guided tours.

FUNDING
Hospitalfield: grant-funded: public and lottery bodies, with charitable trusts: Creative Scotland, National Lottery Heritage Fund, Historic Environment Scotland and the Scottish Government's Tay Cities Deal
- Income was £839,392 in the year to 31 December 2025 against spending of £877,351; income was £1,588,311 in 2024, £2,259,823 in 2023, £902,484 in 2022 and £3,258,815 in 2021 (Scottish Charity Regulator, OSCR).
- National Lottery Heritage Fund: £647,000 awarded on 14 June 2016 for the glasshouses and Victorian fernery, and £2,033,176 awarded on 7 June 2022 for Future Plan Phase 4, the restoration of the house and expansion of its collections (Heritage Fund project pages).
- Future Plan funders: the Future Plan page names Historic Environment Scotland, Creative Scotland, the Foyle Foundation, The Robertson Trust, Garfield Weston Trust, William Grant Foundation, The Pilgrim Trust, The Architectural Heritage Fund, The Leng Trust, Aberbrothock Skea Trust, Mushroom Trust, Northwood Trust and the Scott Finnis Foundation as funders of Phase One (garden and garden buildings, opened 27 May 2021) and Phase Two (studios, completed March 2024), with the Heritage Lottery Fund named for Phase One only.
- Tay Cities Deal: the Future Plan page says the Tay Cities Joint Committee approved £5.5 million of funding from the Scottish Government for the project, subject to match funding being secured.
- Individual giving: Friends pay £50 (individual) or £65 (joint), Donor Friends £250 a year, Patrons £1,000 a year.
- Trustees listed on the OSCR register include George Justin Willey, Leanne Lappin-Aing, Yvonne Brownlee, Philip Buchan, Frances Fowle, Julie-Ann Delaney, Nicola Catterall and Benjamin Cook.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Hospitalfield is public and charitable: Creative Scotland and National Lottery Heritage Fund grants, a Scottish Government allocation through the Tay Cities Deal, named trusts and a Friends scheme.
