MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART TOKYO (MOT): Tokyo, Japan
ID: museum-of-contemporary-art-tokyo-tokyo
Website: https://www.mot-art-museum.jp (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in Kiba Park, Koto-ku, at 4-1-1 Miyoshi, in eastern Tokyo (museum website).
- Opened in March 1995; the Tokyo Metropolitan Government drew up the plans in the 1980s (museum website).
- Designed by Takahiko Yanagisawa of TAK Associates, with about 4,000 square metres of exhibition space on three floors (Wikipedia; museum website).
- Holds about 6,100 works, mainly postwar Japanese art, and an art library of over 280,000 books, catalogues and magazines (museum website).
- Open 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays (with some exceptions around holidays). Tickets for the current exhibition, Tada Minami, are 1,600 yen for adults and 1,100 yen for university students and over-65s, and include the MOT Collection; over-65s enter free on the third Wednesday of each month. The pages read give no accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo: mainly public: Tokyo Metropolitan Government, with sponsors and Bloomberg support for individual events
- It is a Tokyo Metropolitan Government museum. In a 2026 press release the city announced the Tokyo Metropolitan Foundation for History and Culture as its chosen designated manager for seven facilities including MOT, for ten years from 1 April 2027 to 31 March 2037, subject to a vote in the Metropolitan Assembly (Tokyo Metropolitan Government press release).
- The Agency for Cultural Affairs of the Government of Japan supports the 2026 Tada Minami exhibition, whose sponsors include the Otsuka Museum of Art, Kajima Corporation, Takenaka Corporation and Toda Corporation (MOT exhibition page).
- Bloomberg: the MOT exhibition page says Students Day on 10 to 12 October 2026, with free entry for students, is "supported by Bloomberg". No amount is given.
- No budget figure was confirmed from a page read. No reporting found of a sponsor list for the museum as a whole.

Red flags: State censorship row over the Aida Family work (2015)
- At the summer exhibition "An Art Exhibition for Children: Whose Place Is This?" (18 July to 12 October 2015), MOT curators and Tokyo government officials called two pieces by the Aida Family inappropriate for children and asked for changes. The pieces were the six-metre scroll "Manifesto" and a satirical video of Makoto Aida as Prime Minister Shinzo Abe. Aida said on his Tumblr blog that they had demanded he remove both (Artscape, Lucy Birmingham).
- As of late August the museum had not forced him to alter the work (Artscape). Noi Sawaragi writes that chief curator Yuko Hasegawa asked the artists to remove or modify the work, that the work was not removed, that the exhibition closed without incident and that no catalogue was produced (Art-It).
- No reporting found in the pieces read that the request was formally withdrawn.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns one episode, the 2015 request to change the Aida Family work, and it comes from two Japanese art outlets. No criticism found of the museum's funders.
1. Art-It: Noi Sawaragi, "Notes on Art and Current Events 53" (Japanese 22 December 2015; English 22 February 2016)
URL: https://www.art-it.asia/?p=137781
- Argues the case is more serious than it first appears because a curator, whose role is to protect expression from outside pressure, made the request; he calls the museum's silence and refusal to explain "inexcusable" for a public institution.
- Notes the reason for the request remains unclear and Hasegawa issued no comment.
- Contrasts MOT with the Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum, which in 2014 admitted it had requested changes to a work by Katsuhisa Nakagaki.
2. Artscape: Lucy Birmingham, Focus series on the Aida Family installation (2015)
URL: https://artscape.jp/artscape/eng/focus/1509_01.html
- Reports that MOT curators and Tokyo government officials asked for changes to two pieces and that Aida refused to back down.
- Mostly reportage on the dispute rather than a sustained critique.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bloomberg Philanthropies was the target of a protest in London in January 2025. More than 20 artists walked out of the New Contemporaries preview at the Institute of Contemporary Arts on 14 January, and protesters led by the UK wing of the Gaza Biennale called on the ICA to cut ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies, alleging it supports initiatives in Israel and facilitates settlement infrastructure in the West Bank. The ICA called the claims untrue (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 16 January 2025).
- Connection: Bloomberg supports MOT's Students Day in October 2026. No reporting found of a protest aimed at Bloomberg's support at MOT.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly public, through the Tokyo Metropolitan Government and its policy-linked foundation, with corporate sponsors for single exhibitions. The weak points are the 2015 request by curators and city officials to change a satirical work, and Bloomberg's current support for a museum event.
