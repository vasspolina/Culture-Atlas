TURNER CONTEMPORARY: Margate, UK
ID: turner-contemporary-margate
Website: https://turnercontemporary.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Art gallery on the seafront in Margate, Kent, overlooking Margate Main Sands.
- Opened on 16 April 2011. Kent County Council committed to the project in 2000 (Turner Contemporary website). It is a registered charity.
- The site is where Turner once stayed (Art History News, 2011).
- Designed by Sir David Chipperfield: a raised plinth, six crystalline volumes and an acid-etched glass outer layer.
- Programme of changing exhibitions; in 2019 it hosted the Turner Prize.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday and bank holidays, 11am to 5pm. Entry and all exhibitions are free, donations welcome.

FUNDING
Turner Contemporary: mixed: public core funding from Kent County Council and Arts Council England, plus trusts, patrons and corporate sponsors
- Kent County Council: core funding of £510,000 a year, which the gallery said was 15% of projected income for the year to 31 March 2024 (USA Art News, quoting the gallery). Kent Current cites council grants data of £717,500 to the gallery for 2023/24. The council owns the building, according to council cabinet member David Wimble (Kent Current, 16 February 2026).
- Arts Council England: £681,791 a year under the National Portfolio for 2023 to 2026 (USA Art News).
- Capital: in November 2019 Arts Council England gave £495,000 and Kent County Council £1.28 million for upgrades to the building (Turner Contemporary news).
- Original build: a 2005 trade article put the cost at £25m and named Kent County Council, Thanet District Council, the South East England Development Agency, Arts Council England and the European Union as funders, with amounts given only for a £17.6m construction contract.
- Supporters list (undated, on the gallery's website): public funders Kent County Council, Arts Council England and Thanet District Council; major donors Bridget Riley Art Foundation, the Christopher and Henry Oldfield Trust, the Garcia Family Foundation and Peter Selman; corporate sponsors Burberry, Canterbury Christ Church University, Counter Editions, del Renzio & del Renzio and Southeastern; gallery circle White Cube and Xavier Hufkens; project supporters Art Fund, Christie's, Clore Duffield Foundation and Rothschild Foundation. No amounts.

Red flags: Stagecoach sponsorship of the 2019 Turner Prize
- On 1 May 2019 Stagecoach South East was announced as sponsor of the 2019 Turner Prize, to be shown at Turner Contemporary. Stagecoach chairman Brian Souter had funded the 2000 Keep the Clause campaign for Section 28, the anti-gay law (The Art Newspaper, PinkNews).
- Objections: Observer critic Laura Cumming criticised the deal on Twitter and Stonewall commented on Souter's past remarks (PinkNews). London dealer Stuart Shave wrote to director Victoria Pomery calling Souter "an openly known homophobe" (The Art Newspaper).
- Outcome: a day later Tate and Turner Contemporary said "by mutual agreement" they would not proceed with the sponsorship. Stagecoach said it was "absolutely committed to diversity in our company" (ArtReview, The Art Newspaper).
- Director Victoria Pomery told PinkNews: "We are not in a major conurbation and we are constantly trying to bring new partnerships in to play".

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is mostly of the 2011 building and opening show, and of the 2019 sponsor. No criticism found of the current funders.

4. Time Out London: "Revealed: Turner Contemporary Opens" (20 April 2011), author not named
URL: https://www.timeout.com/london/art/revealed-turner-contemporary-opens
- Three stars out of five. Cites "impossible-sounding ambitions about regeneration" and says the first exhibition cannot be called "first rate".
- Criticises the show, not the institution's money.

BOTTOM LINE
The core money is public: Kent County Council and Arts Council England. The weak point already on record is the 2019 Stagecoach sponsorship, which ended within a day. The council funding is also political: in February 2026 a Kent cabinet member suggested on social media cutting the gallery's funding (Kent Current), so the grant is worth watching.
