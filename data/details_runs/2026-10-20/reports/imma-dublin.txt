IMMA: Dublin, Ireland
ID: imma-dublin
Website: https://imma.ie/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Ireland's national museum of modern and contemporary art, at the Royal Hospital Kilmainham, Dublin 8.
- The Government of Ireland established it in 1990 and Taoiseach Charles Haughey opened it on 25 May 1991; the collector Gordon Lambert offered his collection if the State set up a gallery (Wikipedia).
- Name is an acronym of its title, the Irish Museum of Modern Art.
- The building is the Royal Hospital Kilmainham, founded in 1684 by the Duke of Ormonde as a home for retired soldiers and designed by Sir William Robinson (Wikipedia).
- The National Collection numbers over 4,900 artworks (stated by the space); the former stables hold artists' studios and a residency programme (Wikipedia).
- Annie Fletcher has been director since 2018 and the Earth Rising festival has run since September 2023 (Wikipedia). Opening hours and admission were not given on the pages checked.

FUNDING
IMMA: mainly public: state grant-in-aid, plus sponsorship and its own income
- IMMA is a company limited by guarantee funded mainly by state grant-in-aid, with sponsorship and its own income; pension liabilities caused a shortfall, with the chair reporting a €275,000 gap in one year (Wikipedia). The Department of Culture, Heritage and the Gaeltacht is credited in the site footer.
- Hennessy Ireland has funded the Hennessy Art Fund for IMMA Collection since 2016, to buy works by artists not yet in the collection; four artists were chosen in 2016: Kevin Atherton, David Beattie, Rhona Byrne and Dennis McNulty. No amounts are published (stated by the space).
- IPUT Real Estate is partner for the "Living Canvas at IMMA" screenings; the National Transport Authority and Transport for Ireland partner on the "Art in Motion" bus project (stated by the space).
- Soho House and BNP Paribas are named in the petition below; the page says BNP Paribas is still listed as a sponsor of The Freud Circle although the partnership ended in 2019 (petition).
- No reporting found of amounts for any sponsor; the 2024 and 2025 financial statements are posted as PDFs whose text could not be read here.

Red flags: petition to drop Hennessy, Soho House and BNP Paribas
- The petition "Public Art, Public Values: IMMA Must Divest" on Uplift had 500 signatures by 16 September 2025; it does not name its organiser or start date (Uplift).
- Demands: suspend partnerships with Hennessy and Soho House pending review; remove BNP Paribas from IMMA's website; commit to the principles of the BDS movement; update conflict-related funding policies; publish a timeline (Uplift).
- Petition claims, not independently checked here: Hennessy's parent LVMH is linked to human rights violations; Soho House runs a club in Tel Aviv-Jaffa that has displaced Palestinian communities; BNP Paribas finances fossil fuel projects and holds shares in Israeli weapons makers (Uplift, an openly campaigning source).
- No response from IMMA appears on the petition page; no other reporting found of the petition.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The base is state funding with corporate partners. The weak points are the sponsor list the petition targets, Hennessy's acquisition fund above all, and the lack of published amounts. Worth watching: IMMA's reply to the petition and the sponsor figures in the 2025 financial statements.
