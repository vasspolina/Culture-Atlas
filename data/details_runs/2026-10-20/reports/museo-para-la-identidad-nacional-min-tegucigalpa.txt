MUSEO PARA LA IDENTIDAD NACIONAL (MIN): Tegucigalpa, Honduras
ID: museo-para-la-identidad-nacional-min-tegucigalpa
Website: https://www.min.hn

OVERVIEW
- Anthropology and history museum in the former Palacio de los Ministerios in the historic centre of Tegucigalpa (Spanish Wikipedia, El Heraldo).
- Founded on 19 January 2006, under the government of President Ricardo Maduro (El Heraldo, Melissa López, 24 January 2019); Spanish Wikipedia gives the same inauguration date.
- It covers Honduran history from the pre-Hispanic era to the present (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The building dates from 1880, built under President Marco Aurelio Soto, first served as the city's General Hospital, gained a second storey in 1933 (Spanish Wikipedia). El Heraldo's photo caption dates it to 1882.
- Four thematic halls cover the country's geology, geography, historical formation and cultural diversity, plus a Copán Virtual hall and a hall of busts of national heroes (Spanish Wikipedia). About 930,000 people visited between 2006 and 2019 (Spanish Wikipedia); El Heraldo gave an annual average of 70,000 in 2019.
- The Fundación Hondureña para la Identidad Nacional (FHIN) runs the museum; its site describes a new phase tied to the museum's 20th anniversary, with work on renewing the museum, technology and education (stated by the foundation).

FUNDING
Museo para la Identidad Nacional: private non-profit foundation
- The FHIN is a non-profit that runs the museum; its board was named on 25 November 2022 (stated by the museum).
- Board: Thierry Jean Marie Ghislain de Pierrefeu Midence (president), Aline Flores Pavón (vice president), Arturo Zacapa (secretary), Ana Carolina Galeano Burgos (assistant secretary), Lorena Isabel Maduro Andreu (treasurer), Ruy Axel Merriam Rubiano (assistant treasurer), Leonel Zacarias Bendeck Nasser (fiscal), and members Lizzeth Guadalupe Villatoro Flores, José Alfredo Castillo Callejas, Hilda Mercedes Castañeda Moncada and Karla Sofía Simón Saide (stated by the museum).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece, in Hyperallergic, criticises the museum's national story.
1. Hyperallergic: Annette Lin, "How a Museum Wields Its Power in Crafting a National Identity" (26 September 2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/519157/how-a-museum-wields-its-power-in-crafting-a-national-identity/
- Argues that the museum presents Honduras as a Maya-centred nation although the Maya lived only in the western fringes of the country, and that nearly 20% of Hondurans identify as indigenous or minority.
- Quotes the historian Darío A. Euraque, former director of the Honduran Institute of Anthropology and History, who calls this the "Mayanization" of Honduras, a policy dating to the 1940s dictatorship of General Tiburcio Carías Andino.
- Says the displays do not reflect the Lenca, Miskito, Garífuna and Ch'orti' communities, and that the virtual reality and interactive displays paint a "sunny picture" next to the emigration of over 250,000 Hondurans in the last financial year, more than 120,000 dengue cases and nationwide protests during the author's visit.
- Criticises the museum's national story, not its funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by a private non-profit foundation, the FHIN, whose eleven-member board was named in November 2022. The press criticism concerns the national story the museum tells, not its money.
