FONDATION VINCENT VAN GOGH ARLES: Arles, France
ID: fondation-vincent-van-gogh-arles
Website: https://www.fondation-vincentvangogh-arles.org (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 35 ter rue du Docteur-Fanton in the centre of Arles, in a UNESCO World Heritage area, showing Van Gogh paintings next to contemporary art (Foundation website).
- Yolande Clergue founded the Association for the Creation of the Fondation Van Gogh in 1983; Luc Hoffmann gave the project new momentum in 2008, the foundation was established by ministerial decree on 8 July 2010, and it opened on 7 April 2014 with "Van Gogh Live!" (Foundation website; Wikipedia).
- Named for Vincent van Gogh, who worked in Arles (Foundation website).
- Building: the Hôtel Léautaud de Donines, a 15th-century mansion that served as a Banque de France office from 1924. Guillaume Avenard and Hervé Schneider of the FLUOR agency designed the renovation, begun in 2011, giving 2,400 square metres of floor and 1,000 of exhibition space (Foundation website).
- On site: temporary exhibitions, a shop and a terrace overlooking Arles and the ruined Montmajour abbey; the entrance gate is conceived as a hybrid sculpture. Bice Curiger is artistic director (Foundation website; Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily, last admission 17:30; closed until 21 November 2026 and reopening on 22 November with "HARVESTS" (to 4 April 2027). The pages read give no admission prices or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Fondation Vincent van Gogh Arles: private and partner-supported, no amounts published: led by Maja Hoffmann, with the City of Arles, museums and companies as partners
- Luc Hoffmann (1923 to 2016) gave the project new momentum in 2008 (Foundation website). Maja Hoffmann is president of the foundation and "part of the shareholder pool made up of descendants of the founder of the Roche Holding AG", which controls Hoffmann-La Roche (Wikipedia). No page opened says how much she or her family pays the foundation.
- Board: Maja Hoffmann (president), Mustapha Bouhayati (treasurer), Bice Curiger, Emilie Gordenker and François Halard, with government representatives and the Mayor of Arles (Foundation website).
- Institutional partners: Van Gogh Museum, Ville d'Arles, Musée Réattu, Musée Estrine and Abbaye de Montmajour (partners page).
- Corporate partners: Blackwall Green and Hiscox. Corporate sponsors: Christie's, Etat des lieux, L'agence privée - Skinsoft and Le Château des Alpilles (partners page). No amounts or years are given.
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts or the city's grant amount.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the foundation found in art media. Reviews and visitor comments found are positive or descriptive.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Maja Hoffmann also founded LUMA Arles at the Parc des Ateliers. The Art Newspaper (Toby Skeggs, "Arles cultural centre falls foul of planning rules", 30 June 2011) reported that the French National Commission for Historical Sites and Monuments rejected two of five building permit applications for the Gehry-designed LUMA complex because its towers would block the view of a bell tower and disturb Roman-Gallo sarcophagi. The article does not name the Fondation Van Gogh.
- Connection: Hoffmann founded and heads LUMA, and is president of the Fondation Vincent van Gogh Arles (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
No flagged funder was found behind the foundation: its pages name the City of Arles, museums and named companies as partners, and its president is a descendant of the founder of Roche Holding. The weak points are the absence of published accounts or annual reports, and corporate and family sums that are not stated. Worth watching is the concentration of Arles cultural patronage in Hoffmann's hands across the Fondation and LUMA.
