SFMOMA: San Francisco, USA
ID: sfmoma-san-francisco
Website: https://www.sfmoma.org (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum at 151 Third Street, San Francisco, on a former parking lot (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1935 as the San Francisco Museum of Art under director Grace L. McCann Morley; "Modern" was added to the name in 1975 (Wikipedia).
- The Botta building, designed by Swiss architect Mario Botta, opened in January 1995. Snøhetta designed the expansion; the museum closed in 2013 and reopened on 14 May 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Since 2009 it has held the Doris and Donald Fisher collection of about 1,100 works on a 100-year loan. Gap Inc. is the source of the Fisher family wealth (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Monday, Tuesday and Friday to Sunday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., Thursday noon to 8 p.m., closed Wednesday. Adults $30; members and visitors 18 and under free (SFMOMA website).

FUNDING
SFMOMA: private non-profit: foundation grants and public agencies, plus trustee and donor gifts; no corporate sponsor list published
- Foundation and government list (updated 24 June 2025, no amounts or years): Ford, Andy Warhol, Henry Luce, Hearst, Koret, Osher, Walter & Elise Haas Fund, Japan Foundation, Fundación Jumex, Grants for the Arts, the National Endowment for the Arts and the Institute of Museum and Library Services, among 25 named funders, plus three anonymous donors (SFMOMA website).
- The 2016 expansion cost $305 million (Studio International).
- Robert J. Fisher chairs the board (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual operating figures in the sources opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little sustained criticism in art media. The pieces found cover the 2020 staff and former-staff protests and, in a mostly favourable review, the Fisher collection.
1. The Art Newspaper: Scarlet Cheng, "SFMoMA releases a plan for diversity reforms" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/sfmoma-releases-a-plan-for-reform
- After a 30 May Instagram post, former employee Taylor Brandon called the museum's response to the police-brutality protests hypocritical; the museum disabled her comment. The groups No Neutral Alliance and xSFMOMA called for changes in hiring, collecting and programming and for Benezra to step down.
- Five senior staff have left or are leaving, including chief curator Gary Garrels, who resigned after remarks staff saw as racist. The museum's diversity plan acknowledges "longstanding inequities". Benezra's public apology drew more criticism.
2. Studio International: Jill Spalding, review of the Snøhetta expansion (2016)
URL: https://studiointernational.com/index.php/san-francisco-museum-of-modern-art-new-extension-sf-moma-review
- Mostly a positive review. It reports that the Fisher collection has been "faulted as predominantly white, male and blue-chip", and that pushback against the contract rotating the collection back to its installation every 10 years through 2116 "has been vocal".
- Criticises the collection's profile, not the funders.
3. Mission Local (local news, not art press): Sam Lefebvre, "'Toxic donors' are coming under fire at art museums, but not in liberal San Francisco. What will SFMOMA do?" (2019)
URL: https://missionlocal.org/2019/05/toxic-donors-are-coming-under-fire-at-art-museums-but-not-in-liberal-san-francisco-what-will-sfmoma-do/
- SEC filings tie at least 13 trustees or spouses to senior roles at firms invested in military contractors or arms dealers.
- Names trustees and donors including James and Penny Coulter, George Roberts of KKR, Jim Breyer and Charles Schwab. SFMOMA declined to comment: it "will not be participating in your piece".

WIDER CONTEXT
- Epstein and ComplexCity: a document in Justice Department files, quoted by the San Francisco Standard (5 February 2026), suggests Epstein donated John F. Simon Jr.'s ComplexCity to SFMOMA in 2001 under a fractional ownership arrangement. A 2009 letter called it a "fractional and promised gift of Jeffrey Epstein", with SFMOMA holding a 20% ownership interest. The Standard shows no payment by Epstein to SFMOMA and no board or leadership role.
- Thank-you letter: on 17 December 2009 director Neal Benezra wrote to Epstein thanking him for "your continued support in building our collection". SFMOMA called it "a standard form communication containing general donor language" and said it has "no records indicating Epstein provided support after 2001". It deaccessioned ComplexCity in October 2019 and says the work was never exhibited (San Francisco Standard).
- Neri Oxman: in June 2017 she thanked Epstein for supporting her death mask series, and in December 2017 asked him for $25,000 to $50,000; he replied "25k". The money went to her project, not to SFMOMA. Her "Vespers II" was in SFMOMA's 2022 show "Nature x Humanity" (San Francisco Standard).

BOTTOM LINE
The operating money comes mainly from named foundations and public agencies, with no amounts published. The Epstein items (a 20% interest in a work he donated in 2001 and a 2009 thank-you letter) show no payment or leadership role in the pages opened. The weak points are trustees' investments in military contractors reported by Mission Local in 2019 and the undisclosed amounts behind the funder list. Watch for further Justice Department disclosures.
