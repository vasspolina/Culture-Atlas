CORNING MUSEUM OF GLASS: Corning, USA
ID: corning-museum-of-glass-corning
Website: https://www.cmog.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of glass art and history in Corning, New York.
- Created in 1951 as the Corning Glass Center by Corning Glass Works for the company's 100th anniversary; Thomas S. Buechner was the founding director (Wikipedia). Corning Glass Works was renamed Corning Incorporated in 1989 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Harrison & Abramovitz designed the original 1951 building; Gunnar Birkerts designed an addition that opened on 28 May 1980; Thomas Phifer redesigned the 2012 expansion, and the Contemporary Art + Design Wing opened on 20 March 2015 (Wikipedia).
- On site: more than 50,000 glass objects, some over 3,500 years old, and the Rakow Research Library, founded in 1951 (Wikipedia). An October 2024 expansion of The Studio, the hot-glass and teaching space, cost $55.3 million and grew it from 24,000 to 60,000 square feet (Wikipedia).
- Attendance was about 400,000 in 2012 (Wikipedia infobox).

FUNDING
Corning Museum of Glass: founded by Corning Glass Works; Corning Incorporated is its major benefactor
- Total revenue was $59,870,439 in 2025 and $48,617,053 in 2024; contributions were $39,665,203 in 2025 and $28,183,484 in 2024 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 data, EIN 16-0764349). In 2023, revenue was $42,538,297, of which contributions were $25,146,743.
- Urban Glass (Andrew Page, 17 November 2016) wrote that since its founding in 1951 the museum "has been funded primarily by its major benefactor, Corning Inc."; chief operating officer Alan Eusden said "We don't see the unwavering support changing" and that new funding sources would supplement the company's support.
- Public grants: ProPublica (27 March 2017) reported that Corning and the Corning Museum of Glass together won $4.8 million through the Southern Tier regional council, including $1.5 million toward a new museum entrance and a $275,000 grant for a mobile glass-blowing studio on a canal barge.
- Named gifts: the Ben W. Heineman Sr. family donated a major contemporary glass collection in 2005, and the Rakow Commission provides $25,000 each year, funded through the late Dr. and Mrs. Leonard S. Rakow (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- ProPublica and Investigative Post (Charlotte Keith and Masako Melissa Hirsch, "Corning's Glass Brimming With Taxpayer Subsidies", 27 March 2017) reported that Corning Incorporated was awarded $113 million in state and local subsidies between 2009 and 2015, according to an analysis by Investigative Post, ProPublica and the Columbia University Graduate School of Journalism. G. Thomas Tranter Jr., president of Corning's economic development arm, said the figure "far exceeds reality".
- The article reported roughly $85 million in incentives for a 2013 factory expansion in Erwin, about $340,000 per job, and a $590,000 cost to the city of Corning from property tax breaks between 2009 and 2014, according to state reports.
- Connection: Corning Incorporated is the museum's founder and, per Urban Glass in 2016, its major benefactor, and the same article names the museum among the winners of $4.8 million in grants.

BOTTOM LINE
Corning Glass Works founded the museum in 1951, and Urban Glass reported in 2016 that it has been funded primarily by Corning Inc. Form 990 contributions were $28.2 million of $48.6 million revenue in 2024 and $39.7 million of $59.9 million in 2025. The 2017 ProPublica report on subsidies to Corning Incorporated names the museum among the winners of $4.8 million in grants.
