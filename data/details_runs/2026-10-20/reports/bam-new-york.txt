BROOKLYN ACADEMY OF MUSIC (BAM): New York, United States
ID: bam-new-york
Website: https://www.bam.org

OVERVIEW
- Multi-venue performing arts centre in Brooklyn with programmes in film, music, theatre, dance, opera, talks, poetry, visual art and circus (BAM site). Its main building is at 30 Lafayette Avenue in Fort Greene (Wikipedia).
- The New York Legislature incorporated the academy on 16 February 1859 and it opened on 15 January 1861 in a hall on Montague Street, Brooklyn Heights, designed by Leopold Eidlitz. That building burned down on 30 November 1903 (Wikipedia).
- The current building at 30 Lafayette Avenue was designed by Herts & Tallant; its cornerstone was laid on 25 May 1907 and it opened in November 1908 (Wikipedia). It was added to the National Register of Historic Places on 2 May 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Venues include the Peter Jay Sharp Building (the 2,109-seat Howard Gilman Opera House and BAM Rose Cinemas), the 874-seat BAM Harvey Theater at 651 Fulton Street, the Richard B. Fisher Building (opened 2012) and BAM KBH (opened early 2025) (Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Tamara McCaw is president and Amy Cassello artistic director (BAM site).
- Visiting: BAM offers discounted tickets to US veterans and active-duty service members and runs initiatives for senior audiences (BAM site).

FUNDING
Brooklyn Academy of Music: mixed: private donations, an endowment, ticket and other earned income, and government grants
- Fiscal year to June 2024 (ProPublica, Form 990): revenue $47,096,598, of which contributions $38,230,569 and program service revenue $8,211,791; expenses $40,824,840; net assets $138,285,528.
- Fiscal year to June 2023 (ProPublica): revenue $46,585,680; expenses $51,862,473; a loss of $5,276,793.
- Impala, which compiles IRS grant data, lists the BAM Endowment Trust, the Howard Gilman Foundation ($13.3 million in total, 5% of funding), the Ford Foundation ($5.4 million), the Shubert Foundation and the Lawton W. Fitt and James I. McLaren Foundation (the last two named as 2025 funders, no amounts) among its funders, and says government grants were more than 10% of revenue in 2024.
- In June 2023 BAM cut 26 positions, about 13% of its staff; president Gina Duncan's letter said the changes were meant to help BAM weather the "downturn in charitable giving for the arts" and to move away from a model that leans on a shrinking donor base (BroadwayWorld).
- Wikipedia, citing a 2025 New York Times article titled "The Brooklyn Academy of Music Is Fighting to Regain its Mojo", says BAM's earlier executives stayed for decades while their successors left after a few years.

Red flags: protests over Batsheva Dance Company bookings
- 12 November 2014: about 50 supporters of the BDS movement, organised by Adalah-NY, rallied outside BAM on the opening night of Batsheva's run, the premiere of "Sadeh21". BAM acknowledged but did not respond to Hyperallergic's request for comment on the protest (Hyperallergic, Mostafa Heddaya, 12 November 2014).
- 2025: Dancers for Palestine, Theatre Workers for a Ceasefire and Amplify Palestine addressed a letter, dated 15 January, to BAM's leadership asking it to drop Batsheva's 6 to 8 March engagement at the Howard Gilman Opera House. Mondoweiss published it on 23 February 2025 and states that BAM leadership did not respond.
- Unverified: the letter's authors say the Israeli Foreign Ministry calls Batsheva "the best known global ambassador of Israeli culture" and that BAM's booking supports Israel's image; the letter is an advocacy document.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two Hyperallergic pieces cover a protest over programming and a staff union drive.
1. Hyperallergic: Mostafa Heddaya, "50 Protest Batsheva Dance Company Opening at BAM" (2014)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/50-protest-batsheva-dance-company-opening-at-bam/
- Reports the rally and the protesters' "Brand Israel" argument, and describes Batsheva as an "Israeli Foreign Ministry-funded troupe".
- BAM acknowledged but did not respond to a request for comment on the protest.
2. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Workers at the Brooklyn Academy of Music Move to Unionize" (20 May 2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/500306/workers-at-the-brooklyn-academy-of-music-move-to-unionize/
- Administrative and cinema workers signed a petition in April 2019 to join Local 2110 UAW, citing reduced benefits, changes to 401(k) matching and healthcare, and full-time jobs shifted to hourly part-time roles without benefits.
- BAM said it was "not taking a position with respect to the Union" and that it continued to pay 80% of employee premiums.

BOTTOM LINE
BAM is funded by private donors, foundations, an endowment, earned income and government grants; no funder flag applies. Two protests, in 2014 and 2025, targeted its bookings of Batsheva Dance Company; BAM cut 13% of its staff in 2023.
