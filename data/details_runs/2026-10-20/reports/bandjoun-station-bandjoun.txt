BANDJOUN STATION: Bandjoun, Cameroon
ID: bandjoun-station-bandjoun

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run contemporary art centre, museum, residency and farm in Bandjoun, western Cameroon, in the Bamileke highlands (Africultures, The Art Newspaper's French edition).
- Founded by the artist Barthélémy Toguo, who describes it as a "laboratoire sans frontières" (Africultures, translation). Toguo is artistic director, Germain Noubi managing director and Carine Djuidje handles cultural mediation (Contemporary And).
- Opening date: sources differ. Africultures (Erika Nimis, 17 February 2015) says Toguo started it in 2000 and it officially opened in November 2013; Apollo (20 March 2025) says Toguo launched it in 2007; The Art Newspaper's French edition (23 September 2019) says it opened to the public in 2013 and was officially inaugurated on 16 November 2016 after eight years of construction, with the then minister Ama Tutu Muna attending; Contemporary And gives 2013 in its text and 2007 in its background notes.
- Building: concrete and earth walls covered in mosaics designed by Toguo (Africultures); Apollo says roughly half of the complex is museum and gallery and half guest accommodation and facilities. The Art Newspaper's French edition describes a four-level museum and a residency with twelve rooms and a studio space.
- On site: a library and a permanent collection of hundreds of works (The Art Newspaper's French edition); Africultures puts the museum's display of Toguo's personal collection at over a hundred works across three floors, and reports about 25 hectares of farmland in five plots with plantain, maize and vegetables, small-scale poultry and goats, and three hectares of coffee planted in 2010. Contemporary And reports about 750 kg of coffee a year grown, roasted and ground on site.
- Programme: exhibitions including "Mes Amours" (2013) and "Stories Tellers" (2015) (Africultures), and a residency taking three to four artists a year for four to eight weeks plus one international exhibition a year and a December festival (Contemporary And, 27 February 2020). Since 2023 the MO.CO. Esba art school in Montpellier has run a 1.5-month residency at Bandjoun Station for two recent graduates (MO.CO.).

FUNDING
Bandjoun Station: self-funded: the founder's own money, with coffee sales and a friends' association
- Apollo (2025) says the project relies on Toguo's own money and drive because the country offers no government support, and that he paid for an access road himself because the track turns to mud in the rainy season.
- Contemporary And (2020): Toguo is the main funder of the station's artistic direction. Noubi says modest funding makes large-scale activities harder and that a financial deficit affects residencies, temporary exhibitions and workshops.
- Africultures (2015): the association Les Amis de Bandjoun Station supports the project and funds residencies for young artists, and the coffee was planted to sell directly rather than depend on fluctuating world prices.
- Residency partner: MO.CO. says the residency for two graduates is supported by Toguo and helped by MIRANDA 2024, a research and creation grant from Université Paul-Valéry Montpellier. It covers a return flight, a flat fee of €1,500, up to €450 in related costs, lodging and meals (MO.CO.).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The three pieces are largely admiring and report practical difficulties: funding, local reception and access.
1. Apollo: Fleur Macdonald, "'Bandjoun Station is an imposing proposition'" (20 March 2025)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=1021738
- Reports that for years local residents were reluctant to visit, and that Toguo's plans for a contemporary art museum in Yaoundé are stalled by government regulations.
- Quotes Toguo on building the access road himself: "there is no point in waiting for authorities to step in".
2. Contemporary And: Esther Poppe, "Bandjoun Art Station: The Importance of a Peripheral Institution in Cameroon" (27 February 2020)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/bandjoun-art-station-the-importance-of-a-peripheral-institution-in-cameroon
- Interview with Toguo and Noubi; Noubi states that the financial deficit limits the scale of programming.
- Not criticism: an interview in which Toguo and Noubi describe the station.
3. The Art Newspaper (French edition): Monique Ngo Mayag, "Bandjoun Station, cinq ans d'engagements tous azimuts" (23 September 2019)
URL: https://www.artnewspaper.fr/2019/09/23/bandjoun-station-cinq-ans-dengagements-tous-azimuts
- Reports that some neighbours are wary of the exhibitions and that visitors often struggle to find the way. The photographer Yvon Ngassam says the library's books are not catalogued, which makes consulting them difficult.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Bandjoun Station is the artist's own, stated by Apollo and Contemporary And, with a friends' association and coffee sales; Apollo says the country offers no government support. No flag applies. Contemporary And quotes Noubi on a financial deficit, and sources give different opening dates (2007, 2013, 2016).
