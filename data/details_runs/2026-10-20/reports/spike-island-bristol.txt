SPIKE ISLAND: Bristol, United Kingdom
ID: spike-island-bristol
Website: https://www.spikeisland.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre and studio complex on Bristol's harbourside, in an 80,000 square foot former industrial building (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1976 as Artspace Bristol by a group of artists who wanted affordable space; renamed Spike Island in 1998 (Spike Island).
- The artists first occupied a disused Victorian warehouse on Gas Ferry Road; after rising rents and a redevelopment plan forced them out in 1989, they moved into the former Brooke Bond tea-packing warehouse in 1992.
- Redeveloped with lottery funding and private donations into a museum-sized gallery, individual studios, a print studio and a cafe. The space's history page names no architect.
- On site: over 70 subsidised studios, Spike Print Studio, Spike Island Workspace for creative businesses, and the gallery spaces.
- Visiting: gallery open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 5pm; major exhibitions are free; the site links an Access Guide.

FUNDING
Spike Island: mixed public and private: Arts Council England and Bristol City Council, trusts, gallery and individual patrons
- Arts Council England and Bristol City Council are shown as funders on the space's pages; the pages give no amounts or years.
- Trusts and foundations named as partners: The Ampersand Foundation, Art Fund, Henry Moore Foundation, Elephant Trust, Paul Mellon Centre, Nisbet Trust, John James Bristol Foundation, Fenton Arts Trust, and the West of England Combined Authority. The John James Bristol Foundation made "a significant donation" toward a solar panel installation (Spike Green Futures); no amount or year is given (John James Bristol Foundation).
- Hauser & Wirth: platinum Gallery Supporter, and supports three Engagement Fellowships for South West curators running 2025 to 2028, each with financial support and mentorship (Spike Island).
- Other Gallery Supporters: Alison Jacques (platinum); Kiang Malingue, Modern Art and Union Pacific (gold); Antenna Space (bronze).
- Individual patrons by tier: Tony Ades (gold), Phil Toy (silver) and David Greene (bronze) in the Director's Circle; Commissioning Circles include Alia Al-Senussi, Frank Dupois, Judy Idriss, Djenaba Lewis Chamberlain, Bimpe Nkontchou, Maria Sukkar, Lukas Zueger-Knecht and the Estate of Donald Locke. The page gives no amounts or years.
- Corporate partners: Bristol 24/7, Left Handed Giant, Sam Talbot PR.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, arms, gambling, tobacco or Gulf state funder.

Red flags: petition over studio evictions (2020)
- 2020: a studio review process led the board to ask Howard Silverman, a founding member, to leave his studio during the coronavirus lockdown (The Guardian, via ArtsJournal, 4 May 2020).
- Fellow studio holders and artists backed his challenge; some suggested the organisation was squeezing out older tenants in favour of younger ones (The Guardian, via ArtsJournal).
- Bristol 24/7 reported a petition of more than 1,300 signatures asking Spike Island to rethink the process, which was on hold during the outbreak. Spike Island's director and trustees said the procedures followed three years of consultation and called the claims "inaccurate and misleading".
- No reporting found of the review's final outcome.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Spike Island is public grants, charitable trusts, galleries and private patrons, and no reporting found ties it to a flagged industry. The only documented dispute is the 2020 studio review and the petition against it. Worth watching: the Hauser & Wirth support, which is both a platinum gallery supporter and a fellowship funder to 2028, and amounts, which the space does not publish on its supporters page.
