HAKONE OPEN-AIR MUSEUM: Hakone, Japan
ID: hakone-open-air-museum-hakone
Website: https://www.hakone-oam.or.jp/

OVERVIEW
- Sculpture museum at 1121 Ninohira, Hakone, Kanagawa Prefecture (museum website).
- The Chokoku no Mori Art and Culture Foundation says it was launched on 6 September 1968 as the Chokoku no Mori Museum foundation, that the museum opened on 1 August 1969 as Japan's first open-air museum devoted to sculpture, and that it became a public interest foundation on 1 April 2012 (foundation website, translated from Japanese). Japanese Wikipedia gives the opening as 6 September 1969.
- The foundation says it was established by the Fujisankei Group, centred on Fuji Television, Sankei Shimbun and Nippon Broadcasting System (foundation website). The museum website names Nobutaka Shikanai as its founder and first director, who aimed to "contribute to the development of sculpture" because no museum devoted to sculpture existed in Japan; Japanese Wikipedia calls him chairman of the Fujisankei Group.
- The sculptor Takeshi Inoue was commissioned for the museum and grounds (museum website).
- The museum holds a little over 2,000 works, and has held 21 international sculpture competitions to build the collection (museum website). English Wikipedia names Picasso, Henry Moore and Taro Okamoto among the artists and describes a Picasso Pavilion with about 300 works.
- The foundation opened a sister museum, the Utsukushigahara Open-Air Museum in Ueda, Nagano, in 1981 (foundation website).
- Visiting: open 9:00 to 17:00 every day of the year. General admission is 2,000 yen at the counter and 1,800 yen with a web ticket, university and high school students 1,600 yen, junior high and elementary students 800 yen, preschool children free; visitors with a disability certificate pay 1,000 yen and their students and younger visitors pay nothing. Wheelchairs and strollers can be borrowed free at the information desks (museum website).

FUNDING
Hakone Open-Air Museum: mainly self-funded: admission income, with donations and corporate sponsorship of the original construction
- The foundation says its operating funds are mainly covered by museum admission income (foundation donation page, translated from Japanese).
- Donations from individuals and companies are accepted by bank transfer, from 5,000 yen, and qualify for tax deductions because the foundation is a certified public interest foundation (foundation donation page).
- The foundation's website states that the construction funds were sponsored by 173 domestic companies (translated from Japanese).
- English Wikipedia says the museum is affiliated with the Fujisankei Communications Group.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by a foundation that says it is funded mainly by its own admission income. The foundation says the Fujisankei Group, centred on Fuji Television, Sankei Shimbun and Nippon Broadcasting System, established it, and that 173 domestic companies sponsored the construction. Donations from individuals and companies are accepted under public interest foundation status.
