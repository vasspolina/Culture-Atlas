LACMA: Los Angeles, USA
ID: lacma-los-angeles
Website: https://www.lacma.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Los Angeles County Museum of Art on Wilshire Boulevard, beside Hancock Park. Los Angeles County owns the land, a 1916 gift from G. Allan Hancock; the nonprofit Museum Associates runs the museum (Wikipedia).
- Established in 1961 as a separate museum, split from the Los Angeles Museum of History, Science and Art; the William Pereira complex opened in 1965. Michael Govan has been director since 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Peter Zumthor designed the David Geffen Galleries, which replaced the Pereira buildings. The county board approved the design on 8 April 2019; the galleries opened to the public on 4 May 2026 (Wikipedia). Wallpaper describes a 110,000 square foot building spanning Wilshire Boulevard with 26 galleries.
- On site: the galleries, the older campus buildings, free docent tours and a café.
- Visiting: Monday, Tuesday and Thursday 11:00 to 18:00, Wednesday closed, Friday 11:00 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 19:00. The homepage lists no admission prices.

FUNDING
LACMA: mixed: Los Angeles County, private donors and trustees, and earned income
- The county provides about 30 million dollars a year, more than a third of operating expenses; donations and membership dues bring in about 40 million; average expenses are about 92 million dollars (Wikipedia, which carries a citations notice).
- The David Geffen Galleries are 80 percent privately funded and 20 percent funded by the county, which will own the building. Named donors: the County of Los Angeles, David Geffen, Elaine Wynn (board co-chair), the W. M. Keck Foundation, the board of trustees and Steve Tisch, whose gift was the largest single gift (LACMA press release, 22 August 2023). No amounts are given.
- The campaign "Building LACMA" passed its 750 million dollar goal; the building cost an estimated 724 million dollars (Wallpaper; e-flux).

Red flags: Tom Gores and the prison telecom Securus
- Tom Gores, chief executive of Platinum Equity, sat on the board of trustees. Platinum Equity bought Securus Technologies, which runs phone systems in prisons and jails, in 2017 (Hyperallergic).
- A letter by artist Jessica Simmons, signed by more than 100 artists including Andrea Fraser, Sam Durant and EJ Hill, demanded his removal in September 2020. It followed a letter from Color of Change and Worth Rises (Hyperallergic, 16 September 2020).
- Gores resigned in a letter dated 8 October 2020 (Hyperallergic, 9 October 2020).

Red flags: gambling money
- Elaine Wynn co-chaired the board and gave 50 million dollars in 2016 toward the expansion. She co-founded Wynn Resorts, a casino resort company, and owned nearly 9 percent of it at her death in April 2025 (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Substantial criticism of the Zumthor project and of the director, with a separate campaign against a trustee; reviews of the opened building are mixed.
1. The New York Review of Books: Joseph Giovannini, "The Demolition of LACMA: Art Sacrificed to Architecture" (2020)
URL: https://www.nybooks.com/online/2020/10/02/the-demolition-of-lacma-art-sacrificed-to-architecture/
- Argues the plan shrinks 117 galleries to 24 and sends much of the collection to storage; reports over 15,000 petition signatures.
2. Los Angeles Review of Books: Joseph Giovannini, "Inconvenient Truths at LACMA" (2015)
URL: https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/inconvenient-truths-at-lacma/
- Argues the plan is financially risky and that no competition chose the architect.
- Unverified: that trustee Michael Lynton arranged a 25,000 dollar contribution to a political committee of Supervisor Mark Ridley-Thomas before a county vote, from Giovannini; Sony, the supervisor's office and Govan denied wrongdoing.
3. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "Major Artists Demand LACMA Remove Board Member Who Owns Prison Telecom Company" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/588856/open-letter-lacma-tom-gores/
- Reports the artists' letter and Securus's jail call rates of up to 25 dollars for 15 minutes.
4. e-flux Criticism: Juliana Halpert, "The David Geffen Galleries at LACMA" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782449/the-david-geffen-galleries-at-lacma
- Argues the galleries symbolise privatised, commerce-driven museum practice, and that Govan's process was opaque.
- Reports a December 2025 staff vote to unionise.
5. Wallpaper: Janelle Zara, "Review: LACMA is a true gift to Los Angeles" (2026)
URL: https://www.wallpaper.com/architecture/public-buildings/review-lacma-david-geffen-galleries
- Mixed: criticises the architecture, not the funders, and concludes the building is a gift to the city.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Epstein files: Steve Tisch, who gave LACMA's largest single gift for the galleries, appears more than 400 times in the Justice Department's Epstein documents released in January 2026; he was never charged. He said he had "a brief association" and exchanged emails about adult women (Associated Press, 1 February 2026).
- The AP article does not mention LACMA.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mixed: county support plus wealthy individual donors, with the Geffen Galleries 80 percent private. The weak points are the casino fortune behind a board co-chair, the Gores campaign, and the Epstein-file appearances of the largest single donor. Watch the donor list as the galleries open.
