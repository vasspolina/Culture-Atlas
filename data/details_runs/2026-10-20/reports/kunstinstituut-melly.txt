KUNSTINSTITUUT MELLY: Rotterdam, Netherlands
ID: kunstinstituut-melly
Website: https://www.kunstinstituutmelly.nl/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art institute at Witte de Withstraat 50 in Rotterdam's Cool district, showing exhibitions by visual artists, commissions, publications and education projects.
- Founded in 1990 as the Witte de With Center for Contemporary Art, named after the street it stands on, which is named for Witte Corneliszoon de With, a 17th-century naval officer of the Dutch East and West India Companies (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- Renamed Kunstinstituut Melly on 27 January 2021, after Ken Lum's artwork "Melly Shum Hates Her Job" (1989), shown on the facade (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- Building: a four-floor building from the 1870s that was built as a girls' school; galleries on the two upper floors, the ground floor holds MELLY, a multi-use space with a cantina and bookshop, shared with TENT (CBK Rotterdam) and Hiphophuis.
- Directors: Sofía Hernández Chong Cuy joined in January 2018 (The Art Newspaper); Wikipedia lists Gabi Ngcobo from 2024.
- Visiting: Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, Friday until 21:00, closed Monday and Tuesday. Adults EUR 10, reduced EUR 5, free for ages 18 and under. Electric ramp, lift and accessible toilet.

FUNDING
Kunstinstituut Melly: mainly public: the City of Rotterdam and the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, topped up by private funds
- 2025 income was EUR 2.73 million: subsidies EUR 1.92 million, private funds EUR 504,564, indirect income EUR 185,749, own income EUR 93,722 and individuals EUR 35,417 (annual report 2025). Private funds were EUR 285,212 in 2024.
- Multi-year private partners in 2025: Ammodo, Amarte Fonds, Droom en Daad and the Hartwig Foundation.
- Exhibition-specific gifts in 2025: Niemeijer Fonds (Ola Hassanain, "The Watcher"), J.E. Jurriaanse Stichting, the Netherlands-America Foundation, Mondriaan Fonds and Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst.
- The free education space opened in July 2025 with VSBfonds, Elise Mathilde Fonds, ELJA Foundation, Stichting Mostert-Van der Meijden and Zabawas; education work is also backed by Fonds voor Cultuurparticipatie, Fonds 21 and the Daniel van der Vorm Stichting.
- No reporting found of amounts for individual foundations, or of where Ammodo's money comes from.

Red flags: Niemeijer Fonds, tobacco family money
- Stichting Niemeijer Fonds was founded in 1996 by Theodora Niemeijer (1912 to 2004), the daughter of a Groningen tobacco manufacturer. She wanted to do something positive with the wealth she inherited and left part of her estate to the fund (the fund's own site).
- The fund paid for an exhibition at Melly in 2025 (annual report 2025).

Red flags: protest over the Witte de With name
- Cultural critic Egbert Alejandro Martina first demanded the rename in 2017. The institution at first resisted, saying that renaming "would extinguish a link to a history that needs visibility" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara).
- An open letter by artists and activists said the institution had not reckoned with its namesake (Hyperallergic; The Art Newspaper).
- In June 2020 activists left red handprints on the building and the centre removed its name board (Wikipedia). The name was decided on 30 September 2020 by the director and a seven-person supervisory board after a consultation with more than 280 participants (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Moderate criticism, all of the old name and the renaming. No piece found criticises its funders.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "After Years of Debate Over Colonial Ties, Witte de With Center Changes Its Name" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/kunstinstituut-melly-formerly-known-as-witte-de-with-center.md
- Reports Martina's 2017 demand, the institution's first refusal and the open letter.
- Names the institution directly.
2. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Rotterdam's former Witte de With art centre chooses decolonial new name" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/10/02/rotterdams-former-witte-de-with-art-centre-chooses-decolonial-new-name
- Describes the letter's claim that the programme was compromised by the namesake's "history of terror" and the 13-member committee that recommended Melly.
- Reports no criticism of the new name.
3. Contemporary And (C&): Will Furtado, "What's at stake in decolonizing an art institution" (2021)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/whats-at-stake-in-decolonizing-an-art-institution
- Asks whether the new name is a rebrand: it honours an artwork, not people tied to Rotterdam's colonial past, and dropping "Shum" can be read as a neocolonial gesture.
- Argues accountability should extend beyond the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The base funding is public and the accounts are published. The weak points are the tobacco-family endowment behind the Niemeijer Fonds, which pays today, and the undisclosed size of each private gift. Watch the Niemeijer Fonds gifts in future annual reports.
