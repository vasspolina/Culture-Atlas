MAMAC (MUSÉE D'ART MODERNE ET D'ART CONTEMPORAIN): Nice, France
ID: mamac
Website: https://www.mamac-nice.org/en/ (checked 2026-11-20; states the museum is closed for renovation)

OVERVIEW
- The City of Nice's museum of modern and contemporary art, at Place Yves Klein next to Place Garibaldi, and one of the city's 11 museums (museum website).
- Inaugurated on 21 June 1990, after a 1985 agreement with the State on a five-year purchasing programme and a 1987 City and State agreement to finance the building (museum website).
- Building: by the architects Yves Bayard and Henri Vidal, four square towers with a red ochre base and Carrara marble surfaces, linked by glass footbridges, built over the covered Paillon river, with ten exhibition rooms and public roof terraces (museum website).
- Collection: more than 1,400 works by 370 artists, centred on New Realism, American Pop Art, the Nice school, Yves Klein and Niki de Saint Phalle, who made a large donation in 2001; the Klein room relies on long-term loans from the Yves Klein Archives (museum website).
- Closed since 7 January 2024 for renovation as part of the Promenade du Paillon extension, to bring the building up to current ecological standards; about 3,000 people came on the closing day (museum website; Nice Premium, 7 January 2024; Monaco Life, 9 January 2024).
- During the closure the collection is shown elsewhere: "Anne and Patrick Poirier. Memories of the ruins" runs at the Nice Cimiez archaeology museum from 7 November 2026 to 5 September 2027 (museum website).
- Director: Céline Kopp since 1 January 2026; before that she ran the Magasin in Grenoble from 2022 and a contemporary art centre in Marseille for ten years (museum team page; Nice Premium, 25 September 2025).

FUNDING
MAMAC: public: the City of Nice, with a 24.8 million euro renovation commissioned by the City
- Owner and operator: the City of Nice, which signs the site and lists MAMAC among its 11 municipal museums (museum website).
- State: the 1985 agreement brought State money for acquisitions, and the 1987 agreement had the City and the State finance the building (museum website).
- Renovation: the City's tender for the architect's contract set the works budget at 24,778,000 euros before tax and estimated the architect's fee at 3,256,435.80 euros over 72 months; the notice states the project receives no EU funds (BOAMP notice, 19 March 2025).
- Architect: the City chose a group led by Freaks Architecture with In Situ Benaïm-Nivaggioni, Egis, Ducks Scéno and Peutz on 23 February 2026, for 2,749,076.85 euros before tax, and signed in March 2026 (BOAMP award notice, 23 March 2026).
- Friends: the Association des Amis du MAMAC raises money from members towards acquisitions and restoration of works (museum website).

Red flags: 2025 termination of the first renovation architect's contract
- Nice Premium reported that work began in 2024 with structural diagnostics, relocation and securing of the works, then the City ended the contract with the first architect because the project was too far from its expectations; a new architect consultation was launched in early 2025 and the reopening target stayed at 2029 (Nice Premium, 27 February 2025).
- The article names neither the first architect nor any cost of the abandoned design.

Red flags: 2025 change of director
- Hélène Guenin left after nine years; Johanne Lindskog, director of the Nice fine arts museum, was interim director from February 2025; the City announced Céline Kopp's appointment at the end of September 2025 (Nice Premium, 25 September and 30 November 2025).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Nice Premium reported on 30 November 2025 that about a dozen employees of the Magasin in Grenoble, which Kopp had directed since 2022, described sick leaves, dismissals, disciplinary procedures and labour court cases; eight of eleven employees struck for two hours on 19 November 2025, and unions asked for independent mediation and an organisational audit.
- The same article reports that the labour inspectorate refused the dismissal of a staff representative who had reported alleged aggression and sexual harassment, and that management kept up legal action. It carries no reply from Kopp, the City of Nice or the Magasin.
- The dispute concerns the Magasin, not MAMAC; it is relevant because Kopp now directs MAMAC.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: municipal money, with State support in the founding years. Worth watching are the renovation schedule and budget, and the new director's management record.
