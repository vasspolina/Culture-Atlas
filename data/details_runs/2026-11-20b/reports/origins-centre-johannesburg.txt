ORIGINS CENTRE: Johannesburg, South Africa
ID: origins-centre-johannesburg
Website: https://www.wits.ac.za/origins/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of human origins of the University of the Witwatersrand (Wits) in Braamfontein, Johannesburg, on a site above the M1 highway across the road from Braamfontein cemetery (Wits; Mail & Guardian, 13 April 2006).
- Opened by President Thabo Mbeki in 2006 (Wits).
- Built where the fine art department had its studios, known as "The Wedge" (Mail & Guardian, 2006).
- Designed by Craig McClenaghan of Mashabane Rose and Associates, with dark, curving halls and walls "a metre thick" meant to recall caves (Mail & Guardian, 2006).
- On show: a path of hominin innovation from over 2 million years ago, stone tools, objects of symbolic and spiritual meaning, the impact of the colonial front, examples of resistance, and rock art from Wits' Rock Art Research Institute (RARI) (Wits). Artworks include Walter Oltman's woven map of the Earth and Willem Boshoff's "Signs of People" (Mail & Guardian, 2006).
- A new wing, begun in 2013, opened its basement rock art archive on 24 October 2019; Professor Amanda Esterhuysen then headed the centre's management (Wits, 2019).
- It closed in March 2020 for the Covid-19 pandemic and reopened in May 2021 after renovations (Wits, 2021).
- Visiting: Monday to Friday 09:00 to 17:00, Saturday and public holidays 09:00 to 16:00, closed Sunday; tickets are booked online and guided tours in advance; the car park is access controlled and visitors call for the code (Wits).

FUNDING
Origins Centre: university museum: Wits, with renovation money from a national humanities institute and the French Institute
- The centre is part of Wits, whose website presents it and links to a Wits giving page for donations (Wits); South African History Online lists it under the Wits Faculty of Science.
- The 2020 to 2021 renovations were paid for by funding and donations from Wits, the National Institute for the Humanities and Social Sciences (NIHSS) and the French Institute of South Africa (IFAS) (Wits, 2021).
- Wits said in 2019 that the new wing's ground and first floor exhibits would be finished "with additional funding" within two years (Wits, 2019).
- Its rock art collection comes from RARI at Wits (Wits).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One published critique, from a 2019 workshop held at the centre, faults its displays rather than its money.
1. Archive and Public Culture Research Initiative, University of Cape Town: John Wright, "Conversations about Art, Objects and Archives" (2019)
URL: https://humanities.uct.ac.za/apc/articles/2019-12-17-conversations-about-art-objects-and-archives
- Reports on a workshop of the same title at the centre on 18 and 19 November 2019, whose organisers said the centre had been criticised for failing to contextualise many objects and for fixing the meanings of rock art as standing outside time.
- Wright writes that housing displays on the San beside displays on human origins suggests the San are an "ancient" people closer to early humans; Amanda Esterhuysen, chair of the centre and a workshop organiser, agreed the problem urgently needs addressing.
- Cites Christa Kuljian's paper on a displayed photograph of four unidentified women captioned "San are not isolated", linked to Raymond Dart's search for a "pure" bushman type.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: university and public funds, with nothing under a flag. The open questions about the centre are curatorial, not financial.
