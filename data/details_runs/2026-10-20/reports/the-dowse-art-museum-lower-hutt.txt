THE DOWSE ART MUSEUM: Lower Hutt, New Zealand
ID: the-dowse-art-museum-lower-hutt
Website: https://dowse.org.nz/about-us/support-us

OVERVIEW
- Council-run public art and craft museum at 45 Laings Road, Lower Hutt.
- Opened on 31 May 1971 as the Dowse Art Gallery. Renamed the Dowse Art Museum in 1981 under director James Mack (the museum's own history). The idea came in 1963 from the Hutt Art Society and Mayor Percy Dowse; the Hutt City Council voted for the gallery despite public opinion that the money was better spent on pensioners' flats and playgrounds (museum history).
- Named for Percy Dowse, Mayor of Lower Hutt from 1950 to 1970 (museum history). Wikipedia adds that the building was first conceived as a memorial to his wife Mary Dowse, who died in a 1964 road accident.
- Building: the 1971 gallery was designed by R.C. Muston of Structon Group Architects. The museum closed in 2005 for two years, and Ian Athfield won the contest to remodel it in 2006; Wikipedia states the remodel was completed in 2013 and credits Athfield Architects.
- Collection and programme: a craft and contemporary art collection built from the 1970s, and the pataka whakairo Nuku Tewhatewha, returned to the Hutt Valley in 1982. The Dowse hosted the hip-hop oriented Respect festival (2001 photo caption, museum history).
- Visiting: free entry, open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm (museum site, archived January 2025).

FUNDING
The Dowse Art Museum: mainly public: Hutt City Council
- The museum states it "receives most of our funding from Hutt City Council" (museum site, archived 2025).
- The Dowse Foundation takes donations for the museum's collection, exhibitions and education programmes (museum site).
- 2005 redevelopment: total cost $5.906 million, with $1.969 million from the central government, an equal $1.969 million from local government and the Dowse Foundation raising the balance through community fundraising (New Zealand Government media statement, Helen Clark, 23 May 2005).
- The Blumhardt Foundation gave $1.5 million from ceramicist Doreen Blumhardt toward the building's development, which opened the Blumhardt Gallery (museum history).
- Corporate partner: Dulux, named on the museum's Partner with us page (archived January 2025) as one of its current corporate partners. Steel and Tube, a Petone-based company, is named as an early donor to the Free Bus Campaign for school visits, with the Rotary Club of Hutt Valley.
- Community Pathway: engraved bricks from $150 NZD, managed by the Rotary Club of Hutt Valley, with proceeds to the Dowse Foundation for school bus transport.
- Past central government support: the Queen Elizabeth II Arts Council helped buy Colin McCahon's Through the Wall of Death in 1978 (museum history).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Dowse is a local council budget supplemented by the Dowse Foundation, the Blumhardt Foundation gift, a paint company and a steel company.
