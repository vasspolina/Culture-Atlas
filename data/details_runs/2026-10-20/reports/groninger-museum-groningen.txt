GRONINGER MUSEUM: Groningen, Netherlands
ID: groninger-museum-groningen
Website: https://www.groningermuseum.nl/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art and design museum at Museumeiland 1, on islands in the Verbindingskanaal about 300 metres from Groningen central station (stated by the space, Dutch Wikipedia).
- Its roots go back to the Museum van Germaanse Oudheden (1847) and the Provinciaal Kabinet van Oudheden (1874); the first purpose-built museum on the Praediniussingel went up from 1894 to 1907 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- The current building opened in 1994, designed by Alessandro Mendini with Michele De Lucchi, Philippe Starck and Coop Himmelb(l)au, and was renovated in 2010 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- In 1987 Gasunie gave the City of Groningen 25 million guilders, which made the new building possible (Dutch Wikipedia).
- More than 80,000 objects: archaeology, Groningen history, applied arts, old and contemporary art, De Ploeg, fashion, photography and Asian porcelain (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Run since 2025 by artistic director Roos Gortzak; Nynke Stellingsma became interim general administrator after the business director left on 1 January 2026 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday; adults €19.50, students €10, under-19s and Museumkaart holders free; two disabled parking bays on the Ubbo Emmiussingel (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Groninger Museum: mainly public: City and Province of Groningen and the Dutch culture ministry, with Gasunie and GasTerra as sponsors
- 2025 income was €10.405 million: city €4.455 million, province €1.447 million, other structural public grants €442,000, culture ministry €475,000, ticket sales €1.591 million, private foundations €734,000 and charity lotteries €674,000 (annual report 2025).
- Sponsorship income was €20,000 in 2025 (annual report 2025).
- The 2025 report lists four sponsors: Bedrijvenvereniging WEST, GasTerra, Gasunie and Rabobank Stad en Midden Groningen; it thanks GasTerra and Gasunie for the exhibition on how Van Gogh came to Groningen (annual report 2025).
- It also lists the Stichting Fondsbeheer Culturele Relatie-evenementen as a benefactor; the museum's 2002 report says Gasunie set up this foundation to stage a large exhibition every two years, starting with Ilya Repin in 2001 to 2002 (annual reports 2002 and 2025).
- The culture ministry granted €313,390 a year for four years from 2025 (annual report 2025).
- The current partners page lists Gasunie but not GasTerra (stated by the space). GasTerra says it has had no sponsorship budget since 2024 and closes at the end of 2026 (GasTerra).

Red flags: gas company sponsors Gasunie and GasTerra
- GasTerra was the sales office for gas from the Groningen field and is owned 25% by Shell, 25% by ExxonMobil and 50% by the Dutch state (Wikipedia).
- Gasunie held 9% of Nord Stream and wrote the stake off in full, €508 million, in 2022 (NS Energy).
- The parliamentary inquiry found on 24 February 2023 that Groningers' interests were "structureel genegeerd" (structurally ignored, own translation) in gas extraction, which brought the state €363 billion and Shell and ExxonMobil €66 billion (Tweede Kamer).

Red flags: Fossil Free Culture NL protests, 2022 to 2025
- On 8 April 2022 the group performed at the opening of Marit Westerhuis's show "Megalith" at her invitation, demanding the museum cut ties with Gasunie and GasTerra (Art Not Oil, advocacy source).
- Its October 2024 soundscape action, with Extinction Rebellion Groningen, was its sixth at the museum; in June 2025 it installed a memorial of 1,932 dots for recorded gasquakes (Fossil Free Culture NL, advocacy source).
- On 31 October 2025 it staged a silent protest in the exhibition "It's About Time". The museum answered that GasTerra and Gasunie work hard on the energy transition and that it trusts both enough to keep the sponsorships (OOG TV).
- On 30 November 2025 it called the museum the last major Dutch cultural institution taking fossil gas sponsorship (Fossil Free Culture NL, advocacy source). No reporting found of a 2026 action.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The criticism comes from activist actions reported by the group itself and local press.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Dutch Wikipedia states that Gasunie's ties to Gazprom through Nord Stream influenced the museum's programme and led to exhibitions of Russian art; no independent source found for that claim.
- The museum's 2002 report says the Repin exhibition began with a conversation between Gasunie director George Verberg and museum director Kees van Twist, and opened with the Russian culture minister present (annual report 2002).

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from the city, province and state pays for most of the museum; sponsorship is only €20,000 a year. The weak point is the long tie to Gasunie and GasTerra, from the 1987 building gift to 2025 exhibition support, kept despite repeated protest. GasTerra closes at the end of 2026; whether Gasunie stays is worth watching.
