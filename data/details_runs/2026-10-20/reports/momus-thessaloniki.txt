MOMUS: Thessaloniki, Greece
ID: momus-thessaloniki
Website: https://www.momus.gr

OVERVIEW
- MOMus, the Metropolitan Organisation of Museums of Visual Arts of Thessaloniki, runs five institutions: the Museum of Modern Art with the Costakis Collection, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Thessaloniki Museum of Photography and the Experimental Center for the Arts in Thessaloniki, and the Alex Mylona Museum in Athens; the Hamza Bey Mosque "Alkazar" is also listed among its venues (MOMus website).
- Four museums in Thessaloniki and one in Athens joined forces in November 2018 to form MOMus (MOMus website).
- In April 2017 the Culture Minister Lydia Koniordou announced the merger of the State Museum of Contemporary Art and the Macedonian Museum of Contemporary Art as the first step (Greek Travel Pages, 21 April 2017).
- The Museum of Modern Art was founded in 1997, during Thessaloniki's year as European Capital of Culture, by a law sponsored by the Culture Minister Evangelos Venizelos, as the State Museum of Contemporary Art; it is in the renovated Lazariston Monastery in Stavroupoli, west Thessaloniki (Wikipedia).
- The Greek state bought a large part of the Costakis Collection on 31 March 2000 for 14.2 billion drachmas, which formed the museum's first collection (Wikipedia).
- The Experimental Center for the Arts is at Warehouse B1, Pier A, in the Port of Thessaloniki (Wikipedia).
- Management board chair Epaminondas Christophilopoulos, vice chair Jelina Makrantonaki, seven members; general director Fani Tsatsaia (MOMus website).
- All MOMus venues are closed on Mondays. Most open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00 and Thursday 12:00 to 20:00. A myMOMus Card gives access to all venues for six months from the first visit; the Museum of Contemporary Art is free to enter; reduced rates apply to students, visitors aged 12 to 25 and over-65s (MOMus website).

FUNDING
MOMus: mainly public: the Greek Ministry of Culture and the EU Recovery Fund
- Subsidy: in April 2017 Culture Minister Lydia Koniordou said the ministry would subsidise the new body with about 1.2 million euros a year (Greek Travel Pages, GTP editing team, 21 April 2017).
- Acquisitions: a three-year ministry grant of 240,000 euros, 80,000 euros a year, extended the emergency grant received in 2020 to cover 2021 and 2022 and bought 45 new works for the Museum of Contemporary Art and the Museum of Photography (Naftemporiki, 27 June 2021; title translated).
- Recovery Resilience Fund: more than 1 million euros for three MOMus projects, a new audiovisual brand and digital applications, support for producing digital works and international exhibitions, and programmes for visitors aged 65 and over and visitors with vision loss, announced by Deputy Culture Minister Christos Dimas (To Vima, tovima.com newsroom, 21 January 2024).
- Thessaloniki International Fair (TIF): the museum has been housed in the TIF exhibition centre since 1992, and its president, Xanthippi Heupel, said TIF "has been and remains the largest sponsor in the history of the museum" (in.gr, 11 September 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
MOMus is a state museum group paid for by the Greek Ministry of Culture, with an announced annual subsidy of about 1.2 million euros, a 240,000 euro acquisitions grant for 2020 to 2022 and over 1 million euros of EU Recovery Resilience Fund money announced in 2024.
