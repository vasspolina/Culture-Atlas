RUSSIAN PAVILION AT THE VENICE BIENNALE: Venice, Italy
ID: russian-pavilion-at-the-venice-biennale-venice
Website: none confirmed (pavilionrus.com returned a server error when checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National pavilion of the Russian Federation in the Giardini, designed by Alexey Shchusev and built between 1913 and 1914 (Wikipedia).
- Russia owns the building and, the Biennale said, had only to notify it of its request to take part in 2026 (AP, 1 May 2026).
- Russia last showed in 2019, did not show in 2022 (see Red flags) and lent the pavilion to Bolivia in 2024 (AP).
- 2026: "The Tree Is Rooted in the Sky", sound performances by 38 artists, commissioner Anastasiia Karneeva (Meduza).
- Visiting: open to the public only on the preview days, 5 to 8 May 2026, then closed from the 9 May opening to 22 November, with recordings on screens over the windows (Artlyst, 28 April 2026).

FUNDING
Russian Pavilion: state-owned building; exhibition funding unpublished; Novatek paid for the 2021 renovation
- Countries that own their pavilions do not pay to use them (ArtReview, 11 March 2026). No accounts, funder list or board are published.
- Novatek, the gas producer owned by Leonid Mikhelson, paid for the renovation before the 2021 Architecture Biennale (Filonenko, IWM).
- Unverified: that Mikhelson funds the 2026 pavilion and Smart Art, a claim Ukraine's culture ministry made when Ukraine sanctioned Karneeva in April 2026 (Interfax-Ukraine). The ministry opposes Russia's participation.

Red flags: boycott and protest
- 28 February 2022: artists Kirill Savchenkov and Alexandra Sukhareva and curator Raimundas Malašauskas withdrew from the planned Russian project; Savchenkov wrote of "no place for art when civilians are dying" (Designboom, 1 March 2022). The Ukrainian pavilion team issued a statement the same day (ArtReview).
- 2025: Michał Murawski, co-curator of the Ukrainian pavilion, protested at the Russian pavilion, accusing the Biennale and architect Thomas Heatherwick, of collaborating with Russia (Meduza; no month given).
- By 11 March 2026: the European Commission called Russia's return "not compatible with the EU's collective response", and culture ministers of 22 European countries had signed a protest letter (ArtReview, 11 March 2026).
- 30 April 2026: the Biennale's five-member jury resigned (AP, 1 May 2026). 6 May 2026: 50 members of Pussy Riot and FEMEN blockaded the pavilion for at least half an hour (AP).

Red flags: EU grant cancelled
- April 2026: the European Commission announced it would cancel a 2 million euro multiyear grant to the Biennale Foundation over the Russian pavilion and gave it 30 days to respond; the EU confirmed the termination on 21 July 2026 (ArtReview).

Red flags: sanctions and the commissioner's circle
- Karneeva, commissioner since 2021, is the daughter of Nikolai Volobuev, a retired FSB general and former Rostec deputy director; Finestre sull'Arte (4 March 2026) writes that Rostec has been under US and EU sanctions since 2014.
- She co-founded the company Smart Art with Ekaterina Vinokurova, daughter of Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov (Finestre sull'Arte).
- Vinokurova is listed: the US Treasury designated her on 6 April 2022 as Lavrov's adult child; the UK sanctioned her, announced 8 April 2022 (US Treasury, Infobae). ArtReview adds Canada, Australia and Japan.
- No source opened places Karneeva herself on a US, EU, UK or UN list. Ukraine sanctioned her by a decree published on 10 April 2026 (Kyiv Post); that is none of those lists.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Heavy and recent. Critics attack the decision to readmit Russia and the people behind the pavilion, not the art.
1. Artlyst: News Desk, "Russia's Venice Biennale Pavilion Will Be Closed To The Public" (2026)
URL: https://artlyst.com/?p=196501
- Calls the arrangement a compromise that resolves nothing; the legal defence is "technically defensible", but the decision, on current evidence, was not wise.
2. Finestre sull'Arte: Redazione, "Venice Biennale 2026, Russia returns: here's who's behind the regime pavilion" (2026)
URL: https://www.finestresullarte.info/en/news/venice-biennale-2026-russia-returns-here-s-who-s-behind-the-regime-pavilion
- Names Karneeva's family and business ties and Mikhail Shvydkoi's support for the invasion; machine-translated page.
3. ArtReview: "Ukraine Pavilion protests Russian representation at the Venice Biennale" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/ukraine-pavilion-a-statement-on-russian-representation-at-the-venice-biennale
- Statement by Pavlo Makov, Maria Lanko, Lizaveta German and Borys Filonenko: Russia should not be part of the Biennale; the pavilion is "just another piece of loud evidence of Russian cultural appropriation".

WIDER CONTEXT
- Filonenko links Russia's 1993 handover and the 2024 Bolivia loan to Russian gas and lithium deals, arguing culture is tied to resource trade (IWM, 2024).
- The Russian pavilion is one of the national pavilions, so the critique applies to it.

BOTTOM LINE
The building belongs to the Russian state and no funder list is published; Novatek's money is documented for the 2021 renovation, and Mikhelson's funding of the 2026 project is an unverified Ukrainian claim. The weak points are the commissioner's circle, the undisclosed budget and the lost EU grant.
