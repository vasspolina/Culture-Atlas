MUSEU DE ARTE MODERNA DA BAHIA: Salvador, Brazil
ID: museu-de-arte-moderna-da-bahia-salvador
Website: https://artsandculture.google.com/partner/mam-bahia

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum in the Solar do Unhão complex in Salvador; the complex was listed as national heritage by the national heritage agency SPHAN in 1943 (Portuguese Wikipedia, Google Arts and Culture).
- Created by state law in 1959 and opened on 6 January 1960 in the foyer of the Teatro Castro Alves; it moved to the Solar do Unhão in 1963 (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- In 1959 Lavínia Magalhães invited the architect Lina Bo Bardi to direct the museum; she also designed it and led the restoration of the Solar, which began in August 1962 and finished in 1963 (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- Rail tracks used to move production from the site's former snuff factory ("antiga fábrica de rapé") remain in the floor (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- It has five exhibition rooms, a sculpture park and a cinema (Google Arts and Culture). The collection has more than 1,200 pieces from transfers from the former Museu do Estado and from donations, including Portinari, Tarsila do Amaral, Carybé and Rubem Valentim (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- The museum is one of 12 state museums under the Diretoria de Museus, which is linked to the state heritage agency IPAC (Portuguese Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; Google Arts and Culture says it attracts about 200,000 visitors a year.
- After a packed concert at the Solar do Unhão in December 2019, Correio ("MAM em risco: tombado, museu erguido há 400 anos é palco de show e polêmica", 15 December 2019, no byline) quoted experts on the risks of large events in a listed building. The architect Neilton Dórea (UFBA) warned that loud sound causes vibration that can affect the structure, the engineer Adilson Rodrigues da Costa (UFOP) said vibration can spread existing cracks, and the geologist Ricardo Fraga (UFBA) said litter and vibration can affect marine fauna in the Baía de Todos os Santos protected area. The national heritage agency Iphan said it had found no damage but that the shows had no federal authorisation, and notified IPAC. IPAC replied that events go through a paid authorisation process and that waste must be removed within 24 hours.

FUNDING
Museu de Arte Moderna da Bahia: run by the Bahia state government through IPAC
- The renovation of the museum, described in 2014 as the most important building intervention in 50 years, was paid for from the state treasury: R$ 7.5 million for the first stage and R$ 8 million planned for the second (IPAC, "Secretário de Cultura visita obras de reforma do MAM", 19 December 2014). In 2019 IPAC said the museum had received R$ 15 million in renovation (Correio, 15 December 2019).
- Event rental: IPAC told Correio that shows held at the museum go through a paid-use authorisation, with revenue returned to maintaining the museum (Correio, 15 December 2019).
- Restaurant and pier: on 20 December 2023 IPAC announced a highest-bid tender for an onerous concession of just over 630 square metres covering the museum's restaurant, pier and docking area, to run a restaurant and the docking area, with the bidding session set for 15 January 2024 (Mauricio Leiro and Gabriel Lopes, Bahia Notícias, 20 December 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run and paid for by the Bahia state government through IPAC, which told Correio in 2019 that the state had spent R$ 15 million on the renovation, and which takes revenue from event hire for maintenance. The documented private element is the concession tender announced in December 2023 for its restaurant and pier, and the documented dispute is the 2019 concert at the heritage site.
