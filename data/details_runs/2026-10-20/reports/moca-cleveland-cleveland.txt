MOCA CLEVELAND: Cleveland, USA
ID: moca-cleveland-cleveland
Website: https://www.mocacleveland.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 11400 Euclid Avenue in Uptown, University Circle, Cleveland (moCa website, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1968 as The New Gallery, a for-profit gallery in a former dry-cleaning storefront, by Marjorie Talalay, Agnes Gund and Nina Castelli Sundell; it became a nonprofit in 1974, was renamed the Cleveland Centre for Contemporary Art in 1984 and the Museum of Contemporary Art Cleveland in 2002 (Wikipedia).
- The current building, designed by Farshid Moussavi of Foreign Office Architects with Westlake Reed Leskosky, opened on 8 October 2012; it has four storeys, about 34,000 square feet, and a reflective black stainless steel exterior (Wikipedia).
- Jill Snyder was executive director until June 2020; Megan Lykins Reich took over and was named executive director in 2022 (Ideastream, Wikipedia).
- Hours listed on the visit page: Thursday and Friday 13:00 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00. Admission is free for Ohio residents and anyone 18 and under, and USD 10 for adults from outside Ohio.
- Elevator access to all floors; all levels wheelchair accessible except the Kulas Overlook (moCa visit page).

FUNDING
moCa Cleveland: private with public grants: foundations, individuals, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Cuyahoga Arts & Culture and the Ohio Arts Council
- In the fiscal year 2025 revenue was USD 1,978,127, about 81% from contributions; expenses were USD 3,606,176; net loss USD 1,628,049; net assets USD 23,041,004 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 filed 15 June 2026). Expenses have exceeded revenue in most recent years (ProPublica).
- Lead institutional support, in part: Bloomberg Philanthropies, the residents of Cuyahoga County through a public grant from Cuyahoga Arts & Culture, and the Ohio Arts Council (moCa sponsors page, undated).
- The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts supports all exhibitions (moCa sponsors page).
- Named institutional supporters include the Cleveland Foundation, The George Gund Foundation, Art Bridges Foundation, PNC, Dealer Tire and the Kulas Foundation, plus named individuals; the page gives no amounts or dates.

Red flags: cancelled Shaun Leonardo exhibition
- In March 2020 the museum cancelled Shaun Leonardo's solo show "The Breath of Empty Space", which included drawings of police killings of Black and Latino men and boys, saying the images could be traumatising for African Americans in Cleveland (The Art Newspaper, 5 August 2021). The museum cited a "troubling community response" (Hyperallergic).
- Leonardo, who is Black, called the cancellation censorship and wrote that "institutional white fragility led to an act of censorship" (Hyperallergic, 8 July 2020).
- Snyder apologised in June 2020, admitting the museum failed the artist and breached his trust; the museum acknowledged it "did not engage Mr. Leonardo in creating space for dialogue and debate" (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic). She resigned on 19 June 2020 after 23 years, with a statement that did not mention the cancellation (Ideastream).
- Samaria Rice, mother of Tamir Rice, objects to the use of her son's image without her permission (Hyperallergic). The sources read describe a museum decision, not an act by a state body.
- In August 2021 the board replaced its single president with three co-presidents (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2020 cancellation and was heaviest in 2020 and 2021; no criticism of the museum's funders was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Weber, "Samaria Rice, Mother of Tamir Rice, Speaks Out About Art Depicting Her Son After Canceled Exhibition in Cleveland" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/573053/moca-cleveland-the-breath-of-empty-space/
- Frames the case as a conflict over who controls the depiction of victims of police violence: artistic freedom against the family's consent.
- Reports Cleveland activists' and curators' critique that the museum did not consult Black community members or scholars enough.
- Names the museum and Snyder.
2. The Art Newspaper: Nancy Kenney, "A year after a racially tinged controversy, Moca Cleveland revises structure of its board of directors" (2021)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/a-year-after-a-racially-tinged-controversy-moca-cleveland-revises-structure-of-its-board-of-directors
- Reports the board overhaul and quotes the museum's claim that the new structure promotes "equity in decision-making"; quotes Snyder's admission that the museum failed the artist.
- News report rather than an argued critique.

WIDER CONTEXT
- More than 260 British artists, through Artists for Palestine UK, asked five British theatres in May 2026 to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies, saying it is "complicit in grave war crimes" through involvement with Israeli settlements (The New Arab, 29 May 2026).
- The campaign names five British theatres, not moCa. Bloomberg Philanthropies is a lead institutional supporter of moCa.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private foundations and individuals with Bloomberg Philanthropies and two public arts grants; the Form 990 shows expenses above revenue for several years. The weak point is the 2020 cancellation of the Leonardo show, which led to an apology, the director's resignation and a board restructure, not to any funder issue. Worth watching: how long the museum can run deficits, and whether Bloomberg's support is challenged locally.
