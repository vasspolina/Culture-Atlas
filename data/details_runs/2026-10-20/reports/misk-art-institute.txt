MISK ART INSTITUTE: Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
ID: misk-art-institute
Website: https://miskartinstitute.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit arts organisation in Riyadh that runs residencies, training, a grant, public programmes, Misk Art Week and exhibitions; its main venue is the Prince Faisal bin Fahd Arts Hall (Misk Art Institute website).
- Established in December 2017 by the Misk Foundation, which Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman founded in 2011 (Misk Art Institute website, Wikipedia). Artist Ahmed Mater was its founding director (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- The hall was established in 1985 by royal decree of King Fahd as the Fine Arts Hall, the first government building in Riyadh devoted to fine arts, and was later named for Prince Faisal bin Fahd (Arab News, 31 December 2019).
- The hall covers 5,600 square metres and reopened under the Institute at the end of 2019 with the show "Story Behind the Place" for its 35th anniversary (Arab News).
- The Institute has been the commissioner of Saudi Arabia's national pavilion at the Venice Biennale since 2018 (Arab News).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility; the site gives directions to the hall but no visitor details.

FUNDING
Misk Art Institute: state-linked: subsidiary of the Misk Foundation, founded by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman
- The Institute describes itself as affiliated with the non-profit Mohammed bin Salman Foundation "Misk" (Misk Art Institute website). The Art Newspaper called it the artistic arm of the Crown Prince's philanthropic organisation (2017).
- Saudi Culture Minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah chairs the Institute and patronised the 2019 reopening of the hall (Arab News).
- Misk Foundation's own funding sources are not stated in any source read.
- No reporting found of a budget, accounts or donor list for the Institute.

Red flags: Gulf state patronage
- The Institute is a subsidiary of a foundation the Crown Prince founded, and in December 2019 the culture minister chaired it (Misk Art Institute website, Arab News).
- Sahakian wrote that Mater was appointed by the Crown Prince and that Mater called the approach "instructed by the Crown Prince" (Hyperallergic, 2018).

Red flags: institutions declined Misk money after the Khashoggi killing
- In October 2018 Misk had planned to fully fund the Brooklyn Museum exhibition "Syria, Then and Now" and a Met seminar on collecting the Middle East; both museums paid for them themselves instead. The Met's cost was under USD 20,000 (Hyperallergic, Jasmine Weber, 18 October 2018).
- Columbia University cancelled a lecture by Mater scheduled for 22 October 2018, and the Middle East Institute withdrew from the Arab Art and Education Initiative (Hyperallergic).
- In November 2018 the Gates Foundation suspended its planned engagement with the Misk Foundation, calling the killing "extremely troubling" (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is limited to a few pieces from October and November 2018, after the Khashoggi killing. No critical piece from art media after 2018 was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Rijin Sahakian, "A Coalition of The Willing: How Saudi Arabia and US Arts Institutions Partnered on a Cultural Diplomacy Offensive" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/468850/saudi-arabia-and-us-art-institutions/
- Argues Misk is presented as grassroots but is tied to the Saudi government and the Crown Prince, and that Mater and Stephen Stapleton effectively run the Saudi art scene under state direction.
- Argues the Met and Brooklyn Museum acted too late, having long known the kingdom's record, and that positive press from Sotheby's and The Art Newspaper helped rebrand the Crown Prince as a reformer.
- Names the Institute at length.
2. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Weber, "Several Major New York Art Institutions Step Away from Saudi-Funded Initiatives" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/466508/several-major-new-york-art-institutions-step-away-from-saudi-funded-initiatives/
- News report, not an essay: lists the museums that took over funding Misk had planned and the cancelled Columbia lecture.
- States the Institute was founded by the Crown Prince.
3. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, "Do not penalise Saudi Arabian artists for the murder of Jamal Khashoggi" (2018)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/comment/do-not-penalise-saudi-arabian-artists-for-the-murder-of-jamal-khashoggi
- Argues Saudi artists should not be punished for the killing and that fear and self-censorship among them have grown.
- Names the Institute and Mater but does not criticise either; it criticises the Crown Prince by implication.
4. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, "Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince adds art and culture to his portfolio" (2017)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/saudi-arabia-s-crown-prince-adds-art-and-culture-to-his-portfolio
- Not criticism: describes the Institute's plans and does not state its budget or funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute is a Saudi state-leadership project: a Crown Prince foundation subsidiary that the culture minister chaired in 2019, with no budget, accounts or donor list published. The weak points are that opacity and the October 2018 withdrawals of Western partners after the Khashoggi killing. Worth watching: whether the Institute publishes accounts and who funds the hall's programme.
