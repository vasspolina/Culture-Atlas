ORIGINS CENTRE: Johannesburg, South Africa
ID: origins-centre-johannesburg
Website: https://www.wits.ac.za/origins/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of human origins of the University of the Witwatersrand (Wits) at Yale Road and Enoch Sontonga Avenue on Wits' West campus, Braamfontein, Johannesburg, on a site above the M1 highway across from Braamfontein cemetery (Wits; Mail & Guardian, 13 April 2006).
- Opened by President Thabo Mbeki in 2006 (Wits; South African History Online). South African History Online lists it as part of the Wits Faculty of Science.
- Built on the site of the fine art department's studios, known as "The Wedge" (Mail & Guardian, 13 April 2006).
- Designed by Craig McClenaghan of Mashabane Rose and Associates; the Mail & Guardian says he designed the halls to feel like caves, with dark spaces and walls "a metre thick".
- On show: hominin innovation from over 2 million years ago, stone tools, San beliefs, the impact of colonialism and examples of resistance, and a rock art collection from Wits' Rock Art Research Institute (RARI) (Wits). Artworks include Walter Oltman's woven map of the Earth and Willem Boshoff's "Signs of People" (Mail & Guardian).
- Open Monday to Friday 09:00 to 17:00 and Saturday and public holidays 09:00 to 16:00; closed Sunday. Tickets are booked online through Webtickets, and guided tours are booked in advance. The visitor car park is access controlled: call the centre for the code and bring ID (Wits).

FUNDING
Origins Centre: university museum: part of the Wits Faculty of Science
- South African History Online lists the centre as part of the Wits Faculty of Science; it is presented on the Wits website, which links to a Wits giving page for donations (South African History Online; Wits).
- Its rock art collection comes from the Rock Art Research Institute (RARI) at Wits (Wits).

BOTTOM LINE
The Origins Centre is part of the Wits Faculty of Science, so the university is its institutional home, and the centre takes donations through the Wits giving page.
