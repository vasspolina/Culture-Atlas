GALERI PETRONAS: Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia
ID: galeri-petronas
Website: https://www.petronas.com/media/media-releases/galeri-petronas-embarks-new-chapter-ombak-klcc (checked 2026-10-20; the record has no website and no standalone gallery site was found, so this is the owner's release)

OVERVIEW
- Corporate contemporary art gallery of PETRONAS, now an anchor tenant at Ombak KLCC, 20 Persiaran KLCC, in the Kuala Lumpur City Centre precinct (PETRONAS; Kuala Lumpur City guide).
- Established on 12 August 1992 at the Dayabumi Complex, then PETRONAS's headquarters, and moved to Suria KLCC, below the Petronas Twin Towers, in 1998 (Kuala Lumpur City guide; The Star via Asia News Network).
- It closed as a public gallery in May 2021, when PETRONAS realigned its operating model during the Covid-19 pandemic, and reopened at Suria KLCC in 2025 (FMT/Bernama; The Star).
- It left Suria KLCC at the end of July 2026; a "First Glimpse" preview opened at Ombak KLCC on 21 August 2026 and full opening is planned by the end of 2027 (PETRONAS).
- It shows the PETRONAS Art Collection, built since 1993, and has had nearly two million visitors (PETRONAS). Kerina Fauzi heads the gallery (The Star).
- Preview hours: Tuesday to Friday 10am to 6pm, weekends 10am to 6:30pm, closed Mondays; admission varies by programme; nearest station Persiaran KLCC MRT (Kuala Lumpur City guide). No reporting found of accessibility provisions.

FUNDING
Galeri Petronas: corporate-owned: PETRONAS, Malaysia's state oil and gas company
- The gallery and its collection belong to PETRONAS; no separate budget, sponsor list or accounts are published.
- PETRONAS is a Malaysian oil and gas company founded in 1974, wholly owned by the Government of Malaysia (Wikipedia).
- When it closed in 2021, the gallery's permanent staff were moved to other PETRONAS roles and the collection stayed available for loan (FMT/Bernama).

Red flags: oil and gas owner
- PETRONAS works across the oil and gas industry, from exploration and extraction to refining and trading (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia attributes 0.69% of global industrial greenhouse gas emissions from 1988 to 2015 to the company's activities.
- Unverified: PETRONAS was complicit in war crimes in Sudan's Block 5A in 1997 to 2003, from the European Coalition on Oil in Sudan's 2010 report "Unpaid Debt", an advocacy coalition, which asked Malaysia, Sweden and Austria to investigate; Wikipedia says PETRONAS has never publicly responded.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism; the main critical piece is about PETRONAS's 2021 closure of the gallery, not its money.
1. The Edge Malaysia: Tan Gim Ean, "Galeri Petronas' closure: Local artists and gallerists weigh in on future of Malaysian art scene" (2021)
URL: https://www.optionstheedge.com/node/2338
- Collector Pakhruddin Sulaiman rejected the Covid explanation ("Don't blame it on Covid") and called the company's leaders "simply philistines".
- Painter Syed Thajudeen suggested new management viewed the gallery as "a white elephant"; an unnamed gallery owner said it had "no connection with what's happening on the ground" and was "very selective".
- Criticises PETRONAS's commitment to the gallery, not its fossil fuel business.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is a department of PETRONAS, the Malaysian state's oil and gas company, so all its money is oil and gas money. The 2021 closure showed it depends on the company's priorities. Worth watching: whether the Ombak KLCC opening by end 2027 comes with a published budget or programme independence.
