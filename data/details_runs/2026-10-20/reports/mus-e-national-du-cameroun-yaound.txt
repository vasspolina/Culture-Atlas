MUSÉE NATIONAL DU CAMEROUN: Yaoundé, Cameroon
ID: mus-e-national-du-cameroun-yaound
Website: https://cameroon-nationalmuseum.cm/

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Cameroon in Yaoundé, housed in the former presidential palace (museum website).
- President Paul Biya assigned the site to the museum on 17 November 1988 (museum website).
- The building covers 5,000 m² with about 30 rooms; Le360 reports it reopened on 16 January 2014 after about five years of closure for rehabilitation, while AFD dates the reopening to 2015 (Le360, 30 July 2019; AFD).
- Collections cover four cultural areas: Grassfields, Sawa, Soudano-Sahelian and Fang Beti. The museum website describes an Ancient Egypt collection given by the Egyptian embassy.
- The director is Professor Hugues Heumen Tchana, who also coordinates the modernisation project Padmun (Cameroon Tribune, 3 December 2024).
- Visitors can book online for individuals or groups, and a souvenir shop is open during visits (museum website).

FUNDING
Musée National du Cameroun: public: a French debt-conversion grant for its modernisation, with the Cameroonian Ministry of Arts and Culture as counterpart
- AFD financed €1 million for the modernisation of the museum, signed with the Cameroonian government on 26 July 2019 in Yaoundé, equal to 655 million FCFA (Le360, Patricia Ngo Ngouem, 30 July 2019). AFD lists the financing amount as €1,000,000 and the start as 1 October 2019 (AFD).
- The money comes from the third Contrat de désendettement et de développement (C2D) between France and Cameroon. Le360 calls it the second cultural heritage project financed through this instrument.
- AFD describes three components: renovating the museum and fitting out its spaces, better protection and display of collections, and a scientific and cultural policy within a reinforced management plan. AFD says it supports the Cameroon Ministry of Culture in the project.
- Expertise France implemented a €600,000 technical assistance programme, with the French Ministry of Culture as partner and the Cameroonian Ministry of Arts and Culture as counterpart, from 7 September 2020 to 7 September 2024. Experts came from the Arc'Antique laboratory, the École du Louvre, Universcience and the Musée du quai Branly-Jacques Chirac (Expertise France).
- Its work covered governance (organisation chart, communication policy), collections and reserves (inventory, conservation), a scientific and cultural policy, and museographical and architectural pre-programming of the spaces (Expertise France).
- Cameroon Tribune (Michèle Fogang, "Modernisation du Musée national : les avancées appréciées", 3 December 2024, paywalled) reported that at a C2D event on 28 November 2024, Berthe Jeanine Tsafack, coordinator of the Stade-C2D secretariat, toured the museum and said its infrastructure was fully renovated. It describes the project as launched in July 2019 with a subsidy of over one million euros for a three-year term.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a €1 million French grant signed in July 2019, financed through Cameroon's C2D debt conversion with France and delivered through AFD, and a €600,000 Expertise France technical assistance programme that ran from September 2020 to September 2024.
