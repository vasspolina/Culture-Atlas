COMPTON VERNEY: Kineton, UK
ID: compton-verney-kineton
Website: https://www.comptonverney.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Art gallery and Capability Brown parkland in Warwickshire, on the B4086 between Kineton and Wellesbourne, about 10 km from junction 12 of the M40 (Wikipedia).
- The Peter Moores Foundation bought the estate in 1993 and gave it to the Compton Verney House Trust; a preview season ran in 1998 and the gallery opened to the public in March 2004 (Wikipedia). The gallery is now run by Compton Verney House Charity.
- The site's history: the 14th Baron Willoughby de Broke commissioned Robert Adam to propose alterations, Capability Brown redesigned the grounds from 1769, Joseph Watson, a Leeds soap manufacturer, bought it in 1921, and the army requisitioned it in the Second World War (Wikipedia, Compton Verney House).
- Building: a Grade I listed Georgian mansion; the gallery's history page gives £45 million for the restoration project, and Wikipedia says a modern wing was designed by Stanton Williams.
- Collections: six permanent collections, including Neapolitan art, Northern European medieval art, British portraits, Chinese bronzes and British folk art (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, grounds from 10 am and galleries from 10:30 am, closed Monday and Tuesday except bank holiday Mondays. Adult day ticket from £20.45 without donation booked ahead, 18 and under and members free, ages 19 to 25 £10, Universal Credit or Pension Credit holders £2. Free car park with 12 Blue Badge spaces, a tramper bookable in advance and a shuttle from the Welcome Centre to the house.

FUNDING
Compton Verney: visitor income and a founder's endowment, with an Arts Council England grant
- Income was £5,066,800 and spending £4,913,908 for the year to 31 March 2026 (Charity Commission data via Find that Charity). The charity has 113 employees and 14 trustees.
- Gill Sutherland wrote in the Stratford Herald on 10 March 2025 that the endowment of its founder, Sir Peter Moores, whom it calls "the Pools heir", "contributes around 37 per cent of funding", that around three per cent comes from Arts Council England, and that its biggest income stream comes from visitors through memberships, benefactors, donations and admissions.
- The gallery's support page says over 70 per cent of its income comes from visitors, supporters and funders, and carries the line "Supported using public funding by Arts Council England".
- The Peter Moores Foundation, set up in 1964, ran out of funds in April 2014 after giving away over £231 million (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gambling
- Founder fortune: Sir Peter Moores, whose foundation bought the estate in 1993, chaired the Liverpool football pools and retail business Littlewoods from 1977 to 1980; his father Sir John Moores founded it, and the family no longer owns it (Wikipedia).
- Norman Lebrecht wrote in Slipped Disc on 24 March 2016 that "Peter Moores inherited a half-share of a football pools fortune". Giles Waterfield wrote in The Art Newspaper in 2016 that his father founded Littlewoods, that Peter succeeded him as chairman in 1977 and that he was "extremely rich".
- Still funding: the Stratford Herald calls him "the Pools heir" and reports that his endowment contributes around 37 per cent of the gallery's funding (10 March 2025). Waterfield wrote that the Peter Moores Foundation supported Compton Verney with "lavish donations" and that the gallery "was given an endowment to maintain the historic fabric and grounds".

BOTTOM LINE
Compton Verney is a charity whose income was £5.07 million in the year to March 2026. The Stratford Herald puts the endowment from its founder Sir Peter Moores, heir to the Littlewoods football pools fortune, at about 37 per cent of funding in 2025, and Arts Council England at about 3 per cent. Its biggest income stream is visitors.
