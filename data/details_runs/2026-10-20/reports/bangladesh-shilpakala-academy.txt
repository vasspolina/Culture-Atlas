OVERVIEW
- National arts academy and venue at Segunbagicha, Shahbagh, Dhaka. A statutory body under the Ministry of Cultural Affairs (Wikipedia).
- Established on 19 February 1974 by an act of parliament, amended in 1989; Wikipedia's infobox lists Sheikh Mujibur Rahman as founder.
- What it does: workshops, seminars, training, scholarships and grants for artists, and competitions in fine and performing arts. It founded the National Art Gallery (Wikipedia).
- It hosted the Dhaka Art Summit in 2014, 2018, 2020 and 2023; the summit is free to the public (Wikipedia, Dhaka Art Summit).
- Leadership: Liaquat Ali Lucky stepped down on 5 August 2024 and Syed Jamil Ahmed resigned on 28 February 2025. Mohammad Wares Hossain acted from 4 March 2025, and on 14 September 2025 Abul Foyez Md Alauddin Khan, an additional secretary at the Ministry of Cultural Affairs, was given additional charge (The Daily Star).
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
Bangladesh Shilpakala Academy: state body: a statutory academy under the Ministry of Cultural Affairs; no budget or donor list published on the pages opened
- The Executive Council is headed by the minister in charge of the Ministry of Cultural Affairs (Wikipedia).
- The Dhaka Art Summit held at the academy is "almost wholly funded" by the Samdani Art Foundation, set up by the collectors Nadia and Rajeeb Samdani (Georgina Adam, The Art Newspaper, 2016). The summit has official partnerships with the Ministry of Cultural Affairs (New Critique, 2020).
- No reporting found of the academy's budget, accounts or a donor list. Its website failed to load on 24 October 2026 (TLS certificate error).

Red flags: Samdani Art Foundation, producer of the Dhaka Art Summit held at the academy
- Bonik Barta (English edition, 24 September 2024) reported that Rajeeb Samdani's company Golden Harvest has not repaid bond funds raised in 2017: the trustee, Green Delta Capital, said five institutions are owed BDT 392.9 million. The Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating whether market-raised funds were spent in their intended sectors. Golden Harvest's chief financial officer said its sponsorship of the summit was branding and that no company money went to organise it.
- Unverified: that 2019 rights-issue money was redirected to repay loans, from officials and sources cited by Bonik Barta, a Bangladeshi business outlet.
- The Department of Environment fined Rajeeb Samdani BDT 5.017 million in February 2024 over cutting soil from a hill to fill land for a resort project in Sylhet (Bonik Barta).
- Unverified: that Samdani was involved in the theft of artworks from the homes of Sheikh Rehana and Salman F Rahman after 5 August 2024, from a source quoted by Bonik Barta, which says it could not reach him.
- Wikipedia says the Samdani Art Foundation rejected allegations about the Samdanis' ties to the Sheikh family, including Sheikh Rehana.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the academy itself found in art media; the criticism found concerns the summit held there.
1. New Critique: Jonathan Webster, "[Review] Dhaka Art Summit 2020" (2020)
URL: https://newcritique.co.uk/2020/07/06/review-dhaka-art-summit-2020-jonathan-webster/
- Notes the summit is privately funded, works with the Ministry of Cultural Affairs and is held at the academy.
- Reports that at a Hill Artists' Group panel, held in a tent near military security, a group representative looked toward the military representative and said they could not answer a follow-up question; the author calls the summit's "censorship free" claim "not entirely true".
2. Hyperallergic: Faheem Haider, "Dhaka Art Summit Censors Pro-Tibet Artwork at Chinese Ambassador's Request" (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/274460/dhaka-art-summit-censors-pro-tibet-artwork-at-chinese-ambassadors-request/
- At the academy, Ritu Sarin and Tenzing Sonam's Last Words was covered after China's ambassador objected; the organisers complied. Does not say the academy or the government ordered it.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Dr Samina Luthfa, Dhaka University sociologist, in The Daily Star (15 November 2024): the academy "has effectively become an army encampment", and its director general "clearly mishandled" the halting of a performance on 2 November 2024. She says the Group Theatre Federation set schedules and government grants by political loyalty under Awami League policy. This is one interviewee's view.

BOTTOM LINE
The academy is a state body under the culture ministry, no budget was found, and its director general post changed hands three times between August 2024 and September 2025. The weak points are the ministry's control of the post, the academy's use as a venue for the Samdani-funded summit, and claims around the Samdanis that Bonik Barta reports without proof, alongside its reports of unpaid bonds. Worth watching: the academy's budget and whether it keeps the summit.
