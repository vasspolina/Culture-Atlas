FOLGER SHAKESPEARE LIBRARY: Washington, USA
ID: folger-shakespeare-library
Website: https://www.folger.edu (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Research library, museum and theatre at 201 East Capitol Street SE on Capitol Hill (Folger website).
- Opened in 1932 as a gift to the American people from Henry and Emily Folger, who made their first rare book purchase in 1889 (Folger website).
- Holds more than 300,000 objects, which the Folger calls the world's largest Shakespeare collection, with books, manuscripts and prints from the 1500s to the early 1700s; all 82 of its First Folios are shown in a 20-foot "visible vault" (Folger website; FY24 annual report).
- Reopened on 21 June 2024 after a four-year renovation that added a new public wing beneath the 1932 building, with two exhibition halls, a café and additional public space (FY24 annual report; Folger website).
- Farah Karim-Cooper has been director since October 2024 and D. Jarrett Arp chairs the Board of Governors (FY25 annual report); Michael Witmore led the Folger for 13 years before her (FY24 annual report).
- In FY25 it counted 140,820 visitors, audiences of 41,901 at 84 artistic programmes and 5,370 research visits (FY25 annual report).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 18:00, Fridays until 21:00; group tours cost $25 per person (Folger website).

FUNDING
Folger Shakespeare Library: endowed: the Folgers' bequest, managed by Amherst College's trustees, with private gifts
- The Folger calls itself "a private, independently endowed, tax exempt institution" governed by a Board of Governors and administered by the Trustees of Amherst College under the founders' wills; the Trustees are the official body under which the IRS recognises it (FY25 annual report).
- FY25 (to 30 June 2025): total operating revenues and support of $29.4 million, of which $21.1 million came from investment gains appropriated, $3.4 million from gifts and grants, $418,000 from US government grants, $931,000 from interest and $3.6 million from other income; operating expenses were $31.6 million; net assets stood at $633.3 million (FY25 annual report).
- Renovation: The Wonder of Will campaign had raised more than $50 million toward the $80.5 million needed for the renovation and expanded programming by 2023 (FY22-23 annual report).
- Campaign lead donors: Richard L. Adams and Family, Jacqueline Badger Mars, and Stuart and Mimi Rose; benefactors include the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation and the Lee and Juliet Folger Fund, and the National Endowment for the Humanities gave at the builders' level (FY24 annual report).
- FY25 donors of $50,000 or more include D. Jarrett and Nora Arp, Vinton and Sigrid Cerf, the DC Commission on the Arts and Humanities, the Hitz Foundation, the Josephine Fox Education Trust and Jacqueline Badger Mars (FY25 annual report).

BOTTOM LINE
Old endowment money, run through Amherst College, pays most of the bills, with private donors and small federal and city grants on top; none of the named funders carries a flag. Watch the remaining renovation fundraising and the donor lists in future annual reports.
