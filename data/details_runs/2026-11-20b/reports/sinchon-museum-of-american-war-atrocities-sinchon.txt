SINCHON MUSEUM OF AMERICAN WAR ATROCITIES: Sinchon, North Korea
ID: sinchon-museum-of-american-war-atrocities-sinchon
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- War museum in Sinchon County, South Hwanghae Province, reached on a day trip from Pyongyang, dedicated to the killings at Sinchon between 17 October and 7 December 1950, which it attributes to US and South Korean troops (Wikipedia; Travis Jeppesen, Daily Beast).
- Built at the order of Kim Il Sung and opened on 25 June 1960, the tenth anniversary of the outbreak of the Korean War (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2014; Daily NK, 2017). English Wikipedia's infobox gives 26 March 1958, with no source cited.
- Kim Jong Un visited in November 2014, said he came to "strengthen the anti-U.S. lessons for our military and people", and ordered the museum remodelled (Korea JoongAng Daily, 25 November 2014, citing KCNA).
- A larger new building opened in July 2015 on a new site (Wikipedia; Koryo Tours), with many exhibits in English (Koryo Tours).
- On show: survivor testimony, victim counts by district, paintings and photographs of bodies in about a dozen rooms (Patrick Tapy, Sino-NK, 2013); the grounds include a grave of 400 mothers and children and an amphitheatre called the "Revenge-Pledging Place" (Wikipedia; AFP).
- The museum gives a toll of over 35,000 dead (Wikipedia). KCNA claimed it receives 500,000 visitors a year (AFP, 2018).
- Visiting: closed to foreign visitors since spring 2018, amid the preparations for the Singapore summit (Koryo Tours). Groups from schools, army units, factories and official organisations are brought to the amphitheatre to denounce the United States (AFP, 2018).

FUNDING
Sinchon Museum of American War Atrocities: state-run: built and rebuilt on the orders of North Korea's leaders
- KCNA said in July 2015 that the new museum was built per Kim Jong Un's instructions, and that he called the completed building one that "met the demands of a military-first era" (UPI, 22 July 2015).
- Hankyung Business reported that construction began on 26 February 2015 and finished in four months, with workers on overtime to meet Kim's deadline (UPI, 22 July 2015).
- The historian Adam Cathcart (University of Leeds) wrote in July 2015 that North Korea "has pumped significant resources into a refurbished museum in Sinchon"; he gave no figure.
- Unverified: a source in South Hwanghae Province told Daily NK in April 2017 that state-run enterprises were sending employees on visits and that farmers were mobilised in the sowing season.
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA said on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the museum.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Institute for Korean Historical Studies (South Korea) concluded that communists and anti-communist vigilantes both carried out killings, and that the US 19th Infantry Regiment did not take part but failed to stop the South Korean secret police who came with it (Wikipedia). A 2002 report by the Munhwa Broadcasting Corporation concluded that clashes between communists and anti-communist vigilantes caused the killings (Korea JoongAng Daily).
- Novelist Hwang Sok-yong attributes the massacre to a local rivalry that used the fog of war as a pretence (Wikipedia). Cathcart reports that the historian Bruce Cumings found nothing in the archives about a US occupation of Sinchon, and wrote that "no serious writing outside of North Korea has been made to determine if the body count is accurate".
- Cathcart told AFP in 2018 that the first foreign unit on the scene was British, and that the North's account "underpinned a great deal of anti-U.S. propaganda".
- Patrick Tapy (Sino-NK, 17 September 2013) concluded that North Korean historians "do not possess any more evidence of actual US responsibility" than Cumings or Hwang Sok-yong.
- A 1952 report by the International Association of Democratic Lawyers, published in Pyongyang and relying on North Korean oral testimony, accused US forces (Wikipedia, "Sinchon Massacre").
- These disputes concern the account the museum presents, which the state built it to teach.

BOTTOM LINE
State money, spent on the leader's orders to carry an official account of the war that outside historians dispute. The sanctioned government is the funding risk; the mobilised visits for anti-US education are the point to watch.
