WINZAVOD CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Moscow, Russia
ID: winzavod-centre-for-contemporary-art-moscow
Website: https://winzavod.ru/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre at 4th Syromyatnichesky Lane 1/8, Moscow; the nearest metro stations are Kurskaya and Chkalovskaya (stated by the centre).
- Founded by the businessman Roman Trotsenko (Wikipedia) and opened in 2007; his wife Sofya Trotsenko led it from 2007 to March 2012 and by 2014 headed the foundation set up at the centre (Afisha Daily, 14 March 2014). The Art Newspaper reported the plan in 2006.
- Building: a complex of seven late-19th-century industrial buildings on the site of the former brewery "Moscow Bavaria", where Nicephorus Prokofiev opened a malt-brewing factory around 1810 (Wikipedia).
- On site: 12 resident galleries, studios, workshops that opened in 2022, showrooms, a foundation (the Winzavod Charitable Foundation for the Support and Development of Contemporary Art) and educational programmes (stated by the centre).
- Visiting: exhibitions and galleries open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 20:00; some exhibitions are free. No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Winzavod Centre for Contemporary Art: privately funded: the Trotsenko family, gallery rents and its foundation
- Founders' money: in 2006 The Art Newspaper reported Roman Trotsenko and his wife Sophia backing a $4 million project, which he called a "non-profit art development project"; each of five Moscow galleries was to rent about 300 square metres at a discount for five years (John Varoli, 31 March 2006).
- The Winzavod foundation, created in 2008 at Sofya Trotsenko's initiative, began with the "Start" programme for young artists and runs the centre's programmes (The Blueprint, 6 November 2024).
- Ownership: Afisha Daily reported on 14 March 2014, citing Artguide, that Alexander Svetakov of the Absolut investment group would become the centre's owner. The article does not say what became of the deal; Wikipedia names Roman Trotsenko as founder.
- No reporting found of the centre's accounts, a sponsor list or the foundation's annual budget. The centre's partners page names none.

Red flags: sanctions on the founder
- EU: Roman Trotsenko was added to the EU list in June 2024 in the 14th package; the Council describes him as one of the leading Russian entrepreneurs with assets in metallurgy, coal and energy and an adviser to Rosneft head Igor Sechin (Pravo.ru, 10 July 2026). A sanctions register mirror gives listing dates of 24 June 2024 and 16 March 2025. Pravo.ru says his lawyers filed suit against the Council at the EU General Court on 26 May; no ruling is reported. The Official Journal was not opened.
- UK: listed on 24 February 2025 as an "involved person" for owning or controlling LLC AEON Corporation and Novaport Holding LLC (UK list entry, via a sanctions register mirror). His son Gleb Trotsenko was listed the same day as an involved person through his father.
- US: Gleb Trotsenko was added to the OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list on 2 November 2023 under the Russia program EO 14024 (OFAC recent actions page). Panorama.am (3 November 2023) quotes the State Department's basis as operating in Russia's technology sector. No US listing was found for Roman Trotsenko.
- Sofya Trotsenko, who heads the foundation, appears on Ukraine's sanctions register and Canada's list, not on a US, EU or UK list, per the OpenSanctions aggregator.
- Connection: Roman Trotsenko founded and backed the centre. No reporting found of a sanction against the centre or the foundation.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. One news report, from outside the art press, covers a 2015 dispute.
1. The Moscow Times: Daria Litvinova, "Russian gallery owner evicted after hosting auction for political prisoners" (28 October 2015)
URL: https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2015/10/28/russian-gallery-owner-evicted-after-hosting-auction-for-political-prisoners-a50522
- Gallerist Marat Guelman was ordered out of his Winzavod space after hosting a charity auction for people jailed over the Bolotnaya protest.
- Winzavod's director Sofia Trotsenko told the pro-Kremlin Izvestia the contract "outlaws political activities". The landlord said Guelman owed a month's rent; he denied it.
- A news report, not art-press criticism.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Winzavod is private and comes from the Trotsenko family, whose founder is under EU and UK sanctions and whose son is under UK and US sanctions. Nothing is published about the centre's current budget, sponsors or ownership after 2014. Worth watching: the outcome of Trotsenko's EU court case and any sanctions notice naming the centre or its foundation.
