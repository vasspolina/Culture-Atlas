MUSEO DE LA REVOLUCIÓN: Havana, Cuba
ID: museo-de-la-revolucion
Website: http://www.cnpc.cult.cu/ (checked 2026-11-20; did not load)

OVERVIEW
- History museum in the former Presidential Palace at Refugio No. 1, between Avenida de las Misiones and Zulueta, Old Havana (EcuRed, state encyclopedia, archive copy of 24 March 2025).
- Created on 12 December 1959 by Decree No. 17 of the Revolutionary Armed Forces minister, Raúl Castro (EcuRed). Prensa Latina, the state agency, calls it a decree of the Ministry of the Revolutionary Armed Forces (MINFAR) signed by Castro as minister.
- It first sat in the Castillo de San Salvador de la Punta and the base of the José Martí monument, and opened in the palace on 4 January 1974 (EcuRed; Prensa Latina).
- Its first collection was material gathered by Celia Sánchez Manduley, a fighter of the 26th of July Movement (Prensa Latina; EcuRed).
- Building: begun in 1909 as the seat of Havana's provincial government, designed by Rodolfo Maruri and Paul Belau, built by the General Contracting Company, with interiors by Tiffany Studios. President Mario García Menocal took it for the national executive, and it opened as the Presidential Palace on 31 January 1920 (EcuRed; Prensa Latina).
- The Presidency and the Council of Ministers sat there from 1959 until 1965 (EcuRed).
- The Granma Memorial in the grounds, opened on 1 December 1976, holds the yacht Granma, a T-34 tank, two aircraft and the turbine of a US U-2 shot down on 27 October 1962 (EcuRed).
- Declared a National Monument by Resolution No. 01 of 13 March 2010 (EcuRed).
- Figures claimed by state sources: Prensa Latina, the state agency, says more than 30 rooms hold about nine thousand pieces; EcuRed, the state encyclopedia, says more than 300,000 people visit a year, nearly half from abroad.
- Visiting: EcuRed lists opening every day from 10:00 to 17:00.

FUNDING
Museo de la Revolución: state museum, founded by the armed forces ministry (MINFAR)
- An archive copy of 21 December 2013 shows its page on the website of the Consejo Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural, one of the Ministry of Culture's councils (CNPC; EcuRed).

WIDER CONTEXT
- MINFAR is listed under "Ministries" on the US State Department's Cuba Restricted List as first published in the Federal Register (document 2017-24449, effective 9 November 2017). The list says entities owned or controlled by a listed entity are not restricted unless named.
- The connection: MINFAR is the museum's founding ministry; the museum is not named on the list.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is Cuban state money and raises no funder flag in the atlas's terms. Worth watching: which body funds and directs it now.
