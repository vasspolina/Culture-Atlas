SKOWHEGAN SCHOOL OF PAINTING & SCULPTURE: Madison, Maine, USA
ID: skowhegan-school-of-painting-sculpture-skowhegan
Website: https://skowheganart.org

OVERVIEW
- Nine-week summer residency programme for emerging artists on a campus at 39 Art School Road, Madison, Maine, where participants live and work together with resident and visiting faculty artists; the Maine campus is generally not open to the public (Skowhegan).
- Founded in 1946 by the artists Willard "Bill" Cummings, Sidney Simon, Henry Varnum Poor and Charles Cutler, on the Cummings family farm (Skowhegan).
- Building and site: a former farm; the campus was extended under the 75th anniversary campaign with a sculpture shop, ceramics studio, dining hall, studios and staff cottages (Morning Sentinel, 2023).
- On site and in New York: the Maine campus has studios and workshops; a New York space at 136 West 22nd Street, opened in 2014, runs year-round public programmes, archives, publications and the library (Skowhegan).
- Run by co-directors Ruth Estévez and Katie Sonnenborn. A Board of Governors of past participants and faculty oversees the education programme; a Board of Trustees handles fundraising and finances (Skowhegan).
- Since 1952 it has recorded campus lectures, an archive of more than 700 (Wikipedia). Tuition for the nine-week summer programme is $6,000, with aid available (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Skowhegan School of Painting & Sculpture: privately funded: foundations, alumni and individual donors, an annual awards dinner and an endowment
- Form 990 data: expenses were $4,130,268 in 2023 and $3,852,099 in 2022; contributions were $1,767,591 in 2023 and $8,738,356 in 2022 (ProPublica).
- 75th anniversary capital campaign: raised more than $21 million, announced on 12 February 2023 and co-chaired by Eleanor Acquavella, Donald Moffett and Paula Volent (Morning Sentinel). Donors named: Paula J. Volent, the Looker Foundation, Greg and Susie Palm and the Palm Foundation, the Anna-Maria and Stephen Kellen Foundation ($2.5 million grant to the endowment), the Acquavella Family Foundation (sculpture shop), the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation (Frankenthaler Studio), the Pollock-Krasner Foundation (ceramics studio), the Marion Boulton "Kippy" Stroud Foundation (Stroud Cottage), Ann and Graham Gund (Gund Dining Hall) and Agnes Gund (the "Twin A's" staff cottage).
- Endowment: a challenge grant from the National Endowment for the Arts in the 1980s helped create it; the school's about page also names patrons Mildred Brinn, John Payson and the Brown Foundation, Inc. of Houston (Skowhegan).
- Annual giving: the school says it is supported by alumni, individuals and foundations; giving levels run from $400 (Alumni and Faculty Council) to $4,000 for an individual Benefactor (Skowhegan).
- Awards dinner: held since 1971 in New York; proceeds support the school, including need-blind admissions (Skowhegan).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Skowhegan is private: the 75th anniversary capital campaign raised more than $21 million, and the school says it is supported by alumni, individuals and foundations, with the awards dinner and endowment funds. The named funders are family and artist foundations and individuals.
