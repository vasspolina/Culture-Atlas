SOUTH LONDON GALLERY – London, UK
Website: https://www.southlondongallery.org (ok, checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Free contemporary art gallery at 65–67 and 82 Peckham Road, London SE5 8UH. The South London Fine Art Gallery opened on 4 May 1891; its origins go back to the South London Working Men's College and William Rossiter's 1879 art show. John Passmore Edwards funded a lecture hall and library (1893) and a Technical Institute (1898). Southwark Council took over responsibility in 1965, under independent trustees (Wikipedia).
- Director Margot Heller since 2001 (Wikipedia). The second site, a former fire station across the road, opened in 2018 (stated by the space).
- Programme: exhibitions, film, performance, education; Art Block, a free creative space on the Sceaux Gardens estate (galleriesnow).
- Open Wed 12–9pm, Thu–Sun 12–6pm; closed Mon–Tue; free to all (Art Fund, own site). No detailed access information was retrieved.
- Registered charity "South London Fine Art Gallery And Library", no. 312160: year to 31 March 2025, income £2,331,641, expenditure £2,616,954, 69 employees; the sole listed trustee is The SLG Trustee Limited (Open Charities).

FUNDING
South London Gallery: mixed public and private; Arts Council England and Southwark Council core, plus foundations, galleries and individual patrons.
- "Ongoing Gallery Support": Arts Council England, Southwark Council, The Bukhman Foundation, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Garfield Weston Foundation (stated by the space, supporters page, undated).
- Bukhman Foundation: funds Art Block for two years (staff, artist fees, materials); no amount stated (galleriesnow). The foundation is run by Anastasia and Igor Bukhman; the money comes from mobile games company Playrix, founded by Igor and Dmitry Bukhman, worth $18.2bn per Forbes. The family left Russia in 2014 and Playrix closed its Russian offices in 2022 (The Art Newspaper). Anastasia Bukhman also sits on SLG's International Council.
- Corporate partners: arcarta, Articheck, Artsy, Bloomberg, Crozier, El Rayo Tequila, Hallett Independent, Hollybush Gardens, Stockdale, Wedlake Bell (stated by the space).
- Commercial galleries give as benefactors and exhibition funders, including Hauser & Wirth, kurimanzutto, Marian Goodman, White Cube, David Zwirner, Pace, Sadie Coles HQ (stated by the space).
- Fire Station capital campaign: led by National Lottery Heritage Fund, Mayor of London and Arts Council England, with trusts and galleries (stated by the space).
- SLG has hosted the Bloomberg New Contemporaries show (2018, 2019 press releases).

Red flags: Sackler gift returned
- The Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation offered £125,000 in 2016 for an education centre in the £4m fire station project; trustees voted to return it in 2018 out of concern for the gallery's reputation (The Art Newspaper, 22 March 2019; Artforum). The record says "2018"; this is confirmed.

Red flags: Bloomberg Philanthropies campaign elsewhere
- On 28–29 May 2026, an open letter from 261 artists, published via Artists for Palestine UK (an openly pro-Palestinian campaign group), asked the National Theatre, RSC, Young Vic, Royal Court and Almeida to drop Bloomberg Philanthropies (Morning Star, a left-wing paper; The New Arab). The letter does not name SLG.
- Unverified: the letter alleges Bloomberg Philanthropies trains Israeli settlement officials through the Bloomberg-Sagol Centre at Tel Aviv University (claim by Artists for Palestine UK, an advocacy group).

BOTTOM LINE
SLG is a long-running public gallery funded by Arts Council England, Southwark Council, trusts and art-world patrons. It returned a Sackler gift in 2018. Worth watching: Bloomberg Philanthropies, which advocacy groups target over Israel links, though not at SLG.
