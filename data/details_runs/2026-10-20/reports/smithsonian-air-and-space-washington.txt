SMITHSONIAN NATIONAL AIR AND SPACE MUSEUM: Washington, USA
ID: smithsonian-air-and-space-washington
Website: https://airandspace.si.edu/ (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Aviation and spaceflight museum of the Smithsonian Institution, on the National Mall at 650 Jefferson Drive SW, Washington, with a second site, the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center, at Chantilly, Virginia (museum website).
- Formed as the National Air Museum by an act of Congress on 12 August 1946; the Mall building opened on 1 July 1976 (Wikipedia; museum website).
- The Mall building was designed by Gyo Obata of HOK, with Tennessee marble facades and steel-and-glass atria; the Udvar-Hazy Center opened at Dulles International Airport on 15 December 2003, funded by a private donation (Wikipedia).
- The Mall building has been renovated in phases since 2018, estimated at $360 million; the west end reopened on 14 October 2022 with eight new galleries and a planetarium (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds over 70,000 objects (museum website).
- Visiting: admission is free. The Washington museum is open daily 10:00 to 17:30 and requires free timed-entry passes; the Udvar-Hazy Center does not (museum website).

FUNDING
Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum: public and private: federal appropriations through the Smithsonian, plus corporate and individual donors
- The Smithsonian's annual budget is about $1.25 billion, roughly two-thirds from federal appropriations (Wikipedia, Smithsonian Institution).
- Building-transformation donors named on the museum's page include Jeff Bezos, Boeing, RTX, the Thomas W. Haas Foundation, the U.S. National Science Foundation, Kenneth C. Griffin, the Northrop Grumman Foundation, Textron, American Airlines, Delta Air Lines Foundation, United Airlines, Airbus, BAE Systems, Lockheed Martin and Amazon, among 118 names (museum website; no amounts or years).
- Boeing gave $30 million for the Boeing Milestones of Flight Hall, on top of earlier gifts totalling $58 million (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the museum's own operating budget.

Red flags: Arms and defence
- Defence contractors in the donor list: Lockheed Martin, RTX, Northrop Grumman, BAE Systems, General Dynamics (owner of Gulfstream, also listed), Boeing and L3Harris (owner of Aerojet Rocketdyne, also listed) rank among the largest arms producers, 1st to 10th, by 2024 revenue (Wikipedia, citing SIPRI). Each appears on the museum's donor page (museum website).
- Naming: the central hall is named for Boeing. By May 2003 the Smithsonian had agreed to name the museum theater for Lockheed Martin in return for $10 million toward the Dulles annex. Representative Norman Dicks called dropping the name of Samuel Langley, which the theater had carried since 1981, "incomprehensible"; the House Appropriations Committee asked the Regents to review donor agreements such as Lockheed's (Bruce Craig, History News Network, 2003).

Not related: the David H. Koch Hall of Human Origins is at the Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History, a different museum. Koch gave $15 million for that hall, and in 2015 fifteen nonprofits petitioned for his removal from its board (Inside Climate News). No Koch gift to the Air and Space Museum was found.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 1994 to 1995: veterans' groups and members of Congress criticised the museum's planned Enola Gay exhibit as unpatriotic; director Martin Harwit resigned on 2 May 1995 and the exhibit was scaled back (Wikipedia).
- August 2025: a White House letter to Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch launched a review of eight Smithsonian museums, citing Executive Order 14253 and saying exhibits should "celebrate American exceptionalism"; the Air and Space Museum was the first named (collectSPACE, 12 August 2025). The Smithsonian said its work is grounded in scholarly excellence and accurate history.
- Connection to the space: the museum is a Smithsonian unit and is directly named in the review.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a federal institution, so its core money is public, and its building campaign took money from many aerospace and defence companies, with no amounts published. The weak points are corporate naming (Boeing, Lockheed Martin) and the 2025 White House content review. Watch for amounts behind the donor list and the outcome of the review.
