DEUTSCHES HYGIENE-MUSEUM: Dresden, Germany
ID: deutsches-hygiene-museum
Website: https://www.dhmd.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the human being, health and the body, on a site between the Großer Garten and central Dresden (dhmd.de).
- Founded in 1912 at the initiative of Karl August Lingner, the Dresden maker of Odol mouthwash and chief organiser of the 1911 International Hygiene Exhibition, which drew over five million visitors (dhmd.de).
- Its building by Wilhelm Kreis opened in 1930 for the Second International Hygiene Exhibition, with the "Transparent Man" as its main attraction. Bombing in February 1945 destroyed much of the building and collection; Kulka and Partners finished a restoration of about seven years in 2006 (dhmd.de).
- From 1933 to 1937 numerous exhibitions of the museum spread Nazi racial ideology (dhmd.de). On 29 September 2026 seven Stolpersteine were laid outside for Jewish staff and board members forced out in 1933, among them Marta Fraenkel and the publisher and patron Julius Ferdinand Wollf, with a stone naming the museum's role as an instrument of racist policy (dhmd.de).
- In GDR times it was renamed the Deutsches Hygiene-Museum in der DDR; since 1991 it has been reconceived as a "Museum of The Human Experience", with a permanent exhibition, a children's museum, special exhibitions and a conference centre (dhmd.de).
- Iris Edenheiser has been director since 2022 and Lisa Klamka director of finance and administration since 2021. Nearly 250,000 people visited in 2025; the 2026 exhibition is "Wie geht's?" (DHMD activity report 2025).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10 to 17, closed Mondays. Adults EUR 12, reduced EUR 6 (students, asylum seekers, people with severe disabilities); free with the Dresden-Pass (dhmd.de).

FUNDING
Deutsches Hygiene-Museum: mainly public: the Free State of Saxony and the City of Dresden in equal shares
- Run by the Stiftung Deutsches Hygiene-Museum, a non-profit foundation under civil law, funded since 1999 in equal parts by the City of Dresden and the Free State of Saxony. The foundation pays its staff and maintains the listed building it owns (activity report 2025).
- City and state contributions rose modestly until 2021 and have not been adjusted since. Dresden's 2025/2026 double budget cut the foundation's money by 12% of its stagnant 2021 volume; the museum has cut 12% of its staff, thinned its special exhibitions, shortened opening hours and raised prices, and is drawing on reserves (activity report 2025).
- The programme itself runs only on third-party grants and its own income from tickets and conference centre rentals (activity report 2025).
- The board of trustees is chaired by Dresden mayor Annekatrin Klepsch, with Saxon state minister Barbara Klepsch as deputy, and includes a representative of the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (dhmd.de).
- Federal money in 2025: the Commissioner for Culture and the Media funded the exhibition "Freiheit. Eine unvollendete Geschichte", with the Karin und Uwe Hollweg Stiftung; the federal INK investment programme paid for the new studio "Bin ich schön!"; and the Kulturstiftung des Bundes funded "Luft. Eine für alle" and has supported the museum through its "Übermorgen" programme since summer 2025 (activity report 2025).
- Private backers: the Beisheim Stiftung and Commerzbank Stiftung fund "Wie geht's?"; the Klaus Tschira Stiftung and Charlotte Meentzen Kräutervital Kosmetik GmbH fund the permanent exhibition; the Ostdeutsche Sparkassenstiftung funds outreach in rural Saxony; and the Melitta Group, a family firm founded in Dresden, has long sponsored the children's museum and family programmes (activity report 2025).
- The Federal Agency for Civic Education is a long-standing partner of its debate programme, and an Erasmus+ project with the Pauls Stradiņš Medicine History Museum in Riga ended in spring 2025 (activity report 2025).

Red flags: Exhibition backer Beisheim Stiftung and the 2005 finding on Otto Beisheim's SS membership
- The museum's press kit for "Wie geht's?" credits the Beisheim Stiftung and gives its site, beisheim-stiftung.com, which belongs to the Prof. Otto Beisheim Stiftung of Munich (DHMD press kit, foundation site).
- Otto Beisheim was one of the owners of Metro AG. In late 2005, after he offered money to a grammar school in Rottach, Bavaria, students and teachers asked for an investigation; Bavaria's Culture Ministry then said he had been a stormtrooper and low-ranking SS member, with no indication that he took part in war crimes (JTA, 2006).
- Unverified: Beisheim's first company was partly financed with Nazi money, from the Dutch journalist Hans Knoop, citing statements by three SS members that are not public (JTA, 2006).

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly clean public money. The one name to know is the Beisheim foundation; the point to watch is whether shrinking city money leaves the programme more dependent on sponsors.
