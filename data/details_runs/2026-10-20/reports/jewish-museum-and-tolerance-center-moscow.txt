JEWISH MUSEUM AND TOLERANCE CENTER: Moscow, Russia
ID: jewish-museum-and-tolerance-center-moscow
Website: https://www.jewish-museum.ru/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and education centre at Obraztsova Street 11, building 1A, in the Maryina Roshcha district of Moscow (museum site, Wikipedia).
- Opened on 8 November 2012; the estimated cost of setting it up was $50 million (Wikipedia). Chabad helped develop and build it, with the support of the Russian government (JTA).
- Housed in the Bakhmetevsky Bus Garage, an 8,500 square metre building designed in 1926 by Konstantin Melnikov and Vladimir Shukhov; it reopened in 2008 as the Garage Centre for Contemporary Culture before the museum moved in (Wikipedia).
- On site: the museum, a Tolerance Center, a Children's Center and the Schneerson Family Library (Wikipedia).
- Alexander Boroda is listed as director in Wikipedia's infobox and as president of the Federation of Jewish Communities of Russia in JTA.
- Visiting: Sunday to Thursday noon to 22:00, Friday 10:00 to 15:00, closed Saturdays and Jewish holidays; ticket prices are not on the page.

FUNDING
Jewish Museum and Tolerance Center: oligarch-funded: founding donors, an endowment and Russian corporate partners
- Founding donors included Roman Abramovich, Viktor Vekselberg, the Federation of Jewish Communities of Russia and Chabad Lubavitch (Wikipedia). Vekselberg gave $4.5 million toward the roughly $50 million construction (Wikipedia, Viktor Vekselberg).
- Unverified: Putin donated one month of his salary toward construction, from Wikipedia, which cites a May 2014 Calvert Journal article by Alexis Zimberg that was not opened.
- In February 2014 the museum set up an endowment and raised $4.5 million in one evening at a gala dinner; co-founders of the fund were Viktor Vekselberg, Leonard Blavatnik, Roman Abramovich, Vadim Moshkovich, Alexey Likhtenfeld, Alexander Klyachin, Gennady Timchenko and Mikhail Gutseriev (Kommersant, Tatyana Markina, 6 February 2014).
- Kommersant does not say who manages the museum's endowment capital; it names Gazprombank Asset Management as manager of the Hermitage and most of the Peterhof endowments. No reporting found of who manages this museum's endowment.
- Current partners on the museum's undated partners page: VTB Bank, Sberbank, SAFMAR Charitable Foundation, Metalloinvest, Severstal, Dobrota Severa Charitable Foundation, Panasonic Rus and Moscow Transport. The Tolerance Center lists the Federal Agency for Ethnic Affairs and Russian state universities.
- No reporting found of Rosneft as a partner; it does not appear on the partners page.
- No reporting found of annual accounts.

Red flags: sanctioned board members and partners
- The 19-member board of trustees includes Viktor Vekselberg, Roman Abramovich, Leonard Blavatnik, Mikhail Fridman, Mikhail Gutseriev, Vadim Moshkovich, Andrey Kostin, Anton Vaino, Berel Lazar and Ronald S. Lauder (museum site).
- Vekselberg: the US Treasury sanctioned him and Renova Group in April 2018 under Executive Order 13662; the UK, Poland and Australia sanctioned him after the 2022 invasion. He chaired the board until February 2021, then became honorary chairman (Wikipedia).
- Kostin: head of VTB, a museum partner; the US sanctioned him in April 2018, the EU on 23 February 2022, and the US Department of Justice charged him with money laundering and sanctions violations on 22 February 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Vaino: Kremlin chief of staff since 2016; the EU listed him in February 2022 and OFAC on 6 April 2022 (Wikipedia).
- JTA reported in March 2022 that many Russian-Jewish oligarchs who funded Chabad were under sanctions.

Red flags: Putin government backing
- The museum was built with the support of the Russian government (JTA); the form of that support, a budget line or land, is not stated in the sources opened. A 2018 photo in Wikipedia shows Putin and Netanyahu at the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- After the 2022 invasion, Boroda told Interfax that the Federation was not pressured to support the war; Rabbi Misha Kapustin said Russian Chabad had not spoken out against the war in any significant way (JTA).
- Chief rabbi Berel Lazar, a board member, issued a statement on 2 March 2022 calling to "stop the madness so that no more people die" (JTA).
- The museum's director and its chief rabbi trustee sit inside this criticism of Russian Chabad.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private oligarch money plus a state-friendly corporate partner list, and no outside accounts exist. The weak points are a board that includes Vekselberg and Vaino, both on sanctions lists, and Kostin, head of partner VTB and also a trustee, under US and EU sanctions, and the open question of who manages the endowment. No report was found of the museum itself being sanctioned.
