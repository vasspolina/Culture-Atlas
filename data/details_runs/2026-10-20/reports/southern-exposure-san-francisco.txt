SOUTHERN EXPOSURE (SOEX): San Francisco, USA
ID: southern-exposure-san-francisco
Website: https://soex.org

OVERVIEW
- Artist-centred nonprofit gallery and arts organisation at 3030 20th Street in the Mission District of San Francisco, active in the Bay Area since 1974; its mission covers artist-centred support, experimentation, education and public engagement (Southern Exposure).
- Founded in 1974 at Project Artaud, San Francisco's oldest artist-owned live/work space, in a former American Can Company factory, as an artists' collective showing emerging artists as an alternative to commercial galleries (Southern Exposure history page).
- Earlier name: the American Can Collective Gallery; after a lawsuit over the name it became Southern Exposure in February 1976 (Southern Exposure history page).
- A separate board handled business matters from 1988, and it became an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit in 1999 (Southern Exposure history page).
- Locations: Project Artaud from 1974 to 2006, then two small Mission District storefronts and off-site public projects from 2006 to 2009, and the 20th Street building from October 2009 (Southern Exposure history page).
- Programmes: gallery exhibitions and open calls, the Alternative Exposure grant programme, Artists in Education, internships and volunteer opportunities (Southern Exposure history page). Free after-school and summer visual arts programmes for 50 to 65 young artists aged 12 to 21, each ending in a public exhibition at the Mission District gallery (California Arts Council).

FUNDING
Southern Exposure: mixed public and private: government arts grants, a Warhol Foundation partnership, an annual auction and individual donors
- California Arts Council: $20,250 for 2025-26 for the Arts and Youth programme (California Arts Council).
- Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts: Southern Exposure is its San Francisco partner for Alternative Exposure, which gives awards of up to $5,000 to artist-led projects; grantee announcements are listed for 2022 to 2025 (Warhol Foundation).
- National Endowment for the Arts: in March 2025 the organisation said NEA grants "may go away altogether" (Southern Exposure, "Southern Exposure Facing Serious Financial Challenges", 17 March 2025).
- In March 2025 the organisation said government and foundation funding was uncertain. It set a combined target of $400,000: a Short-term Stabilization Fund with $70,000 raised toward $125,000, for strategic planning, leadership support and six months of rent, and an annual auction, "New Suns: Resist and Rejoice", on 18 April with a $275,000 goal through sponsorships, ticket sales and auction sales (Southern Exposure).
- Individual members give from $5 a month (Southern Exposure).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Southern Exposure is grants from the California Arts Council ($20,250 for 2025-26), the Warhol Foundation's Alternative Exposure partnership and the National Endowment for the Arts, with an annual auction, a stabilization fund and individual members. In March 2025 it set a combined fundraising target of $400,000.
