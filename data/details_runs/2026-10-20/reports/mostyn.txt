MOSTYN: Llandudno, UK
ID: mostyn
Website: https://mostyn.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary gallery and visual arts centre at 12 Vaughan Street, Llandudno, North Wales, run by a registered charity (Mostyn Gallery Limited, charity 507842).
- Its roots lie in the Gwynedd Ladies' Art Society, founded in 1894, whose first donor and founding member was Lady Augusta Mostyn; Wikipedia dates the original gallery 1901 to 1913, Andrew Graham-Dixon gives 1902. It was re-established as Oriel Mostyn and officially opened on 11 August 1979, and was renamed Mostyn after its 2010 refurbishment (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Mostyn family, who built the original Edwardian gallery (Wikipedia).
- Building: a Grade II listed red-brick and terracotta building by the Mostyn Estate architect G. A. Humphreys. Ellis Williams Architects carried out the redevelopment, which reopened the gallery in May 2010 and cost just over £5 million (Wikipedia; Graham-Dixon). Before it was a public gallery the building was a drill hall, post office, piano and organ showroom and novelty shop (Graham-Dixon).
- On site: four major exhibition spaces, two smaller areas, a café, a shop (Mostyn Siop) and educational facilities (Wikipedia, Mostyn website).
- Visiting: free entry. Shop and gallery open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:30am to 4:30pm; café Tuesday to Saturday, 9am to 4pm. The site links an Accessibility page, whose contents were not read.

FUNDING
Mostyn: mainly public: Arts Council of Wales, with local councils, charitable trusts and shop income
- Arts Council of Wales: £385,130 in annual portfolio funding for core exhibition activity in the year to 31 March 2025 (accounts). It supplied about 60 per cent of funding in 2013, including £400,000 in 2011-12 (Museums Journal). It gave a £3 million grant towards the 2010 redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- Conwy County Borough Council appoints two officers as trustees, and Llandudno Town Council one; the accounts state no direct income amount from either. The 2024/25 accounts (machine-read text) list restricted funds of £74,814 for capital boilers (labelled SPF and ACW Capital) and £63,869 for learning and development (labelled SPF), with Conwy County Borough Council referenced in both descriptions.
- National Gallery: funded the Jeremy Deller project, for which Mostyn was the Welsh venue (accounts).
- Trusts in 2024/25 (machine-read accounts): Foyle Foundation £25,000 (towards elements of the Jeremy Deller project), Colwinston £20,000, Ashley Foundation £10,000. The support page also names the Garfield Weston Foundation as a major donor, and lists the Henry Moore Foundation, the Ampersand Foundation, the Colwinston Charitable Trust, the Foyle Foundation, the Ashley Family Foundation, the Chalcroft Charitable Trust, Mostyn Estates, Dr Carol Bell, Salisburys Chartered Accountants, Ellis Williams Architects, and Nia Roberts and John Nye; it gives no amounts or years.
- Shop income £120,354 and £50,481 in museums and galleries exhibition tax relief in 2024/25 (accounts).
- Total income: £1,054,295 in the year to 31 March 2025, and £666,440 in the year to 31 March 2026 against spending of £942,458 (OpenCharities).
- Trustees (OpenCharities): Jeremy Salisbury (chair), Adam Francis Smythe, David James Lloyd, E Williams, Nick Clarke, Vanessa Jane Griffiths. The 2025 accounts name Clare Harding, also a trustee, as interim director.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage found is admiring. No criticism of the institution found in art media.
1. Sunday Telegraph: Andrew Graham-Dixon, "The new Mostyn in Llandudno" (30 May 2010)
URL: https://andrewgrahamdixon.com/archive/the-new-mostyn-in-llandudno.html
- Praises the redevelopment as "carried out with flair and sensitivity."
- Traces the gallery to the Gwynedd Ladies' Art Society and a protest at the Royal Cambrian Academy's refusal to admit women.
- Read as an archive excerpt; the full article is behind a login, so only excerpts were read.
2. Museums Journal: Simon Stephens, "Making waves" (2 September 2013)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/features/2013/09/01092013-making-waves/
- A favourable profile, not criticism. Director Alfredo Cramerotti says the gallery draws about 80,000 visitors a year in a town of 18,000, which he links to "the kind of challenges we are facing."

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public grants, mainly from the Arts Council of Wales, with named UK trusts and shop income. No flagged funder appears in the sources read. The weak points are a fall in income to £666,440 in the year to March 2026 against spending of £942,458, and the lack of amounts on the donor page. Worth watching: the next accounts and the director's post, which the 2025 accounts list as interim.
