THE ROOMS: St. John's, Canada
ID: the-rooms-st-johns
Website: https://www.therooms.ca/ (checked 2026-10-24; the admission page opened; /about and /visit returned "not found")

OVERVIEW
- Provincial museum, art gallery and archives in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, with three regional museums, including the Labrador Interpretation Centre in North West River (annual report 2024-25).
- Opened on 29 June 2005, combining the Art Gallery of Newfoundland and Labrador, the Provincial Archives and the Provincial Museum (Wikipedia). It is a Category 1 Crown Corporation of the provincial government (annual report).
- Built on the site of Fort Townshend, an 18th-century fort designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1951; archaeologists and historians opposed the location (Wikipedia).
- Galleries, archives, a cafe and school programmes; 150,324 visitors in 2024-25 (annual report).
- Admission $13.80 adults, $8.97 seniors 60+, students and Canadian military, $6.90 youth 6 to 17, free for children 5 and under and for Indigenous Peoples; free 5 to 9pm on the first Wednesday of each month. Hours and accessibility were not on the pages opened.

FUNDING
The Rooms: mainly public: provincial government, with federal grants, corporate sponsors and earned income
- Annual report 2024-25 (year to 31 March 2025): total revenue $11,375,873 and expenses $11,630,927, a deficit of $255,054. Provincial operating grants were $7,362,409 and a $239,971 capital grant. Commercial operations earned $2,191,649. The province committed $1M a year for five years for capital upgrades from 2024-25.
- Federal: the Canada Council for the Arts gave $250,000 in 2024-25 under a grant first awarded in April 2019; Young Canada Works gave $37,757.
- Corporate sponsorship was $347,367 in 2024-25: Chevron Canada $185,000, BMO $100,000 and Cenovus Energy $20,000. BMO's $100,000 was the final instalment of a $1M ten-year pledge.
- Chevron Open Minds: on 16 December 2008 the province announced a Chevron Canada agreement of more than $375,000 over three years (Government of Newfoundland and Labrador release). The 2024-25 report says Chevron's $185,000 completed its commitment and the programme would conclude "this coming June" (June 2025), continuing as Curious Minds.

Red flags: oil and gas sponsors of school programmes
- Chevron Canada: $185,000 in 2024-25, completing its funding of the school programme, which has run since 2009 (annual report 2024-25, Sponsors page).
- Cenovus Energy: $20,000 in 2024-25 for the Celebrating Diversity Through Identity Edukit (annual report).
- In March 2025 the Rooms presented a proposal to BP Canada Energy Group asking $30,000 for workshops in Curious Minds; the report does not say whether BP agreed (annual report).
- No reporting found that the Chevron sponsorship ended under pressure; the report gives no reason.

Red flags: Innu exhibit dispute with the provincial government
- The Innu Nation cancelled the Innu Pakassiun exhibit in the week before 26 June 2026; it was due to open on National Indigenous Peoples Day at the Labrador Interpretation Centre in North West River (Canadian Press, 26 June 2026; The Independent, 30 June 2026).
- Jodie Ashini, a cultural guardian with the Innu Nation, said executives at The Rooms relayed a government request to leave out an Innu timeline and not link the exhibit's stone tools to the Innu; the Innu cancelled rather than change it (Canadian Press). The Independent reports she said Rooms executives told her to change the timeline or move the exhibit.
- Premier Tony Wakeham, after meeting the Innu Nation, said in a statement that he apologised for matters related to the exhibit and committed to free Indigenous cultural expression (Canadian Press).
- Unverified: that the provincial government asked for the changes, from the Innu Nation, a party to the dispute. Neither report carries a response from The Rooms.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found. The criticism is in news reporting on the 2026 Innu exhibit.
1. The Canadian Press: "Archeologists accuse N.L. government of 'colonial logic' in dispute with Innu" (2026)
URL: https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/06/26/archeologists-accuse-nl-government-of-colonial-logic-in-dispute-with-innu/
- Seven Memorial University archaeologists wrote that the province's position on Innu history uses "colonialist logic"; the Innu Nation says the province promoted a theory that Innu arrived in Labrador about 300 years ago.
- Reports no response from The Rooms.
2. The Independent: Heidi Atter, article on the Provincial Archaeology Office's theory (30 June 2026)
URL: https://theindependent.ca/news/inside-the-provincial-archaeology-offices-theory-that-europeans-inhabited-labrador-before-innu/
- Innu Grand Chief Simon Pokue said the position "amounts to the erasure of Innu history". Quotes no statement from The Rooms.

BOTTOM LINE
The Rooms is mostly provincial money, plus a small share of oil-company sponsorship: Chevron through June 2025, and Cenovus, with a BP Canada request. The weak points are those school-programme sponsors and the Innu Nation's cancellation of its exhibit in June 2026 after a dispute over its timeline. Watch the Curious Minds funding and the outcome of the Innu talks.
