ID: meadows-museum

WIDER CONTEXT
- 22 October 2009: the Monuments Men Foundation announced that two Murillo paintings on display at the Meadows Museum, of Seville's patron saints Justa and Rufina, "estimated to be worth more than $10 million, are believed to have been stolen from the Rothschild family in Paris in 1941"; it said the Nazi ERR code evidencing Rothschild ownership was still visible on the stretcher bar of one painting (Monuments Men Foundation press release, reproduced by the Center for Art Law, 27 October 2009).
- The Foundation said it was continuing its research "to document conclusively whether both paintings were properly restituted to the rightful owners" before they reached the museum (Center for Art Law).
- 7 October 2016: the museum announced that research by its curator Nicole Atzbach had produced "definitive evidence" that both paintings "had in fact been lawfully restituted to the Rothschild family following the war, long before the 1972 sale to the Museum"; it said Saint Justa was returned to Baronne Antoinette Léonino on 7 May 1946 and that corroborating evidence "indicates that Saint Rufina must also have been returned to Léonino prior to December 1947" (Meadows Museum press release, 7 October 2016).
