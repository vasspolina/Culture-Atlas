QATAR MUSEUMS: Doha, Qatar
ID: qatar-museums-doha
Website: https://qm.org.qa/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National museum organisation of Qatar that runs the Museum of Islamic Art and MIA Park, Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art, the National Museum of Qatar, the 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum and two QM galleries, Al Riwaq and Katara.
- Founded in 2005 as the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia); it operates under the patronage of the Amir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani.
- Led by chairperson Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani; the chief executive is Mohammed Al Rumaihi (Wikipedia).
- Venues opened: the Museum of Islamic Art in 2008, Mathaf in 2010, the National Museum of Qatar in 2019 and the Olympic and Sports Museum in 2022 (Wikipedia). Planned: Dadu, Children's Museum of Qatar; the Art Mill Museum (due 2030); the Lusail Museum.
- Visiting: the three main museums are open about 9 am to 7 pm, to 9 pm on Thursday, from 1:30 pm on Friday, each closed one weekday (National Museum Tuesday, Museum of Islamic Art Wednesday, Mathaf Monday). Ticket prices were not on the pages opened. The National Museum has tactile information panels.

FUNDING
Qatar Museums: state-linked: national museum body under the Amir's patronage, with oil company sponsors for new projects
- No funding statement or accounts are posted on the Qatar Museums site, which mentions only a donor contributions programme.
- Dadu is described as Qatar's first publicly funded national museum (Gulf Times, 2022).
- ExxonMobil Qatar became a founding family sponsor of Dadu in December 2021, exclusive sponsor of its Sustainability and Valuing the Environment Gallery; the amount was not disclosed (Gulf Times, 1 December 2021).
- Qatar Shell became a founding family sponsor in April 2022, exclusive sponsor of Dadu's STEM Gallery, under a long-term agreement; the amount was not disclosed (Gulf Times, 18 April 2022).
- No reporting found of annual budget figures in the sources opened.

Red flags: Gulf state body, Qatar government entity
- Qatar Museums is the state's museum body, chaired by Sheikha Al Mayassa and under the Amir's patronage (Qatar Museums site).
- A 2013 openDemocracy essay noted social media criticism of the Qatar Museums Authority for alleged corruption, mismanagement and nepotism (Sarah El-Richani); this is an allegation by commentators, not a finding.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Dadu sponsors
- ExxonMobil Qatar (December 2021) and Qatar Shell (April 2022) are named sponsors of Dadu (Gulf Times).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism of Qatar Museums; the art press covers Qatar's state role in art, mostly in passing, and Mayassa's purchasing power.
1. e-flux Criticism: Jörg Heiser, "On Art Basel Qatar" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782392/on-art-basel-qatar
- Argues Gulf states treat art as state investment to diversify from oil and gas, "beyond mere artwashing".
- Links the fair's artist selection to restrictions on queer and nude work, since LGBTQ+ people remain criminalised in Qatar; says Sheikha Al-Mayassa's purchasing power and backing keep stand fees low.
- Mentions migrant construction crews only in passing.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Qatar opens slavery museum" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/11/26/qatar-opens-slavery-museum
- Reports that human rights organisations have criticised poor labour conditions of migrant workers building stadiums and museums before the 2022 World Cup.
- Does not name Qatar Museums and carries no reply from it.
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Says social media reports claimed Sheikha Mayassa intervened to keep Adel Abdessemed's film "Printemps" from showing at Mathaf; officials cited technical issues.
- Argues the Qatar Museums Authority should do more to support local and Arab artists and justify its mandate, and says debates have focused on how public money is spent.
- General-interest outlet, not an art-press title.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Human Rights Watch's 2026 World Report on Qatar documents wage theft, a monthly minimum wage of QAR 1,000 (about US$274) unrevised since 2021, a heat threshold it calls too high, and deaths of migrant workers linked to the 2022 World Cup that were neither investigated nor compensated. It does not mention Qatar Museums.
- Connection to the space: the Art Newspaper (2015) reports rights groups criticising conditions for workers building Qatar's museums; no reporting found that names a Qatar Museums site or contractor.

BOTTOM LINE
Qatar Museums is the Qatari state's museum body, which makes it a Gulf state body, with oil company sponsors ExxonMobil Qatar and Qatar Shell on its new children's museum. The weak points are the absence of any published accounts and the country-level labour record. Worth watching: sponsor amounts for Dadu and the Art Mill and Lusail projects.
