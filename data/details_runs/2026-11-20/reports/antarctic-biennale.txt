ANTARCTIC BIENNALE: Antarctic Peninsula, Antarctica
ID: antarctic-biennale
Website: https://antarcticbiennale.com (checked 2026-11-20; serves gambling spam)

OVERVIEW
- A shipborne art expedition to the Antarctic Peninsula: the research vessel Akademik Sergey Vavilov sailed from Ushuaia on 17 March 2017 for a 12-day voyage, with talks, performances and screenings on board and works installed at landings (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- Proposed in 2011 by the Russian artist Alexander Ponomarev, its commissioner (Biennial Foundation), whom The Art Newspaper called a former submariner; its first phase was the exhibition "Antarctopia" in the Antarctic Pavilion at the 2014 Venice Architecture Biennale (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- The curator Nadim Samman co-organised it (The Art Newspaper, 2016). An open call for artists under 35 drew more than 500 applications from 59 countries; the winners Sho Hasegawa and Gustav Düsing joined the voyage (Biennial Foundation).
- Artists on board included Tomás Saraceno, Julian Charrière, Yto Barrada, Lara Favaretto, Zhang Enli and Abdullah Al Saadi; the advisory board included Hans Ulrich Obrist, Sam Keller and Sheikha Hoor Al-Qasimi (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- At a landing on Cuverville Island, Joaquín Fargas laid reflective silver sheets over rocks and Düsing tried to freeze a cotton tent (The Art Newspaper, Adrian Dannatt's dispatch, 12 Apr 2017).
- UNESCO granted the project its patronage on 24 January 2017 (Biennial Foundation).
- Samman wrote in 2015: "Ours is a topsy-turvy biennale, so perhaps we will only go once" (The Art Newspaper, 2016).

FUNDING
Antarctic Biennale: privately sponsored: mainly Eugene Kaspersky, with the Oceanic Project Foundation
- Cost and sponsors: the voyage cost "well over a million dollars"; most of it came from Eugene Kaspersky, head of the privately owned cyber security firm Kaspersky Lab, and the collector Qiao Zhibing and his partner Lihsin Tsai also contributed. The author of that account, Anna Somers Cocks, declared in the same article that she chaired the biennale's board of trustees (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- Kaspersky Lab wrote in March 2017: "We are sponsoring the Antarctic Biennale"; Eugene Kaspersky sailed with the expedition, and the company said its mission inspired Fargas's snow-compressing robot, the Glaciator (Kaspersky blog, 23 Mar 2017).
- Main backer: the Oceanic Project Foundation, whose trustees included Marina Abramovic, Sam Keller of the Fondation Beyeler, museum directors and Russian donors, chaired by Somers Cocks, then chief executive of The Art Newspaper (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- BMW Group was a partner of the open call; Thomas Girst, head of BMW Cultural Engagement, introduced the project to the press at Art Basel Miami Beach, where the winners were announced on 1 December 2016 (Biennial Foundation).
- The US ambassador John Blaffer Royall persuaded Erik Owen Arneberg, chairman of the Robert Lee Blaffer Foundation, to put money into a documentary of the voyage directed by Denis Delestrac (The Art Newspaper, 17 Mar 2017).
- The 2014 Antarctic Pavilion in Venice had the support of the AVC Charity Foundation (Biennial Foundation).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 20 June 2024 the US Commerce Department barred Kaspersky Lab, Inc. and its affiliates, subsidiaries and parent companies from providing anti-virus and cyber security products or services in the United States. It also added AO Kaspersky Lab, OOO Kaspersky Group and Kaspersky Labs Limited to its Entity List, an export control list, for "cooperation with Russian military and intelligence authorities" (Bureau of Industry and Security).
- On 21 June 2024 the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control designated twelve executives and senior leaders of AO Kaspersky Lab, and stated that it had not designated Kaspersky Lab, its parent or subsidiary companies, or its CEO (US Treasury).
- Eugene Kaspersky paid for most of the 2017 voyage; the US measures came seven years later and do not name him.

BOTTOM LINE
A one-off private voyage with no running funding to judge. Its weak points are that the account of who paid comes from inside the project, and that the main sponsor's company has since come under US trade restrictions.
