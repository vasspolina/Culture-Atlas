KUNSTHALLE BERN: Bern, Switzerland
ID: kunsthalle-bern-bern
Website: https://kunsthallebern.ch/de/support

OVERVIEW
- Institution for contemporary art at Helvetiaplatz 1 in Bern, reached by tram 6, 7 or 8 or bus 19 from Bern railway station to Helvetiaplatz.
- Founded in 1912 as an association of Bern artists, the Verein Kunsthalle Bern, and opened in 1918; the Berner Tagwacht announced the founding on 23 October 1918 (stated by the Kunsthalle).
- Building: designed by Klauser & Streit, built 1917 to 1918 (Wikipedia). The association sold share certificates from 1912 to finance the building at Helvetiaplatz 1 and has issued new personalised certificates by the artist Maria Eichhorn since 2001, in values of CHF 50, 100 and 500.
- Directors include Harald Szeemann (1961 to 1969), Valérie Knoll (2015 to 2022) and iLiana Fokianaki (since April 2024) (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions, public programmes, an archive, editions and publications, and the Kunsthalle Bar.
- Association board (Vorstand): Lorenza Donati and Berit Seidel (presidency), Markus Gysi (treasurer) and seven further members.
- Visiting: September to May Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, Thursday until 20:00; June to August Tuesday to Saturday 12:00 to 21:00, Sunday 12:00 to 18:00; closed Monday. Admission CHF 10, reduced CHF 6, under 16 free, free for everyone on the first Sunday of the month; members and holders of the Swiss Museum Pass or a Raiffeisen card enter free.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Bern: City of Bern service contract of CHF 1,050,000 a year, with members, patrons, foundations and a patrons' fund
- City of Bern: a service contract (Leistungsvertrag) between the City of Bern and the Verein Kunsthalle Bern runs from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2027 with an annual operating contribution of CHF 1,050,000, not adjusted for inflation during the term. The contribution includes part of the cost of maintaining the building (City of Bern contract).
- Members: from CHF 30 (artists and students), CHF 80 single and CHF 120 couple or family per calendar year in 2024; patron memberships from CHF 500 for individuals and from CHF 1,000 for legal entities in 2024 (annual report 2024).
- Berner Kunstfonds: founded in 1993 by the Verein der Freunde Kunstmuseum Bern, the Verein Kunsthalle Bern and the Bernische Kunstgesellschaft to coordinate private patrons; the 2024 annual report prints a list of named supporters beside the fund's entry (status autumn 2024) that includes BEKB, Die Mobiliar, Helvetia Schweiz AG, Bauart Architekten und Planer AG and private individuals.
- The 2024 annual report's thanks list names Kultur Stadt Bern, Pro Helvetia, the Landis & Gyr Stiftung, the Stiftung Erna und Curt Burgauer, the Ernst Göhner Stiftung, the Ernst und Olga Gubler-Hablützel Stiftung, the Südkulturfonds, the Ursula Wirz-Stiftung, the Stiftung Kunsthalle Bern, philaneo e.V., the Burgergemeinde Bern and the Bernische Kunstgesellschaft, alongside suppliers and partner institutions.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Question 2023.SR.0244: two city councillors of the SVP group, Alexander Feuz and Thomas Glauser, filed a "Kleine Anfrage" in the Bern city council on 23 November 2023 about the "controversial Palestine letter" of the newly elected director of the Kunsthalle (title translated). It asked why the Gemeinderat did not follow the example of the Basel-Stadt government and condemn the approach, why it did not react to the director's letter, whether it would now demand a condemnation of Hamas, and what it would do concretely about the future director and the head of culture. It stated that the letter accused Israel of genocide.
- The Gemeinderat answered on 10 January 2024 that iLiana Fokianaki signed the open letter as a private person, together with 8,000 others, some weeks before the board named her director from April 2024; that the letter's authors issued a statement four days later condemning the Hamas attack; and that the Kunsthalle board and Fokianaki condemned violence against people in a public statement on 17 November 2023. It condemned racism and antisemitism in all forms and saw no need for further measures.
- Connection: the question was filed by a party group in the council of the city that pays the Kunsthalle's operating contribution; the answer set out no measure against the Kunsthalle or its director.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kunsthalle is the City of Bern's CHF 1,050,000 a year under a contract running to the end of 2027, together with members, the Berner Kunstfonds, Swiss foundations and Pro Helvetia. The association publishes annual reports from 2016 to 2025, its statutes and its board.
