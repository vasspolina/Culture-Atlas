DOUAL'ART: Douala, Cameroon
ID: doualart-douala
Website: http://www.doualart.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre and experimental laboratory for new urban practices in African cities, at Place du Gouvernement in Douala (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1991 by Marilyn Douala Bell and Didier Schaub, and registered as a non-profit in 1992; Douala Bell is president, and Schaub was artistic director until his death in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- Its exhibition space, Espace doual'art, opened in 1995 in Bonanjo, in the former cinema of the Palace of Douala Kings, a building owned by the Douala Bell family (Wikipedia).
- It shows an average of 8 exhibitions a year and has produced more than 60 public art projects and events, temporary and permanent (stated by the space). Its permanent staff is 5 people (stated by the space).
- Public art includes La Nouvelle Liberté by Joseph-Francis Sumégné, commissioned by doual'art in 1996 and now a symbol of Douala (Érika Nimis, Ciel variable 101, 2015).
- It organises the SUD Salon Urbain de Douala, a triennial public art festival held in December in 2007, 2010, 2013 and 2017 (Wikipedia; stated by the space).

FUNDING
doual'art: grant-funded: international foundations and agencies
- The site footer, under "Support of", names Arts Collaboratory, Brot für die Welt, ZFD Ziviler Friedensdienst, CPS Peace Work Africa and SUPSI LCV, with no year or amount (stated by the space).
- Wikipedia says the organisation has been supported by ICU Art Projects (since 2010), Enough Room for Space, the Prince Claus Fund, the Mondriaan Foundation, Hivos and Arts Collaboratory (Doen Foundation).
- Wikipedia's SUD Salon Urbain de Douala page names Arts Collaboratory, the Mondriaan Foundation and the Prince Claus Fund as supporters of SUD 2007, and AECID (Madrid), Arts Collaboratory (The Hague), EED (Bonn), Culturesfrance (Paris), the Foundation for Arts Initiatives (New York) and the Cameroonian Ministry of Culture (Yaoundé) as supporters of SUD 2010.
- The Rotterdam-based iStrike Foundation, set up in late 2005 as doual'art's European office, helped launch SUD and its first edition (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind doual'art is grants from international foundations and agencies. Its site footer names Arts Collaboratory, Brot für die Welt, ZFD Ziviler Friedensdienst, CPS Peace Work Africa and SUPSI LCV; Wikipedia adds the Prince Claus Fund, the Mondriaan Foundation and Hivos, and lists the Cameroonian Ministry of Culture as a SUD 2010 supporter. Wikipedia says the Espace doual'art building is owned by the Douala Bell family.
