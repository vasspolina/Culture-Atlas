MUSEO DE LA CIUDAD DE MÉXICO: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-de-la-ciudad-de-méxico-mexico-city
Website: https://www.cultura.cdmx.gob.mx/recintos/mcm (checked 2026-11-20; down)

OVERVIEW
- City museum at Pino Suárez 30 in the Centro Histórico, a few blocks south of the Zócalo, in the former palace of the Counts of Santiago de Calimaya (Mexico City tourism site; Time Out Mexico).
- The site was one of the plots near the Templo Mayor that Hernán Cortés shared out, granted to Juan Gutiérrez Altamirano; Francisco Antonio de Guerrero y Torres rebuilt the palace in the 18th century, and it was declared national heritage in 1931 (Excélsior, 23 August 2013).
- From the late 19th century the heirs let the rooms of the second patio, keeping the upper floor for the Cervantes family, the last owners (Excélsior, 2013); shops took the ground floor and the house became a vecindad (Chilango).
- After a remodelling by Pedro Ramírez Vázquez the museum opened on 31 October 1964 (Excélsior, 2013).
- On site: Joaquín Clausell's studio, whose walls he used as canvases, and a site museum on the building (Time Out Mexico). The director puts the collection at about 1,500 pieces, among them paintings, sculptures, prints and documents (Milenio, 2026).
- Also on site: a 1923 cargo truck and viceregal carriages, the Biblioteca Jaime Torres Bodet and a Fondo de Cultura Económica bookshop, with concerts, dance, theatre, workshops and talks (Chilango, Montse Quintana, 21 February 2023).
- Lizbeth Ramírez Chávez, former director of the Museo Archivo de la Fotografía, took over as director on 1 June 2026 at the invitation of the city culture secretary, Ana Francis López Bayghen Patiño (Vocero; Milenio). Her first show, "Sinfonía de colores", presents instruments decorated with Wixárika beadwork from the collection of Hermes Music, and she plans a permanent room on the history of the building (Milenio, 3 July 2026).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00; general admission 38 pesos (Chilango, February 2023).

FUNDING
Museo de la Ciudad de México: public: Mexico City government through its Secretaría de Cultura
- The city's Secretaría de Cultura runs the museum; it announced the change of director on 1 June 2026 (Vocero).
- The Distrito Federal government decreed the building the seat of the city museum in 1960 (Excélsior, 2013) and acquired it that year (Mexico City tourism site).

Red flags: 2025 protest and lawsuit against Fabián Cháirez's exhibition
- Cháirez's "La segunda venida del Señor", nine oil paintings made between 2018 and 2023, opened at the museum on 2 April 2025 for a run to 30 April, after its removal from UNAM's Academia de San Carlos (Vanguardia; Chilango).
- At the reopening, culture secretary Ana Francis Mor said "no podemos permitir la censura nunca" (we can never allow censorship; translation) (El Financiero, 17 April 2025).
- Seven people protested outside the museum with the slogan "¡No ofendas mi fe, en nombre del arte!" (Vanguardia, Mauro Marines, 17 April 2025). El Financiero reported that Catholic groups called the show "cristianofóbica" and that protests had returned.
- Religious groups filed an amparo suit on 15 April 2025 saying the show violated freedom of worship; the Juzgado Sexto de Distrito en Materia Administrativa granted a provisional suspension, notified on 15 April (Vanguardia).
- The judge then ordered a definitive suspension to be obeyed immediately, and the museum removed the exhibition (UnoTV, 29 April 2025; La Razón, Julio Vázquez, 29 April 2025). UnoTV called the plaintiffs "un reducido grupo de ciudadanos" (a small group of citizens; translation).
- The city Secretaría de Cultura reaffirmed its commitment to artistic freedom (Vanguardia), said it was weighing legal action (UnoTV) and called the ban a "retroceso" (setback; translation) in building a plural and democratic society (La Razón).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2025 the same series was withdrawn from UNAM's Academia de San Carlos after more than 30 amparos brought by the Asociación de Abogados Cristianos (Vanguardia). Judge Francisco Javier Rebolledo Peña had ordered UNAM's rector to suspend the show, then declared the case moot once the academy closed it (Milenio).
- That dispute concerned a different institution; it brought the show to the Museo de la Ciudad.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on Mexico City public money, which raises no flag. Its weak point is exposure to court challenges from religious groups, not its funding.
