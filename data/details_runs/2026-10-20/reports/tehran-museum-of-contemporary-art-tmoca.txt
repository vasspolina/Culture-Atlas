TEHRAN MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART (TMOCA): Tehran, Iran
ID: tehran-museum-of-contemporary-art-tmoca
Website: https://www.tmoca.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads, in Persian; no funder, board or account pages were found on it)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art next to Laleh Park in Tehran (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 14 October 1977 (22 Mehr 1356) under Empress Farah Pahlavi, whose office oversaw art selection, funded by the National Iranian Oil Company (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Kamran Diba with Nader Ardalan, mostly underground, with a spiral walkway and branching galleries (Wikipedia).
- Holds more than 3,000 items: 19th and 20th century Western paintings, prints, drawings and sculpture, and a major collection of Iranian modern and contemporary art (Wikipedia).
- After the 1979 revolution the Western works were kept in the vault; the first post-revolution exhibition of them was in 1999, and most are rarely shown (Wikipedia).
- Closed for renovation in spring 2018 and reopened in early 2021 (Persian Wikipedia). A later show, "Eye to Eye", displayed over 120 Western works, with tickets costing about 14 US cents (Associated Press).
- Head of the museum since April-May 2025: Reza Dabiri-Nejad (Persian Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art: state-owned and state-funded: Iran's Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance
- The museum is a unit of the Deputy for Arts Affairs of the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance (Persian Wikipedia).
- The ministry allocated funding for the renovation in 1396 (2017-18). The official cost of the whole renovation project was 16.5 billion toman; in late 2018 officials said 5 billion had been spent and 3 billion more was needed (Etemad Online, January 2021).
- No reporting found of any private, corporate or foreign funder, of published accounts, or of a board.

Red flags: Sanctions
- The Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance, the museum's owner, is on the US Specially Designated Nationals list: OFAC's recent-actions page of 8 November 2012 lists it under the IRAN-TRA program tag, and the Federal Register notice of 16 November 2012 ties the designations to Executive Order 13628 (OFAC, Federal Register).

Red flags: State censorship
- On 27 December 2016 the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation, for the Berlin State Museums, cancelled a show of 61 works from the collection because Iran had not granted export permits; the show had been due to open on 4 December (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, The Art Newspaper).
- In February 2017 Culture Minister Reza Salehi-Amiri said he had proposed cancelling the Berlin and Rome loan show pending review, citing legal ambiguity in the agreement. The works were shown in Tehran in March 2017 instead (Tehran Times, 8 March 2017).
- Iranian newspapers speculated the works would not return if they left Iran, and some officials who disapprove of art questioned the value of the show (The Art Newspaper).
- Associated Press says Shiite clerics packed the museum's Western art into its vault after 1979, and that a Warhol print of the empress was slashed during the revolution.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself; coverage centres on the 2016 cancellation of the Berlin loan show.
1. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Berlin cancels show of Tehran's Modern art" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/12/28/berlin-cancels-show-of-tehrans-modern-art
- Iranian authorities missed the German deadline for export permits for works by Picasso, Pollock, Rothko and Bacon; Hermann Parzinger said further delays "couldn't be justified".
- Treats the cancellation as a setback for German-Iranian relations after the 2015 nuclear deal.
2. Associated Press (news agency, not art press): "Iran's much long hidden collection of Western art re-emerges despite high tensions with US" (undated copy on Ynetnews)
URL: https://www.ynetnews.com/article/sysf88jm1e
- Describes the vaulted Western collection and the "Eye to Eye" show; quotes curator Jamal Arabzadeh and visitors; no official is quoted.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind TMoCA is the Iranian state: the museum belongs to a ministry that OFAC listed on 8 November 2012. The documented weak point is state control of the collection, shown by the vaulting after 1979 and the 2016 to 2017 loan-show cancellation. Worth watching: how the Western works are shown and whether any foreign loan is allowed again.
