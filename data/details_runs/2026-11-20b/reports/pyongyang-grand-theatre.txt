PYONGYANG GRAND THEATRE: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: pyongyang-grand-theatre
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Theatre at the junction of Yonggwang and Sungri streets in central Pyongyang, about 1 km south of Kim Il Sung Square and directly opposite the Pyongyang Hotel (Koryo Tours). The North Korean website Korean Art places it in Othan-dong, Central District.
- Building began in February 1959 and finished on 13 August 1960; the site covers 75,000 square metres, with about 29,000 square metres of floor space over three floors and 2,190 seats at opening (Korean Wikipedia, citing a 2011 Korea Culture and Tourism Institute study).
- Koryo Tours calls it the first of the major Korean-style projects completed in rebuilt Pyongyang, after years of Soviet-inspired buildings; Korean Wikipedia, citing the architecture professor Ahn Chang-mo, calls it an early work in North Korea's experiments with national building forms from the 1960s.
- It is the main stage of the Pibada (Sea of Blood) Opera Troupe and stages the five "revolutionary operas", among them "The Sea of Blood" and "The Flower Girl" (Korean Wikipedia; Korean Art).
- "The Sea of Blood" premiered there on 17 July 1971 with Kim Il Sung in attendance (English Wikipedia, "Sea of Blood").
- Rebuilt from June 2008 and reopened on 3 April 2009, with new roof tiles, a ceiling showing the eight fairies of Mount Kumgang and 5.1-channel sound; the 26th April Spring Friendship Art Festival opened there on 15 April 2009. North Korean media reported about 1,300 seats afterwards (Korean Wikipedia).
- Korean Art lists a 1,262-seat auditorium, a 600-seat conference hall, a rotating stage, more than a hundred creation rooms, a polyclinic, a nursery school, a restaurant and a shop, and says the theatre hosts political and cultural events as well as performances.
- Visiting: Koryo Tours says foreign tourists seldom go inside, because the operas are not scheduled regularly or announced far ahead; the exterior is a common photo stop, and the restaurant is sometimes open to foreign visitors.

FUNDING
Pyongyang Grand Theatre: state-run: a state theatre and its state opera troupe
- Korean Art, a North Korean website, describes the theatre as the home theatre of the Phibada Opera Troupe since its opening in 1960 (archived copy).
- The resident troupe answers to the ruling party: when the Sea of Blood Opera Troupe toured China in October 2011 at the invitation of China's Ministry of Culture, it was led by Kwon Hyuk Bong, vice director of the party's Propaganda and Agitation Department (Daily NK, 25 October 2011, citing Xinhua).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA said on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the theatre.

Not related: the East Pyongyang Grand Theatre, a different theatre on Munsu Street in the Taedonggang District, opened on 18 May 1989 and used mainly by the Mansudae Art Troupe (Korean Wikipedia). The 2018 Chinese ballet visit and the June 2025 Russian concert attended by Kim Jong Un took place there, not at this theatre (China Daily; Korea Herald).

BOTTOM LINE
State money, with no private funder in view; the sanctioned government is the whole of the risk. Worth watching: foreign troupes and festivals booked into the house.
