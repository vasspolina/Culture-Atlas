YERBA BUENA CENTER FOR THE ARTS: San Francisco, USA
ID: yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-san-francisco
Website: https://ybca.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art, performance and film centre at 701 Mission Street, in the Yerba Buena Gardens area of South of Market, San Francisco.
- Opened in 1993; conceived in a deal Mayor George Moscone made with developers to set aside land and funds for cultural institutions in the South of Market redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- Two buildings: the Gallery and Forum building by Fumihiko Maki, and the Blue Shield of California Theater by James Polshek and Todd Schliemann (stated by the centre).
- Programmes: exhibitions, public art, performances, education and an archive; a 2021 guaranteed income pilot paid $1,000 a month to 130 artists for six months (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday 11am to 8pm and Thursday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. The about page gives no admission prices or accessibility information.

FUNDING
Yerba Buena Center for the Arts: mixed public and private: city arts money, foundations and individual donors
- City money: Proposition E (2018) directs 1.5% of the city's hotel tax to arts and cultural services (Wikipedia). The size of the centre's own city grants was not found.
- Revenue for 2024-25 was $9,315,946 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- The centre's funder line says its programmes are made possible in part by MacKenzie Scott and Dan Jewett, the California Arts Council and #StartSmall, followed by further foundations and donors (YBCA 100 page; the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, William and Flora Hewlett Foundation and San Francisco Arts Commission were named in a search result of the same sentence).
- Granted AI, an aggregator working from IRS filings, lists 17 foundation funders including Hewlett (the largest recurring funder), Koret, Mellon, Doris Duke, Kenneth Rainin and Bernard Osher, plus federal awards from the Institute of Museum and Library Services and the National Endowment for the Arts. Amounts were not verified.
- No donor roster with amounts was readable; the 2023-24 annual report PDF could not be read as text.

Red flags: boycott and protest by exhibiting artists, aimed at funders and board
- Eight artists in the Bay Area Now 9 triennial altered their own works on 15 February 2024 in the "Love Letter to Gaza" action, organised with Jewish Voice for Peace Bay Area and other groups (KQED, Hyperallergic).
- Their demands included an end to censorship, a ceasefire in Gaza, and the removal of "Zionist" board members and funders; Hyperallergic named the Hewlett Foundation and the Koret Foundation as the funders targeted.
- On 21 February 2024 the centre rejected the demands as discrimination on religion, ethnic background or national origin (YBCA statement). The artists then called for a boycott until they met its leadership (KQED).
- Galleries closed for about a month (Wikipedia). Interim CEO Sara Fenske Bahat resigned on 3 March 2024, citing "the vitriolic and antisemitic backlash directed at me personally" (KQED).
- Supervisor Hillary Ronen called a special hearing on the publicly funded centre and called its actions censorship (KQED).
- The dispute began in December 2023, when the centre declined "Free Palestine" on its marquee for artist Lukaza Branfman-Verissimo; Jeffrey Cheung said he was barred from Palestinian flag colours in a mural, which the CEO denied (KQED).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the 2024 protest is detailed; the art-press pieces found report or support the artists' side. No criticism of the centre's funding before 2024 was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Artists Alter Their Work, Protesting San Francisco Institution's 'Silence' on Gaza" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/872425/artists-deface-their-work-protesting-san-francisco-yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-silence-on-gaza/
- Reports the artists' view that the centre restricted pro-Palestine advocacy in their art, and lists their demands, including the removal of the Hewlett and Koret Foundations.
- Gives the CEO's reply: a mural design arrived after a deadline the centre needed for coordination with the city and funders.
2. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Yerba Buena Center for the Arts CEO Resigns Over Pro-Palestine Action" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-ceo-resigns-over-pro-palestine-action
- Reports the resignation and both sides; the artists say their boycott targets institutions, not individuals. Names no funders.
3. Variable West: Ella Ray, "Editor's Note: YBCA's censorship of artists for Palestinian liberation" (2024)
URL: https://variablewest.com/2024/03/10/editors-note-ybcas-censorship-of-artists-for-palestinian-liberation/
- Argues the centre censored artists, removed their work into storage and avoided addressing its ties to Israel; calls on it to cut ties with "Zionist" donors.
- Names the Hewlett and Koret Foundations without specifying the funding ties. Variable West states it "unequivocally supports" the artists, so this is an advocacy position.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre's money is public and foundation money, and nothing read ties it to a flagged industry. The weak points are thin donor disclosure, the 2024 dispute over censorship claims and the demand that two named foundations be dropped, and the CEO's resignation that followed. Watch the board's response to the boycott call and whether donor lists with amounts are published.
