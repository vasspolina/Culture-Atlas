Red flags: Copyright lawsuit by the Anne Frank Fonds, 2021 to 2026
- In 2021 the Anne Frank Fonds of Basel, which holds the copyright in Anne Frank's works, brought proceedings to stop a free online scholarly edition of her manuscripts, published in September 2021 on the initiative of the Anne Frank Stichting and others; the site is geo-blocked from states where the works are protected, and in the Netherlands parts of them are protected until 2037 (Court of Justice of the European Union, press release 98/26, 9 July 2026).
- On 1 February 2022 the Amsterdam court ruled that the Anne Frank House and the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences had not infringed the Fonds' copyright in the Netherlands and that the edition could stay online (Anne Frank House, 8 February 2022).
- The Supreme Court of the Netherlands referred questions to the EU Court of Justice, which ruled on 9 July 2026 that a work in the public domain in some member states can be published free of charge online if state-of-the-art geo-blocking keeps out users in a member state where it is protected, even if a VPN can get round it (Court of Justice of the European Union, 9 July 2026).
