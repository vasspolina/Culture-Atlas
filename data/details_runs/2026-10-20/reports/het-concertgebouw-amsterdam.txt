HET CONCERTGEBOUW: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: het-concertgebouw-amsterdam
Website: https://www.concertgebouw.nl (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Concert hall on Museumplein in Amsterdam; home of the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra, which gave its first concert there on 3 November 1888 (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 11 April 1888; the company Concertgebouw NV was founded in 1882 (NOS).
- Named "Concertgebouw" (concert building); the "Koninklijk" title was granted by Queen Beatrix in 2013 for its 125th anniversary (Wikipedia).
- Adolf Leonard van Gendt designed it, drawing on Leipzig's Gewandhaus; it was built on a pasture outside the city. Emergency restoration ran from 1985 to 1988, when 2,186 rotting wooden piles were replaced with concrete, and Pi de Bruijn designed a modern annex (Wikipedia).
- Main Hall seats 1,974, Recital Hall 437, Choir Hall 150 (Wikipedia).
- The site offers hall rental, sponsoring, and an accessibility page for visitors with disabilities (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Het Concertgebouw: privately funded: gifts and inheritances through a support fund, ticket sales, and project grants
- NOS reported on 31 October 2025, citing the Financieele Dagblad, that the hall has been almost entirely privately funded for nearly 150 years. About €100 million was raised over 25 years through a support foundation, Het Concertgebouw Fonds, which has had ANBI charity status since 2012 (NH Nieuws) and has collected funds for the NV since 2001 (NOS).
- The Concertgebouw Fonds, Van Lanschot and the VriendenLoterij are thanked on the homepage; Van Lanschot Kempen, Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst and Fonds Podiumkunsten appear as logos (stated by the space).
- Dick Boer, a former Ahold chief executive, has chaired the NV since 2017; Jeroen van der Veer, a former Shell chief executive, stepped down as chair of the support foundation in October 2025 (NOS).
- No reporting found of a structural state or city subsidy for the hall itself; the orchestra is funded separately.

Red flags: tax arrangement of the gift structure
- The Belastingdienst's position, reported by the FD, was that the foundation should not have passed gifts tax-free to the NV; gift tax, which can reach 40 percent, was not paid, with possible exposure of tens of millions of euros (NOS).
- Director Simon Reinink said the structure "niet heeft voldaan aan de letter van de wet" ("did not meet the letter of the law", our translation) but that there was no malicious intent; he had no indication that repayment would be demanded (NOS).
- The NV was to become a foundation from 1 January 2026 (NH Nieuws).

Red flags: protest over the Chanukah concert
- On 2 November 2025 the hall ended its cooperation with Stichting Chanukah Concert for the 14 December concert because the lead cantor Shai Abramson, whom his own website calls Chief Cantor of the Israeli army at official occasions, was "a visible representative" of the Israeli military; the hall called his presence "contrary to the Concertgebouw's mission to connect people through music" (NOS, NL Times).
- Pro-Palestinian groups had pressed the hall to bar him; the foundation called the decision an "infringement of freedom of religion" and said it would seek an injunction; Israel's ambassador called it "shameful and reprehensible" (NL Times).
- The concert later went ahead with Abramson as a closed event rather than an open one, which director Simon Reinink called a compromise; about sixty staff, roughly a quarter of the payroll, wrote to the management asking for an explanation (NH Nieuws, citing Het Parool).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found; the debate is in news and opinion outlets, and most of it concerns the Chanukah concert.
1. Joop (BNNVARA): Marcel Kolder, "Waarom het Concertgebouw niet cancelt, maar zijn geweten volgt" (2025)
URL: https://www.bnnvara.nl/joop/artikelen/waarom-het-concertgebouw-niet-cancelt-maar-zijn-geweten-volgt
- Defends the decision: the hall is free to choose performers if it does not discriminate, and targeted a link between a religious event and a military, not Judaism.
- An opinion piece in support; it carries no statements from the organisers or the singer.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private, raised through a tax-advantaged fund. The weak points are the Belastingdienst's objection to that structure, which the hall is restructuring, and the Chanukah dispute with its staff letter. Worth watching: any back-tax demand.
