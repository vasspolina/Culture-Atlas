NATIONAL CENTRE FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS (NCPA): Beijing, China
ID: national-centre-for-the-performing-arts-ncpa
Website: https://www.chncpa.org (checked 2026-11-20; English site https://en.chncpa.org)

OVERVIEW
- Theatre complex at 2 West Chang'an Avenue, Xicheng District, on 21.75 hectares surrounded by an artificial lake, west of Tiananmen Square and the Great Hall of the People (China Daily; English Wikipedia).
- Approved in 1958 and shelved, it was revived when the party centre decided to build it in October 1997 (Steven Pai-hsing Hsiao, Journal of Building and Planning, National Taiwan University, 2015). Paul Andreu's design, developed with Tsinghua University, won the competition announced on 22 July 1999 (CCTV, 15 December 2008).
- Building formally began on 13 December 2001 (Hsiao) and the centre opened on 22 December 2007 (People's Daily, 1 January 2024). Its titanium and glass shell is 212.20 metres long and 46.285 metres high (English Wikipedia).
- Halls: an opera theatre for 2,300, a concert hall for more than 2,000 with China's largest pipe organ, a theatre of nearly 1,000 seats and a multifunction theatre; it has its own chorus, orchestra and opera singers (China Daily).
- Since December 2023 it has run three sites: the main building, the Taihu Stage Art Center (in use since July 2018) and the Beijing Art Center (People's Daily, 1 January 2024).
- In 18 years it produced 119 original productions and more than 600 exhibitions; its World Theatre Alliance has 41 members in 26 countries. Wang Ning heads the centre (Xinhua, 23 December 2025).
- Visiting: building tours Tuesday to Sunday, 9 am to 5 pm, 40 yuan; closed on Mondays (chncpa.org).

FUNDING
National Centre for the Performing Arts: mainly public: the Beijing municipal budget, plus its own service income
- Status: a Beijing municipal public institution at bureau level, set up under a 2007 letter of the city's staffing office (NCPA 2025 final accounts).
- 2025 accounts: income of 1.179 billion yuan, of which 737.3 million (62.55%) came from Beijing's general public budget, 435.3 million (36.93%) from its own service income and 6.1 million from other sources; spending was 1.197 billion (NCPA 2025 final accounts).
- 2026 budget: 733.5 million yuan from the general public budget and 412.1 million in service income (NCPA 2026 budget).
- The centre lists a sponsorship contact and has a development and cooperation department (China Daily; NCPA 2025 final accounts).

Red flags: 2024 partnership with the natural gas group ENN
- On 29 April 2024 ENN Group signed a strategic cooperation agreement with the NCPA, with Wang Ning and ENN chairman Wang Yusuo present: ENN supports the centre's brand building and content, and the NCPA Art Development Foundation and the ENN Charity Foundation set up a joint fund for culture in Beijing, Tianjin and Hebei (Xinhua). The page is stamped 21 April 2023, but its text calls 2024 the current year and reports the Beijing Art Center's opening in December 2023.
- ENN began in urban gas and works across the natural gas industry, from distribution and trading to storage and transmission (ENN Group website).

Red flags: 2000 petitions against the building
- On 10 June 2000, 49 members of the Chinese Academies of Sciences and Engineering, including He Zuoxiu and Wu Liangyong, petitioned the party leadership to reconsider the project as wasteful and unsafe; nine days later 114 architecture and engineering professionals asked for a new design (Hsiao).
- Work stopped in July 2000 and resumed after the feasibility report was approved on 15 March 2001 (Hsiao); Andreu had to cut costs by 25% (Building, 2007).

Red flags: Cost rise of the building, 1999 to 2007
- After the design was chosen in 1999 the brief doubled in area and the cost of the scheme trebled; completion slipped by 20 months, partly because work was halted for a safety review after four people died in a walkway collapse at Charles de Gaulle airport in 2004, for which Andreu was exonerated (Building, Martin Spring, 28 September 2007).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Architecture critics attacked the building's form, cost, safety and energy use, not the centre's programme or funders.
1. Architectural Record: Fred A. Bernstein, "National Center for the Performing Arts" (2008)
URL: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/8114-national-center-for-the-performing-arts
- For eight years and at least $400 million, "The Egg doesn't pack much of a punch"; the dome is far larger than the three halls need.
- Heating and cooling a "city under glass" carries vast environmental costs, and Andreu admits the building is not green by Western standards.
- Criticises the building, not the institution.
2. Aisixiang: Peng Peigen, "紧急挽救中国国家大剧院" ("Urgently save China's National Grand Theatre", own translation) (2004)
URL: https://www.aisixiang.com/data/3588.html
- Peng, a Tsinghua professor and first-round juror, argues the egg reworks Andreu's 1993 Osaka aquarium entry.
- Warns that audiences 15 metres underground, ringed by 50,000 tonnes of water, would struggle to escape a fire or earthquake, and proposes removing the dome.
- Criticises the building, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The NCPA's running money is clean: Beijing's budget and its own service income. The weak point is its tie to a gas company through its foundation, on top of a building that scientists and architects fought over cost and safety. Watch what ENN's fund pays for.
