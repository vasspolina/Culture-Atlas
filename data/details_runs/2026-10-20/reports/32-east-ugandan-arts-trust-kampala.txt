32° EAST | UGANDAN ARTS TRUST: Kampala, Uganda
ID: 32-east-ugandan-arts-trust-kampala
Website: https://ugandanartstrust.org (checked 2026-10-24; redirects to https://32east.org/, which loads)

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit and home for the visual arts community in Uganda, at Plot 212 Sonko Close, Kabalagala, Kampala, behind Equity Bank (stated by the trust).
- Founded in 2011; Myriad USA's 2022 article names the founders as the British nationals Rocca Holly-Nambi and Nikki Elphinstone, and The Skinny (2013) names them as Rocca Gutteridge and Nicola Elphinstone. Teesa Bahana is now director.
- Operated from metal shipping containers for years, then bought a plot off Ggaba Road and built a permanent centre (Myriad USA, 2022).
- Building: the 32° East Arts Centre, designed by London's New Makers Bureau with the Kampala firm Localworks, with rammed-earth walls and earth bricks. It won "Cultural project of the year 2023" at the Dezeen Awards (Dezeen). The trust's June 2024 news page says phase one is delivered.
- Programmes: artist residencies (over 100 artists hosted), the KLA ART festival (five editions), Artachat, membership, drop-in sessions, workshops, a zine club, space rental and fine art printing (stated by the trust). The membership fee is 100,000 UGX a year (Rolling Stone Africa, 2025).
- Visiting: the trust gives an address and phone numbers but lists no opening hours or admission on the pages read; Rolling Stone Africa gives the annual membership fee above.

FUNDING
32° East | Ugandan Arts Trust: foundation-funded and crowd-funded: Sigrid Rausing Trust and other European and US funders, plus individual donors
- Sigrid Rausing Trust: has supported the trust since 2019, with £500,000 in earlier grants and a current grant of £80,000 starting in May 2026, under its Open Societies programme (Sigrid Rausing Trust). The trust's page does not state what the current grant is for.
- Networks: the trust works through Arts Collaboratory, Triangle Network and the Prince Claus Fund (stated by the trust). Arts Collaboratory is "a program promoted by Hivos and DOEN Foundation", and its partner table lists the Ugandan Arts Trust under the project "Artistic Exchange David Bade and 32º East" (Wikipedia). No amounts found.
- Individual donors: a crowdfunding campaign raised an anonymous $10,000 gift and the British artist Susan Rosenberg gave an unstated sum; the "Friends of 32° East" fund at fiscal sponsor Myriad USA held close to $82,000 in March 2022 (Myriad USA). Donations from US donors are tax-deductible through it.
- Planned own income: a café, a shop selling Ugandan handmade products, and rentals of studio space and bedrooms (Myriad USA, 2022).
- No reporting found of a payment by Stichting DOEN, the Segal Family Foundation or the Open Society Foundations to the trust with an amount or date; none of the trust's own pages read names them.
- No annual report or accounts were found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the trust itself was found. Coverage is admiring; two pieces make general remarks on the risks of foreign money and Western ideas in Uganda's art scene and name no funder.
1. Rolling Stone Africa: Kalanzi Kajubi, "32° East is a Sanctuary for Visual Arts in Uganda" (9 May 2025)
URL: https://rollingstoneafrica.com/culture/art-features/32-is-a-sanctuary-for-visual-arts-in-uganda/
- General remarks, not aimed at one funder: artists' funding "almost always comes with stipulations that serve the interests of the funder", and European funding bodies have "archaic understandings of what development should look like".
- Director Teesa Bahana says: "Often they have their own agendas that you then have to fit in with." The piece names no funder as doing this; it mentions Arts Collaboratory and the Prince Claus Fund only as networks.
2. The Skinny: Rosamund West, "32º East: Art of Africa" (3 December 2013)
URL: https://www.theskinny.co.uk/travel/features/32-east-art-of-africa
- Notes that building a contemporary art scene in Uganda is "an incredibly contentious notion" that meets "near constant challenges and criticism", citing the colonial past and the risk of imposing a western idea of art.
- A general remark, not about the trust's funders; names no funder and presents the trust's grassroots approach favourably.

BOTTOM LINE
Nothing found ties the trust's money to a flagged industry or donor; the funders seen are foundations. The weak points are that no page read confirms DOEN, the Segal Family Foundation or the Open Society Foundations as funders, no accounts or annual reports are published, and the trust's director says funders often have their own agendas. Watch the Sigrid Rausing Trust grant and whether the trust publishes a funder list with amounts.
