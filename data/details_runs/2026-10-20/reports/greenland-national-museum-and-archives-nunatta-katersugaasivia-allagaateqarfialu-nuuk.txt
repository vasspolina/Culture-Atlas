GREENLAND NATIONAL MUSEUM AND ARCHIVES (NUNATTA KATERSUGAASIVIA ALLAGAATEQARFIALU): Nuuk, Greenland
ID: greenland-national-museum-and-archives-nunatta-katersugaasivia-allagaateqarfialu-nuuk
Website: https://en.nka.gl/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Greenland's national museum and national archives, at Hans Egedesvej 8 in Nuuk's old colonial harbour area (stated by the space, Wikipedia).
- The museum was founded in 1965, with its first exhibition in the Moravian Brethren Mission House; it merged with the National Archives in 1991 (Wikipedia).
- The museum occupies a warehouse built in 1936 and looks after nearby buildings including a cooper's workshop; the archives sit at Ilimmarfik, part of the University of Greenland (Wikipedia).
- Collections cover archaeology, history, art and handicrafts, including the 15th-century Qilakitsoq mummies and an umiak; the institution also handles heritage protection and archaeological surveys under Greenland law (Wikipedia, annual report 2024).
- Between the start of the Utimut return process and 2001, about 35,000 objects went from Denmark to Greenland institutions; around 100,000 items remained in Denmark (Cultural Property News).
- Open daily 10:00 to 17:00 from 15 April to 15 September and 12:00 to 16:00 in winter; adults 100 DKK, students and children free, with some free Mondays (stated by the space). It had 19,391 exhibition visitors in 2024 (annual report).

FUNDING
Greenland National Museum and Archives: mainly public: Greenland's budget, with Danish foundation grants for research
- The 2024 Finance Act gave an operating grant of DKK 13,610,000, of which DKK 12,273,000 went on salaries for 25 full-time posts, plus DKK 1,385,000 for heritage protection (annual report 2024).
- Own income was about DKK 1,650,000 for the museum, mainly shop sales, admissions and project overheads, and about DKK 188,000 for the archive (annual report 2024).
- Augustinus Fonden: DKK 4.5 million for the three-year Angerlartunnguit research project on the Uunartoq Thule Inuit remains (2025 to 2027), DKK 300,000 for the PoTraCH documentation of Kangerlussuaq and Narsarsuaq airports, and part funding of the Qimmit sled-dog project (2023 to 2026) with the Danish National Research Foundation (annual report 2024).
- Smaller grants in 2024: NAPA DKK 50,000, Beckett-Fonden DKK 100,000, Tips- og lottomidlerne DKK 196,000 and Sermersooq's Sermeq pool DKK 149,928 for the Puggut project; Queen Margrethe II's archaeological fund for redating the Qilakitsoq mummies (annual report 2024).
- A private donor, Connie Downing, has given since 2018, most recently DKK 86,000 in 2024 (annual report 2024).

Red flags: tobacco foundation money (Augustinus Fonden)
- Augustinus Fonden was created in 1942 by the owners of Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, one of the three predecessor companies of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, and now fully owns that company as its holding company (Wikipedia).
- Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker participates on a 27.30% pro-rata basis in Scandinavian Tobacco Group's share buy-back, under agreements from 10 November 2023 (STG company announcement via MFN).
- The foundation's DKK 4.5 million grant is the largest grant listed in the 2024 report and runs to 2027 (annual report 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum and archives run mainly on Greenland's public budget. The weak point is research money from Augustinus Fonden, whose holding company participates on a 27.30% pro-rata basis in Scandinavian Tobacco Group's share buy-back; the largest grant runs to 2027. No accounts or board are published.
