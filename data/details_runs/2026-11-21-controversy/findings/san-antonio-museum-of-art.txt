Red flags: 2022 to 2025 return of nine antiquities to Italy
- The museum repatriated nine antiquities to Italy; eight were identified through photographs seized from Giacomo Medici, a dealer and smuggler convicted in 2004 (The Art Newspaper, Benjamin Sutton, 12 December 2025).
- The ninth, a 2nd century AD marble head of Hermes excavated on Rome's Caelian Hill in the late 19th century, was sold by an Italian dealer without provenance documents to the collector Gilbert M. Denman Jr., who donated it to the museum in 1986; the German scholar Jörg Deterling alerted the museum in 2016, and Italy's Ministry of Culture confirmed the provenance and requested its return (The Art Newspaper, 12 December 2025).
- The head of Hermes was removed from the museum in 2022 and the other eight pieces were repatriated, most recently in September 2025; the eight had passed through dealers and auction houses, including Sotheby's, in New York and London in the 1980s and 1990s (San Antonio Report, Gisell Campos, 18 December 2025).
- Under a 2023 agreement between the museum and Italy's Ministry of Culture, the eight objects stay on display in San Antonio on loan from Italy; director Emily Ballew Neff said: "We look forward to continued collaboration with the ministry" (The Art Newspaper, 12 December 2025).
