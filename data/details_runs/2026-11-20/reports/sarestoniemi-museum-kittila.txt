SÄRESTÖNIEMI MUSEUM (SÄRESTÖNIEMI-MUSEO): Kittilä, Finland
ID: sarestoniemi-museum-kittila
Website: https://www.sarestoniemimuseo.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Home and art museum of the painter Reidar Särestöniemi (1925 to 1981) on his family farm by the Ounasjoki river in Kaukonen village, Kittilä, reached by a narrow gravel forest road (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- It opened to the public in 1985; the road to Särestö was built when the museum was founded (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- The site holds Old Särestö, his birthplace with a main building of 1873; a gallery of 1972, a studio home of 1978 and a café and office building of 1988, all three by the architects Reima and Raili Pietilä. The buildings and grounds are protected under the building protection law (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- The collections hold artworks, an archive, a photo archive and the museum buildings (sarestoniemimuseo.com); in 2025 it counted 1,779 artworks and 21,532 visits (Finnish Heritage Agency museum statistics).
- Anne Koskamo is director. ICOM Finland and the Finnish Museums Association named it Museum of the Year 2026 on 21 May 2026 (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- Visiting: open all year, Tuesday to Friday 11 to 17 and Saturday 10 to 16, closed on public holidays. Adults EUR 15; children, students and pensioners EUR 12; family ticket EUR 42; free with the Museum Card and for children under 7. Dogs are allowed on the grounds but not in the buildings (sarestoniemimuseo.com).

FUNDING
Särestöniemi Museum: mostly self-earned: tickets and sales, with state museum funding and sponsors
- The Kauko Sorjonen Foundation has run the museum since spring 2016; before that the Särestöniemi Museum Foundation ran it (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- 2025 income was EUR 660,917 against spending of EUR 595,629. Admissions gave EUR 295,853 and service and sales income EUR 166,929 (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- State money in 2025 was the statutory state share under the Museums Act, EUR 127,335 (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- Sponsorship in 2025 came to EUR 70,800 (Finnish Heritage Agency). The museum names the main partners of its 2025 Reidar100 centenary year as the Municipality of Kittilä, Agnico Eagle Finland Oy's Kittilä mine and the art organisation Taideorganisaatio Hiljaisuus ry, with Design Hotel Levi, Hettula Oy and AJH Infra as further backers (sarestoniemimuseo.com).
- The 2016 handover followed a money shortage: in 2013 the old foundation's chair Mauri Ylä-Kotola said the museum could not pay for professional museum work even if visitor numbers doubled, and visits had fallen to about 6,000 a year (Yle).

Red flags: Centenary partner Agnico Eagle Finland, fined in 2021 for a death at its Kittilä mine
- In 2021 the Lapland District Court gave Agnico Eagle Finland Oy a corporate fine of EUR 130,000 for a workplace safety offence after an excavator driver working for a local contractor fell 25 metres into an open pit at the Kittilä mine on 15 December 2016 and died; four supervisors were also fined (Kaleva).
- In 2011 residents near the mine complained of discharges into the Seurujoki river, and reindeer herders said the mine had changed grazing routes; the regional environment authority reported higher nitrogen loads but no toxins in the water (Yle).

BOTTOM LINE
Mostly clean: a museum that lives on its own ticket and shop income plus Finnish state money. The weak point is its centenary partnership with a gold miner convicted over a worker's death.
