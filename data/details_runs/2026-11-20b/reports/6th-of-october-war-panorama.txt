6TH OF OCTOBER WAR PANORAMA: Cairo, Egypt
ID: 6th-of-october-war-panorama
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- War museum and memorial to the 1973 October War on Salah Salem Street, Heliopolis, Cairo, on about 32,000 square metres (7.5 feddans) (English Wikipedia; Egypt Independent).
- Mada Masr writes that Hosni Mubarak got the idea during a visit to North Korea; Mubarak inaugurated it on 5 October 1989 (English Wikipedia).
- A cylindrical, fort-like building in an Islamic architectural style; English Wikipedia says North Korean architects gave partial assistance, and Egypt Independent says Koreans built it over five years.
- Inside, a documentary, a moving model of the Suez Canal front and a 360-degree painted mural seen from a rotating platform; tanks, aircraft and guns, Egyptian and Israeli, stand outside (Egypt Independent; English Wikipedia).
- Shbabbek listed five halls in 2023, among them the "Army and People" hall (قاعة جيش وشعب), a digital show on the revolutions of 25 January and 30 June and the bond between army and people (my translation).
- Visiting: open daily 9:00 to 21:00 except Tuesdays in 2010, with tickets of 3 Egyptian pounds for Egyptians, 10 for Arabs and 30 for foreigners (Egypt Independent); Shbabbek gave 100 to 200 pounds and five tours a day in 2023.

FUNDING
6th of October War Panorama: public: the Egyptian state through its armed forces
- Commissioned by President Mubarak (Egypt Independent) and run by the National Military Museums Department (English Wikipedia; Arabic Wikipedia, which says the department took charge on opening day).
- Build: Public Delivery says it was built in collaboration with North Korea's Mansudae Overseas Projects.
- In October 2025 the armed forces opened military museums free until 6 October, including the panorama's outdoor weapons display, for the 52nd anniversary of the war (Egypt Telegraph).

Not related: the Tishreen War Panorama, also called the October War Panorama, in Damascus, Syria. It also had North Korean help but is a separate Syrian museum (English Wikipedia; Public Delivery).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces discuss the panorama: a visitor review that mocks its upkeep and a critical essay on how Egypt presents the war. Neither concerns its money.
1. Egypt Independent: Ali Abdel Mohsen, "Egypt's Museums II: The October War Panorama" (2010)
URL: https://egyptindependent.com/egypts-museums-ii-october-war-panorama/
- Calls the panorama marred by "an almost surreal level of incompetence": misspelt tickets, war vehicles repainted in one flat green, a documentary too degraded to follow, and kiosks selling toys and trinkets.
- Calls the rotating mural striking and realistic, and concludes the visit offers amusement and frustration but no education.
2. Mada Masr: Iman Hamam, "Asleep at the wheel" (2014)
URL: https://www.madamasr.com/en/2014/10/06/feature/culture/asleep-at-the-wheel/
- Asks how the 1973 war came to be presented as a victory "even though it wasn't", and traces the army's mark on Cairo through bridges, public art and museum displays.
- Describes the panorama as a peculiar spot for the victory, set back from the road beside the Ministry of Information and a park with children's fairground rides.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Public Delivery reports that Mansudae Overseas Projects is under many sanctions and that a UN Panel of Experts linked ongoing African statue projects to it in 2020.
- Its work in Cairo dates from the 1980s, before those sanctions.

BOTTOM LINE
The Egyptian armed forces own and run the panorama, with no private sponsor. Its weak points are the North Korean state firm that helped build it and a display that presents the army's version of history, including recent politics.
