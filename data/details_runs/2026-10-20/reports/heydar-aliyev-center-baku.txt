HEYDAR ALIYEV CENTER: Baku, Azerbaijan
ID: heydar-aliyev-center-baku
Website: https://heydaraliyevcenter.az

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre in Baku between Heydar Aliyev International Airport and the city centre (Dezeen, 2 July 2014).
- Commissioned by Ilham Aliyev, the president of Azerbaijan (Dezeen, 2 July 2014). Construction began in 2007 and the centre had a soft-opening ceremony on 10 May 2012 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Heydar Aliyev (1923 to 2003), Ilham Aliyev's father, president of Azerbaijan from 1993 to 2003 (Wikipedia, Dezeen).
- Designed by Zaha Hadid Architects, with Zaha Hadid and Patrik Schumacher leading the design, covering 57,000 square metres (Dezeen, 11 July 2013). The main contractor was DIA Holding (Wikipedia).
- On site: a 1,000-seat auditorium, temporary exhibition spaces, a conference centre, workshops and a museum, which includes the "Mini Azerbaijan" exhibition of models of 24 historical and architectural buildings (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Heydar Aliyev Center: commissioned by the president of Azerbaijan, estimated cost US$250 million
- Wikipedia gives the building cost as an estimated US$250 million.
- The Art Newspaper calls the centre the flagship of the Heydar Aliyev Foundation's building programme in Baku; the foundation's president is the president's wife, Mehriban (The Art Newspaper, 30 April 2015).
- Unverified: Wikipedia states that the main contractor, DIA Holding, is owned by actors linked to the Aliyev family's network of offshore companies, citing a 2013 Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty report.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Three pieces from 1 and 2 July 2014, written after the Design Museum in London gave the building the architecture prize in its 2014 Designs of the Year. They criticise the award and report claims of forced evictions at the site.
1. The Architects' Journal: Richard Waite, "Zaha design award sparks backlash" (2 July 2014)
URL: https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/zaha-design-award-sparks-backlash
- Rebecca Vincent, advocacy director of Art for Democracy, led criticism in the Independent that the prize was unhelpful.
- Hugh Williamson, director of Human Rights Watch's Europe and Central Asia division, said Heydar Aliyev "was an authoritarian leader and his son is now".
- Reports that Human Rights Watch also said the building was constructed after local people living on the site were evicted by force.
2. Art F City: Andrew Wagner, "Zaha Hadid Under Fire for Human Rights Controversies" (1 July 2014)
URL: https://artfcity.com/posts/2014/07/zaha-hadid-under-fire-for-human-rights-controversies/
- Cites The Guardian for the figure of nearly 250 homes cleared for the building, and Human Rights Watch for the claim that Azerbaijan illegally took hundreds of homes in the capital to make way for urban development.
- Quotes a statement from Hadid's firm that the contractor was "internationally accredited by the Geneva-based inspection and verification body SGS", and says Hadid had yet to comment on the issue.
3. Dezeen: Marcus Fairs, "Designs of the Year prize 'about architecture rather than politics' says Design Museum director" (2 July 2014)
URL: https://www.dezeen.com/2014/07/02/design-museum-designs-of-the-year-prize-zaha-hadid-heydar-aliyev-centre-azerbaijan/
- Design Museum director Deyan Sudjic defended the prize as "about architecture rather than politics" and said the jury made its own decision on the best project.
- Reports that human rights groups claim residents were forcefully evicted to clear the site, and cite election rigging and torture of prisoners in Azerbaijan; no group is named.
- Quotes critics: Oliver Wainwright tweeted that Hadid won an award for supporting forced evictions, illegal demolition and bonded labour; Rebecca Vincent said the situation in Azerbaijan is getting worse.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Forced evictions at the site: Human Rights Watch, as reported by The Architects' Journal on 2 July 2014, said the building was constructed after local people living on the site were evicted by force. Dezeen (2 July 2014) reported that unnamed human rights groups made the same claim, and Art F City (1 July 2014) cited The Guardian for nearly 250 homes cleared.
- The connection: the centre was commissioned by Ilham Aliyev, honours his father, and is the flagship of a foundation headed by the president's wife.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre was commissioned by President Ilham Aliyev, and Wikipedia gives its cost as an estimated US$250 million. In July 2014 Human Rights Watch said the site was cleared by forced evictions, and The Guardian, cited by Art F City, put the figure at nearly 250 homes.
