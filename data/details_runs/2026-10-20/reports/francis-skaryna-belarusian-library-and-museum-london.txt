FRANCIS SKARYNA BELARUSIAN LIBRARY AND MUSEUM: London, United Kingdom
ID: francis-skaryna-belarusian-library-and-museum-london
Website: https://skaryna.org/support/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Library, archive and museum of Belarusian books, art and artefacts at 37 Holden Road in North Finchley, a short walk from Woodside Park Underground station (stated by the library).
- It grew out of the Belarusian Catholic Mission, founded in London in 1947; the Mission moved to Marian House on Holden Avenue in 1948 with a collection of 200 to 300 Belarusian books brought by Fr Česlaus Sipovič (Wikipedia).
- It is named for Francysk Skaryna and was called Bibliotheca Alboruthena from 1958; Robert Auty formally opened the library on 15 May 1971 (Wikipedia).
- Ceslaus Sipovich, Auberon Herbert and Paval Navara organised a fundraising campaign in 1969 that allowed the library to acquire a building (Wikipedia).
- On site: a library of over 30,000 volumes, archives and a museum whose items include 18th-century Słuck girdles, banknotes, stamps and religious art (Wikipedia).
- Governance: registered as a charitable trust in 1979, removed from the charity list in 2011, revived by the trustees in 2014 and re-registered in 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Open Days on the first Saturday of each month with no appointment; private visits by email. The building is not wheelchair accessible.

FUNDING
Francis Skaryna Belarusian Library and Museum: community-funded: donations from Belarusians and friends, a small bond endowment and flat rent; no public or corporate funding
- The library states that it receives no regular public or corporate funding. Its support page asks for monthly gifts from £10 and one-off gifts through Stripe, with Gift Aid, CAF Donate and gifts in wills.
- Its 2015 donor report gave donations of over £4,100 that year, modest income from an endowment invested in bonds, and rent from a one-bedroom flat in part of the ground floor that covered essential expenses.
- Ihar Ivanou, writing on the library's site (8 September 2015, updated 2021), says the library has never received public funds and aims to rely on Belarusians and their friends, which he says preserves its independence.
- Ivanou also reports that trustees have received formal and informal requests from Belarus to hand over parts of the collection to state-funded institutions (stated by the library's author).
- Trustees listed on the library's governance page: Karalina Matskevich, Elena Edwards, Rev Siarhiej Stasievich, Yarik Kryvoi, Pavel Shevtsov and Rev Ihar Labacevich.
- No reporting found of named donors or grants. Annual reports for 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025 are listed on the site; their figures were not read.

Not related: Skaryna Press, a London publisher of Belarusian books. It takes its name from Francis Skaryna, and its own page does not mention the library. The Maładečna District Court in Minsk Voblast ruled its Instagram page "extremist" on 15 May 2025; the publisher calls this a politically motivated attempt to pressure its editors, authors and readers.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the library is small and community-based: donations, a bond endowment and flat rent, with no public, corporate or flagged funder found. The weak points are thin financial disclosure (no donor names, and the figures in the annual reports were not read) and the requests from Belarus to hand over parts of the collection, reported by the library's own author. Worth watching are the annual reports and any further such requests.
