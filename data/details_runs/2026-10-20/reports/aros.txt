AROS AARHUS KUNSTMUSEUM: Aarhus, Denmark
ID: aros
Website: https://www.aros.dk (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Aros Allé 2 in central Aarhus, the oldest public art museum in Denmark outside Copenhagen; collecting began in 1847 and the first public show opened on 6 January 1859 (Wikipedia).
- A self-governing institution with an operating grant from the Ministry of Culture; director Rebecca Matthews, board chair Laura Hay Uggla (2025 annual report).
- The present 10-storey building by Schmidt Hammer Lassen opened on 7 April 2004 with 20,700 m² of floor space (Wikipedia).
- Olafur Eliasson's circular rooftop walk "Your rainbow panorama" opened on 28 May 2011, cost DKK 60 million and was sponsored by the Realdania foundation (Wikipedia).
- The Next Level expansion added the 1,000 m² underground Salling Gallery on 20 June 2025; James Turrell's skyspace "As Seen Below, The Dome" was delayed after a subcontractor went bankrupt and was due to open in June 2026 (2025 annual report). Aarhus Municipality was the client and Aarsleff the builder.
- More than 8,000 works; 649,448 visitors in 2025, of whom 556,084 held tickets (2025 annual report).
- From October to May open Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 20:00 and weekends 09:00 to 17:00, closed Mondays; adults DKK 200, under-31s and students DKK 150, under-18s free; lifts to all galleries, accessible toilets and free companion entry with a companion card.

FUNDING
ARoS: mixed: state and municipal grants, ticket and shop income, and private foundations
- In 2025 public grants were DKK 50.3 million: DKK 17.9 million from Aarhus Municipality, including DKK 8.1 million towards the building loan, DKK 31.4 million from the state via the municipality, and DKK 1 million direct from the state (2025 annual report).
- Earned income was DKK 84.0 million, including DKK 29.9 million from tickets; sponsors and foundations gave DKK 29.3 million; the year ended with a DKK 1.6 million surplus (2025 annual report).
- The Salling Foundations, which own Salling Group (Netto, Føtex, Bilka), are listed with DKK 104 million of support to ARoS (lex.dk, no period given) and paid for the Salling Gallery and its fit-out; the New Carlsberg Foundation, Aarhus Municipality and an anonymous donor also supported the wider expansion (ARoS, 2025).
- 2025 exhibition funders included the Augustinus Foundation, Ny Carlsbergfondet, Statens Kunstfond, Stibo Fonden, Lars Larsens Jysk Fond and Toyota-Fonden (2025 annual report).
- Board members include Marianne Bedsted, a director at Salling, and Helle Østergaard Kristiansen, a senior vice president at Equinor (2025 annual report).

Red flags: tobacco money through the Augustinus Foundation
- The Augustinus Foundation supported four 2025 shows: Barbara Kruger, Isaac Julien, Anna Boghiguian and Jenkin van Zyl (2025 annual report).
- The foundation was set up in 1942 at the initiative of tobacco manufacturer Ludvig Augustinus and fully owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (Wikipedia).
- Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker holds 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, which makes cigars and pipe tobacco (Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker).
- No reporting found of a protest at ARoS over this money.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage of the expansion and the Turrell skyspace is admiring; the skyspace was delayed after a roofing subcontractor went bankrupt in 2025 (2025 annual report).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Lex.dk reports that the Salling Foundations gave DKK 250 million in 2019 to a municipal football stadium in Aarhus, just under a third of the DKK 995 million budget, and that Jyllands-Posten journalists examined its effect on the municipal council's co-financing, which raised a debate about foundations without a democratic mandate holding decision power.
- The Salling Foundations have given ARoS DKK 104 million and a Salling director sits on its board.

BOTTOM LINE
ARoS runs on public money, ticket income and Danish foundations, with full audited accounts. The weak point is current support from the Augustinus Foundation, whose holding company owns a quarter of Scandinavian Tobacco Group. Worth watching: how far one retail family's foundations shape the museum's building and board.
