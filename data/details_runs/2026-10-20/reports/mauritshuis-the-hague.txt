OVERVIEW
- Art museum in a 17th-century town palace in The Hague, with 854 objects, mostly Dutch Golden Age paintings by artists such as Vermeer, Rembrandt and Frans Hals (Wikipedia).
- Opened to the public in 1822. It has been an independent non-profit foundation since 1995 and still receives government funding (Wikipedia).
- Named for Johan Maurits van Nassau-Siegen, who built the house between 1633 and 1644 with income from his time as governor-general of Dutch Brazil (Dutch Wikipedia). Jacob van Campen designed it and Pieter Post completed the construction.
- DutchNews reports that Maurits paid for the house with profits from Brazilian sugar plantations that relied on enslaved labour.
- A fire destroyed the interior in 1704; a lottery organised by the heirs of the later owner helped fund rebuilding, finished in 1718 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Renovated 2012 to 2014 and reopened on 27 June 2014 with the neighbouring Plein 26 building and a new underground foyer by Hans van Heeswijk (Dutch Wikipedia). It drew 497,416 visitors in 2025. Martine Gosselink has been director since February 2020 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Mauritshuis: mixed: Dutch state funding and a corporate main sponsor, NN Group, with a supporting foundation
- Government funding continues alongside private income (Wikipedia). No reporting read of the amounts.
- NN Group has been "main sponsor of the Mauritshuis since 2016" and pays for the annual exhibition, the museum playlist "View the Mauritshuis with Your Ears" and the guest-director evenings Maurits&; no amount or terms are stated (NN Group website).
- Stichting Johan Maurits van Nassau, founded in 1957, supports acquisitions and exhibitions; it merged with the Stichting Vrienden van het Mauritshuis in 1986 (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Shell Nederland was a partner for six years until July 2018 and also funded technical research on paintings by Jan Steen. The partnership was not renewed, according to a museum spokesman. The museum's annual report called support from Shell Nederland and two other partners "crucial for the long-term future of the museum"; Shell Nederland said the end came through "mutual decisions" (The Art Newspaper, Martin Bailey, 29 August 2018).
- Fossil Free Culture protested Shell's partnerships with the Mauritshuis and the Van Gogh Museum, calling them "art-washing" (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic, 2018). No dated protest at the Mauritshuis and no stated reason from the museum found.
- No reporting read of a current fossil-fuel funder.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In January 2018 Prime Minister Mark Rutte called the museum's removal of a plaster bust of Johan Maurits from its foyer "crazy" and suggested a name change if it wanted to distance itself from its founder. On 22 January he admitted the Mauritshuis was not a well-chosen example (DutchNews).
- Director Emilie Gordenker said the bust was unnecessary because the museum had created a gallery on Maurits, and rejected a name change (DutchNews).
- Connection: the debate concerns the museum's founder and namesake.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum today is Dutch state funding and corporate sponsorship led by NN Group; no fossil-fuel funder is currently reported. The weak points are the Shell partnership that ended in 2018, which the museum's own report called crucial, and the founder's fortune from plantations that relied on enslaved labour.
