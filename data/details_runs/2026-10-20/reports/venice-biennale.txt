VENICE BIENNALE: Venice, Italy
ID: venice-biennale
Website: https://www.labiennale.org/en (checked 2026-10-24; the donors page for the 2026 art exhibition loads; the organisation page returned 404)

OVERVIEW
- International arts organisation whose main exhibition is held at the Giardini, with about 30 permanent national pavilions and the Central Pavilion, and at the Arsenale in Castello, Venice (Wikipedia).
- The Venice City Council set up the biennial in 1893 and the first exhibition opened on 30 April 1895. It became a foundation in 2004. Pietrangelo Buttafuoco has been president since 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Art and Architecture exhibitions alternate; Music, Theatre, Dance and Film festivals run annually. The 61st Art Exhibition, "In Minor Keys", curated by Koyo Kouoh, who died before it opened, runs 9 May to 22 November 2026 with 99 countries (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: not stated on the pages read.

FUNDING
Venice Biennale: mixed: Italian public funds, tickets and commercial income, plus sponsors and donors
- 2024 budget forecast: public contributions of €33.095 million, and €31.427 million from tickets, publishing and food and beverage, a figure that includes sponsorship and donations estimated at €7.78 million (Biennale budget report 2024, forecast not final accounts). A €169.556 million Ministry of Culture allocation for a development and restoration project is also noted there.
- 2026 donors page lists 46 donors under one heading, including the Teiger Foundation, Getty, the Ford Foundation, Ammodo, LUMA Foundation, Christian Dior Couture, Samsung Foundation of Culture, Batia and Idan Ofer and The Helis Foundation. No amounts are given.
- Sponsors (Milano Luxury Life, 10 May 2026): Bvlgari, exclusive partner for 2026, 2028 and 2030; illycaffè, main sponsor; Zegna, main sponsor of the Italian Pavilion; American Express, official sponsor; Banca Ifis; BPER Banca; Bloomberg Philanthropies, collaborating since 2022. No amounts.
- European Commission: a €2 million grant for 2025 to 2028 was ended after the Commission endorsed withdrawal on 12 July 2026 (Euronews).
Red flags: Helis Foundation, oil family money
- The Helis Foundation, a 2026 donor, was established and funded by the William Helis family in 1955 (foundation website). The advocacy group Healthy Gulf (2019, an advocacy source) calls it the philanthropic arm of Helis Oil & Gas and alleges the company dredged and drilled over 300 acres of Louisiana coast.

Red flags: boycott and protest at the 2026 edition
- Russia's pavilion: the Biennale announced Russia's return in March 2026. Culture minister Alessandro Giuli and the European Commission objected, and an open letter demanded the decision be revised (Hyperallergic). Giuli sent inspectors to examine the Biennale's management and check for sanctions breaches (The Art Newspaper).
- Jury: the awards jury said on 23 April it would not consider countries whose leaders face ICC charges, which would exclude Israel and Russia. After an Israeli pavilion artist filed a legal warning and the Biennale's legal department warned of personal liability, the jury resigned on 30 April (Hyperallergic).
- Strike: on 8 May at least 27 of 100 national pavilions were partly or fully closed in the first cultural strike in the Biennale's history, demanding Israel's exclusion (Hyperallergic, ArtAsiaPacific). The Arsenale was shut by late afternoon.
- Awards: more than 100 participants threatened legal action over remaining on the Visitor Lion ballot; the Biennale said their votes "will not be considered" (Hyperallergic, 3 June 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly Italian public funds, ticket income and a long list of private donors, one of whom, the Helis Foundation, Healthy Gulf links to the oil company Helis Oil & Gas. The documented weak points are the 2026 Russia decision, which cost it an EU grant, the jury's resignation, and the strike. Worth watching: the final 2024 and 2025 accounts, and whether sponsors react to the 2026 protests.
