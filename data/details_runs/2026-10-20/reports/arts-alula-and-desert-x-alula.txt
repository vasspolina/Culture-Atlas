ARTS ALULA AND DESERT X ALULA: AlUla, Saudi Arabia
ID: arts-alula-and-desert-x-alula
Website: https://desertx.org (checked 2026-10-20; no separate Arts AlUla site was found; the visitor site experiencealula.com was read for Wadi AlFann and the 2024 edition)

OVERVIEW
- Arts AlUla sits within the Royal Commission for AlUla (RCU) (Desert X); Desert X AlUla is the biennial held in AlUla in north-west Saudi Arabia as a collaboration between the RCU and the California organisation Desert X (Hyperallergic).
- The RCU was set up in 2017 with administrative and budgeting independence; Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman chairs its board (Saudipedia).
- Desert X AlUla opened its first edition from 31 January to 7 March 2020, curated by Neville Wakefield with curators Raneem Farsi and Aya Alireza, as part of Mohammed bin Salman's economic, cultural and social reform plan (Hyperallergic).
- The third edition (9 February to 23 March 2024) showed 16 works and was free (Qantara, Experience AlUla); the fourth, "Space Without Measure", ran from 15 January to 28 February 2026 with 11 artists, curated by Wejdan Reda and Zoé Whitley (Desert X).
- Desert X AlUla is a pre-opening programme for Wadi AlFann, "Valley of the Arts", a permanent land art site not yet open to the public, due to unveil from 2028 (Desert X, Experience AlUla).
- Visiting: exhibitions run in winter as part of the AlUla Arts Festival; no reporting found of accessibility provision.

FUNDING
Arts AlUla and Desert X AlUla: state-funded: the Saudi Royal Commission for AlUla, produced with a US non-profit
- Desert X AlUla is a collaboration between the RCU and Desert X (Hyperallergic); Desert X is produced by The Desert Biennial, a 501(c)(3) charity in Palm Springs (Desert X, ProPublica).
- Desert X says it is funded by its board, individual donors, foundations and sponsors, and names none for AlUla (Desert X).
- The Desert Biennial's programme service revenue was $2,313,589 in the year to June 2022 and $2,461,308 in the year to June 2024, against $27,400 (2023) and $176,571 (2025); the filing summary does not name the payer (ProPublica, Form 990).
- No reporting found of the RCU's arts budget or of what it pays Desert X.

Red flags: Saudi state patronage under Mohammed bin Salman
- The biennial is a collaboration with a government agency whose board the crown prince chairs (Saudipedia, Hyperallergic).
- In 2021 Palm Springs mayor Christy Holstege cited the 2018 killing of journalist Jamal Khashoggi when the city refused to sponsor Desert X (ArtReview).

Red flags: resignations, a donor exit and a city boycott, 2019 to 2021
- In October 2019 board members Ed Ruscha, Yael Lipschutz and Tristan Milanovich resigned over the Saudi partnership, citing human rights abuses and the Khashoggi killing; Lipschutz called it "completely unethical" (Hyperallergic).
- The MaddocksBrown Foundation, which had given about $13,000 since 2017, withdrew, including $5,000 for 2021 (Hyperallergic).
- In February 2021 Palm Springs declined to sponsor Desert X 2021 and pledged $30,000 for the commission and installation of six sculptures by artist Christopher Myers instead (ArtReview).
- Founder Susan Davis answered that "it is better to engage than to isolate" (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sharp criticism in 2019 to 2021, centred on the resignations and the Palm Springs boycott; later coverage is mostly accepting.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Three Desert X Board Members Resign in Protest of Saudi Collaboration" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/521537/three-desert-x-board-members-resign-in-protest-of-saudi-collaboration/
- Lipschutz: the project is about "striking a deal with a national government that has committed a horrific genocide in Yemen", not artists' dialogue.
- Lipschutz also calls the Saudi government undemocratic, with an appalling record of discrimination against the LGBTQ community; the RCU had not responded to Hyperallergic's request for comment.
2. ArtReview: "Palm Springs boycotts Desert X after Saudi Arabia edition" (2021)
URL: https://artreview.com/palm-springs-boycotts-desert-x-after-saudi-arabia-edition/
- Reports the mayor's call on the organisers to "stop partnering with human rights abusers".
- Notes a backlash from Desert X supporters who valued the free exhibition.
3. Qantara: Naima Morelli, "Desert X AlUla 2024: 'The desert is demanding new ways of seeing'" (2024)
URL: https://qantara.de/en/article/desert-x-alula-2024-desert-demanding-new-ways-seeing
- Says the Western art world is accepting compromises with a state whose laws and rights "decisively" do not align with its own.
- Mostly favourable; Rebecca Ann Proctor says she heard of no controversy around the 2024 edition.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Saudi state money, run through an agency the crown prince chairs, with a US charity as producer. The weak points are that patronage, documented resignations and a city boycott, and no published figure for what the RCU pays. Worth watching: Wadi AlFann's opening and whether Desert X discloses its Saudi income.
