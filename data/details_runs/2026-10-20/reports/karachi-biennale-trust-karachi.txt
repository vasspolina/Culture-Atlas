KARACHI BIENNALE TRUST: Karachi, Pakistan
ID: karachi-biennale-trust-karachi
Website: https://karachibiennale.org.pk/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads; announces that the KB27 edition is cancelled)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit that stages the Karachi Biennale, a free citywide art event held in public venues in Karachi (KB22: eight public venues including heritage sites, free entry, per APP).
- Founded in 2016 as the Karachi Biennale Trust by a group of art professionals and educators; Niilofur Farrukh is listed as co-founder and was CEO through 2025 (the Trust's founders page). The first edition, KB17, was curated by trustee Amin Gulgee.
- The Trust's site names KB17, KB19, KB22 and KB24. KB19's theme was ecology and the environment (Global Voices); KB22 focused on art and technology, curated by Faisal Anwar, for Pakistan's 75th anniversary (APP). The Trust cancelled KB27 "on account of the war conditions prevailing in the region" (stated by the Trust).
- Ten trustees are listed, led by Shanaz Ramzi (Managing Trustee) and Atteqa Malik (Chairperson).
- No reporting found of an annual report or accounts.
- On 27 October 2019, at KB19, plainclothes men ordered Adeela Suleman's "Killing Fields of Karachi", 444 tombstones for the victims of killings of which former police officer Rao Anwar is accused, closed; part was sealed and the rest vandalised. The organisers said the work, "despite the artist's perspective", was "not compatible with the ethos of KB19" and that they were against censorship (Global Voices, Dawn).
- No source found shows that the Sindh government or any funder gave the order: the men said they were from a Pakistani intelligence agency (Global Voices), and Dawn named no agency or person.

FUNDING
Karachi Biennale Trust: corporate-sponsored: Pakistani companies, the Sindh culture department and foreign cultural institutes
- KB22: Engro Corporation was lead partner and contributed Rs. 75 million (Associated Press of Pakistan, June 2022).
- KB24 logos on the Trust's site, with no company named in text (names here are read from logo file names): Gold, Bank Alfalah, HBL and Neo Color; Bronze, Image, JS Bank, Ali Associates, Saga Foundation and Jubilee; Energy Partner, file "ke-logo", which the atlas reads as K-Electric.
- The cancelled KB27 page thanks National Foods, EBM, Bank Alfalah, JS Bank, Vertika, Greaves, Dalda and Khaadi, and lists the Culture Department of the Government of Sindh, British Council, Goethe-Institut and Pro Helvetia as venue partners (stated by the Trust).
- No reporting found of a figure for any sponsor other than Engro, or of total budget.

Red flags: fossil fuels, Engro and K-Electric
- Engro: lead partner of KB22 (Rs. 75 million). Wikipedia lists the Thar Engro plant as a 660 MW lignite plant owned by Engro Powergen Thar Ltd, fed by a mine run by Sindh Engro Coal Mining Company, a joint venture of the Sindh government and Engro Corporation.
- K-Electric: the Trust's page does not spell out the name of its KB24 Energy Partner. Wikipedia lists K-Electric's plants as steam, combined-cycle and gas-fired units at Bin Qasim and Korangi.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2019 closure and the organisers' statement. No reporting found of art-press criticism aimed at the sponsors Engro or K-Electric.
1. Global Voices: Quratulain Tejani, "Pakistan shuts art installation depicting extrajudicial killings" (2019)
URL: https://globalvoices.org/2019/10/30/pakistan-shuts-art-installation-depicting-extrajudicial-killings/
- Reports that organisers backed the closure in a statement, and records the response: Asad Hashim accused them of endorsing censorship; lawyer Mohammad Jibran Nasir spoke in a live video from the venue and later held a press conference; Brad Adams of Human Rights Watch called the closure an attack on free expression.
- Names the Biennale organisers; not a review, a news report with named critics.
2. Dawn: Nimra Khan, "Discourse: Censorship in Art" (2020)
URL: https://www.dawn.com/news/1563415/discourse-censorship-in-art
- Argues censorship in Pakistani art breeds self-censorship and that art institutions must stand up to oppressive systems.
- Quotes Niilofur Farrukh, as Trust CEO, saying before KB19 that the guidelines came from "what the public wants" and that inclusion, not censorship, was the issue.
3. Dawn: report on the organisers' statement (2019)
URL: https://www.dawn.com/news/1513412/sealed-exhibit-on-extrajudicial-killings-not-compatible-with-ethos-of-karachi-biennale-say-organisers
- Records the statement and criticism of it: Reem Khurshid called it a "craven statement" and Aiman Rizvi called it "repulsive". A news report.

BOTTOM LINE
The Biennale runs on corporate and institutional sponsorship with no money from a named founder or family found. The weak points are two fossil-fuel sponsors, Engro (KB22) and, on the reading of a logo, K-Electric (KB24), and the 2019 closure of an installation at KB19, which the organisers backed; no source shows a funder or the Sindh government ordered it. Worth watching: whether the Trust publishes accounts and how it re-launches after cancelling KB27.
