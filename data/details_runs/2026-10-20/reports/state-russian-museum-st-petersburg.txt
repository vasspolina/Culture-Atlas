STATE RUSSIAN MUSEUM: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: state-russian-museum-st-petersburg
Website: https://rusmuseum.ru (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of Russian art in the historic centre of St Petersburg, with more than 450,000 items from the 10th to the 21st century (stated by the museum).
- Founded by decision of Alexander III; Nicholas II signed the decree in 1895 and the museum opened on 19 March 1898 (7 March, old style) as the Russian Museum of Emperor Alexander III (stated by the museum).
- The main building is the Mikhailovsky Palace, built in 1819 to 1825 by Carlo Rossi as the residence of Grand Duke Michael Pavlovich; Vasily Svinyin rebuilt the interiors for the museum (Wikipedia, the museum).
- The complex also includes the Benois Building, the Marble Palace, the Stroganov Palace, St Michael's Castle, Peter I's Summer Palace and two gardens (stated by the museum).
- Directors: Vladimir Gusev from 1988 to 2023, then Alla Manilova since 25 April 2023 (Wikipedia). Gusev became the museum's president.
- Visitors: over 3.6 million in 2024 (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
State Russian Museum: state-run, with the US-sanctioned state-owned bank VTB as general sponsor
- State museum under Russian federal control; no reporting found of its budget, annual report or accounts.
- VTB Bank is general sponsor and has been general partner since 2024, supporting exhibitions of Konchalovsky, Surikov, Grabar, Repin, Petrov-Vodkin, Vereshchagin, Bryullov and "Our Avant-garde", and the Summer Garden Day (stated by the museum).
- No amount is given. Three Russian state bodies held over 90 percent of VTB's capital as of February 2022 (Wikipedia).
- The sponsors page names only VTB in text; no other sponsor is named on it.

Red flags: sanctioned general sponsor
- VTB Bank is the museum's general sponsor (rusmuseum.ru). The US Treasury imposed full blocking sanctions on VTB Bank on 24 February 2022 (US Treasury, 24 February 2022).
- Earlier listings per Wikipedia: US sectoral sanctions on 29 July 2014, the EU on 31 July 2014, the UK on 1 August 2014.

Red flags: state censorship of an exhibition
- July 2023: Meduza reported on 19 July that the museum had cancelled the already installed Timur Novikov show "Klin Novikova". Artist Dmitry Pilikin and art historian Olga Khoroshilova said in social media posts that director Alla Manilova ordered it. The museum's press service said the first version broke technical regulations and held too few Novikov works from the museum's own collection (The Art Newspaper, Moscow Times).
- The show opened on 26 July with works swapped or covered. Curator Alexander Borovsky said the museum should not conceal works to "protect us from idiots": "We chose to conceal" (The Moscow Times). Konstantin Goncharov's velvet donkey penis was covered; Olga Tobreluts's "Adam and Eve" was taken down after a classical sculpture print in it showed a penis (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: museum employees told Sever.Realii the ban cited pornographic content, reported by Meduza, which did not confirm it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism of the institution; the main case is the 2023 Novikov censorship.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "'Cancelled' St Petersburg show honouring Timur Novikov finally opens, with censorship" (2023)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2023/08/15/st-petersburg-show-honouring-timur-novikov-finally-openswith-censorship
- The partial censorship stoked fears that cultural censorship in Russia is intensifying since the invasion of Ukraine; former colleagues of Novikov said their works were censored, "especially to obscure male genitalia".
- The museum cited technical regulations. Describes Manilova as a Kremlin-connected functionary and former deputy culture minister; she declined to comment.
2. Novaya Gazeta: editorial staff (byline Ivan Sidorov), "Zhenshchina-prazdnik na poroge Russkogo muzeya" (2023)
URL: https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2023/04/24/zhenshchina-prazdnik-na-poroge-russkogo-muzeia
- News outlet, not art press; written before her appointment. Anonymous museum staff say she is not an art historian or museum specialist and call her candidacy "the worst of the possible" (translation).
- Unverified: allegations about city festival contracts she oversaw, from Novaya Gazeta, citing documents, a former ministry employee's court testimony and anonymous sources; Manilova is not quoted.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Canada added Manilova, named director in April 2023, to its sanctions list in August 2023, when the amendment appeared in the Canada Gazette; the rationale is that Russia uses cultural figures to promote Kremlin propaganda about the invasion of Ukraine. The article does not say the museum was sanctioned (The Art Newspaper, 31 August 2023).
- Connection: Manilova directs the state museum; she is not shown to fund it.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-run and its only named sponsor is VTB, a state-owned bank on the US blocking list since 24 February 2022. The 2023 Novikov episode is documented censorship at a state museum. The museum publishes no accounts or board, so the scale of state and sponsor money cannot be checked.
