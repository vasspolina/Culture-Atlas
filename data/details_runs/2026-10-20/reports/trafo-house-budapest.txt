TRAFÓ HOUSE OF CONTEMPORARY ARTS: Budapest, Hungary
ID: trafo-house-budapest
Website: https://trafo.hu

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts house at Liliom utca 41 (1094 Budapest), run by the nonprofit company Trafó Kortárs Művészetek Háza Nonprofit Kft. Its programme covers theatre, dance, music, performance, film and workshops, and it has a gallery, a studio and a café (Trafó website).
- The building is a former south Pest transformer station built in 1909, which Hungarian Wikipedia calls a characteristic example of industrial Art Nouveau.
- In 1991 a French anarchist artist group, Resonance, staged performances and concerts in the vacant building; it became an institution in 1998, opening at the Budapest Autumn Festival (Hungarian Wikipedia).
- The Budapest municipal government bought the building with money left over from a world exhibition that was never held, to make a contemporary arts centre succeeding the Fiatal Művészek Klubja on Andrássy út (Hungarian Wikipedia).
- Directors: György Szabó (1998 to 2012), József Nagy (2012 to 2017), Beáta Barda (2017 to 2024), Katalin Erdődi (2025 to 2026) (Hungarian Wikipedia). The Budapest Municipal Assembly chose Erdődi in December 2023 on the recommendation of an expert committee, for a term from 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2029 (444.hu, Tamás Botos, 13 December 2023). Hungarian Wikipedia cites a Papageno report of 26 June 2026 that she is leaving.
- The Workshop Foundation (Műhely Alapítvány), founded in 1992, moved to Trafó in 1998, shares office and technical equipment with it and is responsible for the schedule and dance classes in the Trafó Studio (Workshop Foundation page on trafo.hu).
- Visiting: the gallery is open Tuesday to Sunday 16:00 to 19:00 (to 22:00 on performance days) and closed Mondays (Trafó website).

FUNDING
Trafó House of Contemporary Arts: municipal: maintained by the Budapest city government
- The Trafó website says the house operates "under the maintenance of" the Budapest City Government (translation of "Budapest Főváros Önkormányzata fenntartásában").
- The municipality bought the building and Trafó opened in 1998 (Hungarian Wikipedia).
- Managing director György Szabó said in an interview published on 29 December 2014 that Trafó receives state funding, which gives it a known annual budget, "a better situation than the independent theatres" (Anna Frenyó, Heinrich Böll Stiftung).

BOTTOM LINE
Trafó is a city-run arts house: the Budapest municipality bought the building, maintains the house and appoints its executive director through the Municipal Assembly. Its managing director said in 2014 that the house receives state funding with a known annual budget (Heinrich Böll Stiftung).
