ZKM CENTER FOR ART AND MEDIA: Karlsruhe, Germany
ID: zkm-center-for-art-and-media-karlsruhe
Website: https://zkm.de (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum, research and production centre for media art at Lorenzstraße 19, Karlsruhe, with about 15,000 square metres of exhibition space (Wikipedia, German edition).
- Founded in 1989 by the City of Karlsruhe and the State of Baden-Württemberg as a public-law foundation, and opened on 18 October 1997. Until March 2016 it was called the Zentrum für Kunst und Medientechnologie (Wikipedia, German edition).
- Directors: Heinrich Klotz was founding director; Peter Weibel led it from 1 January 1999 until his death on 1 March 2023; Alistair Hudson has been artistic-scientific director since 1 April 2023, with Holger Neinhaus as managing director (Wikipedia, German edition).
- Building: the former munitions factory Hallenbau A, built 1914 to 1918 by Philipp Jakob Manz and converted to designs by Schweger + Partner from 1993. Rem Koolhaas's competition design for a new building was dropped in 1992 (Wikipedia, German edition).
- On site: the Medienmuseum, the Museum für Neue Kunst, which ran to 2016, a Hertz-Labor, a library shared with the Karlsruhe University of Arts and Design (HfG), and exhibition space. It drew 260,000 visitors a year in 2018 (Wikipedia, German edition).
- Visiting: open 11am to 6pm on the day checked; the visit page lists no prices and offers reduced admission to holders of a bwtarif ticket, JugendticketBW or Deutschland-Ticket. Lifts serve the foyer and each atrium, the foyer toilets are wheelchair accessible, assistance dogs are allowed and accessible tours are offered.

FUNDING
ZKM: mainly public: State of Baden-Württemberg and City of Karlsruhe, with EnBW as long-term sponsor and many project foundations
- Basic funding is split equally between the City of Karlsruhe and the State of Baden-Württemberg (Wikipedia, German edition). The ZKM partners page lists both as founders.
- EnBW Energie Baden-Württemberg: listed on the ZKM partners page as "Partner". A joint EnBW and ZKM press release of 28 March 2022 says EnBW has been the ZKM's main sponsor for more than 15 years and, since 2017, has hosted smaller versions ("satellites") of the ZKM's major thematic exhibitions in the foyer of its headquarters at Durlacher Allee 93, Karlsruhe, with free admission. EnBW's earlier release names its first project under the partnership as the exhibition "Lichtkunst aus Kunstlicht". No amounts are published.
- Project sponsors named on the partners page: BBBank and dm.
- Project funders named on the partners page: Creative Europe, the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and Media, the German Federal Cultural Foundation, the Cultural Foundation of the German Federal States, Baden-Württemberg Foundation, Goethe-Institut, Wüstenrot Stiftung, Ernst von Siemens Kulturstiftung, Robert Rauschenberg Foundation, Schroff Stiftungen, SANNI Foundation, LBBW Foundation, E.ON Foundation, Volkswagen Foundation, Kulturstiftung der Sparkasse Karlsruhe, Arts Council Korea, Musikfonds and the Fördergesellschaft ZKM/HfG. The page gives no years or amounts.
- E.ON Foundation: a non-profit company owned by E.ON SE, with capital of 45.5 million euros in 2019 and annual spending of about 3 million euros (German Wikipedia, which notes parts are out of date since 2019). No reporting found of which ZKM project it funded or how much.
- No annual report or accounts were found on the ZKM site.

Red flags: EnBW, a fossil-fuel sponsor
- EnBW's coal plants include Altbach, Heilbronn, Walheim, Stuttgart-Münster and the Rheinhafen steam power station in Karlsruhe, which also has a gas turbine; in March 2023 EnBW announced it intends to end coal use by 2028 (Wikipedia). It also runs gas-fired plants.
- EnBW is owned in equal 46.75% stakes by a company wholly owned by the State of Baden-Württemberg and by the municipal group OEW (Wikipedia). The State is both a founder of the ZKM and, through that company, a co-owner of EnBW.
- The sponsorship is current: EnBW hosted a ZKM satellite show in spring 2022 and the ZKM partners page still lists it.
- No reporting found of a protest or campaign aimed at EnBW's sponsorship of the ZKM.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The ZKM is mostly funded by the state and the city, with a long-running sponsor, EnBW, which operates coal and gas plants and is half owned by the state. The weak points are the sponsor's fossil fuels, the undisclosed amounts of every sponsor and foundation grant, and the absence of published accounts. Watch whether EnBW's coal exit goes ahead by 2028 and what the E.ON Foundation funds.
