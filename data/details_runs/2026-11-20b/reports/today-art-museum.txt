TODAY ART MUSEUM: Beijing, China
ID: today-art-museum
Website: http://www.todayartmuseum.com/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum (今日美术馆) at Building 4, Pingguo (Pingod) Community, 32 Baiziwan Road, Chaoyang District, near the Beijing CBD (Visit Beijing; museum website).
- The property developer Zhang Baoquan (张宝全) founded it in 2002 and was its first director; it calls itself the first non-profit, non-governmental art museum in China (Visit Beijing; Beijing Daily, 10 May 2012).
- Architect Wang Hui led the design and rebuilding of the five-floor main building, which fuses industrial relics with new work, with 2,500 square metres of exhibition space and a 1,000 square metre main hall with 13.5 metre walls; Wang Jianwei's work "Viewing the Exhibition" sits on the roof (Visit Beijing).
- By 2012 it held more than 5,000 works in two stores, one of about 600 square metres on site and one of 2,000 square metres in north-west Beijing (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- Zhang Ran stepped down as director on 26 March 2025 at the end of a five-year term; deputy director Yan Yan (晏燕) became executive director, and the board, chaired by Zhang Baoquan, planned a worldwide search for a new director (The Paper, Paike user channel, March 2025).
- Recent and current shows include the 13th International Fiber Art Biennale "From Lausanne to Beijing", a touring show of the Bologna illustration exhibition and an immersive Cezanne show (museum website).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays (museum website); free on the first Saturday of each month and on 18 May (Visit Beijing).

FUNDING
Today Art Museum: private non-profit: founded on a property developer's money, now run on sponsorship and earned income
- For its first five years Zhang Baoquan kept the museum running with an average of about 10 million yuan a year of his own money (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- In July 2006 it became the first private museum registered with the Beijing Civil Affairs Bureau as a private non-enterprise unit (民办非企业单位); if it closed, its collection would pass to the bureau, not back to its investors (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- Sponsorship reached 30 million yuan in 2010, and sponsorship plus shop and other income reached 40 million yuan a year by 2012; most exhibitions had a sponsor, and joint-venture companies were the readiest sponsors (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- An early sponsor was the German company Montblanc, whose chairman agreed to fund the museum after a 2006 Fang Lijun show (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- The museum runs eight funds, among them building, exhibition, education and collection funds, and a friends' scheme; its site takes applications to hold exhibitions there (Beijing Daily, 2012; museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
A private non-profit built on a developer's money and kept going by corporate sponsors, shows held on application and shop income. Current sponsors are named only as logos, so who pays now is the gap to watch.
