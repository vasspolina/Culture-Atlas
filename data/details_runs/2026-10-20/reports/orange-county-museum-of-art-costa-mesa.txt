ORANGE COUNTY MUSEUM OF ART: Costa Mesa, USA
ID: orange-county-museum-of-art-costa-mesa
Website: https://ocma.art (redirects to https://langson.uci.edu)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at the Segerstrom Center for the Arts, 3333 Avenue of the Arts, Costa Mesa; since 29 September 2025 the UC Irvine Langson Orange County Museum of Art, run by the University of California, Irvine (UC Irvine).
- Founded in 1962 as the Fine Arts Patrons Pavilion Gallery at the Balboa Pavilion by 13 women. By 1968 it was the Newport Harbor Art Museum, it opened a 23,000 square foot building in Newport Beach in 1977, and it took the name Orange County Museum of Art after a merger completed in July 1996 with Laguna Art Museum (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Morphosis, 53,000 square feet, cost $98 million (UC Irvine); ground was broken in September 2019 and it opened on 8 October 2022 (Wikipedia).
- The combined UC Irvine Langson Orange County Museum of Art holds over 9,000 works, about 4,500 from each of OCMA and UC Irvine's Jack and Shanaz Langson Institute and Museum of California Art. OCMA staff became UC Irvine employees (UC Irvine) and OCMA's board was dissolved (Hyperallergic).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 18:00; admission is free without reservation (museum website).

FUNDING
Orange County Museum of Art: private donors until 2025: the Segerstrom family and other trustees; now a unit of the public University of California, Irvine
- Anton Segerstrom, a South Coast Plaza partner, and his wife Jennifer gave $8 million to the new building; the OC Register calls him OCMA's largest single donor and a longtime trustee (Samantha Dunn, 2 October 2022).
- The Segerstrom family and South Coast Plaza partners donated the land for the Segerstrom Center for the Arts, including the site of the new OCMA. The Avenue of the Arts Gallery is named in memory of Yvonne de C Segerstrom (OC Register, 2022).
- In 2006 Anton Segerstrom helped create a fund for an architectural search and was the largest of four donors who included Weiss, PIMCO co-founder Jim Muzzy and James B. Pick (OC Register, 2022).
- Moti Ferder of Lugano Diamonds, Newport Beach, gave $2.5 million in 2022 to establish free admission for 10 years (Hyperallergic, Wikipedia). An OCMA representative told the Los Angeles Times the pledge is current (Hyperallergic, 1 October 2025).
- The Irvine Company gave $1 million in 1985 to underwrite exhibitions for a decade (Wikipedia).
- Form 990 figures: contributions of $6.73 million and expenses of $13.76 million in 2024, against contributions of $7.47 million and expenses of $13.20 million in 2023 (ProPublica).
- UC Irvine took on the museum on 29 September 2025; gifts are processed through UC Irvine's giving site. Friends memberships start at $100 and giving circles run from $5,000 to $25,000 (UC Irvine). The museum's new name recognises Jack and Shanaz Langson.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the museum's recent years is news reporting on governance changes and the university takeover, not criticism of the art.
1. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "UC Irvine Takes Over Orange County Museum of Art" (1 October 2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1045916/uc-irvine-takes-over-orange-county-museum-of-art/
- Places the takeover after a "rocky period" of leadership shakeups and a financial scandal involving a major donor: the Los Angeles Times described an atmosphere of unrest, and in summer 2024 14 trustees left the board and 10 new appointees replaced them.
- Reports the Ferder accusations and that CEO and Director Heidi Zuckerman would step down at the end of 2025.
- Not criticism of the art.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Mordechai "Moti" Ferder, the Lugano Diamonds head named by Hyperallergic as the donor of the $2.5 million free-admission gift, was accused of fraud, civil theft and other financial crimes in several lawsuits, and resigned as CEO of Lugano Diamonds and Jewelry in May 2025 (Matt Stromberg, Hyperallergic, 1 October 2025).
- Connection: Hyperallergic reports that Ferder's 2022 donation established free admission at OCMA for 10 years, and an OCMA representative said in 2025 that the pledge is current.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum was private until 2025: the Segerstrom family and South Coast Plaza partners gave the land and millions of dollars, with other trustees and Moti Ferder's $2.5 million gift for free admission. It is now part of the University of California, Irvine. Ferder, the donor of the free-admission gift, was accused of fraud in lawsuits reported in 2025.
