MUSEUM LUDWIG: Cologne, Germany
ID: museum-ludwig
Website: https://www.museum-ludwig.de (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art between Cologne Cathedral and the Rhine, in a building it shares with the Kölner Philharmonie.
- Founded by a donation contract signed on 23 February 1976 by Peter and Irene Ludwig and the City of Cologne (museum website); the city undertook to build a museum for works made after 1900. It emerged from the Wallraf-Richartz Museum (Wikipedia).
- Named for Peter Ludwig, a chocolate manufacturer and collector, who joined the firm Leonard Monheim, later renamed Ludwig Schokolade, and built it into one of Germany's leading chocolate businesses (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Peter Busmann and Godfrid Haberer and opened in 1986; since 1994 the museum has had the Bischofsgartenstrasse building to itself (Wikipedia).
- Collection: it combines Josef Haubrich's collection of 1914 to 1939, given to Cologne in 1946, with the Ludwig collection, and holds around 900 Picasso works (Wikipedia).
- The director is Yilmaz Dziewior, who took over in February 2015 (Kaput interview, 2015).
- Visiting: the museum site showed hours of 10:00 to 18:00 on the day checked. No admission prices are listed on the pages read. The site links an accessibility statement, whose contents were not read.

FUNDING
Museum Ludwig: mainly public: the City of Cologne, with the Ludwig gift and support from friends' groups
- The museum is a museum of the City of Cologne ("Ein Museum der Stadt Köln" on its site). The city built the building in return for the 1976 Ludwig gift of 350 works (Wikipedia).
- The Peter and Irene Ludwig Foundation, the Friends of the Wallraf-Richartz-Museum and the Museum Ludwig, and the Gesellschaft für Moderne Kunst am Museum Ludwig are listed as supporters; the page gives no amounts or years.
- The Wolfgang Hahn Prize has a budget of at most 100,000 euros a year (Wikipedia).
- The "We Call It Ludwig" anniversary exhibition appears among projects of the Kulturstiftung des Bundes, the federal cultural foundation; no amount is given.
- The museum names partner categories (museum, exhibition and project partners) but no company on the pages read. No reporting found of a named corporate sponsor or of its budget.
- No reporting found of any current money flowing to the museum from Ludwig's chocolate firm.

Not related: the Ludwig Museum in Koblenz (named as a lender to a 2016 Kiefer show in China, The Art Newspaper, 17 November 2016) and the Ludwig Museum in Budapest, which holds Breker busts of Peter and Irene Ludwig on long-term loan from the Peter und Irene Ludwig Stiftung, Aachen (since 1991). They are separate institutions from the Cologne museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism of the museum. Coverage of its Russian avant-garde fakes research is the main material and is mostly appreciative; no review found makes a case against its funding.
1. The Art Newspaper: Courtney Tenz, "Original or fake? Museum Ludwig puts its Russian avant-garde art to the test" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/10/02/original-or-fake-museum-ludwig-puts-its-russian-avant-garde-art-to-the-test
- Reports that the museum re-examined 49 paintings from the Ludwig collection and found 22 falsely attributed.
- Reports that Galerie Gmurzynska, which sold around 400 paintings to the museum's benefactors, sued for advance access to the research; a regional court rejected the claim on 16 September 2020.
- Does not criticise the museum or the Ludwigs' purchases.
2. Apollo: Madeleine Schwartz, "What a sham! On fakery and the Russian avant-garde" (December 2020 issue; online 16 January 2021)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=955951
- Argues that the flow of avant-garde works into the Western market left a gap that forgers filled, and calls the dealers' defence of a disputed Redko painting "dubious".
- Says Gmurzynska sold the museum hundreds of works, some now judged falsely attributed.
- Says the curators refuse to call any painting definitively fake, and in the same passage notes Galerie Gmurzynska's lawsuit over the exhibition research; ends in scepticism about reaching a conclusion.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 1986 Peter Ludwig sat for busts by the sculptor Arno Breker, whom the Ludwig Museum Budapest describes as "Hitler's favorite sculptor". Wikipedia reports "fierce public criticism" of the commission, and the Budapest museum says the outcry in West Germany concerned Breker's past.
- The connection to Museum Ludwig is that the commission was made by its founding donor.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Cologne city money and a founding gift from a chocolate manufacturer and his wife, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no accounts, board or dated sponsor list are published on the pages read, and that the 2020 research found 22 of 49 examined avant-garde paintings falsely attributed; Galerie Gmurzynska, which sold the Ludwigs around 400 paintings, supplied some of them. Worth watching are the partner categories, which name no companies.
