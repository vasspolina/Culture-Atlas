WHITNEY MUSEUM OF AMERICAN ART: New York, USA
ID: whitney-museum-of-american-art
Website: https://whitney.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of American art at 99 Gansevoort Street in the Meatpacking District, Manhattan.
- Founded in 1930 by Gertrude Vanderbilt Whitney, who started it after the Met declined her donation of over 500 American works; it opened on 18 November 1931 in three converted row houses on West 8th Street, with Juliana Force as first director (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder.
- The current building, by Renzo Piano, opened on 1 May 2015 and cost about $422 million; the museum was at 945 Madison Avenue, in a Marcel Breuer building, from 1966 to 2014 (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include the Whitney Biennial and the Hyundai Terrace Commission on the fifth-floor terrace.
- Visiting: open 10:30 am to 6 pm Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday, to 10 pm Friday, closed Tuesday. Adults $30, seniors and students $24, 25 and under and members free; free Friday nights from 5 pm and free second Sundays. Ramps, elevators and free wheelchairs.

FUNDING
Whitney Museum of American Art: privately funded: trustee and donor giving, earned income and corporate sponsors including Hyundai Motor
- Form 990 data (ProPublica): fiscal year to June 2025, revenue $191,356,000, contributions $50,977,000, expenses $96,002,000, net assets $1,027,457,000; fiscal 2024 revenue $116,740,000, contributions $54,217,000.
- Hyundai Motor: a 10-year partnership announced 6 February 2024 covering the Whitney Biennial, where it is presenting partner, and the Hyundai Terrace Commission. No amount is disclosed (Hyundai).
- 2026 Biennial: sponsor Fondazione Bvlgari; Hyundai Motor as multiyear partner; leadership support from David Cancel and from Stephanie March and Dan Benton; significant support from Sotheby's; Biennial endowments from Emily Fisher Landau, Leonard A. Lauder and Fern and Lenard Tessler (stated by the Whitney).
- Leonard A. Lauder's 2008 gift of $131 million was the largest in the museum's history (Wikipedia).
- No page opened here confirms Tiffany & Co. or Genesis as current funders.

Red flags: arms and defence trustees
- Kanders: vice chairman Warren Kanders was chairman, chief executive and majority owner of Safariland, which makes tear gas; he resigned on 25 July 2019 after weekly protests by Decolonize This Place from March 2019, a staff letter in November 2018 and an open letter from over 100 artists, critics and scholars (The Art Newspaper).
- Biennial: eight artists withdrew work from the 2019 Biennial, citing the museum's failure to respond meaningfully (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).
- Crown: on 23 May 2025 about 50 activists occupied the lobby, naming trustee Nancy Carrington Crown, whose family members are major shareholders of General Dynamics, and sponsor Hyundai Motors, and calling the museum "artwashing genocide". The Whitney did not respond (Hyperallergic).
- Cancelled performance: the protest followed the museum's cancelling of a 14 May 2025 performance on Palestinian mourning organised by the Independent Study Program (Hyperallergic).
- Unverified: activists accuse General Dynamics and Hyundai of ties to the Israeli military, from the protesters' own pamphlets, reported by Hyperallergic.

WIDER CONTEXT
- David A. Ross, Whitney director from 1991 to 1998, resigned from the School of Visual Arts in February 2026 after the Epstein files showed continued contact with Epstein from 2009 to 2016 (The Art Newspaper). The article says he sought donors at the Whitney, including Epstein; no Whitney statement is reported and no gift to the Whitney is stated.

BOTTOM LINE
The Whitney is privately funded, with a billion-dollar balance sheet and named sponsors. Its weak points are trustees and sponsors tied to arms companies: the Kanders campaign that cost the museum a vice chair and eight Biennial artists in 2019, and the 2025 protest over the Crown family and Hyundai. Worth watching: Hyundai's 10-year partnership and any Whitney statement on Ross.
