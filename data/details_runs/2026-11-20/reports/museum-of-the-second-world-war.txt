MUSEUM OF THE SECOND WORLD WAR: Gdańsk, Poland
ID: museum-of-the-second-world-war
Website: https://muzeum1939.pl (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State history museum on Plac Władysława Bartoszewskiego 1 in Gdańsk, with a branch, the Museum of Westerplatte and the War of 1939 (museum BIP; NIK).
- Created on 1 September 2008 by regulation of the Minister of Culture as the Westerplatte Museum, on Prime Minister Donald Tusk's decision, with Paweł Machcewicz as his representative; renamed on 26 November 2008 (Wikipedia).
- The Kwadrat studio of Gdynia won the building competition in 2010; the building has about 23,000 square metres and a 40 metre leaning glass tower, and opened on 23 March 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Director: Rafał Wnuk (museum BIP). Culture minister Hanna Wróblewska appointed a new museum council on 2 July 2024, including Barbara Engelking and Paweł Machcewicz (museum BIP).
- Wnuk told Interia the museum had 560,000 visitors in the year before the interview, its best year so far (Interia).
- The cornerstone of the Westerplatte branch building, by JAZ + Architekci, was laid on 25 April 2026 (National Geographic Polska).

FUNDING
Museum of the Second World War: mainly public: Polish Ministry of Culture and National Heritage
- A state cultural institution organised by the Minister of Culture, financed mainly by the ministry's operating subsidies, which pay for salaries, energy, security and upkeep, and by targeted grants (NIK audit).
- Planned income was PLN 42,537,000 in 2019, PLN 37,133,000 in 2020, PLN 40,770,000 in 2021 and PLN 40,709,000 in 2022 (NIK audit).
- For the Westerplatte branch the ministry signed targeted grants of PLN 26,982,000 in 2020 and PLN 21,429,500 in 2021 (NIK audit).
- The Westerplatte branch is planned at PLN 304,564,000 (National Geographic Polska, 2026), due to finish in 2029 to 2030 (Interia).

Red flags: 2017 merger and dismissal of director Paweł Machcewicz
- On 5 April 2017 the Supreme Administrative Court upheld culture minister Piotr Gliński's order merging the museum with the Museum of Westerplatte; Machcewicz was dismissed on 6 April and Karol Nawrocki became acting director (Museums Journal).
- Supporters of Machcewicz, a former adviser to Tusk, said the merger was a pretext to remove him (Museums Journal).

Red flags: 2017 to 2018 changes to the main exhibition
- In November 2017 a film on the long-term effects of the war was replaced with an animated film on 20th-century Polish history; some 500 academics signed an open letter calling the changes "barbaric" (The Art Newspaper).
- Four exhibition authors, Machcewicz, Piotr Majewski, Janusz Marszalec and Wnuk, sued. In October 2020 the Gdańsk Regional Court dismissed 26 of their 28 demands; on 17 April 2023 the Warsaw Court of Appeal dismissed their appeal (Ministry of Culture statement, 2023).

Red flags: 2024 removal of PiS-era additions
- In March 2024 the culture minister dismissed the PiS-appointed director Grzegorz Berendt; Wnuk was appointed in April (Notes from Poland).
- In June 2024 the museum removed PiS-era additions, including portraits of Maximilian Kolbe and Witold Pilecki, saying it was returning to the authors' original vision; defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz called the removal "unacceptable" (Notes from Poland).

Red flags: audit criticism of Westerplatte grants (NIK, 2019 to 2022)
- The Supreme Audit Office found the museum's finances properly run except for the planning and spending of state money on the Westerplatte branch: by mid-2022 only 12.7 per cent (PLN 6,146,600) of the sums planned for 2020 and 2021 had been spent (NIK audit).
- NIK judged the minister's carrying of 2021 funds into 2022 "nierzetelne" (unreliable; translation), formally lawful but a way around budget rules (NIK audit).

Red flags: 2025 freeze of Westerplatte investment funds
- On 4 April 2025 the culture minister froze PLN 82,915,000 granted to the museum for the Westerplatte programme in the 2025 budget, citing delays (Ministry of Culture answer to Sejm interpellation 10553, July 2025).

Red flags: 2025 attacks on the exhibition "Nasi chłopcy" (Our Boys)
- The Museum of Gdańsk organised the exhibition on Pomeranians conscripted into Hitler's army in collaboration with this museum and the Polish Academy of Sciences, in July 2025 (Notes from Poland).
- President Andrzej Duda called portraying Third Reich soldiers as "ours" a historical falsehood and a moral provocation; the culture ministry accused critics of spreading "disinformation" (Notes from Poland).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage centres on the 2017 government takeover and the rewriting of the exhibition.
1. The Art Newspaper: Julia Michalska, "Outcry over Polish government's changes to Second World War museum" (2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/outcry-over-polish-government-s-changes-to-second-world-war-museum
- Reports the academics' case: the original concept joined Poland's special role with a universal account of suffering, and the changes risk turning the museum into a propaganda institution.
- A museum spokesman answered that Polish taxpayers fund it and it should tell their story.
2. Museums Journal: Patrick Steel, "Polish government sacks Museum of Second World War director" (2017)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/news/2017/04/12042017-polish-government-sacks-museum-of-second-world-war-director/
- News report; carries Machcewicz's plan to defend the exhibition through copyright.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Karol Nawrocki, later head of the Institute of National Remembrance, was sworn in as President of Poland on 6 August 2025 (Notes from Poland).
- He was this museum's acting director from April 2017.

BOTTOM LINE
State money with no private sponsor; the weak point is political control, with each change of government changing the director and the exhibition. Worth watching: the delayed Westerplatte spending.
