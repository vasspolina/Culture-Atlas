NATIONAL HISTORY MUSEUM OF ROMANIA: Bucharest, Romania
ID: national-history-museum-of-romania
Website: https://www.mnir.ro (checked 2026-11-20; did not respond)

OVERVIEW
- Romania's national museum of history at 12 Calea Victoriei, Bucharest, in the former Palace of the Posts designed by Alexandru Săvulescu; it holds about 690,000 objects, among them the crowns of Queens Elisabeth and Mary and a full-size cast of Trajan's Column made in 1939 to 1943 (Ghidul Muzeelor, cIMeC).
- The Palace of the Posts was inaugurated in 1903 in the presence of King Carol I; the central post office worked there until 1970, and the museum opened there in 1972 (Digi24, 2024), on 8 May (Antena 3).
- The building covers 32,000 square metres (Digi24, 2024).
- Interim manager: Cornel Constantin Ilie (Digi24, 2025; Euronews Romania, 2026); Mihaela Simion is interim deputy director (Agerpres, 2026).
- The recovered Coțofenești helmet and two Dacian bracelets were shown at the museum until 3 May 2026; 2,612 people came over one weekend (Digi24, 2026).

FUNDING
National History Museum of Romania: mainly public: Romanian Ministry of Culture
- Culture minister Raluca Turcan announced in October 2024 a restoration of the building estimated at over €100 million, under a loan framework agreement between Romania and the Council of Europe Development Bank; the design contract was worth €1,208,424 before VAT (Digi24, 2024).
- For the stolen loan objects the insurer paid the museum €5,700,000 in August and September 2025, which the museum was to transfer to the state budget (Agerpres, 2025).

Red flags: Ceaușescu personality cult exhibition (before 1989)
- Before 1989 the museum gave ten rooms covering several thousand square metres to the exhibition of gifts to Nicolae and Elena Ceaușescu; it ended when the museum was rearranged after 1989 (Radio Romania International, 2020).

Red flags: 2025 theft of loaned treasures from the Drents Museum
- On the night of 24 to 25 January 2025 thieves blew open a door of the Drents Museum in Assen, Netherlands, and took the gold helmet of Coțofenești and three gold armbands, part of a 670-piece loan from the museum, valued together at almost €6 million (The Art Newspaper; Agerpres).
- The recovery of the helmet and two armbands was announced on 2 April 2026, under a deal Dutch prosecutors reached with three men arrested after the theft; one armband was still missing (PBS NewsHour).

Red flags: 2025 dismissal of director Ernest Oberländer-Târnoveanu
- Culture minister Natalia Intotero dismissed the director after he refused to resign over the theft; Prime Minister Marcel Ciolacu had urged decisive measures (Brussels Times, 29 January 2025).
- The government said the objects left the country without proper permissions; the director said he had not broken any laws (Brussels Times).

Red flags: decay of the building and stalled restoration (2025)
- In December 2025 only the ground floor, about 20 per cent of capacity, was open; former first-floor halls served as stores, water came through the first-floor ceiling and the heating did not work (Digi24, 2025).
- The building was described as in a "jalnică" (pitiful; translation) state, with exhibits kept in corridors and in poor storage; Cristiana Tătaru, head of the numismatic and historic treasury department, said only whitewashing and cleaning are allowed because it is a listed monument (Digi24, December 2025).
- In December 2025 the rehabilitation was stalled at the stage of the documentation for approving the works (Digi24, 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is about the 2025 theft and the loan; criticism of security there is aimed at the Dutch museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Senay Boztas, "Romanian museum considers legal action after 'priceless' golden objects stolen in Netherlands heist" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/01/28/romanian-museum-considers-legal-action-priceless-golden-objects-stolen-netherlands-heist
- Reports that the Romanian museum threatened legal action, according to RTV Drenthe, and that the theft strained relations between Romania and the Netherlands.
- Quotes director Oberländer-Târnoveanu: the objects are so important that they cannot be sold.
- News report, not criticism of the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean state money. The weak points lie in the state's own care of the museum, its building and the treasures it lends.
