NATIONAL GALLERY - ALEXANDROS SOUTSOS MUSEUM: Athens, Greece
ID: national-gallery-alexandros-soutzos-museum
Website: https://www.nationalgallery.gr/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at 50 Vasileos Konstantinou Street, Athens, opposite the Hilton Athens; nearest metro Evangelismos (Line 3).
- Founded by law on 10 April 1900, with Georgios Iakovidis as its first chief curator.
- Named for Alexandros Soutsos, who bequeathed his collection and estate to the Greek state in 1896; the estate merged with the gallery in 1954.
- The central building was designed by Pavlos Mylonas, Konstantinos Mylonas and Dimitris Fatouros and built from 1966 to 1975. Grammatopoulos-Panousakis Architects and D. Vassilopoulos & Associates renovated and extended it; it reopened on 24 March 2021 after eight years with a new Stavros Niarchos Foundation Wing.
- The extension took floor area from 9,720 m2 to 20,760 m2 and added a library, conservation laboratories and a 240-seat conference hall. The 20,000-work collection runs from post-Byzantine to contemporary Greek art; a sculpture branch (National Glyptotheque) and annexes in Sparta, Corfu, Nafplion and Aegina are separate sites.
- Open 10:00 to 18:00 Monday and Thursday to Sunday, 10:00 to 22:00 Wednesday, closed Tuesday. Admission is 10 euros, 5 euros reduced; free on the first Sunday of each month from November to March. Ramps and lifts serve all levels.

FUNDING
National Gallery - Alexandros Soutsos Museum: mainly public: Greek Ministry of Culture and EU funds, with sponsors and foundations paying for exhibitions and projects
- A public law entity run by a nine-member board; the Minister of Culture appoints the Director. Stated by the gallery: public funding covers salaries and fixed operating costs.
- Stated by the gallery: sponsorships and donations cover 60 to 100 percent of the cost of exhibitions, music events and publishing.
- Expansion: total cost 59 million euros. The Ministry of Culture says 42 million euros of public funding was secured; the Stavros Niarchos Foundation gave a 13 million euro grant in 2017 (The Art Newspaper). The homepage shows the EU's ESPA 2014-2020 programme logo beside the Ministry's.
- The Stavros Niarchos Foundation also paid for the renovated interiors and permanent display opened on 11 December 2000 (stated by the gallery).
- The Alexander S. Onassis Public Benefit Foundation equipped the Nafplion annex; the Maria Tsakou Foundation funded preliminary studies for the expansion (stated by the gallery).
- No reporting found of the sponsors behind "The Allure of the Bizarre" (2025).

Red flags: attack on an exhibition and calls to ban it
- On 10 March 2025 Niki party MP Nikolaos Papadopoulos removed and damaged four works by Christoforos Katsadiotis in the group show "The Allure of the Bizarre", which he had called blasphemous in a letter to director Syrago Tsiara (Hyperallergic, The Art Newspaper). He was detained and released; the offence is a misdemeanour (To Vima).
- Parliament's Speaker censured him and cut his salary by 50 percent for one month (To Vima).
- On 10 April 2025, 29 artists, academics and estate representatives published a letter accusing the Ministry of Culture and the gallery of "institutional inaction" over the slow reinstallation; they offered to withdraw their own works until the damaged ones returned (To Vima). Tsiara said they would return within the month under tighter security.
- In September 2025, 400 believers asked the Athens Single-Member Court of First Instance to ban the show or add a warning sign; the court rejected the interim measures, finding no sufficient showing of imminent risk or urgency (Proto Thema, 18 September 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the institution's money or management found in art media. The two art-press pieces found report the 2025 attack and side with the gallery; the criticism of the gallery comes from the artists' letter, reported by a newspaper.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Far-Right Politician Vandalizes 'Blasphemous' Artworks at Athens Museum" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/995478/far-right-politician-vandalizes-blasphemous-artworks-at-athens-museum/
- Reports the 10 March attack and sets it beside attacks on art in Mexico City, Italy, Austria and Houston.
- Names Papadopoulos and Katsadiotis; the critical stance is aimed at Papadopoulos and religious pressure on artists.
- Does not criticise the gallery; it notes the board's condemnation.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Politician detained after attacks on 'blasphemous' works at Greek National Gallery" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/03/12/politician-detained-after-attacks-on-blasphemous-works-at-greek-national-gallery
- Records that the board condemned the vandalism and "any attempts at censorship".
- Does not criticise the gallery.
3. The Art Newspaper: "Greece's revamped National Gallery hosts independence celebrations" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/greece-s-revamped-national-gallery-hosts-independence-celebrations
- Reports the 59 million euro cost, the delays (a miscalculated water level of the underground Ilissos river added costs) and the 13 million euro Niarchos grant.
- Criticises the project's delays, not the funders.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Papadopoulos argued the show was produced at the expense of "the Greek Orthodox taxpayer" (Hyperallergic), which ties the attack to the gallery's public funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is mostly public, with named foundations on the expansion, and none of it falls in a flagged industry. The weak points are that the gallery publishes no sponsor list with amounts, and that its handling of the 2025 attack drew a public letter from artists in its own collection.
