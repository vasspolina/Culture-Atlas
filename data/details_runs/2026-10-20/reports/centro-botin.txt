CENTRO BOTÍN: Santander, Spain
ID: centro-botin
Website: https://www.centrobotin.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art centre of the Fundación Botín on the Albareda dock beside the Jardines de Pereda, on the Bay of Santander near the old town.
- Opened on 23 June 2017, three years later than planned; work began in 2012 (Wikipedia, Spanish).
- Named for the Botín banking family: the Fundación Botín was founded in 1964 by Marcelino Botín Sanz de Sautuola and María del Carmen Yllera Camino.
- Designed by Renzo Piano with Luis Vidal: two volumes on columns, half over the water, with 2,500 square metres of exhibition halls and an education wing; a main road was moved into a tunnel and the gardens were enlarged (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper). The site was a car park (Renzo Piano Building Workshop).
- The land belongs to the Santander Port Authority, to which the foundation pays an annual concession fee (Wikipedia, Spanish).
- Exhibitions, grants and workshops, and an arts-and-emotions education programme developed with Yale University; Fátima Sánchez is managing director and Vicente Todolí chairs the artistic committee.
- From October to May open Tuesday to Friday 10am to 2pm and 4pm to 8pm, weekends 10am to 8pm; from June to September Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 8pm. Exhibitions cost 8 euros at opening, free for Cantabria residents (The Art Newspaper, 2017).

FUNDING
Centro Botín: private foundation money: Banco Santander dividends, plus corporate sponsors including Eni's Plenitude
- The Fundación Botín's budget came entirely from dividends on its 0.89 percent stake in Banco Santander, about 60 million euros a year (Fundación Botín, April 2013).
- The building had a budget of 77 million euros; the foundation spent 21.6 million on it in 2012 (Fundación Botín). The Chronicle of Philanthropy, citing The New York Times, put the project at 106 million dollars in 2014.
- Patrons, paying a fixed annual contribution: Fundación Mutua Madrileña, Fundación Prosegur, Roche España and two public tourism bodies, Cantabria Infinita and Santander Destino (sponsor page, undated).
- Strategic sponsors, paying per exhibition or programme: Fundación EDP, Centro Universitario CESINE and Plenitude (sponsor page).
- Corporate friends include Uría Menéndez and Derwent Group; media partners include El País, Vocento and Cadena SER.
- No reporting found of the amounts paid by any sponsor.

Red flags: gas retailer as strategic sponsor
- Plenitude is controlled by the oil and gas company Eni and sells electricity and gas to households and businesses in six countries, Spain among them (Wikipedia).
- Its Spanish arm, Eni Plenitude Iberia, has its headquarters in Santander and more than 400,000 customers in Iberia (Vozpópuli, 2025); its Spanish site sells fixed and indexed gas tariffs.
- No reporting found of a protest at Centro Botín over Plenitude or Fundación EDP.

Red flags: building on public waterfront land
- In June 2012 the Cantabria high court admitted a challenge by ARCA, Ecologistas en Acción and Tajamar, through the DEBA platform, to Decree 17/2012, which changed the port plan to allow the building on the Albareda pier. They objected to handing a public space to a private foundation and said the pier should stay public (Europa Press via La Administración al Día).
- No reporting found of the outcome of that challenge.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media; the objections on record are local opposition to the site, reported in philanthropy and general press.
1. The Chronicle of Philanthropy, citing The New York Times: "Banker's $100-Million Museum Sows Discord in Spanish Town" (2014)
URL: https://www.philanthropy.com/news/bankers-100-million-museum-sows-discord-in-spanish-town/
- Opponents said the centre was built on prime public waterfront land and accused Emilio Botín and the bank of using it for "private ego and corporate branding".
- Local officials saw it as a gift to the city that could boost tourism.
2. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Spain's new Centro Botín shuns the Bilbao effect" (2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/06/21/spains-new-centro-botin-shuns-the-bilbao-effect
- Mostly not criticism: Piano and Todolí reject Bilbao comparisons.
- Bruce Altshuler of NYU points to discomfort with grand cultural building projects in post-crisis Spain.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre runs on the Botín family foundation, whose money comes from Banco Santander dividends, with a small layer of corporate sponsorship. The weak points are the gas retailer Plenitude, controlled by Eni, as a strategic sponsor and the use of public port land, which campaigners challenged in court. Worth watching: the sponsor amounts, which are not published.
