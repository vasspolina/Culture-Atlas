RUARTS FOUNDATION: Moscow, Russia
ID: ruarts-foundation-moscow
Website: https://ruarts.foundation/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private foundation for contemporary art with a six-storey building at Trubnikovsky Lane 6, Moscow; its stated mission is to "study, systematise, examine and promote Russian art" (stated by the foundation; Moskvich Mag, 20 March 2021).
- Founded in 2003 by the collector Marianna Sardarova; Catherine Borissoff (Катрин Борисов) is art director (stated by the foundation).
- The Trubnikovsky building opened on 17 April 2021 with "Soft Homeland" (Мягкая родина, translation mine), curated by Sardarova and Borissoff from the collection; it holds exhibition halls, a library, an educational centre, a bookshop and a café (The Blueprint, April 2021).
- The collection of more than 2,000 works centres on unofficial Soviet art, post-Soviet and contemporary Russian art (stated by the foundation).
- The separate Ruarts gallery, opened in 2003 in 1st Zachatyevsky Lane (Moskvich Mag, 2021), closed after 21 years and more than 130 shows; its last exhibition ran from 18 September to 4 October 2026. The team now works only through the foundation, whose programme Borissoff says is booked to 2028, beginning in October 2026 with a show of its 2025 grant winners (Moskvich Mag, September 2026).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 20:00. A ticket to "The Triumph of Melancholy" costs 750 roubles, 250 for students and 150 for pensioners and schoolchildren; free for people with group I and II disabilities and children under 7 (stated by the foundation).

FUNDING
Ruarts Foundation: private: founded by the collector Marianna Sardarova around her own collection, with ticket and membership income
- The foundation was set up by Sardarova, and its collection is the founder's own; the foundation says it was created to make "our own collection" public (stated by the foundation).
- Sardarova is married to Rashid Sardarov, owner of the South Ural Industrial Company; Business FM called her co-owner of the RuArts gallery (Business FM, 22 August 2016, citing Izvestia).
- Earned income: tickets, a two-year membership card, guided tours at 5,000 roubles, a bookshop and a café (stated by the foundation).
- The foundation runs its own grant programme for artists (stated by the foundation; Moskvich Mag).

Red flags: founder's husband's oil and gas fortune, reported in 2016 and 2025
- Business FM (2016), citing Izvestia, called Sardarov a "former oil magnate" (бывший нефтяной магнат; translation mine) and developer, and cited Finans magazine's 2011 ranking putting his fortune at 4.9 billion dollars.
- Transparency International Russia, an anti-corruption advocacy group, wrote on 21 March 2025 that his South Ural Industrial Company "extracts and processes oil and gas" and that his business grew through exclusive access to processing Kazakh gas condensate at Gazprom facilities.
- What is shown: the founder is married to Sardarov and the collection is hers. What is not shown: the foundation publishes no accounts, and no source opened says his money pays for it.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Transparency International Russia, citing the French company registry Pappers, wrote that as of February 2024 Rashid and Marianna Sardarov are citizens of Saint Kitts and Nevis. It also wrote that the Panama Papers linked him to dozens of offshore companies in 2016, and that his property in France that was formerly the writer Martin Gray's mansion was seized by French authorities in 2023 in a fraud case, according to Nice Matin. It describes him as having avoided sanctions.
- Unverified: a Novaya Gazeta investigation found that Sardarov moved profits abroad while bypassing the Russian tax system, as reported by Transparency International Russia.

BOTTOM LINE
Ruarts counts as fossil fuel family money only through its founder's marriage; whose money pays its running costs is not public.
