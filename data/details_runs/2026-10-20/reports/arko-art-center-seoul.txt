ARKO ART CENTER: Seoul, South Korea
ID: arko-art-center-seoul
Website: https://www.arko.or.kr/artcenter (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Public art museum in the Daehakro district of Seoul, in Marronnier Park on the former site of Seoul National University, next to ARKO Arts Theater (CIMAM).
- Began in 1974 as Misulhoegwan; moved to its present building in 1979, began curating its own exhibitions in the 1990s, was renamed Marronnier Art Center in 2002 and Arko Art Center in 2005 (CIMAM).
- Named for Arts Council Korea (ARKO), the body that runs it (CIMAM; Arts Council Korea).
- Building: brick, designed by the architect Kim Swoo-geun (CIMAM); The Korea Times (Cathy Rose A. Garcia, 26 May 2009) mentions the centre's red bricks.
- Programmes: thematic exhibitions on social subjects and public programmes; Insa Art Space, opened in 2000, and the IAS Studio programme (CIMAM).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 19:00 (last entry 18:30), until 21:00 on Wednesdays; closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok. Admission is free (centre website).

FUNDING
Arko Art Center: state-run: operated by Arts Council Korea
- Arts Council Korea was established in 1973 and lists the Arko Art Center among its platforms (Arts Council Korea).
- ArtReview (Hyunjin Kim, 12 Aug 2016) places Arts Council Korea among art and culture funding organisations with "a very strong affiliation with the Ministry" of Culture, Sports and Tourism.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One opinion piece criticises the parent body, Arts Council Korea, not the centre.
1. ArtReview: Hyunjin Kim, "Self-censored" (12 Aug 2016)
URL: https://artreview.com/ara-spring-2016-opinion-hyunjin-kim-self-censored/
- Argues that censorship in South Korea has moved from removing artworks after the fact to preventive measures through funding and personnel decisions, "designed to eradicate controversial elements in advance".
- Argues that artists and curators who choose compromise over resistance leave bureaucrats free to manage and control artists.
- Names Arts Council Korea (ARKO) as under scrutiny, and says a blacklist of artists, directors and writers was given to funding juries in theatre and literature, revealed after media coverage and a National Assembly inspection of ARKO. Does not name the Arko Art Center.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Korea Times (Choi Ha-young, 12 Oct 2016): opposition lawmaker Do Jong-hwan cited minutes of a 29 May 2015 Arts Council Korea meeting at a National Assembly audit. Kwon Young-bin, then head of Arts Council Korea, is quoted saying "we can't make decisions on our own".
- Unverified: Hankook Ilbo reported, via The Korea Herald (Yeo Jun-suk, 13 Oct 2016), that a 100-page document listed 9,473 artists and that the presidential office told government agencies, including Arts Council Korea, to cut support to entities linked to them. Cheong Wa Dae and the government denied it, and Culture Minister Cho Yoon-sun told lawmakers there was no such blacklist.
- Yonhap, via The Korea Times (23 Jan 2018): the Seoul High Court raised former presidential chief of staff Kim Ki-choon's sentence to four years and gave former culture minister Cho Yoon-sun two years in prison, ruling that the government's exclusion of critical artists from state support violated the Constitution. The report names neither Arts Council Korea nor the Arko Art Center.
- Connection to the centre: Arts Council Korea operates the Arko Art Center (centre website); the juries named in ArtReview were in theatre and literature.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is public and comes through Arts Council Korea. The 2015 to 2016 artist blacklist is tied in the sources to the parent body and its funding juries in theatre and literature, and the 2018 court findings concerned the presidential chief of staff and the culture minister. Worth watching is how the parent body awards support, since the centre sits inside it.
