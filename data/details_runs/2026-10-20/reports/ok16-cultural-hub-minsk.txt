OK16 CULTURAL HUB: Minsk, Belarus
ID: ok16-cultural-hub-minsk
Website: https://ok16.by (checked 2026-10-24; now serves an unrelated travel blog, with posts dated 2021)

OVERVIEW
- Former cultural hub in three converted workshops of the Minsk machine-tool plant at 16 Oktyabrskaya Street, Minsk (Kalektar).
- Belgazprombank bought the workshops in 2017 and began developing a cultural site there (Kalektar). Nasha Niva (3 June 2021) gives the same year and says the premises were converted into a cultural hub called Ok16.
- Viktar Babaryka, then chairman of the bank's management board, "came up with a proposal to create an art-space OK16 organising exhibitions and theatrical performances" (Wikipedia). Maria Kalesnikava was its art director (Wikipedia, Nasha Niva).
- Positioned as an "incubator of initiatives, an experimental space" for large-scale social and cultural ideas; it hosted the TEART festival, the EMERGE IT conference, theatre and performance work, film screenings, lectures and exhibitions (Kalektar).
- Closed: Kalektar records that on 7 January 2021 the hub suspended its work, and gives the formal reason as an order of the Ministry of Emergency Situations. The independent theatre TOK cancelled its performances because the space closed (PEN Belarus, 12 January 2021).
- Visiting: the hub has been closed since January 2021. No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
OK16 cultural hub: corporate: Belgazprombank, owned by Gazprom and Gazprombank
- Belgazprombank owned the buildings and sponsored the hub (Kalektar, PEN Belarus). No reporting found that could be opened of the purchase price or of any operator that rented the site.
- Gazprom and Gazprombank each hold 49.8% of Belgazprombank (Wikipedia).
- Babaryka chaired the bank's management board from July 2000 until he resigned on 12 May 2020 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of an operating budget, ticket income or other funders.
- No reporting found of a relaunch, its team or its funders.

Red flags: owned by Gazprom and Gazprombank
- Gazprom and Gazprombank hold 49.8% each of the bank that bought the buildings and sponsored the hub (Wikipedia).

Red flags: sanctions on the bank's controllers
- Gazprombank, which holds 49.8% of Belgazprombank, was added to the UK consolidated asset-freeze list on 24 March 2022 (Kelley Drye) and designated by the US Treasury on 21 November 2024 (US Treasury).
- Belgazprombank itself: Canada and Ukraine added it to their sanctions lists in 2023 (Wikipedia). On 24 February 2025 the EU banned EU companies from transacting with Belgazprombank, BelVEB and a VTB branch in Shanghai, saying they used Russia's SPFS messaging system to get around EU sanctions (Interfax).

Red flags: state closure and prosecution of its leaders
- The hub stopped work on 7 January 2021 on a Ministry of Emergency Situations order (Kalektar). PEN Belarus, an advocacy group, lists it among venues closed in a wave of pressure on culture and calls the closure "part of the attack on independent Belarusian culture."
- Babaryka was detained in June 2020 and sentenced on 6 July 2021 to 14 years in prison for bribery and tax evasion; he was released and deported to Ukraine on 13 December 2025 (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper reported that his arrest is "widely regarded as politically motivated" (Sophia Kishkovsky, 24 August 2020).
- Kalesnikava, art director of OK16 from 2017, became head of Babaryka's presidential campaign in May 2020, was seized in Minsk on 7 September 2020, was sentenced on 6 September 2021 to 11 years in prison, and was handed over to Ukraine on 13 December 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Unverified: a Russian discount chain, Svetofor, will open on the OK16 site, from an unnamed Minsk woman interviewed by The Village Belarus and republished by Nasha Niva on 6 October 2022. The article does not mention a sale by Belgazprombank or an auction.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The hub was paid for by Belgazprombank, a bank owned equally by Gazprom and Gazprombank. Gazprombank is on UK and US sanctions lists, and the bank itself is on Canadian and Ukrainian lists and under an EU transaction ban. The weak points are that ownership, and the prosecution by the Belarusian state of its art director and of the bank's former chairman, who proposed it. Watch whether a relaunch names a funder; none was found.
