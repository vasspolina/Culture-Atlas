BUXTON CONTEMPORARY: Melbourne, Australia
ID: buxton-contemporary-melbourne
Website: https://buxtoncontemporary.com

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery on the University of Melbourne's Victorian College of the Arts Southbank campus, in the Melbourne Arts Precinct; the University of Melbourne runs it through its Museums and Collections area (museum site).
- Initiated in 2014 through a gift from the Michael and Janet Buxton Foundation: the Buxtons gave their collection of contemporary Australian art to the university and funded the building (stated by the museum). It opened in 2018.
- Michael and Janet Buxton are listed as its Patrons.
- Building designed by Fender Katsalidis and shortlisted for a Victoria Architecture Award in the Public Architecture category (Open House Melbourne).
- Holds the Michael Buxton Collection, more than 350 works by 59 artists (Open House Melbourne).

FUNDING
Buxton Contemporary: privately endowed: the Buxton gift of art, construction funds and operating funds to the University of Melbourne
- A 3 December 2014 announcement on MAB's website gives Michael Buxton's gift to the University of Melbourne as $26 million: $10 million of artworks and $16 million towards construction of the gallery; it states "The Buxton family will retain a 40 per cent share of the new centre's board." The Chronicle of Philanthropy, citing The Age, reported the same day a $22 million gift: an $8.5 million collection of more than 300 works and $13.5 million toward construction.
- Open House Melbourne states the gift included funds to build the museum and to operate it; Julie Shiels in The Conversation (2018) likewise describes the gift as including operating funds.
- The Buxton Contemporary Circle is a membership program that supports new artists (museum site).
- MAB describes Michael Buxton as "one of Melbourne's wealthiest and most prominent property developers"; Wikipedia calls him an art collector and property developer.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Conversation: Julie Shiels, a lecturer at RMIT University's School of Art, "Private collectors are saving Australian art, but they can't do it on their own" (4 April 2018). She argues that declining government arts funding is not filled by philanthropy, that tax deductions through the Cultural Gift Program are no substitute for public money aimed at innovation, and that collectors' taste cannot cover all contemporary work. Her criticism is aimed at reliance on philanthropy, not at Buxton Contemporary; she writes that the Buxton gift includes a collection, a new museum and operational funding and praises the rigour with which Buxton conceived the space.
- Student protesters at the University of Melbourne in May 2024 demanded disclosure and divestment of the university's ties to weapons makers, naming Lockheed Martin, Boeing and BAE Systems; on 22 May the university committed to disclose its research grant arrangements (The Melburnian, Georgie Atkins, 28 May 2024).
- Connection: Buxton Contemporary is run by the University of Melbourne (museum site); the Melburnian article concerns the university's research grants and does not mention the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a 2014 gift from the Michael and Janet Buxton Foundation (stated by the museum), announced as $26 million by MAB and $22 million by The Age via the Chronicle of Philanthropy. MAB's announcement states that the Buxton family keeps 40 per cent of the founding board.
