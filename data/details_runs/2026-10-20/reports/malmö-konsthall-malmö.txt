MALMÖ KONSTHALL: Malmö, Sweden
ID: malmö-konsthall-malmö
Website: https://malmokonsthall.se

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art hall at S:t Johannesgatan 7, Malmö; it describes itself as part of Malmö stad and sits within the city's Kulturförvaltning (culture administration) (Malmö Konsthall website).
- Opened on 22 March 1975; discussions about a dedicated contemporary art space in Malmö had run since the 1930s (Malmö Konsthall, "50 years" page).
- Designed by the Lund architect Klas Anshelm (1914 to 1980), with a glass facade facing Magistratsparken, a large north-facing skylight and 526 lanterns with individually adjustable light (Malmö Konsthall, "50 years" page). Wikipedia says it was built from 1971 to 1974 in concrete, glass, wood and aluminium and won the 1974 Kasper Salin Prize.
- A 1994 renovation connected it to the neighbouring brick Hantverkshuset, adding a bookshop, a children's area and a restaurant (Wikipedia).
- No permanent collection; about 2,000 square metres of exhibition space, which the Konsthall calls "basically just a white box" (Malmö Konsthall website). It has staged close to 600 exhibitions and three large ones a year (Malmö Konsthall website).
- Artistic directors: Eje Högestätt 1975 to 1986, Björn Springfeldt 1986 to 1990, Sune Nordgren 1990 to 1996, Bera Nordal 1997 to 2002, Lars Grambye 2003 to 2007, Jacob Fabricius 2007 to 2013, Diana Baldon 2014 to 2016, and Mats Stjernstedt from 2017 (Malmö Konsthall, "50 years" page). Malmö stad announced Stjernstedt's appointment on 12 September 2016, with Culture Director Elisabeth Lundgren quoted.
- On site: the restaurant SMAK, a bookshop, the children's workshop Verkstan and the youth programme Malmö Konsthall U (Malmö Konsthall website).
- Admission is free. Open Tuesday to Sunday, closed on Mondays and major holidays; the site links an accessibility statement (Malmö Konsthall website).

FUNDING
Malmö Konsthall: public: a unit of the City of Malmö's culture administration
- The Konsthall is part of Malmö stad's Kulturförvaltning (Malmö Konsthall website). Culture Director Elisabeth Lundgren gave the city's reasons for appointing Mats Stjernstedt as its head in 2016 (Malmö stad, 12 September 2016).
- Admission to exhibitions and programmes is free (Malmö Konsthall website).

BOTTOM LINE
The Konsthall is run by the City of Malmö as part of its culture administration, and entry is free. Its funding is municipal.
