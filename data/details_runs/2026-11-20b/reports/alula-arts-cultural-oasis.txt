ALULA ARTS & CULTURAL OASIS: AlUla, Saudi Arabia
ID: alula-arts-cultural-oasis
Website: https://www.experiencealula.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The arts and culture programme of AlUla, an oasis region of 22,561 square kilometres in north-western Saudi Arabia home to the Hegra World Heritage Site (Arab News, 2021).
- Run by the Royal Commission for AlUla (RCU), which the Ministry of Finance set up in July 2017 (Arab News); its governor since then is Prince Bader bin Abdullah bin Farhan Al Saud (RCU website).
- Arts strands: Wadi AlFann, permanent desert works by Ahmed Mater, Manal Al Dowayan, James Turrell, Agnes Denes and Michael Heizer, behind its first target of a 2024 opening; the Desert X AlUla biennial, first held in 2020; and the AlJadidah Arts District (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- The AlUla Contemporary Art Museum, designed by Lina Ghotmeh and directed by Candida Pestana, was named on 1 February 2026 at "Arduna", an 80-work show co-curated with the Centre Pompidou (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- AlUla had 265,000 visitors in 2024 and targets one million by 2030 (The National).

FUNDING
AlUla Arts & Cultural Oasis: state-funded: the Saudi government through the Royal Commission for AlUla
- Saudi Arabia put in $2 billion of seed funding; $3.2 billion for priority infrastructure was to come from public-private partnerships, within a $15 billion masterplan in three phases to 2035 (Arab News, 28 April 2021).
- RCU chief tourism officer Phillip Jones said AlUla's budget is 14 billion riyals for 2025 and a similar amount for 2026, secured by the Public Investment Fund until 2030, untouched by the kingdom's spending cuts (The National, 27 October 2025).
- The RCU is offering private investors 21 projects worth 6 billion riyals, aiming for private money to make up 40 per cent of funding (The National).
- Desert X AlUla is organised with the RCU (The Art Newspaper, 2022); Artforum reported in 2019 that the RCU funds it.
- The Centre Pompidou partnership, announced in 2023, sits within a France and Saudi Arabia intergovernmental agreement (The Art Newspaper, 2026).

Red flags: Gulf state body in charge since 2017
- The RCU's site is an official Saudi government website; Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman has chaired its board since July 2017, and the Minister of Culture, the Minister of Tourism and the Governor of Madinah sit on it (RCU website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism targets the Saudi state patronage behind AlUla's art.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Three Desert X Board Members Resign in Protest of Saudi Collaboration" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/521537/three-desert-x-board-members-resign-in-protest-of-saudi-collaboration/
- Ed Ruscha, Yael Lipschutz and Tristan Milanovich cited the Saudi government's human rights violations and the killing of Jamal Khashoggi; registration-walled, only the headline and standfirst were read.
2. Hyperallergic: "Desert X Funder Pulls Out in Protest of Saudi Collaboration" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/523254/desert-x-funder-pulls-out-in-protest-of-saudi-collaboration/
- Relays the Los Angeles Times report that the MaddocksBrown Foundation withdrew its funding; registration-walled, only the headline and standfirst were read.
3. Artforum: "Three Board Members Resign from Desert X over Collaboration with Saudi Arabia" (2019)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/news/three-board-members-resign-from-desert-x-over-collaboration-with-saudi-arabia-80990
- Lipschutz called the RCU-funded show "completely unethical", a deal with a government "that is completely undemocratic".
4. Artforum: "Desert X Loses Donor over Controversial Partnership with Saudi Arabia" (2019)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/news/desert-x-loses-donor-over-controversial-partnership-with-saudi-arabia-81046
- Citing the Los Angeles Times: MaddocksBrown had given Desert X about $13,000 since its founding; its board chair Linda Brown said the foundation was "disappointed that Desert X has chosen this path".
5. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Desert X AlUla, the inaugural art biennial in a historic Saudi Arabian valley, announces participants" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/01/17/desert-x-alulathe-inaugural-art-biennial-in-a-historic-saudi-arabian-valleyannounces-participants
- The initiative "was met with a barrage of criticism"; Christopher Knight of the Los Angeles Times called it "morally corrupt".
6. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Accessible to all or elitist artwashing? Desert X opens second Saudi Arabia edition" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/02/10/desert-x-alula-saudi-arabia-2022-who-is-it-for
- Says the government improves its image through what some commentators call "art washing".
- Sumantro Ghose, the RCU's artistic programming director, calls the government's wish to weave art into the country "genuine and not cynical" and points to art teaching in secondary schools.
7. Al-Monitor: Robbie Corey-Boulet, "Saudi artists marvel at surprise patron: their own rulers" (2022, drawing on AFP)
URL: https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/06/saudi-artists-marvel-surprise-patron-their-own-rulers
- Critics call such projects "artwashing"; Mater's childhood friend, the poet Ashraf Fayadh, was sentenced to death in 2015, cut to eight years on appeal.
- Mater welcomes state backing; Al Dowayan says she has never been censored.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The October 2019 board resignations and donor withdrawal protested the partnership of Desert X, a Californian biennial, with the RCU, which runs and pays for AlUla's art.

BOTTOM LINE
AlUla's art programme is a Saudi state project, paid for and directed by the government. Worth watching: which private investors and foreign institutions join.
