GIBBES MUSEUM OF ART: Charleston, USA
ID: gibbes-museum-of-art-charleston
Website: https://www.gibbesmuseum.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 135 Meeting Street, Charleston, in a Beaux Arts building that opened on 11 April 1905 (Wikipedia).
- Run by the Carolina Art Association, founded in 1858; the museum was formerly the Gibbes Art Gallery (Wikipedia).
- Named for James Shoolbred Gibbes, who left $100,000 for "erection of a suitable building for the exhibitions of paintings"; relatives contested the will, the New York Supreme Court upheld the gift in December 1901 and the association received the money in 1903 (Wikipedia).
- Frank Pierce Milburn designed the building (Wikipedia).
- A two-year, $13.5 million renovation ended with reopening on 28 May 2016 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds over 10,000 works, mainly American and many tied to Charleston or the South (Wikipedia).
- Angela Mack, who joined the Gibbes in 1981 as associate curator and became CEO in 2023, announced her retirement for October 2025 (Charleston City Paper staff, "Gibbes Museum's executive announces retirement", 29 May 2024).

FUNDING
Gibbes Museum of Art: contribution-funded: contributions were 84.2% of fiscal 2025 revenue, with rental and earned income
- The Carolina Art Association (EIN 57-0323047) reported revenue of $5,961,167 and expenses of $4,991,874 for the fiscal year ending June 2025. Contributions were $5,018,995, 84.2% of revenue, and rental property income was $584,438 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Net assets were $19,019,082 at the end of fiscal 2025; revenue was $7,213,637 in 2023 and $5,291,398 in 2024 (ProPublica).
- Under Angela Mack the museum completed a $17.5 million building renovation and a $14.5 million capital campaign and doubled its endowment through legacy gifts, according to the City Paper (29 May 2024).
- In April 2022 the museum received $60,000 from the Gaylord and Dorothy Donnelley Foundation for an exhibition on Edward I.R. Jennings and Aubrey Beardsley, and two Art Bridges Foundation grants totalling $76,015 for the William H. Johnson exhibition "Fighters for Freedom" (SC Arts Hub, Jason Rapp, 28 April 2022).

BOTTOM LINE
Contributions made up 84% of the Gibbes's $5.96 million revenue in fiscal 2025 (ProPublica), and foundation grants in 2022 included Donnelley and Art Bridges (SC Arts Hub). Its tax filings are public through ProPublica.
