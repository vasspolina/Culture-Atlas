DIA BEACON: Beacon, USA
ID: dia-beacon
Website: https://www.diaart.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of art from the 1960s to the present at 3 Beekman Street in Beacon, New York, near the Hudson River, a five-minute walk from Beacon station on Metro-North's Hudson Line and about 60 miles north of New York City (Wikipedia).
- Run by the Dia Art Foundation, founded in 1974 by Philippa de Menil, Heiner Friedrich and Helen Winkler (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 2003 in a former printing plant built in 1929 by Nabisco; the building was added to the National Register of Historic Places on 18 April 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Robert Irwin worked on the renovation with Alan Koch, Lyn Rice, Galia Solomonoff and Linda Taalman of OpenOffice; Irwin also designed the entrance court, a grove of flowering fruit trees and a formal garden (Wikipedia).
- On site: works by Andy Warhol, Dan Flavin, Richard Serra and Michael Heizer, galleries for On Kawara, Agnes Martin, Blinky Palermo and Robert Ryman, temporary exhibitions, monthly Gallery Talks, Community Free Days for neighbouring counties and an education programme (Wikipedia).
- Jessica Morgan joined Dia as director in 2015 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Dia Beacon: endowment, private contributions and foundation grants through the Dia Art Foundation
- The Dia Art Foundation (Dia Center for the Arts Inc) reported total revenue of 41,856,742 dollars for the fiscal year 2025: contributions 24,122,046 dollars and investment income 14,446,583 dollars, with total assets of 273,547,036 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Dia's founding money came from Philippa de Menil, heir to the Schlumberger oil fortune. Between 1980 and 1982 the Schlumberger stock fell from about 87 to about 30 dollars a share, Dia took a 3.87 million dollar Citibank loan against it in 1983, and in 1985 Dia's board was reshaken and a Sotheby's auction raised about 1.3 million dollars (Wikipedia). This is history.
- The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation awarded Dia Center for the Arts a 1,000,000 dollar matching grant on 16 June 1995 for a special initiative programme (Mellon Foundation grant database).
- A 78 million dollar capital campaign was announced in June 2018 and its target was raised to 90 million dollars in May 2019 (Wikipedia); by December 2020 Dia had raised 80 million dollars of it, most of it going to the endowment (The Art Newspaper, Nancy Kenney, 4 December 2020).
- The trustees include Nathalie De Gunzburg as chairman and Howard Rachofsky as treasurer (ProPublica, FY2025 filing).

Red flags: Sponsor dropped under pressure
- Sackler name: Dia's spokesperson told Hyperallergic (Valentina Di Liscia, 21 December 2020) that in 2018 Marissa Sackler stood down from Dia's board and Dia "discontinued the Sackler naming associated with our public programs". Wikipedia's list of things named after the Sackler family says Dia created a Sackler Institute in 2016 and removed the name in 2019.
- Hyperallergic (Jasmine Weber, 12 July 2021) lists Dia Art Foundation among institutions that removed the Sackler name, citing the advocacy of P.A.I.N. and public awareness of Purdue's role in the opioid epidemic.
- Protest: Nan Goldin told Hyperallergic (Di Liscia, 2020) that P.A.I.N. wanted to hold a protest at Dia, "one of the largest known recipients of Sackler funding", and cased the museum a couple of times, but the action never took place.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One press item, on staff unionisation across the Dia Art Foundation.
1. Far Out Magazine: Thomas Coll, "Dia Art Foundation announces plans of unionisation" (19 July 2022)
URL: https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/dia-art-foundation-unionisation/
- Workers sought to join the United Auto Workers; the proposed union covered 135 full-time and part-time staff in New York, Beacon, Long Island and New Mexico.
- Workers cited low wages, pay inequities, lack of job security and full-time positions, unstable schedules and no clear career track.
- Not criticism of the programme or funders; it concerns working conditions across the foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Dia Beacon is the Dia Art Foundation's endowment income and private contributions, with a Mellon Foundation matching grant in 1995 and a capital campaign for the endowment. Dia discontinued the Sackler naming of its public programs in 2018 or 2019 (sources differ), and Nan Goldin's group P.A.I.N. wanted to protest at Dia.
