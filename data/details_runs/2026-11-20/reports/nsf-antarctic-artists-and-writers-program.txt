ANTARCTIC ARTISTS AND WRITERS PROGRAM (POLAR STEAM): McMurdo Station, Ross Island, Antarctica
ID: nsf-antarctic-artists-and-writers-program
Website: https://www.nsf.gov/od/opp/ant/polar-steam (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A US National Science Foundation programme that takes artists and writers to Antarctica to make work that helps the public understand the continent and the science done there. The atlas places it at McMurdo Station, one of three US year-round stations (NSF).
- It took shape in the 1980s after years of campaigning by NSF programme manager Guy Guthridge; by 2015 it sent one or two people a year (Pacific Standard, 2015).
- Since 2022 Oregon State University has run it, together with the Polar Educators programme for teachers, under an NSF cooperative agreement called "Polar STEAM: Where Curiosity Thrives", from 1 September 2022 to 31 August 2028. The principal investigator is Kari O'Connell (NSF award 2221990).
- Rules of the last standalone call: projects must reach US audiences; no installations or performances in Antarctica; no short journalistic projects; teams of one or two people. That call (19-568) is now archived (NSF).
- Past participants include the filmmaker Werner Herzog ("Encounters at the End of the World") and the writer Richard Panek (Wikipedia), the artists Todd Anderson and Ian van Coller (2019), and Kirsten Carlson and Michelle Schwengel-Regala (2017) (NSF).
- Applicants must show that their project has to be done in Antarctica: "not desirable, not advantageous, but necessary", in the words of NSF's Peter West (Pacific Standard, 2015).

FUNDING
Antarctic Artists and Writers Program: wholly US federal, given as logistics in kind and run by Oregon State University under a US$4 million agreement
- NSF manages the US Antarctic Program, and successful projects get its logistics, stations, field camps and transport, plus round-trip economy flights from the US. The programme gives participants no money for any purpose (NSF).
- Participants fly in the planes that bring the scientists, are housed on the same bases and get the same food and protective clothing (Pacific Standard).
- Award 2221990 to Oregon State University has an estimated total of US$4,000,000, fully obligated: US$2.2 million in fiscal 2022, US$950,000 in 2023, US$350,000 in 2024 and US$500,000 in 2025. It is funded by NSF's Office of Polar Programs and its Directorate for STEM Education and covers both programmes; the Science Education Resource Center evaluates it (NSF award record).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The coverage read is descriptive. The one hostile piece is from a conservative political outlet, not the art press.
1. Washington Free Beacon: Elizabeth Harrington, "Taxpayers Pay for Poets to Travel to Antarctica" (2014)
URL: https://freebeacon.com/issues/taxpayers-pay-for-poets-to-travel-to-antarctica/
- Presents the trips of poets, novelists and artists as a cost to taxpayers, naming participants and quoting their work. Its subtitle puts the programme's spending at US$31.5 million.
- That figure counts NSF education and climate-literacy grants, such as US$5.6 million to Columbia University, as part of the programme; the article itself estimates airfares for 126 trips at about US$302,400.
- Quotes NSF's Peter West calling the programme a "very tiny fraction" of NSF's Antarctic operation.
- Openly partisan.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The administration's fiscal 2026 budget request proposed a 70 per cent cut to polar science research and ending NSF's lease of the icebreaker Nathaniel B. Palmer; for now NSF planned to try to keep its land stations, McMurdo among them, operating (Scientific American, Douglas Fox, August 2025).
- NSF announced in 2024 that it would fund no new projects for the 2024-25 and 2025-26 field seasons, and the Trump administration cancelled the construction budget for McMurdo (ExplorersWeb, April 2025).
- The programme depends on the same NSF logistics, stations and polar budget.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is clean and wholly public. The weak point is that the programme rests on a single federal agreement inside a shrinking polar budget. Worth watching: whether NSF renews Polar STEAM.
