MUSEO CASTILLO DEL MORRO SAN PEDRO DE LA ROCA: Santiago de Cuba, Cuba
ID: museo-castillo-del-morro-san-pedro-de-la-roca
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Fortress at the entrance of Santiago de Cuba's bay, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list on 6 December 1997 under criteria iv and v, on a 94-hectare property (UNESCO, archived copy of 2 January 2025; EcuRed, state encyclopedia).
- Construction began in 1638 under the governor Pedro de la Roca y Borjas, after a visit by the Italian military engineer Juan Bautista Antonelli; an English attack destroyed it in 1662 and it was rebuilt and enlarged between 1663 and 1669 (EcuRed; UNESCO).
- Used as a military prison in the independence war; Bartolomé Masó, Flor Crombet and Emilio Bacardí Moreau were among the prisoners (EcuRed; Havana Times).
- The US army occupied it after 1898, and in 1904 it was registered as Cuban state property (EcuRed).
- Restored from 1962 under Francisco Prat Puig; declared a National Monument by resolutions 9/1979 and 147/1997 (EcuRed; UNESCO).
- A museum opened inside on 23 July 1978 as the Museo de la Piratería and took its present name in 1997 (EcuRed).
- On show: rooms on piracy, on the colonial defence system and on the 1898 naval battle, with objects raised from the sunken Spanish cruiser Cristóbal Colón; cells, a chapel with a 17th-century carved Christ, and a collection of flintlock weapons (EcuRed).
- EcuRed names Georgina Sarmiento Martínez as director, gives about 70,000 visitors a year without stating a year, and lists opening from 8:00 to 19:30 every day.
- A cannon ceremony is held at sunset (OnCuba).
- UNESCO noted conservation problems in 1997, the most serious the severe degradation of wooden elements from unsuitable timber and pests, and calls for interventions on them and on eroded walls.

FUNDING
Museo Castillo del Morro San Pedro de la Roca: public: Cuban state property, managed by the provincial heritage centre
- UNESCO states that the castle is owned by the Cuban state, that the responsible national agency is the Consejo Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural, and that the Centro Provincial del Patrimonio Cultural de Santiago de Cuba manages it.
- The Consejo Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural is one of the Ministry of Culture's councils (EcuRed).
- Planning: a management plan for the monument was drawn up in 1994 by the provincial monuments office and the provincial physical planning administration (UNESCO).
- Repairs, 2020 to 2021: the official daily Granma, quoted by OnCuba in April 2021, reported works by the Oficina del Conservador de Santiago de Cuba, the provincial heritage centre, the monuments restoration company and other local bodies. They replaced the main bridge's wooden parts with a hard timber because of weather and termites, and were to continue in 2021 with railings, the Balcón de la Reina, paths, car park and cabins.

Not related: the Castillo de los Tres Reyes del Morro in Havana and the Castillo de San Sebastián in San Juan, Puerto Rico. EcuRed groups them with this castle as fortresses designed by the Antonelli family; they are separate sites.

BOTTOM LINE
The castle is Cuban state property run through the Ministry of Culture's heritage system, with no outside funder named. Its weak point is the upkeep of the fabric, for which only state-media accounts of repairs exist. Worth watching: UNESCO monitoring and the cost of the next repairs.
