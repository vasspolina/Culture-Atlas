MÉMORIAL ACTE: Pointe-à-Pitre, Guadeloupe
ID: memorial-acte
Website: https://memorialacte.fr (checked 2026-10-24; fails the secure connection: the certificate is issued for a hosting cluster, not this name, so no page was read)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial, museum, performing arts venue and congress centre on the former Darboussier sugar factory site in Pointe-à-Pitre; its official name is Centre caribéen d'expressions et de mémoire de la Traite et de l'Esclavage (French Wikipedia).
- The idea dates to 1998. Victorin Lurel and the Regional Council launched the project, which the Comité international des peuples noirs proposed. The Guadeloupean studio BMC won the design competition in January 2008 and the first stone was laid on 27 May 2008 (French Wikipedia).
- President François Hollande inaugurated it on 10 May 2015; it opened to the public on 7 July 2015 and became a public cultural cooperation body in 2019 (French Wikipedia).
- Building: 7,800 square metres on a 1.2-hectare site, joined by a 275-metre footbridge to the 2.2-hectare garden Morne Mémoire (French Wikipedia).
- The permanent exhibition covers six archipelagos and 39 islands. It won the Council of Europe Museum Prize in 2017 (French Wikipedia).
- Directors: Jacques Martial (2015 to 2019), Laurella Yssap-Rincon (2019 to 2024), then Isabelle Vestris (since December 2024); Raphaël Lapin has chaired the board since 2024 (French Wikipedia).
- Visiting: no opening hours or prices were read. On 27 May 2026 entry to the permanent exhibition was free and the museum offered its first critical tour of it (RCI).

FUNDING
Mémorial ACTe: public: mainly Région Guadeloupe
- Cost: €83 million; the construction budget rose from €21 million to €83 million, drawing criticism from the state audit court (The Art Newspaper, 2021).
- Victorin Lurel, then president of the Regional Council, championed the project; the cost drew criticism with unemployment above 25% (RCI).
- The Région pays an operating subsidy; the then-director said in 2021 it paid only half of the 2020 subsidy (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of private donors or a sponsor list.

Red flags: audit findings and a financial inquiry
- 6 October 2023: the prosecutor general of the Cour des comptes sent a 21-page request to the court's contentious chamber, describing "inorganisation totale et un non-respect flagrant des règles administratives et financières" (total lack of organisation and flagrant non-compliance with administrative and financial rules; my translation). Jacques Martial received €91,382.87 on leaving without board approval; eleven staff who invoked a Covid withdrawal right were paid for over a year without working (Le Courrier de Guadeloupe, 5 February 2024).
- 20 November 2023: the Chambre régionale des comptes published a report citing irregularities that "paralysent son fonctionnement", "risques majeurs de contentieux et de fraude", untraced cash and "ingérence de la Région" (RCI).
- 22 April 2024: the Parquet national financier searched the Guadeloupe regional council, a social landlord, a property developer and a cultural engineering firm in a preliminary inquiry that follows a 2019 Cour des comptes report on irregularities in at least three public tenders and on EU funds. RCI names no one and reports no outcome (25 April 2024).
- Unverified: dismissed director Laurella Yssap-Rincon says €420,000 from second-half 2019 ticket sales is unaccounted for. Board chair Georges Bredent accused her of authoritarianism; regional president Ary Chalus had called her claims an attempt to slander him (The Art Newspaper, 3 August 2021).
- RCI (2025): the Basse-Terre administrative court annulled her dismissal; a former director was convicted in January 2024 of favouritism in a public contract.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The art press covers the governance dispute, not the exhibition; the one critique of content is a scholarly blog.
1. The Art Newspaper: Vincent Noce, "Director of slavery memorial in Guadeloupe sacked after reporting €420,000 missing from museum's budget" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/08/03/director-of-slavery-memorial-in-guadeloupe-sacked-after-reporting-euro420000-missing-from-museums-budget
- Reports Yssap-Rincon's dismissal on 22 July 2021, her allegations of missing ticket money, and the cost rise from €21 million to €83 million. Almost 100 artists and cultural figures signed a petition calling the dismissal a "witch hunt".
- Names Bredent, who cited "a certain opacity in management", and Chalus, who declined to comment.
2. Bert Hoffmann, GIGA and Freie Universität Berlin, "Re-Colonizing Memory" (boasblogs, 2020; a scholarly blog, not art media)
URL: https://boasblogs.org/dcntr/re-colonizing-memory/
- Argues the museum, a French state project, builds a positive story of the Republic, dilutes the specific crime of the transatlantic slave trade and avoids calling it a crime.
- Opens with Columbus; calls the museum an important step in bringing slavery into public debate.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public, led by the Région. The weak points are the cost rise from €21 million to €83 million, the 2023 audit findings, the Région's influence over the site and the unresolved ticket-income allegations. Worth watching: the outcome of the national financial prosecutor's inquiry.
