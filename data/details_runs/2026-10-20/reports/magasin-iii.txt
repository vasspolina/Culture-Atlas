MAGASIN III: Stockholm, Sweden
ID: magasin-iii
Website: https://www.magasin3.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum for contemporary art with its collection in Stockholm's Freeport (Frihamnsgatan 28) and a branch, Magasin III Jaffa, at 34 Olei Zion in Jaffa, Tel Aviv.
- Founded in 1987 by David Neuman and Robert Weil; it began as Magasin 3 Stockholm Konsthall, with no collection, and later became Magasin III Museum for Contemporary Art (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of what the name refers to.
- Building: a 1920s warehouse in the Freeport, an industrial space (Weil Foundation). The architecture firm DinellJohansson reworked the entrance and added a library and seminar room for the reopening after the 2017 closure (e-flux). The Jaffa space is 180 square metres (Kunstkritikk).
- Programmes: a collection of over 1,000 artworks (Weil Foundation), a master's programme in curating at Stockholm University, and Accelerator, an exhibition space at Stockholm University that opened in September 2019.
- Visiting: the Stockholm museum ended its public exhibition activities in May 2024 after a water leak in January 2024 and the landlord's renovation work, which is estimated to finish "not before 2027" (museum press release); the home page lists no opening hours or admission. In Jaffa, the exhibition "So Let Us Now Begin at the Beginning" runs from 4 June to 27 November 2026.

FUNDING
Magasin III: self-funded by its owner family: the Robert Weil Family Foundation, paid for by the family-owned investment company Proventus
- The museum describes itself as "an independent cultural institution under the auspices of the privately owned Proventus AB and the Robert Weil Family Foundation."
- The foundation was established in 2014 (Weil Foundation). Proventus "provides the funding that enables the foundation to fulfill its mission" (Weil Foundation).
- Proventus was founded in 1969, is owned by Robert Weil and family, and has been organised as a family office since 2017. It describes itself as "active in European capital markets" with a focus on long-term asset management (Weil Foundation). Wikipedia dates Weil's earlier company Weil Invest to 1969 and its merger with Proventus to 1980, and lists Proventus holdings and restructurings in glass, furniture and consumer brands (Kosta Boda, Artek, Aritmos) and in banking (Götabanken).
- No reporting found of fossil-fuel or energy holdings at Proventus: neither the Weil Foundation page nor Wikipedia lists any.
- Magasin III was Proventus' first endeavour, and Proventus maintained it (Weil Foundation). Robert Weil is chairman and founder of the foundation (Weil Foundation).
- Foundation board: Robert Weil, Sandra Weil, Andreas Weil and Pia Forsgren (Weil Foundation).
- In 2023 the museum said it is fully privately funded (CHART).
- No reporting found of amounts, annual accounts or outside donors.
- No reporting found of state or government funding.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found. The one art-press piece that raises a political question about the museum is an interview at the opening of the Jaffa branch.
1. Kunstkritikk: Karl Lydén, "- No. Or rather, yes and no" (2018)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/no-or-rather-yes-and-no/
- Opens by linking the US recognition of Jerusalem as Israel's capital to renewed debate over a cultural boycott of Israel, and puts the question to the museum's chairman.
- Neuman said artists will not be required to address Israeli society, that he would support work on the conflict and on the government's efforts to limit public debate, and that he rejects isolation as a strategy.
- An interview, not a critique; the museum's chairman answers the question.
2. CHART: interview with David Neuman and Tessa Praun, "Collecting for the private museum" (2023)
URL: https://chartartfair.com/journal/editorial/collecting-for-the-private-museum-magasin-iii
- Not criticism: the museum says private funding spares it constraints that national museums face.
- No author is named on the page.
No reporting found of a boycott call, open letter or protest aimed at Magasin III or the Weil family.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a single private source: the Weil family's Proventus, passed through the Robert Weil Family Foundation. No reporting found of a flagged funder, state money or a documented protest. The weak points are that no accounts, budgets or outside donors are published, and that the museum exhibits in Jaffa, which raised the cultural-boycott question at its 2018 opening.
