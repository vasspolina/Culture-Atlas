HONG-GAH MUSEUM: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: hong-gah-museum-taipei
Website: https://hong-gah.org.tw

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum (鳳甲美術館) on the 11th floor of the Huan-Yu Trade Building, 166 Daye Road, Beitou District, Taipei; it is within walking distance west of Qiyan Station on the Taipei Metro Red Line (hong-gah.org.tw, Wikipedia).
- Founded by the businessman and collector Chiu Tsai-hsing (Andrew Chew); it opened to the public on 25 October 1999 and moved to its current floor in 2008 (hong-gah.org.tw, Wikipedia).
- Named for Chiu's father, Chiu Feng-chia (邱鳳甲) (Chew's Culture Foundation page).
- Operates under Chew's Culture Foundation (邱再興文教基金會), founded in 1991 by Chiu with figures from business, culture and law (Chew's Culture Foundation page).
- The space has an entrance hall, a library, multi-purpose classrooms and special exhibition spaces (hong-gah.org.tw).
- Programme: solo exhibitions by Taiwanese and international artists, lectures and education, and the biennial Taiwan International Video Art Exhibition, started in 2008. Projects with the National Culture and Arts Foundation (2004, 2010) and the Beitou Culture Foundation (2014); a dual-venue presentation of the seventh video art exhibition with C-LAB (2020); the 2022 show "Ocean and Interpreters" won a Taishin Arts Award (hong-gah.org.tw).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:30 to 17:30, closed Mondays and on the Dragon Boat Festival, Mid-Autumn Festival and Chinese New Year; admission is free; groups of five or more can book guided tours a week ahead (hong-gah.org.tw).

FUNDING
Hong-gah Museum: self-funded: founder Chiu Tsai-hsing's private money
- Global Views (遠見) wrote in July 2006 that the museum runs entirely on private funds, without conglomerate backing; it charges no admission and its annual running cost was NT$14 million (Wang Yi-fen, "樂趣〉邱再興16年投入 科技老董播種藝術", 28 July 2006).
- Chiu's wealth came from electronics companies he founded in the 1970s and early 1980s, including 寰宇電子, 光元電子 and 光達電子; he stopped investing in electronics in 1990 and has said he never sold his artworks (Money Weekly, 高永謀, 22 May 2012). Global Views named him as chairman of 繼業 (Jiye) in 2006 and as head of the electronics company 繼業電子 in 2001 (遠見, 黃茜芳, 1 November 2001).
- The museum became a member of the Council for Cultural Affairs' local culture museums network in 2003 (hong-gah.org.tw).
- The foundation has received Wen-Hsin Awards: a special prize from the Council for Cultural Affairs in 2006, then from the Ministry of Culture the Arts Talent Cultivation Award in 2013, the Best Creativity Award in 2017 and the Long-Term Sponsorship Award in 2019 (Chew's Culture Foundation page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Chiu Tsai-hsing's own, made in electronics and spent through his family foundation; Global Views reported in 2006 that it runs on private funds alone at NT$14 million a year. The museum charges no admission, and the foundation has received Wen-Hsin Awards from the Council for Cultural Affairs and the Ministry of Culture.
