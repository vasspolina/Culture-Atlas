WHITE COLUMNS: New York, USA
ID: white-columns-new-york
Website: https://www.whitecolumns.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art space at 91 Horatio Street in Greenwich Village, next to the Whitney Museum and the High Line; it reopened there in April 2018 (White Columns). Wikipedia calls it New York's oldest alternative non-profit art space.
- Founded in 1970 in SoHo by artists including Jeffrey Lew and Gordon Matta-Clark (White Columns, Wikipedia).
- Earlier names and sites: 112 Workshop, also called 112 Greene Street. It took the name White Columns on moving to Spring Street, in 1979 according to Wikipedia and 1980 according to the gallery. It moved to Christopher Street in 1991, then in 1998 to a site on the edge of the Meatpacking District, then in 2018 to Horatio Street (Wikipedia).
- Programme: exhibitions, talks, screenings and events, plus the record label The Sound of White Columns. It describes its focus as artists who have not yet received wider critical, curatorial or commercial attention (White Columns).
- Matthew Higgs is Director and Chief Curator; Erin Somerville is Deputy Director and Curator (White Columns).
- Visiting: free admission; open Tuesday to Saturday, 11 am to 6 pm (the gallery's footer), with Tuesday to Friday listed for summer hours; groups of up to 20 during gallery hours (White Columns).

FUNDING
White Columns: mixed: public arts grants, foundations, and individual and gallery donors
- Fiscal year to August 2025: revenue $849,046, expenses $1,346,990, contributions $660,400, net assets $1,792,303. FY2024: revenue $1,232,904, expenses $1,259,657, contributions $982,590 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- Public funds: its support page says programmes are supported in part by public funds from the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs and the New York State Council on the Arts (White Columns).
- Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts: $100,000 in multi-year program support in the Fall 2022 cycle (Warhol Foundation).
- Support page (titled "Effective Sept. 2025"), list headed "Additional support is provided by", about 40 foundations and funds including: Andy Warhol Foundation, Arison Arts Foundation, Berkowitz Contemporary Foundation, Blavatnik Family Foundation, Cy Twombly Foundation, David Rockefeller Fund, Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, Henry Luce Foundation, Jacques and Natasha Gelman Foundation, Robert Lehman Foundation, Ruth Foundation for the Arts, Speyer Family Foundation, Stavros Niarchos Foundation, Willem de Kooning Foundation and Vanguard Charitable (White Columns).
- Supporters giving $1,000 or more include Marieluise Hessel, Beth Rudin DeWoody, Cindy Sherman, Jerry Speyer and Katherine Farley, Matthew Marks Charitable Trust, and the galleries Jack Shainman, Paula Cooper and Michael Werner. The 2025 benefit sponsors are Eric Diefenbach and J.K. Brown, William Peelle Jr., Sebastian Rodriguez, Betsy and Jonathan Stapleton, and Leslie and Michael Weissman (White Columns).
- Board: Gregory Miller (president), Stephen Abramson (treasurer), Polly Apfelbaum, Gavin Brown, Marilyn Minter, Kevin Yao; Higgs received $143,864 in compensation in FY2025 (White Columns, ProPublica).

Red flags: Blavatnik Family Foundation (fossil fuels)
- The Blavatnik Family Foundation is listed as additional support on the support page titled "Effective Sept. 2025" (White Columns).
- It is the foundation of Len Blavatnik, who made his fortune in the privatisation of state-owned aluminium and oil assets after the Soviet collapse and held a stake in TNK-BP through AAR, which Rosneft bought in 2013 for $28 billion (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
White Columns is a small non-profit financed by city and state arts funds, arts foundations, and individual and gallery donors, with annual revenue between $0.8 million and $1.7 million over the last five years. The Blavatnik Family Foundation, listed as additional support in September 2025, is the one funder tied to a fossil-fuel fortune; the other funders named on the support page are arts foundations, public agencies, galleries and individuals.
