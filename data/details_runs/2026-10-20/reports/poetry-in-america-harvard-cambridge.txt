POETRY IN AMERICA (HARVARD): Cambridge, USA
ID: poetry-in-america-harvard-cambridge
Website: https://www.poetryinamerica.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public television series and educational project that teaches American poems through conversations with poets, scholars and public figures; it is a programme, not a building.
- Created, hosted and directed by Harvard professor Elisa New; it began as online course content (InDepthNH, 2024). The series premiered in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Produced by the nonprofit Verse Video Education Inc., which ProPublica lists in Brookline, Massachusetts, with New as president.
- Distribution: GBH, the Boston PBS station, premiered it in 2018; the fourth season ran on Arizona PBS (Crimson; InDepthNH). In 2025 PBS stopped distributing it and cancelled the fifth season (Wikipedia).
- Education programmes include online courses and in-school programmes; the largest, at Arizona State University, lets high school students in Title I schools earn three college credits (InDepthNH).
- Visiting: no venue or admission; the site lists full episodes and a courses section.

FUNDING
Poetry in America: grant- and donor-funded: foundations, a federal agency, private gifts and university support
- New raised money through grants and names the National Endowment for the Humanities and the Sloan Foundation as donors (InDepthNH, 2024).
- Verse Video Education reported revenue of $1,715,821 in 2024, almost all contributions ($1,627,044), and $1,517,902 in 2016 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- Harvard gave the nonprofit office space and paid part of its staff salaries (The Boston Globe, as reported by The Harvard Crimson).
- Epstein gave $110,000 in 2016, and emails show New and Epstein discussed a donation of $500,000 (Crimson; Wikipedia).
- Leon Black, an Apollo executive, made a donation to New that Epstein brokered (Crimson). In a 2015 email New wrote that the Leon Black gift "changed everything" for her at Harvard (Globe).
- No reporting found of the National Endowment for the Humanities or Sloan amounts; a Sloan grant page could not be opened.

Red flags: Epstein-linked
- 2016: Jeffrey Epstein gave Verse Video Education $110,000 (Crimson, citing the Globe). Harvard had barred its affiliates from taking Epstein's gifts in 2008; New told the Globe she did not know of the bar.
- Emails released by the House Oversight Committee in November 2025 show Epstein advised New on a grant proposal, discussion topics and interviewees (Globe). New met Epstein numerous times from 2013 to 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Harvard's 2020 report on Epstein ties acknowledged the $110,000 but did not examine it because it was not a direct gift to Harvard (Crimson). Harvard opened a new review in November 2025 that did not say whether it would cover the nonprofit (Crimson).
- GBH says it cut ties with New in 2023 over the Epstein association; New's spokesperson says she ended the relationship in 2022 (Crimson). PBS stopped airing the show in late 2025 (Crimson).
- New's statement says she regrets accepting Epstein's donation (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The series was funded in part by Epstein: a documented $110,000 gift in 2016, plus a brokered gift from Leon Black and a discussed $500,000, with Harvard supplying space and salary support. PBS and its station partners dropped the show over the link, and Harvard opened a new review in November 2025. Worth watching: the outcome of that review and whether the nonprofit publishes its own accounting of the gifts.
