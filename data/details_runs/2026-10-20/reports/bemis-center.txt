BEMIS CENTER: Omaha, United States
ID: bemis-center
Website: https://www.bemiscenter.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Artist residency and contemporary art centre at 724 S. 12th Street in the Old Market Historic District of downtown Omaha, Nebraska.
- Founded in 1981 by Jun Kaneko, Tony Hepburn, Lorne Falk and Ree Schonlau as "Alternative Worksite" (stated by the centre).
- It was formerly housed in the Bemis Omaha Bag Company Building at 614 South 11th Street, severely damaged in a fire in 1999. In 1984 Schonlau set up a consortium of the City of Omaha, the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, private and corporate foundations and the Mercer family, who owned the vacant Bemis Bag Building (Wikipedia).
- Its home since 1995 is a 100,000 sq. ft. former McCord-Brady wholesale grocery warehouse. A capital campaign launched in 2009 added five live/work studios and the 9,000 sq. ft. Okada Sculpture + Ceramics Facility, completed October 2011 (stated by the centre).
- On site: exhibitions, private live/work residency studios, a Curator-in-Residence post (since 2017), a Sound Art + Experimental Music programme (since 2019) with the LOW END music venue, and public programmes.
- Visiting: free admission to exhibitions and LOW END performances. Open Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11am to 5pm, Thursday 11am to 9pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. The centre states it is committed to accessible facilities; the Accessibility page was not read.

FUNDING
Bemis Center: grant- and donor-funded: foundations, government arts funders and local donors
- Sponsor page, $20,000+ tier (undated): Art Bridges, Claire M. Hubbard Foundation, Douglas County, Heider Family Foundation, Holland Foundation, Mammel Family Foundation, the Mellon Foundation, National Endowment for the Arts, National Indemnity Company, Nebraska Arts Council / Nebraska Cultural Endowment, Omaha Steaks, Ruth Foundation for the Arts, Sherwood Foundation, Millwork Commons and a number of named individuals and families.
- Mellon Foundation: $500,000 over 36 months, awarded 6 September 2018, for a new residency programme for sound artists; a second $500,000 over 40 months, awarded 25 May 2022, to continue it (Mellon grant records).
- Public money: Douglas County (county), the NEA (federal) and the Nebraska Arts Council (state) are on the sponsor page. Humanities Nebraska / Nebraska Cultural Endowment sits in the $5,000 to $9,999 tier.
- Accounts (Form 990 data on ProPublica): FY2025 revenue $3,532,405 and expenses $2,535,329; FY2024 revenue $1,910,739 and expenses $2,392,590; FY2023 revenue $1,786,406 and expenses $2,362,095. Contributions were about 93.8% of FY2025 revenue; net assets were $4,745,970. Executive Director Chris Cook received $167,493 in 2025.
- Todd Simon is board president (About page); the sponsor page lists a Todd D. Simon Family in the $20,000+ tier.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, arms, tobacco, gambling or Epstein-linked funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Bemis looks clean: it comes from foundations, public arts funders and local donors, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are dependence on contributions (about 94% of FY2025 revenue), operating deficits in FY2023 and FY2024, an undated sponsor list, and a board president whose name matches a top-tier sponsor listing. Worth watching is the stated downtown facility renovation and its capital campaign.
