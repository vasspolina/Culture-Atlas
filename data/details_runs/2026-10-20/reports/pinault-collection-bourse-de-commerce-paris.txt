PINAULT COLLECTION (BOURSE DE COMMERCE): Paris, France
ID: pinault-collection-bourse-de-commerce-paris
Website: https://www.pinaultcollection.com

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum of the Pinault Collection at 2 rue de Viarmes in Paris, in the former Bourse de Commerce near the Louvre.
- The Pinault Collection is "the legal entity holding the artistic and cultural assets" of François Pinault, founded in 1999 and held by Groupe Artémis; it also runs Palazzo Grassi and Punta della Dogana in Venice (Wikipedia).
- The museum opened on 22 May 2021 (Wikipedia); the collection counts about 10,000 works (Pinault Collection website).
- The building began as a circular wheat exchange built in 1763 to 1767, was rebuilt by Henri Blondel in 1888 to 1889, and housed commodities trading until floor trading ended in 1998 (Wikipedia).
- Tadao Ando designed the conversion, with a concrete cylinder inside as the main gallery (Wikipedia). Ando's changes were reversible because of the building's heritage rules (The Spaces, 19 May 2021).
- The museum is open every day except Tuesday and 1 May, 11 am to 7 pm, with late opening until 9 pm on Fridays; entry is free from 5 to 9 pm on the first Saturday of each month.

FUNDING
Pinault Collection (Bourse de Commerce): privately funded: the Pinault family, through Groupe Artémis
- Pinault Collection is a private company held by Groupe Artémis, with François Pinault as president (Wikipedia).
- Groupe Artémis is an investment holding company, 100 percent owned by Financière Pinault and presided over by François-Henri Pinault since 2003; it also holds the Pinault family's stake in the luxury group Kering, plus Christie's and, through its wine arm Artémis Domaines, Château Latour (Wikipedia).
- In 2016 Paris mayor Anne Hidalgo offered Pinault a 50-year lease on the city-owned building for a lump sum of 15 million euros plus yearly fees; Wikipedia gives the renovation cost as $195 million.
- In April 2019 Artémis said the Pinault family would not claim the tax benefit the Bourse project could receive under the 2003 sponsorship law, saying it was "out of the question to charge French taxpayers" (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 18 April 2019).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In April 2019 Jean-Jacques Aillagon, a former French culture minister and director general of the Pinault Collection, called for tax relief of up to 90 percent for companies donating to rebuild Notre-Dame; critics on social media questioned his standing to offer such advice given his role as an adviser to François Pinault, and Artémis, which pledged 100 million euros, said its donation would not be tax-deductible (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 18 April 2019, "Director of Pinault Collection proposes controversial 90% tax breaks for Notre Dame donors").
- Connection to the space: the Pinault Collection, of which Aillagon was director general in 2019, runs the Bourse de Commerce.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Bourse de Commerce is the Pinault family's, through Groupe Artémis, under a 50-year lease from the City of Paris that includes a 15 million euro lump sum. Artémis stated in 2019 that it would not claim the sponsorship-law tax benefit on the project.
