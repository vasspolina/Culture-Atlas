PALM SPRINGS ART MUSEUM: Palm Springs, USA
ID: palm-springs-art-museum-palm-springs
Website: https://www.psmuseum.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 101 Museum Drive, Palm Springs, with 28 galleries, two sculpture gardens, an 85-seat lecture hall and a 433-seat theater (museum website).
- Founded in 1938 as the Palm Springs Desert Museum at La Plaza in downtown Palm Springs; it built its first permanent structure in 1958 and changed its name to Palm Springs Art Museum in April 2005 (museum website). Wikipedia gives the renaming year as 1982.
- The board named Christine Vendredi executive director on 29 September 2025; she had been interim director since April and joined as chief curator in April 2024 (Palm Springs Post, 29 September 2025). Adam Lerner, director since summer 2021, resigned in the spring (Los Angeles Times, Christopher Knight, 10 November 2025). The board chair is Craig Hartzman (museum website).

FUNDING
Palm Springs Art Museum: mixed: donations, endowment draws and earned income
- A 501(c)(3) non-profit. Fiscal year ending June 2024: revenue 9,732,898 dollars, expenses 11,442,380 dollars, a deficit of 1,709,482 dollars, net assets 37,136,838 dollars; contributions of 6,159,778 dollars were 63.3 percent of revenue (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer). Expenses also exceeded revenue in fiscal years 2022 and 2023.
- Endowment: slightly over 17 million dollars against an operating budget of about 10.5 million dollars; endowment draws over the past decade totalled about 8 million dollars against about 500,000 dollars in contributions (Los Angeles Times, 18 November 2025).
- Deaccessioning: in fall 2020 the museum began deaccessioning some artworks in its collection (museum website). The Los Angeles Times reports that during the 2020 pandemic closure it sold a 1974 Helen Frankenthaler painting for 4.7 million dollars at auction (Knight, 18 November 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns governance and finances, not the programme, and comes from the Los Angeles Times critic Christopher Knight and a news site reporting a whistleblower complaint.
1. Los Angeles Times: Christopher Knight, "Commentary: Palm Springs Art Museum faces trustee revolt over new director" (10 November 2025)
URL: https://www.aol.com/news/commentary-palm-springs-art-museum-200256338.html
- Patsy Marino, chair of the search committee, quit the board citing "inappropriate interference and attempts to influence the process"; two more of 22 trustees have since left.
- Marino's letter says the committee "did not interview a single outside candidate" and that the job was not posted to professional websites for four months after Lerner's departure was announced. Knight argues the committee became a hiring panel for an in-house candidate.
- Board chair Craig Hartzman called it "a very fair process" and said four of six committee members approved the choice. The museum states that one trustee resigned over the process and other departures were unrelated (museum press release, 13 November 2025).
2. Los Angeles Times: Christopher Knight, "Commentary: Audit questions roil the Palm Springs Art Museum" (18 November 2025)
URL: https://www.aol.com/news/commentary-audit-questions-roil-palm-203405698.html
- On 15 January the accounting firm Eide Bailly attached a "letter of material weakness" to the audit of the museum's 2024 books, citing a "deficiency in internal control" in six areas including endowment spending reporting and admissions revenue recording.
- The 2019 ending endowment balance was 3 million dollars higher than the 2020 beginning balance; a trustee task force's notes call it "highly unlikely" the funds were stolen.
- At least eight trustees have resigned since spring; the remaining 19 are below the 20-member minimum in the bylaws.
- Board chair Craig Hartzman did not respond to requests for comment.
3. USA Art News: Helen, "Whistleblower Complaint Alleges Misconduct at Palm Springs Art Museum" (29 April 2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/whistleblower-complaint-alleges-misconduct-at-palm-springs-art-museum
- Unverified: an unnamed whistleblower, in emails in July and December 2025 to California Attorney General Rob Bonta and U.S. Attorney Adam Gordon (Southern District of California), alleges that the museum moved money between accounts to cover cash shortages, left 544,403 dollars unpaid to its art acquisition account as of 30 June 2024, and reclassified restricted gifts as unrestricted in 2019 and 2020.
- A museum spokesperson told USA Art News that a special committee under the Audit Committee hired a law firm and a forensic accounting firm for an independent investigation.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on donations, endowment draws and earned income. Its documented weak points are operating deficits in fiscal years 2022 to 2024, the auditor's letter of material weakness, trustee resignations after the director hire in September 2025, and an unverified whistleblower complaint on restricted funds. No flagged-industry funder is documented.
