DRUG ELIMINATION MUSEUM: Yangon, Myanmar
ID: drug-elimination-museum
Website: https://drugmuseum.gov.mm (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum on 15.11 acres at the corner of Kyun Taw and Hanthawaddy roads, Kamayut Township (museum website), on the former site of Kyandaw Cemetery, once the city's largest (Wikipedia).
- Built under the guidance of Senior General Than Shwe, chairman of the State Peace and Development Council; Lieutenant General Khin Nyunt, the council's Secretary (1), laid the foundation stone on 25 July 1998 and opened it on 26 June 2001 (museum website).
- Its inscription states two aims: that the whole people, youth and national races included, learn lessons and abstain from drugs, and that the international community learn of successive governments' efforts, among them military operations from 1975 to 1988 and a 15-year plan for 1999 to 2014 (museum website).
- A four-storey building, ground floor included (museum website); in 2013 the first exhibit floor held maps of trafficking routes, military records of seizures, mannequins re-enacting army drug-war operations and a diorama blaming a Westerner for bringing in drugs, the second paintings of junta generals and a display on the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control, and the third a five-scene story of a boy's decline to death and a mock rehabilitation centre (Vice).
- Visiting: daily 9.00 to 16.00 except Mondays and public holidays; 100 kyat for Myanmar visitors and 3 US dollars for foreigners, cameras 200 kyat or 5 dollars (museum website).

FUNDING
Drug Elimination Museum: state: the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control under the Ministry of Home Affairs
- Operator: the museum's website is tied to the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control (CCDAC), whose chairman is the Union Minister for Home Affairs; the chairman's messages on the site are signed by ministers Soe Htut (2022), Htun Htun Naung (2025) and Nyunt Win Swe (2026) (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one critical piece describes a neglected relic of junta propaganda.
1. Vice: Alex Palmer, "From Insanity to Death: Inside Burma's Drug Eradication Museum" (2013)
URL: https://www.vice.com/da/article/inside-burmas-drug-eradication-museum/
- Quotes a general's words near the entrance: "The big nations are giving human rights, democracy and drug elimination as excuses".
- Describes unkempt and nearly abandoned grounds, a broken interactive display, staff napping at the front desk and rare visitors, mostly curious foreigners.

BOTTOM LINE
State money, which alone is no red flag on the atlas. The weak point is its origin and content as a junta propaganda project.
