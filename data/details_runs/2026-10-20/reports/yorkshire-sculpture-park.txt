YORKSHIRE SCULPTURE PARK: Wakefield, UK
ID: yorkshire-sculpture-park
Website: https://ysp.org.uk

OVERVIEW
- Open-air sculpture park of about 500 acres in the grounds of Bretton Hall, an 18th-century estate at West Bretton, Wakefield, West Yorkshire (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Peter Murray and opened in 1977; Wikipedia calls it the UK's first sculpture park.
- The hall was a family home until the mid-20th century and then became Bretton Hall College (Wikipedia).
- The grounds hold 18th-century follies, an ice house, a camellia house, a deer shelter converted into an installation by James Turrell, and the Grade II* listed St Bartholomew's Chapel, built in 1744 and restored as gallery space (Wikipedia).
- Indoor spaces include the Bothy Gallery, the Longside Gallery shared with the Arts Council Collection, and The Weston, a visitor centre with a gallery, restaurant and shop that was shortlisted for the Stirling Prize in 2019 (Wikipedia). The Peter Murray Gallery, YSP Learning and a Country Park are named on the park's accessibility page.
- Visiting: an entry ticket is required and the gates close at 17.00 in winter and 18.00 in summer. Standard entry costs £10 (£12 with a donation or Gift Aid, £4 after 3pm), concessions £7; under-18s, essential companions and pass holders enter free. The Weston gallery, shop and restaurant are free to enter without a ticket. Car parking is free.
- Accessibility: the Visitor Centre is step-free with lifts; four off-road mobility scooters can be hired free; manual wheelchairs can be borrowed for the galleries; there are Blue Badge spaces, a Changing Places toilet and large print and braille materials. Some outdoor paths are uneven and steep.

FUNDING
Yorkshire Sculpture Park: public and private mix: Arts Council England, Wakefield Council, the Bramall Foundation, the Dunard Fund, Roger Evans, individuals and corporate partners
- Arts Council England: £4,010,397 over 2023 to 2026, matching what YSP had received for the previous 12 years and equal to 17% of annual income (stated by YSP, press release of 4 November 2022). YSP director Clare Lilley called it a "standstill award".
- The supporters page names five core funders, which it says "underpins all our work": Arts Council England, Roger Evans, the Liz and Terry Bramall Foundation, Wakefield Council and the Dunard Fund.
- Corporate partners named on the same page, who support YSP through donations, sponsorship and in-kind support: Archivist, Ashleigh Signs, HSBC, Luscombe Drinks Ltd, and Sandal MINI and BMW.
- Friends and Patrons provide annual income (stated by YSP).

BOTTOM LINE
The documented money behind YSP is Arts Council England's £4,010,397 for 2023 to 2026, 17% of annual income (stated by YSP). Wakefield Council, the Liz and Terry Bramall Foundation, the Dunard Fund, Roger Evans, Friends and Patrons and corporate partners including HSBC also support the park.
