HEARD MUSEUM: Phoenix, USA
ID: heard-museum-phoenix
Website: https://heard.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of American Indian art at 2301 N. Central Avenue, Phoenix, near the Encanto/Central Avenue light rail station.
- Founded in 1929 by Dwight B. and Maie Bartlett Heard to house their collection; much of its archaeological material came from the La Ciudad ruin they bought in 1926 (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founders, the Heard family.
- Bennie Gonzales, architect of Scottsdale City Hall, designed parts of the building; branches in Surprise and North Scottsdale closed in 2009 and 2014 (Wikipedia).
- More than 40,000 items, a library and archives of over 34,000 volumes, an exhibition on Native children's experience of government boarding schools, and the Heard Museum Guild Indian Fair and Market, held each March since 1958 (Wikipedia).
- Open 10 am to 4 pm daily from September to May and Tuesday to Sunday in summer; school groups free, $5 a person with a SNAP EBT card under Museums for All, and free entry for Bank of America cardholders on the first full weekend of each month.

FUNDING
Heard Museum: private non-profit: individual and family donors, trustees and the Indian Fair
- Form 990 for the year to September 2025: revenue $23,249,082, of which $18,166,069 in contributions and $2,721,375 from programme services; expenses $14,062,323; total assets $42,613,367 (ProPublica). Fiscal 2024 revenue was $10,668,248.
- Director and CEO David M. Roche holds the Dickey Family Director post and was paid $415,011 (ProPublica; the board page gives his title); Karen Abraham chairs the board.
- In September 2026 the museum announced a $36 million campaign alongside a $12 million campus project: an expanded restaurant, a Creativity Hub and a new entrance (Hoodline). No donors were named.
- The Indian Fair raises money for the museum's programmes (stated by the space). No reporting found that names APS or SRP as Indian Fair sponsors; the sponsorship packet checked names no sponsors in readable text.

Red flags: former coal executive on the board
- Gregory H. Boyce is a current trustee (stated by the space).
- Boyce spent 12 years at Peabody Energy, an American coal mining company (Wikipedia), and retired as executive chairman in 2015 (University of Arizona); he was chief executive from 2006, and Peabody filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 13 April 2016 (Wikipedia).
- He joined the board of Newmont Mining in 2015 and is past chairman of the National Mining Association (University of Arizona).
- No reporting found of a gift from Boyce or Peabody to the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found. The one documented dispute is a 2007 donor complaint about an exhibition, reported in the philanthropy press.
1. The Chronicle of Philanthropy: Alison Stein Wellner, "Battling a 'Holy' War" (2007)
URL: https://www.philanthropy.com/news/battling-a-holy-war/
- Two major donors objected to Hulleah Tsinhnahjinnie's video "November 8, 2005" in "Holy Land: Diaspora and the Desert", which compared Native Americans' loss of land to the Jahalin Bedouins' loss of theirs to Israel.
- Director Frank Goodyear kept the video on view with a label saying it reflected only the artist's views, and said the museum would have been better prepared to respond to the criticism; Bill Straus of the Anti-Defamation League's Phoenix chapter called it one-sided.
- Shows donor pressure on the programme; the donors kept giving.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly private donations, with no corporate or fossil fuel funder documented. The weak point is the board: trustee Gregory Boyce led Peabody Energy, a coal company, until 2015. Worth watching: the names behind the $36 million campaign and the Indian Fair sponsors, which the sources checked do not list.
