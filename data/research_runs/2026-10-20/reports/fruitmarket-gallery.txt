FRUITMARKET GALLERY – Edinburgh, UK
Website: https://www.fruitmarket.co.uk (support pages loaded 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at 45 Market Street, in a former 1938 fruit and vegetable market. Opened in 1974. Renovated by Richard Murphy in 1994; a £4.3M expansion by Reiach and Hall into a neighbouring warehouse reopened in July 2021. Free entry. Director: Fiona Bradley (Wikipedia).
- Scottish charity SC005576 (OSCR).

FUNDING
Fruitmarket Gallery: mainly public (Creative Scotland), with trusts, foundations, galleries and patrons.
- Income was £2,126,247 and spending £2,655,141 in the year to 31 Mar 2025 (OSCR).
- Creative Scotland Multi-Year Funding: £866,667, then £1,133,333 and £1,133,333, totalling £3,133,333 (Edinburgh Reporter, Jan 2025; the year columns are unlabelled).
- Programme supporters it names: Art Fund, Barry Fish, Bloomberg, Edinburgh World Heritage, EVOC, Eva Hesse Charitable Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, Hinrichsen Foundation, Young Start (National Lottery Community Fund), Paul Hamlyn Foundation, Jane Hamlyn (Frith Street Gallery), Queensberry House Trust, RS Macdonald Charitable Trust, Thomas Dane Gallery, William Grant Foundation, White Cube (own site, undated).
- Grants recorded on 360Giving, 2002–2025, total £1,055,282. They include Paul Hamlyn £233,000, Wolfson £260,000, William Grant £175,000, Garfield Weston £155,000 and Foyle £135,000.
- No reporting found of City of Edinburgh Council funding in sources retrieved this run.

Red flags: Baillie Gifford sponsorship ended under campaign pressure
- On 7 June 2024 the Fruitmarket ended a six-year sponsorship from Baillie Gifford worth £25,000 a year, "in light of recent discussions of Baillie Gifford's support of culture in Scotland" (The Scotsman).
- The campaign came from Arts Workers for Palestine Scotland (an activist group) and Fossil Free Books. They cite the firm's investments linked to Israel and to fossil fuels. Baillie Gifford calls these claims "seriously misleading". Unverified: the claimed figure of nearly £10bn invested in Israel-linked companies (activist claim, disputed by the firm).
- Baillie Gifford's own sponsorship directory still listed "The Fruitmarket Gallery" when checked on 2026-10-20.

BOTTOM LINE
Funding is largely public, plus mainstream UK trusts and commercial galleries. In 2024 it dropped Baillie Gifford, a small sponsor in money terms, after campaigning. Worth watching: Baillie Gifford's own directory still lists it, which is either out of date or means some link remains.
