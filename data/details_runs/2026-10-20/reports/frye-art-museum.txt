FRYE ART MUSEUM: Seattle, USA
ID: frye-art-museum
Website: https://fryemuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Free modern and contemporary art museum at 704 Terry Avenue in the First Hill neighbourhood of Seattle, with a collection of nearly 1,600 works (Frye Art Museum, 2025 annual report).
- Founded under the will of Charles and Emma Frye, who collected paintings in their First Hill home; the museum opened in 1952 a few blocks from it (Frye Art Museum). Charles Frye owned a Seattle meatpacking plant, and his will required free admission (Wikipedia).
- The original building was designed by Paul Thiry; Rick Sundberg expanded and renovated it in the mid-1990s (Wikipedia). The museum names Olson Sundberg Kundig as the architects of its building.
- Organised as a 501(c)(3) private operating foundation (Frye Art Museum).
- Shows living and contemporary artists; 2025 exhibitions included Hugh Hayden, Alex Katz, Jamie Wyeth and Beau Dick (2025 annual report).
- Admission is always free (Frye Art Museum). Jamilee Lacy is executive director (2025 annual report).

FUNDING
Frye Art Museum: investment-funded: distributions made up 75.6 percent of fiscal year 2025 revenue, with donors and public arts agencies
- Fiscal year 2025: revenue of $9,178,533 and expenses of $9,254,774. Investment distributions were $6,942,691 (75.6 percent of revenue), donations and other support $1,548,989 (16.9 percent), earned store and event revenue $275,807 (3 percent), other $203,275 (2.2 percent), memberships $83,529 (0.9 percent) and in-kind donations $74,149 (0.8 percent) (2025 annual report).
- Funding agencies named in 2025: 4Culture, ArtsFund (with the Guendolen Carkeek Plestcheeff Fund for the Decorative & Design Arts) and the City of Seattle Office of Arts & Culture (2025 annual report).
- Foundations named in 2025: Art Bridges, the Richard & Maude Ferry Foundation, Pruzan Foundation, Raynier Institute & Foundation and Teiger Foundation (2025 annual report).
- Corporate donors named in 2025: Gates Foundation Matching Gifts Program, Gates Ventures, Microsoft Matching Gifts Program, The Partners Group, Perkins Coie LLP, Pivotal, Skyline at First Hill Retirement Community, Starbucks, T-Mobile and Treadwell Insurance (2025 annual report).
- Individual donors giving $100,000 or more in 2025: Doug Carmean and Margaret Morris, and Sherry Raisbeck. Stephanie and Jon DeVaan, Gail A. Goralski, Julie and Rogers Weed, and Stuart and Lucy Williams gave $25,000 to $99,999 (2025 annual report).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, union picket and petition after the April 2020 layoffs
- Who and when: on Friday 10 April 2020 the museum laid off 21 non-exempt part-time and on-call employees, one third of the workforce according to Hyperallergic, citing pandemic losses. Frye workers who are members of the Art Workers Union (AWU), which represents the museum's security guards, held a socially distant picket outside the museum that day (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 13 April 2020; The Spectator, Michaela Moore, 23 April 2020).
- Demands: workers started an online petition asking the museum to reinstate the 21 workers and provide full pandemic pay and health coverage through the rest of the shutdown (Hyperallergic).
- Allegation: protesters said two union representatives were unjustly targeted while contract negotiations were ongoing; Caitlin Lee, a founding AWU member and bargaining committee member, was one of those laid off (Hyperallergic).
- Museum response: Joseph Rosa, then director and CEO, said two of the 21 were AWU members and that "Union affiliation had no bearing on the lay-offs"; he put the museum's losses at over $100,000 between March and May 2020. The six trustees suspended their stipends and upper management, including the director, took a salary cut from 6 April 2020 (Hyperallergic).
- Background: the union was started by museum security staff, and the board had first declined to recognise it, before a vote overseen by the National Labor Relations Board that the union won unanimously (The Spectator, Seattle University student newspaper).

BOTTOM LINE
The Frye is funded mostly by investment distributions (75.6 percent of fiscal year 2025 revenue) at a museum founded under Charles and Emma Frye's will, with the rest from donors, foundations and Seattle public arts agencies named in its annual report. The 2020 layoffs of 21 part-time staff drew a union picket and a petition, and the director said union membership was not the reason.
