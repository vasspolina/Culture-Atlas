A.M. QATTAN FOUNDATION: Ramallah, Palestine
ID: am-qattan-foundation-ramallah
Website: https://qattanfoundation.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural and educational development organisation based in Ramallah, West Bank, registered in the UK as a charity (number 1029450) with company number 02171893 (foundation site footer).
- Established in London in 1993 by the Palestinian philanthropist Abdel Mohsin Al-Qattan; his son Omar Al-Qattan chairs it (The Art Newspaper, 25 June 2018; Wikipedia).
- Building: the Qattan Cultural Centre in Ramallah, designed by the Spanish firm Donaire Arquitectos and due to open to the public on 28 June 2018, with an area of about 7,700 square metres (The Art Newspaper, 25 June 2018). Completion was scheduled for 2016, but security and construction problems delayed it (The Art Newspaper).
- On site: a library of over 22,000 books, a gallery, a theatre, artist studios, a guesthouse, a restaurant and outdoor spaces (foundation site). The foundation also runs the Qattan Cultural Centre in Gaza and the Mosaic Rooms in London, which the foundation announced on 15 January 2026 is becoming an independent organisation (foundation site; foundation news).
- Programmes: a Culture and Arts Programme with grants and studio grants, exhibitions, a cinema programme and education work for children aged 4 to 18 (foundation site; strategy summary).
- Visiting: tours are booked by email (foundation site).

FUNDING
A.M. Qattan Foundation: mainly family-funded, with Swedish funding for a visual arts programme
- The Al-Qattan family provides at least 60% of the foundation's funds and an endowment was set up for the building (The Art Newspaper, 2018). The Art Newspaper put the building cost at $15 million to $18 million.
- Sweden funded the "Visual Arts: A Flourishing Field" project; on 24 October 2019 the foundation signed grant agreements totalling about 5.5 million Swedish kronor (about $540,000) with four Palestinian arts organisations (foundation news, 30 October 2019).
- The Mosaic Rooms in London became an independent organisation and distinct legal entity, announced on 15 January 2026 (foundation news).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

- Describes delays and cost overruns from an inexperienced contractor, and the difficulty of moving materials across the Israeli border; the problems are stated by Omar Al-Qattan.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the foundation is mainly the founder's family, plus Swedish programme grants. The weak point is that the family supplies at least 60% of the funds. Worth watching: how the Mosaic Rooms' independence in 2026 changes the foundation's finances.
