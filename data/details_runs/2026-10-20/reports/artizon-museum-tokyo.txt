ARTIZON MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: artizon-museum-tokyo
Website: https://www.artizon.museum/en/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1-7-2 Kyobashi, Chuo-ku, Tokyo, with galleries on three floors in the lower section of Museum Tower Kyobashi (e-flux, Artizon site). Its best-known holdings are Impressionist works and modern Japanese Western-style paintings (e-flux).
- Founded as the Bridgestone Museum of Art by Ishibashi Shojiro, founder of Bridgestone, on the second floor of the Bridgestone Building in Kyobashi; it opened on 8 January 1952 (Artizon history page).
- In 1956 Ishibashi established the Ishibashi Foundation, which took over the museum; in 1961 he gave the foundation most of his collection (Artizon history page).
- The museum closed for renovation on 18 May 2015. Museum Tower Kyobashi was completed on 1 July 2019, and the museum reopened in January 2020 as the Artizon Museum, a name combining "ART" and "HORIZON" (Artizon history page, e-flux).
- The earlier name came from Bridgestone, the company Ishibashi founded in 1931, whose name reverses the meanings of the characters in his surname (Artizon founder page).
- Open 10:00 to 18:00, until 20:00 on Fridays; closed Mondays; entry closes 30 minutes before closing. Students enter free with an online reservation (Artizon site).

FUNDING
Artizon Museum: private foundation, operated by the Ishibashi Foundation, which the founder of Bridgestone set up in 1956
- The Ishibashi Foundation was accredited as a Public Interest Incorporated Foundation in 2012 (Artizon history page). Its two programmes are museum operations and grants for arts, culture and education (Ishibashi Foundation site).
- Its balance sheet at 31 December 2025 shows total assets of 691.38 billion yen and net assets of 691.09 billion yen. Basic assets of 468.73 billion yen include investment securities of 442.76 billion yen, art works of 7.23 billion yen, buildings of 13.16 billion yen and land of 5.58 billion yen (Ishibashi Foundation balance sheet).
- 2026 officers: chairman 石橋 寬, managing director 西嶋 大二, five directors, two auditors and seven councillors (Ishibashi Foundation site, list dated 18 March 2026).
- Earlier museum: the Ishibashi Museum of Art in Kurume, built by Ishibashi in 1956, was handed to the City of Kurume in 2016 and renamed the Kurume City Art Museum (Artizon history page).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bridgestone's Firestone unit was sued on 17 November 2005 in a US federal court under the Alien Tort Statute by 23 Liberian children who worked on its rubber plantation in Liberia (International Rights Advocates).
- The Seventh Circuit opinion, written by Judge Richard Posner and posted on 11 July 2011, left summary judgment for Firestone in place on the child-labour claims because the plaintiffs had not shown a sufficiently clear rule of customary international law on child labour, and held that corporations can be liable under the Alien Tort Statute (International Rights Advocates; Business and Human Rights Resource Centre).
- Connection to Artizon, as history: Shojiro Ishibashi founded Bridgestone in 1931 and set up the Ishibashi Foundation, which runs the museum, in 1956; the museum was called the Bridgestone Museum of Art until it closed in 2015 (Artizon history and founder pages).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by a private public-interest foundation built on its founder's collection and assets, with total assets of 691.38 billion yen at the end of 2025. The founder's company, Bridgestone, faced the Liberian child-labour suit through its Firestone unit; the suit named Firestone, not the foundation. The foundation's balance sheet shows 442.76 billion yen of investment securities.
