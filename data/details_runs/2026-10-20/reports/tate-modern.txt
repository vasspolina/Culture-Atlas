TATE MODERN: London, UK
ID: tate-modern
Website: https://www.tate.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Gallery of modern and contemporary art at Bankside, London SE1 9TG, one of Tate's galleries (stated by the space).
- Housed in the former Bankside Power Station, designed by Sir Giles Gilbert Scott and built between 1947 and 1963; it closed in 1981 (Wikipedia).
- Herzog & de Meuron won the conversion competition in January 1995; the Queen opened Tate Modern on 11 May 2000. The Switch House extension opened on 17 June 2016 (Wikipedia). Tate Modern had 4,432,890 visitors in 2024-25 (Tate Annual Report).
- Visiting: open Sunday to Thursday 10am to 6pm and Friday to Saturday 10am to 9pm; entry to the collection is free, exhibitions are ticketed; wheelchairs can be borrowed free (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Tate Modern: mainly public and philanthropic: government grant-in-aid, corporate partners and private donors
- Tate group income in 2024-25 was 167.15 million pounds, including grant-in-aid 41.44 million, capital grant-in-aid 12.46 million and fundraising 41.68 million (Tate Annual Report 2024-25).
- Corporate partners in 2024-25 include Anthropic, Bank of America, Bloomberg, Hyundai Motor and UNIQLO. Hyundai Motor extended its support, including the Turbine Hall commission, to 2036 (Tate Annual Report).
- The Blavatnik Family Foundation gave grants to "Sargent and Fashion" at Tate Britain in 2024-25 and is a current donor (Tate Annual Report).
- The extension was funded by 50 million pounds from the UK government and 7 million from the London Development Agency (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- BP sponsored Tate for 26 years. On 11 March 2016 it announced the deal would end in spring 2017, citing an "extremely challenging business environment"; it had last renewed in 2011. Tate disclosed in January 2015, after a three-year tribunal fight, that BP paid 4 million pounds between 1990 and 2006 (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 11 March 2016).
- Len Blavatnik gave more than 50 million pounds in 2011, the largest gift in the gallery's history, and the Switch House was renamed the Blavatnik Building in May 2017 (Wikipedia). His Access Industries group bought 40 percent of Tyumen Oil in 1997 for 800 million dollars; Rosneft paid 28 billion dollars for its 50 percent stake in TNK-BP in 2013 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Opioids
- 2019: Tate said it would take no further Sackler money, having received over 4 million pounds from Sackler family trusts. On 8 February 2022 The Art Newspaper reported Tate would remove the Sackler name from five places: the escalators, lifts and a gallery at Tate Modern, and the central octagon and a gallery at Tate Britain (Cristina Ruiz). Tate said removal was "mutually agreed" and gave no reason.

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- Liberate Tate protested BP's sponsorship from 2010 (Wikipedia). Culture Unstained, a campaign site, credits the Art Not Oil coalition and arts workers with ending the BP deal (April 2016).
- 26 November 2024: more than 900 artists and art workers signed an open letter asking Tate to cut ties with Zabludowicz Art Trust, Zabludowicz Art Projects and Outset Contemporary Art Fund, citing Israel, ahead of the 3 December Turner Prize ceremony. Euronews reports no response from Tate.
- 2024: protesters outside Tate Modern accused Blavatnik of helping stifle criticism of Benjamin Netanyahu; his spokesperson denied involvement (Wikipedia, Len Blavatnik). The page gives no exact date.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism of Tate's funders, mostly BP and the Sacklers.
1. The Art Newspaper: Cristina Ruiz, "Tate's U-turn on Sacklers" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/02/08/tates-u-turn-on-sackler-naming
- Tate reversed its 2019 position that it did not intend to remove references to the Sacklers; it declined to say why.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Tate chair reveals story behind Sackler name removal" (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/13/tate-chair-reveals-story-behind-sackler-name-removal
- Chair Roland Rudd said he told the Sacklers that Tate would "have a problem" and protests would "get really nasty".
3. The Art Newspaper: Anny Shaw, "BP ends 26-year sponsorship deal with Tate" (2016)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/11/bp-ends-26-year-sponsorship-deal-with-tate
- Liberate Tate organised many demonstrations against the sponsorship; BP pointed to business conditions, not protests.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ukraine sanctioned Blavatnik personally in December 2023, a listing by that country alone (Wikipedia, Len Blavatnik). Critic Ann Marlowe called his donations "influence-buying and whitewashing". The connection: his foundation funds Tate and his name is on the building.

BOTTOM LINE
Tate Modern's core money is public grant-in-aid and a wide set of corporate and private donors. The weak points are BP money until 2017, Sackler money until 2019, the Blavatnik name on the building with continuing Blavatnik Family Foundation grants, and the 2024 letter over Zabludowicz and Outset links. Worth watching: Tate's response to that letter and its ethics committee decisions.
