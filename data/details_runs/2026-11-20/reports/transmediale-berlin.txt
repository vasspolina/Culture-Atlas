TRANSMEDIALE: Berlin, Germany
ID: transmediale-berlin
Website: https://transmediale.de (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual Berlin festival for art and digital culture, run by transmediale e.V., "an independent and not-for-profit cultural association based in Berlin" (transmediale website).
- Founded in 1988 as VideoFilmFest, a side programme of the Berlinale's International Forum of New Cinema, initiated by Hartmut Horst and Micky Kwella; renamed VideoFest in 1989, transmedia in 1997 and transmediale in 1998 (Wikipedia).
- Kulturprojekte Berlin organised the festival until 2020, when the association took over its organisation and management (Kulturprojekte Berlin); the office and the transmediale studio are at silent green Kulturquartier, Gerichtstraße 35 (transmediale website; e-flux).
- Artistic directors: Andreas Broeckmann (from 2001), Stephen Kovats (from 2007), Kristoffer Gansing (from 2011) and Nóra Ó Murchú (2021 to 2024); since then each edition has had its own curators (Wikipedia).
- The managing director is Filippo Gianetta; the advisory board is Nora Al-Badri, Asia Bazdyrieva, Gabriele Horn, Jussi Parikka and Inke Arns (transmediale website).
- The 39th edition ran from 28 January to 1 February 2026 at silent green and at CANK, Karl-Marx-Straße 95, curated by Neema Githere and Juan Pablo García Sossa (e-flux).
- Next: the 40th anniversary edition, "transmediale RFC 40". The festival "groundstates", curated by Barbara Cueto, opens at silent green on 28 January 2027; the exhibition "fragile currents", curated by Antonina Stebur, runs at the Akademie der Künste, Hanseatenweg, from 28 January to 7 March 2027 (transmediale website).

FUNDING
transmediale: mainly public: the German Federal Cultural Foundation (Kulturstiftung des Bundes) and the Berlin Senate
- Main funder: the Kulturstiftung des Bundes has funded the festival as a "cultural institution of excellence" since 2004 (transmediale website; Kulturstiftung des Bundes).
- Structural funding: the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion (transmediale website).
- The credits of the 2022 edition list additional funding from the Berlin Senate, the culture programme of the European Union, the Bundeszentrale für politische Bildung (bpb) and Medienboard Berlin-Brandenburg (Haus der Kulturen der Welt archive).
- Funding cut, 2026: in a news item dated 17 September 2026 the festival said the bpb, "one of our longstanding main funders", had decided to discontinue its support after more than 20 years. With Medienboard Berlin-Brandenburg's withdrawal the year before, the festival put the loss at "roughly 120.000€" (transmediale website).
- The 2026 partner programmes were presented with HAU and the Schering Stiftung (e-flux).

Red flags: 2025 open letter over the leadership change and the removed exhibition
- An open letter by !Mediengruppe Bitnik, Aram Bartholl, Constant Dullaart and Danja Vasiliev was sent on 1 February 2025 to transmediale e.V. (Filippo Gianetta and Magdalena Ritter) and its advisory board, and forwarded to the nettime list by Andreas Broeckmann the next day (nettime).
- It asked the leadership to "make transparent the basis for the decisions it took" and said Ó Murchú's departure in summer 2024 was "not transparently communicated with the community of artists and participants" (nettime).
- In September 2026 the festival again advertised for curators for a single edition, that of 2028 (transmediale website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One essay on the festival's 2024 statement and Berlin's political climate, and one open letter on the 2025 leadership change and cancelled exhibition.
1. Spike Art Magazine: Adina Glickstein, "The Highs and Lows of transmediale 2024" (2024)
URL: https://www.spikeartmagazine.com/articles/essay-the-highs-and-lows-of-transmediale-2024
- Frames the 2024 festival as shaped by Berlin's political climate and the war in Gaza.
- Reads the festival's statement, which called for "safeguarding a free and respectful space for dialogue, openness and exchange", as constrained because the festival "depends on state funding".
- Says the panel "Anti-Colonial Tech through Resistance and Discomforts" was pulled on the day; its panelists said: "For us, it is impossible to speak about anti-colonial technologies under these conditions."
2. nettime-l: Andreas Broeckmann forwarding an open letter to transmediale, "Re: <nettime> Transmedialle intel?" (2025)
URL: https://nettime.org/Lists-Archives/nettime-l-2502/msg00004.html
- Argues that a lead-curator contract of 10 months (April 2025 to February 2026) means a new curator for every edition.
- Objects to "fully removing the exhibition element" from the 2026 edition. In the same thread Ursula Damm writes that the festival "has now ultimately become a media-theoretical conference".

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2024 withdrawals: the #StrikeGermany boycott asked international artists to withdraw from publicly funded German cultural institutions and events. Mario Santanilla, Holly Childs, Gediminas Zygus and Avril Corroon withdrew from transmediale 2024 before it opened (Spike Art Magazine).
- The Berlin Senate's clause: culture senator Joe Chialo introduced it on 21 December 2023, requiring funding recipients to oppose antisemitism as defined by the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance. Nearly 6,000 cultural workers and artists signed an open letter against it, and the Senate dropped it just over a month later (The Art Newspaper, Catherine Hickley, 22 January 2024).
- transmediale is publicly funded, which is why the boycott and the clause reached it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind transmediale is German public arts funding, federal and Berlin. The weak points are its dependence on a few public funders, shown when the bpb and Medienboard withdrew, and the 2025 dispute over year-by-year curators. Watch whether the lost money is replaced.
