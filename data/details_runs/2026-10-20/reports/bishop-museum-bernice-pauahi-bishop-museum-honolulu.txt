BISHOP MUSEUM (BERNICE PAUAHI BISHOP MUSEUM): Honolulu, USA
ID: bishop-museum-bernice-pauahi-bishop-museum-honolulu
Website: https://www.bishopmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum of natural and cultural history on a 15-acre campus in Honolulu, Hawai'i, serving as the state's museum of natural and cultural history (Maui Now, 2022).
- Founded in 1889 by Charles Reed Bishop in memory of his late wife; it opened to the public in June 1891 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Bernice Pauahi Bishop (1831 to 1884), the last legal heir of the Kamehameha Dynasty (Wikipedia).
- Building: Hawaiian Hall and Polynesian Hall were built in 1898 in the Richardsonian Romanesque style; Wikipedia's infobox credits architect William F. Smith.
- On site: the Jhamandas Watumull Planetarium, the Richard T. Mamiya Science Adventure Center (opened November 2005), Pauahi Hall with an entomology research centre, and Pākī Hall with the Hawaiʻi Sports Hall of Fame and a public library and archives. The museum also runs the Amy B.H. Greenwell Ethnobotanical Garden on the Big Island (Wikipedia).
- In January 2023 the board voted unanimously to terminate CEO Melanie Ide, General Counsel Barron Oda and Vice President Wesley "Kaiwi" Yoon, who had been on paid leave since 1 July 2022, after an independent investigator interviewed more than 40 current and former staff about workplace conditions. Officials said the report would not be made public (Hawaiʻi Public Radio, 13 January 2023).
- Visiting: open daily 9am to 5pm, closed on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year's; one admission price covers all signature galleries. Service dogs are permitted.

FUNDING
Bishop Museum: mixed: state and federal grants, Hawai'i foundations and Hawai'i corporate sponsors
- Revenue was $28,096,033 in the year to June 2025 (contributions $23,880,761; expenses $20,274,339; net assets $88,540,506), $21,081,455 in 2024 and $21,545,161 in 2023 (Form 990 data, ProPublica).
- State of Hawai'i: in May 2022 the museum was to receive $17.5 million for the coming year, $7.5 million for operations and $10 million for capital improvement projects (Maui Now, 7 May 2022).
- Government funders listed on its funders page: the Institute of Museum and Library Services, National Endowment for the Arts, National Science Foundation, NOAA, National Park Service, U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, the State of Hawai'i, the Hawai'i Tourism Authority and the four Hawai'i counties.
- Foundations listed on the same page include Kamehameha Schools, the Charles Reed Bishop Trust, Harold K.L. Castle Foundation, Mellon Foundation, Paul G. Allen Family Foundation, Disney Conservation Fund, Freeman Foundation, Schmidt Family Foundation and Hawaiian Electric Industries Charitable Foundation.
- Corporate partners by tier (museum website): $100,000 and above, Hawaiian Airlines and Hawaiian Native Corporation; $50,000 to $99,999, Outrigger Resorts & Hotels and Pasha Hawaii; $25,000 to $49,999, First Hawaiian Bank, Aqua Aston Hospitality, Hawaiian Telcom, Wayfinder Hawaii and Matson; $15,000 to $24,999, Kyo-ya Hotels & Resorts, UMU, ProService Hawaii and Prince Waikiki; $10,000 to $14,999, The First Insurance Company of Hawaii and Halekulani; $5,000 to $9,999, SERVCO.
- In-kind supporters: Kona Brewing Co., Zippy's, Shibby Stylee and Waiakea.

Not related: Bishop Museum of Science and Nature Inc in Bradenton, Florida, a separate nonprofit (ProPublica).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is state and federal grants, Hawai'i trusts and foundations, and local corporate sponsors, with contributions making up most of its revenue (about $23.9 million of $28.1 million in the year to June 2025). In January 2023 the board dismissed the CEO and two executives after a workplace investigation; officials said the investigator's report would not be made public (Hawaiʻi Public Radio).
