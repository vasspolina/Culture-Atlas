TOWNHOUSE GALLERY: Cairo, Egypt
ID: townhouse-gallery-cairo
Website: none found (thetownhousegallery.com did not resolve, checked 2026-10-25; down)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art space in downtown Cairo, in the Antikhana neighbourhood less than half a mile from Tahrir Square (Apollo).
- Established in 1998 by William Wells (Wikipedia, Contemporary And); Contemporary And calls it the first independent art space in Egypt dedicated to promoting contemporary art.
- Main building at 10 Nabrawy Street, in an 1890s building (Apollo, 31 August 2018).
- Programmes over the years: the Nitaq Festival, PhotoCairo (from 2002), the Rooftop Studio Project (2009), residencies, an art library and outreach workshops (Wikipedia).
- Affiliated spaces: the Rawabet Theatre (launched 2006, Egyptian Streets) and the Factory, a converted paper factory used as temporary space after the building collapsed (Apollo, Contemporary And).
- Shut after a state raid in December 2015, the main building partly collapsed in April 2016 and the main space reopened in April 2018 (Apollo).
- Status: Contemporary And (15 September 2020) says Rawabet and the Factory announced closures in 2019 when their rental agreements ended, and that the Townhouse was expected to shut or change, with no official announcement. A Contemporary And places page dated 13 October 2025 describes the gallery in the present tense.

FUNDING
Townhouse Gallery: non-profit with no published funder list; the Ford Foundation is named
- A Bridgespan article of 27 November 2013 (no author named) says "modest sums from Walker (and Ford)" helped make the Townhouse "a beehive of activity around democracy building in Egypt". The page gives no amounts.
- The rebuilding after the 2016 collapse was paid for by "a funding campaign" (Apollo, 2018). The article does not say who ran or funded it.
- Contemporary And names no funders for the gallery; its places page (13 October 2025) says the gallery "has recently become a foundation".

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Townhouse itself found in art media. The pieces below criticise state action against it, and one asks a question about institutions that depend on one person.
1. Contemporary And: Marwa Eslayed, "The End of Many Endings?" (15 September 2020)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/magazines/the-end-of-many-endings/
- Traces the 2019 closures of Rawabet and the Factory and the expected end or change of the Townhouse.
- Lists theories: Wells being refused entry to Egypt, restrictions on independent cultural work, dependence on foreign funding and the gallery's independence from state control. Says Wells's absence "has had a great impact on the gallery's contemporary art programme" and raises the question of institutions that depend on one individual.
- The piece was written through a mentoring programme funded by the Ford Foundation, which is also a Townhouse supporter per Bridgespan.
2. Apollo: Amina Diab, "The remarkable rebirth of the Townhouse Gallery in Cairo" (31 August 2018)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/the-remarkable-rebirth-of-the-townhouse-gallery-in-cairo/
- Criticises the authorities, not the gallery: the 2015 raid, repeated attempts to demolish the damaged building, and a 2013 law that restricts NGO activity and foreign funding.
- Says the 2013 law "reduced artistic activity to around a quarter of what it was in 2012".
3. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Cairo's Townhouse Gallery remains shut contrary to reports" (18 February 2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/cairo-s-townhouse-gallery-remains-shut-contrary-to-reports
- Reports that censorship and tax authority inspectors found "administrative irregularities" and seized paperwork and archives. Arts professionals feared a wider political crackdown.
- Criticises the state action, not the gallery.

WIDER CONTEXT
- December 2015: an inter-agency raid by the Censorship Authority, Tax Authority, National Security Agency and a Ministry of Manpower office searched the gallery and the Rawabet Theatre and took computers, documents and USB drives (Mada Masr, quoted by Egyptian Streets, 29 December 2015). No official reason was stated in that report; The Art Newspaper (Gareth Harris, 18 February 2016) reports the authorities cited "administrative irregularities".
- Director William Wells told The Art Newspaper (Anny Shaw, republished by Contemporary And on 30 November 2016) that the new venue must follow the rules that apply to all cultural institutions: "There is no flexibility."
- No page opened reports funding from the Egyptian state; the raid is context for the space, not a funding concern.

BOTTOM LINE
No reporting found of a state, corporate or Gulf funder behind the Townhouse; the only named funder in the pages read is the Ford Foundation, without amounts. The weak points are the lack of published accounts or a funder list, a website that no longer resolves, and an unconfirmed current status: Contemporary And reports the Townhouse was expected to shut or change in 2019.
