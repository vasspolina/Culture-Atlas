GAPAR AITIEV KYRGYZ NATIONAL MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS: Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan
ID: gapar-aitiev-kyrgyz-national-museum-of-fine-arts-bishkek
Website: https://artmuseum.kg (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum on Sovietskaya Street, across from the opera house and near Ala-Too Square in central Bishkek (SRAS Museum Studies Abroad).
- Grew from a picture gallery opened by the Kyrgyz regional committee of the All-Union Communist Party in 1934 (Voices on Central Asia); it opened on 1 January 1935 with 72 Russian works from the State Tretyakov Gallery and the Russian education commissariat, chosen by its founder, the painter Semyon Chuikov (museum site).
- It became a state museum in 1944 and a national museum in 1998 (director Mira Dzhangaracheva to 24.kg, 2019).
- Named for Gapar Aitiev, one of the first Soviet Kyrgyz artists, born in 1912 in what is now Osh (SRAS).
- The current building, a brutalist design by the architect Shailo Djekshenbaev, opened in 1974 (SRAS).
- About 18,000 items: about 4,000 paintings, 9,600 graphic works, 1,000 sculptures and more than 3,000 pieces of decorative and applied art; Kyrgyz applied art (embroidery, felt, wood carving, weaving, leather, jewellery) is the core, beside Russian painting of the 18th to 20th centuries (museum site). The ethnographic collection was founded in 1967 (Voices on Central Asia).
- Temporary shows are on the first floor and the permanent display on the second (SRAS). The museum put photographs of more than 4,000 paintings and about 1,000 sculptures online on artmuseum.kg in January 2020 (24.kg).
- Aigul Mambetkazieva, deputy director and chief curator, was appointed director by the culture minister in December 2019 (24.kg; Kaktus.media).
- Visiting: more than 50,000 visitors a year in 2019 (Dzhangaracheva to 24.kg); guided tours in Russian, Kyrgyz or English can be booked, and the student fee was 20 som in 2018 (SRAS). The permanent display closed on 27 and 28 September 2025 for repairs (24.kg).

FUNDING
Gapar Aitiev Museum: public: Kyrgyzstan's culture ministry
- Owned and operated by the Ministry of Culture, Information, Sports and Youth Policy, which oversees its day-to-day running and the upkeep of building and collections (SRAS); the minister signs the director's appointment (24.kg, 5 December 2019).
- In August 2019 the director said the state budget covered only salaries and utilities; the building's plant room had run for almost 50 years, fire alarms worked only manually, climate control had failed for years and paintings were "просто гибнут" (simply perishing; translation). A video surveillance system costing about 500,000 som was unaffordable, and the culture minister had promised money for a capital repair in 2021 (24.kg, 1 August 2019).
- She planned to raise money from embassies, private donors, banks and philanthropic organisations (24.kg, 2019).
- In September 2018 the state procurement portal allocated at least 550,000 som to replace the museum's wooden window frames with aluminium ones by the Turkish firm ALUGAL (24.kg).
- In 2022 a visitor wrote that the building did not seem to have the facilities to keep the art in good condition, with dented frames and works stored haphazardly (SRAS).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage centres on the state's censorship of the 2019 Feminnale and the nationalist campaign behind it, not on the museum's own conduct.
1. Artnet News: Taylor Dafoe, "Kyrgyzstan Censored a Feminist Art Show at the National Museum of Fine Arts. Now Its Director Has Resigned" (2019)
URL: https://news.artnet.com/art-world-archives/museum-kyrgyzstan-censorship-1722195
- Reports that officials removed works, including Zoya Falkova's punching-bag sculpture, and that the minister said the director had been relieved of her duties while she said she resigned.
- Only excerpts were read.
2. Human Rights Watch: Hillary Margolis, "Feminist Art Exhibit Threatened in Kyrgyzstan" (2019)
URL: https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/12/06/feminist-art-exhibit-threatened-kyrgyzstan
- Argues the government should protect the artists and organisers and support free expression instead of limiting access to the art.
- A human rights organisation, not art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The first Feminnale of contemporary art, 56 artists from 22 countries, opened at the museum on 28 November 2019 (Human Rights Watch; New East Digital Archive). Culture Minister Azamat Zhamankulov met its opponents and ordered works he called "provocative" and "amoral" removed (Human Rights Watch); six pieces went, Falkova's "Evermust" among them (New East Digital Archive), and Julie Savery's nude performance was cancelled (ArtReview).
- On 2 December the minister said that "under the flag of feminism, campaigns with a naked woman were held in Parliament and in a church" and set up a commission (24.kg). On 5 December MP Kurmankul Zulushev called for a police investigation (Human Rights Watch).
- Director Mira Dzhangaracheva left after threats against her and her staff, including to "tear me apart, to rape me"; the government opened an investigation into the threats on 6 December 2019 (Human Rights Watch). She said she stepped down voluntarily; 24.kg reported the minister removed her. A protester from the nationalist group Kyrk Choro called the show pornographic (New East Digital Archive).
- In June 2019 her predecessor, Yuristanbek Shygaev, asked to leave after media reported he had taken two originals from storage and returned them damaged (24.kg).
- In August 2021 the painter Taalaibek Usubaliev said there was talk of removing his painting of six presidents from a show at the museum, with no direct order (24.kg).
- These cases took place at the museum, under the ministry that funds it and appoints its director.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for and controlled by the Kyrgyz state, and the money is too little to keep the collection safe. The weak point is that control, used to remove art in 2019; whether the state pays for the long-promised capital repair is worth watching.
