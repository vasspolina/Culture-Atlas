GUGGENHEIM ABU DHABI: Abu Dhabi, UAE
ID: guggenheim-abu-dhabi
Website: https://www.guggenheimabudhabi.ae/ (checked 2026-10-20; blocks automated visits with a bot check, so no page could be read)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art in the cultural district of Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, beside the Louvre Abu Dhabi and the Zayed National Museum (Wikipedia, Hyperallergic).
- Abu Dhabi signed the agreement with the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation in July 2006; construction began in 2011, stalled, and the opening slipped from 2013 to 2015, 2017, 2023 and 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Guggenheim Foundation, which runs the programme; Mariët Westermann is director and CEO (Hyperallergic).
- Designed by Frank Gehry: 11,600 m² inside with 28 galleries around a central atrium, plus 23,000 m² of outdoor exhibition space across 10 sculptural cones and terraces; built by BESIX subsidiary Six Construct with Trojan Contracting under a 2021 contract (Khaleej Times, Wikipedia).
- The collection covers art from the 1960s to now, with a focus on West Asia, North Africa and South Asia; about 970 works at the start (Khaleej Times, Wikipedia).
- Opens on 11 December 2026; general admission Dh70, free for under-18s, UAE students and over-60s (Khaleej Times, 18 September 2026). No reporting found of opening hours or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Guggenheim Abu Dhabi: Gulf state body: Abu Dhabi government, through its development company and culture department
- A 2008 Guggenheim statement said Abu Dhabi's Tourism Development and Investment Company (TDIC) would own the museum and the Foundation would manage its programmes (Wikipedia).
- The museum is developed by the Department of Culture and Tourism, Abu Dhabi, with the Guggenheim Foundation (Khaleej Times).
- No reporting found of the building cost, the fees paid to the Guggenheim Foundation, or any private or corporate funder.

Red flags: migrant labour abuse on Saadiyat Island
- Human Rights Watch interviewed 113 Saadiyat workers in 2014 and found steep recruitment fees, withheld wages, passport confiscation, poor housing and deportations after strikes; TDIC's own monitor, PwC, found 88% of 1,050 workers interviewed had paid recruitment fees (Architectural Record, 2015).
- HRW did not interview Guggenheim site workers in 2014 because work there was on hold; its researcher said pressure must stay on the project because TDIC, the government developer, runs it (Architectural Record).
- TDIC's chief executive Ali Al Hammadi called workers' rights "an international matter" (Architectural Record).

Red flags: artist boycott and protests (Gulf Labor Coalition)
- In March 2011 more than 130 artists and writers pledged not to work with the project until workers' recruitment fees were repaid and an independent monitor published findings (Arab News).
- In February 2014 Gulf Labor activists dropped banners including "1% Museum" from the spiral of the Guggenheim in New York (Democracy Now).
- In 2015 its offshoot G.U.L.F. occupied the Guggenheim in New York and the Peggy Guggenheim Collection in Venice (Hyperallergic, 2026).
- In April 2016 the Guggenheim broke off talks; Richard Armstrong said Gulf Labor kept shifting its demands and spread "mistruths" (Hyperallergic, 2016).
- In July 2026 co-founder Andrew Ross said none of the reasons for the action had been adequately resolved (Hyperallergic, 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is Abu Dhabi state property, with the Guggenheim Foundation running the programme; no fees or costs are published. The weak points are the documented abuse of migrant workers on Saadiyat Island and a boycott that its organisers say was never resolved. Worth watching: whether the Foundation or DCT publishes labour monitoring for the final build.
