SURSOCK MUSEUM – Beirut, Lebanon
Website: https://sursock.museum (ok, checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum in the 1912 villa of collector Nicolas Ibrahim Sursock on Rue Sursock, Rmeil district. Sursock died in 1952 and left the villa to the city as a trust (waqf); his will names the president of the Beirut Municipality as its mutawalli (trustee). A museum committee (later "General Committee") oversees it. (Museum history page; Wikipedia)
- Used as a presidential guesthouse 1953-57; opened as a museum in 1961. Closed 2008-2014 for an expansion adding four underground floors (US$12M, Jean-Michel Wilmotte and Jacques Abou Khaled), growing floor area from 1,500 to 8,500 sq m; reopened 8 October 2015. (Wikipedia)
- Badly damaged in the 4 August 2020 port explosion; reopened 26 May 2023 after a restoration of about $2.5M. (Hyperallergic)
- Collection of 800+ works. Open Wednesday-Sunday 10:00-18:00; admission free; all galleries wheelchair accessible. (Museum, plan your visit)

FUNDING
Sursock Museum: waqf-owned, run on donations, grants and sponsorship; no financial statements found.
- Founding trust: Nicolas Sursock's bequest of the villa and estate, administered with the Beirut Municipality president as trustee (stated by the space). No reporting found of the size of any endowment or of an annual municipal grant.
- Restoration 2020-23: Italian Agency for Development Cooperation €1M via UNESCO's LiBeirut initiative; ALIPH; French Ministry of Culture; Goethe-Institut; AFAC; Arab Regional Centre for World Heritage; DHL; Christie's fundraiser (Hyperallergic, independent; supporter page, stated by the space). Director Zeina Arida told Blooloop after the blast that the museum had $1M in grants against a $3M need.
- Patrons 2024-2026: Robert A. Matta Foundation, Kamal F. Tabet; donors and Friends tiers of named individuals and families (supporter page).
- 2025 grants: Agenzia italiana per la cooperazione allo sviluppo; Institut français du Liban (supporter page).
- 2026 energy sponsors: Energy Assistance, Fondation CMA CGM, Pierre Dammous & Partners, Fondation Engie. Operational sponsors: Commercial Insurance, Tinol, Mitsulift (supporter page).
- Director Karina al-Helou said in March 2026 the museum has been fundraising since 2020 amid "extreme vulnerability", for energy, maintenance and staff (L'Orient Today).

Red flags: Fossil-fuel corporate foundations as energy sponsors
- Fondation Engie is listed as a 2026 Energy Sponsor. Engie is a French utility whose core business, per Wikipedia, still depends largely on fossil fuels, including gas transmission and LNG imports; the French state holds about 24%.
- Fondation CMA CGM, foundation of the French shipping group, is also a 2026 Energy Sponsor (stated by the space). No reporting found of protest about either.

Red flags: Withdrawn wartime fundraising appeal (March 2026)
- L'Orient Today (independent) reported the museum published and withdrew a donation appeal tied to the renewed war and displacement; critics said it was out of touch. The director said the text was "not authorized in either form or substance."

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a waqf owned for public benefit, with free admission, kept going since 2020 by foreign-government grants, private patrons and corporate in-kind sponsors. The point to watch is the 2026 energy sponsorship by Fondation Engie and Fondation CMA CGM; no financial statements are published, so the share each source covers is unknown.
