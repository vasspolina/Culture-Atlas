INSTITUT DU MONDE ARABE: Paris, France
ID: institut-du-monde-arabe-paris
Website: https://www.imarabe.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural institution, museum and library at 1 Rue des Fossés Saint-Bernard, 75005 Paris, by the Jussieu and Cardinal Lemoine metro stops.
- Created under a 1980 international agreement between France and the Arab states that founded it, 22 countries according to the Cour des comptes.
- Its stated mission is to enable "the study, knowledge and understanding of the Arab world, its language, its civilisation" (our translation, stated by the space).
- The pages checked do not name the architect of the building.
- On site: a museum, a library, a shop, exhibitions and Arabic-language courses, including an internationally recognised Arabic proficiency certificate (Cour des comptes).
- Closed Monday; museum Tuesday to Friday 10 am to 6 pm, weekends and holidays 10 am to 7 pm; library Tuesday to Sunday 1 pm to 7 pm (stated by the space). Admission prices were not on the pages checked.

FUNDING
Institut du monde arabe: mainly public: the French foreign ministry, with corporate patrons
- The French Ministry of Europe and Foreign Affairs provides more than half of the Institute's revenue as a subsidy (Cour des comptes, December 2024); Brut gave the ministry's share as half of the budget, €12.3 million a year (Brut, February 2026).
- The founding Arab states have stopped paying their share of operating costs as the original agreement defined it; the Institute has used up half of its own funds since its creation; the accounts balanced in the last two years reviewed (Cour des comptes).
- The Cour des comptes recommended that the ministry work with Arab countries to redefine their involvement, particularly their funding, and pursue closer ties with AFALULA.
- TotalEnergies Corporate Foundation is major sponsor in a 20-year partnership, backing "Parfums d'Orient" (26 September 2023 to 17 March 2024) and "ARABOFUTURS" (23 April 2024 to 15 January 2025) (stated by the TotalEnergies Foundation).
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts; the site's list of patrons could not be opened.

Red flags: Jack Lang, president from 2013 to 2026, and Jeffrey Epstein
- Lang's name appears 673 times in the documents the US Department of Justice published on 30 January 2026 (Brut).
- Emails cited by AFP show a donor to the Institute writing in 2017 that Lang had pushed for Epstein to attend his birthday; other messages attributed to Lang thank Epstein for his generosity and ask for a ride to an event (Brut).
- Reuters says Lang corresponded with Epstein from 2012 to 2019.
- France's financial prosecutor (PNF) opened a preliminary investigation on 6 February 2026 into Lang and his daughter Caroline for aggravated laundering of tax fraud; police had searched the Institute by 16 February (Reuters). No charges have been brought and Lang denies wrongdoing, citing "naïveté" (Brut).
- Lang resigned as president in February 2026 after Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot summoned him (Euronews, Brut).
- No reporting found of Epstein money reaching the Institute.

Red flags: TotalEnergies sponsorship
- TotalEnergies Foundation sponsored Institute exhibitions in 2023 to 2025 (TotalEnergies Foundation).
- Greenpeace France named the Institute among TotalEnergies' cultural partners, with the Louvre, quai Branly and Pompidou, calling the sponsorship a "soft power" strategy (L'Info Durable, 2021). The page quotes no response from TotalEnergies.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media; the coverage is of Lang and the investigation.

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute is run on French state money, with its founding Arab states no longer paying their share and TotalEnergies as a major sponsor. The weak points are the Epstein files naming Lang, who led it for 13 years, and the fossil fuel sponsorship. Worth watching: the PNF investigation and the Institute's choice of a new president.
