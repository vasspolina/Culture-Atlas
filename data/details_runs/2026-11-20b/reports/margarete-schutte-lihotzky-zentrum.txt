MARGARETE SCHÜTTE-LIHOTZKY ZENTRUM: Vienna, Austria
ID: margarete-schutte-lihotzky-zentrum
Website: https://www.schuette-lihotzky.at/de/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and research centre in the architect's last flat, at Franzensgasse 16/40 in Vienna's 5th district; the centre calls it the only architect's flat open to the public in Vienna (centre website).
- Named for Margarete Schütte-Lihotzky (1897 to 2000), who moved in at the age of 70 and lived there for 30 years (ORF Wien, centre website).
- The flat and its furnishings were listed as a monument in 2021 and the museum opened in 2022; the centre has belonged to the Iconic Houses network since December 2022 (centre website).
- The flat covers 55 square metres with a 35 square metre terrace; ORF reported in May 2021 that the original kitchen would return to replace the current one, and the architect Renate Allmayer-Beck carried out the restoration from 2022 to 2024 (ORF Wien, centre website).
- It hosts the research project "Architektur Pionierinnen" on women architects in Austria, podcasts, and an annual film evening with VHS Wiener Urania since 2017 (centre website).
- The architect Christine Zwingl founded the centre in 2021 and chairs the association behind it; Judith Augustinovič has led the centre since January 2026 (centre website).
- Visiting: Tuesday and Friday 14:00 to 18:00, closed on public holidays; 6 euros, 4 euros reduced, free under 18 and for members; step-free access on request (centre website).

FUNDING
Margarete Schütte-Lihotzky Zentrum: mainly public, plus donations and in 2021 the building owner Wertinvest
- Operator: the Margarete Schütte-Lihotzky Club (association register number 854118344), founded in 2013, which says its money comes from public grants, donations, membership fees and sponsorship (centre website).
- Public funders listed: the Bundeskammer der ZiviltechnikerInnen, the federal arts ministry, the City of Vienna culture department (MA 7), the Margareten district and Gesiba (centre website).
- The centre states that public grants were cut at short notice and that it now depends on private donations; it offers sponsorship packages for 2026 and seeks backers for its first book, naming donors of 100 euros or more (centre website).
- Members pay 30 euros a year (centre website).
- ORF Wien reported in May 2021 that the building's owner, Wertinvest, was one of the project's financial backers, and that its representative Daniela Enzi said the flat was being restored with the Federal Monuments Office and the association. The centre's funders page does not list Wertinvest.

Red flags: 2017 to 2026 UNESCO Red List over Wertinvest's Heumarkt tower
- Vienna's historic centre was on UNESCO's list of World Heritage in Danger from 2017 because of the Heumarkt project of Michael Tojner and his Wertinvest; the World Heritage Committee removed it on 27 July 2026, although its advisory body ICOMOS had recommended keeping it on (vienna.at).
- The tower has shrunk from a planned 73 metres to 49.95 metres and still needs an environmental impact assessment (vienna.at).

Red flags: 2019 fraud investigation of Michael Tojner over Burgenland housing companies
- After a complaint by Land Burgenland, the WKStA anti-corruption prosecutors investigated Tojner, 15 other people and eight companies over the loss of non-profit status of the housing companies Gesfö, Riedenhof and Pannonia, on suspicion of serious fraud and breach of trust; his lawyer rejected all accusations (ORF Wien, June 2019). Industriemagazin counted 16 individuals and nine companies and reported searches at about 50 sites.
- In May 2026 the WKStA dropped its cases against nine people and the auditor KPMG Austria, but still investigates 13 people or companies; Burgenland puts its damage at 163 million euros, and Tojner denies all accusations (trend, 8 May 2026).
- Unverified: in 2018 Greens club chairman David Ellensohn filed a fraud complaint alleging that non-profit flats built by Gesfö were let to tourists through a company part-owned by Wertinvest; this is a party politician's complaint, and Wertinvest called the letting "rechtlich einwandfrei" (my translation: legally sound) (ORF Wien).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In April 2014 members of the Architekturinitiative Wien, including Friedrich Achleitner and Gustav Peichl, wrote to planning councillor Maria Vassilakou that the Heumarkt project took public space in breach of the competition brief and that a luxury residential tower could not be justified there (ORF Wien, 2014).
- The letter concerns the project of Wertinvest, the owner of the centre's building and a backer of its 2021 restoration.

BOTTOM LINE
The public money is clean. The weak point is the 2021 backing from the building's owner, whose proprietor is under a fraud investigation he denies.
