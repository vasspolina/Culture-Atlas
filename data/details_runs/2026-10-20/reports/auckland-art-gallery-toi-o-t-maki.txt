AUCKLAND ART GALLERY TOI O TĀMAKI: Auckland, New Zealand
ID: auckland-art-gallery-toi-o-t-maki
Website: https://www.aucklandartgallery.com

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery on the corner of Wellesley Street East and Kitchener Street, Auckland, entered through the main doors at 1 Kitchener Street (Auckland Art Gallery site).
- Established in 1888 as the first permanent art gallery in New Zealand, sharing a building with the Auckland public library and opening with donations from Sir George Grey and James Tannock Mackelvie; the Mackelvie Trust Collection is still a core holding (Wikipedia).
- The 2011 redevelopment by the Australian firm FJMT with Auckland-based Archimedia increased exhibition space by 50%, restored parts of the building to its 1916 state and reduced 17 floor levels to six; it won the 2013 World Building of the Year award. It cost NZ$113 million, of which Auckland City Council contributed just under NZ$50 million (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds more than 18,000 works (Wikipedia). The Gallery is owned and operated by Tātaki Auckland Unlimited, an Auckland Council-controlled organisation (Annual Review 2024/25). Attendance in 2024 to 2025 was 497,998 against a target of 500,000 (Annual Review 2024/25).
- Open every day 10:00 to 17:00 except Christmas Day. General collection exhibitions are free for everyone; special exhibitions can carry a charge.

FUNDING
Auckland Art Gallery Toi o Tāmaki: public (Auckland Council through Tātaki Auckland Unlimited), with corporate partners, trusts and an endowment foundation
- Corporate partners in 2024 to 2025: BMW, Van Cleef & Arpels, HSBC, Anthony Harper, Baker Tilly, Beca, Cordis, Omnigraphics and Trinity Hill (Annual Review 2024/25). The exhibition "A Century of Modern Art" (June to September 2025) was supported by HSBC, Cordis and the Auckland Art Gallery Foundation.
- Trusts and foundations: the Joyce Fisher Charitable Trust, Friedlander Foundation, Sue Fisher Art Trust and Sir William and Lady Lois Manchester Charitable Trust fund transport, staffing and resources so students can visit; Lottery Environment and Heritage supported the Heritage Restoration Project (Annual Review 2024/25).
- Auckland Art Gallery Foundation: gave over NZ$500,000 to the Gallery in 2024 to 2025, its largest annual gift; its endowment grew 78% to NZ$6,180,000 (Annual Review 2024/25).
- The Chartwell Trust has been the long-term home of the Chartwell Collection at the Gallery since 1997 (2,134 works) and has supported the North Terrace commissions since 2012 (Annual Review 2024/25).
- The Contemporary Benefactors, founded in 2013 with 127 members, funded "Olafur Eliasson: Your curious journey". The trustees of Gibbs Farm donate a day each year for a fundraiser, which had 1,000 ticket holders in November 2024 (Annual Review 2024/25).

Red flags: partner bank HSBC, 2012 US money-laundering and sanctions settlement
- HSBC is listed among the Gallery's current partners and backed "A Century of Modern Art" (June to September 2025) (Annual Review 2024/25); the Gallery's partnerships page lists HSBC among its Members.
- On 11 December 2012 the US Department of Justice announced that HSBC Holdings and HSBC Bank USA agreed to forfeit US$1.256 billion and enter a deferred prosecution agreement, with a further US$665 million in civil penalties; wire reports put the total at US$1.92 billion (Justice Department release; Arkansas Democrat-Gazette).
- The release cites violations of the Bank Secrecy Act, including at least US$881 million in drug proceeds laundered, among them proceeds of the Sinaloa Cartel in Mexico and the Norte del Valle Cartel in Colombia, and violations of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the Trading with the Enemy Act through transactions for customers in Cuba, Iran, Libya, Sudan and Burma (Justice Department release).
- The settlement dates from 2012, more than five years ago; HSBC's partnership with the Gallery is current.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Spinoff, Simon Wilson, "Why is Auckland slowly strangling its art gallery?" (12 December 2017): argues that Auckland Council, led by Mayor Phil Goff, underfunds the Gallery. The Gallery's operating budget was NZ$6.9 million in the latest financial year, down 25% from NZ$9.2 million in 2012, while visitors reached 550,000 against a target of 470,000.
- Wilson writes that benefactor and sponsor money "doesn't keep the doors open", and he opposes an entry fee for overseas visitors, who are about 60% of visitors. He names Goff, the Gallery's director Rhana Devenport and Paul Brewer of Regional Facilities Auckland.
- The column's target is the council, which owns the Gallery through Tātaki Auckland Unlimited, not the Gallery itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The Gallery is publicly owned and funded through Auckland Council, with corporate partners, trusts and an endowment on top. The name to know is HSBC, a current partner that settled US money-laundering and sanctions cases in December 2012. The council's funding of the Gallery, criticised in The Spinoff in 2017, is the other point to watch.
