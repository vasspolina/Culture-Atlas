WAVE HILL: Bronx, USA
ID: wave-hill-bronx
Website: https://www.wavehill.org

OVERVIEW
- Public garden and cultural center on a 28-acre site on a ridge above the Hudson River in Riverdale, the Bronx (Wave Hill, Wikipedia).
- Founded as a public garden in 1965 (Wave Hill). The Perkins-Freeman family deeded the estate to the City of New York in 1960, at the suggestion of Robert Moses (Wikipedia). Wave Hill is one of New York City's 39 city-owned cultural institutions (Wave Hill FY2025 annual report); Wave Hill Incorporated is a 501(c)(3) charity with tax exemption dated June 1965 (ProPublica).
- Named for the estate. Its earlier name was Perkins Garden, until Parks Commissioner Henry Stern renamed it Wave Hill in 1987 (Wikipedia).
- Site and building: William Lewis Morris built the original fieldstone house in 1843; publisher William Henry Appleton owned it from 1866 to 1903; the banker George Walbridge Perkins bought it in 1903. Mark Twain leased it from 1901 to 1903 and Arturo Toscanini lived there from 1942 to 1945 (Wikipedia). It is a New York City landmark and has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1983 (Wikipedia).
- On site: gardens, a conservatory, Glyndor House with art galleries, Wave Hill House with a cafe, Armor Hall for concerts, and a Winter Workspace artist residency from January to April (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, closed Mondays; grounds 10 am to 5:30 pm. Adults $10, students and seniors 65 and over $6, children 6 and over $4, members free, Thursdays free all day (Wave Hill).

FUNDING
Wave Hill: city-owned site run by a nonprofit funded by government support, contributions and grants, investment income and earned income
- Wave Hill Incorporated reported revenue of $7,720,166 and expenses of $9,202,157 in the fiscal year to June 2025, and net assets of $83,347,264 (ProPublica, from its Form 990).
- The earlier years show the same pattern: revenue of $7,188,386 against expenses of $8,773,980 in FY2024 (ProPublica).
- The FY2025 annual report puts government support at 26% of income, contributions and grants at 31%, investment income at 25%, earned income at 14% and in-kind support at 4%; its statement of activities gives total public support and revenue of $9,585,726, including $2,469,136 of investment income and gains (Wave Hill).
- Wave Hill's corporate and foundation page describes Foundation Partners giving unrestricted operating grants and Corporate Members getting access to rent the grounds and facilities at "highly competitive rates" (Wave Hill).
- Prospect Hill Foundation gave $25,000 in 2024 to The Campaign for Wave Hill (Prospect Hill Foundation).
- In 2005 Wave Hill was one of 406 New York City institutions to receive part of a $20 million Carnegie Corporation grant made possible by a donation from Mayor Michael Bloomberg (Wikipedia).
- Board chair Ezra Borut, treasurer D. Bryce O'Brien II and secretary Lisa Cader lead a board whose directors are listed with no compensation; President and Executive Director Ray B. Oladapo-Johnson received $301,847 in reportable compensation in FY2025 (ProPublica).

BOTTOM LINE
Wave Hill sits on land the Perkins-Freeman family gave to the City of New York in 1960, and the nonprofit Wave Hill Incorporated runs it. Its Form 990 shows revenue of $7.7 million and expenses of $9.2 million in FY2025, and its annual report puts government support at 26% and contributions and grants at 31% of income. The funders named in the sources are the Prospect Hill Foundation and the Carnegie Corporation.
