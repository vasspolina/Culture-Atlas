MUSEUM FÜR NATURKUNDE: Berlin, Germany
ID: museum-fuer-naturkunde-berlin
Website: https://www.museumfuernaturkunde.berlin

OVERVIEW
- Natural history museum in Berlin, with more than 30 million zoological, palaeontological and mineralogical specimens, including over 10,000 type specimens (Wikipedia).
- Established in 1810 as part of the Berlin University, now Humboldt University; its mineral collections trace back to the Prussian Academy of Sciences in 1700. It was long called the Humboldt Museum (Wikipedia).
- On 1 January 2009 it became a foundation under public law and joined the Leibniz Association; it left the university the same year. Johannes Vogel is the director (Wikipedia).
- The central exhibit is the mounted Giraffatitan (formerly Brachiosaurus brancai), described by Wikipedia as the largest mounted dinosaur skeleton in the world, with bones collected at Tendaguru in Tanzania from 1909 to 1913. The museum also shows the Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton Tristan.
- A US air raid on 3 February 1945 heavily damaged the east wing, and about 75 percent of the collection was brought to safety (Wikipedia). A Senate report of February 2019 says the building still bears the damage of the Second World War and decades of deferred repairs.
- Renovation has run since 2008. By February 2019 the second construction phase, covering 2,933 square metres of exhibition space, was largely complete, and the Senate report planned that visitors could for the first time reach all floors of the main building without barriers (Berlin Senate Chancellery report to the Main Committee, 12 February 2019).

FUNDING
Museum für Naturkunde: mainly public: federal government and State of Berlin
- Operating split: the museum was established by law as the Leibniz Institute for Evolution and Biodiversity Research and included in joint federal and state funding under Article 91b of the Basic Law. Joint funding covers the research tasks, which make up 80 percent of operating and equipment investment costs; the remaining 20 percent, the museum share, is paid by the State of Berlin alone. Construction tied to research is paid by the federal government and Berlin 50:50 (Berlin Senate Chancellery for Science and Research, report to the Main Committee of the House of Representatives, 12 February 2019).
- Renovation: public funds totalling 61 million euros had been spent on the museum since the renovation began in 2008 (same report).
- Future Plan: the Berliner Zeitung (Torsten Harmsen, 22 January 2020) reported a total cost of 660 million euros for the museum and Humboldt University together, shared equally by the federal government and Berlin. The first phase, to about 2028 on that article's timetable, covers renovation of the north wing at an estimated 299 million euros. The Senate Chancellery report of 12 February 2019 gives the total cost as 660 million euros, 330 million euros of it an income from the federal government. The museum's own page says that in 2018 it secured funding for the Future Plan from the German Bundestag and the State of Berlin.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: it is a Leibniz Association foundation funded jointly by the federal government and the State of Berlin, with Berlin alone paying the museum share. The Future Plan, at about 660 million euros, is split 50:50 between the two.
