MUSEUM OF JURASSIC TECHNOLOGY: Los Angeles, USA
ID: museum-of-jurassic-technology
Website: http://www.mjt.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum at 9341 Venice Boulevard, Culver City, California (museum website); Wikipedia places it in the Palms district of Los Angeles.
- Founded by David Hildebrand Wilson and Diana Drake Wilson in 1988 (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia reports that the museum calls itself "an educational institution dedicated to the advancement of knowledge and the public appreciation of the Lower Jurassic" and that the term's relevance to the collections is never explained.
- Wikipedia lists more than thirty permanent exhibits, including micro-miniature sculptures carved from human hair, micromosaics made from butterfly scales and dogs of the Soviet space programme.
- A fire on 8 July 2025 caused minor damage, according to the fire department; no one was hurt and the museum reopened on 7 August 2025 (LAist, Robert Garrova, 9 August 2025).
- Visiting: open Thursday and Friday 2:00 pm to 8:00 pm, Saturday and Sunday noon to 6:00 pm; the museum recommends reserving tickets online (museum website). Admission is a suggested amount, $15 for adults, with children 12 and under free and other groups paying $12 or $3. Visitors with non-collapsible strollers are asked to check them at the entrance; visits are self-guided.

FUNDING
Museum of Jurassic Technology: small private non-profit: contributions from individuals, foundations and public arts funds
- Accounts (Form 990, ProPublica): fiscal 2024 revenue $678,768, expenses $662,601, contributions $685,538; fiscal 2023 revenue $785,213, expenses $585,234, contributions $787,551. Total assets were $2,039,525 at the end of 2024.
- Officers on the latest filing: Marina McDougall (President), David Wilson (Vice President), Mark Rossi (Treasurer), Matthew Coolidge (Secretary), Scott Rubel and Nada Subotincic (board members).
- Getty Foundation: two grants totalling $210,000 for research and implementation of "A Veiled Gazelle", the museum's exhibition on Islamic geometries of medieval al-Andalus, opening in September 2024 as part of PST ART (Hyperallergic, Matt Stromberg, 1 July 2024).
- Mike Kelley Foundation: $28,000 from its fourth annual Operational Support Grants, which helped bring the new permanent exhibition to life (Hyperallergic, 1 July 2024).
- Founder David Wilson received a MacArthur Foundation fellowship in 2001 (Wikipedia).
- Funder list: an undated page on the museum's site (mjt.org/donors.html) names 35 funders, including the National Endowment for the Arts, the Los Angeles County Arts Commission, the City of Los Angeles Department of Cultural Affairs, the California Arts Council, the Getty Grant Program, Bloomberg Philanthropies and the Lannan and MacArthur Foundations.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Getty)
- The Getty Foundation gave two grants totalling $210,000 for the museum's 2024 exhibition (Hyperallergic, Matt Stromberg, 1 July 2024).
- The Getty Foundation is one of the programmes of the J. Paul Getty Trust, whose endowment came from J. Paul Getty's bequest of nearly $660 million in Getty Oil stock, received in full in 1982 (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum spent $662,601 in fiscal 2024, funded by contributions of $685,538. Its 2024 grants are the Getty Foundation's $210,000 and the Mike Kelley Foundation's $28,000. The Getty money is the one flagged line: the Trust's endowment comes from Getty Oil stock.
