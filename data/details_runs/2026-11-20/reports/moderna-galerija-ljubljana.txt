MODERNA GALERIJA (MG+MSUM): Ljubljana, Slovenia
ID: moderna-galerija-ljubljana
Website: https://www.mg-lj.si (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Slovenia's national museum of modern and contemporary art, on two sites in Ljubljana: MG+ at Cankarjeva 15 and the Museum of Contemporary Art Metelkova (+MSUM) at Maistrova 3 (museum site).
- Founded by decree of the government of the People's Republic of Slovenia on 30 December 1947, in effect from 3 January 1948, on the initiative of the art historian and diplomat Izidor Cankar (museum site).
- Edvard Ravnikar designed the main building in the 1930s; it served as a military warehouse until 1945. +MSUM opened in late November 2011 in a former army barracks (museum site).
- Martina Vovk led the museum as acting director from 1 June 2023 and was appointed director from 15 May 2024 (Government of Slovenia). The museum has 33 employees (public information catalogue).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 am to 6 pm. Adults pay EUR 7, or EUR 10.50 for both sites; entry is free on the first Sunday of the month (museum site).

FUNDING
Moderna galerija (MG+MSUM): mainly public: the Republic of Slovenia through the Ministry of Culture
- The founder is the Republic of Slovenia (public information catalogue).
- Cash income in 2024 was EUR 2,635,932, down from EUR 2,802,617 in 2023. The state budget supplied EUR 2,390,387 of it: EUR 2,323,687 for running costs and EUR 66,700 for investment (annual report for 2024, pages 57 and 58).
- The rest included EUR 208,237 of other non-tax revenue, EUR 5,000 of domestic donations and EUR 20,400 from other European institutions and foreign states (same report).
- The main building was paid for from the estate of the industrialist Dragotin Hribar (museum site). Slovenian Wikipedia dates the Hribar family's gift to 1936.
- The Ministry of Culture allotted the museum the Metelkova barracks building in 1995 and renovated the museum quarter around it (museum site).

Red flags: 2020 removal of director Zdenka Badovinac
- The ministry advertised Badovinac's post three times and in November 2020 named the poet and art historian Robert Simonisek (spelling as in Apollo) interim director. Badovinac called her removal "absolutely political" (Apollo, Dorian Batycka, 23 December 2020).
- The ministry answered that appointing new directors had been routine under left-wing governments for decades (Apollo).
- An unsigned text posted by CIMAM on 10 March 2022, in the staff's voice, says the ministry changed the museum's statutes against "loudly voiced opposition" and changed the requirements for the post to enable the appointment of its "favoured candidate", Aleš Vaupotič, one of many "politically motivated changes" of directors.

Red flags: 2022 staff letter against director Aleš Vaupotič
- On 8 March 2022, 27 employees, 79.4 percent of all staff by their own count, wrote to the Government and the Ministry of Culture demanding Vaupotič's resignation or dismissal (CIMAM).
- Their grievances: he removed part of the permanent exhibition without telling its curators, mishandled contracts of external and part-time workers, approved inflated budgets and pressured employees (CIMAM).

Red flags: 2023 councils' rejection of Vaupotič's accounts and his resignation
- The museum's council and expert council refused to approve the annual report for 2022 and the work programme and financial plan for 2023. From April 2023 the museum was therefore financed at 80 percent under budget-execution law (ministry statement, reported by N1 and RTV Slovenija).
- The two councils described Vaupotič's management in letters to the ministry as "strokovno nekompetentno, negospodarno in netransparentno" (my translation: professionally incompetent, uneconomical and non-transparent).
- In May 2023 the ministry opened a procedure for his early dismissal. Vaupotič, appointed in April 2021 under Culture Minister Vasko Simoniti, resigned two weeks later (RTV Slovenija). He attributed the councils' refusal to changes in their membership (N1).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism in art and museum media concerns political control of the museum's leadership from 2020 to 2022, not its funding sources.
1. Apollo: Dorian Batycka, "Concerns rise about cultural freedom in Slovenia" (2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/slovenia-arts-government/
- Places Badovinac's removal among the Janša government's changes of cultural directors.
- Matevž Čelik, removed as director of the Museum of Architecture and Design, says two of the three jurors choosing his successor were appointed by the ruling party and that the requirement of professional experience was dropped.
2. CIMAM: "A Courageous Action by the staff of Moderna galerija, Ljubljana" (2022)
URL: https://cimam.org/museum-watch/museum-watch-actions/a-courageous-action-by-the-staff-of-moderna-galerija-ljubljana/
- CIMAM's Museum Watch Committee shares the staff letter with its members and calls it courageous.

WIDER CONTEXT
- An open letter of 17 November 2020 to Culture Minister Vasko Simoniti, with more than 1,000 signatures, accused the government of curtailing cultural freedoms and called the planned Museum of Slovenian Independence a "propaganda institution". The government replied on 13 December 2020 that this was incorrect (Apollo).
- The letter's target is the ministry that founded Moderna galerija and appoints its director.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is clean state money with public accounts. The weak point is the ministry's control of the director's post, which staff and the museum's own councils contested from 2020 to 2023. Worth watching: how the next change of government treats the post.
