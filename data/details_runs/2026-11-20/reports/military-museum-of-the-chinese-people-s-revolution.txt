MILITARY MUSEUM OF THE CHINESE PEOPLE'S REVOLUTION: Beijing, China
ID: military-museum-of-the-chinese-people-s-revolution
Website: http://www.jb.mil.cn (checked 2026-11-20; returned a server error)

OVERVIEW
- China's national military museum at No. 9 Fuxing Road in Haidian district, on the western extension of Chang'an Avenue south of Yuyuantan (Beijing municipal government, Visit Beijing, Wikipedia).
- Planned in 1958 as one of the Ten Great Buildings for the tenth anniversary of the People's Republic; the Central Military Commission approved its name on 12 March 1959, Mao Zedong wrote the name board, and it opened on 1 August 1960, Army Day (Visit Beijing, China Daily).
- The main building is 94.7 metres high and carries a six-metre gilded PLA emblem; its 4.9 metre bronze doors were cast from shell casings sent by units on the Fujian front (Visit Beijing).
- The Propaganda Department of the Party Central Committee listed it in July 1997 in its first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- It was rebuilt from September 2012 to July 2017 and now covers 159,000 square metres with 43 exhibition halls (China Daily, state media).
- The collection holds over 180,000 relics, 1,793 of them rated first class, with more than 80,000 historical photographs; the Hall of Weapons shows aircraft, missiles, tanks and captured Japanese, American and UN equipment (Beijing municipal government, Wikipedia).
- A gifts hall shows more than 500 presents received by the PLA from foreign armed forces in nearly 100 countries (Visit Beijing).
- On 21 July 2017 Xi Jinping and the Politburo Standing Committee visited the exhibition for the PLA's 90th anniversary there, where he called for building the army into a "world-class military" (Xinhua via gov.cn, state media; own translation).
- Visiting: 9:00 to 17:00, tickets issued until 16:00, closed Mondays except national holidays (Beijing municipal government).

FUNDING
Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution: state-funded: the People's Liberation Army
- Visit Beijing describes the museum as a deputy corps-level unit of the PLA under the General Political Department, with the department's directly affiliated work department as administrative supervisor and its propaganda department as professional supervisor.
- Its stated tasks are to collect and display the history of the armed struggle led by the Communist Party and to stage exhibitions around "the central work of the Party, the state and the army" (Visit Beijing; own translation).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism on record is a passing remark in an academic book review.
1. MCLC Resource Center: Joseph R. Allen, review of Kirk A. Denton, "Exhibiting the Past" (2015)
URL: https://u.osu.edu/mclc/book-reviews/allen
- Describes the museum, as treated in Denton's book, as "conservative", a place "where the cult of Mao lingers, and not in an ironic sense".
- A brief mention in a review of a wider study of Chinese state museums.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Chinese state's, paid through the army whose history it presents, and carries no funder flag. The weak point is purpose: it is run as a political education site of the PLA.
