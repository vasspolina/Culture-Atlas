NY CARLSBERG GLYPTOTEK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: ny-carlsberg-glyptotek
Website: https://www.glyptoteket.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Art and antiquities museum at Dantes Plads 7, Copenhagen (museum website).
- Built from the private collection of the brewer Carl Jacobsen, first shown in 1882 at his villa; he donated it in 1888 to the Danish State and the City of Copenhagen, and the purpose-built museum opened on 1 May 1897 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the original 1897 red-brick building by Vilhelm Dahlerup, a 1906 wing by Hack Kampmann, a 1996 addition by Henning Larsen, and a central Winter Garden with palms, mosaic floors and a copper-and-wrought-iron dome (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Greek, Roman and Etruscan antiquities, more than 1,900 Egyptian pieces, Near Eastern works, French 19th-century painting and sculpture including all of Degas's bronzes, and Danish Golden Age painting; more than 10,000 works in total (Wikipedia).
- Also on site: a picnic café, a shop and a roof terrace open in good weather (museum website).
- Visiting: closed Mondays; Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday 10:00 to 21:00. Adults pay DKK 150 at the museum (DKK 135 online); under 27 and students DKK 120; under 18 free; entry is free on the last Wednesday of each month. The museum's visit page gives no accessibility details and points to a separate accessibility page.

FUNDING
Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek: foundation-funded: the Carlsberg Foundation and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation, with a further 150 million kroner from the A.P. Møller family foundation
- Carlsberg Foundation and Ny Carlsberg Foundation: announced on 5 March 2026 DKK 1.5 billion in total, DKK 750 million from each, for restoring the listed buildings under the "Glyptotek of the Future" project. The full project budget is DKK 2 billion. The Ny Carlsberg Foundation had already given DKK 80 million in 2016 to 2025 for preliminary studies and design (Carlsberg Foundation).
- Origin of the money: the Carlsberg Foundation was established in 1876 by J.C. Jacobsen and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation in 1902 by Carl and Ottilia Jacobsen (Carlsberg Foundation). Carl Jacobsen transferred his Ny Carlsberg brewery to the Carlsberg Foundation in 1902, and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation was set up in part to support art in Denmark by operating the Glyptotek (lex.dk). It co-funded the 1996 Henning Larsen extension with the Carlsberg Foundation and financed a renovation of visitor facilities in 2004 to 2006 (lex.dk).
- A.P. Møller og Hustru Chastine Mc-Kinney Møllers Fond til almene Formaal: DKK 150 million towards restoring the dome and the Winter Garden. The foundation owns A.P. Møller Holding, which holds a large stake in Mærsk (TV 2 Kosmopol). The Glyptotek will close to visitors from 2028 for the work (TV 2 Kosmopol).
- Augustinus Fonden: supported the purchase of Gauguin's "La neige à Copenhague" in 2022, together with the Ny Carlsberg Foundation and Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond (Ny Carlsberg Foundation). No amount is given. No reporting found of a 2025 Augustinus Fonden grant of DKK 1.53 million to the Glyptotek.
- The Carlsberg Foundation describes the Glyptotek as a self-governing museum. No reporting found of the size of any state operating grant or of the ticket income.

Red flags: tobacco money (Augustinus Fonden)
- Augustinus Fonden supported the 2022 Gauguin purchase (Ny Carlsberg Foundation).
- Augustinus Fonden owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, its holding company (Wikipedia). That company held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group as of 1 May 2025, and C.W. Obel more than 10%; no other holder is listed above 5% (Scandinavian Tobacco Group investor page).

Red flags: antiquities bought from dealers later tied to looting
- Robert Hecht: the Glyptotek bought Etruscan and Greco-Roman objects from the American dealer Robert Hecht, who was on trial in Rome in 2009 (ArtsJournal, 2009).
- Italy: on 5 July 2016 the museum and Italy announced an agreement to return objects, with loans and wider cultural cooperation. A list from the Danish newspaper Politiken named 501 objects and fragments (Chasing Aphrodite).
- Turkey: on 26 November 2024 the museum decided to return a bronze head of Septimius Severus, bought in 1970, to Türkiye, ending an 18-month dispute. It concluded the head came from Bubon (The Local Denmark).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No review in the art press criticises the museum's programme or its funders. The criticism found concerns how it acquired antiquities, in heritage and trade press and a blog on the antiquities trade, not in the main art magazines.
1. Chasing Aphrodite (blog on the antiquities trade): no byline shown on the page, "The Danish Connection: Holding on to Loot at the Ny Carlsberg Glypotech of Copenhagen" (2013, updated 5 and 7 July 2016)
URL: https://chasingaphrodite.com/2013/03/14/the-danish-connection-holding-on-to-loot-at-the-ny-carlsberg-glypotech-of-copenhagen/
- Says Giacomo Medici's criminal sentence and Polaroids seized from his warehouse linked several Glyptotek objects to looting, including a chariot-race relief, a winged sphinx roof ornament and an Etruscan prince's tomb from the Colle del Forno necropolis.
- Says the museum first denied buying anything from Medici or his associates after 1970, then changed its position as documents emerged; negotiations with Italy began in 2008 and had stalled by 2009.
- Quotes Robert Hecht's memoir on his close relationship with the former director Mogens Gjødesen. The 2016 updates record the return agreement and the list of 501 objects.
- Only the tag page that summarises the post was read, not the full post.
2. ArtsJournal: Laura Collins Hughes, "Danish Museum Resists Italy's Bid For Artifacts' Return" (2009)
URL: https://artsjournal.org/?p=44471
- Reports that Italy had settled with American museums and collectors but found the Glyptotek harder to resolve matters with, over objects bought from Hecht.
- An archive excerpt only; the original outlet is not named on the page.
3. The New York Times: Elisabetta Povoledo, "Those Italian Artifacts Actually Were Looted, Danish Museum Now Says" (2016)
URL: https://news.culturecrime.org/entry/povoledo2016those-italian-a.html
- Only a database summary of the article was read: it says the pieces were stolen from a tomb near Fara and passed through Hecht's hands.
- Paywalled at the source; the full article was not read. News report, not art-press criticism.
4. The Local Denmark: "Denmark to return severed head of ancient statue to Türkiye" (2024)
URL: https://www.thelocal.dk/20241127/denmark-to-return-severed-head-of-ancient-statue-to-turkiye
- The museum bought the head in 1970 without information on its exact origin; in 1979 a curator proposed it belonged to a headless statue in the United States.
- In May 2023 Rune Frederiksen, the museum's director of collections, said "we are not as sure as we perhaps were 25-30 years ago." Gertrud Hvidberg-Hansen, the director, said finds from Bubon "have been sold illegally to collectors and museums around the world."
- Reports a decision by the museum; not an outside critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The restoration money comes from the foundations the Jacobsen family created and from the Møller family foundation. The weak points are Augustinus Fonden's 2022 gift, whose parent company holds a large stake in Scandinavian Tobacco Group, and the museum's record of buying antiquities from dealers later tied to looting, which it settled with Italy in 2016 and with Türkiye in 2024. Watch how the remaining 25% of the restoration budget is raised.
