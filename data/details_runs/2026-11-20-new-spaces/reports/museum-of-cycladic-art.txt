MUSEUM OF CYCLADIC ART: Athens, Greece
ID: museum-of-cycladic-art
Website: https://cycladic.gr/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of Aegean, ancient Greek and Cypriot art in the Kolonaki district of central Athens.
- Founded by Nikolaos and Aikaterini (Dolly) Goulandris to house their collection and inaugurated on 26 January 1986, a year after the N.P. Goulandris Foundation was set up.
- The founders began the collection in the 1960s, and later gifts include the Karolos Politis collection of 128 objects and the Thanos N. Zintilis collection of Cypriot antiquities, given in 2002.
- The museum is a non-profit legal entity under private law, supervised by the Ministry of Culture, and its collections belong to the Greek State (stated by the space).
- Visitors enter through the Stathatos Mansion at Vasilissis Sofias Avenue and 1 Irodotou Street while the Neophytou Douka Street entrance is under construction.
- Open Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday 10:00 to 20:00 and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Tuesday. Admission is EUR 12, reduced EUR 9, free for under 18s and for visitors with disabilities and a companion.

FUNDING
Museum of Cycladic Art: privately funded: the founders' foundation, tickets, friends, sponsors and Greek charitable foundations
- The museum states that it receives no state funding.
- Both founders came from shipowning families: the museum describes Nikolaos Goulandris and Dolly's father as shipowners.
- Charitable foundations shown on the supporters page include the Stavros Niarchos Foundation, the Latsis Foundation, the Leventis Foundation, DESTE, NEON and TIMA.
- Company supporters shown there include Aegean (official air carrier), Blue Star (maritime transport), Eurolife, IKEA, PwC, Atlantic Bulk Carriers, Euroseas and Enesel, most of them for the Museum Kits school programme.
- The European Union co-funded the museum's digital project under the Greek ESPA 2021 to 2027 programme.
- The museum posts audited annual statements for 2019 to 2022 on its Financial Data page, in Greek.

Red flags: protest over the Stern collection deal, 2022
- On 2 November 2022 the museum opened a one-year display of 15 Cycladic objects from the collection of the US billionaire Leonard N. Stern, under an agreement between Greece and the Metropolitan Museum of Art covering 161 objects, ratified by Parliament in September 2022 (Reuters).
- Protesters stood outside the museum at the opening with a banner reading "They're stolen" (Reuters).
- Five unions of archaeologists, conservators and ministry employees called the agreement a scandal and asked how the objects left the Cyclades (Reuters).
- On 23 December 2022 the Association of Greek Archaeologists called for the agreement to be cancelled, saying it "legitimizes the trafficking of antiquities and counterfeit monuments" (Archaeology Wiki).
- Professor Christos Doumas, who first published the Goulandris collection's Cycladic objects in 1968 (stated by the space), wrote to the ministry and the museum asking for a committee to check the authenticity of the Stern objects (Archaeology Wiki).
- Unverified: at least four or five of the Stern figurines shown in Athens are fakes, from Doumas in an online interview quoted by Archaeology Wiki.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2022 Stern deal and comes from Greek archaeologists. It targets the ministry's agreement more than the museum's programme.
1. Archaeology Wiki: Archaeology Newsroom, "The agreement on the Stern Collection antiquities should be cancelled" (2022)
URL: https://www.archaeology.wiki/blog/2022/12/23/the-agreement-on-the-stern-collection-antiquities-should-be-cancelled/
- Carries the Association of Greek Archaeologists' demand that Greek experts examine every Stern object and that the ministry document each provenance.
- Says published documents place one Stern figurine with the trafficker Gianfranco Becchina.
- Names the Museum of Cycladic Art only as a recipient of Doumas's letter.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private and Greek, from a shipowning family's foundation and from other shipping and business foundations, with no state grant. The weak point is the museum's part in the Stern arrangement: protesters stood at its door in 2022 and five unions called the agreement a scandal. Worth watching: the provenance checks the archaeologists asked for.
