SÀN ART: Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
ID: san-art-ho-chi-minh-city
Website: https://san-art.org

OVERVIEW
- Independent, artist-initiated, non-profit contemporary art organisation in Ho Chi Minh City. The name "sàn" means "platform" (benefit auction announcement, e-flux, December 2014).
- Founded in October 2007 (e-flux); the first exhibition opened on 3 October 2007 (Zoe Butt, SFMOMA Open Space, 27 September 2012). The four founders are Dinh Q. Lê, Tuan Andrew Nguyen, Phu Nam Thuc Ha (Phunam) and Tiffany Chung (Courtney Malick, SFAQ, 10 October 2014).
- It began as a reading room and international lecture programme, and later added curated exhibitions, an artist residency, workshops and talks (e-flux).
- Programmes named in the press: Sàn Art Laboratory, a studio and residency with six-month sessions for three artists, each receiving $1,000 (SFAQ); Conscious Realities, a three-year project of public lectures and closed workshops (SFAQ); and A.Farm, a free five-month residency camp for Southeast Asian visual artists in 2019 (Viet Nam News, 2 March 2019).
- Zoe Butt served as executive director and curator; ArtAsiaPacific reported on 12 September 2016 that she would step down in December of that year (Brady Ng).
- ArtAsiaPacific (Brady Ng, 12 September 2016) reports that in October 2015 the country's Cultural Police scrutinised the residency programme, and an exhibition planned for the following month featuring residency artists was cancelled. After warnings from the Vietnamese government in February 2016, Sàn Art "temporarily terminated its residency program". Government officials maintain that Sàn Art does not have a licence to host foreigners at its events.
- The e-flux directory lists the address as 132 Ben Van Don, Ward 6, District 4.

FUNDING
Sàn Art (Ho Chi Minh City): donor and benefit-auction funded: international organisations and individuals; Sàn Art states that Vietnam gives it no financial support
- A December 2014 benefit auction, "Thresholds of Motion", ran online on Paddle8 from 4 to 18 December 2014 with works by 24 artists including Dinh Q Lê, Tiffany Chung, Khvay Samnang and The Propeller Group. Proceeds went to infrastructure costs, artwork production grants and related exhibition and education expenses. The announcement quotes Dinh Q Lê: "Within Vietnam, there is no financial support for the work we do at Sàn Art", and thanks the international supporters of its first seven years (e-flux).
- Dinh Q. Lê is named as chairman of Sàn Art's board in that announcement.
- Butt said in 2016 that she could not realise certain goals at Sàn Art "due to limitations of space, legal status and funds" (ArtAsiaPacific).
- The 2019 A.Farm camp gave each participant a US$220 travel fund, an $850 production cost and a $180 monthly allowance (Viet Nam News).

BOTTOM LINE
Sàn Art is an independent non-profit founded by artists, financed by benefit auctions and by international organisations and individuals, and it states that Vietnam gives it no financial support. ArtAsiaPacific reported in 2016 that the Cultural Police scrutinised its residency in October 2015, that an exhibition was cancelled, and that Sàn Art ended its residency programme after government warnings in February 2016.
