NATIONAL CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ARTS: Minsk, Belarus
ID: national-centre-for-contemporary-arts-minsk

OVERVIEW
- State art centre in Minsk with two venues: a Stalinist-style building at Prospekt Nezavisimosti 47 and a building at Nekrasova Street 3, built for artists in the early 1980s, with 940.7 square metres of exhibition space in total (Russian Wikipedia).
- Established on 1 October 2015 by a Council of Ministers resolution (Russian Wikipedia gives that date in its text and cites a decree of 10 August 2015, no. 673, in its links), merging the Museum of Modern Fine Arts (1997 to 2015) and the Creative Workshops Centre of Contemporary Arts (2011 to 2015); it answers to the Ministry of Culture (Russian Wikipedia).
- The predecessor Creative Workshops were founded by Mikhail Savitsky, who offered three-year residencies to artists; after his death in 2010 the workshops closed in 2011 (Russian Wikipedia).
- Collection of 4,936 works by 892 Belarusian and foreign artists from the 20th and early 21st centuries (Russian Wikipedia).
- Directors: Natalya Sharangovich (2015 to 2017) and Sergei Krishtapovich (2018 to 2025) (Russian Wikipedia).
- It ran exhibitions, education, research and international cooperation, took part in special projects at the Venice Biennale in 2015 and 2017 and organised the Minsk Triennial (Russian Wikipedia). Nasha Niva adds that it supported experimental projects in visual, theatre and music art, worked with young artists and curators and took part in international projects including Belarus's national pavilions at the Venice Biennale. Charter97 says it organised the national pavilions at three Venice Biennales.
- Closure: the centre announced that exhibitions at Nekrasova Street 3 would stop from 22 June 2026 (Nasha Niva, 4 May 2026). The Council of Ministers agreed to liquidate it; the resolution was published on the national legal portal on 5 August 2026 and liquidation is to finish by 30 November 2026. The last exhibition closed on 2 August, and about 50 employees lose their jobs (Charter97, 5 August 2026).
- Charter97 reports, from two unnamed sources, that the collections and the Yakub Kolas Street site pass to the National Art Museum and the Nekrasova Street building to the Belarusian State Academy of Arts.
- Unverified: Charter97, an opposition outlet, writes that some link the closure to Andrei Vasilevsky, the new head of the Artists' Union and the Academy's vice-rector for administrative work, and that social media users link it to the renovation of the National Art Museum and the construction of a new Academy building.

FUNDING
National Centre for Contemporary Arts: state-funded: Belarus state budget through the Ministry of Culture
- The centre was created by a Council of Ministers resolution and answers to the Ministry of Culture (Russian Wikipedia).
- On liquidation, the Council of Ministers directed the centre's unused budget funds to the Belarusian State Academy of Arts and the National Art Museum (Charter97, 5 August 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the Belarusian state budget, and the state that created it has now ordered it liquidated by 30 November 2026, with its unused funds passing to the Academy of Arts and the National Art Museum.
