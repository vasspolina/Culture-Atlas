FUNDACIÓN PROA: Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: fundación-proa-buenos-aires
Website: https://proa.org (checked 2026-10-20; the record's website field points to Tecpetrol's community page instead)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre at Av. Pedro de Mendoza 1929, at Caminito in La Boca, Buenos Aires (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1996 (Wikipedia); founding members are Paolo Rocca, Zulema Fernández and Adriana Rosenberg, who is president (stated by the space).
- El País, republished by eju.tv in 2013, reported that the Rocca family, owners of Techint, set up Proa to revive the neighbourhood with strong exhibitions, starting with one house and later holding three.
- The first building was an Italianate-fronted house; the Milan studio Caruso-Torricella, which had converted it in 1996, designed the 2008 headquarters, keeping the restored facade and adding two glass facades (Wikipedia).
- Three floors with four exhibition rooms, a multimedia auditorium, a bookshop of more than 9,000 titles, a café with a terrace, an education department and the Proa21 programme (stated by the space).
- Proa also runs the culture programme of the Techint Organization worldwide (stated by the space).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 7pm; free on Wednesdays, otherwise $7,000 general and $5,000 for students, teachers and retirees, free for under-12s; the whole building is accessible to people with reduced mobility (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Fundación Proa: corporate-funded: the Techint group (Tenaris, Ternium, Tecpetrol)
- Proa states it has the "apoyo permanente" (permanent support) of Tenaris and Ternium, and the support of the Techint Organization, which it calls the most important industrial group in Argentina, with about 60,000 employees.
- Tecpetrol, the group's oil and gas company (Wikipedia), states that it supports Proa and takes its programme to the cities where it has fields (Tecpetrol community page).
- Proa offers a free Bloomberg Connects digital guide in 40 languages (stated by the space); the page does not say who pays for it.
- Visitors pay a "bono contribución" on Thursday to Sunday.
- No reporting found of a budget, annual report or accounts.

Red flags: oil and gas money through Tecpetrol
- Tecpetrol names Proa as one of its arts and culture programmes (Tecpetrol community page, October 2026).
- Tecpetrol produces shale gas at Fortín de Piedra in Vaca Muerta, about 13% of Argentina's gas output (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Tenaris bribery settlement with the SEC
- On 2 June 2022 Tenaris paid more than $78 million to settle US SEC charges under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, without admitting or denying the findings.
- The SEC found that its Brazilian subsidiary paid about $10.4 million in bribes to a Brazilian government official between 2008 and 2013 to win Petrobras business, funded by companies affiliated with Tenaris's controlling shareholder.
- In 2011 Tenaris entered a non-prosecution agreement with the DOJ and a deferred prosecution agreement with the SEC over bribes paid in Uzbekistan (SEC).

Red flags: founder Paolo Rocca in the Cuadernos bribery case
- In November 2018 federal judge Claudio Bonadio charged Rocca, Techint's CEO, in the Cuadernos case over bribes to officials of the former planning ministry, with an embargo of 4,000 million pesos and a ban on leaving the country (BAE Negocios).
- Techint executive Luis Betnaza said payments linked to the group's Venezuelan company Sidor were made to protect staff; Rocca denied knowing of them (BAE Negocios).
- The federal appeals court, Sala I, later revoked the charge and ordered "falta de mérito", finding his role in the holding did not link him to the case (El Litoral).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage of Proa's exhibitions is admiring; no review, essay or open letter found addresses its Techint funding.

BOTTOM LINE
Proa is a company art centre: the Techint group pays for it and the group's CEO is a founding member. The weak points are the group's oil and gas arm Tecpetrol, Tenaris's 2022 SEC bribery settlement and Rocca's place in the Cuadernos case. Worth watching: whether Proa ever publishes its accounts.
