VLEESHAL CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Middelburg, Netherlands
ID: vleeshal-middelburg
Website: https://vleeshal.nl (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art centre at Simpelhuisstraat 7, Middelburg, in a Gothic building that was a meat market and later the town hall (Vleeshal).
- Began in 1975 as a venue of the Middelburg Municipal Cultural Service, set up to create an art collection; restructured in 1992 and renamed Stichting Beeldende Kunst Middelburg (Vleeshal).
- Named for the Vleeshal, the Dutch word for meat hall, the building's former use (Vleeshal).
- Ran a second venue on Zusterstraat from 2000 to 2017, which closed on 1 October 2017 because of municipal cutbacks (Vleeshal).
- Manages Middelburg's municipal art collection and public art policy; since 2005 the collection has been on long-term loan at MHKA in Antwerp (Wikipedia). Also runs the Oemoemenoe programme with local Zeeland groups and businesses.
- Vivian Ziherl became managing director in September 2025, after an interim directorship by Julia Mullié from February 2025; Roos Gortzak led it for about ten years before (Vleeshal).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Friday 13:00 to 17:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00; a sensory-friendly opening is scheduled for 22 November 2026, 8:30 to 10:00 (Vleeshal home page).

FUNDING
Vleeshal: mainly public: Dutch state and the City of Middelburg
- The site's "Supported by" line shows two logos: the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science and the Municipality of Middelburg (Vleeshal).
- Its 2024 annual report says the Raad voor Cultuur (Council for Culture) advised positively for the second time in a row, so Vleeshal stays in the national Cultural Basic Infrastructure for 2025 to 2028. 
- The 2024 income table (2024 annual report, actual figures): total income 1,121,538 euros, of which total subsidies 1,068,110 euros (structural subsidies 1,023,426 euros from the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science and the city, plus incidental public subsidies); sponsor income 0. The report names two 2024 incidental grants: the province's Impuls Jongerencultuur scheme for the Team V project and the Mondriaan Fund's Kunstenaarshonorarium scheme.
- The report says private fund income fell short of the planned 30,000 euros a year for 2022 to 2024, and that its research found relatively few companies in the region willing to sponsor; it describes the "Pay it Forward" scheme, in which companies buy entry tickets to give away, as an alternative to sponsorship.
- The city: Vleeshal's second venue closed in 2017 because of municipal cutbacks (Vleeshal).
- Posts annual reports for 2019 to 2025, financial reports for 2019 to 2022 and an ANBI form (Vleeshal).
- Supervisory board: Monique van Beek, Trudi de Bruijne, Iris Cornelis, Stijn Huijts, Liesbeth Labeur and Jerry Troy, who is chair (Vleeshal; Vleeshal announcement of Vivian Ziherl).
- Vleeshal B.V. is wholly owned by the foundation (2024 annual report).
- Vleeshal also lists a Friends scheme; the pages read give no amounts.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.

Not related: the Vleeshal in Haarlem, a gallery of the Frans Hals Museum in a building designed by Lieven de Key that opened in 1604 (Wikipedia). It is a different organisation in a different city.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Vleeshal is public: in 2024 subsidies were 1,068,110 of 1,121,538 euros of income, from the Dutch state through the Cultural Basic Infrastructure and the City of Middelburg, with published annual reports and accounts. The weak point is that municipal cutbacks closed its second venue in 2017 and private income stayed below plan. Worth watching: the 2025 to 2028 subsidy period and the new director's first programmes.
