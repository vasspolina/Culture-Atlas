THE STATE HERMITAGE MUSEUM: Saint Petersburg, Russia
ID: state-hermitage-museum-spb
Website: https://hermitagemuseum.org (checked 2026-11-20; its security certificate failed standard verification)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of art and culture in a complex of six historic buildings on Palace Embankment, including the Winter Palace, the Small, Old and New Hermitage and the Hermitage Theatre; modern art hangs in the General Staff Building (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1764, when Catherine the Great bought paintings from the Berlin merchant Johann Ernst Gotzkowsky (Wikipedia).
- A 1996 presidential decree placed the Hermitage under the patronage of the President of the Russian Federation (Decree No. 862 of 12 June 1996).
- Mikhail Piotrovsky has directed it since 1992; Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin signed an order reappointing him for five years (Expert).
- The government created the post of museum president, appointed by the government on the Ministry of Culture's proposal, who also chairs the board of trustees, the fundraising council and the international advisory board (Sobaka.ru). Vladimir Potanin chaired the board of trustees in 2010 (Rossiyskaya Gazeta, search result only).
- Visiting: foreign tourists pay more than citizens of Russia and Belarus; entry is free on the third Thursday of each month and daily for students (Wikipedia). It recorded 2,812,913 visitors in 2022 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
The State Hermitage Museum: mainly public: the Russian federal budget, with Gazprom, Magnit, Sber and Samsung sponsoring galleries and shows
- The 1996 decree ordered the government to fund current maintenance from federal appropriations and to list the Hermitage's upkeep as a separate federal budget line from 1997 (Decree No. 862).
- News.ru, reviewing 2017 accounts, found the Hermitage received the largest state subsidy of the ten museums it studied, more than 5 billion rubles, and spent over 2 billion rubles on salaries (News.ru via Rambler, 19 July 2018).
- For 2026 the federal budget gives the Hermitage a capital investment subsidy of 929 million rubles, 6.2 times the previous year's (Delovoy Peterburg, 27 May 2026).
- Gazprom was general sponsor of the "Peter the Great Gallery" in the Winter Palace, opened on 14 January 2022 by Gazprom's management board chairman Alexey Miller, and helped restore Peter's ivory chandelier (Komsomolskaya Pravda). Two more halls opened with Gazprom's support on 21 June 2022 (AK&M, reprinting a Gazprom release).
- The retailer Magnit has been a "strategic partner" since an agreement signed at the end of 2023 covering exhibitions, restoration, theatre and publishing; it paid for a restoration in 2023, backed a Snyders show in 2024 and was general partner of an art deco fashion show from 30 April 2025 (Magnit release on Retail.ru).
- Sber was general partner of "On the Sweetness of the World. Contemporary Art of India", opened on 4 June 2026 in the General Staff Building (Lenta.ru). Samsung Electronics was technical partner of a provincial portrait show (Samsung newsroom).

Red flags: EU sanctions on the director, 2026
- The Council of the European Union listed Piotrovsky in its 20th sanctions package, adopted on 23 April 2026, as a "close associate" of Putin who "actively supported and justified" the war, citing his backing of the absorption of Ukrainian museum items and of excavations in occupied Crimea (The Art Newspaper, 29 April 2026).
- Canada had sanctioned him in August 2023, the first country after Ukraine to do so (The Art Newspaper, 31 August 2023).

Red flags: Gazprom sponsorship, 2022
- Gazprom, the state gas company, was general sponsor of the Peter the Great Gallery opened in January and June 2022 (Komsomolskaya Pravda; AK&M).

Red flags: 2022 suspension of ties by foreign partners
- After the invasion, Hermitage Amsterdam returned a loan show and cut ties, the Hermitage Foundation UK and the Italian foundation suspended support, the International Advisory Board announced an "indefinite suspension" on 15 March 2022, and the Bizot group suspended Piotrovsky's membership (The Art Newspaper, 4 April 2022).

Red flags: 2026 Accounts Chamber finding on the storage complex
- In its review of 2025 federal culture spending, the Accounts Chamber found shortcomings in building the Hermitage's production base and storage complex and a risk of ineffective use of 25.5 million rubles; unspent balances stood at 358.1 million rubles on 1 January 2026 (Delovoy Peterburg).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Press criticism since 2022 concerns the director's support for the war and the museum's ties to the state.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Isolated by Russia boycotts, Hermitage director calls for 'cultural bridges between the nations'" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/04/04/russia-boycott-hermitage-museum
- Piotrovsky had not condemned the invasion; he told the paper in 2021 that "Putin's been my person since the 1990s", and Putin gave him the Order of Friendship in 2016.
- Ukrainian cultural figures condemned the silence of Russian museum directors.
2. USA Art News: "State Hermitage Museum curator quits over Russia's war in Ukraine" (2022)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/state-hermitage-museum-curator-quits-over-russias-war-in-ukraine
- Dmitry Ozerkov, head of contemporary art, resigned on 2 October 2022, saying "I don't intend to have anything in common with today's Russia".
- Reports Piotrovsky's June 2022 Rossiiskaya Gazeta interview: "We are all militarists and imperials."
3. Novaya Gazeta Europe: Anna Matveeva, "The emperor" (2025)
URL: https://novayagazeta.eu/articles/2025/10/20/the-emperor-en
- Traces Piotrovsky from reformer to supporter of the war, who publicly backed Putin in the 2012, 2018 and 2024 elections.
- Cites a 2021 Proekt report that businesses closely tied to the Hermitage belonged to friends of the Piotrovsky family, and reports staff departures.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013 the Accounts Chamber found inflated restoration contracts at the Hermitage, with estimated damage above six billion rubles; Culture Ministry official Boris Mazo was later sentenced to eight years (Novaya Gazeta Europe).
- Hermitage archaeologist Alexander Butyagin, who led excavations at Myrmekion in occupied Crimea, was arrested in Poland in December 2025 at Ukraine's request and released to Russia in a prisoner exchange on 28 April 2026 (The Art Newspaper). Ukrainian prosecutors notified him of suspicion in 2024 and changed the charge in 2026 to violations of the laws and customs of war (United24 Media).
- Unverified: Ukraine's Defence Intelligence says the Myrmekion expedition removed 14 artefacts (United24 Media, Ukrainian state outlet). No court has ruled on the charges.

BOTTOM LINE
The Hermitage is a state museum paid for mainly from the federal budget, with corporate sponsors that include the state gas company. Its weak points are a director under EU sanctions for backing the war, a fresh audit finding on its building programme, and its isolation from former partners abroad.
