DEFENCE SERVICES MUSEUM: Naypyidaw, Myanmar
ID: defence-services-museum
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Military museum at the northern edge of Naypyidaw, next to a stadium built for the 2013 Southeast Asian Games; it opened in March 2012 (New Mandala, 2015).
- The site covers 603 acres on the outskirts of the capital (LSE blog, 2023), in Zayathiri Township (Recoil).
- The square site is split into quadrants holding an Air Force park, a Navy park and a shared artillery and armour park, around seven single-storey U-shaped halls: two for the Air Force, two for the Navy and three for the Army (Recoil).
- Exhibits include battle paintings, models of supply chains, missiles, rifles and signals intelligence equipment, portraits of Ne Win, Aung San and Than Shwe, and photographs of Min Aung Hlaing visiting factories (LSE blog); outdoors stand historic aircraft and Stuart and Sherman tanks (Recoil).
- Visitors are few: occasional school groups and military personnel during a 2020 visit (LSE blog); foreign visitors come mostly on official business, defence attachés among them (New Mandala).
- Visiting: 9.00 to 16.00, closed on Mondays and state holidays (Recoil).

FUNDING
Defence Services Museum: military: Myanmar's armed forces
- Operator: New Mandala describes it as the military officers' own museum, a "Tatmadaw showcase" with a big budget, lights and bilingual explanations (New Mandala, 2015).

Red flags: US sanctions on the armed forces' commanders, 2019 and 2021
- The US Treasury designated Commander-in-Chief Min Aung Hlaing and his deputy Soe Win on 10 December 2019 under Executive Order 13818, as leaders of a military whose members engaged in serious human rights abuse (US Treasury).
- After the coup of 1 February 2021, the Treasury designated both again on 11 February 2021 under a new executive order on Burma, among 10 individuals and three entities; the same action designated Lieutenant General Ye Win Oo, then Joint Secretary of the State Administration Council (US Treasury).
- Ye Win Oo took over from Min Aung Hlaing as Commander-in-Chief of Defence Services on 30 March 2026 (DMG).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Scholars describe the museum as the armed forces' own account of the nation; the critiques appear in academic blogs and press columns.
1. New Mandala: Nicholas Farrelly, "Past and future, the military still matters" (2015)
URL: https://www.newmandala.org/past-and-future-the-military-still-matters/
- The museum presents the armed forces' nationalist ideology and their view of their own central role in society, filtered through strategists, historians and spin doctors.
- Calls it "the closest that most people will ever get to the straight-shooting perspective of the military brass".
- First published in The Myanmar Times.
2. LSE Southeast Asia blog: Dominique Dillabough-Lefebvre, "The Nation Forgetting: Rehabilitating Memory in the Defence Service Museum in Myanmar" (2023)
URL: https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/seac/2023/07/07/the-nation-forgetting-rehabilitating-memory-in-the-defence-service-museum-in-myanmar/
- Calls it "a monument to the nation as imagined and projected by the military junta", tracing the violent incorporation of "internal enemies" on the periphery.
- Argues it forgets the military's shifting alliances with armed groups, such as the Border Guard forces and the United Wa State Army, and leaves the landless farmers who make up most of the population out of the national story.

BOTTOM LINE
Money and control both sit with a sanctioned military leadership. The museum is the armed forces' self-portrait, and its content follows from that.
