STPI CREATIVE WORKSHOP & GALLERY: Singapore, Singapore
ID: stpi-creative-workshop-gallery-singapore
Website: https://www.stpi.com.sg

OVERVIEW
- Print and paper workshop and contemporary art gallery at 41 Robertson Quay, by the Singapore River (Wikipedia; Little Day Out).
- Formed in 2002 as the Singapore Tyler Print Institute in line with the Singapore government's Renaissance City Plan, which e-flux (6 January 2017) ties to making Singapore a leading arts centre in Southeast Asia. Deputy Prime Minister Tony Tan opened it on 10 April 2002 (Wikipedia).
- The American master printer Kenneth E. Tyler's equipment and expertise moved to Singapore; the presses were shipped from his New York studio and a paper mill was installed (Wikipedia, which credits Renaissance City Plan director Liu Thai Ker with persuading him).
- Renamed STPI - Creative Workshop & Gallery in 2017, its 15th anniversary year (e-flux; Wikipedia).
- Building: a restored 19th-century warehouse of 4,000 square metres holding printmaking presses, a paper mill, a 400 square metre gallery, a guest workshop, an artist studio and artist apartments (Wikipedia).
- Visiting artists live in the guest apartments and make work in the workshop; STPI says it has collaborated with over 90 artists (Wikipedia).
- It describes itself as a non-profit and sits alongside National Gallery Singapore and Singapore Art Museum in the national Visual Arts Cluster (STPI website).
- Little Day Out (20 July 2023) gives opening hours of Monday to Saturday 10 am to 7 pm and Sunday 11 am to 5 pm, closed on public holidays.

FUNDING
STPI: non-profit supported by donors and patrons and "various funding sources" (STPI website)
- Patrons Programme: Individual Patron S$2,500 and Corporate Patron S$5,000, with benefits including studio visits, curator tours, a limited edition print and a signed exhibition catalogue. STPI states a goal of S$300,000 "this year" on its Support Us page (undated; the site footer reads 2026).
- Donations of any size are accepted; patron contributions carry a 250 percent tax deduction and are eligible for dollar-for-dollar matching from the Cultural Matching Fund (STPI Support Us page).
- Board: Hogi Hyun, Lito Camacho, Nathaniel Gunawan, Koh Lin-Net, Lynette Pang, Sam Kok Weng, Tan Chuan Yong, Jim Amberson, Eugene Tan and Kenneth Tan (STPI About page).
- Government origin: the government's Renaissance City Plan, accepted and unveiled in parliament on 9 March 2000, led to STPI's founding (Wikipedia).

Not related: Software Technology Parks of India (STPI), an Indian government agency founded in 1991 to promote software exports. It has nothing to do with the Singapore workshop.

BOTTOM LINE
STPI is a non-profit print workshop and gallery that was formed in 2002 in line with a Singapore government arts plan. Its website says it relies on various funding sources and asks patrons and donors for money, with the Cultural Matching Fund matching gifts dollar for dollar. Its ten board members are named on its About page.
