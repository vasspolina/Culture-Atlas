MUSEU LASAR SEGALL: São Paulo, Brazil
ID: museu-lasar-segall-são-paulo
Website: http://www.museusegall.org.br

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the work of the artist Lasar Segall, at Rua Berta 111 in the Vila Mariana district of São Paulo (Wikipedia).
- Conceived by Jenny Klabin Segall, the artist's widow; her sons Mauricio Segall and Oscar Klabin Segall founded it in 1967 as a non-profit association (Google Arts & Culture).
- In 1985 it was incorporated into the Fundação Nacional Pró-Memória, now part of the Instituto Brasileiro de Museus (IBRAM) under the Ministry of Culture, as a special unit (Google Arts & Culture).
- It occupies Segall's former home and studio, designed in 1932 by his brother-in-law, the Russian-born architect Gregori Warchavchik (Google Arts & Culture). Segall lived and worked there until his death in 1957 (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia counts more than 3,000 works by Segall in the collection, including paintings, sculpture, drawings and prints.
- On site: guided visits, courses in engraving, photography and literary creation, cinema screenings, and a library specialising in performing arts and photography (Google Arts & Culture).

FUNDING
Museu Lasar Segall: mainly public: a federal museum under IBRAM, supported by a friends' association
- Federal body: the museum is a unit of IBRAM, the federal museums institute under the Ministry of Culture (Google Arts & Culture).
- Support: the Associação Cultural de Amigos do Museu Lasar Segall (ACAMLS), a non-profit civil society organisation, supports the museum with money from public and private institutions and individuals (Google Arts & Culture).
- Petrobras: on 2 October 2013 Jornal de Brasília reported the Programa Petrobras Ministério da Cultura de Museus, Petrobras sponsorship of R$ 20 million for 12 institutions. For São Paulo the article says the money was to be used for the "requalificação" of the Museu Lasar Segall and for a museums search portal.
- Reopening: an exhibition listing (My Art Guides) says the museum resumed activities in 2015 after 18 months closed for infrastructure renovations.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: it is a federal IBRAM unit, backed by a friends' association of public and private donors. The one documented corporate sponsor is Petrobras, an oil company, whose museums programme, announced in October 2013, was to fund the museum's requalification; the museum reopened in 2015 after 18 months of infrastructure works.
