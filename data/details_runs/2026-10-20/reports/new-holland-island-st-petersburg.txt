NEW HOLLAND ISLAND: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: new-holland-island-st-petersburg
Website: https://newhollandsp.ru/en/information (checked 2026-10-24; working; the visit page returned HTTP 404)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural and public space on a man-made island in central St Petersburg, run by the non-profit ANO New Holland (project website).
- Peter the Great founded the island in 1719. It served the Russian Navy as a warehouse and administrative site, a ship-model testing site, a naval prison and a radio station (project website).
- The Ministry of Defence left the buildings in 2004; a Foster + Partners redevelopment plan stalled (Wikipedia).
- New Holland Development won the bid to restore and redevelop the island in November 2010 (project website).
- Dasha Zhukova chose the design by the New York firm WORKac (Prospect). Her Iris Foundation organises the summer programme (project website).
- On site: a park, Garden Walk, Herb Garden, beach, playgrounds and restored buildings including the Foundry, the Commandant's House and House 12 (project website). House 17, a former warehouse on the Moika, was due to open in summer 2026 with an exhibition space, bookshop, concert hall and restaurant (Business Petersburg, 14 May 2026).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility: the visit page was unavailable.

FUNDING
New Holland Island: privately funded: Roman Abramovich's company group and the Iris Foundation
- Millhouse LLC, founded by Roman Abramovich, won the 2010 tender to restore the buildings and reorganise the island (Fontanka, 8 June 2022). The Iris Foundation, run by Abramovich and his former wife Dasha Zhukova, produced the public space concept (Fontanka).
- Abramovich committed "at least" 12 billion roubles for a seven-year redevelopment and received a long-term lease; his spokesman called the figure a "preliminary estimate" (Prospect, 2011).
- By February 2024, more than 12 billion roubles had been invested and three of five construction stages were complete. New Holland Development, with 5.86 billion roubles of charter capital, was wholly owned by Cyprus-based Protonius Investments Ltd according to Kontur.Focus; the paper says Protonius is generally considered controlled by Abramovich. The company's participants had recently been hidden in the Russian company register (Business Petersburg, Evgenia Ivanova, 28 February 2024).
- The Art Newspaper reported in April 2024 that Russian media said the company was still controlled by Abramovich, and that New Holland had become an independent non-profit.
- No reporting found of public money paid into the project, or of a published sponsor list.

Red flags: sanctions on Roman Abramovich and work suspended
- On 10 March 2022 the UK froze Abramovich's assets and banned him from entering the UK, with six other oligarchs (UK government press release).
- On 15 March 2022 the EU added him to its list of individuals facing asset freezes and travel bans over the invasion of Ukraine. The EU cited his "privileged access" to President Putin and his stake in the steel group Evraz (Associated Press, 15 March 2022).
- In June 2022 the project's press service said restoration and construction work had stopped. Unverified: Fontanka links the stop to the freezing of Abramovich's assets in mid-March 2022; the project would not say, and the city government did not answer (Fontanka, Alina Ampelonskaya, 8 June 2022).
- In August 2023 the management company told Fontanka the project was still "frozen" with no start date (Fontanka, Galina Boyarkova).
- Business Petersburg (28 February 2024) says the EU and UK added Abramovich to their sanctions lists in spring 2022 and that he tried to challenge this in court without success; the US did not sanction him. It reports three of five stages complete and does not link the sanctions to the work stoppage.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of New Holland found in art media. Coverage is news reporting; the one critical essay is from 2011 and predates the sanctions.
1. Prospect: Shaun Walker, "Rebuilding Russia" (2011)
URL: https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/essays/49432/rebuilding-russia
- Asks why Abramovich committed funds before a design competition or feasibility studies, and notes the payback period could be optimistic.
- Says Russia's wealthy are expected to invest in national projects and stay out of politics, and that the deal's terms are not public. General magazine, not art press; written before the sanctions.
2. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/04/26/head-of-new-holland-art-centre-in-st-petersburg-founded-by-dasha-zhukova-and-roman-abramovich-resigns-after-husbands-social-post
- News report, not criticism: general director Roxana Shatunovskaya resigned on 25 March 2024 after her husband, Nikolay Konashenok, posted about the Crocus City Hall attack; the Russian government listed him as a "terrorist and extremist" on 17 April 2024.
- Does not mention sanctions.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind New Holland is private, from Abramovich's companies. The weak points are the March 2022 UK and EU sanctions on Abramovich, the 2022 work stoppage that Fontanka linked to them, the hidden ownership register. Watch whether House 17 opens and how the project's ownership is disclosed.
