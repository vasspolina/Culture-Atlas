WIDER CONTEXT
- The Rafael Viñoly building opened three years late and £9m over budget (Museums Journal, Gary Noakes, 16 February 2015). The Art Newspaper put the cost at £27m, up from an initial estimate of £17m, with £8m from the Arts Council's Lottery Fund (The Art Newspaper, Martin Bailey, 1 October 2015).
- On 12 February 2015 Arts Council England placed Firstsite under special funding arrangements and left it out of the National Portfolio for 2015-18, citing "continuing concerns" about business plans and governance. Its grant for 2015/16 was to be released only as conditions were met, including a viable business plan, "strong new governance" with a new chair and refreshed board, and a "viable" staffing structure (Museums Journal, 16 February 2015).
- Director Matthew Rowe declined to give the size of the deficit and said he regretted not engaging a forensic financial controller when he joined in January 2013 (Museums Journal, 16 February 2015).
- Bob Russell, the local Liberal Democrat MP, said he had warned at the outset that the project was not financially sustainable, and called it "a disgrace" and "a shambles" (Museums Journal, 16 February 2015).
- Rowe resigned in April 2015. Arts Council acting chief executive Althea Efunshile warned that unless Firstsite improved its governance and business model it "could face the removal of our funding" (The Art Newspaper, 1 October 2015).
