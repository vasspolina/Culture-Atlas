BRAEMHUIS (RENAAT BRAEM HUIS): Antwerp, Belgium
ID: braemhuis-renaat-braem-huis
Website: https://www.herita.be/nl/monumenten/braemhuis (checked 2026-11-20; the English page at herita.be/en/house-braem refused the automated check with a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Live-work house and studio of the modernist architect and planner Renaat Braem at Menegemlei 23 in Deurne, a district of Antwerp, in a residential neighbourhood; it is named for him (Herita).
- Braem and his wife, the graphic artist Elza Severin, planned it from about 1950; he filed the building permit application in January 1955, the shell went up from February 1957 and the finishing ran into early 1958 (Onroerend Erfgoed inventory).
- A brick facade hides a concrete skeleton; split levels inside, with stairs, panelling, parquet and built-in cupboards in wengé, oregon and limba wood (inventory).
- Braem lived there until 1997. In 1999 the house and all its contents were given to the Flemish government, which had it restored in 2000 to 2002 by the architects Walter Slock and Willem Hulstaert; it became a house museum in 2003 (inventory). It has been a protected monument since 3 April 1995 (inventory).
- Since 2024 the heritage non-profit Herita has worked with the City of Antwerp to open the house to the public, and is studying its restoration needs and whether it can take a role in its conservation (Herita).
- Herita describes the house as no megalomaniac architect's home: the plot is modest and the rooms are full of objects the couple collected (Herita).
- Visiting: group visits only. Expeditie De Stad runs 75-minute tours for 8 to 12 people at 20 euros a person, every day during the day except Sundays and public holidays (Expeditie De Stad). The house is not accessible to wheelchair users because it is kept in its original state (Flemish Architecture Institute, Heritage Day listing, 21 April 2024).

FUNDING
Braemhuis: public: given to the Flemish government in 1999 and opened by the heritage network Herita with the City of Antwerp
- The Flemish government received the house and contents as a gift in 1999 and paid for the 2000 to 2002 restoration (inventory).
- Herita vzw, recognised on 1 May 2024 as the first Flemish heritage network, had a one-year transitional cooperation agreement with the Flemish government for 2025 worth 6.3 million euros, approved on 13 December 2024, for maintenance and restoration at its sites; the decision does not name the Braemhuis (vlaanderen.be).
- Herita's 2025 accounts filed with the National Bank of Belgium show operating income of 7.84 million euros, of which subsidies were 6.19 million, gifts 32,274 euros, admissions 209,196 euros and operating fees 589,329 euros; it had 38 full-time staff and a profit of 176,237 euros. The accounts do not break down income by site.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the house is Flemish public money, through the government and the heritage network it subsidises. No private sponsor or flagged funder is linked to it; worth watching is who pays for any restoration that follows Herita's study.
