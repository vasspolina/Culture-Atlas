ID: doualart-douala

WIDER CONTEXT
- 1996: after doual'art had La Nouvelle Liberté, a sculpture about 12 metres high by Joseph-Francis Sumégné, erected at the Deïdo roundabout, "the polemic of a local newspaper" launched a campaign against it; "the artist and the organisers were threatened" and the sculpture "could not be completed" (springerin, Christian Hanussek, "La Nouvelle Liberté oder le Nju-Nju du Rond-Point", issue 4/2007).
- The campaign centred on the artist's Bamiléké background; Hanussek links it to an attempt to turn Sawa residents against the Bamiléké ahead of the 1997 presidential election. The writer Lionel Manga is quoted: "The scandal was not the work of art itself, but the artist's background" (springerin).
- The statue stayed in place. Doual'art had obtained municipal permission and raised funds from private sponsors and French cultural co-operation before it went up (springerin).
- December 2017: at SUD 2017, the Salon urbain de Douala organised by doual'art and held from 5 to 10 December 2017 (Biennial Foundation), a giant self-portrait by the French artist Sylvie Blocher holding a sign reading "Bien que je n'en aie pas le droit, je vous présente mes excuses" stood at a crossroads in Bonakouamouang, with the agreement of the Communauté urbaine de Douala (Le360, Elisabeth Kouagne, 7 December 2017).
- Le360 reported that part of the neighbourhood rejected the work, some residents preferring monuments to national historical figures, and that it was "déboulonnée et détruite" ("taken down and destroyed", translated) by André Blase Essama, whom it describes as one of the city's most fervent defenders of the nationalist cause. Blocher said the work asked whether "une personne ou un pays peuvent-ils se reconstruire ou s'émanciper s'ils n'ont pas reçu d'excuses" (Le360).
