FOTOMUSEUM WINTERTHUR: Winterthur, Switzerland
ID: fotomuseum-winterthur-winterthur
Website: https://www.fotomuseum.ch

OVERVIEW
- Photography museum at Grüzenstrasse 44 and 45, Winterthur, run as the Stiftung Fotomuseum Winterthur (the museum's partners page; Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1993 by the photographer George Reinhart, a nephew of the art collector Oskar Reinhart (Wikipedia).
- Since autumn 2003 it has run the Center of Photography with Fotostiftung Schweiz, sharing a bistro, library, seminar rooms and shop; the Center occupies two buildings on opposite sides of Grüzenstrasse, with about 1,000 m2 of exhibition area (Wikipedia).
- It closed its exhibition spaces in June 2023 for renovation and expansion, planned to run until spring 2025; the new building houses the collection galleries (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds about 4,000 photographs, bought, donated or on permanent loan; the museum runs the SITUATIONS programme on photography in the digital age, led by director Nadine Wietlisbach, and the annual Plat(t)form forum for emerging European photographers (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 11:00 to 17:00, Wednesday 11:00 to 20:00 (free entry from 17:00), Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, closed Monday (the museum's partners page, December 2025).

FUNDING
Fotomuseum Winterthur: mainly private and self-earned, with about one third public money from the Canton of Zurich and the City of Winterthur
- Winterthur city report (3 July 2024): the museum was funded from the start mainly by firms, foundations and private persons plus its own earnings; canton and city together cover about one third of an annual budget of about CHF 3 million, and self-financing is close to 70%. The museum has reported a yearly deficit since 2015.
- City of Winterthur: annual culture grant of CHF 460,000 under the current contract, CHF 738,000 a year proposed for 2025 to 2032, CHF 5,904,000 in total (Winterthur city report, 2024).
- Canton of Zurich: operating grant rising by CHF 270,000 to CHF 770,000 a year from 2024, approved for four years (quoted in the city report).
- Private operating support: the Verein Fotomuseum Winterthur gave CHF 500,000 a year earlier and CHF 350,000 a year since 2020; the Volkart Stiftung gave CHF 215,000 a year from 2003 until the support ran out at the end of 2022 (city report).
- Partners page (archived copy of 2 December 2025): public sector the City of Winterthur and the Canton of Zurich; main partners the Verein Fotomuseum Winterthur and the Volkart Stiftung; partners Hunziker Betatech, Monterosa and Ringier (logos linking to their sites); under Stiftungen the Fineheart Foundation, Gerda Techow Gemeinnützige Stiftung, Hans F. Tellenbach-Stiftung, Niarchos Stiftung für junge Künstler, Stiftung Salientes, Verena und Roland Geissmann Stiftung and Walter Haefner Stiftung, and a logo linking to the Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach-Stiftung (krupp-stiftung.de).
- The Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach-Stiftung funds curator fellowships in photography at partner museums including this one, about 60,000 euros per fellowship (Krupp-Stiftung press release, 27 March 2025).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mostly its own earnings and private support (the Verein, foundations and companies), with about one third from the Canton of Zurich and the City of Winterthur, which has proposed CHF 738,000 a year for 2025 to 2032. The Krupp-Stiftung, a foundation partner that funds curator fellowships hosted at the museum, is the largest shareholder of thyssenkrupp, whose Marine Systems unit builds warships and made 2,187 million of the group's 32,837 million euros of sales in 2024/25.
