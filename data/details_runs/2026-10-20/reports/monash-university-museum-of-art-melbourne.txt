MONASH UNIVERSITY MUSEUM OF ART: Melbourne, Australia
ID: monash-university-museum-of-art-melbourne
Website: https://www.monash.edu/muma/about

OVERVIEW
- University museum of contemporary art on the Caulfield campus of Monash University, Melbourne, known as MUMA (Wikipedia).
- Grew from a fund created in 1961 by Vice Chancellor Louis Matheson to buy work by living Australian artists; Patrick McCaughey created the Monash University Gallery in 1975; it was renamed Monash University Museum of Art in 2002 (Wikipedia).
- The current building on the Caulfield campus, designed by Kerstin Thompson and completed in 2010, includes the Ian Potter Sculpture Court (Wikipedia).
- The ABC describes MUMA as owned by Monash University, with a 50-year history of exhibitions (ABC, 26 March 2025).

FUNDING
Monash University Museum of Art: university museum: owned by Monash University
- Monash University owns the museum and took the decision to postpone its 2025 exhibition (ABC, 26 March 2025).

Red flags: indefinite postponement of an exhibition by the owner, 2025
- Monash University, which owns the museum, postponed "Stolon Press: Flat earth" indefinitely in March 2025. It was due to open on 8 May after 18 months of planning and included work by Khaled Sabsabi. The ABC understood it to be the first exhibition pulled in the gallery's 50-year history (ABC, Nicola Heath and Daniel Browning, 26 March 2025).
- Reason given: a Monash spokesperson said that after consulting communities the university had "identified there is a need for the Museum to deepen its collaboration and engagement on this exhibition", and that postponing "will allow this important work to be undertaken" (ArtReview, 26 March 2025). The spokesperson did not say whether the postponement was tied to Sabsabi's withdrawn Venice appointment or whether a new date had been chosen (Hyperallergic, Maya Pontone, 26 March 2025, updated 27 March).
- Stolon Press's founders Tom Melick and Simryn Gill said they did not agree to it (Hyperallergic).
- Penelope Benton, executive director of the National Association for the Visual Arts (NAVA), called the decision "a devastating blow to artistic and academic freedom and the integrity of our public institutions", and Ben Eltham, president of the NTEU Monash branch, said "a university censoring its own art gallery is a really bad idea" (Visual Arts Australia, 27 March 2025).
- Unverified: Josh Milani, Sabsabi's gallerist, said MUMA's curators "started receiving pressure from their superiors" after Sabsabi's Venice commission was pulled; this is Milani's claim, not a Monash statement (ABC; ArtReview).
- The exhibition ran from 29 May to 12 July 2025 (Milani Gallery).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is of Monash University's 2025 postponement of one exhibition, not of MUMA's funders or programme.
1. ArtReview: no named author, "Khaled Sabsabi exhibition cancelled following Venice Biennale sacking" (26 March 2025)
URL: https://artreview.com/khaled-sabsabi-exhibition-cancelled-following-venice-biennale-sacking-monash-university
- Reports that Monash postponed the show weeks after Sabsabi and curator Michael Dagostino were withdrawn as Australia's Venice Biennale representatives, gives Milani's claim of pressure on curators and prints Monash's written reply.
2. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Australian University Scraps Show Featuring Work of Lebanese-Born Artist" (26 March 2025, updated 27 March)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/australian-university-scraps-show-featuring-work-of-lebanese-born-artist
- Reports the postponement of the show, due to run from 8 May to 5 July, and the objection of Stolon Press's founders; quotes Milani calling it a "direct result of Creative Australia's abandonment of him".
3. Visual Arts Australia (NAVA): Penelope Benton and Ben Eltham, "Monash University's postponement of exhibition featuring Khaled Sabsabi undermines artistic and academic freedom" (27 March 2025)
URL: https://visualarts.net.au/news-opinion/2025/monash-universitys-postponement-exhibition-featuring-khaled-sabsabi-undermines-artistic-and-academic-freedom/
- The authors argue the decision rests on assumptions about Sabsabi rather than the work, which they call quiet and meditative, and that giving in to pressure erodes trust and academic freedom.
- They cite Monash's removal of the right to student protest without consultation and urge Monash to reinstate the show.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2025 Creative Australia's board voted to drop Sabsabi and curator Michael Dagostino from the 2026 Venice Biennale, after Coalition arts spokesperson Claire Chandler raised concerns in parliament about works of his from more than 15 years earlier. Mikala Tai (head of visual arts), program manager Tahmina Maskinyar and board member Lindy Lee resigned (ABC).
- Connection: ArtReview and Hyperallergic tie the Monash postponement, about a month later, to that decision; Monash did not say whether the two were linked (Hyperallergic).

BOTTOM LINE
MUMA is a university museum owned by Monash University. The documented issue is Monash's indefinite postponement of the Sabsabi exhibition in March 2025, a first in the gallery's 50 years, which the show's organisers, NAVA and the NTEU opposed; the exhibition ran from 29 May to 12 July 2025.
