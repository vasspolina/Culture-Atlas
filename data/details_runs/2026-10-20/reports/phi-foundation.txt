PHI FOUNDATION: Montréal, Canada
ID: phi-foundation
Website: https://phi.ca (checked 2026-10-25; loaded)

OVERVIEW
- Not-for-profit contemporary art foundation in Old Montréal, with exhibitions at 451 rue Saint-Jean and 407 rue Saint-Pierre (phi.ca).
- Founded by Phoebe Greenberg, who was inspired by the Fondation Cartier; it opened on 5 October 2007 as DHC/ART at 451 rue Saint-Jean (phi.ca; Canadian Art, Leah Sandals, 2017).
- Renamed PHI Foundation for Contemporary Art in 2019 (phi.ca).
- Building and site: DHC/ART opened in two heritage buildings in Old Montréal (Canadian Art, 2017). The separate PHI Centre at 407 rue Saint-Pierre opened in 2012 and houses the Habitat Sonore listening room (phi.ca); the Globe and Mail (2022) describes the Centre as in theory a for-profit enterprise.
- Planned expansion: PHI Contemporain will restore heritage buildings including the Pierre Du Calvet and Louis-Viger houses and build a new building on vacant land (Government of Canada, 2022).
- Visiting: the Globe and Mail (2022) says Foundation admission is free while the Centre charges tickets; Canadian Art (2017) says all exhibitions are free. No reporting found of opening days; phi.ca says hours vary by exhibition. No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
PHI Foundation: mainly private: Phoebe Greenberg's family wealth, with public money for the expansion
- Everett-Green (Globe and Mail, 23 December 2016) described DHC/ART as "sustained entirely by one benefactor," Greenberg, and cited a Canadian Business survey putting the Greenberg family's property interests, through Minto Group, at about $1.58 billion. Wikipedia says she is the daughter of Irving Greenberg, a co-founder of Minto Group in 1955.
- Operating money: the Globe and Mail (Kate Taylor, 15 July 2022) says the Foundation is a non-profit financed entirely with private money; it does not say how much comes from Greenberg or her family. Greenberg told Canadian Art (2017) that not relying on public funding lets the foundation move quickly on exhibitions. In Momus (2019) she said the for-profit PHI Centre receives some government project funding, exhibition support and sponsorship.
- Expansion money: on 29 April 2022 the Government of Canada and the Government of Quebec announced $26,645,740 for PHI Contemporain, with more than $13.3 million from Quebec and over $13.3 million from Canada through the Community, Culture and Recreation Infrastructure stream of the Investing in Canada Infrastructure Program (Government of Canada news release). The Globe and Mail (2022) put the expansion budget at $100 million. The release does not state PHI's own contribution.
- Corporate partners: the Partners Circle launched in 2025 with levels of $5,000, $10,000 and $15,000 a year. The page shows logos for SiriusXM, Solotech, Davies, Hydro-Québec, Silvercreek and JodoinLamarrePratte under "Our Partners" and does not say they are Circle members (phi.ca).
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts or a board list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. Coverage is mostly admiring; the one criticism found concerns how little Canadian art the Foundation shows.
1. Momus: Sky Goodden, "The Seductive Branding of Phoebe Greenberg" (2019)
URL: https://momus.ca/the-seductive-branding-of-phoebe-greenberg/
- An interview with Greenberg (20 February 2019) about her personal brand and her institutions.
- Notes that DHC/ART shows little Canadian art, which has drawn criticism; Greenberg answers that the Foundation focuses on audiences rather than Canadian artists.
- Does not examine the institutions' finances or governance in depth.
2. The Globe and Mail: Kate Taylor, "Montreal's Phi Foundation wants to make art an urban routine" (2022)
URL: https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/art-and-architecture/article-montreals-phi-foundation-wants-to-make-art-an-urban-routine
- Notes that Phi's success has been met "with some envy" in Montréal.
- Contrasts Phi's private backing with the Darling Foundry, "reliant entirely on grants and fundraising," which has waited 13 years for provincial money. Not criticism of Phi itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Foundation is one family's real estate fortune, with a public capital grant of $26.6 million from two governments for the expansion; no flagged-industry funder was found. The weak points are that no accounts or board list are published and that the family contribution is not quantified in any source read. Worth watching the corporate Partners Circle and the public money in the expansion.
