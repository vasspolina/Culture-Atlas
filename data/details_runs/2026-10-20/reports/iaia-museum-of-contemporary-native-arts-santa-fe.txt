IAIA MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY NATIVE ARTS: Santa Fe, USA
ID: iaia-museum-of-contemporary-native-arts-santa-fe
Website: https://iaia.edu

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary Indigenous art in the Santa Fe Federal Building, the former post office, at 108 Cathedral Place near the Santa Fe Plaza and St Francis Cathedral (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1991 by the Institute of American Indian Arts (IAIA), which still owns it (Wikipedia). IAIA was co-founded in 1962 with funding from the Bureau of Indian Affairs, ran a high school program from 1962 to 1979, and was chartered by Congress as a nonprofit in 1986 (Wikipedia, Institute of American Indian Arts).
- Building: the Santa Fe Federal Building, a Pueblo Revival structure built in 1920 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places on 15 August 1974 (Wikipedia, Institute of American Indian Arts).
- Collection: nearly 10,000 works from 1962 onward, stored in a 7,000 square foot facility on the IAIA campus 12 miles south of town (Wikipedia).
- On site: the Allan Houser Sculpture Garden and Art Park in the west courtyard, showing rotating works by Houser on loan from the Allan Houser Foundation (Wikipedia).
- In August 2025 it partnered with the National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts on a show of Taiwanese Indigenous works. As of March 2025 Patsy Phillips was director and Manuela Well-Off-Man chief curator (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free admission on Fridays through December 2026 under an Art Bridges grant (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
IAIA Museum of Contemporary Native Arts: mainly public through its parent: federal appropriation to IAIA, with foundation grants
- The museum is part of IAIA, which receives about 75% of its operating expenses from federal funding. Congress appropriated $13.48 million for IAIA covering July 2026 to September 2027 (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan, 22 January 2026).
- IAIA's Form 990 for 2023 shows revenue of $30,141,322, of which $24,273,876 was contributions and grants and $5,104,528 was program service revenue, against expenses of $24,331,198 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Ford Foundation: in 2020 the museum was one of 20 recipients of America's Cultural Treasures funding, an initiative led by the Ford Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Art Bridges Foundation: a 2025 Access for All grant pays for free Friday admission through December 2026 (Wikipedia).
- IAIA's endowment was $28.7 million in 2025 (Wikipedia, Institute of American Indian Arts).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Trump administration's FY2026 budget proposal suggested eliminating IAIA, and the funding bill the Senate passed covered it anyway (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan, "Congress Funds Institute of American Indian Arts", 22 January 2026).
- The FY2027 proposal, submitted to Congress on 3 April 2026, would eliminate about $13 million in federal funding for IAIA. IAIA president Shelly C. Lowe said: "Eliminating IAIA's federal appropriation will weaken educational opportunity." Senator Ben Ray Luján pledged to fight what he called "devastating cuts" (Source New Mexico, Danielle Prokop, 8 April 2026, republished by Indian Country Today on 12 April 2026).
- The museum is part of IAIA, so the proposals target the institution that owns and funds it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public and philanthropic: a federal appropriation to its parent, IAIA, plus Ford Foundation and Art Bridges grants.
