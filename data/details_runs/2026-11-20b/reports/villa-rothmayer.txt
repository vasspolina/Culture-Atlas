VILLA ROTHMAYER: Prague, Czechia
ID: villa-rothmayer
Website: https://www.muzeumprahy.cz/en/visit/rothmayerova-vila-6 (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- House museum at U Páté baterie 896/50, Prague 6, Břevnov, on the border with Střešovice near the Central Military Hospital (Museum of Prague; Expats.cz).
- The architect Otto Rothmayer built it as his family home in 1928 and 1929 from concepts by Jože Plečnik, whose student and collaborator he was at Prague Castle and Lány Castle; it is named for him (Expats.cz).
- His wife, the textile artist Božena Rothmayerová, had her study there, and the spiral staircase carries artefacts he brought home from Prague Castle (Museum of Prague).
- The garden holds glass sculptures by René and Miluše Roubíček, and Rothmayer's friend Josef Sudek photographed it for his "Magic Garden" series (Expats.cz).
- The family lived there until 2008; the architect's son, the photographer Jan Rothmayer, sold it to the city on condition that it be restored and opened to the public (Novinky; Czech Wikipedia). The museum dates the purchase to 2007.
- It has been a protected cultural monument since 3 August 2010 (Czech Wikipedia).
- Restored in 2012 and 2013, interiors and part of the garden, to its look of the late 1950s and early 1960s; handed to the Museum of Prague in 2014 and opened in September 2015 with the exhibition "The Story of One House and One Family" (Museum of Prague).
- The garden renovation inventoried every element and pruned the trees to evoke its atmosphere of the 1950s, when Sudek and Jan Rothmayer documented it (Zprávy památkové péče, 2013).
- In 2024 the museum's collections work focused on reinstalling the exhibition in the villa (annual report 2024).
- On 28 May 2026 Expats.cz reported that the museum had opened the garden free on Tuesdays, Thursdays and weekends; Eliška Zlatohlávková heads the museum's villa department. The museum's visit page still says the garden is closed in winter and open only on guided tours.
- Visiting: guided tours every two hours with prior reservation, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 16:00 and Thursday 12:00 to 18:00; tickets CZK 250, reduced CZK 180 (Museum of Prague).
- Visitors: 3,558 in 2024 (annual report 2024).
- The villa and the Müller Villa are members of the Iconic Houses network, which promotes them on its website (annual report 2024).

FUNDING
Villa Rothmayer: mainly public: the City of Prague through the Museum of Prague
- Owner and operator: the City of Prague owns the villa and the Museum of Prague runs it (Museum of Prague); Hlídač státu records the museum as a contributory organisation founded by a regional self-government.
- Running costs: in 2024 the museum received a non-investment contribution of CZK 227,913.4 thousand; its own revenue was CZK 23.1 million and its costs CZK 254.9 million (annual report 2024). The report does not split out the villa.
- Restoration: Czech Wikipedia puts the cost of the three-year reconstruction and restoration at CZK 23 million.
- Grants: Hlídač státu counts 109 subsidies received by the museum, worth CZK 104 million, without saying which buildings they paid for.

BOTTOM LINE
City money through one municipal museum pays for the villa, with no private sponsor. Worth watching: who pays for the 2026 garden opening and the reinstalled exhibition.
