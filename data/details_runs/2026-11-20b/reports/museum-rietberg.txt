MUSEUM RIETBERG: Zurich, Switzerland
ID: museum-rietberg
Website: https://rietberg.ch/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the arts of Asia, Africa, the Americas and Oceania at Gablerstrasse 15 in Zurich, administered by the City of Zurich; it holds some 32,600 objects and 49,000 photographs (museum website).
- Founded after Zurich voters approved in 1949 converting the Villa Wesendonck into a museum for the collection Baron Eduard von der Heydt had promised the city; it opened in 1952 as the "Museum Rietberg der Stadt Zürich" under Johannes Itten (museum website).
- Named for the Rieter family of Winterthur, who lived in the villa from 1872 to 1945 (museum website).
- Buildings: three 19th-century villas and a coach house, among them the Villa Wesendonck of 1857, plus an underground extension by Alfred Grazioli and Adolf Krischanitz, entered through the glass "Emerald" pavilion, opened on 18 February 2007 (museum website).
- Site history: Adolf Hitler spoke at the Villa Schönberg on 30 August 1923 at a fundraising meeting for the NSDAP, before the site became a museum (museum website).
- Directors: Johannes Itten, Elsy Leuzinger from 1956, Eberhard Fischer 1973 to 1998, Albert Lutz 1998 to 2019 and Annette Bhagwati since 2019 (museum website).
- Season: the special exhibitions "Amazonian Legacies" and "Red" are on now (museum website).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday to 20:00, closed Monday; special exhibitions 18 francs (14 reduced), collections 14 (12); free for under-16s, refugees with status S, N or F and KulturLegi holders (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum Rietberg: public: City of Zurich, with private sponsors and donated collections
- City budget: the 2026 budget sets the museum's spending at 18,672,400 francs against income of 4,586,600 francs, leaving the city a net cost of 14,085,800 francs; in 2024 it spent 19,480,388 francs and earned 6,711,504 (City of Zurich, budget 2026 as adopted by the city parliament).
- Investment: the budget sets 150,000 francs for cultural objects (Kulturgüter) in 2026, as in 2025; the 2024 accounts show 856,625 francs (City of Zurich, budget 2026).
- Sponsors: the museum thanks Parrotia-Stiftung, Swiss Re, Vontobel-Stiftung, Schwyzer-Stiftung, Clariant Foundation and Avina Stiftung (museum website).
- Collections: about 80 percent of the holdings came as gifts or bequests from private collectors, among them Eduard von der Heydt, Johannes Itten, Alice Boner and Thomas Schmidheiny (museum website).
- Friends: the Rietberg Society has over 4,000 members; fees run from 35 francs (25 and under) to 2,500 francs for life, and it supports collecting, exhibitions and acquisitions. Patrons can also join Rietberg & Co. or the Rietberg Circle (museum website).
- Federal money: the Benin Initiative Schweiz, which the museum co-led, was funded by the Federal Office of Culture (SRF, 20 March 2026).

Red flags: Benin works looted in 1897, ownership transferred to Nigeria in 2026
- On 20 March 2026 Zurich and Geneva signed over ownership of 28 Benin objects to Nigeria, 11 of them from the Museum Rietberg; 27 more in Basel, Neuchâtel and St. Gallen were still pending (SRF).
- British troops looted the Benin royal palace in 1897; of 96 objects the Benin Initiative Schweiz studied from 2021 to 2024, 55 came certainly or most probably from that looting (SRF).
- Nigeria's National Commission for Museums and Monuments filed the formal request in July 2024 for the state and the Kingdom of Benin (Finestre sull'Arte, 2026).
- Esther Tisa, the museum's head of provenance research, said the aim was to tell this injustice and make amends through the transfer; some objects stay in Switzerland on loan and the rest go to Nigeria (SRF).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Eduard von der Heydt, whose collection founded the museum, was a member of the NSDAP until he became a Swiss citizen in 1937 and left the party in 1939 (English Wikipedia). The museum showed "Von Buddha bis Picasso. Der Sammler Eduard von der Heydt" in 2013 (English Wikipedia).
- Avina Stiftung, one of the museum's sponsors, was founded in 1994 by Stephan Schmidheiny, who left its board in 2017 (Avina Stiftung). In April 2025 he was sentenced to nine years and six months for aggravated manslaughter over asbestos deaths at the Eternit plants he owned in Casale Monferrato. On 10 November 2026 Italy's Court of Cassation set that conviction aside, refusing the prosecutors' request to confirm it, and ordered a new appeal trial covering 66 victims (Il Post).
- Connection: von der Heydt's gift is the museum's founding collection, and Avina Stiftung is a current sponsor.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: city money with foundation and corporate support. Worth watching is the outcome of the Schmidheiny retrial.
