KUNSTHALLE BASEL: Basel, Switzerland
ID: kunsthalle-basel
Website: https://www.kunsthallebasel.ch (checked 2026-10-25; online, but the page showed only the name and two links, with no visitor information)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art exhibition space at Steinenberg 7 in central Basel, next to the city theatre and opposite the Stadtcasino concert hall (e-flux; Wikipedia).
- Run by the Basler Kunstverein (Basel Art Association), founded in 1839. The Kunsthalle opened in 1872, after an 1864 merger of the Basler Künstlergesellschaft and the Basler Kunstverein (Wikipedia).
- Mohamed Almusibli has been director and chief curator since 2024; Elena Filipovic directed it from 2014 to 2023 (Wikipedia; e-flux).
- Building: designed by Johann Jakob Stehlin-Burckhardt, with decorative work by Arnold Böcklin, Karl Brünner, Ernst Stückelberg and Charles Iguel. Renovated in 1969 to 1973 and in 2004 by Miller & Maranta, when the Swiss Architecture Museum moved into the first floor (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibition rooms, the Basler Kunstverein library of about 30,000 items and a photo archive (Wikipedia). Wikipedia says the building was paid for with revenue from two Rhine ferry services.
- Visiting: Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 11am to 6pm, Thursday 11am to 8:30pm, Saturday and Sunday 11am to 5pm (e-flux listing). No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Basel: public subsidy from Canton Basel-Stadt, with third-party grants, property rent and members (shares from the canton's 2023 model budget)
- Canton Basel-Stadt: the canton's culture funding page lists a net CHF 950,000 for the Basler Kunstverein and Kunsthalle Basel in the 2026 budget. Grants run for four years in the usual case (bs.ch). The page does not show figures for 2024, 2025 or 2027.
- Budget split: the Basel-Stadt government's proposal to the Grand Council of 13 September 2023 (Ratschlag 23.1277.01) asked for CHF 950,000 a year for 2024 to 2027. Its model budget for 2024 onward puts the canton at 32% of income, third-party funding at 30% (about CHF 660,000 a year), property income at 22% and membership dues at about 8%, with entry fees and other income at about 8% (stated in the proposal).
- The proposal's accounts list dues from members, company members and Friends, and third-party funding for exhibitions, without naming any member or donor. The association's annual reports could not be opened.
- No reporting found of fossil fuel, arms, gambling or tobacco funders.

WIDER CONTEXT
- November 2023: after Mohamed Almusibli, the director named to start in March 2024, signed two open letters on Gaza (one by more than 4,000 artists calling for a ceasefire, one in Artforum calling on institutions to end their silence), Joël Thüring, a member of the Basel cantonal parliament for the right-wing Swiss People's Party (SVP), called him an "Israel hater" on X and called on the cantonal government to withdraw funding from the Kunsthalle unless the appointment was annulled (Swissinfo, 20 and 23 November 2023).
- Neither letter first mentioned the 7 October Hamas attack; the Artforum letter was later revised to include it (Swissinfo).
- The Kunsthalle refused to reverse the appointment, and the Basler Kunstverein, which runs it, said it was shocked by "openly racist and hateful reactions". A petition backing the Kunsthalle and its incoming director had almost 2,500 signatures (Swissinfo).
- This was a demand by one party's parliamentarian, aimed at the Kunsthalle's public funder, not a decision of the cantonal government or parliament. No reporting found of a cut; the canton's 2026 budget lists CHF 950,000 for the Kunstverein and Kunsthalle (bs.ch). The director concerned leads the Kunsthalle.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kunsthalle is the Canton Basel-Stadt subsidy of CHF 950,000 a year (32% of income in the canton's 2023 model budget), third-party funding of about 30%, property rent of about 22% and member dues, and no flagged industry was found. The weak point is political pressure, not funding: an SVP parliamentarian demanded in November 2023 that the canton withdraw the subsidy over the director's open letters, and no cut is reported.
