DELFINA FOUNDATION: London, UK
ID: delfina-foundation-london
Website: https://www.delfinafoundation.com (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit arts residency in two Edwardian townhouses at 29 and 31 Catherine Place, Victoria, central London, 0.2 miles from Victoria Underground station.
- Founded in 2007 by the Spanish-born patron Delfina Entrecanales CBE, as successor to the Delfina Studio Trust (Delfina Studios, 1988 to 2006), which ran artists' studios in Stratford and then in a former chocolate factory on Bermondsey Street.
- Named for its founder, who died in 2022 aged 94; she retired from the board in 2020. Aaron Cezar is the founding director.
- A £1.4 million project by Studio Octopi (London) and Shahira Fahmy Architects (Egypt) expanded it into the adjacent 31 Catherine Place in January 2014, doubling residency places from four to eight (Wikipedia).
- Residencies of up to three months, thematic programmes such as Collecting as Practice and The Politics of Food, open calls, public events and a library.
- Most public events are free. The main entrance has four steps and no ramp, which the foundation says makes access impossible for many wheelchair users; a staff-operated platform lift links the ground floor and basement, where there is an accessible toilet.

FUNDING
Delfina Foundation: mixed: partnership fees, grants and donations, with a founder's family loan for the building
- Income for the year to 31 March 2025 was £1,441,484: partnerships £750,556, grants and donations £546,357, other income £144,571; spending was £1,279,654 (charity accounts).
- The founder gave seed money and rent-free space from 2007; that support ended in 2020, and the foundation now raises all its income (stated by the space).
- Current partners and funders on its site include the Mondriaan Fund, Samdani Art Foundation, Charles Wallace India Trust, Inlaks Shivdasani, the Rory and Elizabeth Brooks Foundation, SAHA Association, SongEun and Tate; seven regional patron networks; and galleries including David Zwirner, Lisson Gallery, Sadie Coles, Thaddeus Ropac, Lawrie Shabibi and Alserkal Avenue (partners page, undated).
- The 2024-25 accounts also name the Ministry of Culture, Taiwan (ROC), Goethe-Institut UK, British Council Pakistan, Wellcome Collection and the Finnish Institute as collaborators.
- Building: the founder owned it and it passed to her grandchildren (The Art Newspaper, 2024). In 2023 her son and trustee Charles Wansbrough lent £5.4 million at low interest to secure it and gave £600,000 to launch the £7 million "A Home for Artists" campaign (stated by the space). The 2025 accounts record the building as owned by the charity at £5,450,000, bought with a 2% loan from an unnamed trustee, with £5,613,948 outstanding.
- No reporting found of the amount paid by any single partner.

Red flags: Saudi government residency partner
- Saudi Arabia's Visual Arts Commission, set up in 2020 under the Ministry of Culture with the culture minister chairing its board (Saudipedia), ran an open call with Delfina that selected six residents in 2024-25 (charity accounts).
- On 21 January 2026 the commission announced its Intermix Residency in London with Delfina Foundation, January to March 2026, on the theme of national identity and cultural dialogue (Al Riyadh Daily).
- No reporting found of the amount the commission pays.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is obituaries of the founder and news of the building campaign.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation runs on partnership fees, trusts, galleries and patron networks, and its building now rests on a large loan from the founder's son. The weak point is a paid residency partnership with Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Culture, whose amounts are not published. Worth watching: how the £5.4 million loan is repaid and whether Gulf state partnerships grow.
