PIRELLI HANGARBICOCCA: Milan, Italy
ID: pirelli-hangarbicocca
Website: https://pirellihangarbicocca.org/en/pirelli-hangarbicocca/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit foundation for contemporary art in a former industrial building in the Bicocca district of Milan, about 15,000 square metres in three areas: the Shed, the Navate and the Cube.
- Set up in 2004 and made an official foundation in 2008; the exhibition galleries opened in their present form in 2012 (Wikipedia). Pirelli is listed as founder.
- Named for its founder and funder, the tyre maker Pirelli.
- The building recalls the Ansaldo Group, founded in 1886 by Ernesto Breda, which made railway carriages and locomotives; rails used to test locomotives are still visible in the room with the Kiefer installation (Wikipedia).
- On site: solo exhibitions by Italian and international artists, a public programme of events and discussions, publications, educational courses, a bookshop and a bistrot. The permanent display is Anselm Kiefer's "The Seven Heavenly Palaces" (2004 to 2015). Vicente Todolí has been artistic director since 2012.
- Visiting: free admission; exhibitions open Thursday to Sunday, 10:30 am to 8:30 pm, closed Monday to Wednesday. The site links to an accessibility statement but does not describe physical access.

FUNDING
Pirelli HangarBicocca: corporate-funded: Pirelli, founder and supporter
- The foundation was conceived and is supported by Pirelli (stated by the foundation). No amounts are published.
- Technical sponsors listed: Mainstreaming, M&C Saatchi Milano and K-Way. Their roles are not described.
- Board: chairman Marco Tronchetti Provera, with Maurizio Abet, Federica Barbaro, Andrea Casaluci and Ilaria Tronchetti Provera as directors; Alessandro Bianchi is general manager.
- Tronchetti Provera's consortium (Marco Polo Industrial Holding, with Camfin) is Pirelli's largest shareholder at 26.2%; Sinochem holds 20.1% after selling 14% to Lumina Crown, a vehicle of Czech businessman Michal Strnad, in July 2026 (just-auto).
- No reporting found of public funding or accounts for the foundation itself.

Red flags: Sanctions, Pirelli's Russian plants and the Rostec stake
- Rostec, a sanctioned Russian defence group, holds 25.005% of Pirelli Tyre Russia, which runs plants in Kirov and Voronezh (The Insider; European Rubber Journal).
- Pirelli and Yokohama Rubber are the only major foreign tyre makers that have kept operating in Russia since 2023; neither was legally required to leave (European Rubber Journal).
- Unverified: on 4 June 2026 Grizzly Research alleged that the Kirov plant shares territory and infrastructure with a Rostec-owned institute that makes tyres for Russian military equipment, reported by The Insider. The allegation is not confirmed by any independent investigation in the sources opened.
- Pirelli rejected the report on 4 June 2026 as untrue, said it does not produce tyres for military use and instructed lawyers to act against those spreading it (European Rubber Journal).
- In May 2014 Rosneft chief Igor Sechin bought 13% of Pirelli for 552.7 million euros (The Insider). No reporting found here of whether that stake is still held.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation is paid for by Pirelli, and the money is clean of any documented art-world controversy. The weak point is the parent company: its Russian subsidiary is 25% owned by sanctioned Rostec and is the subject of a short seller's allegation that Pirelli denies. Worth watching: any regulator or court finding on the Kirov plant, and whether the foundation publishes accounts.
