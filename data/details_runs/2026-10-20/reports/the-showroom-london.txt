THE SHOWROOM: London, United Kingdom
ID: the-showroom-london
Website: https://www.theshowroom.org (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Not-for-profit contemporary art gallery at 63 Penfold Street, London NW8 8PQ, in Marylebone near Edgware Road (museum site; Wikipedia).
- Established in 1983 at a site in Bethnal Green, East London (Wikipedia). It moved to Penfold Street in 2008 according to Wikipedia and in 2009, from Bonner Road, according to the Showroom's own history page. Founders are not named on that page.
- Directors: David Thorp (1988 to 1992), Kim Sweet (1992 to 1998), Kirsty Ogg (1998 to 2008), Emily Pethick (2008 to 2018), Elvira Dyangani Ose (2018 to 2021); Gabriela Salgado was appointed in July 2022 (Showroom history page; Wikipedia).
- Building: the Penfold Street premises were converted by the Berlin architects ifau and Jesko Fezer (Wikipedia). The Showroom's history page gives no further building details.
- What is there: a gallery with commissions, off-site projects, events, talks, screenings, performances and publications, and a public programme that includes the Imagining Futures workshops (Showroom website).
- Visiting: gallery open Wednesday to Saturday 10am to 6pm during exhibitions. The page read lists no admission prices and no accessibility information, and links to a "Plan your visit" page that was not read.

FUNDING
The Showroom: mainly public and trust-funded: Arts Council England, Paul Hamlyn Foundation and local London funders
- Income for the year to 31 March 2025 was £408,830 against expenditure of £349,095 (Open Charities); it was £266,023 in 2024.
- Arts Council England: £130,247 core grant under the National Portfolio Organisations scheme in 2025 and in 2024, the largest single grant listed (2025 accounts).
- Paul Hamlyn Foundation: £72,500 in 2024-25, year one of a £217,000 grant over 36 months (2025 accounts).
- Other named funders in 2025: John Lyon's Charity £42,000; Finnish Institute in the UK and Ireland £15,235; Westminster City Council £10,000; Westminster Foundation £10,000; FRAME Contemporary Art Finland £4,866 (2025 accounts).
- Tax relief: £79,139 in Museums and Galleries Exhibition Tax Relief (2025 accounts).
- Individuals: the accounts record a £10,000 donation from trustee Mercedes Vilardell and £10,000 from benefactors. The Supporters Scheme has Friends from £100 a year, Patrons from £1,000 and a Director's Circle at £3,000 to £5,000 (Showroom website).
- Esmée Fairbairn Foundation does not appear in the 2025 accounts. No reporting found of its grants to the Showroom.
- The trustees say costs were brought in line with income for the first time in three years; unrestricted reserves were £170,553 on the balance sheet (2025 accounts).
- Management: Gabriela Salgado is director and Scott Lawrimore managing director; Alan Higgs, chair of the board, signed the 2025 accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Showroom is an Arts Council England core grant, a three-year Paul Hamlyn Foundation grant, local London funders, tax relief and individual giving, with no flagged-industry funder found. The weak points are the small scale (income of £408,830 in 2025 after £266,023 in 2024) and dependence on a few grants. Worth watching: the later years of the Paul Hamlyn grant and the next Arts Council funding round.
