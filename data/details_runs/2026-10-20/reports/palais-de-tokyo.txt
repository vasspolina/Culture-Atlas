PALAIS DE TOKYO: Paris, France
ID: palais-de-tokyo
Website: https://palaisdetokyo.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the west wing of the Palais de Tokyo in Paris; the French state owns the west wing and the City of Paris the east wing, which houses the Musée d'Art Moderne de Paris (Wikipedia).
- The building was inaugurated on 24 May 1937 and designed by André Aubert, Marcel Dastugue and Paul Viard. Nicolas Bourriaud and Jérôme Sans launched the "Site de création contemporaine" in March 2002 (Wikipedia).
- Anne Lacaton and Jean-Philippe Vassal expanded it in 2012 to 22,000 square metres (Wikipedia). Guillaume Désanges has been director since 2022 (Wikipedia).
- It is "reputed to be the biggest non-collecting contemporary art museum in Europe" (Wikipedia). It has a bookshop, restaurants and outreach programmes (museum website).
- Visiting: open 12:00 to 22:00. Full price 13 euros, reduced 9 euros (18 to 25, students, over 60); free for under 18, job seekers, people with disabilities and a companion, and others (museum website).

FUNDING
Palais de Tokyo: mixed: French state plus corporate patrons
- The museum's footer says it receives the annual support of the French Republic, Richard Mille and Fondation Engie. No reporting found of the state grant's size; the activity reports were too large to read here.
- Season partners: Revolut and Tessi. Exhibition partners include Havaianas, Vlisco, ADAGP and the Elie Khouri Art Foundation (museum website).
- "Sustainable Palais" patrons: Richard Mille, Fondation Engie, Ruinart, Matmut pour les arts, Swiss Life and others (museum website). Benefactor members: Hopscotch, HBSS France, Peclerc Paris. Friend members: Hugo Boss, G7, Arthur D. Little.
- No reporting found of amounts for any partner.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Fondation Engie)
- Fondation Engie is an annual supporter and funds the museum's inclusive outreach (museum website).
- Engie "covers the whole gas chain"; in 2018 natural gas made up 55% of its installed capacity and the French state held 23.64% of it. Gazprom contracts made up about 20% of its gas sales and consumption in 2021 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gulf state body (Qatar 2020)
- "Notre monde brûle", a guest exhibition organised by Doha's state-run Mathaf, Arab Museum of Modern Art, was due to run from 21 February to 17 May 2020 as part of the Qatar-France 2020 Year of Culture (ArtAsiaPacific, 10 January 2020).
- Yves Michaud said the partnership aims to "bribe French society and soften its stance on human rights issues in the Persian Gulf region". The museum said on 9 January 2020 it would not cancel the show (ArtAsiaPacific).

Red flags: protest over a sponsor (La Poste, 2018)
- On 16 August 2018 about twenty striking postal workers occupied the entrance hall for half an hour, with a banner reading "La Poste finance des expos mais ferme ses bureaux !" (translation: La Poste funds exhibitions but closes its offices). La Poste called it "une opération de mécénat entre deux institutions publiques" (Carenews).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of controversy exists, but little critique of the Palais's funding. The 2024 row is a patron's objection to its politics; the 2020 row is about a Qatari guest show.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Palais de Tokyo patron row intensifies after French art world figures sign letter of support" (2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/05/14/palais-de-tokyo-patron-row-intensifies-after-french-art-world-figures-sign-letter-of-support
- Sandra Hegedüs left the Amis du Palais de Tokyo and withdrew her funding after 15 years, saying "I don't want to be associated with the new, very political direction at the Palais de Tokyo."
- More than 100 figures, among them Chris Dercon and Camille Henrot, signed an open letter defending its programming freedom.
- Criticism of the programme by a patron, not of its funding. No statement from Désanges is quoted.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: Kylie Yeung, "Artists protest Palais de Tokyo Mathaf collaboration over LGBTQ issues" (2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/artists-protest-palais-de-tokyo-mathaf-collaboration-over-lgbtq-issues
- Artists and critics objected that an LGBTQ-friendly French museum was partnering with a museum of a state that criminalises homosexuality. Names the Palais and Mathaf.

BOTTOM LINE
The Palais is funded by the French state and named corporate patrons, none with a published amount. The weak points are Fondation Engie, the 2020 Qatari guest show and the 2018 protest over La Poste's sponsorship.
