ARTER: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: arter-istanbul
Website: https://www.arter.org.tr (checked 2026-10-20; the home page loads, the English about and visit pages return 404)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in Dolapdere, Beyoğlu, a working-class Istanbul neighbourhood (Qantara), run as a non-profit by the Vehbi Koç Foundation (VKV).
- Opened on 8 May 2010 with "Starter", more than 160 works by 87 artists; it showed at Meymenet Han on Istiklal Street until 2018 and opened its Dolapdere building on 9 September 2019 (Wikipedia).
- The 18,000 square metre building is by Grimshaw with Istanbul's Turgut Alton Architects, won in a competition; it has 3,000 square metres of galleries, a sculpture terrace and a facade of convex and concave rhomboid panels (The Art Newspaper, Grimshaw).
- Inside: the 168-seat Sevgi Gönül Auditorium, the Karbon theatre for up to 332, a library, learning spaces, a bookshop and Bistro by Divan (Grimshaw, Arter).
- The collection, started in 2007, holds more than 1,300 works, about half by Turkish artists; Ömer Koç, a third-generation Koç and collector, chairs Arter, and Melih Fereli is director (The Art Newspaper).
- Entry was free for residents of the area in 2019 (Qantara); no reporting found of current hours or prices, as the site's visit pages could not be read.

FUNDING
Arter: family-foundation-funded: the Vehbi Koç Foundation, from Koç Holding wealth
- The Vehbi Koç Foundation owns and runs Arter through its Arter Kültür Sanat İktisadi İşletmesi; the home page names no sponsor (Arter).
- The building and collection cost more than €120 million, according to the Koç Foundation (The Art Newspaper, 2019).
- VKV owns 7.3% of Koç Holding; the Koç family holds a further 19.7% and the family vehicle Family Danışmanlık 43.7% (Koç Holding investor presentation, December 2025).
- Koç Holding has also sponsored the Istanbul Biennial since 2007, extended to 2036 (Habertürk, 2022).
- No reporting found of Arter's own budget or accounts.

Red flags: Koç Holding's oil refining (Tüpraş)
- Tüpraş, a Koç Holding company, holds 70% of Turkey's refining capacity, about 30 million tons, and is Europe's seventh-largest refinery (Koç Holding, 2025).
- Its revenue was TL598 billion in the first nine months of 2025 (Koç Holding).

Red flags: Koç Holding's arms maker (Otokar)
- Otokar, a listed Koç company, calls itself the "leading land systems manufacturer in Turkish defense industry", with capacity for 950 tactical armoured vehicles a year (Koç Holding, 2025).
- It had a €857 million backlog of 4x4 armoured vehicle orders at the third quarter of 2025, mostly from a Romanian joint venture to build Cobra II vehicles (Koç Holding).
- Koç's role in the Istanbul Biennial draws regular criticism over the foundation's ties to the arms sector (Qantara, 2019).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism, and none of the art itself; coverage centres on gentrification in Dolapdere and Koç's arms ties.
1. Qantara: Ulrich von Schwerin, "The Arter, art and gentrification: Modern art makeover in Istanbul?" (2019)
URL: https://qantara.de/en/node/7422
- Fereli is aware the museum is pushing up rents and property prices, but says "The neighbourhood has embraced us and loves us".
- Historian Orhan Esen says the future "is only just beginning" for some while others are shut out.
- Notes regular criticism of Koç's biennial role over the arms sector.
2. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "'Game-changer' museum to open in Istanbul in time for city's biennial" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/07/02/game-changer-museum-to-open-in-istanbul-in-time-for-citys-biennial
- Not criticism: Fereli denies that government bureaucracy targeted the Koç family over the delayed opening, after Koç Holding faced tax audits and lost a naval contract in 2013.
- Reports a 2017 attack by conservative Islamists on a show of Ömer Koç's collection held elsewhere.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013 protests targeted 13th Istanbul Biennial events over sponsor Koç Holding; at a 10 May action activists wore Koç banners and the names of gentrifying neighbourhoods, and over 100 people in the Turkish art world issued a statement criticising the curators' response (ArtInfo, republished by ArtLeaks).
- Arter was one of the main venues of the 13th and 14th Istanbul Biennials (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
Arter is paid for by the Vehbi Koç Foundation, whose wealth is a stake in Koç Holding, owner of Turkey's largest oil refiner and a maker of armoured vehicles. Both are current. Worth watching: Arter publishes no accounts of its own, and Dolapdere's rents.
