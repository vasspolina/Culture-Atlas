PALAIS DE LA PORTE DORÉE (MUSÉE NATIONAL DE L'HISTOIRE DE L'IMMIGRATION): Paris, France
ID: palais-de-la-porte-doree-musee-national-de-l-histoire-de-l-immigration
Website: https://www.palais-portedoree.fr (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- A palace at 293 avenue Daumesnil in the 12th arrondissement, at the entrance to the Bois de Vincennes, holding the national museum of the history of immigration, a tropical aquarium and the historic palace rooms (palais-portedoree.fr).
- It was built as a permanent museum of the colonies for the 1931 International Colonial Exhibition, whose stated aim was to justify colonisation to a sceptical French public; Léon Jaussely and Albert Laprade designed it, and Laprade drew the colonnade and the facade, a carved stone "tapestry" (palais-portedoree.fr).
- The director, Constance Rivière, in post since September 2022, called the task one of turning "this centre of colonial propaganda" into a place to reflect on history (Apollo).
- The museum opened quietly in 2007 and was formally inaugurated by President François Hollande in 2014 (Apollo).
- Its permanent exhibition reopened in June 2023 after three and a half years of work: chronological, built on 11 dates from 1685, with the space almost doubled and 80% of exhibits new (Apollo).
- The palace counted nearly 750,000 visitors in 2025, the museum over 274,000 and the aquarium nearly 400,000 (Paris Zigzag).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 to 17.30 on weekdays and 10 to 19 at weekends. Museum or aquarium EUR 12 (reduced EUR 9), both EUR 16; free for under-26s, jobseekers, asylum seekers and refugees, and for everyone on the first Sunday of the month. The spaces are accessible to disabled visitors (palais-portedoree.fr).

FUNDING
Palais de la Porte Dorée: public: the French state, through four ministries
- Run by a public administrative body under the joint supervision of the ministers for integration, culture, national education and research. Created in 2007 as the Cité nationale de l'histoire de l'immigration, it has also run the aquarium and the palace since 1 January 2012 (Palais de la Porte Dorée contribution to the CNCDH, 2022).
- In 2022 it held framework partnerships with France Terre d'Asile, La Cimade, Emmaüs Solidarité, the cities of Sarcelles and Montreuil, the Seine-Saint-Denis department, the foreign ministry and the interior ministry's directorate for integration and nationality (CNCDH contribution).

Red flags: 2007 resignation of eight historians over the immigration and national identity ministry
- In May 2007 Gérard Noiriel and seven colleagues resigned from the museum's scientific committee in protest at President Nicolas Sarkozy's new "ministry of immigration and national identity" (La Vie des idées).
- Noiriel said that linking immigration with national identity had always carried negative images of foreigners in France, and that staying would have meant endorsing the ministry (La Vie des idées).

Red flags: 2010 to 2011 occupation by undocumented workers
- From 7 October 2010 about 500 undocumented workers, most from sub-Saharan Africa and backed by the CGT union, occupied the museum hall to demand the regularisation promised in a June 2010 agreement with the immigration ministry (AFP via CCME).
- The management closed the building on 28 January 2011 to end the occupation and reopened it the following Tuesday. CGT spokesman Raymond Chauveau said riot police stood at the gates and the workers had little choice (AFP via CCME).
- The Ligue des droits de l'homme spoke of "government pressure" on the management and asked for the police to leave (Politis; atlas translation).

Red flags: 2023 dispute over a Louis XIV metro poster
- From 13 June 2023 a poster for the reopened museum showed Louis XIV with the words "Spanish mother, Austrian grandmother: it's crazy how many foreigners made French history" (atlas translation).
- Michel Guiniot, a Rassemblement National deputy, called it disinformation in a written question of 4 July 2023 and asked the culture minister to stop the campaign. The ministry replied on 12 March 2024 that the poster was not misleading (National Assembly).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media; coverage of the 2023 reopening is mostly favourable, with the colonial building and political exposure as the recurring concerns.
1. Apollo: Valeria Costa-Kostritsky, "The invention of Frenchness" (2023)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/france-immigration-museum-paris-porte-de-la-doree/
- The museum's fortunes have always depended on the government of the day, from a quiet 2007 opening under Sarkozy to a delayed inauguration under Hollande.
- It is still housed in a building haunted by France's colonial past; the work of putting the palace in context was unfinished.
- Former director Pap Ndiaye was made education minister and then dropped by Emmanuel Macron, which some saw as a move to appease the far right; the museum will be left documenting harsher immigration laws.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money from the French state, with no private sponsor on record. The weak points are political: a museum founded in a colonial propaganda building and exposed to each government's immigration policy.
