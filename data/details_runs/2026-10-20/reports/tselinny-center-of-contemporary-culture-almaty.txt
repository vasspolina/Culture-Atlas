TSELINNY CENTER OF CONTEMPORARY CULTURE: Almaty, Kazakhstan
ID: tselinny-center-of-contemporary-culture-almaty
Website: https://tselinny.org/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the About, Visit and Partnership pages carried mostly navigation text)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre in a rebuilt Soviet cinema in Almaty, Kazakhstan.
- Founded by businessman Kairat Boranbayev, who bought the former cinema in 2015 (Orda; Kunstkritikk dates the purchase to 2016). It opened to the public in September 2025 after a seven-year reconstruction (Vlast). It is directed by Jamilya Nurkalieva; the artistic director is Boranbayev's daughter, Alima Kairat (Kunstkritikk).
- The cinema opened in 1964 with about 1,500 seats, later became a hookah lounge and nightclub, and closed in 2013 (Orda, Vlast).
- Architect Asif Khan led the redesign with Zaure Aitayeva; the sgraffito by Yevgeniy Sidorkin was reinstalled in fragments (Brooklyn Rail).
- There is no permanent collection; the building holds exhibitions, performances, film programmes and courses (Tselinny website).
- Visiting: exhibitions are free for people with disabilities, WWII veterans, children under 7 and CIMAM members (Tselinny website). Opening days, hours and prices were not found.

FUNDING
Tselinny Center: self-funded: the founder's money, from a fortune built in oil and gas, real estate, retail and pharmaceuticals
- The project was fully funded by Boranbayev, who declined to discuss finances (Orda).
- Boranbayev's fortune comes from investments in "oil and gas, real estate, retail and pharmaceuticals" (The Art Newspaper). The Art Newspaper describes the centre as "financed by Kazakh entrepreneurs".
- The centre runs a Patrons' Club with 25 named patrons; the page gives no dates, amounts or use of funds (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of a state funder or a corporate sponsor of the centre.

Red flags: founder's conviction and asset handover
- Oil and gas: the founder's fortune includes oil and gas (The Art Newspaper). No page read gives the share of oil and gas in his group's revenue, so no source shows it as the largest line.
- Conviction: in March 2023 Boranbayev was sentenced to eight years for aiding embezzlement of state property tied to a 14.5 billion tenge purchase of liquefied petroleum gas through the Aziyagazchunja company (Orda, 2 January 2024). Orda does not name the court.
- Appeal: the sentence was cut to six years in September 2023, the court replaced the unserved part with restricted freedom in October and he was released in November (Orda).
- Assets handed over: on 14 September 2023 he returned assets worth 90 billion tenge to the state: an oil field, a gas distribution company, a service company, hotel and shopping complexes, a fitness chain and office space. On 4 October 2023 shareholders agreed to give the state 100% of JSC Phystech II, which holds the North Karazhanbas oil field, and the shares passed to the State Property and Privatization Committee (Orda, 13 October 2023).
- Payments: Orda also says he paid 14.5 billion tenge in compensation, transferred 66 million dollars to the Education Infrastructure Support Fund and returned 140.7 billion tenge in total.
- Boranbayev told the court the charges stemmed from his ties to the Nazarbayev family (Orda). The Art Newspaper quotes director Nurkalieva: "The situation has been fully resolved, and currently there are no implications for the museum and its funding."

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism. Most coverage is admiring or a partner piece; one essay and one feature raise the founder's record.
1. Kunstkritikk: Anders Kreuger, "Can the Subaltern Build a Museum?" (2025)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/can-the-subaltern-build-a-museum/
- Presents Tselinny as an alternative to the Alma museum.
- Calls Boranbayev "another Kazakh oligarch" jailed on "charges of embezzlement in relation to gas sales" after the January 2022 unrest.
2. Trebuchet Magazine: Veronica Simpson, "Kazakhstan: Close Encounters With Central Asian Culture, Contemporary Art And Post-Soviet Capitalism" (2025)
URL: https://www.trebuchet-magazine.com/?p=529496
- Says his fortune "comes from oil and gas, sport, pharmaceuticals and real estate" and reports his 2023 sentence, citing The Art Newspaper.
- Notes Tselinny has "no collection to show off or leverage". Descriptive, not a case against the centre.
3. The Art Newspaper: Tom Seymour, feature on new Kazakh art venues (2025)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2025/04/30/as-kazakhstan-cautiously-strengthens-ties-with-western-europe-new-art-venues-herald-a-change-of-direction
- Reports the conviction, that he was released on probation after handing over part of his assets, and the director's reply. No criticism of the centre itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one man's: Boranbayev's fortune includes oil and gas, though no page gives its share, and he was convicted in 2023 of aiding embezzlement and handed the state assets worth 90 billion tenge, 140.7 billion in total by Orda's count. The centre has no state or corporate funder on record. Worth watching: how the Patrons' Club money is used, and whether the centre publishes accounts or a governing board; neither was found.
