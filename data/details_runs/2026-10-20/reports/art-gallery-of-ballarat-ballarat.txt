ART GALLERY OF BALLARAT: Ballarat, Australia
ID: art-gallery-of-ballarat-ballarat
Website: https://www.artgalleryofballarat.com.au/explore/about-us/the-organisation (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art gallery at 40 Lydiard Street North, Ballarat, Victoria, on Wadawurrung Country. The gallery website says the building is closed for building upgrades, including the gallery shop, and directs visitors to Backspace Gallery at 43 Mair Street.
- Opened on 11 September 1884 as the Ballarat Fine Art Gallery in rented space at the Ballarat Academy of Music, opened by Governor Henry Loch. The benefactor James Oddie is described as its "patron and founder", and a public association was formed in 1886 (Wikipedia). The word "fine" was dropped from the name in 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Renaissance Revival, designed by Tappin, Gilbert and Dennehy, built from 1887 to 1890 and opened by Alfred Deakin in 1890; extended in 1927, 1987 and 2001 and restored in 2009 and 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Ownership: privately owned until financial difficulties led to the building and collection passing to Ballarat City Council in the late 1970s (Wikipedia).
- Collection: over 11,200 works as of November 2022, with a focus on Australian art; the Eureka Flag has been in the collection since 1895 and is on long-term loan to the Eureka Centre (Wikipedia). The City of Ballarat's 2025 notice says more than 11,500 works.
- Closure: the whole gallery closes from Monday 3 March (2025) and was expected to reopen in early 2026 (City of Ballarat notice, 24 Jan 2025).
- Visiting: free entry since 2008 (Wikipedia). 113,734 visitors in 2022/23, up 33% on the year before; almost 190,000 in 2018/19 (City of Ballarat, 31 Aug 2023).

FUNDING
Art Gallery of Ballarat: mainly public: City of Ballarat, with Victorian Government grants and charitable foundations
- Owner and operator: the City of Ballarat owns the building and collection (Wikipedia). It funds a 5.5 million dollar project for a new heating, ventilation and air-conditioning system, plus lift refurbishment, in the 2025 works (City of Ballarat).
- Victorian Government: 536,707 dollars through the Regional Collections Access Program for LED skylights, and 388,850 dollars announced in 2024 to complete a master plan (City of Ballarat). The state also sanctioned 2,000 pounds for purchases in 1886 and shared a 1.85 million dollar restoration with the council in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Other grants: a 50,000 dollar Department of Transport Flexible Local Transport Program grant for regional and remote school groups, and a grant from the Sir Wilfred and C H (Roger) Brookes Charitable Foundation through the Perpetual Impact grants for the Baskets of Knowledge programme (City of Ballarat, 31 Aug 2023).
- The gallery site shows City of Ballarat and Creative Victoria logos in its footer.
- Acquisition funds are kept separate from operating funds and both are reported publicly (stated by gallery director Louise Tegart, The Courier, 25 Jan 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is in local press: a 2022 opinion exchange and a 2017 letter, both about management.
1. The Courier (local press): Louise Tegart, gallery director, "OPINION: In defence of the Art Gallery" (25 January 2022)
URL: https://www.thecourier.com.au/story/7588434/opinion-in-defence-of-the-art-gallery
- A reply to an earlier opinion piece, "Diversification of art collection too costly", published on 4 January. Tegart answers its claims that works were "mothballed", that works would be sold off, and that audiences were declining before COVID-19.
- Tegart wrote "We have not 'mothballed' artworks", saying some long-displayed works were taken down for conservation before upcoming exhibitions; that acquisition and deaccessioning follow policies and approvals; that more than 100 works go out on loan each year; and that surveys show much of the audience is first-time visitors. These are statements by the gallery.
2. The Courier (local press): Louis Alexander, reader letter, "Gallery director is a critical role for the future" (3 November 2017)
URL: https://www.thecourier.com.au/story/5031929/gallery-director-is-a-critical-role-for-the-future/
- Criticises the recruitment of a new director after Gordon Morrison's retirement because the post had not been advertised, and argues that the gallery is too closely tied to the current director's taste for medieval music and gothic themes.
- A reader's letter, not an art critic's piece.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public: the City of Ballarat as owner, with Victorian Government grants and a few charitable foundations; none of the named funders is flagged. The weak points are the management criticism in local press and the closure for upgrades. Worth watching are the city-funded works and the state-funded master plan.
