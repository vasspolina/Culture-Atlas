MOMA (THE MUSEUM OF MODERN ART): New York, USA
ID: moma-the-museum-of-modern-art
Website: https://www.moma.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum on West 53rd Street in Midtown Manhattan (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 7 November 1929; moved in 1939 to its building by Philip L. Goodwin and Edward Durell Stone, expanded in 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Christophe Cherix is director (Wikipedia), after Glenn Lowry (Form 990).
- Adult admission is $30; free for New York State residents on Friday evenings (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
MoMA: private: gifts, sales of assets, investment returns and admissions
- Fiscal year to June 2025: $362.0 million revenue, including $147.8 million (41%) sales of assets and $96.1 million (27%) contributions; $2.31 billion net assets (Form 990, ProPublica).
- MoMA "eschews government funding" (Wikipedia).
- David Rockefeller gave $77 million toward the 2002 to 2004 expansion and pledged $100 million to the endowment in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- A performance and installation gallery bears the names of Marie-Josée and Henry Kravis (The Art Newspaper, 2024).

Red flags: Leon Black, trustee who paid Jeffrey Epstein from 2012 to 2017
- Black, former MoMA chair, remains a trustee; he paid Epstein at least $158 million in 2012 to 2017 for purported tax and estate planning (The Art Newspaper, 19 August 2025).
- Senator Ron Wyden put the total at $170 million, said Black has given no "credible explanation" and cited Black's $62 million settlement with the US Virgin Islands "for all issues related to Jeffrey Epstein" (CBS News, 4 June 2026).
- Black's spokesperson cites an independent review that found no wrongdoing; MoMA did not respond (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: sexual abuse allegations against Black in files released in early 2026 (Hyperallergic newsletter); Black denies them.

Red flags: Glenn Dubin, trustee subpoenaed over Epstein transactions in 2020
- The Art Newspaper (2025) calls trustee Dubin "another billionaire client of Epstein's".
- In September 2020 the US Virgin Islands subpoenaed Dubin, his wife and his former fund Highbridge Capital for records of financial dealings with Epstein, including three wire transfers in 2014 to 2016; the Dubins said they were "completely unaware" of Epstein's conduct (ABC News, 9 September 2020).
- Unverified: Virginia Giuffre testified in 2016 that Epstein directed her to have sex with Dubin; he denies it (ABC News).

Red flags: 2024 Gaza sit-in naming trustees
- 10 February 2024: at least 500 protesters occupied the atrium after handing out brochures reading "MoMA trustees fund genocide, apartheid, settler colonialism"; they named Paula Crown, over General Dynamics, and honorary chair Ronald Lauder (The Nation, partisan, written by participants).
- Paula H. Crown is a trustee who gave $100,000 to $149,999 in 2023-24 (MoMA 2024 Annual Report); her late husband James Crown was lead director and a major shareholder of the defence company General Dynamics (New York Times obituary on Artdaily, 2023).

Red flags: 2024 climate protest against board chair Marie-Josée Kravis
- 14 February 2024: climate groups unfurled a "MoMA: Drop KKR" banner in the atrium, naming the private equity firm her husband Henry Kravis co-founded, and called for her removal over the couple's fossil fuel ties (The Art Newspaper).

WIDER CONTEXT
- January 2020: 37 artists in a Gulf Wars show asked MoMA to separate itself from trustees tied to private prison companies (Hyperallergic, standfirst only).
- From 9 April 2021 the ten-week Strike MoMA targeted trustees Black, Steven Tananbaum, Dubin, Steven Cohen and Larry Fink (The Art Newspaper, March 2021).
- March 2021: after an open letter by more than 150 artists, Black did not stand for re-election as chair (Hyperallergic newsletter; The Art Newspaper).
- Honorary chair Ronald Lauder appears more than 900 times in Epstein files released in January 2026, mostly assistants' emails arranging 2017 meetings (ARTnews, February 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
Private money; the weak point is the board, with two Epstein-linked trustees and others targeted over fossil fuels and arms. Watch Black's and Dubin's seats.
