VANUATU CULTURAL CENTRE (VANUATU KALJORAL SENTA): Port Vila, Vanuatu
ID: vanuatu-cultural-centre-vanuatu-kaljoral-senta-port-vila
Website: https://vanuatuculturalcentre.gov.vu/

OVERVIEW
- National cultural institution of Vanuatu in the capital, Port Vila; it describes itself as a statutory body whose role covers preserving, protecting and promoting Vanuatu's cultural heritage (centre's website).
- Wikipedia gives its founding as 1955 and describes it as an umbrella organisation with the National Museum, a National Film and Sound Unit, the National Archives, the National Library and Public Library, a Fieldworkers' Unit, the Tafea Cultural Centre in Lenakel (Tanna) and the Malekula Cultural Centre.
- The Oral Traditions Collection Project began in 1976 (Wikipedia).
- The National Museum has a shop in its entrance hall selling gifts, souvenirs, books and carvings; shop revenue is used to develop the museum's collection (centre's website).
- The centre publishes a monthly newsletter, Tamtam (centre's website).
- A magnitude 7.3 earthquake struck near Port Vila in December 2024. UNESCO states the centre, which houses the National Museum, National Library and Archives and the Public Library, "sustained significant structural impacts that compromised essential services and placed irreplaceable collections at risk" (UNESCO).
- Curator Kaitip Kami said 13 display cases were destroyed and objects fell from their cases; items lost included thousands-of-years-old Lapita pottery, described as smashed and unsalvageable, while ancestral remains on lower shelves survived (AAP via The Canberra Times, Ben McKay, 20 May 2025).
- Local custom requires that objects be repaired by the tribes they came from, which means flying people in once funds are available (AAP via The Canberra Times).

FUNDING
Vanuatu Cultural Centre: Vanuatu government funds the curator's post; earthquake recovery aided by UNESCO's Heritage Emergency Fund
- The curator's position is government-funded and there is no extra money for upkeep of the collection (AAP via The Canberra Times, 20 May 2025).
- UNESCO Heritage Emergency Fund: finances a rapid damage assessment, an updated inventory of affected collections and restoration of objects and archival materials at the centre, running until May 2026 (UNESCO, published 11 December 2025).
- UNESCO thanks the donors to its Heritage Emergency Fund: the Qatar Fund for Development, Norway, Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Monaco, Estonia, ANA Holdings Inc, the Netherlands, Slovakia, Luxembourg, Andorra, Poland, Lithuania and Serbia. These are the donors to UNESCO's fund; UNESCO states the centre's work is funded "through funding from the UNESCO Heritage Emergency Fund (HEF)".
- The Australian Museum scoped the museum's needs; its outreach was funded through the US Embassy's "Pasifika Tahui" project, whose future funding is uncertain (AAP via The Canberra Times, 20 May 2025).
- The centre's website links an appeal titled "National Museum GoFundMe - Earthquake Aftermath" to an external site.

BOTTOM LINE
The Vanuatu government funds the curator's post. After the December 2024 earthquake, UNESCO's Heritage Emergency Fund finances assessment and restoration at the centre, and a US Embassy project funded the Australian Museum's outreach.
