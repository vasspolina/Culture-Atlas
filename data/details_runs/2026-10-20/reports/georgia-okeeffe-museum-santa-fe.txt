GEORGIA O'KEEFFE MUSEUM: Santa Fe, USA
ID: georgia-okeeffe-museum-santa-fe
Website: https://www.okeeffemuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Private non-profit museum devoted to Georgia O'Keeffe, with galleries at 217 Johnston Street in Santa Fe, a Welcome Center in Abiquiú and two historic O'Keeffe homes in northern New Mexico (museum website; Albuquerque Journal).
- Founded in November 1995 by Anne Windfohr Marion and John L. Marion, part-time Santa Fe residents; opened on 17 July 1997 (Wikipedia).
- The main building was designed by Richard Gluckman with Allegretti Architects (Wikipedia).
- The museum took over care of O'Keeffe's Abiquiú home and studio in 2006; its Research Center opened in July 2001 (museum website).
- Director: Cody Hartley, who joined in 2013; Robert Kret directed from 2009 to early 2019 (Wikipedia; board and staff page).
- The collection holds 140 O'Keeffe oil paintings (museum website) and nearly 1,200 objects (Wikipedia).
- A $75 million, 54,000-square-foot new campus at 123 Grant Avenue had a topping-off ceremony scheduled for 6 April 2026, with the opening expected in 2028 (museum press release, 31 March 2026; Albuquerque Journal, John Miller, 11 August 2025).
- Visiting: Santa Fe galleries are open daily 10 AM to 5 PM; the Abiquiú Welcome Center is open Tuesday to Sunday 8 AM to 4 PM.

FUNDING
Georgia O'Keeffe Museum: private philanthropy and earned income: a family foundation started it; admissions, retail and an endowment sustain it
- 2025 revenue and support was $28,299,420 against expenses of $12,377,584; net assets stood at $225,756,044 (audited statements, 2025).
- Within that: contributions, donations and grants $5,983,298; admissions $2,922,769; retail $1,584,872 (net of cost of sales); tours $876,525; memberships $363,449; endowment support for current activities $1,910,979; investment income $14,275,280.
- Founding gift: Anne Marion founded the museum with a $10 million gift from the Burnett Foundation, which she led (Wikipedia, Anne Windfohr Marion).
- The museum's tribute to John L. Marion (4 June 2026) describes "a $25 million gift from The Burnett Foundation, Anne's family philanthropic organization", which the Marions secured to help the museum prepare for expansion.
- The Burnett Foundation was formerly the Anne Burnett Tandy and Charles Tandy Foundation and is based in Fort Worth (TCU, 2022). Wikipedia says Marion inherited four West Texas ranches including the 6666 Ranch and established Burnett Oil Company in 1980, serving as its chairman of the board.
- New campus: 96% of the $75 million is raised from 114 individuals and organisations, including a $750,000 National Endowment for the Humanities matching challenge grant and support from the Frankenthaler Climate Initiative (museum press release, 31 March 2026). The Albuquerque Journal reported about 91% came from 98 individual donors.
- The 2014 sale of O'Keeffe's "Jimson Weed" at auction for $44.4 million funded an acquisition fund (museum tribute).
- The museum's trustees number 30, chaired by David Warnock; the founders are listed as Anne W. Marion and John L. Marion (board and staff page).
- Corporate membership levels run from $500 to $5,000 and above (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private philanthropy, led by the Marions' Burnett Foundation (a $10 million founding gift and a later $25 million), together with admissions, retail, tours, an endowment and a donor campaign for the new campus. Audited statements for 2025 are posted on the museum's site.
