DHAKA ART SUMMIT: Dhaka, Bangladesh
ID: dhaka-art-summit-dhaka
Website: https://www.samdani.com.bd/dhaka-art-summit (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-commercial, biennial exhibition and research platform for art and architecture related to South Asia, held at the Bangladesh Shilpakala Academy in Dhaka.
- Produced by the Samdani Art Foundation, set up in 2011 by Nadia Samdani and Rajeeb Samdani (Wikipedia); first held in April 2012, then in 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Diana Campbell Betancourt has been artistic director or chief curator since 2014; the sixth edition, "Bonna" (3 to 11 February 2023), drew more than 500,000 visitors (Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions, talks, the Samdani Art Award for artists based in Bangladesh, research projects and commissions. The foundation also runs Srihatta, an art centre and sculpture park in Sylhet.
- No seventh edition had opened by October 2026; ArtReview reported in 2024 that it would open in 2026, and the foundation's October 2026 programme announcement names no summit dates (e-flux).
- Free and ticketless.

FUNDING
Dhaka Art Summit: private: the Samdani family's foundation, with sponsorship from their Golden Harvest group
- The Samdani Art Foundation pays for the summit; the Ministry of Cultural Affairs is named as collaborator and the Shilpakala Academy as venue (summit page).
- Rajeeb Samdani is managing director of Golden Harvest Group, a Dhaka conglomerate with agro, food, IT and logistics companies, which sponsors the summit (Wikipedia).
- Golden Harvest's finance chief said its sponsorship was branding, that no company money went to organise the summit, and that some paid for banners (Bonik Barta, 2024).
- Funders named for foundation projects include the Getty Foundation (Photosynthesis fellows) and Pro Helvetia (Samdani Art Award 2026 at Kunsthalle Basel) (e-flux, 2026).
- No reporting found of the summit's budget or of amounts from any funder.

Red flags: founder's company under regulator investigation
- Bonik Barta reported on 24 September 2024 that the Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission was investigating whether Golden Harvest Agro Industries spent its 2019 rights-issue money as intended; the company's finance chief said the probe was ongoing.
- Golden Harvest raised BDT 340 million of a BDT 500 million bond in 2017 and has not repaid investors; trustee Green Delta Capital said five institutions are owed BDT 392.9 million (Bonik Barta).
- In 2020 the regulator scrutinised the company's interest-free loans of 3.79 billion taka to sister companies (Wikipedia).
- The Department of Environment fined Rajeeb Samdani BDT 5.017 million in February 2024 over cutting soil from a hill to fill land for a resort project in Sylhet; the article says Samdani could not be reached (Bonik Barta).

Red flags: reported closeness to the Hasina family
- Bonik Barta wrote that the Samdanis came closer to the Sheikh family at a 2020 centenary exhibition of Bangabandhu's photographs, attended by Sheikh Hasina, Sheikh Rehana and Saima Wazed Putul; in 2021 Rajeeb Samdani jointly organised an exhibition with the Centre for Research and Information (Bonik Barta, 24 September 2024).
- Unverified: that artworks Samdani supplied were among those stolen from the home of Sheikh Rehana after 5 August 2024, and that Golden Harvest money was spent on the foundation, from Bonik Barta, citing unnamed sources.
- The foundation called the report false and motivated, said Samdani has never dealt in art and was not in the country on 5 August, and said the paper had made a commercial proposal to it during the 2023 summit (Bonik Barta, 2 October 2024, my summary of the Bengali text).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Mostly admiring coverage; criticism is limited to a few points in reviews, about government ties and curation, not about the money.
1. New Critique: Jonathan Webster, "[Review] Dhaka Art Summit 2020" (2020)
URL: https://newcritique.co.uk/2020/07/06/review-dhaka-art-summit-2020-jonathan-webster/
- Argues the summit's close links with the government, through the culture ministry and the Shilpakala Academy, make its claim to be "censorship free" "not entirely true", citing a Hill Artists' Group speaker who could not answer a question near military security.
- Faults Raphael Hefti's smoke performance for contradicting the summit's sustainability principles, calling it "wilful or blissful ignorance".
2. e-flux Criticism: Natasha Ginwala, "Dispatch: Dhaka Art Summit" (2014)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/235599/dispatch-dhaka-art-summit
- Mostly positive; notes the summit runs on private funding without examining it.
- Criticises the talks, where most panelists "sped through dry presentations on acquisition policy".

BOTTOM LINE
The summit is the private project of one industrial family, held at the Shilpakala Academy with the culture ministry as partner. The weak points are the regulator's probe and unpaid bonds at the family's Golden Harvest company, and press claims of closeness to the Hasina family, which the foundation denies. Worth watching: the outcome of the securities probe and whether the summit returns.
