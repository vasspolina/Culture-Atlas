MUSEO SOUMAYA: Mexico City, Mexico
ID: museo-soumaya
Website: https://www.museosoumaya.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private non-profit art museum with a main building at Plaza Carso in the Nuevo Polanco area and a first building at Plaza Loreto in San Ángel, Mexico City (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Carlos Slim; the first building at Plaza Loreto opened in 1994 on the site of a former paper mill, and the Plaza Carso building was inaugurated in February 2011 and opened to the public that March (Wikipedia).
- Named for Soumaya Domit, Slim's wife, who died in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- The Plaza Carso building was designed by Fernando Romero, Slim's son-in-law: six storeys clad in hexagonal aluminium tiles, at a cost of about $70 million, inside a development that cost almost $800 million (Wikipedia). Architectural Record also gave the museum cost as roughly $70 million.
- Holds more than 66,000 works, among them pre-Hispanic sculpture, Mexican art of the 19th and 20th centuries, European old masters and a large set of Rodin casts (Wikipedia).
- The museum lists four sites, all with free admission: Plaza Carso (daily, 10:30 to 18:30), Plaza Loreto (Wednesday to Monday), Atrio de San Francisco and Casa Guillermo Tovar de Teresa. The site gives no accessibility information.

FUNDING
Museo Soumaya: self-funded: Carlos Slim's fortune, through Fundación Carlos Slim
- The museum states that it is run by the Fundación Carlos Slim.
- Wikipedia states that admission is free and operating costs are covered by Slim's fortune.
- Expansión (2013) reports that Slim buys and assembles the collection himself.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, government grant, board or annual accounts for the museum.

Red flags: Name to know: Carlos Slim, telecoms dominance
- On 6 March 2014 the Instituto Federal de Telecomunicaciones (IFT), Mexico's telecoms regulator, determined that América Móvil's companies Telmex, Telnor and Radiomóvil Dipsa (Telcel) are the "preponderant economic agent" in telecommunications (Forbes México).
- On 8 March 2017 the IFT issued a further resolution on the same units, ordering Telmex-Telnor to create a legally independent entity for local loop and shared passive infrastructure services. América Móvil said it would challenge it (TelecomTV).

Red flags: Name to know: Carlos Slim, Metro Line 12
- CICSA, the construction firm of Slim's Grupo Carso, built the elevated section of Mexico City's Metro Line 12 in a consortium with ICA and Alstom and delivered it in 2013. The elevated section collapsed on 3 May 2021, killing 26 people. A city-commissioned report by DNV found two beams had buckled because bolts were missing over a significant length, and also found poor welds (Bloomberg Línea).
- Grupo Carso stated that CICSA "did not cause and is not responsible for" the collapse. CICSA nonetheless agreed with the city government to repair the collapsed section and reinforce the structure it built, and to contribute to a supplementary compensation fund for victims (Bloomberg Línea).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism exists but is mostly from the 2011 opening and aimed at the building and the collection's quality, not at Slim's funding. No piece found examines the 2014 or 2021 matters in relation to the museum.
1. Architectural Record: unsigned post, "Carlos Slim's Soumaya Museum Dazzles and Disappoints in Mexico City" (2011)
URL: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/blogs/1-off-the-record/post/1899-carlos-slim-s-soumaya-museum-dazzles-and-disappoints-in-mexico-city
- Summarises two reviews. Benjamin Genocchio (Artinfo) called the design derivative, saying it borrows from Frank Lloyd Wright's Guggenheim interior and Frank Gehry's Bilbao exterior, and that Romero "didn't get the contract on merit".
- Nicholas Casey (Wall Street Journal Magazine) credits the building to Slim's wealth and collection, Mexico City's rise as an art destination and Romero's ambition.
- Criticises the architecture and the commission, not the funding.
2. Expansión: unsigned, "Arte sin medida" (2013)
URL: https://expansion.mx/expansion/2013/11/22/arte-sin-medida
- Art critics Teresa del Conde and Raquel Tibol say Slim buys pieces at auction as a single lot, which prevents care over each purchase. Georgina Cebey, a museology teacher, calls the display eclectic and excessive.
- Museum director Alfonso Miranda defends the collection and the museum's honesty with the public.
- Criticises the collection and the display, not the funders. Business magazine rather than art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is Slim's own: paid for by his foundation, free to enter, with no published accounts, funder list or board. The weak points are the owner's documented record, the 2014 telecoms dominance ruling and the Metro Line 12 collapse that his construction arm agreed to repair, and the lack of any independent oversight of the museum. Worth watching: whether the foundation ever publishes accounts.
