PORTLAND MUSEUM OF ART: Portland, USA
ID: portland-museum-of-art-portland-maine
Website: https://www.portlandmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 7 Congress Square in Portland, Maine, with a public car park at 120 Free Street (museum site).
- Founded in 1882 as the Portland Society of Art; Wikipedia calls it the largest and oldest public art institution in Maine.
- The Charles Shipman Payson Building, designed by Henry Nichols Cobb of I. M. Pei & Partners, opened in 1983 (Wikipedia).
- More than 22,000 artworks from the 18th century to the present, including Homer, Monet and Picasso (Wikipedia).
- Mark Bessire is executive director (ProPublica).
- A planned expansion at 142 Free Street, an 1830 building the museum bought in October 2019, is part of a $100 million capital campaign (Portland Press Herald, 5 March 2025). The museum's Blueprint page links to LEVER Architecture's concept for the site. Wikipedia states the museum demolished the building in September 2025.
- Visiting: the museum's site lists Tuesday to Thursday and weekends 10:00 to 18:00, Friday 10:00 to 20:00, closed Monday; adults $20, seniors and students 22 and over $18, visitors 21 and under free. Entry is free on Fridays 16:00 to 20:00 and on the third Thursday of each month; SNAP/EBT cardholders also enter free.

FUNDING
Portland Museum of Art: contribution-led: foundations and corporate sponsors, with a Maine Arts Commission grant
- Finances: in the fiscal year ending January 2025 the museum reported revenue of $14,577,792, expenses of $11,834,623 and contributions of $8,133,829, 55.8 percent of revenue (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, filing of 15 December 2025).
- Art for All, the museum's free and low-cost access programme, lists foundation support from the Avangrid CMP Foundation, Elmina B. Sewall Foundation, KHR McNeely Family Foundation, Horizon Foundation Inc., Franklin P. and Arthur W. Perdue Foundation, Morton-Kelly Charitable Trust, New Hampshire Charitable Foundation, P.W. Sprague Memorial Foundation, Roy A. Hunt Foundation, William J. J. Gordon Family Foundation and John Marin Foundation (museum site).
- Corporate sponsors listed there: Allagash Brewing Company, Bissell Brothers Brewing, CPRC Group, KeyBank, Maine Health, Northern Light Mercy Hospital, Systems Engineering, TD Charitable Foundation, WEX and WR Wright-Ryan (museum site).
- Public money: Art for All is funded in part by a grant from the Maine Arts Commission, an independent state agency supported by the National Endowment for the Arts (museum site).
- Capital campaign: the $100 million campaign for the expansion had raised $48.5 million by March 2025 (Portland Press Herald).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The documented criticism concerns the demolition of 142 Free Street for the museum's expansion, raised by a preservation group and reported by local press, not by art publications.
1. Greater Portland Landmarks: Alison Frazee, Director of Advocacy, "The Column Advocacy News" (17 April 2025, advocacy source)
URL: https://www.portlandlandmarks.org/columnblog/april-17-2025
- The group decided not to appeal the judge's ruling on 142 Free Street, though it disagreed with the outcome.
- It had asked the museum to document the building, salvage reusable materials, create exhibits about the site and avoid demolition until the new project was funded and approved. It states the museum had committed to this in earlier Planning Board documents.
- It states the museum previously demolished another building on its campus for a project that never happened, and that site is still empty.
2. Portland Press Herald: Megan Gray, "Dispute over Portland Museum of Art expansion now in judge's hands" (5 March 2025, local press)
URL: https://www.pressherald.com/2025/03/05/dispute-over-portland-museum-of-art-expansion-goes-to-court/
- Greater Portland Landmarks sued the City of Portland in June 2024 and named the museum as a party in interest. The council had voted 6 to 3 to reclassify the building as non-contributing, although the city's historic preservation and planning boards had recommended keeping its classification.
- The group's director called the vote an abuse of discretion; the museum's attorney, Mary Costigan, said the council was not bound by the boards' recommendations. Wikipedia reports that Justice Deborah Cashman upheld the reclassification in March 2025.

WIDER CONTEXT
- TD Bank N.A. pleaded guilty on 10 October 2024 to conspiring to fail to maintain an anti-money laundering programme, fail to file accurate currency transaction reports and launder monetary instruments, in a combined $1.8 billion resolution (US Department of Justice).
- Connection: the museum's Art for All page lists the TD Charitable Foundation among its corporate sponsors. A 21 October 2009 article on vermontbiz.com, "TD Bank supports area organizations with $29,000 through charitable foundation", says the foundation is the charitable giving arm of TD Bank N.A.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is contributions, 55.8 percent of revenue in the year to January 2025, plus a Maine Arts Commission grant for the Art for All programme. Named funders include the Avangrid CMP Foundation, KeyBank, Allagash and the TD Charitable Foundation.
