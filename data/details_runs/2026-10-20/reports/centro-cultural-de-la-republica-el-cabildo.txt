CENTRO CULTURAL DE LA REPUBLICA EL CABILDO: Asunción, Paraguay
ID: centro-cultural-de-la-republica-el-cabildo
Website: not checked: no official site could be reached (a guessed government address did not resolve on 2026-10-24); facts below come from Wikipedia and Paraguayan press

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre of the Paraguayan National Congress in the former Cabildo (town council) building on Avenida de la República, Asunción (Última Hora, 2021).
- Opened on 14 May 2004 (English Wikipedia says 14 March) at the initiative of Carlos Mateo Balmelli, then president of Congress, with Chilean president Ricardo Lagos and the writer Augusto Roa Bastos present; Margarita Morselli was director general (ABC Color, 2019). Morselli led it for 20 years until Aníbal Saucedo Rodas replaced her in July 2024 (La Nación).
- The building's refit and current design date from the government of Carlos Antonio López, with the Spanish architect Pascual Urdapilleta linked to the design; Spanish Wikipedia gives 1857 as the inauguration. It housed the executive until 1894 and Congress until 2003 (Spanish and English Wikipedia).
- On site: the Sala del Barro, the Sala de Arte Sacro, museum rooms for the Cabildo, music and film, the Augusto Roa Bastos library and two temporary exhibition rooms (Spanish Wikipedia).
- A fire on the night before 25 December 2020 destroyed the Congress Symphony Orchestra's rehearsal hall and other rooms in the newer block; the heritage building and museum halls were not affected (Última Hora, 25 December 2020). It reopened to the public in May 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: in February 2021 the ground-floor spaces were free and the temporary exhibition rooms were open Monday to Friday 8am to 1pm (Última Hora). No current hours were found.

FUNDING
Centro Cultural de la República El Cabildo: public: the Paraguayan National Congress's budget
- It depends on the National Congress (Última Hora, 2021). Its director Saucedo Rodas said in July 2024 that its budget was 31,000 million guaraníes, shared with the Congress's Central Library and Archive (La Nación, 12 July 2024). The Senate head Basilio Núñez called the budget the main obstacle (La Nación).
- In July 2025 Decree 4309 raised Congress's budget by G. 4,280 million, of which G. 1,880 million is for repairing and extending El Cabildo, funded by an external loan (Última Hora, 30 July 2025). La Nación described the G. 1,880 million as a budget expansion authorised by the decree and does not mention a loan; it reported the sum for a two-storey building with a rehearsal room, exhibition room and heritage store next to the Casa Bicentenario de la Música (La Nación, 1 August 2025).
- No reporting found of private, corporate or foreign donors, a board or published accounts for the centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism, and none in art magazines. Two Última Hora pieces criticise the centre's cultural policy and a Congress spending decree that includes it.
1. Última Hora: unsigned, "Artistas piden a Lugo la destitución de Margarita Morselli" (2017)
URL: https://www.ultimahora.com/artistas-piden-lugo-destitucion-margarita-morselli-n1104822
- A manifesto signed by 300 artists and cultural activists asked Congress president Fernando Lugo to remove Morselli. The artist Ricardo de la Vega spoke for the group.
- They argued that cultural policy should be popular and reach the whole population, not a select group in Asunción, and that neighbourhoods such as the Chacarita had been sidelined for years.
- They called the hiring of the foreign group Il Divo for the 2011 bicentennial, with thousands of local artists left out, a very serious mistake. The piece carries no reply from Morselli.
2. Última Hora: Redacción ÚH, "Congreso tiene luz verde para gastar en construir un obelisco" (2025)
URL: https://www.ultimahora.com/congreso-tiene-luz-verde-para-gastar-en-construir-un-obelisco
- Calls the Decree 4309 spending, including the G. 1,880 million for El Cabildo, "un despilfarro impropio de un país en crisis" (translation: a waste unfit for a country in crisis), and says Congress presidents acquired buildings to house political allies, using art and culture as cover.
- Names no problem with El Cabildo's programme; the criticism targets Congress.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Última Hora says Congress has seven other premises besides its main building, costing over G. 118,880 million by the Senate's own inventory, and criticises an official car park and a "quincho de oro" in the inner courtyard.
- El Cabildo depends on Congress (Última Hora, 2021), and its extension is paid from the decree the article criticises.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the Congress budget, with a 2025 extension added by an executive decree; no private funder or flagged industry was found. The weak points are that no accounts or governance are published, that the sources differ on whether a loan pays for the extension, and that the extension is part of a spending decree Última Hora called a waste. Watch the construction of the new block.
