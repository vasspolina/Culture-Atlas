NEW YORK ACADEMY OF ART: New York, USA
ID: new-york-academy-of-art-new-york

OVERVIEW
- Graduate school of figurative and representational art at 111 Franklin Street in Tribeca, New York (school website).
- Instruction began in 1980 as the New York Drawing Association; Stuart Pivar is credited as primary founder and Andy Warhol as a key founder and funder (Wikipedia).
- Building: a five-storey, 42,000 square foot building designed by Benjamin Warner in 1861. The school began operating there in 1990, and a 2001 fire damaged half the interior (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: a two-year MFA with about 100 students, a one-year Certificate of Fine Art, a four-week summer programme and continuing education classes (school website; Wikipedia).
- Public events: the Tribeca Ball, first held in 1994, and the Take Home a Nude auction at Sotheby's (Wikipedia). Chubb Fellows exhibitions are listed on the school site.
- No reporting found of gallery visiting hours or admission: the school's site lists studio tours booked online.

FUNDING
New York Academy of Art: privately funded: tuition, fundraising events, foundations and individual donors
- Warhol's bequest after his death in 1987 let the school buy its Franklin Street headquarters, and the Warhol Foundation had given more than $1 million by 2004. Pivar gave key early support (Wikipedia).
- Van Cleef & Arpels was title sponsor of the 15 April 2025 Tribeca Ball (Tribeca Citizen, 11 April 2025, a post labelled as sponsored by the Academy). The post does not call the ball a fundraiser.
- No reporting found of a published donor list or annual report that could be opened.

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein
- Epstein sat on the Academy's board from 1987 to 1994 (Hyperallergic, Rhea Nayyar, 16 March 2026).
- After his 2008 Florida guilty plea, Epstein paid $25,000 for a Tribeca Ball table in each of 2012 and 2014 and $30,000 to the Portrait Scholarship fund in 2014, after an invitation from Eileen Guggenheim (Hyperallergic, Rhea Nayyar, 16 March 2026). These examples come to $80,000 and do not reconcile with the $65,900 total, which Hyperallergic describes as Epstein's contributions to events in 2012 and 2014; the article does not explain the difference. It adds that the Academy gave $30,000 to the charity in 2020 to match the 2014 scholarship gift.
- In March 2026 the Academy redirected $65,900 to Girls Educational and Mentoring Services (GEMS), a nonprofit supporting sexually exploited and trafficked girls and young women; its statement to alumni was dated 12 March. Board members agreed to match the sum so student resources were not cut (USA Art News, 16 March 2026).
- The board's email cited "serious failures in judgment and governance" and said the Academy should not have accepted the contributions; the Academy said it would develop an ethics committee to review donor-support policies (Hyperallergic).
- Board chair Eileen Guggenheim stepped down on 1 April 2026, a month earlier than planned, and remains on the board (USA Art News).
- Unverified: alumna Maria Farmer alleges Guggenheim introduced her to Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell in 1995 and told her to sell Epstein a painting at a discount; USA Art News reports Guggenheim denies introducing them and does not recall the conversation. Hyperallergic says Farmer filed a report with the FBI that went ignored for a decade.
- A petition to remove Guggenheim as chair collected more than 2,100 signatures; the Academy called it "filled with false information, innuendo, and lies". Its organiser retracted it (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 7 May 2020, with a note dated 17 February 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
The school's regular money is private and little of it is published. The documented problem is Epstein: he sat on the board from 1987 to 1994 and paid for Academy events after his 2008 plea, which the Academy's board now calls a failure of judgment; it redirected $65,900 to a charity in March 2026. Watch the new ethics committee and whether the Academy publishes a donor list.
