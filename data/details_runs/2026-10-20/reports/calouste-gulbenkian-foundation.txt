CALOUSTE GULBENKIAN FOUNDATION: Lisbon, Portugal
ID: calouste-gulbenkian-foundation
Website: https://gulbenkian.pt (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private Portuguese foundation for the arts, science, education and charity, with its Lisbon headquarters at Avenida de Berna 45A, near Praça de Espanha.
- Founded on 18 July 1956 under the will of Calouste Sarkis Gulbenkian, an Armenian oil magnate who lived in Lisbon; it marked its 70th anniversary in July 2026.
- The foundation's premises opened in 1969, designed by Ruy Jervis d'Athouguia, Alberto Pessoa and Pedro Cid, in a garden by Gonçalo Ribeiro Telles and António Viana Barreto.
- The Centro de Arte Moderna, designed by Leslie Martin and opened in 1983, reopened in 2024 after a four-year, 58 million euro renovation by Kengo Kuma with landscape architect Vladimir Djurovic, adding 900 square metres of galleries beneath the building and the existing gardens (The Spaces).
- The Calouste Gulbenkian Museum closed for more than a year of works on a renovation designed by Teresa Nunes da Ponta and reopened on 18 July 2026 under director Xavier F. Salomon.
- The foundation also runs the Gulbenkian Orchestra and Choir, the Gulbenkian Art Library, an Armenian Communities Department, a UK branch and a cultural centre in Paris; the Gulbenkian Ballet ran from 1965 to 2005.
- The museum opens Wednesday to Monday 10:00 to 18:00, Saturday to 21:00; tickets cost 6 to 14 euros (Time Out Lisboa). No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation: self-funded endowment: Calouste Gulbenkian's oil fortune, now an investment portfolio
- Total assets were 4,008.93 million euros at the end of 2025, almost all of it an investment portfolio of 3,881.77 million euros (Relatório e Contas 2025).
- Net cost of activities in 2025 was 133.40 million euros (Relatório e Contas 2025); revenue was 11.66 million euros, down 64.3 percent as external funding for research projects at the Instituto Gulbenkian de Ciência moved to the GIMM Foundation (Lusa, June 2026).
- The endowment comes from Gulbenkian's oil wealth. The foundation owned the oil company Partex, whose business Gulbenkian founded in 1938, and which made up about 18 percent of its total investments (Observador).
- It sold Partex to PTTEP, the Thai state oil and gas company, for 622 million US dollars (about 555 million euros); the deal was signed on 17 June 2019 and completed on 4 November 2019 (Observador).
- No reporting found of what the investment portfolio holds today, or of a published fossil fuel exclusion policy.

Red flags: oil money behind the endowment
- The foundation's wealth comes from Calouste Gulbenkian's oil fortune, and until November 2019 it owned the oil company Partex.
- On 14 March 2016 ten protesters entered a biofuels market conference held at the foundation; one said the foundation owned Partex, part of a consortium with Repsol holding the Algarve and Alentejo exploration blocks (Algarve Daily News).
- Environmentalists criticised the gap between the foundation's aims and its Partex wealth; after activists called in 2017 for an end to fossil fuel investment, Partex was sold in 2019 (Jornal Económico).
- When the sale closed, president Isabel Mota said energy could not be ruled out for future investment (Observador).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in the art press. The criticism on record comes from Portuguese national press and concerns governance and building decisions.
1. Jornal Económico com Lusa: "Gulbenkian 70 anos: Fundação marcou o país por 'fazer o que faz falta' apesar de polémicas, diz António Barreto" (April 2026)
URL: https://jornaleconomico.sapo.pt/?p=1399595
- The 2005 closure of the Gulbenkian Ballet caused a major controversy in the arts; the board replaced the company with grants and project funding.
- Former museum director Penelope Curtis (2015 to 2020) criticised the board after leaving for taking decisions without consulting the museum team.
- Sociologist António Barreto said he dislikes the new Centro de Arte Moderna and the loss of part of the garden.
- Also records the environmentalists' objection to the Partex wealth.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation lives on its own endowment, which is Calouste Gulbenkian's oil fortune; it owned an oil company until 2019 and faced protest over it. The weak points are that origin and the lack of any published account of what the 3.9 billion euro portfolio now holds. Worth watching: whether energy holdings return to the portfolio, as the president left open in 2019.
