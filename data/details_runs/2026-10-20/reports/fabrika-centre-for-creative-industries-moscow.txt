FABRIKA CENTRE FOR CREATIVE INDUSTRIES: Moscow, Russia
ID: fabrika-centre-for-creative-industries-moscow
Website: https://fabrikacci.com

OVERVIEW
- Private creative cluster at Perevedenovsky Lane 18, Moscow, in the buildings of the former Oktyabr technical paper factory. It holds artists' studios, exhibition halls, a theatre space and creative businesses.
- The cluster opened in 2005 with the "No Comment" project for the First Moscow Biennale. Asya Filippova began work as General Director of the Oktyabr technical paper factory in 2003 and leads the centre (Fabrika's about page).
- In the 1870s Elizaveta Andreevna Dislen bought the plot; the partnership was nationalised in 1924, and the plant became a technical paper factory in 1941 (Fabrika's about page). The Oktyabr factory still made technical paper in 2016 (The Art Newspaper Russia).
- The site hosts the Ad Marginem publishing house, PIRANESI lab, Liquid Theatre, the Big Cartoon Festival and the Moscow Film School pavilions (Fabrika's about page).
- Programmes: an artist-in-residence programme since 2008 that has taken in more than 70 international artists, and Fabrika Studios since 2014 (Fabrika's about page).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 20:00; exhibitions are free (Fabrika's about page).
- On 22 July 2025 the Fabrika buildings and five neighbouring plots were added to Moscow's comprehensive territorial development programme; nearly half of the territory is to be built up with multi-unit housing. Filippova said Fabrika declined an offer to take part in the reconstruction and is discussing moving with its main residents (Meduza, Asya Zolnikova, 7 November 2025).

FUNDING
Fabrika: self-funded: rent from studios and the Oktyabr paper mill, as stated by director Asya Filippova
- Filippova called Fabrika "a private independent organisation, fully self-sustaining": rent from residents covers running costs and funds its non-commercial projects (Snob, interview by Natalya Alekhina, 24 April 2025, title translated).
- In 2016 she said Fabrika had never had a large volume of external investment, that its income came from paper production and renting work spaces, and that its main support was its owner (The Art Newspaper Russia, Anastasia Petrakova, 18 August 2016, title translated).
- Filippova said the centre owns its buildings and land (Meduza, 7 November 2025).
- Art Focus Now reports that the factory's owner moved paper production elsewhere and that rent from small businesses leasing the spaces paid for free exhibitions and for studio access and production budgets for mid-career artists chosen by an annual open call (Ekaterina Wagner, 9 February 2026).
- The 2015 Fabrika Studios session ran with the support of E.K. ArtBureau (Fabrika's about page).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Novaya Gazeta, Grigory Mirsky, "Жилой или живой? ЦТИ «Фабрика» объявил о скором закрытии" ("Residential or Living? CTI Fabrika Announces Imminent Closure", title translated), 2025: criticises Moscow's comprehensive territorial development programme for treating former industrial zones as "depressive", and argues that redeveloped old factories such as Vinzavod, Flacon, Artplay and GES-2 keep links to the past that demolition does not. It says that in a destroyed civil society institutions like Fabrika cannot count on support against the state machine. The piece does not criticise Fabrika. URL: https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2025/11/19/zhiloi-ili-zhivoi
- Art Focus Now, Ekaterina Wagner, "Farewell to Fabrika: A Transient Oasis of Freedom", 9 February 2026: reports that the city authorities consider the land "ineffective" and plan to demolish the complex for housing, and that appeals to save it were ignored. It quotes curator Mikhail Sidlin calling such spaces "a space of freedom". The piece does not criticise Fabrika. URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/farewell-to-fabrika-a-transient-oasis-of-freedom/
- Connection to Fabrika: both pieces criticise the city's programme, and Fabrika is the site the programme lists for housing.

BOTTOM LINE
Fabrika is a private centre that, in its director's words, lives on rents and the Oktyabr paper mill. Its buildings and land were added in 2025 to Moscow's territorial development programme, which plans housing on nearly half of the territory.
