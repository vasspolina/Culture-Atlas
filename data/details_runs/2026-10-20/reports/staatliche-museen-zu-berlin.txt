STAATLICHE MUSEEN ZU BERLIN: Berlin, Germany
ID: staatliche-museen-zu-berlin
Website: https://www.smb.museum/en/home/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Group of Berlin museums, libraries and research institutes with collections of European and non-European art, archaeology and ethnology, spread over sites including Museum Island, the Kulturforum, Charlottenburg, Dahlem and the Humboldt Forum (Wikipedia, smb.museum).
- Founded in 1823 by King Friedrich Wilhelm III of Prussia as the Königliche Museen, the Royal Museums (Wikipedia). It belongs to the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation (SPK), set up in 1957 to preserve the cultural property of the former state of Prussia (smb.museum).
- Sites include the Altes Museum, Alte Nationalgalerie, Bode Museum, Neues Museum and Pergamon Museum on Museum Island, a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1999; the Gemäldegalerie and Neue Nationalgalerie at the Kulturforum; and the Ethnological Museum and Museum of Asian Art in the Humboldt Forum (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is free for under-18s, people receiving benefits such as Bürgergeld, and holders of annual passes; students, trainees in national service and severely disabled visitors pay half (smb.museum). No reporting found of opening hours or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Staatliche Museen zu Berlin: mainly public: the federal government, Berlin and the other German states through the SPK, plus Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe sponsorship
- The SPK's 2025 operating budget is about €275.3 million: federal allocations about €196.4 million, the State of Berlin about €38.6 million, the other Länder about €20 million. A further €143.1 million is construction money, about €138.7 million of it for new construction projects funded by the Federal Office for Building and Regional Planning (SPK budget page). No figure for the museums' own share was found.
- Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe, the savings-bank group, has been main sponsor since 2011. In 2014 it renewed for three years, covering exhibitions, cultural education for young people and external representation. In July 2017 it renewed for two more years, with the Sparkassen-Kulturfonds, Berliner Sparkasse and, for the first time, the insurer Feuersozietät Berlin Brandenburg (smb.museum press releases). No amounts are stated. No reporting found of the partnership's terms after 2019.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is mostly of the SPK's structure and of restitution, not of the museums' funders. The strongest pieces are news reports of an expert review and of the Benin dispute.
1. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Panel recommends dissolution of 'dysfunctional' Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation" (8 July 2020)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/panel-recommends-dissolution-of-dysfunctional-prussian-cultural-heritage-foundation
- Reports, citing Die Zeit, that a panel of academic advisers commissioned in 2018 by Culture Minister Monika Grütters called the foundation "dysfunctional" and "structurally overwhelmed".
- The panel proposed splitting it into four bodies: one for the Berlin state museums, the State Library, the Secret State Archive and the Ibero-American Institute.
2. The Art Newspaper: Chiara Zampetti Egidi, "Overdue review shakes up Germany's largest cultural institution" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/01/23/overdue-review-shakes-up-german-culture-foundation
- The board approved a reform in December 2022: a board of up to seven members, only two of them from the national museums, and more authority and own budgets for the institutions.
- Quotes the Tagesspiegel calling the response to the reform paper "devastating"; financing decisions were postponed.
3. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Yet again, a US court dismisses Nazi-era Guelph Treasure art lawsuit" (2023)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/07/18/once-again-a-us-court-dismisses-guelph-treasure-art-lawsuit
- The descendants of the Frankfurt dealers who bought part of the Guelph Treasure in 1929 say the sale was coerced; their lawyer Nicholas O'Donnell argues that Germany's refusal to recognise it as coerced conflicts with the Washington Conference Principles. The foundation argues it is immune from US jurisdiction; on 13 July 2023 a US appeals court upheld the denial of a ruling on the merits.
- Germany's Advisory Commission rejected the claim in 2014, finding the sale was not due to persecution.
4. AFP via Hürriyet Daily News: "Row erupts in Germany over restitution of bronzes" (13 June 2023)
URL: https://hurriyetdailynews.com/row-erupts-in-germany-over-restitution-of-bronzes-183882
- A news report, not an art-press essay. After Nigeria's outgoing president Muhammadu Buhari said returned bronzes would go to the Oba of Benin, German officials disputed the terms. SPK president Hermann Parzinger said the declaration did not put future returns in doubt.
- The Nigerian art historian Peju Layiwola called the German reaction overblown and "an excuse" not to return the objects.
- Berlin's Ethnological Museum holds around 530 Benin objects.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museums is public money from the federal government, Berlin and the other states, plus a savings-bank group as main sponsor, and none of the reporting found shows a flagged funder. The weak points are governance and restitution: an expert panel called the SPK dysfunctional, and the Guelph Treasure and Benin returns remain disputed. Worth watching is how the SPK reform and its financing decisions play out.
