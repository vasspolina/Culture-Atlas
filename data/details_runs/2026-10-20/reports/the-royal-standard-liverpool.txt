THE ROYAL STANDARD: Liverpool, United Kingdom
ID: the-royal-standard-liverpool
Website: https://www.the-royal-standard.co.uk/

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led studio and gallery complex at 5 Mann Street, Baltic Triangle, Liverpool. It calls itself "Liverpool's oldest and largest artist-led studio and gallery" and "home to Liverpool's largest community of artists."
- Founded in 2006 in a small pub in Dingle by four Liverpool-based artists (the Royal Standard's about page; The Skinny, 1 June 2016).
- In 2008 it moved to a larger industrial space on Vauxhall Road and relaunched for the Liverpool Biennial 2008. It moved to the Northern Lights site in Cains Brewery in 2017 because of "a precarious rolling contract" at Vauxhall (The State of the Arts, Devon Forrester-Jones, 21 March 2017).
- Building: the Cains Brewery site has about 11,500 square feet, five extra studios and a project space; communal studios cost 56 pounds a week and studio members have 24-hour access (The State of the Arts). The Skinny reported 40 studios, project spaces and two gallery spaces in 2016.
- Programme: studios, public events, residencies, exhibitions and gallery hire (the website).
- Run by directors who serve unpaid two-year terms (The Double Negative, C. James Fagan, 11 May 2016).

FUNDING
The Royal Standard: small public and lottery grants: Arts Council England and National Lottery Community Fund grants, with donations
- Registered charity 1152968 under the name TRS Gallery, registered 19 July 2013; income 56,069 pounds in the year to 30 August 2024 (Charity Commission data via Find that Charity).
- Arts Council England grants recorded by Find that Charity: 14,186 pounds (June 2016, exhibition and anniversary programme), 10,078 pounds (October 2016, relocation and artistic programme), 38,047 pounds (June 2017, one-year bid), 14,890 pounds (November 2018), 14,960 pounds (July 2019, organisational and strategic development) and 29,600 pounds (April 2020, organisational support).
- The National Lottery Community Fund: 2,886 pounds from Awards for All in November 2017 for a one-year project to improve the social space.
- The organisation has relied partly on Arts Council England and project grants (The Double Negative, 2016). The website asks for donations to keep studio rent low and fund paid opportunities.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece, from 2016, on the strains of running the space on unpaid two-year directorships.
1. The Double Negative: C. James Fagan, "'It's my dream job, but it's voluntary': Trials And Triumphs At The Royal Standard" (11 May 2016)
URL: https://www.thedoublenegative.co.uk/?p=18994
- Marks the tenth anniversary; discusses unpaid directors, short terms that mean the organisation is constantly reinvented, and whether a larger, costlier venue would suit it.
- Notes that the space has at times appeared off the beaten track and has been perceived as insular, which the author links to its location on the city's edges.
- The article was commissioned by CVAN NW as part of a programme funded by Arts Council England.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Royal Standard is small: income of 56,069 pounds in the year to 30 August 2024, Arts Council England grants recorded between 2016 and 2020, and a National Lottery Community Fund grant, with donations.
