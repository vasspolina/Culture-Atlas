URBAN SHAMAN CONTEMPORARY ABORIGINAL ART: Winnipeg, Canada
ID: urban-shaman-contemporary-aboriginal-art-winnipeg
Website: https://www.urbanshaman.org (checked 2026-10-25; live, footer dated 2020, news posts to December 2025)

OVERVIEW
- Indigenous artist-run centre at 203-290 McDermot Avenue, Winnipeg (Urban Shaman contact page).
- Established in 1996 (Urban Shaman); its incorporation papers were signed in March 1996, at the house of Lita Fontaine, who recalls it (Windspeaker, 2016).
- Windspeaker (2016) credits Louis Ogemah with the idea of the name "Urban Shaman"; Daina Warren says it came from Norval Morrisseau, who called himself an Urban Shaman. Ogemah is now president of the board (Urban Shaman).
- Dedicated to contemporary First Nations, Metis and Inuit art; the site calls it one of three Aboriginal artist-run centres in Canada (Urban Shaman).
- Spaces: the site lists Main, Marvin, AND and Santa Fe galleries. Staff named on the site include Artistic Director Justin Bear L'Arrivée and Operations Director Debbie Keeper.
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday, 12:00 to 5:00 pm; admission is free (contact page). The home page states: two steps at the street entrance, five more to the elevator, washrooms and the Woodshop not wheelchair accessible, accommodations by email or phone.

FUNDING
Urban Shaman: federal grants, with membership and donations
- Canada Council for the Arts: 280,000 dollars over two years in fiscal 2017-18, under Creating, Knowing and Sharing, Indigenous Organizations stream (Canada Council, 14 November 2018). The Council calls it the only Indigenous contemporary non-profit art gallery in Winnipeg.
- Canadian Heritage: an Indigenous Languages and Cultures Program agreement with Urban Shaman Inc. worth 340,000 dollars, 1 April 2021 to 31 March 2023, to preserve and revitalise Indigenous languages and cultures (Open Government grants database).
- Members and donors: the site sells memberships, takes donations, and holds an annual 50-500 Members Market (Urban Shaman news, December 2025). Proceeds from auctioned pieces at its 20th anniversary gala went to the gallery's operations (Windspeaker, 2016).
- The site's Supporters and Partners page shows nine logos with no names, years or amounts, so it names no funder in text. No page opened confirms funding from the Manitoba Arts Council, the Winnipeg Arts Council or the Province of Manitoba.
- No reporting found of annual reports or accounts on the site; the Staff and Board page names three directors: Louis Ogemah (president), Riva Symko and Albert McLeod.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms, Gulf state or other flagged funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The coverage found is descriptive: anniversary pieces and a funder's profile.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Urban Shaman is public arts and heritage funding from Canada's federal bodies, plus members and donors. The weak points are that the site's supporter page names nobody, no accounts or annual reports are posted, and recent funding after the 2021 to 2023 federal agreement was not found. Worth watching: who funds it from 2023 on, and the access limits of its building.
