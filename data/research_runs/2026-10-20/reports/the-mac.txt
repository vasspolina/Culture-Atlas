THE MAC – Belfast, UK
Website: https://themaclive.com (ok, checked 2026-10-20; some subpages returned errors to the fetch tool)

OVERVIEW
- Metropolitan Arts Centre, an arts centre in Belfast's Cathedral Quarter with two theatres (120 and 350 seats), three galleries, rehearsal and dance studios, education rooms and an artist studio. Designed by Hacker Hall McKnight; opened April 2012 after an £18M build. (CLAD, March 2012)
- Registered NI charity 100808, "The MAC (Metropolitan Arts Centre)", for advancement of arts education; 11 trustees listed. (OpenCharities)
- Open Tuesday-Sunday from 9am, galleries 11am-5pm (site footer). Kids in Museums partner logo shown. Admission and access details not retrieved.

FUNDING
The MAC: mainly public, with trusts and a few corporate sponsors.
- Income £2,986,762 and spending £3,302,232, year to 31 March 2025 (OpenCharities, from the NI charity register).
- Arts Council of Northern Ireland: principal funder; over £1M a year (Arts Professional); listed in the 2025 Annual Funding Programme (NI Assembly deposited paper, June 2025). Money comes from the NI Department for Communities and the National Lottery.
- Capital build 2012: Department for Culture, Arts and Leisure nearly £11M; Arts Council NI, Belfast City Council, Garfield Weston Foundation, Monument Trust (CLAD).
- Belfast City Council and Department for Communities: shown as capital funders on the supporters page; council gave £75k a year to 2019-20 plus bailouts (Museums Journal).
- Supporters page logos (no alt text; names read from file names): Henry Moore Foundation, Ulster Garden Villages, Clever Fulton Rankin (law firm), National Lottery Dormant Accounts, Art Fund, Foyle Foundation, Public Health Agency, Shared Island (Irish government), Wexford County Council.
- Freelands Foundation (founded 2016 by Elisabeth Murdoch): funds a three-year art-education fellowship from its Autumn 2023 Fund (Irish News, July 2024). Amount not reported in that article.

Red flags: Repeated financial distress
- January 2018: Department for Communities gave £275k via Arts Council NI and Belfast City Council £25k to close a "short-term funding gap", after a £40k council bailout in October 2017 (Museums Journal; Arts Professional).
- About 2023: auditors flagged "a material uncertainty" over the group's ability to continue; board forecast a £150,028 deficit for 2022-23; two external audits of financial management; Arts Council NI moved to quarterly payments during a review (Arts Professional). Outcome of the review not found.
- FY2025 accounts show spending above income by about £315k (OpenCharities).

BOTTOM LINE
The MAC is mainly funded by Northern Ireland public money and UK trusts; no ethically contested sponsor was found. What is worth watching is its finances: a 2018 bailout, a 2023 going-concern warning and a deficit again in FY2025.
