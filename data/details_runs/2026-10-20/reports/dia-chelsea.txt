DIA CHELSEA: New York, USA
ID: dia-chelsea
Website: https://www.diaart.org

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition space of the Dia Art Foundation at 537 West 22nd Street in Chelsea, Manhattan; Dia began its presence on West 22nd Street in 1987 with the opening of the Dia Center for the Arts (Wikipedia).
- Run by the Dia Art Foundation, founded in 1974 by Philippa de Menil, Heiner Friedrich and Helen Winkler (Wikipedia).
- Reopened after a two-year renovation that combined three former buildings into one space of 32,500 square feet, about 20,000 of it street-level exhibition space; Architecture Research Office designed the project and kept the industrial buildings, with new facades uniting the three (Wallpaper, Pei-Ru Keh).
- On site: exhibitions that run for about nine months, a space for public performances and educational programmes, and the Dia Bookstore, which returned to New York with the reopening (Wallpaper).
- The opening exhibitions were two commissions by Lucy Raven, the film Ready Mix (2021) and an installation from her Casters series (2021); Dia also extended Joseph Beuys's 7000 Eichen along West 22nd Street to 38 basalt columns and trees (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting: admission is free, as at all of Dia's New York sites (Wallpaper; The Art Newspaper).

FUNDING
Dia Chelsea: endowment, private contributions and foundation grants through the Dia Art Foundation
- The Dia Art Foundation (Dia Center for the Arts Inc) reported total revenue of 41,856,742 dollars for the fiscal year 2025: contributions 24,122,046 dollars and investment income 14,446,583 dollars, with total assets of 273,547,036 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- The Chelsea expansion and renovation cost 20 million dollars. In December 2020 Dia director Jessica Morgan said Dia had received 80 million dollars of its hoped-for 90 million dollar campaign, most of it going to the endowment (The Art Newspaper, Nancy Kenney, 4 December 2020). Wikipedia says the campaign was announced in June 2018 at 78 million dollars and raised to 90 million dollars in May 2019.
- Dia's founding money came from Philippa de Menil, heir to the Schlumberger oil fortune. Between 1980 and 1982 the Schlumberger stock fell from about 87 to about 30 dollars a share, Dia took a 3.87 million dollar Citibank loan against it in 1983 and in 1985 Dia's board was reshaken (Wikipedia). This is history.
- The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation awarded Dia Center for the Arts a 1,000,000 dollar matching grant on 16 June 1995 for a special initiative programme (Mellon Foundation grant database).

Red flags: Sponsor dropped under pressure
- Sackler name: Dia's spokesperson told Hyperallergic (Valentina Di Liscia, 21 December 2020) that in 2018 Marissa Sackler stood down from Dia's board and Dia "discontinued the Sackler naming associated with our public programs". Wikipedia's list of things named after the Sackler family says Dia created a Sackler Institute in 2016 and removed the name in 2019.
- Hyperallergic (Jasmine Weber, 12 July 2021) lists Dia Art Foundation among institutions that removed the Sackler name, citing the advocacy of P.A.I.N. and public awareness of Purdue's role in the opioid epidemic.
- Protest: Nan Goldin told Hyperallergic (Di Liscia, 2020) that P.A.I.N. wanted to hold a protest at Dia, "one of the largest known recipients of Sackler funding", and cased the museum a couple of times, but the action never took place.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Dia Chelsea is the Dia Art Foundation's endowment income and private contributions; the 20 million dollar renovation sat inside a campaign aimed mostly at the endowment. Dia discontinued the Sackler naming of its public programs in 2018 or 2019 (sources differ), and Nan Goldin's group P.A.I.N. wanted to protest at Dia.
