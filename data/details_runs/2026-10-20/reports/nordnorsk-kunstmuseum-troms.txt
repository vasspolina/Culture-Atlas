NORDNORSK KUNSTMUSEUM: Tromsø, Norway
ID: nordnorsk-kunstmuseum-troms
Website: https://nnkm.no

OVERVIEW
- Regional art museum for Northern Norway with three venues: Sjøgata 1 in Tromsø, Storgata 40 in Bodø, and the Nordover Art Center, Road 225-2, Longyearbyen on Svalbard (museum website).
- Established as a foundation in 1985 with the Arts Council Norway, the University of Tromsø, the National Gallery and Riksgalleri as initial supporters. Frode Haverkamp was appointed first director in autumn 1986, and the museum opened to the public in February 1988 in rented rooms above the Tromsø Artist's Union at Muségata 2 (museum history page).
- Tromsø building: a post and telegraph office designed in 1917 by architect Søren Andreas Wiese Opsahl, used by the Troms police from 1971 to 1996. The museum moved in in December 2001 (museum history page).
- Bodø venue: a 1949 to 1950 building made for Bodø Sparebank, taken on a long-term rental agreement after the Bodø establishment was announced in September 2023; it opened on 25 February 2025 (museum history page).
- Collection of over 2,000 works by artists from the 17th century to the present (museum website). The museum is working toward a new building in Tromsø with Tromsø Municipality and the Arctic Philharmonic.
- On 30 March 2020 the board removed director Jérémie McGowan, in post since 2016, without giving a reason. On 1 April three artist board members, Joar Nango, Inger Blix Kvammen and Trygve Luktvasslimo, resigned, writing that the matter had been handled in a very poor way (NRK, "First the museum director was fired, now parts of the board are resigning in protest", 1 April 2020, title translated).
- Visiting: Tromsø and Bodø are open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 17:00; Svalbard is open daily, 11:00 to 17:00 (museum website).

FUNDING
Nordnorsk Kunstmuseum: mainly public: Norwegian state budget, with Nordland county and Bodø municipality for the Bodø venue
- Operating budget of about NOK 32 million (EUR 3 million) in 2022; 96 percent came from public-sector grants, there were no commercial sponsors, and the rest came from non-profit foundations (Kunstkritikk, Live Drønen, "Money Problems", 30 November 2022).
- Operating costs in Tromsø and on Svalbard are funded by the state budget; the Bodø venue is funded by the state budget, Nordland county council and Bodø municipality (museum history page).
- In the early years the national government decided to provide 100 percent of the operating costs (museum history page).
- Director Katya García-Antón said museums should ideally be fully state-funded and called corporate and private sponsorship in Norway "at an early stage" (Kunstkritikk, 30 November 2022).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: the Norwegian state, plus Nordland county and Bodø municipality for Bodø. Kunstkritikk reported in 2022 that 96 percent came from public-sector grants, with the rest from non-profit foundations and no commercial sponsors.
