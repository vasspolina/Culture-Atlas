MUSÉE BORIBANA: Dakar, Senegal
ID: musee-boribana
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary African art in Dakar, at the Rond-point Place ONU, Boulevard Dial Diop at Boulevard du Canal 4 (Senegalese culture ministry page, archive copy of 10 July 2026).
- Founded by the Senegalese anthropologist Boubacar Koné, who has since died, and his wife, the actress CCH Pounder (culture ministry page; DakArtNews, 6 July 2026).
- Sources give different founding years: 1990 (culture ministry), 1992 (DakArtNews) and 1998 (Enquête+, 2015; French Wikipedia).
- "Boribana" is Mandinka for "the race is over" (culture ministry page).
- The building is shaped like a grand piano, with four exhibition rooms on two levels, artists' residency studios, storage and a documentation room (culture ministry page), on a land title of 1,000 square metres (Enquête+).
- The founders later added a library, an outdoor cinema, studios and residency spaces, and ran a Young Artists Prize (DakArtNews).
- It opened with 34 works (DakArtNews); Enquête+ counted at least 400 works by artists of several nationalities in 2015.
- The collection holds sculpture, ceramics, paintings, tapestries, photographs and video, and grows through acquisitions (culture ministry page).
- A conservator, Hawa Mara Ba, formerly a cultural adviser at the Ministry of Culture and Communication, took up her post in March 2015 (Enquête+, citing APS).

FUNDING
Musée Boribana: state-owned since December 2014; built with the founders' private money
- Private origin: French Wikipedia says it was founded with private funds. Pounder told DakArtNews that the couple could not afford the provenance research and authentication older African works need, so they collected contemporary art, and that Koné wanted people to know "we had built it ourselves".
- State since 12 December 2014: Koné gave the museum to the Senegalese state, a gift praised by President Macky Sall and the culture minister (culture ministry page); Enquête+ adds that he also left the state the land and the art holdings.
- French Wikipedia, which carries a verification warning, says instead that the state decided to buy the museum back; the ministry and Enquête+ describe a gift.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One founder's account criticises the museum's state since the handover to the state.
1. DakArtNews: Remy Mallet, "She Helped Build Senegal's First Private Museum of Contemporary Art. Today, CCH Pounder Collects 'for the Future'" (2026)
URL: https://dakartnews.com/2026/07/06/cch-pounder-contemporary-african-art-collector/
- Pounder says that on her first return after the donation she was devastated by the museum's condition, and that she let it go to keep her memories of it.
- The interview names no official and gives no date for the visit.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum was built with the founders' own money and has been Senegalese state property since 2014, so none of its funding falls under a flag. Worth watching: whether the state publishes a budget for it and answers the co-founder's account of its decline.
