NATIONAL GALLERY OF AUSTRALIA: Canberra, Australia
ID: national-gallery-of-australia
Website: https://nga.gov.au (checked 2026-10-24; the site refused automated page requests with HTTP 403, so its 2023-24 annual report PDF was read instead)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at Parkes Place East, Parkes, Canberra, inside the Parliamentary Triangle, on Ngunnawal and Ngambri Country.
- Established by the Australian Government in 1967; formerly the Australian National Gallery, renamed under director Betty Churcher (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by Colin Madigan of Edwards Madigan Torzillo and Partners, begun in 1973, opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1982 and cost 82 million dollars (Wikipedia).
- Collection of more than 166,000 works as of 2012, with a National Sculpture Garden; the Gallery is running a 60 million dollar philanthropic campaign to rebuild the garden (annual report).
- Open 10am to 5pm daily except Christmas Day; entry is free (annual report). Major exhibitions are ticketed.

FUNDING
National Gallery of Australia: mainly public: Commonwealth funding, with corporate partners, philanthropists and a foundation
- In 2023-24 revenue from government was 66.6 million dollars and own-source revenue 35.1 million dollars, of which contributions were 18.4 million (annual report 2023-24).
- Corporate partners gave 696,500 dollars in cash and 2,390,307 dollars in kind in 2023-24. Strategic Partners: ACT Government through VisitCanberra, and Seven West Media. First Nations Art Partner: Wesfarmers Arts. Major Partners: Pallion, Qantas and ISPT. Legal Partner: Maddocks.
- Pallion describes itself as a Sydney precious metals company founded in 1951.
- The Council Chair is Ryan Stokes AO; the National Gallery of Australia Foundation board includes Kerry Stokes AC and Roslyn Packer AC (annual report).
- No reporting found of a Mazda sponsorship of the 2024 Gauguin show on the pages read.

Red flags: Palestinian protest material covered
- In February 2025 the Gallery covered Palestinian protest items, a "free Palestine" T-shirt, a fist patch and a badge, in a show by the Pacific collective SaVĀge K'lub, citing security concerns (ArtReview).
- Curator Rosanna Raymond said she was censored before reluctantly agreeing; Nasser Mashni of the Australian Palestine Advocacy Network called it "a disgraceful act of censorship" (ArtReview, quoting The Guardian).
- The Gallery said it covered only protest material and that a Palestinian flag remained visible.

Red flags: Gina Rinehart portrait
- In May 2024 Olympian Kyle Chalmers and Swimming Queensland chief executive Kevin Hasemann pressed the Gallery to remove Vincent Namatjira's portrait of Gina Rinehart; Hasemann wrote to the director that the portraits were "of deep concern". Rinehart's company has paid over 40 million dollars to Swimming Queensland (Yahoo News, citing the Sydney Morning Herald).
- The Gallery said it welcomes public dialogue about its collection. The article reports no removal.

Red flags: Qantas penalties
- Qantas, a current Major Partner, agreed in May 2024 to a 100 million dollar penalty over selling tickets on more than 8,000 flights it had already cancelled (The Nightly); it admitted its system failed customers but denied breaking the law.
- On 18 August 2025 the Federal Court fined Qantas 90 million dollars for illegally outsourcing about 1,700 ground staff in 2020, in a case brought by the Transport Workers' Union (Forbes Australia).

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the money is Commonwealth funding, and the corporate share is small, under 700,000 dollars in cash in 2023-24. The weak points are a Major Partner with two large penalties (Qantas), the 2025 covering of Palestinian material, and the pressure over the Rinehart portrait. No reporting found of the amounts behind the Wesfarmers or Pallion partnerships.
