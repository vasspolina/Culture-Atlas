KROPKA (BELARUSIAN COMMUNITY SPACE): Tbilisi, Georgia
ID: kropka-belarusian-community-space-listed-in-place-of-krokva-tbilisi

OVERVIEW
- Belarusian community centre in a two-room apartment with a courtyard in a residential building in Tbilisi's Sololaki neighbourhood (Transitions, Tanya Hendzel, "A Belarusian Island in the Caucasus", 22 July 2024).
- Opened in March 2022 as a coworking space, conceived by Belarusian activists who fled repression in Belarus; it soon grew into meetings and events (Transitions). "Kropka" means "Point" (Transitions).
- Day-to-day operations were run by a volunteer, Olga; Viktoria handled administration and was paid by donor organisations (names are pseudonyms in the article) (Transitions).
- Programme: Belarusian, English and Georgian conversation clubs, board games, film screenings, language classes, psychological support groups, art therapy, literary meetings, crafts and book swaps. By mid-2024 it had held more than 700 events for over 5,000 visitors, mostly Belarusian exiles (Transitions).
- A 2023 Paper Kartuli listing described it as a free coworking space for volunteers and activists of Belarusian civil communities, open 10:00 to 21:00 except Thursdays, with room for up to 25 people, a projector, wifi and a kitchen ("От крафтовых баров до арт-объединения. 5 проектов в Тбилиси, которые запустили беларусы", 29 September 2023).
- Pozirk ("Belarusian community center in Tbilisi shutting down", 22 December 2024) announced that Kropka would shut down on 10 January 2025 after three years, with about 800 events and roughly 8,500 participants. Pozirk names as reasons the fall of the Belarusian population in Georgia from about 15,000 to 12,000 over two years and restrictive Georgian laws, including an anti-LGBTQ+ law and a "foreign influence" law.

FUNDING
Kropka: donor-funded: international foundation grants and visitor donations
- Grants from international foundations cover around two-thirds of the cost of rent, materials for events and refreshments; events and the coworking space are free and visitors can leave a donation (Transitions).
- Viktoria's post as administrator is paid by donor organisations (Transitions).
- Viktoria says donations alone cannot sustain Kropka because many Belarusians in Georgia have unstable finances (Transitions).
- Transitions writes that Kropka keeps clear of interactions with Georgian authorities, especially since the adoption of a "foreign agent law"; the sociologist Natalia Ryabova, quoted there, says the law requires organisations that receive foreign funding to register as foreign agents.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Pozirk: Georgia's "foreign influence" law was inspired by a Russian law that the Kremlin uses to persecute opponents; Pozirk also writes that Georgian authorities recently rejected several Belarusians' asylum applications and that activists and journalists are often barred from re-entering the country.
- Wikipedia names the law the "Law on Transparency of Foreign Influence" and gives 14 May 2024 as the date of its third reading in parliament, passed 84 votes to 30.
- Connection: Pozirk lists the law among the reasons for Kropka's closing, and Transitions says Kropka, which relies on international foundation grants, avoids Georgian authorities since the "foreign agent law" was adopted.

BOTTOM LINE
Kropka was a volunteer-run Belarusian exile community space paid for by international foundation grants covering around two-thirds of the cost of rent, event materials and refreshments, plus visitor donations and one administrator paid by donor organisations. Pozirk announced its closing on 10 January 2025.
