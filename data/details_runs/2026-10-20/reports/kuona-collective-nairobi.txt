KUONA COLLECTIVE: Nairobi, Kenya
ID: kuona-collective-nairobi
Website: https://kuonatrust.org

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run collective and studio centre in Kilimani, Nairobi. Wikipedia states that Kuona Artists Collective was established in Kilimani in 2017 following the closure of Kuona Trust.
- A New York Times piece reposted by Ginanne Brownell says the Kuona Trust closed in September and the Collective opened in December on the same grounds, as an artist-run space where artists offer studio space, art open days and residencies.
- The National Museums of Kenya page lists the Kuona Artists Collective among its galleries as Kenya's oldest contemporary art centre, founded in 1995, with dedicated studios, exhibitions, residencies, workshops and mentorship for emerging artists.
- The predecessor Trust was founded in 1995 by Rob Burnett to offer one-off artist workshops, and by 2016 it had over 35 artist studios, a library, exhibition space and residency programmes (Craig Halliday, Africanah, 2016). A 2004 entry on the Communication Initiative site places its start at the National Museum of Kenya and dates its international residencies from 1998.
- At the Trust, artists worked in long metal sheds around a central gathering area, sharing small open studios (Isabel S. Wilcox, Happening Africa, 4 November 2016).
- History of the Trust's closure: Business Daily (10 November 2016, "Art fair opens under the shadow of Kuona Trust scandal", no author named on the page) reported that the Board of Trustees shut the offices in September 2016 so that a donor, Forum Syd, could audit the accounts. It reported that the audit was complete with results to be shared, that "multi-million shillings are missing" and that some donor-funded projects stalled. After the offices closed, a five-member artists' committee organised a fundraising sale; about 35 artists were based at the Trust.
- Business Daily also reported that the Kenyan Ministry of Foreign Affairs paid Sh1 million by cheque to the Trust for a life-sized sculpture of Wangari Maathai by Kevin Oduor, that the ministry queried the Trust's former director Sylvia Gachia on why funds had not been released to the artist, and that Oduor never received the funds needed to buy all the art materials and has yet to complete the work.
- Happening Africa (4 November 2016) says the Trust was closing because of budget problems and a lack of funding.
- Unverified: the New York Times piece reposted by Brownell says the Trust "ran out of funding and also had an alleged misappropriation of funds"; it does not say who alleges it.

FUNDING
Kuona Collective: artist-run space on the grounds of the former Kuona Trust
- Funding of the Trust, as documented: artists rented studio space, sold work from the studios and took commissions (Happening Africa, 4 November 2016). During the 2016 closure artists donated works for sale, with a share of each sale going to the Trust (Business Daily, 10 November 2016).
- Earlier supporters of the Trust: the Royal Netherlands Embassy in Nairobi, the National Museums of Kenya and German Development Cooperation (GTZ) are listed as partners in the 2004 Communication Initiative entry.
- Wikipedia lists Kuona Trust, marked closed, as a partner of Arts Collaboratory, a programme established in 2007 by Hivos and the DOEN Foundation and carried out with the Mondriaan Foundation, with the projects "15 Years Legacy of Kuona Trust" and "Urban Wasanii International Artist's Workshop 2008".
- Kobo Trust sponsored three artists for one year, covering studio rent and art materials (Kuona Trust newsletter, 11 July 2013).
- Forum Syd, a Swedish NGO, was a donor of the Trust and commissioned its special audit in 2016 (Business Daily).

BOTTOM LINE
The Collective is an artist-run space that opened on the grounds of the former Kuona Trust. The Trust worked with Dutch, German, Swedish and Kenyan state partners and donors, and closed in 2016 after a donor audit, with Business Daily reporting missing funds. That reporting concerns the Trust, the Collective's predecessor.
