WELLCOME COLLECTION: London, UK
ID: wellcome-collection
Website: https://wellcomecollection.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Free museum and library on Euston Road, near Euston station, exploring the past, present and future of health (museum website).
- Opened in 2007 in the old Wellcome Building, after the Wellcome Trust moved its offices into the adjoining Gibbs Building by Michael Hopkins and Partners, completed in 2004; an earlier Wellcome Historical Medical Museum stood on Wigmore Street (Wikipedia).
- Named for Sir Henry Wellcome, the pharmaceutical entrepreneur whose will set up the Wellcome Trust in 1936 (Wellcome).
- A 2015 renovation by Wilkinson Eyre Architects cost £17.5 million (Wikipedia, citing a February 2015 report).
- Holds rare books, artworks, films, personal archives and objects on health and medicine (museum website). Wellcome counts 403,000 visitors to it in 2024/25 (Wellcome website).
- On 27 November 2022 it permanently closed "Medicine Man", the 15-year-old display of Henry Wellcome's collection, saying it "perpetuates a version of medical history that is based on racist, sexist and ableist theories" (The Art Newspaper, 28 November 2022).
- Exhibitions, events and the library are free; galleries and library open 10:00 to 18:00 on the day checked; captions or BSL on all video and audio, BSL tours of major exhibitions and induction loops (museum website).

FUNDING
Wellcome Collection: endowed: the Wellcome Trust's investment portfolio, about £39.9 billion
- The Collection is part of Wellcome, a charitable foundation (museum website). Wellcome's "sole source of funds" is its investment portfolio, worth £39.9 billion on 30 September 2025 after a 10.2% return; it spent £1.9 billion on its charitable work in 2024/25 (Wellcome annual report press release).
- The portfolio spans publicly listed shares, private equity, venture capital, property, hedge funds and other investments; Wellcome publishes a list of the listed shares it holds directly (Wellcome).
- Wellcome Trust (charity 210183) reported £589.5 million income in 2024/25, £538.3 million of it from investments, and £2.05 billion in spending (Charity Commission register).
- Origin of the money: Henry Wellcome's pharmaceutical company, left to the Trust in 1936; the Trust first sold shares in it in 1985, when it became Wellcome plc, and sold Wellcome plc to Glaxo in 1995. Wellcome says it is not funded by GSK or any other pharmaceutical company (Wellcome).
- Wellcome plans to spend £16 billion between 2022 and 2032 (Wellcome).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from two sides: that the Collection did too little to face Henry Wellcome's colonial collecting, and that by closing "Medicine Man" it went too far against its founder.
1. The Art Newspaper: Tom Seymour, "London's Wellcome Collection accused of cultural vandalism after closing 'racist, sexist and ableist' display of artefacts" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/11/28/wellcome-collection-accused-of-cultural-vandalism-after-closing-racist-sexist-and-ableist-display-of-artefacts
- Reports that some in the museum community and the public likened the closure of "Medicine Man" to cultural vandalism.
2. The Conversation: Anaïs Walsdorf, "Why Wellcome closed its Medicine Man exhibition, and others should follow suit" (2022)
URL: https://theconversation.com/why-wellcome-closed-its-medicine-man-exhibition-and-others-should-follow-suit-196171
- Argues the gallery told history from a narrow, Eurocentric perspective and that true decolonial action means returning human remains and objects.
- Cites a summer 2022 report that found Wellcome "perpetuated systematic racism" against staff. The author is a former Wellcome Collection employee.
3. The Spectator: Ross Clark, review of "Hard Graft: Work, Health and Rights" (2024)
URL: https://spectator.com/?p=516506
- Says the Collection now dismisses Henry Wellcome as "an evil colonialist" and accuses UK arts institutions of "trashing their own history"; an opinion review.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2015: the Guardian launched a petition, signed by more than 93,000 people, asking the Wellcome Trust and the Gates Foundation to divest from the top 200 fossil fuel companies; the Trust's direct holdings then included Shell and BP, worth £260 million together (Civil Society, 2015).
- The Trust called climate change "one of the greatest contemporary challenges" and a "highly complex issue" on which it engaged with the businesses it invests in (Civil Society, 2015).
- 2022: Wellcome sold its BP and Shell shares to concentrate on "non-cyclical industries", saying the sales were "not a reflection of the companies' approach to the energy transition" (Civil Society, 2022). Wellcome aims to bring its portfolio to net zero by 2050 at the latest (Wellcome).
- The campaign targeted the Trust's investments, which pay for the Collection.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one private endowment's, and none of it carries a flag. Worth watching: what the endowment holds in fossil fuels today and how the Collection handles its founder's colonial-era collecting.
