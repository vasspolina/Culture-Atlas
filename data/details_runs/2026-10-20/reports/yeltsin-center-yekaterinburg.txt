YELTSIN CENTER: Yekaterinburg, Russia
ID: yeltsin-center-yekaterinburg
Website: https://yeltsin.ru/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural centre at ulitsa Borisa Yeltsina 3, Yekaterinburg (stated by the Center).
- Set up under the 2008 federal law on centres of the presidents' historical heritage; the operator is the foundation "Presidential Center of B.N. Yeltsin". It opened on 25 November 2015, with about 500 guests including Vladimir Putin and Dmitry Medvedev (Wikipedia, Yeltsin Center).
- Named for Boris Yeltsin, the first president of Russia.
- Architect Boris Bernaskoni (Wikipedia).
- On site: the Boris Yeltsin Museum, an art gallery, an archive and library, and programmes for children and teenagers (stated by the Center).
- Visiting: the site page opened lists no opening hours, prices or accessibility details; reduced tickets exist for the museum and gallery only, sold at the box office.

FUNDING
Yeltsin Center: mainly public with corporate and personal donors: federal budget, a regional loan and named donors
- Construction: almost 5 billion roubles from the state and another 2 billion roubles as a loan from the regional authorities (Novaya Gazeta, 25 November 2015). The Association of Lawyers of Russia site, republishing Meduza's 24 November 2015 report, gives 4.98 billion roubles from the federal budget and a 2 billion loan from the Sverdlovsk region government.
- Named donors: plaques at the entrance name Vladimir Putin as a private individual, the company Gazprom, and the businessmen Vladimir Potanin and Roman Abramovich (same report).
- Board of trustees: chair Anton Vaino; members include Alexander Voloshin, Naina Yeltsina, Artyom Zhoga and Denis Pasler (Yeltsin Center). The Center posts accounting reports on its site.
- Current budget and donor figures: a March 2026 Meduza article, written with the outlet Bereg, covers the Center's finances, but only its headline and lede could be opened, so no figures from it are used here. No reporting found of what each named donor gave.

Red flags: state pressure on programming and staff
- Statement: the Center published a call to stop military action on 25 February 2022. The Center's press service told the outlet It's My City that it removed the text at the demand of law enforcement agencies, without saying what the complaints were; the web archive last held it on 1 April 2022 (Novaya Gazeta Europe, 21 June 2022; Meduza, 21 June 2022).
- Foreign agent check: on 15 June 2023 Deputy Justice Minister Oleg Sviridenko said the ministry had begun a review of whether the Center's activity fell under the foreign agent law. The Center said it expected the review to find no grounds (The Insider, 15 June 2023). No outcome found.
- Fine: the Verkh-Isetsky court of Yekaterinburg fined first deputy executive director Lyudmila Telen 45,000 roubles for "discrediting the army" over a repost of "No to war!" from the page of Tatyana Yumasheva, Yeltsin's daughter. The protocol followed a complaint by State Duma deputy Mikhail Delyagin; the report is dated 20 August 2025 (Novaya Gazeta Europe).
- Cancellation: the presentation of Nina Khrushcheva's book, set for the evening of 26 November 2024, was cancelled for "technical reasons" after pro-government Telegram channels insulted her and the movement Zov Naroda complained to the Prosecutor General's Office and the Justice Ministry (Mediazona, 25 November 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism from art outlets found. The pieces found are from independent Russian news media, not art press, and cover pressure on the Center.
1. Meduza (an independent Russian news outlet; republishing Bereg): "We gave up the opposition stance fairly quickly" (10 March 2026)
URL: https://meduza.io/feature/2026/03/10/my-otkazalis-ot-frondy-dovolno-bystro
- The lede says the Center was for many years almost an opposition platform that irritated many, and that after 24 February it was not closed but forced into silence (my translation).
- Only the headline and lede could be read; the body was not available. Not an art-press piece.
2. Novaya Gazeta Europe (independent Russian news outlet): news report on the Telen fine (20 August 2025)
URL: https://novayagazeta.eu/articles/2025/08/20/zamglavy-eltsin-tsentra-oshtrafovali-za-repost-zapisi-net-voine-so-stranitsy-docheri-pervogo-prezidenta-rf-news
- Reports the fine; her lawyer Mikhail Biryukov says she deleted the post before officials began checking her pages and that the 90-day limitation period had expired. Not an art-press piece.
3. Mediazona (independent Russian news outlet): news report on the Khrushcheva cancellation (25 November 2024)
URL: https://zona.media/news/2024/11/25/vne-sistemy
- Reports the cancellation after denunciations by pro-government channels and Zov Naroda. Not an art-press piece.

BOTTOM LINE
The Center was built with state money and a regional loan, and its donors are named Russian businessmen and companies, including Gazprom; no foreign funder was found. The weak points are the pressure documented since 2022: the deleted statement at law enforcement's demand, the 2023 foreign agent review, the Telen fine and the cancelled book event. Worth watching: current state and donor shares of income, which the Center's accounts would show.
