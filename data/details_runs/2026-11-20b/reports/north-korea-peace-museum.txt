NORTH KOREA PEACE MUSEUM: Panmunjom, North Korea
ID: north-korea-peace-museum
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum at Panmunjom, just north of the Military Demarcation Line in the Demilitarized Zone near Kaesong, set up in the buildings once used by North Korean and UN officials to negotiate and sign the 1953 armistice (Koryo Tours, February 2020).
- Koryo Tours says the original flags and agreement lie under glass on a table in the centre of the room, among photos and artefacts of the time.
- Korea Times columnist Jon Dunbar, who visited in 2010 and 2018, describes the Armistice Talks Hall, where the agreement was signed in 1953, and next door a Signing Pavilion holding many artefacts.
- In the pavilion Dunbar saw an axe under a glass case in 2010 and, in 2018, a wooden club beside it: the weapons of the incident of 18 August 1976 in which, in the Western account, North Koreans killed two Americans.
- Visiting: tourists come by bus from Pyongyang, a 2 to 3 hour drive, or from Kaesong, and go on to the Joint Security Area after the museum (Koryo Tours).

FUNDING
North Korea Peace Museum (Panmunjom): state-run: a museum of the North Korean state on the North Korean side of the Demilitarized Zone
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the museum.
- The army checks and guides visitors: military personnel carry out checks in the car park and a Korean People's Army guide explains the site before the buses go on to the museum (Koryo Tours). The US Treasury added the Korean People's Army to its sanctions list on 1 June 2017 (OFAC); the notice names the army, not the museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One travel column, not art criticism, describes the museum's presentation of the 1976 incident.
1. Korea Times: Jon Dunbar, "[HOLIDAY IN NORTH KOREA] Touring both sides of Korea's DMZ" (2021)
URL: https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/globalcommunity/20210216/holiday-in-north-korea-touring-both-sides-of-koreas-dmz
- Sets the Western account of the 1976 killings against the handwritten Korean label, which he renders as "the axe and the club that American imperialists used to kill our guards at Panmunjom", and says the display gives no further detail and guides give that corner of the room a wide berth.
- Describes the talks hall in 2010 and 2018 crowded with Chinese tourists paying little attention to the guides, and says the North uses the Joint Security Area as a theatrical stage.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for by the North Korean state, and the US sanctions on that state and its army are its one Red flag.
