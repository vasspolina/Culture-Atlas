MUSEO REINA SOFÍA: Madrid, Spain
ID: museo-reina-sof-a
Website: https://www.museoreinasofia.es (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Spain's national museum of 20th-century art in Madrid, inaugurated on 10 September 1992 and named for Queen Sofía (Wikipedia).
- Its collection covers art from 1881 to the present and houses Picasso's Guernica (Jacobin).
- Housed in the former General Hospital of Madrid, designed by José de Hermosilla and largely built by Francisco Sabatini; the hospital closed in 1969 and renovation began in 1980.
- Ian Ritchie added three glass towers in 1989. Jean Nouvel designed an 8,000 m² extension that opened in October 2005 at a cost of €92 million.
- Also runs two Retiro Park galleries, the Palacio de Velázquez and the Palacio de Cristal, with free admission; the Palacio de Cristal is closed for architectural works.
- Open Monday and Wednesday to Sunday, closed Tuesdays. General admission €12; discounts for under-18s, over-65s and students at ticket offices. Free on set days and for visitors with a disability of 33% or more plus one companion. Almost all public areas are wheelchair accessible; the Vaults Room is not.

FUNDING
Museo Reina Sofía: mainly public: the Spanish state, with named corporate patrons and foundations
- Corporate patrons named by the museum: Inditex, Fundación "la Caixa", Fundación Mapfre and Fundación Mutua Madrileña. The page gives no amounts or years.
- Partners named: Ayuntamiento de Madrid, Comunidad de Madrid, Fundación Daniel y Nina Carasso, Fundación Occident, Fundación ONCE, Estrella Damm, Fundación Damm, illycaffè, HP, El País, Cadena SER, iryo, Vocento and Condé Nast. Supporters also include Iberia, IKEA Ibérica and Sotheby's.
- La Caixa: an agreement signed on 23 December 2025 with the banking foundation «la Caixa» sponsors the public educational programme, a maximum of €300,000 for one year, extendable by up to four more (state gazette BOE, 10 January 2026).
- Fundación Mapfre: an agreement signed on 28 December 2022 for up to €175,000 for conservation and restoration of contemporary art, for one year, extendable by up to four more (BOE, 9 January 2023).
- No reporting found of the museum's current budget split between state money and sponsorship.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (la Caixa and Naturgy)
- Current funding: the banking foundation «la Caixa» signed a sponsorship agreement with the museum on 23 December 2025: up to €300,000 for the public educational programme, for one year, extendable by up to four more (BOE, 10 January 2026). The museum also lists Fundación "la Caixa" as one of its four corporate patrons.
- Shareholding: CriteriaCaixa, the investment holding company of La Caixa, is the largest shareholder of the energy company Naturgy. On 17 December 2025 it bought a further 2% for €485 million, taking its holding to 25.9% (Ara).
- No reporting found of a campaign aimed at the museum over la Caixa's money.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media aimed at the museum's sponsors. The criticism found is political and managerial: right-wing attacks on its former director and a parliamentary demand for a full inventory. Most art-press coverage defends the museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Tom Seymour, "Extreme right attacks contributed to resignation of Madrid's Reina Sofía museum director, open letter says" (2023)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/02/06/extreme-right-attacks-resignation-madrid-reina-sofia-museum-director
- Reports that the newspaper ABC said the museum broke its own rules by renewing director Manuel Borja-Villel's contract in 2013 and 2018 without open competition, and, in a separate article, accused him of overseeing "political propaganda" in exhibitions. The museum denied this and said the government backed both renewals.
- Borja-Villel resigned on 20 January 2023. An open letter signed by more than 1,900 people called the attacks a "cultural war".
- Defends the museum rather than criticising it.
2. e-flux Notes: open letter on Borja-Villel's departure (30 January 2023), signed by directors, curators and scholars including Benjamin Buchloh and Rosalind Krauss
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/516152/on-the-departure-of-manuel-borja-villel-as-director-of-the-museo-nacional-centro-de-arte-reina-sof-a
- Argues the attacks were a politically driven "cultural war" aimed at the museum's model, not one director.
- Not criticism of the museum: it defends it.
3. USA Art News: "Helen", "Reina Sofía Faces Audit and Political Pressure Over Missing Artworks" (21 May 2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/reina-sofia-faces-audit-and-political-pressure-over-missing-artworks
- A parliamentary committee voted 20 to 13 to give the museum until 31 December 2026 to produce a complete inventory, and said the ministry should remove director Manuel Segade if it fails. The Popular Party and the far right backed it; the Socialists abstained.
- Reports gaps in tracking its 25,000-plus works, including a 2021 donation, and says Spain's Court of Auditors earlier criticised its internal controls. The museum accepts the problems and has launched a records platform, Artis.

BOTTOM LINE
Sponsor money is documented in the state gazette, but the museum publishes no amounts for most patrons. The weak point is la Caixa, which sponsors the education programme and whose holding company is the largest shareholder of Naturgy. Worth watching: the 31 December 2026 inventory deadline.
