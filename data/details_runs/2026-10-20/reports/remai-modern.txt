REMAI MODERN: Saskatoon, Canada
ID: remai-modern
Website: https://remaimodern.org

OVERVIEW
- Public art museum at 102 Spadina Crescent East in Saskatoon, Saskatchewan, on the west bank of the South Saskatchewan River (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Incorporated as the Art Gallery of Saskatchewan on 9 July 2009 after a plan to renovate the Mendel Art Gallery was abandoned; it opened to the public on 21 October 2017 and became Remai Modern in 2016 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Ellen Remai, whose donation helped fund it; the collection grew out of the Mendel Art Gallery, which operated from 1964 to 2015 (museum website).
- Bruce Kuwabara of KPMB Architects designed the building (Wikipedia).
- Aileen Burns and Johan Lundh are the co-executive directors and CEOs (museum website).
- Open Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 10 am to 5 pm, Thursday and Friday 10 am to 9 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday (museum website).
- 204,352 people visited in 2025, and the museum reported a surplus of $95,027 (2025 annual report).

FUNDING
Remai Modern: mainly public: City of Saskatoon operating grants, with a lead private donor, arts-council grants and corporate sponsors
- 2025 operating revenue was C$13.087 million: City of Saskatoon grants C$6.652 million, other grants C$728,000, self-generated revenue C$3.655 million, donations and sponsorships C$1.952 million, interest C$99,000. The City of Saskatoon grants are about 51% of revenue. The annual report states that the accounts are audited by Deloitte LLP (2025 annual report).
- Core funders named in the 2025 annual report: the Frank and Ellen Remai Foundation (lead donor), the City of Saskatoon, the Canada Council for the Arts, SK Arts and Sask Lotteries.
- Operating grants in the 2025 donor list: the Canada Council for the Arts, the Sask Lotteries Trust Fund for Sport, Culture and Recreation (via SaskCulture) and SK Arts. Project grants: Canadian Heritage, Creative Australia, the Embassy of Sweden, the Government of Saskatchewan, PrairiesCan, the Terra Foundation for American Art and Tourism Saskatchewan.
- 2025 sponsors listed include RBC Wealth Management, the Saskatchewan Indian Gaming Authority, SaskEnergy, SaskTel, SGI, TD Canada Trust and Wyant Group.

Red flags: Gambling
- The Saskatchewan Indian Gaming Authority is a 2025 sponsor of Remai Modern and "generously supported" the exhibition Tarralik Duffy: Klik My Heels (2025 annual report).
- Wikipedia states that SIGA is owned by a consortium of the 74 First Nations band governments in Saskatchewan and operates seven tribal casinos and the online casino PlayNow.com; it generated a record $378 million in gross revenue in fiscal year 2024-25 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- SaskEnergy is a 2025 sponsor, and presented the museum's Fireside event series for the third year in 2025 (2025 annual report).
- SaskEnergy is a Crown corporation of the Saskatchewan government that supplies natural gas in the province and serves over 405,000 customers (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns governance, not the art: a 2019 board shake-up and the management behind it.
1. Canadian Art: Jen Budney, "What the Remai Modern Shakeup Really Means" (2019)
URL: https://canadianart.ca/features/what-the-remai-modern-shakeup-really-means/
- Argues the dispute is "about accountability and transparency in management and governance" and not a clash over programming vision, a claim she calls "a red herring".
- Points to staff turnover, a harassment investigation of the CEO by the Saskatchewan Human Rights Commission, a top-down management style and weak public accountability, and recommends public release of board agendas and minutes and reporting attendance as actual visitors.
- Names CEO Gregory Burke, who announced his resignation in December 2018, and ousted board secretary Alison Norlen; says the gallery's "world-class" ambitions shifted its focus from its public mission.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In March 2019, five of the previous 12 board members stayed on; board secretary Alison Norlen was not reappointed and five others resigned or did not seek another term, two weeks after city council appointed five new members. Outgoing chair Scott Verity described a "difficult" governance relationship with the City of Saskatoon; Mayor Charlie Clark would not link workplace complaints to the turnover (Global News, Ryan Kessler, 11 March 2019).
- City council voted unanimously to appoint five new members to the 12-person board, and two of the five continuing members were city councillors (Global News).

BOTTOM LINE
Remai Modern is mainly funded by the City of Saskatoon, which provided about 51% of its C$13.1 million revenue in 2025, plus a lead private donor, the Frank and Ellen Remai Foundation, and arts-council grants. Two 2025 corporate sponsors fall in flagged categories: the Saskatchewan Indian Gaming Authority (gambling) and the natural gas utility SaskEnergy (fossil fuels). In 2019 the city council appointed five new board members, and the outgoing chair described a difficult governance relationship with the city.
