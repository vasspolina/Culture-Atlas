DEUTSCHES HISTORISCHES MUSEUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: deutsches-historisches-museum-berlin
Website: https://www.dhm.de

OVERVIEW
- National history museum at Unter den Linden 2, 10117 Berlin, run by the Deutsches Historisches Museum Foundation (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1987 by the Federal Republic of Germany and the Land of Berlin to mark Berlin's 750th anniversary; after reunification in 1990 it took over the buildings and collections of the GDR's Museum für Deutsche Geschichte (stated by the space). It first opened in the Reichstag building in former West Berlin (Wikipedia).
- Prof. Dr. Raphael Gross, a Swiss historian, was appointed president by the Board of Trustees on 24 November 2016 and took office on 15 April 2017; the collection holds some 1,000,000 objects (stated by the space).
- Buildings: the Zeughaus of 1695 and the adjacent exhibition hall designed by I. M. Pei, opened in 2003 (Wikipedia). The Zeughaus and the overview exhibition on German history are closed for construction (stated by the space); the Berliner Zeitung dates the closure to 2021.
- The Pei building stays open with temporary exhibitions, daily from 10 am to 6 pm; the museum offers information in German Sign Language and Easy Language, a tactile orientation plan and barrier-free guided tours (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Deutsches Historisches Museum: funded by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (stated by the space)
- The museum's site footer reads "Funded by the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media" (stated by the space).
- Since 30 December 2008 the museum has been a public law foundation of the federal government (Wikipedia). Its Board of Trustees has five members from the Federal Government, the Bundestag and the Länder, and Dr. Konrad Schmidt-Werthern is chairman (stated by the space).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces: a 2017 review of a 2016 exhibition and 2026 press coverage of the delayed Zeughaus renovation.
1. Contemporary And (C&): Jessica Aimufua, "Where Are You Going, White Man?" (10 April 2017), review of the exhibition "German Colonialism: Fragments Past and Present"
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/where-are-you-going-white-man
- Calls the exhibition "an undeniably German and therefore Eurocentric perspective on colonial rule"; the Namibian and Tanzanian curators "were only called in" when the concept was largely complete.
- The review calls the "Kolonfiguren" approach "dangerous" because it equates satirical exaggeration and racist stereotyping; the accompanying information "remains largely superficial".
- The exhibition "displays too many objects in a confined space", around 500 exhibits on 1,000 square metres, and the installation on the Black Movement in Germany is "almost hidden in the far corner of the exhibition hall".
2. Berliner Zeitung: Kai Kleinwächter, "Sanierung außer Kontrolle: Zeughaus bleibt noch für Jahre zu" (23 January 2026)
URL: https://www.berliner-zeitung.de/article/sanierung-ausser-kontrolle-zeughaus-bleibt-noch-fuer-jahre-zu-10015732
- The closure began in 2021 with damage to the air-conditioning system, when only a technical repair of the climate system was planned; the work now also covers IT and energy efficiency, a second visitor entrance and additional toilets.
- Raphael Gross does not expect the Zeughaus to reopen before 2031, according to rbb24 as cited by the paper, and calls 2031 a positive scenario.
- The paper reports renewed debate over costly construction projects and calls for interim solutions to show parts of the new permanent exhibition.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the German federal government, through the Commissioner for Culture and the Media, with the foundation overseen by a board of federal, Bundestag and Länder representatives. The documented criticism concerns an exhibition's treatment of colonial history and the delayed Zeughaus renovation.
