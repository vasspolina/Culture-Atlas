PATRIOT PARK: Kubinka, Russia
ID: patriot-park
Website: https://patriotp.ru (checked 2026-11-20; the domain did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Military theme park of Russia's armed forces in Kubinka, about 60 kilometres outside Moscow, on about 5,500 hectares, beside the long-standing Kubinka tank museum (Al Jazeera, 2015; RFE/RL).
- A pet project of former defence minister Sergei Shoigu, sometimes called a "military Disneyland" (AP).
- First used as the venue of the Army-2015 arms fair, which Vladimir Putin opened on 16 June 2015; he said the park would be a major part of military-patriotic education of the young, and it was due to open to the public in 2016 (Al Jazeera, 2015).
- The Main Cathedral of the Russian Armed Forces in the park was consecrated on 14 June 2020, with Patriarch Kirill leading the service (RFE/RL).
- Shoigu said visitors, especially the young, would be able to operate military equipment, fire weapons and make parachute jumps (The Moscow Times, 2015).

FUNDING
Patriot Park: state-funded: Russia's Defence Ministry
- The Defence Ministry allocated over 20 billion roubles (about $363.5 million) for the park, including 7 billion roubles for the central exhibition and congress hall, according to tender data cited from Kommersant (The Moscow Times, 2015).
- The 8.3 billion rouble contract for the expo centre went to the state contractor Oboronstroi in a May 2015 tender in which no other firm bid (The Moscow Times, 2015).
- Plans included a hotel, offices and a residence for Shoigu, due in 2017 (The Moscow Times, 2015).

Red flags: embezzlement of park funds (convictions 2025 to 2026)
- On 5 August 2024 the Investigative Committee arrested the park's head, Vyacheslav Akhmedov, and Major General Vladimir Shesterov of the Defence Ministry's innovations department on suspicion of embezzling funds for the park (AP).
- The arrests followed the April 2024 jailing of Shoigu's deputy Timur Ivanov on bribery charges and Shoigu's removal as defence minister in May 2024 (AP).
- On 15 August 2025 the Odintsovsky City Court in the Moscow region sentenced Akhmedov to five years in a medium-security prison and a 300,000 rouble fine for large-scale fraud and forgery: from 2022 to 2024 he and Shesterov falsified park construction papers, while nearly 26 million roubles of work went into former deputy defence minister Pavel Popov's private property (The Moscow Times).
- A military court sentenced Shesterov to six years in prison in July 2025 (The Moscow Times).
- On 10 April 2026 the 235th garrison military court in Moscow sentenced Popov to 19 years in a maximum-security prison and an 85 million rouble fine on five counts, including large-scale fraud and bribery; the court found he had embezzled 25.8 million roubles from the park. His lawyer said he would appeal (Meduza).

Red flags: 2023 display of captured Western weapons from Ukraine
- At the Army-2023 forum in the park in August 2023, Russia showed an American M777 howitzer, an Australian Bushmaster and a French AMX-10 captured in Ukraine, a drone bomb painted "to Berlin" and "to London", and a photo display on alleged "Ukrainian Nazism", next to stands where children assembled mock firearms (The Moscow Times).

Red flags: Canadian sanctions, 2024
- Canada added the "Military-Patriotic Recreation Park of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation" to Schedule 1 of its Special Economic Measures (Russia) Regulations by an amendment registered on 21 February 2024 (Canada Gazette, SOR/2024-32; Justice Laws).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage comes from news reporting, which treats the park as Defence Ministry propaganda and, since 2024, as a corruption case.

BOTTOM LINE
Defence Ministry money, spent on war propaganda for the young and partly stolen by the officials who ran it.
