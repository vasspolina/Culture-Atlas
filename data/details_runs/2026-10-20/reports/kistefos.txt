KISTEFOS: Jevnaker, Norway
ID: kistefos
Website: https://www.kistefosmuseum.com

OVERVIEW
- Industrial museum, sculpture park and contemporary art museum at Kistefossveien 112 in Jevnaker, on the Randselva river (stated by the museum).
- The wood pulp mill on the site was founded in 1889 by Anders Sveaas and produced pulp until the mid-1950s; his grandson Christen Sveaas set up the museum in 1996 (stated by the museum). Wikipedia says Sveaas bought the company back in 1993 and acquired 85 percent of it in 1995 with the aim of creating an industrial museum and a contemporary sculpture park.
- The original machinery and buildings are kept as a "Living Factory" (stated by the museum).
- The Twist, an exhibition building designed by Bjarke Ingels Group (BIG), crosses the Randselva and opened in September 2019 with "Hodgkin and Creed: Inside Out" (The Art Newspaper, Federico Florian, 18 September 2019). It cost £18.5 million (The Art Newspaper); the Museums Association lists NOK 200 million (£17.1 million).
- Exhibitions run in The Twist and in the former factory hall Nybruket Galleri. In 2025 the main exhibition was Christina Quarles and a second show was Kathleen Ryan's first large museum exhibition; the season drew 90,000 visitors (annual report 2025, Norwegian).
- Christen Sveaas' Art Foundation, set up in October 2019, holds about 800 artworks donated in 2020 from his private collection, over 850 with later donations; Kistefos exhibitions are developed with it and are often based on its works (stated by the museum).
- A new visitor centre and a new museum building are under construction (annual report 2025). The Directorate for Cultural Heritage (Riksantikvaren) decided in 2025 to protect (fredning) Kistefos Træsliberi (annual report 2025).
- The 2027 season runs from 2 May to 17 October; adult admission is NOK 210, seniors NOK 180, ages 20 to 25 NOK 110, children 0 to 19 free; the sculpture park is open all year (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Kistefos: owner-funded: the founder Christen Sveaas through his company Kistefos AS and his foundations, with state, municipal and savings-bank foundation grants
- The museum is run by the foundation Stiftelsen Kistefos-Museet. Its 2025 operating income was NOK 156.5 million: sales NOK 16.5 million, donations for art and investment NOK 97.0 million, other grants and gifts NOK 43.1 million (annual report 2025).
- Kistefos AS: NOK 37.8 million for sculptures and NOK 59.1 million for investments and projects in 2025. The museum says its development "has been made possible through generous donations from Christen Sveaas and his companies" (annual report 2025, translated).
- Stiftelsen Kistefos-Museets Driftsfond: NOK 18.75 million for operations in 2025. Anders Sveaas Allmennyttige Fond: NOK 4.02 million for projects (annual report 2025).
- Riksantikvaren: NOK 3.05 million in 2025, including NOK 1.765 million for management, operations and maintenance of the historic buildings and a roof repair on the pulp mill (annual report 2025).
- Local and county public bodies in 2025: Akershus county council NOK 428,488, Jevnaker municipality NOK 400,000, Ringerike municipality NOK 350,000 (annual report 2025).
- Savings-bank foundations in 2025: SpareBank 1-stiftelsen Jevnaker Lunner Nittedal NOK 10 million for a project plus NOK 710,357 for entry and NOK 405,866 for schools; Sparebankstiftelsen DNB NOK 3.5 million; SpareBank 1-stiftelsen Gran NOK 525,000 for a project plus NOK 209,270 for entry; Sparebank 1 Ringerike Hadeland NOK 160,000 (annual report 2025).
- Other private foundations in 2025: Stiftelsen Sat Sapienti NOK 500,000 and Stiftelsen Fritt Ord NOK 75,000 (annual report 2025).
- A 2025 sculpture gift, Nairy Baghramian's Resting Arms, was donated by Kistefos AS and AS Holding (annual report 2025). Annual reports from 2010 to 2025 are posted on the museum's site.
- Where the money comes from: Wikipedia calls Sveaas the founder and sole owner of Kistefos AS, his investment company, and lists its investments as dry cargo shipping, offshore supply vessels, financial services, private equity, venture capital, technology-founded investments and real estate development, plus the forestry of Kistefos Træsliberi.

BOTTOM LINE
Kistefos is mainly funded by its founder: in 2025 Kistefos AS gave NOK 97.0 million of the museum's NOK 156.5 million operating income, and the museum itself says its development rests on donations from Christen Sveaas and his companies. Public money (Riksantikvaren, the county council and municipalities) and savings-bank foundations add smaller named amounts, itemised in the annual report.
