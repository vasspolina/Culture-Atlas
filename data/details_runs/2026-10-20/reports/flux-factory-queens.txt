FLUX FACTORY: Queens, USA
ID: flux-factory-queens
Website: https://fluxfactory.org

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led non-profit arts space in Long Island City, Queens, at 39-31 29th Street (Flux Factory history page), with a second venue, Flux IV, at 56-21 2nd Street (Flux Factory).
- Began in 1994 when seven artists rented a former spice factory on the Williamsburg waterfront in Brooklyn; it became a 501(c)(3) in 1999 (Flux Factory history page). ProPublica lists its tax exemption as issued in November 1999.
- It was priced out of Williamsburg in 2002 and displaced by eminent domain in 2009 (Flux Factory history page). It has rented a converted greeting card factory in Long Island City since 2009 (Hyperallergic, Valentina Di Liscia, 17 November 2021).
- Programmes: a residency for about 40 artists a year with affordable studios and shared workshops, four group exhibitions a year, roughly 75 free events a year, about 100 commissioned artworks a year through open calls, Satellite Projects abroad, the Rhizome Project formalised in 2020, the Windmill Community Garden and a Governors Island residency pilot in 2021 (Flux Factory history page).
- In 2021 it bought its 29th Street home and a ground-floor space of 3,000 square feet in Gotham Point's South Tower on the Hunters Point waterfront, which became Flux IV (Hyperallergic). Nat Roe was executive director in 2021 (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).

FUNDING
Flux Factory: public and philanthropic grants: New York City Department of Cultural Affairs, New York State Council on the Arts and the Andy Warhol Foundation
- New York City: about $5 million from the city's Department of Cultural Affairs capital allocations paid for both 2021 purchases. Restrictive covenants give the city first lien rights and keep both properties as cultural facilities for 30 years (The Art Newspaper, Justin Kamp, "In rare victory for an artist-run space, New York's Flux Factory buys its building", 6 December 2021). It was the second time in 40 years that the city program had bought an entire space; the first was the Chocolate Factory theater.
- New York State Council on the Arts: $115,000, reported on 19 December 2022 (Queens Post, Christian Murray, "More Than 40 Arts Organizations in Western Queens Awarded State Funding").
- Andy Warhol Foundation: two years of program support totalling $60,000, for projects including the 2014 exhibitions and events; its third multi-year grant after awards for December 2006 to December 2008 and June 2010 to June 2012 (Flux Factory post "We're rich(ish)!").
- Bloomberg Philanthropies: Flux Factory's post "Flux Hearts Bloomberg Philanthropies" reports a $15,000 grant through its Arts Advancement Initiative, which gives unrestricted general operating funds for up to two years to small and mid-sized New York City arts organisations.
- Form 990 figures (ProPublica): revenue of $4,550,264 in 2021, $358,197 in 2022, $299,707 in 2023 and $1,501,765 in 2024, with contributions of $4,429,080, $302,535, $257,580 and $1,437,613. Net assets were $5,027,896 at the end of 2024.
- Flux sought $50,000 from donors in 2021 for startup costs of the new space (Hyperallergic). Its website lists an Annual Art Auction and Gala on 7 November 2026 at Flux IV.

BOTTOM LINE
Flux Factory's money is public and philanthropic: about $5 million in New York City capital funds bought its buildings in 2021, with smaller grants from the state arts council and the Warhol Foundation. It is an artist-led non-profit with revenue of $358,197 in 2022, $299,707 in 2023 and $1,501,765 in 2024.
