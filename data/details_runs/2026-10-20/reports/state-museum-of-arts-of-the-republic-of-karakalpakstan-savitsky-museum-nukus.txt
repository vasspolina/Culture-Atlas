STATE MUSEUM OF ARTS OF THE REPUBLIC OF KARAKALPAKSTAN (SAVITSKY MUSEUM): Nukus, Uzbekistan
ID: state-museum-of-arts-of-the-republic-of-karakalpakstan-savitsky-museum-nukus
Website: https://museum.kr.uz

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in Nukus, Karakalpakstan, holding close to 100,000 works from the mid-20th century on, including Russian avant-garde and local folk art (The Art Newspaper, 2025). Wikipedia gives more than 82,000 items.
- Established in 1966 at the initiative of Igor Savitsky, its first curator (Wikipedia).
- Named for Igor Savitsky (The Art Newspaper calls it the I.V. Savitsky State Art Museum); The Art Newspaper describes Savitsky as an "art-loving Soviet bureaucrat" who collected Modern art from Russia and Central Asia that was risky to collect in the Soviet Union.
- Wikipedia reports a new building in 2003 and two further buildings since. The exhibition space was renovated by the Italian architect Massimiliano Bigarello, who updated the layout, flooring and lighting (The Art Newspaper).
- The director is Gulbahar Izentaeva, appointed at the beginning of 2025 (The Art Newspaper's 2019 article called her director since 2017). She reported 69,966 visitors since January 2025, 26% of them foreigners (The Art Newspaper).
- In the 1990s the international Friends of the Nukus Museum raised funds and awareness (The Art Newspaper, 2025); the Dutch-registered charity wound down in March 2019 (The Art Newspaper, 2019).

FUNDING
Savitsky Museum (Nukus): state museum; the government-backed Art and Culture Development Foundation (ACDF) pays for specialists, restoration, renovation and digitisation
- ACDF: a foundation set up in 2017 by decree of President Shavkat Mirziyoyev that operates under the Cabinet of Ministers (Sharjah Architecture Triennial). The Art Newspaper (2025) calls it a government-backed Uzbek body that promotes the country's cultural heritage internationally.
- What ACDF pays for: Izentaeva said ACDF gives the museum no money directly. It pays for specialists and restorers, funded the renovation of the new premises and hired a private company to take over digitisation, with 3,000 works catalogued so far (The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- ALIPH: a US$30,000 project listed for 2026, funded by the European Union, for climate control systems and specialised tools for the museum's restoration lab (ALIPH).
- Gayane Umerova became ACDF executive director in 2020 (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critical piece is a 2019 report on the state's handling of the museum's directorship.
1. The Art Newspaper: George Nelson, "Uzbekistan's troubled Nukus Museum embroiled in new row" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/05/08/uzbekistans-troubled-nukus-museum-embroiled-in-new-row
- ACDF, then described as run by the culture ministry, advertised an open call for a new director in February 2019. Director Izentaeva said she learned of it only from her deputy, and ACDF officials Timur Kamalov and Gayane Umerova said they were unhappy with her performance but gave no details.
- The former director Marinika Babanazarova, dismissed by the Uzbek government in 2015, said officials "wanted their own people in the museum" and that paintings had been damaged since she left. She denied the 2015 allegations that she stole paintings and replaced them with fakes.
- David Pearce, former chairman of the Friends of Nukus Museum, said gifts worth up to $15,000, including books and audio guide equipment, disappeared after Babanazarova left. He said the Friends wrote to the ministry and "nothing has happened". Kamalov rejected talk of moving the collection to Tashkent and said it belongs in Nukus.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hyperallergic (Nevdon Jamgochian, "The Bukhara Biennial Will Break Your Heart", 27 October 2025) calls the Bukhara Biennial, supported by the state-funded Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation, a case of artwashing and says its existence "depends on a restrictive autocratic structure". The piece does not name the Nukus museum.
- The Spectator Australia (Alex Diggins, "Am I a useful idiot visiting Uzbekistan's first art biennial?", 3 January 2026) quotes an unnamed artist who said the biennial had "art-washy vibes"; the author travelled on a trip sponsored by ACDF, which the piece calls the show's organisers. The piece does not name the Nukus museum.
- Connection: ACDF supports the Bukhara Biennial (Hyperallergic) and organises it (The Spectator Australia), and is the body that paid for the Savitsky Museum's specialists, restorers and renovation (The Art Newspaper, 2025).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Uzbek state: ACDF, a foundation created by presidential decree, pays for its specialists, restorers, renovation and digitisation, and a small EU-funded ALIPH grant supports its restoration lab. The museum's management was disputed in 2019, when ACDF advertised for a new director and its former director alleged that officials wanted their own people in post. ACDF's wider cultural programme draws artwashing criticism in the art press.
