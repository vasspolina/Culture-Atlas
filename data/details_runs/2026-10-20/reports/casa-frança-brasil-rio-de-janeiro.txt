CASA FRANÇA-BRASIL: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
ID: casa-frança-brasil-rio-de-janeiro
Website: https://casabrasil.rj.gov.br/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre of the Rio de Janeiro state government, under the Secretaria de Estado de Cultura e Economia Criativa, at Rua Visconde de Itaboraí 78 in Centro.
- Opened as Casa França-Brasil on 29 March 1990 from a multi-use project by French museologist Pierre Castel; state culture secretary Darcy Ribeiro conceived the redevelopment in 1983 (Wikipedia). It took the name Casa Brasil in 2025 after a curatorial repositioning (stated by the space).
- The building is the city's first Praça do Comércio, designed by Grandjean de Montigny of the 1816 French Artistic Mission for King João VI and opened on 13 May 1820; it became the customs house in 1824 and housed the 2nd Tribunal do Júri from 1956 to 1978 (Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions, an artist residency and education courses for artists, curators and cultural mediators; director Tania Queiroz and general coordinator Jocelino Pessoa (stated by the space).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, with free entry (Super Rádio Tupi, April 2026). No accessibility information was found.

FUNDING
Casa França-Brasil (Casa Brasil): mainly public, with Petrobras as official sponsor through Lei Rouanet
- The Rio de Janeiro state government owns and runs the centre.
- Petrobras is the official sponsor. The restructuring project was selected in Petrobras's Novos Eixos call, in the line Ícones da Cultura Brasileira, and is carried out by the organisation V ARTE with the federal Ministry of Culture through the Lei Rouanet (stated by the space; Super Rádio Tupi).
- The Novos Eixos call, announced on 23 February 2024, set aside 250 million reais of Petrobras money for culture (Bahia Notícias).
- The Fundação Roberto Marinho was one of several bodies that supported the restoration (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the amount Petrobras pays Casa Brasil.
- No reporting found of a sponsor that dropped the centre under pressure.

Red flags: Petrobras oil money
- Petrobras, the Brazilian oil company, is the current official sponsor (stated by the space).
- Petrobras plans nearly 100 billion US dollars of fossil fuel investment and planned about 3 billion dollars of drilling off northeastern Brazil, where Ibama approved its plan in May 2025 despite its own technical evaluation finding it insufficient (DeSmog with Agência Pública, September 2025).
- Critics accused Petrobras of using paid climate and science influencers to improve its image; Petrobras called the accusations unfounded (DeSmog).
- No reporting found of a protest aimed at Casa Brasil over Petrobras.

Red flags: official closure of an exhibition, 2019
- Rio's culture secretariat closed the exhibition "Literatura exposta" early in January 2019, before a performance by the collective És Uma Maluca in which two naked women read a text on torture under the military dictatorship (ArtReview).
- Culture secretary Ruan Lira cited a breach of contract; curator Alvaro Figueiredo called it censorship and said Governor Wilson Witzel had asked for the closure (ArtReview).
- The director had earlier advised removing a recording of Jair Bolsonaro's speeches from an installation; the artists staged the performance outside the building the next evening (ArtReview).

Not related: Casa Brasil at the Paris 2024 Olympics. It was a fan space at Parc de la Villette run by the Brazilian Olympic Committee with Embratur and Sebrae, and has nothing to do with the Rio cultural centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the centre in art media; the one piece found concerns the 2019 state closure of an exhibition.
1. ArtReview: staff, "Exhibition at Casa França-Brasil in Rio de Janeiro closed down" (2019)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-14-jan-2019-casa-franca-brasil-rio-de-janeiro-censored/
- Reports the early closure of "Literatura exposta" and the blocked performance by És Uma Maluca.
- Names culture secretary Ruan Lira, Governor Wilson Witzel and curator Alvaro Figueiredo, and places the closure next to Bolsonaro's abolition of the federal culture ministry.
- The link to the governor rests on the curator's claim.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is a state venue whose current programme runs on Petrobras sponsorship through the Lei Rouanet, so its main outside money is oil money from a company expanding drilling at the mouth of the Amazon. The other weak point is the 2019 official closure of an exhibition, which artists and the curator called censorship. Worth watching: the size and term of the Petrobras sponsorship, and any campaign that names Casa Brasil.
