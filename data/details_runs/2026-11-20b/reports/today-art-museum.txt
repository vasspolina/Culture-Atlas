TODAY ART MUSEUM: Beijing, China
ID: today-art-museum
Website: http://www.todayartmuseum.com/ (checked 2026-11-20; timed out)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum (今日美术馆) at Building 4, Pingguo (Pingod) Community, 32 Baiziwan Road, Chaoyang District, near the Beijing CBD (Visit Beijing).
- The property developer Zhang Baoquan (张宝全) founded it in 2002 (Beijing Daily, 10 May 2012); it calls itself the first non-profit, non-governmental art museum in China (Visit Beijing).
- Architect Wang Hui led the design and rebuilding of the main building, which fuses industrial relics with new work; the complex covers 4,000 square metres and the main building 1,400, with Wang Jianwei's work "Viewing the Exhibition" on its roof (Visit Beijing).
- By 2012 it held more than 5,000 works in two stores, one of about 600 square metres on site and one of 2,000 square metres in north-west Beijing (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- Zhang Ran stepped down as director on 26 March 2025 at the end of a five-year term; deputy director Yan Yan (晏燕) became executive director, and the board, chaired by Zhang Baoquan, planned a worldwide search for a new director (Southern Metropolis Daily, 13 March 2025). Yan was still executive director in November 2026 (Beijing Daily, 5 November 2026).
- From December 2025 to 1 March 2026 it hosted one of five sections of the 13th "From Lausanne to Beijing" International Fiber Art Biennale (China News Service, via Tsinghua University).
- Visiting: open 10am to 6pm, closed during the Spring Festival; free on the first Saturday of each month and on 18 May (Visit Beijing).

FUNDING
Today Art Museum: private non-profit: founded on a property developer's money, now run on sponsorship and earned income
- For its first five years Zhang Baoquan kept the museum running with an average of about 10 million yuan a year of his own money (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- In July 2006 it became the first private museum registered with the Beijing Civil Affairs Bureau as a private non-enterprise unit (民办非企业单位); if it closed, its collection would pass to the bureau, not back to its investors (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- Sponsorship reached 30 million yuan in 2010, and sponsorship plus shop and other income reached 40 million yuan a year by 2012; most exhibitions had a sponsor, and joint-venture companies were the readiest sponsors (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- In 2006, after the artist Fang Lijun showed new work there, the then director Zhang Zikang won sponsorship from the chairman of the German company Montblanc (Beijing Daily, 2012).
- By 2012 it ran eight funds, among them building, exhibition, education and collection funds, and sought money from members and donors (Beijing Daily, 2012).

BOTTOM LINE
Private money with no flagged funder on record. Who sponsors it now is the gap to watch.
