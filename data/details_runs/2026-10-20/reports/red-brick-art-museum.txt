OVERVIEW
- Private art museum in the No. 1 International Art District, Chaoyang District, in north-east Beijing (Visit Beijing, Our China Story).
- Founded by the collector and entrepreneur Yan Shijie (阎士杰) and his wife Cao Mei; planning began in 2007 and the museum opened in May 2014 (Our China Story, Chinese edition).
- Architect: Dong Yugan, a professor at the Architecture Research Center of Peking University, who designed the garden-style site (Our China Story, ArchDaily).
- Building and site: the site covers about 20,000 square metres, with exhibition space of nearly 10,000 square metres (Visit Beijing). The grey bricks come from local village houses and the red bricks are associated with factory buildings (Our China Story, English edition).
- On site: nine exhibition spaces, two public leisure spaces, a children's activity room, a multipurpose hall and artist residency studios (Our China Story).
- Visiting: closed Mondays except on holidays; 10:00 to 17:30 from 1 October to 30 April and 10:00 to 18:00 from 1 May to 30 September (Our China Story). No reporting found of the admission price or accessibility arrangements.

FUNDING
Red Brick Art Museum: self-funded: the founding couple's private money
- The founders invested nearly RMB 400 million over eight years, and the museum opened in May 2014 (Our China Story, Chinese edition: "投资近4亿人民币，历时8年", our translation). No independent figure found.
- Founder Yan is described only as a collector and entrepreneur; no reporting found of his company or the source of his wealth.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, state funder, donor list or board.
- No reporting found of published accounts or annual reports.

Red flags: 2017 protest at an opening over a dead employee
- On 30 December 2016 Liu Yukuan (刘彧宽), the museum's public relations manager, died suddenly. On 27 May 2017 his parents came to the opening of an exhibition of French contemporary art at the museum, holding his portrait and handing out leaflets. More than ten museum staff removed them and forced them into two cars (The Paper, commentary by Gu Yizhi, 4 June 2017).
- The family said the museum had not paid Liu's last month of wages and that his death was from overwork. Unverified: the unpaid wages and overwork claims, made by the family as reported by The Paper; the museum denies the wage claim.
- The museum's statement called the unpaid wages claim false, but admitted that "lack of experience" in handling the day had hurt the feelings of Liu's parents, and apologised (The Paper, our translation of "经验不足").
- Photos and accounts reported bruises and abrasions on the parents' hands and bodies. The Paper said the dispute ended with no agreed settlement. No reporting found of a later outcome.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little. No review in the art press criticising the museum was found. The one critical piece is a commentary on a news site, not the art press, by a former museum professional, and it concerns the museum's conduct at the 2017 protest, not its art or funding.
1. The Paper (澎湃新闻): Gu Yizhi, "开幕式成'打戏',美术馆建设的'道'依然崎岖" ("The opening becomes a brawl, the road of museum building is still rough", our translation) (2017)

- Argues that comforting the family should have come first and that museums have a humanitarian duty to do so.
- Says that without a neutral mediating mechanism, compensation talks were bound to fail, and that financial difficulty is no excuse for neglecting care for workers.
- Says the museum's poor crisis response damaged its reputation and that professionalism and foresight are lacking across the sector. Names Red Brick Art Museum and the dead employee's parents. The page describes the author as a former media director of two private museums, in Shanghai and Beijing.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the founders' own, with no outside funder reported, but no figures or accounts are published beyond the stated RMB 400 million building cost. The weak points are the unpublished source of the founder's wealth and the 2017 episode in which staff removed a dead employee's parents from an opening, with a wage dispute the museum denied. Worth watching: any funder, accounts or board the museum later publishes.
