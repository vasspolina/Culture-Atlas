TEATRO NACIONAL DE CUBA: Havana, Cuba
ID: teatro-nacional-de-cuba
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Theatre complex on the Plaza de la Revolución, Avenida Paseo, Vedado, Havana (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Construction was approved in 1951 under President Carlos Prío Socarrás and the first stone was laid on 29 July 1952 (Spanish Wikipedia); Havana Times says Fulgencio Batista laid it, to a design by architect Julio Conesa.
- The original plan took Radio City Music Hall in New York as its model and called for three halls (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The Sala Covarrubias opened in 1960 but stopped staging shows in March 1961 because of its condition; the complex was inaugurated on 3 September 1979 with a gala for the Sixth Non-Aligned Movement summit (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Two halls: the Sala Avellaneda, named for the writer Gertrudis Gómez de Avellaneda, and the smaller Sala Covarrubias, named for the actor Francisco Covarrubias (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Seats: the Wikipedia data box gives 2,254 for Avellaneda and 805 for Covarrubias, and Havana Times also gives 2,254 for Avellaneda; the Wikipedia text gives 3,500 combined.
- Sculptures by Rita Longa, Roberto Estopiñán, Alfredo Lozano and Tomás Oliva, and murals by Raúl Martínez and René Portocarrero, were placed there in 1959 and 1960 (Havana Times, 2012).
- The Ballet Nacional de Cuba staged The Nutcracker in the Sala Avellaneda in December 2024 and January 2025 (the company's archived site).
- On 19 June 2026 the Ballet Nacional cancelled two performances of Giselle set for 27 and 28 June because the theatre's air conditioning had broken down and could not be repaired in time (CiberCuba, citing Cubadebate).
- The closing gala of Cubadisco 2026, set for the Sala Covarrubias on 24 May, was suspended for "razones ajenas a nuestra voluntad" (reasons beyond its control, my translation), the theatre said, and moved to the Pabellón Cuba (Directorio Cubano).

FUNDING
Teatro Nacional de Cuba: state-funded: the Ministry of Culture through the Consejo Nacional de las Artes Escénicas
- EcuRed lists the theatre among the institutions of the Consejo Nacional de las Artes Escénicas, one of the bodies created in 1989 within the Ministry of Culture's system.
- Construction: two months after the first stone, contracts went to Purdy & Henderson as civil contractor and Arroyo y Menéndez as technical director of the works (Spanish Wikipedia).
- 2012 renovation of the Sala Avellaneda, begun more than a year earlier: all glass in the façade of more than 1,000 square metres and the outdoor lighting replaced, the marble polished, and, according to a worker Havana Times quoted, all seats replaced.
- In March 2023 Igor Corcuera Cáceres, director of the National Concert Band, said the theatre's staff "don't have the budget or resources" to fix the leaks and that it needed investment from higher up (Havana Times, from 14ymedio).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two Havana Times reports cover the building: musicians criticising the state's upkeep in 2023, and the 2012 renovation.
1. Havana Times: Juan Diego Rodríguez (14ymedio), "Electrocution Risk from Leaks at Havana's National Theater" (2023)
URL: https://havanatimes.org/features/electrocution-risk-from-leaks-at-havanas-national-theater/
- Reports leaks that flooded the stage of the Sala Avellaneda during a Ballet Nacional performance in March 2023, with water near electrical equipment; the show went on.
- Musicians quoted say the building is overrun with termites, the Sala Covarrubias has bats and termites, and the National Symphony Orchestra's only harp is at risk; the flutist Alberto Rosas blames the "corruption of a failed system".
- The bassoonist Osmany Hernández says he was ordered not to reveal the complaints on social media; the article does not say who gave the order.
- 14ymedio saw a worker on the roof afterwards.
2. Havana Times: Isbel Díaz Torres, "Cuba's National Theater Completely Restored" (2012)
URL: https://havanatimes.org/features/cubas-national-theater-completely-restored/
- Reports the renovation before the 23rd Havana ballet festival; the critic Elvis Fuentes calls Osneldo García's removed kinetic sculpture anachronistic where it stood and says no account of the project was made.
- Criticises a placement decision, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
A state theatre paid for by the Ministry of Culture, with no private sponsor. The weak point is upkeep: leaks and termites in 2023 and broken air conditioning in 2026.
