ART SONJE CENTER: Seoul, South Korea
ID: art-sonje-center
Website: https://artsonje.org

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre at 87 Yulgok-ro 3-gil, Jongno-gu, Seoul (the centre's own site). Wikipedia places it in Samcheong-dong.
- Founded and chaired by Heeja K. Chung (Chung Hee-ja), whom the centre's site calls "a pioneering female business leader and a distinguished patron of the arts and culture in Korea"; opened in 1998. It grew out of the "Sprout" exhibition programme held in 1995 in a hanok house and a Western-style house on the site.
- The centre says Chung founded it to honour her love of art and to remember her late son, Kim Son-je. The centre's site says Chung also founded the Sonje Museum of Art in Gyeongju in 1991.
- Building: three floors and a basement on a street corner that was previously a traditional hanok home, designed by the architect Kim Jong-sung (the centre's facilities page).
- On site: exhibition spaces of 174 to 466 square metres, a learning space, a 238-seat basement Art Hall with a proscenium stage and a digital cinema projector, an inner hanok-style garden and a rooftop garden. The centre's programme covers exhibitions, screenings, performances, publications, new productions and research.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00; closed Monday. Tickets are sold through a Naver booking page.

FUNDING
Art Sonje Center: private non-profit, stated by the centre to be an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's arts work
- The centre states that it was founded as an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's social-sharing work in the arts (artsonje.org, About).
- The Daewoo Foundation page on artsonje.org says the foundation's projects "have been primarily funded by the late Daewoo Group chairperson Kim Woo Choong since 1978". Chung married Kim in 1964; he died in 2019 (artsonje.org, Founder page).

Not related: the Wooyang Museum of Art in Gyeongju. It was the Art Sonje Museum, run by Daewoo Development; Wooyang Industrial Development bought Daewoo Development at auction in 2012 and renamed it. The Seoul centre kept the Art Sonje name (USA Art News). Chung sought the return of 188 artworks from Wooyang; the court ordered three of them returned to her (USA Art News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review praises a show at the centre and has one reservation about it.
1. Art & Market: Ian Tee, "Editor's Notes: Exhibitions in Seoul" (2026)
URL: https://www.artandmarket.net/exhibition/2026/04/23/editors-notes-exhibitions-in-seoul
- Praises "Spectrosynthesis Seoul" at the centre as an "unapologetically queer" show and credits the curators for including many Korean artists.
- Says the show needed "more breathing room" for the nuances among works and contexts to come through.
- Criticises the show, not the institution.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Kim Woo-choong, founder and former chairman of Daewoo, was sentenced on 30 May 2006 by the Seoul Central District Court to 10 years in prison, forfeiture of more than 21 trillion won and a fine of 10 million won. The court found accounting fraud of over 20 trillion won, 9.8 trillion won of illegal financing, 19 trillion won diverted abroad and $100 million embezzled (AP via NBC News).
- Daewoo collapsed under massive debts in the wake of the 1997 to 1998 Asian financial crisis (AP via NBC News).
- Connection to the centre: the centre says it was founded as an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's commitment to social sharing practices in the arts, and the foundation's page on the centre's site says its projects have been primarily funded by Kim since 1978. Chung, the centre's founder, married Kim in 1964.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is a family-founded private non-profit that says it was founded as an extension of the Daewoo Foundation's arts work; the foundation says Kim Woo-choong primarily funded its projects from 1978. Kim was convicted of fraud and embezzlement in 2006, a case that belongs to the founder personally.
