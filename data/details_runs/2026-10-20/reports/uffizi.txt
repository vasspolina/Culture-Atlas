UFFIZI: Florence, Italy
ID: uffizi
Website: https://www.uffizi.it (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Florence; the Uffizi Galleries also run the Pitti Palace and the Boboli Gardens (Uffizi site).
- Giorgio Vasari began the building in 1560 for Cosimo I de' Medici to house the city's administrative offices, hence "uffizi" ("offices"); it was completed in 1581 (Wikipedia).
- The Medici collections passed to the city under the family pact negotiated by Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici; the gallery opened to the public in 1769 (Wikipedia).
- Simone Verde has been director since January 2024; the museum had about 5.25 million visitors in 2024 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Uffizi ticket costs 25 euros on the day and 29 euros if booked ahead, with a 2 euro reduced ticket; entry is free on the first Sunday of each month (Uffizi site).

FUNDING
Uffizi: state museum with private donors
- Public body: the Uffizi site carries the Ministry of Culture logo linked to cultura.gov.it. No reporting found of the state budget line or the museum's accounts.
- Veronica Atkins: 4.5 million euros (about 4.8 million dollars) to restore the Boboli Gardens amphitheatre, announced 10 March 2023, described as the largest donation by a private individual to a Florentine museum. Finestre sull'Arte names her as president of the Dr. Robert C. and Veronica Atkins Foundation, and says earlier gifts through the Friends of the Uffizi Galleries paid for the Terrazzo of Maps and the Valois tapestries (the Uffizi donation page gives the amount without a year).
- Edwin L. Wiegand Foundation, Reno, Nevada: 1 million dollars for the new layout of the Vasari Corridor, announced 11 October 2023 (Finestre sull'Arte; the Uffizi donation page gives the amount without a year).
- Ferragamo: a donation of 600,000 euros in 2014 to re-hang eight rooms of the Third Corridor, described by the superintendent as a "generosa erogazione liberale", not a sponsorship (Artribune, 12 May 2014).
- Fondazione CR Firenze: partner in the "Uffizi Diffusi" programme of exhibitions in small Tuscan museums, announced 17 December 2024; the page gives no amounts (Uffizi site).
- The Uffizi's donation page names Atkins and the Wiegand Foundation and lists gifts of artworks, with no years for most entries; it does not name Ferragamo or Fondazione CR Firenze.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 5 April 2026: workers of Florence's museums, with the union Sudd Cobas, held a banner reading "Basta vite precarie" in front of Botticelli's Venus inside the Uffizi. They asked for the stabilisation of long-serving temporary staff and no new hires on on-call contracts, and the union denounced the "sistema malato degli appalti" (the sick contracting system, our translation) (Open, 6 April 2026, Bruno Gaetani; L'Espresso, 6 April 2026, Valentina Romagnoli). Neither piece names a funder as a target, and Open reports no response from the Uffizi.
- Unverified: L'Espresso writes that the long-serving temporary workers have been "senza salario da ottobre" (without pay since October); Open does not report this and neither piece names who made the claim. The Uffizi's statement quoted in L'Espresso concerns a separate hacking incident.
- 22 July 2022 and 13 February 2024: Ultima Generazione activists glued themselves, and then attached images, to the glass over Botticelli's Primavera and Birth of Venus; neither painting was damaged (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Uffizi's funders found in art media. The pieces found concern its marketing and a display.

- Social media users mocked the new circular case for the Tondo Doni, comparing it to a washing machine and a loudspeaker woofer; the director Eike Schmidt answered with humour.
- Criticises the display, not the institution's funding; no critic is named.
2. Linkiesta: Guia Soncini, "Ferragni agli Uffizi e noi" (20 July 2020; a newspaper column, not art press)
URL: https://www.linkiesta.it/2020/07/chiara-ferragni-uffizi-fedez/
- On the row over Chiara Ferragni being photographed before a Botticelli for Vogue Hong Kong: Soncini calls both the critics of the "commercialisation of culture" and the defenders mistaken.
- Reports that Tomaso Montanari criticised the 2017 Gucci show at the Uffizi, and that Christian Raimo criticised the Ferragni shoot.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Uffizi is public with named private gifts; the donors found (the Atkins foundation, the Wiegand Foundation, Ferragamo, Fondazione CR Firenze) show no flagged-industry link in the pages read. The weak points are that the museum posts few figures, the donation page carries few dates, and the state budget line was not found. Worth watching: the contract-worker dispute.
