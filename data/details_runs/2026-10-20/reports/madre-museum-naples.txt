MADRE (MUSEO D'ARTE CONTEMPORANEA DONNAREGINA): Naples, Italy
ID: madre-museum-naples
Website: https://www.madrenapoli.it

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at Via Luigi Settembrini 79, in Naples' historic centre (museum website; arte.it).
- Founded by the Campania Region; the Fondazione Donnaregina per le arti contemporanee runs it (arte.it).
- Named for the Palazzo Donnaregina, which takes its name from the 13th-century monastery of Santa Maria Donnaregina (arte.it).
- The Campania Region bought the building in 2005 and the Portuguese architect Álvaro Siza Vieira designed its restoration and conversion into a museum. It opened on 10 June 2005 with site-specific installations on the first floor, and the upper floors opened to the public by 2006 (arte.it).
- On site: permanent site-specific installations, temporary exhibitions, a library and media library, and a bookshop and café; the collection project began in 2013 (arte.it). The library and media library are closed to the public for now (museum website).
- Eva Elisa Fabbris was named director in January 2023, after candidates were evaluated by an examining committee and the board of directors chose her; she succeeded Kathryn Weir (Finestre sull'Arte, 21 January 2023).
- Open Monday and Wednesday to Saturday 10:00 to 19:30, Sunday 10:00 to 20:00; closed Tuesday. Free entry on the first Sunday of each month.

FUNDING
Madre: mainly public: the Campania Region
- At the end of 2021 public contributions totalled 3.4 million euros, mainly from the Campania Region. For 2022 the museum expected 4.7 million euros from the Region, covering operating costs and planned exhibitions. Operating costs in 2021 were 3.2 million euros (Il Sole 24 Ore, Maria Adelaide Marchesoni, 13 December 2022).
- The same article states that a dedicated fundraising activity had not yet been developed.
- The Region owns the building; the foundation's then president Pierpaolo Forte said in 2012 that further technical measures were planned to insulate the museum from regional political changes (Artribune, 12 December 2012).
- The foundation's president is Angela Tecce, who thanked the Campania Region for its support at Fabbris's appointment (Finestre sull'Arte, 21 January 2023). The foundation's board also includes Maria Letizia Magaldi as vice president and Achille Bonito Oliva as counsellor (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two items, both about money and governance rather than programme: the museum's dependence on public funding and debts it carried in 2012.
1. Il Sole 24 Ore: Maria Adelaide Marchesoni, "Cercasi nuovo direttore per il Madre, troppo dipendente dai contributi pubblici" (2022)
URL: https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/cercasi-nuovo-direttore-il-madre-troppo-dipendente-contributi-pubblici-AETJApOC
- The headline frames the museum as too dependent on public contributions: 3.4 million euros of public money at the end of 2021, mainly from the Campania Region.
- No dedicated fundraising had been developed. Names no critic; the concern sits in the framing.
2. Artribune: Giovanna Procaccini, "Fondazione Donnaregina e Madre. Da otto a uno" (2012), interview with Pierpaolo Forte
URL: https://www.artribune.com/?p=106831
- Forte says the museum had more than 8 million euros in debts when he arrived, reduced to just over 1 million, and that the foundation needs a clean balance sheet before approaching private-sector partners.
- The interviewer raises concerns about personalism in the museum's institutional relations; Forte answers that fixed terms for the president and the director keep the museum from becoming a matter of personal control. He presents the Progetto XXI collaboration with the Fondazione Morra Greco as a no-cost exploration of younger art, to which Morra Greco contributes material and intellectual resources.

BOTTOM LINE
Madre is public money: the Campania Region founded it, owns its building and supplied the bulk of a 3.4 million euro public contribution in 2021.
