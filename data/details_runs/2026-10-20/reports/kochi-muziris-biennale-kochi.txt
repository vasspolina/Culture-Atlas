KOCHI-MUZIRIS BIENNALE: Kochi, India
ID: kochi-muziris-biennale-kochi
Website: https://www.kochimuzirisbiennale.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- International exhibition of contemporary art held every two years in Fort Kochi and Mattancherry, Kochi, Kerala, with talks, film programmes, workshops and residencies (stated by the Biennale).
- The Kochi Biennale Foundation was set up in 2010 by the artists Bose Krishnamachari and Riyas Komu after Kerala's culture minister approached them; the first edition opened in December 2012 (Wikipedia).
- The sixth edition, "For the Time Being", curated by Nikhil Chopra with HH Art Spaces, ran from 12 December 2025 to 31 March 2026 (Kerala Chief Minister's announcement via The Week; e-flux). The seventh, in 2027 to 2028, is curated by Kader Attia (stated by the Biennale).
- Leadership: Krishnamachari, President of the Biennale and a trustee of the Foundation, resigned in January 2026, citing family reasons; chairperson Venu Vasudevan said he could not give further details (Onmanorama, 14 January 2026). The Biennale's site now lists Jitish Kallat as President. The Week's 2024 report named V Venu as the new Foundation chairperson.
- No reporting found of ticket prices, opening days or accessibility from a page opened.

FUNDING
Kochi-Muziris Biennale: mixed: Kerala state grants, private patrons and cultural institutes; recent amounts unpublished
- Kerala government: Rs 5 crore released in December 2010 and a further Rs 4 crore sanctioned in September 2011 (Madhyamam, 2015). Wikipedia says the state's share fell from Rs 9 crore in the first edition to Rs 3 crore in the second, and that Rs 7.5 crore was announced in 2016. No reporting found of a state grant for 2025 to 2026; the Chief Minister announced the edition without figures (The Week).
- 2025 to 2026: philanthropist Minal Bajaj, trustee and Honorary Director of the Jamnalal Bajaj Foundation since 2009, is a Platinum Patron of the sixth edition; the Biennale's own news item of 11 December 2025 gives no amount.
- Earlier private funding: the Feroze Gujral Foundation, the Goethe Institute and embassies (Open Magazine); in 2018 LuLu Financial Group's Adeeb Ahamed pledged Rs 1 crore (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of annual accounts or a current patron list beyond Bajaj.

Red flags: protest by artists, 2022
- On 23 December 2022, 53 invited artists, collectives and one residency published an open letter on e-flux after the opening was postponed the night before 12 December. It cited "opaque financial planning and last-minute fundraising" and missing contracts, and asked the Board to review and overhaul the Foundation's management (e-flux). The News Minute counted 47 artists and six collectives.
- The Foundation went ahead with the inauguration on 12 December and opened the main exhibition on 23 December (The News Minute).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is mostly about finances and management, from artists and the Indian press, not from art critics on the work.
1. e-flux Notes: "Open letter from the artists of the Kochi-Muziris Biennale 2022-23" (2022)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/510681/open-letter-from-the-artists-of-the-kochi-muziris-biennale-2022-23
- Chaotic funding, contracts and financial planning despite an edition two years late, with forty new commissions announced at the same time.
- Warns that artists and production staff bore an unreasonable burden; calls for "a complete reform".
2. Scroll.in: report on the open letter (2022)
URL: https://scroll.in/latest/1040463/open-letter-kochi-muziris-biennale-artists-flag-organisational-challenges-that-led-to-postponement
- Reports that less than ten percent of the show was ready the day before opening; the letter says management ignored many concerns.
- Krishnamachari called the event "very challenging on several fronts" and said processes were being improved. A news report, not criticism by a critic.
3. Open: Georgina Maddox, "The Show Goes On" (page last updated 2019, on the first edition)
URL: https://openthemagazine.com/art-culture/the-show-goes-on
- Reports that the state froze further payments after a Financial Inspection Wing report flagged irregularities in how the funds were disbursed, and a vigilance inquiry opened; the government withdrew its nominated representatives.
- Unverified: that the Rs 5 crore state grant was misused, from critics led by the sculptor Kanayi Kunhiraman (Open).
- Names Krishnamachari and Komu; Krishnamachari said a committee including Union and state officials had approved the Rs 73 crore budget, and Komu welcomed the inquiry.
4. Madhyamam: "CAG raps Kochi Biennale Foundation for unjustified fund use" (2015)
URL: https://madhyamamonline.com/kerala/2015/jun/30/cag-raps-kochi-biennale-foundation-unjustified-fund-use
- India's auditor flagged Rs 38.03 lakh spent on the Foundation's Mumbai office as unrelated to hosting the Biennale and called the tourism department's defence "not tenable". A news report.

BOTTOM LINE
No flagged industry funder was found behind the Biennale. The weak points are the finances: the 2015 audit finding, the 2010s dispute with the state, and the 2022 artists' letter on opaque planning, with no accounts published now. Worth watching: how the Foundation is run after the founder's January 2026 resignation, and whether patrons beyond Minal Bajaj are named.
