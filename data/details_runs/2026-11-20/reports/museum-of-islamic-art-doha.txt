MUSEUM OF ISLAMIC ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: museum-of-islamic-art-doha
Website: https://mia.org.qa (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Islamic art on an artificial peninsula about 60 metres off the Doha Corniche, in a park of roughly 290,000 square metres (Wikipedia).
- One of the first projects initiated by Qatar Museums (stated by the museum). The then-emir, Sheikh Hamad, opened it on 22 November 2008 and the public was admitted from 8 December 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Designed by I. M. Pei after the fountain of the Ibn Tulun Mosque in Cairo; built by the Turkish firm Baytur Construction (Wikipedia).
- The collection runs from the 7th to the 20th centuries and comes from three continents, from Spain to China (stated by the museum).
- Reopened on 5 October 2022 after a rehang that put more than 1,000 objects on show for the first time and added a restored 19th-century Damascus Room (The Art Newspaper, 31 August 2022).
- Director Shaika Nasser Al-Nassr, at the museum since 2007, succeeded Julia Gonnella, director from 2017, who moved to the Lusail Museum (Artforum).
- On site: a library of over 21,000 books (Qatar Living), a cafe and Alain Ducasse's restaurant IDAM (museum site).
- 2026 exhibitions: "Empire of Light: Visions and Voices from Afghanistan", until 30 May (The Art Newspaper), and "Uzbekistan: Heritage in Motion", 146 objects, until 28 November (The Peninsula).
- Visiting: Saturday to Tuesday 9am to 7pm, Thursday 9am to 9pm, Friday 1:30pm to 7pm, closed Wednesday (museum site). Non-resident adults pay QAR 50; Qatar residents with ID and children aged 16 and under enter free (Qatar Living, July 2026).

FUNDING
Museum of Islamic Art: state-owned: Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity; no private donor or sponsor named
- The museum is overseen by Qatar Museums, which Wikipedia describes as "a Qatari government entity" founded in 2005 and formerly called the Qatar Museums Authority.
- In early 2016 the emir approved cuts to culture and other budgets. Citing the Financial Times, The Art Newspaper reported that Qatar Museums let 240 staff go; two unnamed people close to the situation said the museum's own costs were ringfenced. Qatar Museums declined to confirm the figures or comment on the budget (The Art Newspaper, Georgina Adam, 10 March 2016).
- The Aga Khan Trust for Culture handled most of the administrative work for the 2026 Afghanistan exhibition (The Art Newspaper, 6 February 2026).
- Qatar Museums opened the 2026 Uzbekistan exhibition with the Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation, "in partnership with Visit Qatar" (The Peninsula, 3 September 2026).

Red flags: Gulf state body: run by Qatar Museums since the 2008 opening
- Qatar Museums works under the patronage of the Amir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, and is chaired by Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site), the emir's sister (openDemocracy).
- The Art Newspaper described the 2022 overhaul as one of the cultural initiatives "designed to boost Qatar's soft power status", timed for the Fifa World Cup.

Red flags: 2005 investigation of Sheikh Saud, who built the collection
- Sheikh Saud bin Muhammed Al Thani, culture minister from 1997, was entrusted with creating the collections for Qatar's planned museums, a Museum of Islamic Art among them (Wikipedia). Bidoun writes that the museum's collection was "built up largely by" him.
- In 2005 he was investigated for misuse of public funds and dismissed (Wikipedia). Bidoun describes a fall "amid speculation of financial irregularities" that "left him under house arrest".
- Wikipedia adds that it was sometimes unclear whether collections he assembled belonged to him or to Qatar.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum as an institution. The critical pieces concern its displays and the state spending around it.
1. Bidoun: Antonia Carver, "Museum of Islamic Art" (2009)
URL: https://bidoun.org/articles/museum-of-islamic-art
- The galleries are "almost devoid of information"; labels are minimal.
- Objects are "imbued with power and meaning more through the aesthetics of display than for their place in history".
2. The Art Newspaper: Georgina Adam, "Football outscores culture as Qatar spending slumps" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/10/football-outscores-culture-as-qatar-spending-slumps
- News report, not a critique of the museum. Its unnamed sources say staff sign strict confidentiality agreements.
- One source says Sheikh Tamim's cost-cutting "doesn't seem very structured or thought out".
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Reports criticism of how public money was used and where often unannounced acquisitions ended up. Does not criticise the Museum of Islamic Art.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013 social media users led by a Qatari journalist accused the Qatar Museums Authority of corruption, mismanagement and nepotism (openDemocracy).
- The same article reports social media objections to Damien Hirst's bronzes, estimated at $20m, and social media claims that Sheikha Mayassa stopped a film at Mathaf; officials cited technical issues.
- The museum is overseen by the body these accusations name.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on Qatari state money with no budget or accounts published, which makes it a Gulf state body. The weak points are that opacity and the record of the minister who built the collection. Worth watching: whether Qatar Museums publishes accounts or a sponsor list.
