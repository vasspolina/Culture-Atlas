ESPACIO CULTURAL PATIÑO: La Paz, Bolivia
ID: espacio-cultural-patino
Website: https://patino.org/en/places/espacio-simon-i-patino-a-la-paz/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre of the Patiño foundations in the Sopocachi neighbourhood of La Paz, in the Guayaquil building on Ecuador Street (Patiño foundation website).
- Created by the Simón I. Patiño Foundation, it opened on 14 September 1984 in the Alameda building on El Prado Avenue and moved permanently to Ecuador Street in 1996 (Patiño foundation website).
- Named for Simón I. Patiño, born 1 June 1860, died 20 April 1947 (Wikipedia).
- Building: it bought two houses facing the former Nunciature, one lived in by Baltazar Rodó and one by Julio Sanjinés Goitia, and built on the site to a design by the late Juan Carlos Calderón, with two large exhibition halls, a theatre and a terrace (Los Tiempos, 7 December 2018). The foundation says a further building on Ecuador Avenue is under construction, with interactive exhibition spaces and full accessibility.
- On site: exhibitions of contemporary art, photography and painting; CEDOAL, a documentation centre on Latin American arts and literatures founded in 2001; CAP, a pedagogical centre since 2008; C+C Espacio, a comic centre opened in 2003; and C-Musical, begun in 2013, which keeps an audio collection of Bolivian music and hosts concerts (Patiño foundation website).
- More than 35,000 people visit each year (Patiño foundation website). In La Paz the foundation also runs the Doña Albina theatre (2025 annual report).

FUNDING
Espacio Cultural Patiño: funded by the Patiño family foundations in Bolivia and Geneva
- Owner: the Fundación Universitaria Simón I. Patiño, created in Bolivia in 1931 by Simón I. Patiño, owns the centres in Bolivia (fundacionpatino.org).
- Money: in 1958 his heirs created the Fundación Simón I. Patiño in Geneva "para garantizar la sostenibilidad económica" (translation: to guarantee the economic sustainability) of the foundations' work. Its mission is to manage the funds, and the foundations call the centres ones "que hemos creado y que financiamos" (that we created and finance) (fundacionpatino.org).
- Who decides: at 31 December 2025 the Geneva foundation's nine-member board was chaired by Benoît Merkt, with Nicolas du Chastel as vice-chair, Elisabeth de Watteville as secretary and Felipe Ortiz-Patiño a member; Nicolas Joray was general director (2025 annual report).
- Donations: the foundation's site asks for donations, bequests and "allies", and credits "thousands of beneficiaries each year thanks to your donations" (patino.org).
- Reporting: the Geneva foundation posts activity reports for 2022 to 2025. The 2025 report counts over 45,000 visitors to its venues in Cochabamba, La Paz and Santa Cruz, more than 50 exhibitions and 140 cultural events, and 44 scholars.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Source of the fortune: in 1894 Patiño took a deed to land near Llallagua in settlement of a $250 debt, located a tin vein there in 1900 and built control of the Catavi, Siglo XX, Uncia and Huanuni mines; in the 1940s he controlled the international tin market. The 1952 Bolivian Revolution confiscated the Patiño Mines (Wikipedia, Simón Iturri Patiño).
- Catavi mine: Patiño acquired it in the 1900s. The camp kept its largely indigenous workforce "in rigidly segregated conditions", apart from the American managers, with housing, water, shops, transport and bathrooms divided (Wikipedia, Catavi mine).
- Catavi massacre, 21 December 1942: about 7,000 miners had struck for five days for a 100% wage rise, and the military fired on a marching crowd for six hours. The official report listed 19 dead and 400 wounded; workers' estimates reached 400 dead. Families lived in company huts without running water or toilets (Wikipedia, Catavi massacre). The Catavi mine page calls it "Patiño's Catavi mine".
- The centre belongs to foundations created by Patiño in 1931 and by his heirs in 1958, so the tin fortune is the origin of the money that pays for it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is family foundation money with no corporate, state or flagged funder today. The weak point is history: the Geneva endowment traces back to Patiño's tin mines, with their record of segregation and the 1942 Catavi massacre. Watch what the Geneva foundation discloses about its funds.
