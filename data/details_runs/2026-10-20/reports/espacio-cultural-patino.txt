ESPACIO CULTURAL PATIÑO: La Paz, Bolivia
ID: espacio-cultural-patino
Website: https://patino.org (checked 2026-10-24; live, in English, Spanish and French)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre of the Patiño foundations in the Sopocachi neighbourhood of La Paz, on Ecuador Street (Patiño foundation website).
- Opened on 14 September 1984 in the Alameda building on El Prado Avenue, created by the Simón I. Patiño Foundation; it moved permanently in 1996 to the Guayaquil building on Ecuador Street (Patiño foundation website).
- Named for Simón I. Patiño (1 June 1860 to 20 April 1947), who created the Fundación Universitaria Simón I. Patiño in 1931 (Wikipedia; Patiño foundation website).
- Building: it bought two houses in Sopocachi, formerly the Rodó family's and Julio Sanjinés Goitia's, and opened a new building designed by the late Juan Carlos Calderón with two large exhibition halls, a theatre and a terrace facing Illimani (Los Tiempos, 7 December 2018). The foundation says a further new building on Ecuador Avenue is under construction.
- On site: exhibitions of contemporary art and photography; CEDOAL, a documentation centre on Latin American arts and literature founded in 2001; CAP, a pedagogical centre since 2008; C+C Espacio, a comic centre opened in 2003; and C-Musical, begun in 2013, which preserves Bolivian audio recordings and hosts concerts (Patiño foundation website).
- Visiting: no reporting found of opening days, admission or accessibility on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Espacio Cultural Patiño: funded by the Patiño family foundations; the pages opened do not say where their money comes from today
- The Fundación Universitaria Simón I. Patiño was created in Bolivia in 1931 by Simón I. Patiño. In 1958 his heirs created the Fundación Simón I. Patiño in Geneva "to guarantee the economic sustainability" of the foundations' activities (translation); its mission is to manage the funds, and the foundations say they finance the centres they created in Bolivia (Patiño foundation website, Spanish page).
- No page opened states the source of the foundations' funds, income, donors or accounts. Neither the foundation pages nor the Wikipedia biography link the foundations' present money to the tin mines.
- No reporting found of a corporate or state funder of the La Paz centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The one press piece found, a Los Tiempos column by Lupe Cajías ("Espacio Patiño renovado", 7 December 2018), calls the building "magnífico" and the curation "impecable" (translation) and does not explain how the foundation is funded.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Historical, not current funding: Patiño acquired a mountain near Llallagua in 1894 in settlement of a debt, found a tin vein there in 1900 and bought the Catavi, Siglo XX, Uncia and Huanuni mines; by the 1940s he controlled the international tin market. The 1952 Bolivian revolution confiscated the Patiño Mines (Wikipedia, Simón I. Patiño).
- Catavi mine: Patiño acquired it in the 1900s. The largely indigenous workforce was kept in rigidly segregated conditions, away from the American managerial staff, with housing, water access, shops and toilets divided along those lines (Wikipedia, Catavi mine).
- Catavi massacre, 21 December 1942: 7,000 miners on a five-day strike sought a 100% wage rise, and the military fired for six hours. The official report counted 19 dead and 400 wounded; workers' estimates reached up to 400 dead. Families lived in company huts without running water or toilets. That page does not name the mine owner; the Catavi mine page names Patiño as the owner (Wikipedia, Catavi massacre).
- The Patiño biography on Wikipedia does not discuss labour conditions, and no statement by the foundations on this history was found.
- Connection: the centre belongs to foundations created by Patiño (1931) and his heirs (1958). No page opened shows that the centre's money today comes from the tin fortune.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is paid for by two family foundations; no corporate or state funder was found, and no page opened gives their income, donors or accounts. The weak point is that the origin of the funds is not stated, while the family's tin fortune has a documented history at Catavi (conditions, the 1942 massacre) that is kept here as background. Worth watching are the new building under construction and whether the Geneva foundation publishes financial detail.
