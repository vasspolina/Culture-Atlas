DOX CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Prague, Czechia
ID: dox-centre-for-contemporary-art-prague
Website: https://www.dox.cz

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in a converted factory in the Holešovice district of Prague (Poupětova 1, Praha 7), run by the company DOX Prague, a.s.
- Founded by Leoš Válka with his business partner Robert Aafjes; Válka bought the building in 2002 and DOX has operated as a non-governmental, non-profit, non-collecting institution since 2008 (DOX). Radio Prague dates the opening to late 2008.
- Building: a factory from 1911 converted by architect Ivan Kroupa. The Gulliver Airship, a 42-metre steel and wood structure, was designed by Martin Rajniš with Válka; the DOX+ multifunctional hall, used mainly for stage arts, was designed by Petr Hájek (DOX).
- On site: the DOX+ hall, the Gulliver Airship, a design shop, a bookstore and the Fine Art Archive. Programming spans art, literature, theatre and music, with education and family programmes (DOX).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m.

FUNDING
DOX Centre for Contemporary Art: mainly private: investor-owners topped up by a small City of Prague subsidy
- Private start: Válka had insufficient capital, so Václav Dejčmar, Libor Winkler and Karel Janeček joined as investors (DOX). In Radio Prague (Ian Willoughby, 19 March 2012) Válka described four backers putting 250 million crowns into the project, and the interviewer said Válka had put in 50 million crowns or more.
- Current shareholders: Václav Dejčmar, Karel Janeček and Libor Winkler, according to Echo24 (Petr Kaiser, 17 February 2019). DOX's own page lists Dejčmar, Winkler and Eduard Kučera as shareholders.
- Owners' businesses: Válka started a mid-sized residential construction and reconstruction business (DOX). Janeček co-founded the algorithmic trading company RSJ (English Wikipedia). Winkler is managing partner of the investment group RSJ, with interests in Ukrainian biomass heating plants, wineries and restaurants (Czech Wikipedia). Dejčmar is a co-owner of RSJ and other firms (Czech Wikipedia).
- Public money: DOX states it receives "financial support of the City of Prague". Radio Prague (2012) put the city grant at about 5 million crowns a year, roughly 13 to 14 percent of an operating budget of around 40 million crowns. Echo24 (2019) wrote that neither state nor city contributed significantly to the construction, and that contributions to operations and projects together make up less than one fifth of the budget. Novinky.cz (2017) cited a regular 7 million crowns a year from the previous grant period.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns the programme and the expertise of DOX's leadership, raised in 2017 when Prague's magistrate refused a multi-year grant.
1. Novinky.cz (Právo): Petr Kovář, "Když si DOX čte pod lavicí" (title: "When DOX reads under the desk", translated) (23 January 2017)
URL: https://www.novinky.cz/clanek/kultura-salon-petr-kovar-kdyz-si-dox-cte-pod-lavici-40021854
- The article reports that the Prague magistrate refused to grant DOX a multi-year grant; DOX could still apply for a one-year grant for 2018.
- Grant commission member Marek Pokorný said things were fine while Jaroslav Anděl led DOX, and cited the exhibition "Skvělý nový svět" as an example of one that stayed on the surface.
- The artist Pavel Sterec said in a video on Artyčok.tv that the 7 million crown annual grant is too large a share of the funds distributed to live arts.
- Válka rejected the criticism of professional inadequacy as "claim against claim" and called the last two years the best in DOX's history. Kovář wrote that DOX's position on the Czech gallery map is currently irreplaceable. The piece is from a national daily, not an art magazine.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind DOX is private: founder Leoš Válka and the investors Václav Dejčmar, Karel Janeček and Libor Winkler, plus a City of Prague subsidy that Radio Prague put at 13 to 14 percent of the budget in 2012 and Echo24 put, with state money, below one fifth in 2019. The documented dispute is the 2017 refusal of a multi-year city grant amid criticism of the programme and its leadership's expertise.
