RIJKSMUSEUM: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: rijksmuseum-amsterdam
Website: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- National art and history museum at Museumstraat 1, Amsterdam.
- Founded in The Hague on 19 November 1798 on the proposal of Isaac Gogel, opened as the National Art Gallery in 1800 and moved to Amsterdam in 1808 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Pierre Cuypers won the 1876 design contest and the building opened on 13 July 1885; the 2003 to 2013 renovation by Antonio Cruz and Antonio Ortiz cost 375 million euros (Wikipedia).
- On site: the main galleries, a restaurant, a research library, gardens with annual sculpture exhibitions, and a satellite branch at Schiphol airport.
- Visiting: open daily 9 to 17h; adults pay 25 euros, under-18s are free, CJP and EYCA holders pay 12.50 euros, Museumkaart and Friends holders enter free; book a time online. Wheelchair accessible, with lifts on all floors.

FUNDING
Rijksmuseum: mixed: ticket sales, state subsidy, and corporate and private partners (KPN, ING, VriendenLoterij, funds)
- The museum's website lists the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science as its founder. XR states that 2023 revenue was 123.3 million euros, of which 55.8 million came from tickets and 39 million from subsidies (NL Times, 7 September 2024); no figure from the museum was opened.
- Hart van Nederland (23 May 2023) states that the museum depends on partners, funds and private donations for a third of its income.
- ING: main sponsor since 2005 of the museum and Rijksmuseum Schiphol; it has funded an online programme since 2020 (stated by the museum). No amount is published. Fossielvrij NL's lawyer Bénédicte Ficq estimated ING's share at under 1% of the museum's funding (Banken.nl, 17 May 2024); XR put it at under 0.6% of income (NL Times, 7 September 2024).
- KPN: main sponsor since the 2013 reopening, giving annual funding plus telecom, data and ICT services (stated by the museum); no amount published.
- VriendenLoterij: partner since 1998; with three other museums it gives more than 8 million euros a year combined for acquisitions (stated by the museum).
- Anonymous donor: 12.5 million euros in 2023 for ten years of garden sculpture exhibitions, the museum's largest private monetary gift (Hart van Nederland).
- Project funders named by the museum include AkzoNobel, Ammodo, Bagri Foundation, Bank of America Foundation, Mellon Foundation, Getty Foundation, Mondriaan Fonds, Bloomberg Philanthropies, CHANEL and Pon.

Red flags: ING sponsorship and fossil fuel finance
- ING is a main sponsor of the Rijksmuseum and Rijksmuseum Schiphol and has worked with the museum since 2005 (museum's ING page, undated).
- Campaigners call ING the largest fossil-fuel financier in the Netherlands: Extinction Rebellion (XR) in a press release of 14 March 2024, and Fossielvrij NL, as reported by Banken.nl. Both are campaign groups.
- 16 February 2024: XR interrupted the opening of the Frans Hals exhibition (NL Times). 17 March 2024: XR demonstrated on Museumplein (XR press release).
- 17 May 2024: Fossielvrij NL announced it would hand the museum a petition, "Rijks zegt NEE", with more than 5,400 signatures (Banken.nl).
- 7 September 2024: XR chained itself to the fences of the tunnel leading to the entrance and the museum closed (NL Times). 1 March 2025: XR's "visitor strike" reserved tickets it did not use (NL Times).
- ING's reply: the economy still needs oil and gas; the activists' approach toward the museum is "unacceptable" (translation) and they should contact the bank directly (Banken.nl).

Red flags: Saudi Aramco, a Gulf state body
- Unverified: Fossil Free Culture Netherlands, an activist group, says the Rijksmuseum ended a partnership with Saudi Aramco in 2017 (Museums Association, 18 September 2020). The museum did not respond to the Museums Association, and no other source opened confirms the partnership.
- Saudi Aramco is a state-owned Saudi oil company (Wikipedia).
- The article says the group has held no protests at the museum; no source opened gives a reason for the end.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the funding in art media; the sponsorship protest was covered in Dutch general news. The one art-press piece found criticises how the museum treats its own history.
1. The Art Newspaper: Carine Zaayman, "The Big Review: Slavery at the Rijksmuseum" (2021)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2021/07/09/the-big-review-slavery-at-the-rijksmuseum
- Argues it largely ignores the Rijksmuseum's own role in maintaining imperialism, treating the institution as "made new, unsullied".
- Does not mention funders or sponsors.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's income is ticket sales, state subsidy and, for a third, partners, funds and private gifts (Hart van Nederland). The weak points are the ING sponsorship, which XR and Fossielvrij NL have protested since 2024, and an unverified activist claim that a Saudi Aramco partnership ended in 2017, which the museum has not answered. Watch whether ING stays a main partner.
