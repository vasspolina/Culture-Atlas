NTT INTERCOMMUNICATION CENTER (ICC): Tokyo, Japan
ID: ntt-intercommunication-center-icc-tokyo
Website: https://www.ntticc.or.jp/en/about/ (checked 2026-10-25; loaded; its visitor page returned 404, so visiting details come from a Tokyo Art Beat listing)

OVERVIEW
- Media art centre on the 4th floor of Tokyo Opera City Tower, 3-20-2 Nishishinjuku, Shinjuku, Tokyo; Hatsudai Station is about a 3-minute walk from the East exit (Tokyo Art Beat).
- A cultural facility run by NTT East Corp. (stated by ICC); it opened on 19 April 1997 to mark 100 years of telephone service in Japan, after about eight years of preparation from 1990 (stated by ICC).
- Building: a floor of the Tokyo Opera City Tower. Medosch (Telepolis, 15 August 2001) says ICC "has given up one floor" and the permanent collection on display shrank.
- What is there: long-term media art displays, special exhibitions on single artists or themes, a summer programme for children, talks, performances, symposia, gallery tours, workshops, and the ICC HIVE visual archive on the 5th floor (stated by ICC).
- Visiting (ICC Annual 2026 listing): open 11:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays. Admission for that show is 800 yen for adults and 600 yen for university students; free for high school students and younger, people 65 and over, and disability-certificate holders with one companion. No reporting found of wheelchair details.

FUNDING
NTT InterCommunication Center (ICC): corporate-run: NTT East, the telecom operator
- ICC describes itself as "a cultural facility run by NTT East Corp." and lists no other funder (stated by ICC).
- Ozaki Tetsuya's 2005 column quotes a 1998 statement by ICC's first director (not named there) that the project would not be allowed to die because of NTT's "social responsibility."
- Ozaki (2005) says ICC sits under NTT Learning Systems, part of the NTT Group, with decisions made by NTT East. He puts visitors at about 40,000 to 50,000 a year and staff at just under 20, mostly on contract, temporary or part-time terms.
- Lovink (nettime, 16 September 2000) says ICC "has lost half of its budget" and will move to a smaller space inside Tokyo Opera City.
- No reporting found of any government, foundation or private donor behind ICC. No reporting found of ICC's own accounts; NTT East publishes group results.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little recent criticism. All three items date from 2000 to 2005 and concern its management and its dependence on NTT's budget decisions. Items 1 and 3 are nettime mailing-list posts, not original art-press articles; item 2 is from Telepolis.
1. ART iT / REALTOKYO, forwarded to the nettime mailing list by Andreas Broeckmann on 20 August 2005: Ozaki Tetsuya, "Behind the Scenes #003" (2005)
URL: https://nettime.org/Lists-Archives/nettime-l-0508/msg00021.html
- Unverified: Ozaki reports an insider rumour that ICC would close at the end of financial year 2005 (around March 2006); NTT East's PR office said it was investigating closure as "one option." The library and lobby cafe had closed in March 2005 and the exhibition schedule ran only to December 2005.
- Unverified: insiders said staff were told to "finish up all paperwork" by March 2006. The piece reports no official decision and no plan for the 14-work collection.
- Read as a repost on the nettime list, not at the original outlet.
2. Telepolis (original article, not a repost): Armin Medosch, "Japan: Corporate Restructuring Affects Media Art Institutions" (2001)
URL: https://www.telepolis.de/article/Japan-Corporate-Restructuring-Affects-Media-Art-Institutions-3452061.html
- Argues that ten years of recession and what he calls "corporate restructuring" put Japanese media art institutions in doubt; ICC survived but shrank, giving up a floor, with the permanent collection on display shrinking.
- Says the slimmed-down ICC's activities "do not look that dismal." Telepolis is not an art-press title. Quotes no ICC or NTT official.
3. nettime mailing list: Geert Lovink, e-mail interview with Yukiko Shigata, posted 16 September 2000
URL: https://nettime.org/Lists-Archives/nettime-l-0009/msg00128.html
- Lovink's question cites criticism of ICC as a "megalomaniac approach" and a "mafia type of organization." He names no critic. Shigata is a curator at Canon ArtLab, not at ICC.
- Shigata answered that ICC's invitation to Survival Research Laboratories "provided a kind of critic to ICC from inside," though it would not silence critics in general. No answer from ICC or NTT is in the post.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind ICC is a single telecom operator's cultural budget, and no flagged funder or documented controversy was found. The weak points are that ICC depends on NTT East's decisions: Lovink reported in 2000 that it lost half its budget, Ozaki reported closure rumours in 2005, and still publishes no funder list or accounts of its own. Worth watching whether NTT East keeps ICC at its current scale.
