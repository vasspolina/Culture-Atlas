MUSÉE D'ART DE JOLIETTE: Joliette, Canada
ID: musée-dart-de-joliette-joliette
Website: https://www.museejoliette.org

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Joliette, Quebec, in front of the Outaragawe sipi (L'Assomption River); its architecture is inspired by the international style and the ideas of Le Corbusier (museum website).
- Origins in 1943, when Father Wilfrid Corbeil of the Clercs de Saint-Viateur set up a painting gallery at the Séminaire de Joliette. In 1966 the Clercs entrusted the collection to a committee; the museum moved to the former Scolasticat Saint-Charles in 1969. Construction of the present building began on 26 August 1974 and it opened to the public on 25 January 1976 (museum website, history page).
- Collection of about 8,300 works in four areas: Canadian art, European art, contemporary art and archaeology (museum website).
- In 2024-2025 more than 65,000 people took part in its activities, including nearly 39,000 for the projected digital artwork Ondulation 2025. The year included eight exhibitions and thematic hangings, a residency in contemporary dance, 149 new acquisitions and a renewed permanent exhibition (2025 annual report).
- Governance: a board of directors chaired by Emmanuel Préville-Ratelle; Annie Gauthier is director general and chief curator (2025 annual report).

FUNDING
Musée d'art de Joliette: non-profit supported by Quebec and Canadian public bodies, its own foundation and corporate and family partners
- Public bodies and programmes named in the 2025 annual report: Ministère de la Culture et des Communications du Québec, Conseil des arts du Canada, Patrimoine canadien (a grant in spring 2025 to review and optimise its storage spaces), Musées numériques Canada (financed the research for the virtual exhibition "Actions collectives", online since 14 June 2025) and Ville de Joliette. Ondulation 2025, a digital artwork launched in May 2025, was supported by the Quebec government and the Ville de Joliette.
- Fondation du Musée d'art de Joliette: granted $644,300 to the museum in 2024-2025, of which 35% went to educational programmes, 23% to exhibitions, 22% to conservation and 20% to temporary exhibitions and events (2025 annual report).
- Power Corporation du Canada: paid for the restoration of three works (Jeanne Rhéaume, Ghitta Caiserman, Raymonde Gravel), supports the renewed permanent collection room (named salle Power Corporation du Canada) and partnered on the virtual exhibition "Actions collectives" (2025 annual report).
- The exhibition "Rosalie D. Gagné : Alchimiste contemporaine" was presented by the famille Jacques Martin; Caisse Desjardins de Joliette et du Centre de Lanaudière presents the museum's creative workshops, artist meetings and talks (2025 annual report).
- Annex 7 of the report, "Les partenaires de premier plan", is an alphabetical list of partners that includes Énergir, Fondation Famille Pierre Lassonde, Fondation Hydro-Québec, Fondation J.A. DeSève, Famille Parizeau, Power Corporation du Canada and Caisse Desjardins de Joliette et du Centre de Lanaudière; the museum's partners page names the same seven. The foundation's report lists Banque Nationale under "Partenariats et commandites".
- Audited financial statements to 31 August 2025 are in the annual report. The president's message in it describes museums in Quebec and Canada facing a withdrawal of government funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a non-profit funded by Quebec and Canadian public bodies, its own foundation and corporate and family partners. Power Corporation du Canada supported the restoration of three works, the virtual exhibition and a named collection room.
