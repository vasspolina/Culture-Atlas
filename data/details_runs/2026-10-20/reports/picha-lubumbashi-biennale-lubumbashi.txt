PICHA / LUBUMBASHI BIENNALE: Lubumbashi, DR Congo
ID: picha-lubumbashi-biennale-lubumbashi
Website: https://biennaledelubumbashi.com

OVERVIEW
- Artist-led art centre and research organisation in Lubumbashi, the second-largest city in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with its address at 7 Avenue du Parc; it runs the Lubumbashi Biennale.
- Founded in 2008 by a collective of Congolese artists and cultural workers including Sammy Baloji and Jean Katambayi Mukendi (The Art Newspaper, Gameli Hamelo, 19 August 2026).
- "Picha" means "image" in Swahili, according to the Biennale's About page.
- The Biennale grew out of Baloji's invitation to the 2007 Bamako Encounters photography biennial, organised by Simon Njami (The Art Newspaper).
- Picha's collective includes Baloji, George Senga, Rosa Spaliviero and Jean Katambayi, and Picha also runs Ateliers Picha, an annual program of residencies, workshops and exhibitions (Biennale About page).
- Picha runs exhibitions, residencies, training programmes, workshops and art projects (The Art Newspaper).
- The ninth edition, BL9, runs from 15 October to 15 November 2026 in several spaces across Lubumbashi, with Picha Art Center as the central hub; admission is free.

FUNDING
Picha / Lubumbashi Biennale: self-funded by its founders, with private donors and French and Belgian cultural agencies
- The Art Newspaper reports that Picha was historically self-funded by its founders, with support from private individuals and international bodies including French and Belgian cultural agencies, and that it has no state funding.
- ArtReview (Louise Darblay, "Artist Sammy Baloji on Colonial Legacies, New Stories and Creating the Lubumbashi Biennale", 18 January 2021) asked how the Biennale is maintained "without state support"; Baloji answered that "we're not really expecting any support".
- In August 2026 Picha launched an "urgent" GoFundMe campaign citing rising operating costs and dwindling international support; the target is 10,000 euros and over 6,000 euros had been raised when The Art Newspaper published. The money covers rent, programming and the ninth Biennale.
- Baloji describes funding for the arts in the DRC as "fragile" (The Art Newspaper).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Picha is the founders' own, private donors and French and Belgian cultural agencies, with no state funding, as The Art Newspaper reports; Baloji told ArtReview in 2021 that "we're not really expecting any support". In August 2026 it raised money by crowdfunding to cover rent, programming and the ninth Biennale.
