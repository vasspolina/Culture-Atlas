MARRES, HOUSE FOR CONTEMPORARY CULTURE: Maastricht, Netherlands
ID: marres-maastricht
Website: https://marres.org

OVERVIEW
- House for contemporary culture in a monumental townhouse at Capucijnenstraat 98, in the historic centre of Maastricht (Marres website).
- Established in 1999, when the house and its garden were opened to the public (Marres website).
- The house and garden were commissioned in 1680 by Henricus Thisius van Ingenop, then mayor of Maastricht. The name comes from the brewing family that lived in the house for three generations (Marres website).
- Programme: exhibitions, a public garden, the restaurant Marres Kitchen and education work; in 2022 Marres staged six exhibitions, workshops in the series "Training the Senses" and garden activities (Marres website; annual report 2022). It received 11,544 visitors in 2022 and 4,331 in 2021, including 1,513 secondary school pupils, 876 primary pupils and 230 higher-education students (annual report 2022).
- Run by a supervisory board and a managing director appointed in 2013 on a permanent contract (annual report 2022). The 2022 supervisory board was Nelleke Barning (chair), Roberto Lanckohr (vice chair), Noor van Putte, Laurie Cluitmans, Hans Bongartz and Arsine Nazarian; chair and vice chair swapped roles on 1 January 2023 (annual report 2022).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 12:00 to 17:00, with Marres Kitchen also open 18:00 to 22:00. Admission is by ticket; on free Friday evenings the exhibition is open 12:00 to 20:00 with free entry from 17:00, supported by the Elisabeth Strouven Fund. The city garden is freely accessible (Marres website).

FUNDING
Marres: publicly supported: structural support from the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, Province of Limburg and Municipality of Maastricht, plus project funds
- Structural support comes from the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, the Province of Limburg and the Municipality of Maastricht; project support is listed on individual project pages (Marres website, "Support and partners").
- 2022 income was 1,149,687 euros against an expected 1,011,818. Extra money included 30,000 euros from the RAOCCC support fund, 30,000 euros from the Maastricht municipal recovery fund and several education funds. The year closed with an operating deficit of 109,312 euros before 125,000 euros of released reserves (annual report 2022).
- The municipal recovery fund also paid for garden investments and a garden programme in 2022 (annual report 2022).
- The youth programme Extended was funded by Fonds 21, the VSB fonds, KanunnikSalden and Sint Clemens; the exhibition "Kolkata: Run in the Alley" listed the Mondriaan Fonds as extra income (annual report 2022).
- The exhibition "Rubyfruit Jungle" by Celina Eceiza was made with the social production centre Stichting Werkhuis, the Cultuurfonds and volunteers; the youth collective Join Extended is run by Marres and the Jan van Eyck Academie (Marres website).
- Marres holds a sponsor dinner and party at TEFAF each year; it was held again in 2022 (annual report 2022).
- Education and project partners include the Bonnefanten, Fashionclash, Jan van Eyck Academie, Maastricht University, Z33 and Zuyd Hogeschool (Marres website).

BOTTOM LINE
Marres receives structural support from three public funders, the Dutch education and culture ministry, the Province of Limburg and the Municipality of Maastricht, with project money from named funds such as the Mondriaan Fonds, Fonds 21 and the VSB fonds. Its 2022 income was about 1.15 million euros.
