BAG FACTORY ARTISTS' STUDIOS: Johannesburg, South Africa
ID: bag-factory-johannesburg
Website: https://bagfactoryart.org.za (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Artists' studios, gallery and residency programme at 10 Mahlathini Street, Newtown, Johannesburg, on the border of Fordsburg and Newtown, next to the Oriental Plaza and close to Newtown Junction (stated by the organisation).
- Founded in 1991 as Fordsburg Artists' Studios, trading as Bag Factory Artists' Studios, by Dr David Koloane, the British philanthropist Robert Loder and the arts administrator Sandra (Sandy) Burnett (Contemporary And). The Mail & Guardian reported in 1995 that the studios opened in the disused Speedy Bag Factory early in 1991, as workspace for artists linked to Thupelo, and placed them in Fordsburg.
- Named for its first home, the disused Speedy Bag Factory; the Mail & Guardian reported the retrospective "The Speedy Bag Factory: The First Four Years" at the Civic Gallery in 1995. No source opened explains the name itself.
- Building: an old converted factory warehouse with high ceilings and drywall partitions. Insulation is weak, so studios are cold in winter and hot in summer (stated by the organisation). The organisation finalised the purchase of the building in 2020; a first phase of roof maintenance was finished in 2021, and a capital campaign for a larger renovation and extension is planned.
- On site: studios for resident artists, a visiting artist programme (up to eight a year), a visiting curator programme (up to four a year), self-funded residencies from US$500 a month, exhibitions, open studios, a shop and Factory Circle membership. A library named after Koloane, who died in 2019, is planned.
- Visiting: office open Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00 or by appointment; the current exhibition, "Site Forming", runs 10 October to 6 November 2026, Monday to Friday 10:00 to 16:00. No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
Bag Factory Artists' Studios: no funders named: studio rents, residency fees and unnamed international partners
- The organisation says its visiting artist and curator programme is "partially funded, dependent on international partnerships and access to funding". It names no funders on its about, residencies or home pages.
- Studio rents were subsidised from the start; Loder bought the Fordsburg building in January 1991 with help from local sponsors, whom the Mail & Guardian did not name (1995).
- Self-funded residents pay US$500 a month for stays of three months or more, plus a US$200 open-studio fee. Visiting artists donate one artwork to the organisation.
- It is affiliated with the Triangle Arts Trust (art-factories listing, last modified 2004). Funding from the Triangle network is not confirmed in the pages opened.
- Partners on the 2026 DALRO Visual Arts Merit Award: DALRO, LL Editions and the William Humphreys Art Gallery. Artist Sam Nhlengethwa founded and funds the Young Womxn Studio Bursary.
- Funding from the National Arts Council of South Africa is not confirmed in the pages opened. No reporting found of how the 2020 building purchase was paid for, of accounts, or of a board list.
- Loder (1934 to 2017) worked for the Anglo American Corporation in Johannesburg and Lusaka from 1957 to 1966, founded the African Arts Trust in 1959, and with Anthony Caro started the workshops that became the Triangle Arts Trust (Wikipedia). No reporting found that Anglo American money funds the Bag Factory.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found, and none from the art press. The one critical piece is a 1995 general newspaper article.
1. Mail & Guardian: Ian Tromp, "Of rat traps and art prats" (1995)
URL: https://mg.co.za/article/1995-09-22-of-rat-traps-and-art-prats
- The studios were widely seen as a young, predominantly white avant-garde, which the writer rejects as untrue. Sandy Burnett said the studios were not the core of a new movement.
- Joachim Schönfeldt described the studios as "genetically engineered" to balance race and gender, though some studios sat empty.
- The retrospective at the Civic Gallery felt disjointed, "so disparate it feels like a student show". Kendell Geers was evicted under a two-month occupancy rule while on a scholarship in France.
- Concludes the studios' idea is still sound. Not an art-press outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Bag Factory is mostly unnamed: studio rents, residency fees and international partners, with no flagged funder found. The weak point is transparency: no funder list, accounts or board are published on the pages opened, and the funding of the 2020 building purchase is not stated. Worth watching: the capital campaign for the building.
