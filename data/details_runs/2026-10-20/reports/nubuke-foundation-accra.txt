NUBUKE FOUNDATION: Accra, Ghana
ID: nubuke-foundation-accra
Website: https://nubukefoundation.com

OVERVIEW
- Visual art and cultural institution at 7 Lome Close, East Legon, Accra, described as a creative hub for contemporary art, heritage and culture in Ghana (MyJoyOnline, Odile Tevie, 2 August 2026).
- Established in April 2006 by Odile Tevie, Kofi Tutu Agyare and Kofi Setordji (MyJoyOnline, 2 August 2026). Its first project was a photographic book, Ghana: A Portrait, released before Ghana's 50th independence anniversary; Tevie is director and Setordji artistic director (Daily Graphic, 15 October 2016).
- Programmes include exhibitions, poetry and drama evenings and workshops, and the Woori Festival, which runs every two years, with the next edition in 2027 (foundation website).
- Runs the Centre for Clay and Textiles in Loho, near Wa in the Upper West Region, which aims to "promote, support and stimulate artisanal practice in clay pottery and textile weaving" (foundation website, Daily Graphic).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 17:00 (foundation website).

FUNDING
Nubuke Foundation: privately founded, with project funding from the EU and the British Council
- The Wa Centre for Clay and Textiles is a beneficiary of the ACP-EU Culture programme, in which the AWA (Art West Africa) consortium of the Koré Centre in Ségou and Institut Français distributes European Union funding. The consortium was selected by the EU and the Organisation of African, Caribbean and Pacific States (foundation Funders page).
- A digital collaboration project with Assemble (UK) and the Textile Department of the University of Applied Arts Vienna was supported by the British Council Digital Collaboration Fund (foundation Funders page).
- Google Arts and Culture is listed as a partner on the Funders page.
- On the foundation's tenth anniversary in 2016, the founders invited the public to support its mission (Daily Graphic, 15 October 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation was started by three private founders. Its documented outside money is project funding: European Union money through the ACP-EU Culture programme for the Wa centre and a British Council fund for a digital collaboration project.
