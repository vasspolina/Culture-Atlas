ATENEO ART GALLERY: Quezon City, Philippines
ID: ateneo-art-gallery-quezon-city
Website: https://ateneoartgallery.com/ (checked 2026-10-24; the web address no longer serves the gallery: it redirects to an unrelated gambling-prediction site, which was not opened)

OVERVIEW
- University art museum in the Soledad V. Pangilinan Arts Wing of Areté, Ateneo de Manila University, Katipunan Avenue, Loyola Heights, Quezon City.
- Established in 1960 around the collection of Fernando Zóbel, a painter, art scholar and Ateneo teacher, who donated over 200 artworks as a study collection for students (Wikipedia). The Quezon City government listing calls it the first museum of Philippine modern art.
- It first sat in Bellarmine Hall in 1961, moved to the ground floor of Rizal Library in 1967 and moved to the Arts Wing in Areté in 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Areté, the Ateneo Creative Hub, was designed by W.V. Coscolluela & Associates as a four-storey building with two wings joined by a bridgeway. In April 2014 the university president said 75% of its funding was secured in pledges and commitments (The Guidon).
- On site: permanent collection on the upper-ground and second floors, contemporary and experimental art and the annual Ateneo Art Awards on the third floor, and the Ben Chan ArtSuite for workshops, lectures and film screenings. Wikipedia says the collection holds over 500 works. The Quezon City government listing gives the floors, the awards and the ArtSuite.
- Visiting: open Monday to Saturday 9:00 to 17:00 and Sunday 9:00 to 16:00; regular admission P50; free for senior citizens, children under 12 and Ateneo students and personnel (Quezon City government listing). No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
Ateneo Art Gallery: university museum: Ateneo de Manila University, with partners for its awards
- The gallery is part of the university; no reporting found of its budget or of a separate funder list.
- The collection began with Zóbel's gift (Wikipedia calls it a bequest). Zóbel belonged to the Zóbel de Ayala family and worked for Ayala Corporation until he resigned in 1961 to paint full time (Wikipedia).
- Ateneo Art Awards, 2019: Art Asia Pacific Magazine and The Philippine Star sponsored the art criticism prizes, and the Embassy of Italy Purchase Prize placed the winning work in the embassy's collection. Winners became eligible for international residency grants with La Trobe University, Artesan Gallery + Studio and Liverpool Hope University (The Guidon, 19 August 2019).
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, arms, gambling or other flagged funder.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is a university budget, a founding gift from a member of the Ayala family, and prize partners, with no flagged funder found. The weak point is that its web address no longer serves the gallery and no budget, donor list or accounts could be read, so funding rests on third-party listings. Worth watching for a restored site.
