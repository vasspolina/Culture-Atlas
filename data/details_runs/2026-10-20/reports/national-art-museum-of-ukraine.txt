NATIONAL ART MUSEUM OF UKRAINE: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: national-art-museum-of-ukraine
Website: https://namu.ua (checked 2026-10-24; the English pages load)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum at 6 Hrushevskoho Street, Kyiv, with a collection of more than 40,000 exhibits from the 12th century to the present (museum site).
- First exhibition held in August 1899; officially opened in December 1904 as the Kyiv Art-Industrial and Scientific Museum of Emperor Nikolai Alexandrovich. Renamed many times (the museum says 12 times); the current name dates from 1994 (Wikipedia; museum site).
- Building completed in 1898, designed by Moscow architect Petro Boitsov and reworked by the Polish architect Wladyslaw Horodecki, with sculptural decoration attributed to Emilio Sala. Construction cost 249,000 rubles, of which 100,000 came from the imperial government and 108,000 from the Tereshchenko family (Wikipedia).
- First director: Mykola Biliashivsky. The current director listed is Yulia Lytvynets (Wikipedia).
- The permanent exhibition was removed on 24 February 2022 for security reasons; the museum runs temporary exhibition projects, and events pause during air raid alerts (museum site). Wikipedia states a Russian air attack on 24 May 2026 broke all its windows.
- Visiting: Thursday to Sunday 12:00 to 18:00. Full ticket UAH 150; schoolchildren, students and pensioners UAH 100; military personnel free. The site has a text-size and contrast menu; no step-free access details were found.

FUNDING
National Art Museum of Ukraine: public: a state museum; private help is small and mostly unnamed
- State museum under the Ministry of Culture; its income and spending plans must be agreed with the ministry (former director Mariia Zadorozhna, LB.ua interview, 2015).
- 23 September 2020: the Cabinet gave UAH 5.6 million from the state reserve fund for emergency repair after a facade collapse on 8 August: about UAH 1.983 million for the sculptural groups on the facade and roof and about UAH 3.617 million for the main facade and areaways (Ministry of Culture announcement).
- 2016: UAH 1 million from the state budget for project documentation; unnamed patrons paid for facade lighting and for repair of the second-floor halls; a crowdfunding appeal sought UAH 199,385.74 for parquet repair (LB.ua, 5 July 2016).
- Donations go to its own charitable foundation, "Charitable Foundation of National Art Museum of Ukraine"; the support page names no donors or amounts (museum site).
- Collector Stella Benyaminova, linked to the Stedley Art Foundation and the "Friends of NAMU" circle, supplied fire extinguishers during the Maidan (Zadorozhna interview, LB.ua).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor list, annual report or accounts.

Red flags: 2012 staff protest over the director
- 16 October 2012: museum staff issued an open letter demanding the removal of acting director Tatiana Mironova and that she be barred from the permanent post. The next day staff began an open protest with a sign in the entrance hall (Art Workers' Self-defense Initiative statement, ArtLeaks, 17 October 2012).
- Mironova had been acting director since April 2012, after the Ministry of Culture removed long-time director Anatoliy Melnyk. She also owns the commercial Mironova gallery in Kyiv (ArtLeaks).
- A petition from art workers asked for an open competitive hiring process (ArtLeaks). Zadorozhna says the competition commission selected no applicant and the museum council then recommended her candidacy (LB.ua).
- The initiative is an advocacy group of art workers.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found dates from 2012 and concerns the director's appointment and commercialisation, not the museum's funding.
1. ArtLeaks: Art Workers' Self-defense Initiative, "Open Statement Regarding the National Art Museum of Ukraine (NAMU)" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/10/17/art-workers-self-defense-initiative-open-statement-regarding-the-national-art-museum-of-ukraine-namu/
- Appointing Mironova over staff objections would halt the museum's development and push it towards commercialisation at the expense of art.
- Asks for open, transparent, competitive hiring and a director with museum experience.
2. ArtLeaks: Mykola Ridnyi, "Ukrainian Museums: Between Two Evils" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/10/17/ukrainian-museums-between-two-evils/
- Criticises Mironova's plan to make the museum "a brand", cites car sponsorship at the entrance as commercialisation, and calls her candidacy "extremely problematic" given her gallery.
- Also criticises Soviet-style bureaucracy in other Ukrainian institutions.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Ukrainian state money, with small unnamed private gifts and a foundation that names no donors. The weak points are the 2012 conflict over a director who ran a commercial gallery, ministry influence over appointments and programming, and the absence of published accounts. Worth watching: how the museum and its foundation report donations during the war.
