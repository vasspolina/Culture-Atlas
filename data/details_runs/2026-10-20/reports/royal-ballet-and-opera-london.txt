ROYAL BALLET AND OPERA: London, UK
ID: royal-ballet-and-opera-london
Website: https://rbo.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24; working, redirects to www.rbo.org.uk)

OVERVIEW
- Parent body of The Royal Opera and The Royal Ballet, based at the Royal Opera House in Covent Garden, London (stated by the organisation).
- The companies were rebranded as the Royal Ballet and Opera in 2024; the building keeps the name Royal Opera House (Wikipedia).
- The Theatre Royal, Covent Garden opened on 7 December 1732 as a playhouse; it became the Royal Opera House in 1892 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Edward Middleton Barry designed the current theatre, dating from 1858; the 1997 to 1999 reconstruction cost 213 million pounds, paid for with Arts Lottery money distributed by Arts Council England plus private fundraising (Wikipedia).
- On site: a 2,256-seat Grade I listed main auditorium, the 400-seat Linbury Studio Theatre and the Paul Hamlyn Hall atrium; the operator is the Royal Opera House Covent Garden Foundation.
- Visiting: open daily from 12 noon; step-free entry at Bow Street and the Covent Garden Piazza, lifts, accessible toilets, free priority booking for accessible performances and a free companion ticket.

FUNDING
Royal Ballet and Opera: mixed: Arts Council England, box office and tax relief, philanthropists and corporate partners
- Accounts for the year ending 27 August 2023: total income 170.7 million pounds, of which box office 52.6 million, Arts Council England grant 23.6 million, theatre tax relief 20.6 million, fundraising and sponsorship 37.6 million and commercial and other income 46 million (Civil Society).
- Principal Partner of The Royal Opera: Rolex, headline partner from the 2024/25 season after an earlier partnership as Exclusive Timepiece (stated by the organisation).
- Sponsors listed: Alvarez & Marsal, Bank of America, Barbie, Boodles, Corient, Coutts, Kirkland & Ellis, Sofas & Stuff, Van Cleef & Arpels, Veuve Clicquot. Corporate Associates include American Express, Mastercard, Morgan Stanley and Barclays.
- Principal philanthropist: the Julia Rausing Trust. Season Principals (list updated 1 April 2025): the Clore Duffield Foundation, Dunard Fund, Oak Foundation, the Mohn Westlake Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Aud Jebsen, and named individuals.
- No reporting found of individual sponsor or donor amounts.

Red flags: BP sponsorship, fossil fuels
- BP sponsored the house for 33 years. The house said in January 2023 that it was "no longer in receipt of BP money", and the house told The Guardian it had "agreed that the partnership would not extend beyond 22 December" 2022, when BP's contract came to an end (WhatsOnStage, 25 January 2023). The contract was not renewed.
- Culture Unstained, a campaign group, says that, based on the house's accounts, BP's sponsorship was under 0.5% of annual income, and that it understands a five-year BP deal began in 2018 (Culture Unstained statement republished by Art Not Oil, advocacy sources).

Red flags: campaign against BP at the house, sponsor not renewed
- 10 June 2015: composers Kate Honey and Chris Garrard held up an "End Oil Sponsorship" banner during La Bohème; Honey said the money gave BP "a legitimacy that it does not deserve" (Slipped Disc, 12 June 2015).
- 2016: musicians and composers, including John Luther Adams and Paul Griffiths, backed a letter asking the house to cut its ties to BP (Culture Unstained statement in WhatsOnStage).
- 2019: more than 200 figures from classical, folk and popular music wrote to London mayor Sadiq Khan about the BP-branded screenings in Trafalgar Square (WhatsOnStage, The Friend).
- The house gave no reason for the end beyond the contract coming to an end (WhatsOnStage).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is about BP sponsorship, from campaigners and music coverage; no art-critic essay on the funding was found.
1. Slipped Disc: Norman Lebrecht, report on the La Bohème protest (2015)
URL: https://slippedisc.com/2015/06/composers-lead-disruption-of-covent-garden-opera
- Reports the protest by Honey and Garrard, who argued that BP's money legitimised it and that BP supported repressive regimes in West Papua and Azerbaijan.
- Quotes the house, via the Financial Times: BP had stood by it "through thick and thin" since 1988.
2. The Friend: Rebecca Hardy, "Royal Opera House ends BP sponsorship" (2023)
URL: https://thefriend.org/article/royal-opera-house-ends-bp-sponsorship
- Reports the house had faced protests and criticism for letting BP "artwash" its brand; not an art publication.
- Names BP, the Royal Shakespeare Company, the National Portrait Gallery and National Galleries Scotland; quotes a house representative saying it is "no longer in receipt of BP money" and includes no statement from BP.

BOTTOM LINE
The house's money comes mainly from ticket sales, tax relief, Arts Council England and a large donor and sponsor base, with tiers published. The weak point was BP, a sponsor for 33 years until December 2022 after protests from 2015; no current fossil fuel sponsor was found in the lists opened. Watch the sponsor lists for any new oil, gas or Gulf state partner.
