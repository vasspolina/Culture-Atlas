LAGOS BIENNIAL: Lagos, Nigeria
ID: lagos-biennial-lagos
Website: https://lagos-biennial.org/

OVERVIEW
- Not-for-profit contemporary art biennial in Lagos, organised by the Àkéte Art Foundation, a Lagos-based artist collective registered with Nigeria's Corporate Affairs Commission (lagos-biennial.org).
- "Created and founded by artists in 2017"; Folakunle Oshun is its founder and artistic director (lagos-biennial.org; The Lagos Review, Toni Kan, 10 November 2025).
- The first edition, "Living on the Edge" (14 October to 22 December 2017), had its core exhibitions at the Nigerian Railway Corporation in Yaba, in a running shed the biennial's site says was built in 1955 (Biennial Foundation; lagos-biennial.org); the second edition used the remains of Independence House, an abandoned 25-storey building donated to Nigeria by the British colonial administration at independence in 1960 (lagos-biennial.org).
- The fourth edition, "REFUGE", ran from 3 to 10 February 2024 at Tafawa Balewa Square on Lagos Island, with Kathryn Weir and Folakunle Oshun as artistic directors (The Lagos Review, Toni Kan, 22 September 2023).
- The fifth edition, "The Museum of Things Unseen", was announced for October 2026, curated by Sam Hopkins, Chinyere Obieze and Furen Dai (The Lagos Review, Toni Kan, 10 November 2025).
- The Biennial plans a museum of modern and contemporary art, the Àkéte Collection, funded by sales of a limited-edition book of 65 copies, "The Making of an African Capital of Culture (Lagos Biennial I-V)", launched on 4 November 2025 at the Shell Zenith Hall, MUSON Centre, Lagos (The Lagos Review; e-flux).

FUNDING
Lagos Biennial: foundation-funded: grants from the Terra Foundation for American Art, with airline, residency and agency partners
- Terra Foundation for American Art: two grants to the Àkéte Art Foundation, 150,000 dollars in 2023 for the Lagos Biennial and 100,000 dollars in 2025 for the fifth edition (Terra Foundation grants database).
- Royal Air Maroc is the official travel carrier under a three-edition partnership announced on 22 September 2023 (The Lagos Review, Toni Kan).
- G.A.S. Foundation partnered with the 2024 edition, hosting research residencies for artists before it; A White Space Creative Agency (AWCA) Lagos supported the production (The Lagos Review, 22 September 2023; G.A.S. Foundation, 27 September 2023).
- Book sales: the 65-copy limited edition, priced from 7.5 million naira (about 5,000 dollars), supports the planned Àkéte Collection museum (The Lagos Review, 10 November 2025).
- The site names the Lagos State Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture as an endorser (lagos-biennial.org).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two reviews of the 2024 edition criticise display and curation.
1. Contemporary And: Joseph Omoh Ndukwu, "A Refuge of Sorts" (16 February 2024)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/magazines/a-refuge-of-sorts/
- Some works were poorly presented: Bruce Onobrakpeya's sculptures "seemed lazily assembled and out of place," and Ibrahim Mahama's "Yakachana" "seemed lame in effect."
- Henrique Oliveira's "Island" and Feda Wardak's "Hanging Imaginaries" "seemed to yearn for more, or for less," and some works "appeared merely performative."
- Names the 1976 assassination of Murtala Muhammed: the car he was killed in stands in the National Museum beside the square, and apart from remarks at symposiums none of the biennial's works addressed it.
- Criticises the display of the art, not the institution.
2. ArtThrob: Sinazo Chiya, "Refuge: A Tapestry of Contradictions and Convergence at the 2024 Lagos Biennial" (1 March 2024)
URL: https://artthrob.co.za/2024/03/01/refuge-a-tapestry-of-contradictions-and-convergence-at-the-2024-lagos-biennial/
- The organising principles "edged towards over-curation," though the author says this did not matter much.
- Earlier editions ran for over a month; this one was on view for a week.
- Describes entry by QR code scanned on tablets, a drone and security guards with machine guns; the author took part in one project, "Sowing Watermelon Seeds," held after biennial hours.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Lagos Biennial is a foundation grant from the Terra Foundation for American Art (150,000 dollars in 2023 and 100,000 dollars in 2025), supported by airline and residency partners and by book sales for the planned museum. Press coverage of the 2024 edition criticised display and curation.
