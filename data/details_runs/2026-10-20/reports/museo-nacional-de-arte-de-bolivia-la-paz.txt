MUSEO NACIONAL DE ARTE DE BOLIVIA: La Paz, Bolivia
ID: museo-nacional-de-arte-de-bolivia-la-paz
Website: https://www.fundacionculturalbcb.gob.bo/notas-de-prensa-mna/2022/np-mna-023-2022.php (checked 2026-10-24; the press-note page loads, but the foundation's home page returned a 522 error)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in the Palacio Diez de Medina, in front of Plaza Murillo, La Paz; the main entrance is on Calle Socabaya (Wikipedia).
- Part of the Fundación Cultural del Banco Central de Bolivia (FC-BCB), which describes it as the state's most important and extensive repository of plastic and visual arts. The museum marks 3 August as its anniversary and turned 56 in 2022 (FC-BCB press note); Wikipedia gives 1964 for its establishment.
- The house was first the residence of Mayor Francisco Tadeo Diez de Medina y Vidango, then belonged to the Counts of Arana and the Marquises of Villaverde, and was the Gisbert hotel at the end of the 19th century. In 1964 it was adapted as the museum, keeping its two courtyards and three levels (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the architect of the adaptation.
- The permanent collection includes colonial paintings by Melchor Pérez de Holguín and Gregorio Gamarra, plus contemporary art (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Arte de Bolivia: public: Bolivian state, through the Banco Central de Bolivia's cultural foundation
- The Banco Central de Bolivia must give the Fundación Cultural the resources it needs, under Article 82 of Law 1670 (Article 4 of Supreme Decree 4243, 2020). No reporting found of an annual budget figure for the museum.
- The foundation oversees nine Bolivian cultural institutions and has a board replaced in March 2020; Guillermo Mariaca became president in April 2020 (CIMAM, 2020).
- In 2022 the museum took custody of works by Ricardo Pérez Alcalá donated to the foundation by Alicia Medrano of Mexico (FC-BCB press note).
- No reporting found of a private sponsor, donor list or published accounts for the museum.

Red flags: Protest over the forced exit of the museum's director
- Dismissal: on 1 June 2020 the foundation asked director Max Jorge Hinderer Cruz, in post since March 2019, to "make available" his position (CIMAM). He and Elvira Espejo Ayca of the National Museum of Ethnography and Folklore were the only two directors of nine institutions asked to resign, and both were dismissed (Apollo, Valeria Costa-Kostritsky, 29 July 2020). Lucía Querejazu Escobari replaced Hinderer (Apollo).
- Reasons: the board president said the requests followed the change of presidency; CIMAM disagrees (CIMAM). Hinderer called the dismissals "symbolic violence" and Espejo said she did not "fit in the current situation"; both link them to the political shift after Evo Morales left office in November 2019 (Apollo).
- Protest: CIMAM wrote to the president on 23 June 2020 asking the board to withdraw the requests, and had received no reply by 29 June (CIMAM).
- Aftermath: in February 2021 the foundation reinstated Espejo and Máximo Pacheco as an "acto de desagravio" for dismissals by the "gobierno de facto". Hinderer was not included, which Correo del Sur says "causó extrañeza" (my translation: caused surprise). Iván Castellón is named museum director (Correo del Sur, 18 February 2021).

Red flags: Foundation management
- Unverified: Jorge Aliaga, a former director of the foundation's Santa Cruz centre, alleged in 2015 an irregular attempt to buy a La Paz property valued at 10 million bolivianos, and that only 6 of 20 million bolivianos allocated to the Santa Cruz centre were spent; from eju.tv (Emilio Martínez, 19 June 2015). The purchase was suspended after his complaints. No reporting found of a response.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2020 dismissal of Hinderer; art-press coverage is one magazine feature and one museum-association statement. No criticism of the collection or programme found.
1. Apollo: Valeria Costa-Kostritsky, "Status anxiety: the battle over culture in Bolivia" (2020)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/bolivia-museum-directors-sacked/
- Reports Hinderer's and Espejo's dismissals, the replacement of the foundation's board and the absence of an official reason.
- Quotes CIMAM calling them "museum professionals highly respected amongst the wider international contemporary museum community".
2. CIMAM Museum Watch Committee: statement on the Museo Nacional de Arte (29 June 2020)
URL: https://cimam.org/museum-watch/museum-watch-actions/la-paz-international-museum-best-cases-danger/
- Says the request lacked due process, praises the museum's programmes and asks the board to withdraw the requests and to base leadership decisions on open discussion.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and flows through a Banco Central foundation, with no private sponsor named. The weak points are the board's 2020 removal of its director, which the director links to politics, Hinderer's exclusion from the 2021 reinstatements, and the lack of published museum accounts. Worth watching: who runs the museum now and whether the foundation publishes its budget.
