DESIGNMUSEUM DANMARK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: designmuseum-danmark-copenhagen
Website: https://designmuseum.dk (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- State-recognised museum of Danish and international design at Bredgade 68 in the Frederiksstaden district of central Copenhagen.
- Founded in 1890 partly at the initiative of Industriforeningen; known as the Danish Museum of Decorative Art and then the Danish Museum of Art & Design (Kunstindustrimuseet) until 2011 (Wikipedia). Anne-Louise Sommer is director and Henrik Tvarnø chairs the board.
- Since 1926 it has occupied the former Frederik's Hospital, built 1752 to 1757 and given by the banker Emil Glückstadt; Kaare Klint and Ivar Bentsen converted it (Wikipedia). It closed from 18 March 2020 to 19 June 2022 for a full restoration (annual report 2023).
- Exhibitions including the permanent "Danish Modern" (from 2024), a design library and archive, a design workshop for learning, the Grønnegården garden, a shop and the café FORMAT. A new knowledge house (Videnshuset) for the library, learning and research, designed by Praksis Arkitekter, is planned for 2026.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursdays to 20:00. Adults DKK 140, under-27s and students DKK 90, under-18s free. The ground floor, café, shop and library are wheelchair accessible; there is no lift to the first floor.

FUNDING
Designmuseum Danmark: mixed: earned income, a state operating grant and private foundations
- In 2024 income was DKK 71,751,567, including a state operating grant of DKK 17,504,668, admissions of DKK 29,857,489, shop and café DKK 12,922,869 and non-public grants of DKK 4,337,264 (annual report 2024).
- In 2023 income was DKK 64,547,157, with a state grant of DKK 16,482,199; the museum says its own earnings are over two thirds of turnover and the state grant under a third (annual report 2023, my translation).
- Building support in 2023 and 2024: Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond, A.P. Møller Fonden, Augustinus Fonden, C.L. Davids Fond og Samling, OJD (Annie & Otto Johs. Detlefs' Fonde) and Villum Fonden. A.P. Møller Fonden paid for the roof restoration and Villum Fonden for the design workshop (sponsors page).
- The Videnshus was financed in 2023 by Augustinus Fonden, C.L. Davids Fond og Samling and Villum Fonden (annual report 2023).
- "Danish Modern" (2024) was funded by Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond, Hermod Lannungs Museumsfond, Knud Højgaards Fond and William Demant Fonden (annual report 2024).
- Other named supporters include Ny Carlsbergfondet, Nordea-fonden, Bikubenfonden, Velux Fonden, Toyota-fonden, Ole Kirks Fond and Statens Kunstfond (sponsors page, undated).
- No reporting found of the amounts given by individual foundations.

Red flags: tobacco money behind building and Videnshus gifts
- Augustinus Fonden was set up in 1942 by the tobacco maker Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker, which it now fully owns; that company owns 65% of Skandinavisk Holding, which owns 51% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group (Wikipedia).
- Augustinus Fonden is one of three funders of the planned Videnshus (2023) and is thanked for building support in the 2023 and 2024 annual reports.
- It was also main sponsor of the exhibition "Dansk Design Nu" in 2015 and 2016 (sponsors page).
- No reporting found of a protest at the museum over the Augustinus gifts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is announcements of the 2022 reopening and of "Danish Modern".

BOTTOM LINE
The museum earns most of its own money from tickets, its shop and café, with a state grant of under a third and many Danish foundations paying for buildings and exhibitions. The weak point is Augustinus Fonden, whose wealth rests on a controlling chain into Scandinavian Tobacco Group and which co-funds the new Videnshus. Worth watching: the funder credits when the Videnshus opens.
