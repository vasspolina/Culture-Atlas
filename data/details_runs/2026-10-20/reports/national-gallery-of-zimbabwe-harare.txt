NATIONAL GALLERY OF ZIMBABWE: Harare, Zimbabwe
ID: national-gallery-of-zimbabwe-harare
Website: https://nationalgallery.co.zw

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at 20 Julius Nyerere Way, Harare, with regional galleries in Bulawayo and Mutare (Wikipedia; the gallery's site lists Bulawayo, Mutare and Tengenene galleries).
- Opened on 16 July 1957 as the National Gallery of Rhodesia, designed and directed by Frank McEwen, and officially opened by Queen Elizabeth The Queen Mother (Wikipedia). A Board of Trustees was set up under the National Gallery Act of 1952 and Salisbury City Council took responsibility for building and administration (Wikipedia).
- Collection of close to 6,000 works (Wikipedia).
- Raphael Chikukwa is executive director and Fadzai Muchemwa is curator (Wikipedia).
- The Bulawayo gallery opened in 1970 and moved in 1993 to Douslin House, a building from 1901; the Mutare gallery opened in 1999 in Kopje House (Wikipedia).
- The gallery will host the 58th CIMAM Annual Conference in Harare from 20 to 22 November 2026 (the gallery's site).

FUNDING
National Gallery of Zimbabwe: state-governed with foundation and sponsor support: a minister appoints its board chair, and a 2024 Mellon Foundation grant and sponsors add funds
- In November 2022 Minister Kirsty Coventry of the Ministry of Youth, Sport, Arts and Recreation appointed Justice Maphios Cheda (Rtd) chair of the gallery's Board of Trustees, under the Public Entities and Corporate Governance Act (Business Times, 20 November 2022).
- The Mellon Foundation awarded the gallery a $500,000 grant on 2 December 2024 "to support infrastructure and leadership", over 24 months (Mellon Foundation grant page).
- The gallery's sponsors page says it receives significant support from its Board of Trustees and contributions from "many dedicated Sponsors and Partners".

Red flags: state censorship at the Bulawayo gallery (2010)
- Owen Maseko's "Sibathontisele" ("Let's Drip On Them"), three installations and twelve paintings on the Gukurahundi massacres of Ndebele civilians, opened at the National Gallery in Bulawayo on Thursday 25 March 2010 (The Standard, Judith Todd, 28 March 2010; Wikipedia, Owen Maseko). Wikipedia says Maseko was arrested in March 2010, less than 24 hours after the exhibition opened.
- The New York Times (Celia W. Dugger, 24 January 2011, republished by the Honolulu Star-Advertiser) reports that the exhibit was closed down the day after it opened and the artwork banned; it names no body that closed or banned it. Maseko was detained at the central police station, moved to prison in leg irons and then released on bail, and faced charges of insulting the president and communicating falsehoods prejudicial to the state, the latter carrying up to 20 years.
- The Standard reports that Vote Thebe, acting director of Bulawayo's National Gallery, went with police to the Charge Office while the exhibition was being closed, and that Maseko was also at the Charge Office.
- Wikipedia reports charges under the Public Order and Security Act of undermining the authority of Robert Mugabe and causing offence to persons of a particular race or religion; in September 2010 the trial was postponed pending a Supreme Court review of whether criminalising creative arts infringes freedom of expression.
- Unverified: the Home Affairs Ministry and the Board of Censors banned the exhibition, from an SW Radio Africa interview with Maseko (host Lance Guma, aired 2 September 2010, posted by Nehanda Radio on 23 September 2010), a partisan source critical of the government. In it Guma says the Board of Censors banned the show after Home Affairs called it a "tribally-biased event", and Maseko says his urgent High Court application to reopen it was denied because the works were held as evidence.
- The New York Times reports that Voti Thebe, who heads the National Gallery, said he allowed Maseko to make the exhibit as a way to contribute to reconciliation.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery answers to a government ministry: the minister appointed its board chair in 2022. It has a 2024 Mellon Foundation grant of $500,000 and contributions from sponsors and partners. In 2010 Owen Maseko's Gukurahundi exhibition at its Bulawayo gallery was closed and banned, and the artist was detained and charged.
