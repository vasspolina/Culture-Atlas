WIDER CONTEXT
- In December 1988 "Robert Mapplethorpe: The Perfect Moment" opened at the Institute of Contemporary Art, which received a $30,000 grant from the National Endowment for the Arts for it. In June 1989, under pressure from Congress, the Corcoran Gallery of Art in Washington cancelled the touring show, and in October 1989 Congress passed an NEA appropriations bill requiring grant recipients to pledge they would not produce "obscene" works (The Christian Science Monitor, "Chronology of a Controversy", 31 July 1990).
- The NEA appropriations bill required any grant the ICA requested to be posted on Capitol Hill for 30 days before consideration because of its role in the Mapplethorpe exhibition. In summer 1990 the National Council for the Arts turned down two of three ICA grants that peer panels had approved. The rejection was later reversed (The Daily Pennsylvanian, Peter Spiegel, 19 November 1990).
- ICA director Patrick Murphy said the rejection was based on the grants' content but was meant as a punishment to the museum: "The feeling was that to give a grant to the Mappelthorpe museum would be an affront to Congress" (The Daily Pennsylvanian).
