NATIONAL GALLERY - ALEXANDROS SOUTSOS MUSEUM: Athens, Greece
ID: national-gallery-alexandros-soutzos-museum
Website: https://www.nationalgallery.gr/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at 50 Vasileos Konstantinou Street, Athens, opposite the Hilton Athens; nearest metro Evangelismos (Line 3) (gallery site, Wikipedia).
- Founded by law on 10 April 1900, with Georgios Iakovidis as its first chief curator (gallery site). Named for Alexandros Soutsos, who bequeathed his collection and estate to the Greek state in 1896; the estate merged with the gallery in 1954.
- The central building was designed by Pavlos Mylonas, Konstantinos Mylonas and Dimitris Fatouros and built from 1966 to 1975. Grammatopoulos-Panousakis Architects and D. Vassilopoulos & Associates renovated and extended it over eight years, with a new Stavros Niarchos Foundation Wing; it reopened on 24 March 2021 (Wikipedia). The extension took floor area from 9,720 m2 to 20,760 m2.
- The National Glyptotheque and annexes in Sparta, Corfu, Nafplion and Aegina are separate sites.
- Open Monday and Wednesday to Sunday, closing at 18:00 and at 22:00 on Wednesday; closed Tuesday. Admission is 10 euros, 5 euros reduced; free on the first Sunday of each month from November to March. Ramps and lifts serve the building.

FUNDING
National Gallery - Alexandros Soutsos Museum: mainly public: Greek Ministry of Culture, with sponsors and foundations paying for exhibitions and projects
- A public law entity run by a nine-member board; the Minister of Culture appoints the Director. Stated by the gallery: public funding covers salaries and fixed operating costs.
- Stated by the gallery: sponsorships and donations cover 60 to 100 percent of the cost of exhibitions, music events and publishing.
- Expansion: total cost 59 million euros. The Ministry of Culture says 42 million euros of public funding was secured; the Stavros Niarchos Foundation gave a 13 million euro grant in 2017 (The Art Newspaper).
- Stated by the gallery: the Stavros Niarchos Foundation also paid for the interiors of the 2000 permanent display, the Onassis Foundation equipped the Nafplion annex and the Maria Tsakou Foundation funded the expansion's preliminary studies.
- No reporting found of the sponsors behind "The Allure of the Bizarre" (2025).

Red flags: attack on an exhibition and calls to ban it
- On 10 March 2025 Niki party MP Nikolaos Papadopoulos removed four works by Christoforos Katsadiotis from the group show "The Allure of the Bizarre", after a 6 March letter to director Syrago Tsiara demanding its removal. Two works had their glass shattered; he said they slipped from his hands. He was detained and released, and the offence is classed as a misdemeanour (Hyperallergic, The Art Newspaper, To Vima).
- Parliament's Speaker, Nikitas Kaklamanis, censured him and cut his salary by 50 percent for one month (To Vima, 12 March 2025).
- On 10 April 2025, 29 artists, academics and estate representatives wrote to the Culture Minister, Tsiara and the board, citing "institutional inaction and legal inconsistency" over the slow reinstallation; they asked for their own works to be removed until the damaged ones returned. Tsiara said the works would return within the month under tighter security (To Vima).
- In September 2025, 400 believers asked the Athens Single-Member Court of First Instance to ban the show or add a warning sign; the court rejected the interim measures, finding no sufficient showing of imminent risk or urgency (Proto Thema, 18 September 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the institution's money or management found in art media. The two pieces on the 2025 attack side with the gallery.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Far-Right Politician Vandalizes 'Blasphemous' Artworks at Athens Museum" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/995478/far-right-politician-vandalizes-blasphemous-artworks-at-athens-museum/
- Reports the 10 March attack and sets it beside attacks on art in Mexico City, Italy, Austria and Houston; aims its criticism at Papadopoulos and religious pressure on artists.
- Does not criticise the gallery; it notes the board's condemnation.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Politician detained after attacks on 'blasphemous' works at Greek National Gallery" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/03/12/politician-detained-after-attacks-on-blasphemous-works-at-greek-national-gallery
- Records that the board condemned the vandalism and "any attempts at censorship"; does not criticise the gallery.
3. The Art Newspaper: William Summerfield, "Greece completes long-awaited National Gallery revamp for Independence Day bicentenary" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/greece-s-revamped-national-gallery-hosts-independence-celebrations
- Reports the 59 million euro cost, the 13 million euro Niarchos grant and delays since the planned 2015 completion, mainly from a miscalculated water level of the underground Ilissos river.
- Reports the delays; does not criticise the funders.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Papadopoulos argued the show was produced at the expense of "the Greek Orthodox taxpayer" (Hyperallergic), which ties the attack to the gallery's public funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is mostly public, with named foundations on the expansion, and none of it falls in a flagged industry. The weak points are that no sponsor list with amounts was found, and that its handling of the 2025 attack drew a public letter from artists and estates in its collection.
