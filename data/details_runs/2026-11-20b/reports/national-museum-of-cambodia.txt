NATIONAL MUSEUM OF CAMBODIA: Phnom Penh, Cambodia
ID: national-museum-of-cambodia
Website: https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.kh/ (checked 2026-11-20; down, returned a 502 error; the museum's older site, https://www.cambodiamuseum.info, dates from 2013)

OVERVIEW
- Cambodia's national museum of Khmer art and archaeology on Street 13 in central Phnom Penh, next to the Royal Palace, with its visitor entrance at the corner of Streets 13 and 178 (museum's 2013 site).
- The foundation stone was laid on 15 August 1917; called the Musée du Cambodge in 1919, it was inaugurated as the Musée Albert Sarraut, after the Governor-General of Indochina, on 13 April 1920 in the presence of King Sisowath (museum's 2013 site).
- George Groslier designed the building on Cambodian temple prototypes from ancient bas-reliefs; the design was altered in 1924 (museum's 2013 site).
- France ceded the museum on 9 August 1951, and Chea Thay Seng became the first Cambodian director in 1966. It closed from 1975 to 1979 under the Khmer Rouge and reopened on 13 April 1979 (museum's 2013 site).
- About 90 per cent of the collection is stored in a basement subject to flooding in the wet season; work with UNESCO to protect it began in 1993. The museum also oversees Cambodia's other museums (museum's 2013 site).
- Visoth Chhay is director (Expertise France).
- Returns of looted objects: Douglas Latchford's daughter, Nawapan Kriangsak, agreed in 2021 to give his whole collection to Cambodia, to be shown in a planned extension of the museum (The Art Newspaper, Vincent Noce, 2021); 74 objects from his estate arrived on 27 February 2026 to join the collection (The Star).
- In August 2024 Cambodia celebrated the return of 70 artifacts, including pieces from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, Jim Clark and the Lindemann family; among them was a larger-than-life-sized Dhrishtadyumna statue from Koh Ker, reunited with the pedestal the museum had kept (ICIJ, 2024).
- On 20 November 2025 the Hungarian collector István Zelnik returned 296 objects at the museum, among them 183 Khmer silk textiles; the ministry said many were looted during the civil war (AKP).
- A France-funded study set out a 10-year plan to modernise and expand the museum into neighbouring Royal University of Fine Arts buildings, which are due to be handed over by the end of 2026 (Cambodianess, October 2026).
- Visiting, as given on the 2013 site: daily 8:00 to 17:00, last tickets 16:30; foreigners 10 US dollars (5 dollars aged 10 to 17), Cambodians 500 riels, children and school groups free; tours in Khmer, English, French and Japanese; no photography in the galleries.

FUNDING
National Museum of Cambodia: public: the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts, with French and other foreign aid
- The museum works under the auspices of the Cambodian Department of Museums (museum's 2013 site); the Minister of Culture and Fine Arts, Phoeurng Sackona, asked France on 29 June 2022 to help rehabilitate it (Expertise France).
- France paid 900,000 euros for a feasibility study from October 2024 to October 2026, run by Expertise France with the Musée Guimet under the French Embassy; the ministry says its next steps include securing funding for the works (Cambodianess).
- Earlier aid: support from the governments of Australia and France after loan exhibitions from 1992, and French funding for the stone and wood conservation workshop, including salary supplements for its staff (museum's 2013 site).
- The 2013 site thanks past contributors: the Leon Levy Foundation, the Getty Conservation Institute, Friends of Khmer Culture Inc., UNESCO, the Japanese government and the Bickford Family Trust, which sponsored the site with Friends of Khmer Culture. It says the museum lacks adequate funding, equipment and trained staff, and asks for donations.
- Earned income: tickets, a museum shop and donation boxes (museum's 2013 site).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2008 the collector Douglas Latchford received the Grand Cross of the Royal Order of Monisaraphon for financial contributions to Cambodia's national museum; in 2009 he returned six gold pieces and received the Sahak Metrei friendship medal (Bangkok Post, 21 February 2023, an article from The New York Times that carries no byline on the Post's page).
- Latchford attended a function at the National Museum with Deputy Prime Minister Sok An on 12 June 2009 (ICIJ, photo caption).
- US federal prosecutors in New York indicted Latchford in 2019 for a leading role in the looting of Cambodia. He died in 2020, and his estate agreed to pay 12 million US dollars and return a 7th-century bronze in a forfeiture (ICIJ, 2024; The Art Newspaper).

BOTTOM LINE
Public and foreign-government money, with no flagged current funder. The weak point is the museum's past patronage by a collector later indicted for trafficking Khmer antiquities.
