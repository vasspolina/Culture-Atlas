NATIONAL GALLERY OF MODERN ART (NGMA): New Delhi, India
ID: national-gallery-of-modern-art-ngma
Website: https://ngmaindia.gov.in/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- India's national museum of modern art, at Jaipur House by India Gate, New Delhi, on eight acres (Wikipedia; the gallery's pages on Google Sites).
- Established on 29 March 1954 by the Government of India; Hermann Goetz, a German art historian, was its first curator (Wikipedia).
- Jaipur House, a former residence of the Maharaja of Jaipur, is a butterfly-shaped building with a central dome, built in 1936 to a design by Sir Arthur Blomfield (Wikipedia).
- 17,039 artworks by about 2,000 artists, from the mid-18th century to the present, with about 12,000 square metres of exhibition space in Delhi (the gallery's pages; Wikipedia).
- Branches: Mumbai, in the former Sir Cowasji Jehangir Public Hall; Bengaluru, opened on 18 February 2009 in the Manikyavelu Mansion at 49 Palace Road; and Kolkata (the gallery's pages).
- The Director General is Sanjeev Kishor Goutam, in post since December 2023 (Wikipedia). The Director is Nidhi Choudhari, an Indian Administrative Service officer on central deputation, confirmed by a government notification of 7 August 2026 with a tenure to 20 August 2029 (Indian Masterminds).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6:30pm. Entry is 20 rupees for Indian citizens aged 18 and over and 500 rupees for foreign nationals (Tour My India, a travel listing, not the gallery).

FUNDING
National Gallery of Modern Art: public: Government of India, Ministry of Culture
- The gallery is a subordinate office of the Ministry of Culture, Government of India (the gallery's pages).
- Budget: the Union Budget for 2025-26 gave 126.63 crore rupees to museums such as the National Museum and the National Gallery of Modern Art, within a Ministry of Culture outlay of 3,360.96 crore rupees (ETV Bharat, 2 February 2025).
- Early collection money: Nehru approved 50,000 rupees from National Museum funds for the Egan collection of Amrita Sher-Gil paintings (Wikipedia).
- The Ministry told the press in 2019 that the gallery's advisory committees in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Delhi had not been dissolved and that their terms had recently ended (Tribune, PTI).

Red flags: 2000 removal of a Surendran Nair painting on a ministry order
- September 2000: at the exhibition "CombineVoice for the New Century", Culture Ministry Secretary P. V. Vaidynatha Ayyar ordered director Mukta Nidhi Samnotra to remove Surendran Nair's painting of a nude Icarus on the Ashoka Pillar, on the ground that it treated the national emblem irreverently. Samnotra agreed; all 25 artists withdrew their works in protest (The File Room archive, an anti-censorship project; ArtsJournal, citing the BBC, 5 September 2000).

Red flags: 2019 interruption of Amol Palekar's speech at the Mumbai branch
- In February 2019, at the opening of an exhibition in memory of the artist Prabhakar Barwe, the actor-director Amol Palekar criticised the Ministry for scrapping the advisory committees of the Mumbai and Bengaluru branches and was repeatedly interrupted by some NGMA members (Tribune, 11 February 2019).
- Director General Adwaita Gadanayak said the committees' earlier recommendations would be honoured (Latestly, 11 February 2019).

Red flags: 2023 exhibition built on Narendra Modi's radio broadcast
- May 2023: the Delhi gallery hosted "Jan Shakthi: A Collective Power", curated by Alka Pandey, with themes from Narendra Modi's radio broadcast "Mann Ki Baat"; South First called it "reportedly pro-government" (South First, 13 July 2023).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The objections found are to ministry control of programming, and none comes from a dedicated art outlet.
1. DailyO: Gautam Benegal, "No Podium for Palekar: When art is attacked, it's the people who lose" (2019)
URL: https://dailyo.in/politics/amol-palekar-ngma-mumbai-bjp-rss-censorship-hindutva-29398
- Argues that a policy in force since October 2018, giving the Ministry of Culture sole authority over NGMA exhibitions, threatens artistic freedom; local artists' committees chose the Mumbai and Bangalore shows before it.
- Calls the gallery a public institution, not "a government fiefdom", and questions the Delhi gallery's auction, announced on 27 January 2019, of gifts given to Modi.
- A news site, not an art outlet.
2. South First: Sreerag PS, "Artists stand in solidarity with museum employee fired for criticising pro-government art exhibition" (2023)
URL: https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/art-world-stands-in-solidarity-with-museum-employee-fired-for-criticising-artists-who-collaborated-with-pro-modi-show/
- Reports that Sandip K Luis lost his contract at the Kiran Nadar Museum of Art after a 15 May 2023 post criticising the artists behind "Jan Shakthi".
- Criticises the artists and the Kiran Nadar Museum, not NGMA.
3. ArtsJournal: Douglas McLennan, post citing the BBC (2000)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=171538
- Reports that a painting of a small nude figure was removed from a Delhi exhibition and all 25 artists withdrew.
- Does not name NGMA.

WIDER CONTEXT
- DailyO places the 2019 episode within pressure on Indian artists by Hindutva groups, citing the arrest of the Baroda art student Chandramohan and the 8 December 2016 attack on the Jaipur Art Summit.
- NGMA is the state's own gallery, so the Ministry's decisions are the ones DailyO criticises.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind NGMA is the Government of India's. The weak points are a documented ministry order over what is shown (2000) and complaints about a state-friendly programme in 2019 and 2023. Watch for a budget line for the gallery alone and for who curates its shows.
