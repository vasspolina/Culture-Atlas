MUSEO AMPARO: Puebla, Mexico
ID: museo-amparo-puebla
Website: https://museoamparo.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum at 2 Sur 708 in the Centro Histórico of Puebla (museum site).
- Founded through the Fundación Amparo by banker Manuel Espinosa Yglesias and his daughter Ángeles Espinosa Yglesias Rugarcía; opened in 1991 (museum site; Wikipedia gives February 1991 for the opening).
- Named for Amparo Rugarcía de Espinosa, Espinosa Yglesias's wife; the museum was founded in her memory (museum site).
- Occupies colonial buildings on the site of Puebla's first hospital, San Juan de Letrán, founded in 1538. Later uses included girls' colleges, a priests' house, an old people's home and private homes; from 1871 part of the building was the home of Vicente Espinosa Bandini, grandfather of the founder (museum history page).
- TEN Arquitectos, led by Enrique Norten, renewed the building in three stages: February 2013 (temporary galleries, library, auditorium), December 2013 (1,848 m² of temporary exhibition space) and April 2015 (1,285 m² for the pre-Hispanic collection). The museum now covers almost 9,000 m² (museum history page).
- Collections of pre-Hispanic, colonial, modern and contemporary Mexican art, with temporary exhibitions and educational programmes (museum site).
- Open Wednesday to Monday, 10:00 to 18:00 (museum site). No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility on the museum's pages.

FUNDING
Museo Amparo: privately funded by the founding family's foundation
- Fundación Amparo, which Espinosa Yglesias founded in 1979 in memory of his wife, was funded from his own money rather than company resources (Wikipedia). The museum was one of its projects.
- Espinosa Yglesias made his money in cinemas and banking. He became the largest shareholder and board president of Banco de Comercio (Bancomer) in 1958 and general director in 1959; Bancomer was Mexico's largest bank when the government expropriated private banks in 1982 (Wikipedia).
- The foundation was later led by his daughter Ángeles until her death, and is now directed by his grandson Javier Alonso Espinosa (Wikipedia).
- The museum's site names no sponsors or patrons. No reporting found of a corporate or state funder behind the museum.
- No reporting found of accounts, donors or a board; the foundation's site links an annual report, which was not read.

WIDER CONTEXT
- FEMSA: on 10 April 2017, organisations and neighbourhoods in San Cristóbal de las Casas, Chiapas, marched to demand that the Coca-Cola FEMSA concession be cancelled or its water extraction capped. Protesters said the plant extracts more than 750,000 litres of water every day (SIPAZ, republished by the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre).
- Connection to the museum: from 26 September 2026 to 25 January 2027 the museum hosts "El hogar que habito. Obras de la Colección FEMSA" (museum site). Quién does not say who funds the show. No reporting found of FEMSA funding the museum or its foundation, and no reporting found of a protest aimed at the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a single family's foundation built on a banker's fortune, with no named corporate or state funder. The weak points are the lack of published accounts, donors or a board, and the 2026 to 2027 exhibition of the FEMSA collection, whose terms are not reported. Worth watching: whether the museum publishes funder information alongside corporate exhibitions.
