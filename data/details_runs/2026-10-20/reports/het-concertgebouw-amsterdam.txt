HET CONCERTGEBOUW: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: het-concertgebouw-amsterdam
Website: https://www.concertgebouw.nl (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Concert hall on Museumplein in Amsterdam; the Concertgebouw Orchestra gave its first concert there on 3 November 1888 (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 11 April 1888; the company Concertgebouw NV was founded in 1882 (NOS).
- Named "Concertgebouw" (concert building); the "Koninklijk" title was granted by Queen Beatrix in 2013 for its 125th anniversary (Wikipedia).
- Adolf Leonard van Gendt designed it, inspired by Leipzig's Gewandhaus; it was built on a pasture outside the city. Emergency restoration from 1985 to 1988 replaced 2,186 wooden piles with concrete, and Pi de Bruijn designed a modern annex (Wikipedia).
- Seats: Main Hall 1,974, Recital Hall 437 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Het Concertgebouw: privately funded: gifts and inheritances through a support fund, ticket sales, and project grants
- NOS reported on 31 October 2025, citing the Financieele Dagblad, that the hall has been almost entirely privately funded for nearly 150 years. About €100 million was raised over 25 years through a support foundation, Het Concertgebouw Fonds, which has had ANBI charity status since 2012 (NH Nieuws) and has collected funds for the NV since 2001 (NOS).
- The homepage thanks Van Lanschot Kempen, Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst and Fonds Podiumkunsten (stated by the space).
- Dick Boer, a former Ahold executive, has chaired the NV since 2017; Jeroen van der Veer, a former Shell executive, left the foundation's chair in October 2025 (NOS).
- The hall's Fondsen page thanks: VriendenLoterij, Ammodo, Brook Foundation, Don Quixote Foundation, Fonds 21, Maurice Amado Foundation, Mondriaan Fonds, P.W. Janssen's Friesche Stichting, Blom-de Wagt Foundation, Rens-Holle Stichting, Stichting de Weille Ogier, Stichting Edwin Bouw Fonds, Stichting Gifted Art, Stichting Thurkow Fonds, Stichting VLB Fonds, Stichting Zabawas, Triodos Foundation, Turing Foundation, VandenBroek Foundation, VandenEnde Foundation, Vogelgezang Foundation, VSB Fonds and Zadelhoff Cultuur Fonds (stated by the space). No amounts are given.
- MP Keijzer's written question of 30 March 2026 puts municipal subsidy at about 4 percent of the hall's income; Minister Letschert answered on 12 May 2026 that her ministry has no direct subsidy relationship with the hall (Tweede Kamer).
- No reporting found of a state subsidy.

Red flags: tax arrangement of the gift structure
- The Belastingdienst considers that the foundation should not have passed gifts tax-free to the NV; gift tax can reach 40 percent, and NOS put the gifts and bequests at tens of millions of euros (NOS).
- Director Simon Reinink said the structure "niet heeft voldaan aan de letter van de wet" ("did not meet the letter of the law", our translation) but that there was no malicious intent; he had no indication that repayment would be demanded (NOS).
- The NV was to become a foundation from 1 January 2026 (NH Nieuws).

Red flags: protest over the Chanukah concert
- On 2 November 2025 the hall ended its cooperation with Stichting Chanukah Concert for the 14 December concert because lead cantor Shai Abramson was "a visible representative" of the Israeli military (NL Times); NOS wrote that his website calls him Chief Cantor at official army occasions.
- Pro-Palestinian groups had pressed the hall to bar him; the foundation called the decision an "infringement of freedom of religion" and said it would file for summary proceedings (kort geding) (NL Times, NOS); Israel's ambassador called it "shameful and reprehensible" (NL Times).
- The concert later went ahead with Abramson as a closed event rather than an open one, which director Simon Reinink called a compromise; about sixty staff, roughly a quarter of the payroll according to Het Parool, wrote to the management asking for an explanation (NH Nieuws).
- In March 2026 MP Keijzer asked the education minister about new hall guidelines that let it bar artists involved in discrimination, violence or serious breaches of international law, including war crimes and genocide. The minister confirmed the guidelines exist, said institutions decide their own programming, and said a court rules on legality (Tweede Kamer, 12 May 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found; the debate is in news and opinion outlets.
1. Joop (BNNVARA): Marcel Kolder, "Waarom het Concertgebouw niet cancelt, maar zijn geweten volgt" (2025)
URL: https://www.bnnvara.nl/joop/artikelen/waarom-het-concertgebouw-niet-cancelt-maar-zijn-geweten-volgt
- Defends the decision: the hall is free to choose performers if it does not discriminate, and targeted a link between a religious event and a military, not Judaism.
- An opinion piece in support; it carries no statements from the organisers or the singer.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private, raised through a tax-advantaged fund. The weak points are the Belastingdienst's objection to that structure, which the hall is restructuring, the Chanukah dispute with its staff letter, and the 2026 exclusion guidelines questioned in parliament. Worth watching: any back-tax demand and any use of the guidelines.
