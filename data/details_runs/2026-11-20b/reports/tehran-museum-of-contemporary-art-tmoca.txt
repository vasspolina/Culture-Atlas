TEHRAN MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART (TMOCA): Tehran, Iran
ID: tehran-museum-of-contemporary-art-tmoca
Website: https://www.tmoca.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art beside Laleh Park, Tehran, formerly Farah Park (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 14 October 1977 under Empress Farah Pahlavi, two years before the 1979 revolution; designed by her cousin Kamran Diba with Nader Ardalan, drawing on the architecture of desert towns such as Yazd and Kashan (Wikipedia; Etemad Online).
- It holds more than 3,000 items, including 19th and 20th century Western painting and sculpture and a major Iranian collection (Wikipedia); The Art Newspaper puts its value at about $3bn, with around 60 works by Picasso.
- After 1979 the Western works were kept in the vault; the first post-revolution show of them was in 1999 (Wikipedia). In 1994 De Kooning's "Woman III" was exchanged for folios of the Shah Tahmasp Shahnameh (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- Directors: Ehsan Aghaei was dismissed in March 2022 after an acrobat was swung into Noriyuki Haraguchi's oil-pool work "Matter and Mind" (Artforum); Reza Dabirinezhad has led it since April-May 2025 (Persian Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- War: the museum closed during the US-Israeli strikes that began on 28 February 2026, removed about 80% of the oil from the Haraguchi pool, and after the 8 April ceasefire reopened with a weekly "Art and War" programme (The Art Newspaper, 14 May 2026). It is marking its 50th year with a talk series (museum website).
- Tickets for the 2024 "Eye to Eye" show cost about 14 US cents (Associated Press).

FUNDING
Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art: state-owned and state-funded: Iran's Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance
- Owner: the museum is a unit of the Deputy for Arts Affairs of the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance (Persian Wikipedia).
- Founding money: the art was bought under the empress's office with funds from the National Iranian Oil Company (Wikipedia) and the Plan and Budget Organization (Persian Wikipedia).
- Renovation: the ministry allocated the money in 1396 (2017-18) (Etemad Online, January 2021).
- Acquisitions: works bought at the 2003 Tehran biennial were paid for by the government-funded Institute for the Promotion of Contemporary Visual Arts, partly with Ministry of Culture money, according to the biennial's secretary Gholam Reza Nami (The Art Newspaper, 2019).

Red flags: owner on the US sanctions list
- The Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance, the museum's owner, has been on the US Specially Designated Nationals list since 8 November 2012 under the IRAN-TRA programme (OFAC); the Federal Register notice of 16 November 2012 ties the designation to Executive Order 13628.

Red flags: 2018 to 2021 renovation delays and cost
- The museum closed on 29 April 2018 for a renovation officials said would take six months; it reopened in February 2021, 30 months later (Etemad Online; Persian Wikipedia).
- On 26 October 2018 Alireza Karandish, the ministry's deputy for development and resources, said 5 billion toman had been spent and 3 billion more would finish the work; the stated final cost was 16.5 billion toman, and a ministry official's progress report on 28 December 2020 gave no spending figures (Etemad Online).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the handling of the collection: missing works, the 2016 loan cancellation and a 2023 garden that critics read as decline.
1. The Art Newspaper: "Artist accuses Tehran Museum of Contemporary Art of selling off works at a premium" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/03/05/artist-accuses-tehran-museum-of-contemporary-art-of-selling-off-works-at-a-premium
- Unverified: Rokni Haerizadeh says the museum bought his painting cheaply and it was later auctioned for $86,680; Barbad Golshiri and Behnam Kamrani fear their works left the collection.
- Former director Sami Azar says a separate state institute bought and later sold the works; director Ehsan Aghaei declined to comment on the missing works.
2. Hyperallergic: Pamela Karimi, "Why on Earth Did Tehran's Contemporary Art Museum Plant Vegetables in Its Yard?" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/why-on-earth-did-tehran-contemporary-art-museum-plant-vegetables-in-its-yard/
- Says critics saw the May 2023 vegetable garden, since removed, as a symbol of the museum's "decades-long decline".
- Behind a registration wall; only the headline and standfirst were read.
3. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Berlin cancels show of Tehran's Modern art" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/12/28/berlin-cancels-show-of-tehrans-modern-art
- Iranian authorities missed the deadline for export permits for works by Picasso, Pollock, Rothko and Bacon; Hermann Parzinger said further delays "couldn't be justified".

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 27 December 2016 the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation ended its agreement for a Berlin show of 61 works from the collection, due to open on 4 December (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin; The Art Newspaper).
- In February 2017 Culture Minister Reza Salehi-Amiri said he had proposed cancelling the Berlin and Rome show pending review over "legal ambiguity"; the works went on view in Tehran in March 2017 (Tehran Times).
- Iranian newspapers speculated the works would not come back, and some officials questioned the show's value (The Art Newspaper). These were acts of the ministry that owns the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from a sanctioned Iranian ministry. The weak points are state control of the collection and loose stewardship: an overrun renovation and works that artists say left the collection.
