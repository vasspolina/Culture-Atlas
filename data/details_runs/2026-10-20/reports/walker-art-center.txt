WALKER ART CENTER: Minneapolis, USA
ID: walker-art-center
Website: https://www.walkerart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on Lowry Hill in Minneapolis, with the Minneapolis Sculpture Garden across the street; the campus covers 17 acres (Wikipedia).
- Grew from the lumber baron Thomas Barlow Walker's private collection, which he began showing to the public for free at his home in 1879; the Walker Art Galleries opened in 1927 and the Walker Art Center opened in January 1940 with Works Progress Administration help (Wikipedia).
- Named for T.B. Walker.
- Original building by Edward Larrabee Barnes, opened in 1971 and expanded in 1984; Barnes also designed the Sculpture Garden (1988). Herzog & de Meuron designed the expansion that opened in April 2005 (Wikipedia).
- On site: 65,000 square feet of exhibition space, the McGuire Theater, the Walker Cinema, a library and archives (stated by the Walker); the Sculpture Garden is always free and open daily from 6 am to midnight.
- Open Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10 am to 5 pm and Thursday 10 am to 9 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday. Adults $18, seniors $16, students $12; free for children 18 and under, members and citizens of Tribal Nations, and on Thursday nights and the first Saturday. Elevators, lifts and wheelchairs are available.

FUNDING
Walker Art Center: corporate-sponsored and privately endowed: Principal Foundation, U.S. Bank, Ameriprise, Best Buy, Cargill Foundation, 3M and Bloomberg Philanthropies, plus a state arts grant
- The Walker's corporate support page (undated, page footer 2026) lists Bloomberg Philanthropies and Principal Foundation at $100,000 and above; Ameriprise Financial and U.S. Bank and U.S. Bank Foundation at $50,000 to $99,000.
- At $25,000 to $49,000: 3M, Augeo, Best Buy, the Cargill Foundation, The Creative Partners Group, Faegre Drinker Foundation, Pohlad Companies, RBC Wealth Management, Thomson Reuters, Thrivent and United Health Foundation.
- Bloomberg Philanthropies also appears under in-kind gifts, with no amount or purpose given for either listing.
- The Walker receives a grant from the Minnesota State Arts Board, funded through the state legislature (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against Bloomberg Philanthropies aimed at the Walker.

Red flags: protests aimed at the Walker
- Scaffold, 2017: about 100 protesters, including Dakota community members, gathered outside the Sculpture Garden on 27 May 2017 to demand removal of Sam Durant's "Scaffold", a work referring to the 1862 hanging of 38 Dakota men (Smithsonian Magazine). On 29 May the Walker delayed the garden's reopening from 3 to 10 June for a mediation led by Dakota elders, and said the artist and museum were open to dismantling the work (MPR News). By 31 May executive director Olga Viso had announced the Walker would remove it (Smithsonian Magazine).
- Union rally, 2025: about 100 people rallied outside the Walker on 10 April 2025 for the Walker Worker Union, which called the March 2025 dismissals of union president Michelle Maser and union secretary Gabriela Bruner "union busting" and demanded their reinstatement. The Walker said it disagreed "in the strongest terms", that it complied with its collective bargaining agreement, and that a restructuring finished in March 2025 created and eliminated a small number of union and non-union roles (MPR News, 11 April 2025).
- The same union said gallery assistants lost the right to sit as an accessibility accommodation within the past year and a half; the Walker cited insurance and artwork protection, required a doctor's note for sitting, and said it offers "pathways to secure accommodations" (MPR News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2017 Scaffold episode and on labour conditions; the pieces found are mostly about how the Walker handled the protest. No criticism of the Walker's funders found in art media.
1. Hyperallergic: Jillian Steinhauer, "Sam Durant Doesn't Need Defending" (7 June 2017)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/383929/sam-durant-doesnt-need-defending/
- Argues that critics who cast Durant as a victim misread the power balance between the Walker and the Dakota.
- Names the Walker and defends its decision to engage in mediation; does not criticise the Walker's handling.
2. ArtsJournal: Matthew Westphal, on Olga Viso's departure (2017)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=135724
- Reports four sources close to the board tying Viso's exit, announced on 14 November 2017, to high staff turnover and the Scaffold protests.
3. MPR News: Alex V. Cipolle, union rally report (2025)
URL: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/11/union-rally-targets-walker-art-center
- Reports the union's claims on firings, hours and seating, and the Walker's written statement in reply. The Walker declined an interview.
- A news report, not an art-press critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The Walker's money comes from named Minnesota corporations, foundations and a state grant, and the only name to know is Bloomberg Philanthropies at $100,000 and above, with no campaign found against it at the Walker. The weak points are the 2017 Scaffold protest and the 2025 labour dispute. Worth watching: the tier lists carry no year.
