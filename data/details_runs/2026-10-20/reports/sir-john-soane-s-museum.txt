SIR JOHN SOANE'S MUSEUM: London, UK
ID: sir-john-soane-s-museum
Website: https://www.soane.org (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- House museum at 12 to 14 Lincoln's Inn Fields, Holborn, London, the former home of the architect John Soane (Wikipedia, annual report).
- Established by the Sir John Soane's Museum Act 1833 (royal assent 20 April 1833), which took effect on Soane's death in 1837; Soane pursued the Act to disinherit his son George, with whom he had a long feud. The Act required the house and collections to be kept "as nearly as possible" as they were at his death (Wikipedia).
- Named for its creator, John Soane, who bought No. 13 in 1806 and rebuilt it in 1808 to 1809 and 1812. No. 12 was connected in 1889 to 1890; No. 14, rebuilt by Soane in 1823 to 1824, was bought back by the trustees in 1997 with Heritage Lottery Fund help (Wikipedia).
- What is there: about 45,000 objects and about 30,000 architectural drawings, including Hogarth's "A Rake's Progress", four Canalettos, and the sarcophagus of Seti I that Soane bought for 2,000 pounds in 1824 (Wikipedia). It recorded 163,508 visitors in 2024-25, a record (annual report).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 5pm, last admission usually 4:30pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. Entry is free with no booking, and there can be a wait to get in. Visitors with access needs are asked to ring ahead on 020 7405 2107; lifts were installed in 2011 to 2013 (soane.org, Wikipedia).
- Director: Will Gompertz, since January 2024 (Wikipedia). Chair of Trustees: Lord Sassoon (annual report 2024-25).

FUNDING
Sir John Soane's Museum: mixed: UK government grant-in-aid, trusts and individuals, and its own trading
- Status: a Non-Departmental Public Body sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS), audited by the Comptroller and Auditor General, and accounts laid before Parliament (annual report 2024-25).
- Total income for the year to 31 March 2025 (2024-25): 4,221,788 pounds, up from 4,018,221 pounds (annual report and accounts presented to Parliament, 17 December 2025).
- DCMS Grant-in-Aid 2024-25: 1,165,000 pounds for running costs plus 32,000 pounds capital and 62,000 pounds restricted capital, 1,259,000 pounds in all (annual report). A further 48,000 pounds came from the DCMS Public Bodies Infrastructure Fund.
- Other donations and legacies: 1,362,808 pounds, including legacies of 70,498 pounds. Grants for salary underwriting and other projects: 612,173 pounds (annual report).
- Named supporters in the 2024-25 report: the Linbury Trust (an energy audit), the Leverhulme Trust (a short film), the Wates Foundation (a virtual tour), the Deborah Loeb Brice Foundation ("amongst its closest and most generous supporters"), Sir John Soane's Museum Foundation in America, the Trustees of Sir John Soane's Museum Trust, Hamish Forsyth, a trustee, and his wife Sophie (the Soane Medal 2024), and the John R Murray Charitable Trust (archive cataloguing). The report gives no amounts for these donors.
- Trading: retail sales 438,945 pounds, room hire 252,760 pounds, licensing 22,187 pounds and visitor tours and ticketed events 101,385 pounds, through the museum and its subsidiary Soane Museum Enterprises Ltd (annual report). Fourteen brand licensing partners are named, including Hector Finch, Adelphi Paper Hangings, Chesneys and Haddonstone (soane.org).
- Past: the 2011 to 2016 "Opening up the Soane" project cost about 7 million pounds, funded by the Monument Trust, the Heritage Lottery Fund, the Soane Foundation in New York and other trusts (Wikipedia).
- The museum says it must raise 1.5 million pounds every year from individuals, trusts, foundations and companies (soane.org).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum's funding or governance found in art media. Reviews of its exhibitions are favourable.
1. Architectural Record: Chris Foges, "An Exhibition at Sir John Soane's Museum Examines the Neoclassical Architect's Role as Modernist Forebear" (2025)
URL: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/17375-an-exhibition-at-sir-john-soanes-museum-examines-the-neoclassical-architects-role-as-modernist-forebear
- Calls "Soane and Modernism: Make It New" insightful but says one pairing, Soane's Bank of England work against Alvaro Siza's housing, is "An interesting comparison, if not strong evidence of modernity."
- Criticises one comparison in the show, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean on what is published: a UK government grant of about 1.2 million pounds, named trusts and individuals, and its own trading, with audited accounts laid before Parliament and no flagged funder found. The weak points are that the donor names appear in the report text without amounts, and that the Deborah Loeb Brice Foundation is the only donor the report names as a closest supporter. Worth watching: the next annual report's donor detail and the size of the grant-in-aid.
