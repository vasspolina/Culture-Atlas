SEJONG CENTER FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS: Seoul, South Korea
ID: sejong-center-for-the-performing-arts
Website: https://www.sejongpac.or.kr (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Performing arts centre at 175 Sejong-daero, Jongno District, in central Seoul, set up by the Seoul Metropolitan Government (Sejong Center site).
- Completed in 1978 and re-founded as a foundation in 1999 (Sejong Center site).
- Spaces: the Grand Theater with 3,022 seats, the M Theater with about 609, the Chamber Hall with 443, the S Theater, a black box of about 300, two halls of the Sejong Museum of Art and an arts academy (Sejong Center site).
- Resident companies: the Seoul Metropolitan traditional orchestra, dance company, musical company, chorus, opera company, theatre company and ballet (Sejong Center site; names translated from Korean).
- The ballet company was founded in 2024 as a public contemporary ballet company (NewDaily, 27 November 2025).
- President Ahn Hosang (안호상), a former director of the National Theater, marked one year in office in October 2022 and turned the centre into a "production theater" built around its own companies (Asia Economy, 21 October 2022).
- The resident companies take 42% of the total budget; in the three years before Ahn took office they drew 12.3% of the audience (Asia Economy).
- The 2026 season has 27 works and 226 performances, 23 of them by the resident companies (Hankyung, 22 December 2025).
- Seat occupancy rose from 82.99% in 2021 to 92.08% in 2024, and theatre audiences from 340,000 to 660,000 (Seoul Shinmun column, 2 September 2025).

FUNDING
Sejong Center: public body: a foundation of the Seoul Metropolitan Government
- City contributions: in the 2021 council audit, Seoul Metropolitan Council member Choi Young-ju (최영주) said the centre depended on city contributions more than the TBS media foundation, yet Seoul proposed an 11.8% increase for 2022. By the end of September 2021 the centre had earned 3.3 billion won of a 19 billion won own-income target (the member's item on Seoul Shinmun's council news page, 9 November 2021).
- Sponsorship: income from sponsors and patrons reached a record 1.6 billion won in 2025, from advertising tie-ins on shows and exhibitions, patrons' association gifts and sponsorship of spaces such as the central staircase (NewDaily, 18 December 2025). It names a KT listening event and a Disney Korea "Avatar: Fire and Ash" pop-up among 2025 collaborations.
- Patrons' association: corporate tiers run from 10 million won to over 500 million won and individual tiers from 3 million to over 50 million won (Sejong Center site).

Red flags: 2023 rehearsal injury and 2025 death of a freelance chorus singer
- Ahn Young-jae (안영재), a chorus singer in the Seoul Metropolitan Opera's "The Magic Flute" at the Sejong Center in March 2023, was struck by a stage set weighing over 400 kg during a rehearsal and was diagnosed with a spinal cord injury (Pressian; Segye Ilbo, 26 October 2025).
- As a freelancer on a verbal contract with a private choir he had no industrial accident insurance and paid his treatment costs himself; he was suing for damages when he died on 21 October 2025 (Segye Ilbo; Pressian).
- The Sejong Center said it was uncertain whether the accident happened on stage or caused his condition, said he left by an unscheduled route, and said it was in litigation to establish the facts (Segye Ilbo).
- On 24 October 2025 a network of scholars and experts on serious accidents held a press conference in front of the Sejong Center calling for accident insurance for performers. The board of KANE (유럽한국예술인협회), an association of Korean artists in Europe, said the Seoul Metropolitan Opera, the Sejong Center and Seoul city had failed in their responsibility (Pressian).
- Questioned at the council audit of 12 November 2025, the centre's president said a theatre is a dangerous place where accidents can happen at any time and promised to do his best to prevent a repeat (item of council member 아이수루 on Seoul Shinmun's council news page, 14 November 2025).

Red flags: 2025 council audit questions over the cost of the opera "Faust"
- At the audit of 12 November 2025, Seoul council member 아이수루 (Democratic Party), vice-chair of the culture committee, said the Seoul Metropolitan Opera's April 2025 "Faust" cost 920 million won and took in 310 million won, against 740 million won spent and 770 million won earned on its 2023 productions "The Magic Flute" and "Turandot". The source is her own item on Seoul Shinmun's council news page (14 November 2025).
- She said "Faust" drew 5,662 people against a target of 7,708; the president answered that it had been harder to cast leading singers this time.

BOTTOM LINE
The Sejong Center runs on Seoul city money with a small private sponsorship income, none of it from a flagged funder. The weak points are the centre's dispute over responsibility for a freelance singer's fatal stage injury and a council member's questions over opera costs. Worth watching: the outcome of the damages case.
