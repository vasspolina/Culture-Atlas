KÜNSTLERHAUS BETHANIEN: Berlin, Germany
ID: k-nstlerhaus-bethanien-berlin
Website: https://bethanien.de/en/

OVERVIEW
- Residency house and exhibition venue in Kreuzberg, Berlin. Its International Studio Programme brings around 40 artists a year from across the world, who show work in exhibitions, open studios and events (Bethanien).
- Founded in 1974 by Dr. Michael Haerdter in a former deaconess hospital on Mariannenplatz, after a plan to demolish the abandoned hospital was halted by protest (Bethanien history page).
- The name refers to the biblical town associated with Lazarus (Bethanien history page).
- In 2010, under Christoph Tannert, the house moved to the Lichtfabrik on Kohlfurter Straße, a building the brothers Felix and Leo Israel built in 1912 as a lighting factory. Nicolas Berggruen Holdings GmbH bought the site in 2008 (Bethanien history page).
- In 2024 the house marked its 50th anniversary; Antje Weitzel succeeded Tannert and launched the project "Becoming B", which re-examines the house's name, identity and mission (Bethanien history page).
- Visiting: the showrooms at Kottbusser Straße 10 are free and open Wednesday to Sunday, 2 to 7 p.m., during exhibition periods. Studios and office are at Kohlfurter Straße 41 to 43.

FUNDING
Künstlerhaus Bethanien: institutionally funded by the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion, with 32 named partner organisations
- Bethanien states that it is institutionally funded by the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion.
- The partners and sponsors page names 32 partner organisations. They include Arts Council Korea, Canada Council for the Arts, Conseil des Arts et des Lettres du Québec, Creative New Zealand, the Danish Arts Foundation, IASPIS, KfW Stiftung, Mondriaan Fonds, the National Arts Council Singapore, the Office for Contemporary Art Norway, the Ministry of Culture of Taiwan, the Agency for Cultural Affairs of Japan and DAAD Artists-in-Berlin Program.
- Planned cut: on 13 December 2024 the taz (Beate Scheder, in a joint article on Berlin cultural cuts) reported that the Senate planned to cut Bethanien's funding by "fast 150.000 Euro", and that the funding had never covered all costs. Weitzel was quoted: "Residencies haben keine Lobby" (translated: residencies have no lobby).
- In a joint statement with KW Institute for Contemporary Art, n.b.k. and nGbK, Bethanien objected to the Senate's planned 2025 cuts to the Berlin culture budget (n.b.k. website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One open letter in 2019, about a single exhibition.
1. Dazed: Amelia Abraham, "An Afrofuturism exhibition is being criticised for excluding black artists" (31 July 2019)
URL: https://www.dazeddigital.com/art-photography/article/45455/1/afrofuturism-elon-musk-art-exhibition-black-artists-diversity-exclusion-berlin
- The collective Soap Du Jour, which Dazed describes as activists, artists and curators, wrote an open letter to the curator Christoph Tannert about the group show "Space Is The Place" at Bethanien. Of its 22 artists, only one was a person of colour, Song-Ming Ang.
- The letter stated that "not a single black artist could be meaningfully accommodated" in the show, argued that Afrofuturism is about the emancipation of people of colour, and questioned the show's link to Elon Musk, who it said "leads a new kind of colonialism".
- Tannert told artnet News, quoted by Dazed, that more than half of the artists shown at the venue each year are women, some of them women of colour, and that "Curatorial freedom is as valuable as artistic freedom". The house posted on Facebook that the artist list was not diverse and that it took the criticism "very seriously".

BOTTOM LINE
Bethanien states that it is institutionally funded by the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion, and its partners page names 32 partner organisations, among them foreign arts councils and ministries.
