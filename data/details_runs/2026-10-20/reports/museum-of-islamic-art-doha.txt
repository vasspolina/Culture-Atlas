MUSEUM OF ISLAMIC ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: museum-of-islamic-art-doha
Website: https://mia.org.qa (checked 2026-10-24; the about page loads; www.mia.org.qa returned a self-signed certificate error)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Islamic art on an artificial peninsula about 60 metres off the Doha Corniche, surrounded by a park of roughly 290,000 square metres and linked to the mainland by two bridges (Wikipedia).
- One of the first projects initiated by Qatar Museums (stated by the museum); the then-emir opened it on 22 November 2008 (Wikipedia) and it opened to the public on 1 December 2008 (Bidoun; Wikipedia gives 8 December).
- Collection of Islamic art from the 7th to the 20th centuries, from Spain, Egypt, Iran, Iraq, Turkey, India and Central Asia.
- Designed by I. M. Pei, inspired by the fountain of the Ibn Tulun Mosque in Cairo; limestone exterior, five floors, a central tower and dome; the interior galleries were designed by Wilmotte Associates and the building was constructed by the Turkish firm Baytur.
- On site: a library of more than 21,000 books on Islamic art, a cafe and a gift shop (Qatar Living), the IDAM restaurant (Wikipedia) and MIA Park.
- Closed for a ten-month renovation from late 2021 and reopened on 5 October 2022 (museum site); Wikipedia counts 18 renovated galleries.
- Visiting: open Saturday to Tuesday 9am to 7pm, Thursday 9am to 9pm, Friday 1:30pm to 7pm, closed Wednesday (museum site). Admission QAR 50 for non-resident adults; free for Qatar residents with ID and children aged 16 and under (Qatar Living, June 2026; its FAQ gives the non-resident price as typically around QAR 100). The museum's own page lists no prices. No reporting found of wheelchair access details in the sources read.

FUNDING
Museum of Islamic Art: state-owned: owned by Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity; no sponsor or donor named
- Qatar Museums owns the museum (Wikipedia) and is chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site).
- Two unnamed people close to the situation said the museum's costs are ringfenced while budgets were cut elsewhere, after the emir approved cuts to culture and other budgets in early 2016 (The Art Newspaper, Georgina Adam, 10 March 2016).
- Qatar Museums does not disclose its budget and declined to comment on it in 2016 (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor, private donor or published accounts for the museum.
- No official construction cost found. A 2008 blog gives $300 million; no outlet confirms it.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Status: Qatar Museums is "a Qatari government entity", founded in 2005 and formerly the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia). It operates under the patronage of the Amir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani (Qatar Museums site).
- Owner: the museum is owned by Qatar Museums (Wikipedia), which also runs Mathaf and the National Museum of Qatar (Qatar Museums site).
- Leadership: Qatar Museums is chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site, Wikipedia).
- 10 March 2016: citing the Financial Times, The Art Newspaper reported that 240 Qatar Museums staff had been let go, leaving fewer than 800 of 1,200 two years earlier. Qatar Museums declined to confirm the figures.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum as an institution. The critical pieces found concern its displays and the state spending around it; coverage of the opening was mostly descriptive.
1. Bidoun: Antonia Carver, "Museum of Islamic Art" (2009)
URL: https://bidoun.org/articles/museum-of-islamic-art
- The galleries are "almost devoid of information"; labels are minimal and audio guides "offered some description".
- Objects are "imbued with power and meaning more through the aesthetics of display than for their place in history"; astrolabes sit on glass shelves "as though it were an installation".
- Notes the "Bilbao-inspired brand-building potential" of the museum and a desire to offer education to local young people, and calls "building bridges" "a hackneyed concept tacked on to nearly every cultural initiative in the Gulf".
2. The Art Newspaper: Georgina Adam, "Football outscores culture as Qatar spending slumps" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/10/football-outscores-culture-as-qatar-spending-slumps
- Reports the 2016 cuts to Qatar Museums and says the Museum of Islamic Art's costs were ringfenced.
- News report on spending, not a critique of the museum; relies on anonymous sources.
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Critics questioned how public money was used and where often unannounced acquisitions ended up (2013).
- Does not criticise the Museum of Islamic Art; it cites the museum as evidence that Qatar Museums cultivated local and regional culture.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013, social media users led by a Qatari journalist accused the Qatar Museums Authority of corruption, mismanagement and nepotism (openDemocracy, Sarah El-Richani).
- The same article says some Qataris active on social media lamented Damien Hirst's sculptures. It gives two explanations for why Abdessemed's film "Printemps" was not shown at Mathaf: social media reports that Sheikha Mayassa intervened, and officials' statement that there were technical issues.
- The museum is owned by Qatar Museums, the body these accusations name.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Qatari state money channelled through Qatar Museums, with no private or corporate funder named in any source found. The weak points are the lack of published accounts or budget and the museum's place inside a state culture body. Worth watching: whether Qatar Museums ever publishes accounts or a sponsor list.
