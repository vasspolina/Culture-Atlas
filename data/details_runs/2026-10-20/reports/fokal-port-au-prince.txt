FOKAL: Port-au-Prince, Haiti
ID: fokal-port-au-prince
Website: https://fokal.org

OVERVIEW
- Haitian national foundation working in education, human rights, civic engagement, arts and culture and sustainable development, based at 143 Avenue Christophe, Port-au-Prince (FOKAL).
- Name in full: Fondation Connaissance et Liberté / Fondasyon Konesans Ak Libète, the Foundation for Knowledge and Liberty. In January 1995 Aryeh Neier, then president of the Open Society Institute, contacted Michèle Duvivier Pierre-Louis to create it; the foundation was created in April 1995, she appointed seven volunteers as the first board in July 1995 and it received public utility status in 2000 (FOKAL).
- Pierre-Louis was its executive director from 1995 to 2008 and a FOKAL post of 29 May 2025 calls her its president; FOKAL's history page lists Maxime D. Charles as board president since January 2015. Lorraine Mangonès is executive director (FOKAL annual report 2024).
- The annual report 2024 describes FOKAL as a national foundation within the Open Society Foundations, a network of foundations created by George Soros.
- Programmes: grants to civil society, human rights and smallholder farming groups; a library programme, with the Monique Calixte Library moving to Maison Chenet, where new buildings were completed in December 2024; the Centre d'Art; the Dufort House for Haitian art and heritage; the Lekòl Nou education project; and the Martissant Park (annual report 2024).
- The report counts 32,000 library visitors and 1,805 internet users in 2024 among the results of Kellogg-funded work.
- The Martissant Park and neighbourhood initiative ran from 2006 to 2021; from mid-2021 armed gangs took control. The report says the gang war forced the permanent closure of the park to the public and that FOKAL is preparing an exit strategy from the area; in 2024 it kept a small team of gardeners, rural guards and a field agronomist there and 111 scholarships for Martissant children across 7 schools (annual report 2024).

FUNDING
FOKAL: foundation-funded: Open Society Foundations until 2024, now W.K. Kellogg Foundation, Ford Foundation, Citi-CAF, the European Union, the French Development Agency and the diaspora fund Ayiti Demen
- Open Society Foundations: in 2024 OSF gave FOKAL its last grant, after 29 years of support. The financial director, Marie Edmonde Jeune, writes that OSF began an organisational transformation in 2021, FOKAL reduced its budget and staff from 2022 with technical and financial accompaniment from OSF, and in 2024, as OSF's national foundations were phased out, a "generous parting financial package" was offered to FOKAL (annual report 2024).
- The report calls the W.K. Kellogg Foundation its primary supporter for Martissant Park and says the higher education programme has had a three-year contractual grant from it since 2021. It names WKKF, Ford and Citi-CAF as the philanthropic donors FOKAL will strengthen relationships with, and credits key financial partnerships in 2024 with WKKF, Citifoundations, the Ford Foundation, the European Union and the Agence Française de Développement. In its sustainable development section it lists donor withdrawal by OSF, the EU, AFD and USAID.
- Agence Française de Développement finances Lekòl Nou and La Fabrique des Arts; a funding agreement for the latter was signed on 9 November 2022 with FOKAL leading a consortium with the Centre d'Art and Association Quatre Chemins. The Government of Canada funds the civic education project AJULIH with Avocats Sans Frontières Canada. The Fondation de France funded five more videos in the Timoun San Tras project.
- Ayiti Demen, the US non-profit that supports FOKAL, received new contributions of $609,068 in 2024 from over a hundred individuals and from foundations, and awarded grants of nearly $561,617. Its grants came from the Mellon Foundation, the W. Talbott Hillman Foundation, the Congregation of Saint Joseph of Peace, Silicon Valley Community Foundation, Northern Trust Charitable, the Roy and Patricia Disney Foundation, the Wyncote Foundation and the Greene Family Foundation (annual report 2024).
- FOKAL has asked the Haitian government to pay overdue subsidies it owes the Martissant project under decrees and signed conventions; the efforts are ongoing (annual report 2024).
- An external audit firm gave a positive rating to the foundation's administrative and financial management (annual report 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
FOKAL began as an Open Society Foundations national foundation, which gave its last grant in 2024 after 29 years. Its 2024 report names financial partnerships with the Kellogg, Ford and Citifoundations philanthropies, the European Union and the French Development Agency, and US donors reached through Ayiti Demen, which received $609,068 in 2024. Gang control closed Martissant Park to the public.
