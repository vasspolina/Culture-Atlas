CIAP (NOW JESTER): Genk, Belgium
ID: ciap-genk
Website: https://www.jester.be

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary visual art organisation on the C-mine site in Genk, in three pavilions at Schachtboklaan 11, where it has operated since 2023; it hosts residencies, studios, workspaces, exhibitions, screenings and performances (Club Paradis, Jester).
- CIAP was founded in 1976 in Hasselt by a group of art-loving citizens (ArtRabbit) and relocated to C-mine in 2020 (C-mine).
- Jester was formed in 2021 through the merger of FLACC and CIAP (Jester).
- C-mine is the former Winterslag coal mine: the city of Genk bought the site from the Limburg reconversion company LRM in 2001, and the name C-mine dates from 2005 (Wikipedia).
- The new building opened in November 2023 with studios for ceramics, wood and metalwork, a digital lab, an exhibition space, office space and a residency space; VRT reported a total cost of 4.5 million euros (Yente Van der Sande, VRT NWS, 17 November 2023).
- The team is led by director Brenda Guesnet, with Karel Op 't Eynde as curator (Jester).
- The organisation says its work is rooted in the social, economic, demographic and ecological questions of the mining site and its neighbouring multicultural cités (Club Paradis, Daily Lazy).
- Visiting: exhibitions are open Wednesday to Sunday, 13:00 to 18:00; free parking, about 3 km from Genk station (Jester).

FUNDING
Jester (formerly CIAP): public: Flemish Government and City of Genk
- Jester states it is supported by the Flemish Government and the City of Genk (Jester).
- VRT reported that Flanders and the city of Genk both invested heavily in the 4.5 million euro building (VRT).
- The City of Genk bought the C-mine site from LRM in 2001 (Wikipedia).
- The Flemish Government, the City of Genk and the Czech Centre Brussels are listed as supporters of the October 2024 exhibition by Julie Bena and Kamil Bouzoubaa-Grivel (Club Paradis).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind CIAP, now Jester, is public: Jester states it is supported by the Flemish Government and the City of Genk, and VRT reports that both invested in the 4.5 million euro building on the C-mine site, which the city bought in 2001.
