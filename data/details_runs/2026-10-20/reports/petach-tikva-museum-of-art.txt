PETACH TIKVA MUSEUM OF ART: Petach Tikva, Israel
ID: petach-tikva-museum-of-art
Website: https://www.petachtikvamuseum.com

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 30 Arlozorov Street in Petach Tikva, in a museum complex on the outskirts of the city beside Independence Park.
- Established in 1964 and reopened in 2000; Wikipedia says its building first opened in 1952 and reopened in 2004 after renovation.
- Drorit Gur Arie was director and chief curator from 2000 to 2019; Reut Ferster has been director and Dr. Irena Gordon chief curator since 2020.
- The complex also holds the Yad Labanim house for the city's fallen soldiers, the Founders Museum, the Oded Yarkoni Archives, the Institute of Visual Art and an auditorium.
- The museum shows Israeli and international painting, sculpture, photography, video, film, architecture, installation and performance, and holds Israeli art from the 1920s to the present.
- Open Monday, Wednesday and Friday 9:00 to 13:00, Tuesday and Thursday 16:00 to 20:00, Saturday 9:00 to 14:00. The museum states it is accessible to wheelchairs and visitors with mobility disabilities, including elevator services.

FUNDING
Petach Tikva Museum of Art: donor-supported: a Friends association and British Friends of the Art Museums of Israel fund programmes
- The Friends of the Petah Tikva Art Museum Association was founded in 2015 and funds exhibitions, original artworks, additions to the collection, and education and community projects (the museum's Friends page).
- Membership as a Friend costs 1,800 NIS a year; a Patron pays 23,000 NIS and is named on the Donors Page. Larger project donations earn association membership and, by agreement, a credit in museum publications (the museum's Friends page).
- The association's board lists Reut Ferster as chairwoman, with Irena Gordon, Orit Hasson Walder, Irit Levin, Ziv Barnea, Hezie Lavi, Lisa Shiloach-Uzrad, Sigalit Ben Hayoun, Revital Peretz Ben-Asher and Rachel Shaul Danzig as members (the museum's Friends page).
- British Friends of the Art Museums of Israel supports the museum's programmes; its page lists four 2024 programmes: Art Bus, Art Education and Society, A Meeting with Art, and an animation-based educational programme for 2019 and 2020 (BFAMI).

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2014: The New York Review of Books (Marisa Mazria Katz and Mairav Zonszein, "Israel's War on Culture", 22 March 2018) reports that after pressure from a few right-wing activists and a group called Mothers of Soldiers Against B'Tselem, the Petah Tikva municipality called on the museum to close the video installation of Arkadi Zaides's dance piece Archive, which used footage of soldiers and settlers in the West Bank supplied by B'Tselem. The museum refused.
- The Musrara festival site says the video installation Capture Practice, which came from the same research by Zaides as Archive, was commissioned by the museum in 2014.

BOTTOM LINE
The documented money is private giving: the Friends association, whose memberships cost 1,800 and 23,000 NIS a year, and British Friends of the Art Museums of Israel, which supports named education programmes. In 2014 the museum refused the Petah Tikva municipality's call to close a Zaides video installation, according to The New York Review of Books.
