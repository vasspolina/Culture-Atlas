MOSAIC ROOMS: London, UK
ID: mosaic-rooms-london
Website: https://www.mosaicrooms.org

OVERVIEW
- Gallery and cultural space for art from the Arab region at 226 Cromwell Road, Kensington, London SW5 0SW (Mosaic Rooms website, The Art Newspaper).
- Launched in November 2008 as a project of the A.M. Qattan Foundation, conceived by foundation trustee Omar al-Qattan; on 15 January 2026 the A.M. Qattan Foundation announced that it would operate as an independent organisation and a legal entity distinct from the foundation (A.M. Qattan Foundation).
- Registered UK charity number 1183882; trustees are Dr Selma Taysir Dabbagh (chair), Lamiaa Soliman (treasurer), Celeste Ricci, Maisha Akil Scafe-Smith, Dr Samaneh Moafi, Christopher Rawcliffe, Marwan Kaabour and Zainab Sahar Beyad (Mosaic Rooms website).
- The building is 19th century; a year-long refurbishment added a recording suite, a salon for workshops and lectures, a playroom for local families, a bigger bookshop and café, better gallery access and a redesigned garden that is now the route to the new entrance, with a permanent stained-glass commission by Dima Srouji, "Four Moons from Home" (2026) (The Art Newspaper).
- Reopened on 18 February 2026 with a solo show by Bouchra Khalili (The Art Newspaper).
- Director Pip Day was previously director of the Leonard & Bina Ellen Art Gallery at Concordia University in Montreal, which dismissed her in late 2024 after less than six months. The Art Newspaper reports that local media linked the dismissal to her support for Palestine and that the university said it could not discuss employee matters. The Link (Maria Cholakova, 16 September 2025) reports that on 10 October 2024 Concordia's Campus Safety and Prevention Services postponed a screening scheduled by the gallery and a partner group, and that five advisory council members resigned in January 2025.

FUNDING
Mosaic Rooms: private: the A.M. Qattan Foundation and the Al-Qattan Charitable Trust
- The A.M. Qattan Foundation, based in the UK and Ramallah, fully funded the Mosaic Rooms until the 2026 change (The Art Newspaper).
- The Al-Qattan Charitable Trust, the foundation's largest donor, paid for the year-long refurbishment (The Art Newspaper). The Mosaic Rooms website says it "is generously supported by Al-Qattan Charitable Trust".
- The Mosaic Rooms re-incorporated as a charity so that it can seek private philanthropy and public grants; director Pip Day says the goal was to transition into a public institution that belongs to London rather than to the family (The Art Newspaper).
- The Mosaic Rooms invites funding from individuals, trusts, foundations and corporate sponsors (Mosaic Rooms website).
- The Qattan family money: Wikipedia's article on Omar al-Qattan says his father Abdel Mohsin Al-Qattan first joined the Kuwaiti government as a senior manager and later founded a construction company, and that Omar has been deputy chair of the family firm, Al-Hani Construction and Trading, since 2005.

BOTTOM LINE
The Art Newspaper reports that the A.M. Qattan Foundation fully funded the Mosaic Rooms to date, and the Al-Qattan Charitable Trust, the foundation's largest donor, paid for the 2026 refurbishment. According to Wikipedia, the family's money comes from a Kuwaiti construction company founded by Abdel Mohsin Al-Qattan.
