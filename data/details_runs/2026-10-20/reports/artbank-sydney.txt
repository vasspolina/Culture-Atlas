ARTBANK: Sydney, Australia
ID: artbank-sydney
Website: https://www.artbank.gov.au/

OVERVIEW
- Australian Government art-leasing programme that buys works from living Australian artists and leases them to businesses, government agencies and individuals. Collection stores are in Sydney, Melbourne and Perth (Wikipedia).
- Established by the Australian Government in 1980, modelled on Canada's Art Bank. Arts minister Bob Ellicott saw the Ottawa collection in 1979 and persuaded Prime Minister Malcolm Fraser; the Treasurer, John Howard, approved A$250,000 in seed funding (Wikipedia).
- The collection began with 600 works from the National Gallery of Australia (Wikipedia). It had grown to 10,950 works by the time of the 2023 audit (Government News).
- The Melbourne store opened in Armadale in 1996 and moved to a converted warehouse in Collingwood in 2018, which also hosts a studio programme for artists and designers (Wikipedia).
- Leases run six months to a year at A$165 to A$5,500 a year, and businesses can claim a 100% tax deduction for leasing (Wikipedia).
- Part of the Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications and the Arts (Canberra Times); Wikipedia names Zoe Rodriguez as director as of October 2023.

FUNDING
Artbank: self-funded by leasing income, inside a federal department
- Wikipedia reports that by 1992 the programme was profitable enough for government funding to be cut, and that it has since operated on self-generated income.
- Revenue in 2021 was A$2.8 million, of which A$566,000 (20%) was spent on 71 acquisitions (Government News, on the 2023 audit).
- More than 4,350 works were leased to 390 clients in 2021 to 2022 (Government News).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In April 2023 the Australian National Audit Office (Auditor-General Grant Hehir) found that the department's handling of Artbank "has not been appropriate" (Government News, Canberra Times).
- Acquisitions did not follow the Commonwealth Procurement Rules and skipped open or competitive processes; value for money was not documented or demonstrated, and public reporting was limited (Region Canberra, Canberra Times).
- About 40% of the collection was leased against a target of 70%; 30% was never leased from July 2015 to June 2022 and a further 29% was leased for less than half the time it was available (Government News, Region Canberra, Canberra Times).
- Approvals for removing over 70 works were obtained after the fact, though the works had been sold more than four years earlier (Region Canberra).
- Acquisition spending fell from A$507,993 for 179 works in 2016 to 2017 to A$396,462 for 75 works in 2021 to 2022; applications to the artist pitch roadshows fell from 40 in 2016 to three in 2021 (Government News).
- The department defended its approach by citing a rule allowing "limited tender". Its secretary, Jim Betts, wrote that he was "concerned that aspects of the narrative may unduly and adversely impact the reputation and operation of Artbank" (Canberra Times).
- Connection to Artbank: the audit examined Artbank's own acquisitions, leasing and records, which the department runs.

BOTTOM LINE
Artbank runs on leasing income inside a federal department. The weak points are governance and procurement, documented by the April 2023 national audit, which found leasing below target and acquisitions outside the procurement rules.
