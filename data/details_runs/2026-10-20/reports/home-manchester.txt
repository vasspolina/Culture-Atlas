HOME: Manchester, UK
ID: home-manchester
Website: https://www.homemcr.org

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre with cinemas, theatres and gallery space on First Street in Manchester (Wikipedia).
- Formed by the merger of two Manchester arts organisations, Cornerhouse and the Library Theatre Company; the building was completed in April 2015 and officially opened on 21 May 2015 (Wikipedia).
- HOME is a trading name of Greater Manchester Arts Centre Ltd, a charity (514719) and company limited by guarantee (homemcr.org).
- Designed by the Dutch firm Mecanoo of Delft; the project cost about £25 million (Wikipedia).
- On site: five cinema screens, a 500-seat theatre, a 150-seat flexible studio and gallery space for contemporary art (Wikipedia). The building opens at 10am Monday to Saturday and at 11am on Sundays (homemcr.org).
- Dave Moutrey, long-time chief executive and artistic director, has left; HOME is led by joint chief executives Karen O'Neill and Sita McIntosh (The Mill, 7 April 2024).

FUNDING
HOME: Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation under a service contract with Manchester City Council, with trusts and patrons
- HOME is an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation and operates under a service contract with Manchester City Council (Wikipedia).
- Manchester City Council, the Garfield Weston Foundation and Arts Council England funded the building project (Wikipedia).
- Income was £7,202,451 and spending £6,628,759 in the year to 31 March 2025 (Charity Commission data via Find that Charity).
- Founding supporters named on the Our Supporters page: the Garfield Weston Foundation, the Granada Foundation, Weightmans, Transport for Greater Manchester, the Oglesby Charitable Trust, Manchester School of Art, the Wolfson Foundation and the University of Salford. Founding patrons include Arnold and Brenda Bradshaw and Stephen and Jane Sorrell (homemcr.org).

Red flags: cancellation of a Gazan writing event, March to April 2024
- HOME announced "Voices of Resilience", an event of Gazan writing organised with Comma Press and due on 22 April 2024, then cancelled it itself, saying "In the face of recent publicity around Voices of Resilience, we have cancelled this event" and citing the safety of its team, audiences and artists (The Art Newspaper, 4 April 2024).
- The Jewish Representative Council of Greater Manchester wrote to HOME claiming that participating writer Atef Abu Saif is antisemitic and objecting to the word "genocide" in the publicity. Comma Press called the allegations "baseless and libellous" and agreed to remove "genocide" from the event's materials (The Art Newspaper, The Mill).
- More than 300 artists, curators and cultural workers signed an open letter to HOME saying that cancelling an event based on the ethnicity and nationality of its participants is discrimination; about 100 artists removed work from a HOME display in protest (The Art Newspaper, citing the BBC).
- HOME reversed the cancellation on 4 April 2024, apologised for distress caused and said it no longer had safety concerns; its statement of 5 April said the event would take place at HOME, created and curated by Comma Press, and it was held on 22 April 2024 (The Mill, The Art Newspaper, World Socialist Web Site for the 4 April date, Wikipedia).
- Partisan: the World Socialist Web Site called the cancellation a "cowardly capitulation" to pressure from local supporters of Israel and the reversal a "political victory" (Robert Stevens, 5 April 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism concerns one episode, the 2024 cancellation of the Gazan writing event, and is reported by The Art Newspaper and The Mill.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Cancelled Palestinian event at Manchester venue must be reinstated, say artists and curators" (4 April 2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/04/04/cancelled-palestinian-event-at-manchester-venue-must-be-reinstated-say-artists-and-curators
- Reports the open letter of more than 300 signatories, including Phil Collins and Gavin Wade, that calls the cancellation discrimination and asks HOME to explain what it will do to repair its commitment to anti-racism, including anti-Palestinian racism.
- Reports the Jewish Representative Council of Greater Manchester's letter to HOME and HOME's statement that it cancelled the event after recent publicity, citing safety.
2. The Mill: Mollie Simpson, "Too close to HOME? Inside the meltdown over an event about 'genocide' in Gaza" (7 April 2024)
URL: https://manchestermill.co.uk/too-close-to-home-inside-the-meltdown/
- Sets out the sequence from the 25 March announcement to the reversal, including the sticker over the word "genocide" on the book cover; sources told The Mill that HOME's co-chief executive Karen O'Neill suggested withdrawing the book from the event.

BOTTOM LINE
HOME is funded as an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation under a service contract with Manchester City Council, with trusts and patrons named on its supporters page; its income was £7.2 million in the year to March 2025. The documented issue is the March 2024 cancellation and reversal of the Gazan writing event, which HOME itself decided.
