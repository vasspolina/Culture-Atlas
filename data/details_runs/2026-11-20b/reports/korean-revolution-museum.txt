KOREAN REVOLUTION MUSEUM: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: korean-revolution-museum
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State history museum on Mansu Hill in central Pyongyang, next to the Mansudae Assembly Hall, with its back façade facing the Mansu Hill Grand Monument (Wikipedia).
- Founded on 1 August 1948 in the Central District of Pyongyang on Kim Il Sung's initiative, as the National Central Liberation Struggle Museum; renamed in August 1960 (RFA).
- Opened to the public in August 1955, reopened in January 1961 east of Kim Il Sung Square, and moved to Mansu Hill in April 1972 for Kim Il Sung's 60th birthday (RFA).
- RFA gave its size as 50,000 square metres on a 240,000 square metre site (my translation from the Korean).
- KCNA counted more than 90 rooms in 2008, from the 1860s to the present, with tens of thousands of relics and two large semi-panoramic paintings, Pochonbo Battle and Defensive Battle on Height 1,211; RFA's 2016 programme counts 100 rooms.
- Visitors: KCNA claimed more than 27 million since its founding (2008).
- Wikipedia dates a renovation with a new floor to 2011 and 2012 and a major renovation completed in 2017. State media reported on 28 March 2017 that Kim Jong Un toured the renovated museum, where he embraced its curator, Hwang Sun Hui (North Korea Leadership Watch).
- South Korea's Ministry of Unification records that Kim Jong Un thanked the officials and workers who refitted the museum in November 2019, January 2020 and June 2020, and lists opening ceremonies at the museum in March 2017 and, for the Party's 75th anniversary, in October 2020.
- A new hall opened in April 2022 for the tenth anniversary of Kim Jong Un's rise to power (Korea Herald, from Yonhap).

FUNDING
Korean Revolution Museum: state-funded: the Government of North Korea, with KCNA tying it to the Workers' Party
- KCNA described the museum in 2008 as a centre of ideological education of the Workers' Party of Korea.
- Leadership: the Ministry of Unification records that Kim Jong Un paid his respects in January 2020 at the bier of Hwang Sun Hui, the museum's late director. Its 2022 directory leaves the director's post blank and lists Pak Kyong Sik (박경식) as first deputy director and Kim Chun Il (김춘일) as primary Party committee secretary (my romanisations).
- Gifts to staff: in August 2017 a ceremony handed gifts to the museum's lecturers and staff, with Kim Jong Im, later described as former Party History Institute director, giving the speech (Ministry of Unification, 2022 personnel profiles).
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government and the Workers' Party, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).
- The museum is a state institution, and KCNA ties it to the Party the order names.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art criticism. One radio programme analyses the museum's political purpose; two pieces report on its renovation and new hall.
1. Radio Free Asia (Korean service): Lee Hyun-ki with Im Chae-uk, "Memorial museums of North and South Korea" (2016)
URL: https://www.rfa.org/korean/weekly_program/d1b5c77cbb38d654c0b0cc45/uniculture-11102016145608.html
- Im, introduced as a critic of North Korean culture, argues that memorial museums exist to publicise a state's legitimacy or a regime's justification (my translation).
- Says much of the display centres on Kim Il Sung, and sets South Korea's Independence Hall in Cheonan, opened in August 1987, beside it as the counterpart.
2. Korea Herald (Yonhap): "N. Korea opens new exhibition hall to mark 10th anniv. of Kim's leadership" (2022)
URL: https://www.koreaherald.com/view.php?ud=20220411000167
- Reports, from KCNA, photos and videos of Kim Jong Un's "achievements of immortal leadership" since his May 2016 pledge to build a responsible nuclear weapons state.
- News report; it does not criticise the museum.
3. North Korea Leadership Watch: "KJU Visits Korean Revolution Museum" (2017)
URL: https://web.archive.org/web/2020/http://www.nkleadershipwatch.org/2017/03/31/kju-visits-korean-revolution-museum/
- Summarises state media on the tour and names the Party and state officials who attended; a summary of state claims, not criticism. Read from an archived copy.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the North Korean state's, and KCNA presents it as a Party institution. The weak point is that the US blocking order covers both that state and that Party.
