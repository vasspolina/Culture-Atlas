ESPERANZA BASE MUSEUM: Hope Bay, Antarctica
ID: esperanza-base-museum
Website: https://www.argentina.gob.ar/ejercito/antartida/base-esperanza (checked 2026-11-20; Argentine Army page on the base)

OVERVIEW
- A small museum inside Esperanza Base, a year-round Argentine station at Punta Foca on Hope Bay, at the north-eastern tip of the Antarctic Peninsula; the base also has a post office, a chapel, a school and the radio station LRA 36 (Ministry of Defence, 17 Dec 2023; Argentine Army).
- The base was inaugurated in December 1952 as Bahía Esperanza Army Base by Captain Jorge Edgar Leal, later leader of the Argentine overland expedition to the South Pole (Ministry of Foreign Affairs).
- In 1978 the Fortín Sargento Cabral village of seven families was added; Emilio Marcos Palma, born there on 7 January 1978, was the first documented human birth in Antarctica (Ministry of Foreign Affairs). The chapel of San Francisco de Asís opened on 28 February 1976 (Ministry of Foreign Affairs).
- More than forty buildings, including the Argentine Antarctic Institute's Esperanza Multidisciplinary Laboratory and a seismic station run with the Trieste geophysical observatory; research covers glaciology, geology, palaeontology, limnology and biology (Ministry of Foreign Affairs).
- In December 2023 the base chief, Lieutenant Colonel Gustavo Cordero, said 66 people lived there: officers and NCOs of the three armed forces, civilian staff and children (Ministry of Defence). Six families with 18 children arrived for the 2023 winter (Argentine government, 12 Apr 2023).
- Visiting: an occasional landing on cruise itineraries; residents give tours of the base and the museum and set out refreshments and a few items for sale. Oceanwide Expeditions puts visitors at upwards of 1,100 tourists a year. Remains of the Swedish Antarctic Expedition of 1901 to 1903 are nearby (Oceanwide Expeditions).

FUNDING
Esperanza Base museum: state: the Argentine armed forces' base, paid for by the Argentine state
- Operator: now the Esperanza Joint Antarctic Base, staffed by the three armed forces and civilian staff of the Joint Chiefs of Staff; Defence Minister Luis Petri addressed its staff on the base's 71st anniversary (Ministry of Defence, 2023; Argentine government, 2025).
- Supply: the Argentine Navy icebreaker ARA Almirante Irízar delivers the winter fuel and carries the wintering families to the base (Argentine government, 2023 and 2025).
- Science: the National Antarctic Directorate and Argentine Antarctic Institute run the laboratory, with tide gauge work under an agreement with the US NOAA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs).
- School: Provincial School No. 38 "Presidente Raúl Ricardo Alfonsín" has been part of the education ministry of Tierra del Fuego province since mid-1997 (Argentine government, 2023).
- Earned income: residents sell a small selection of items to cruise visitors (Oceanwide Expeditions).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Army states that the base's work aims "to consolidate the country's sovereignty in the Antarctic Territory and make our position known to the other countries of the world" (Argentine Army; translated from Spanish). The Ministry of Defence says it was founded to build a village of families that would consolidate Argentina's sovereign rights over its Antarctic territory (Ministry of Defence, 2023).
- Argentina's Antarctic claim overlaps British and Chilean claims, and none has wide international recognition (Wikipedia).
- On 1 February 1952 an Argentine Navy party fired machine guns over the heads of a British Falkland Islands Dependencies Survey team at Hope Bay; Argentina later apologised (Wikipedia).
- The museum is part of the base that the state set up to back this claim.

BOTTOM LINE
A museum inside a military base, paid for by the Argentine state, which says openly that the base exists to back its contested Antarctic claim. The weak point is that the museum serves the claim. Watch how it presents the 1952 Hope Bay clash and the British and Chilean claims.
