AZ MUSEUM (ANATOLY ZVEREV MUSEUM): Moscow, Russia
ID: az-museum-anatoly-zverev-museum-moscow
Website: https://museum-az.com

OVERVIEW
- Private single-artist museum at 2nd Tverskaya-Yamskaya 20-22, Moscow, between the Mayakovskaya and Belorusskaya metro stations (museum site). Snob describes silver letters on a beige marble facade near Mayakovsky Square.
- Opened in May 2015; Natalia Opaleva is founder and general director, and Polina Lobachevskaya is co-organiser and art director (Vedomosti, Snob, Interfax). Snob says Lobachevskaya had curated a major Zverev show at the Tretyakov Gallery in 1999.
- Named for the artist Anatoly Zverev; Snob describes the facade lettering "AZ" as his initials.
- Building: a mansion of about 500 square metres on three storeys with three exhibition halls (Forbes Russia); Vedomosti reports a small house reconstructed to high European standards.
- Collection: Opaleva's Zverev collection, over 1,500 works according to Forbes Russia and about 1,500 according to Snob, and a gift from Alika Kostaki, daughter of the collector Georgy Costakis, of more than 700 works (Vedomosti), given in 2013 (Interfax). The museum closed for renovation and reopened on 21 November 2025 with "Anatoly Zverev. Open Storage," more than 260 works, on view until 4 October 2026 (Interfax); the museum site says it is extended until 1 November. Interfax adds a third-floor space with a pop-up cinema, concert space, lecture hall and workshop space.
- Visiting: open daily 12:00 to 21:00; Monday admission is free; closed on the first Tuesday of each month; tickets are sold at the museum box office (museum site).

FUNDING
AZ Museum: self-funded: the founder's own money
- Forbes Russia (Viktoriya Kostoeva, 19 January 2016) says the $7.5 million mansion was paid for entirely from Opaleva's own funds and that she treats the museum as a charity project, not a business. Asked who funds the museum, she told Vedomosti (27 May 2015) that a private museum runs on "private money" (translation).
- Forbes Russia (2016) reports tickets were 400 rubles with half price on weekday daytimes, and about 5,000 visitors since the museum opened.
- Opaleva's other roles (Forbes Russia, 2016): vice-president of Lanta-Bank and holder of 20.36% of the gold-mining company Vysochaishy.
- Kostaki gift: Alika Kostaki gave more than 700 works collected by her father, about half the museum's holdings (Vedomosti); Snob puts the gift at about 700 works.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Lanta-Bank, where Forbes Russia described Opaleva as vice-president in 2016: OpenSanctions says the bank "is subject to sanctions" and lists it as sanctioned in 10 sources, including the US OFAC SDN list, the EU Financial Sanctions Files and the UK FCDO list. It lists Sergey Dokuchaev as chairman of the management board since 1 February 2010.
- OpenSanctions records Opaleva as a person in the Russian company register; its page says she "has not been found on international sanctions lists" and is not linked to any risk indications in its database. It shows her holding 33.34% of the Moscow company Legion K LLC since 3 October 2012, with Dokuchaev listed at 33.33% from the same date. The Legion K page says the company has not been found on international sanctions lists; its sanction-ownership tags come from graph-based tagging that the page calls a non-official source.
- Connection to the museum: Opaleva is the museum's founder, and Forbes Russia says she paid for the mansion from her own funds.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded by its founder, Natalia Opaleva, from her own money. OpenSanctions lists Lanta-Bank, where Forbes Russia described her as vice-president in 2016, as sanctioned, and says she herself has not been found on sanctions lists.
