KAMPALA ART BIENNALE: Kampala, Uganda
ID: kampala-art-biennale
Website: https://kampalabiennale.org (checked 2026-10-24; online, now an archive of past editions)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale based in Kampala, with a stated focus on East African art; its tagline is "Expose | Educate | Exchange."
- Founded in 2014 by Daudi Karungi, its founding director, through the Kampala Arts Trust, a collective of visual and performance art practitioners (Biennial Foundation). Produced by Kampala Arts Trust and Afriart Gallery (stated by the biennale).
- Editions: KAB14 "Progressive Africa" (2014), KAB16 "Seven Hills" (2016), KAB18 "The Studio" (2018) and KAB20 "Get Up, Stand Up!" (2020).
- The biennale states that KAB14 and KAB16 together drew over 30,000 visitors.
- No building of its own: the 2016 edition used the Uganda Museum and the 2018 edition a warehouse (The Independent, Uganda; Africanah).
- Simon Njami curated KAB18 and KAB20; KAB18 paired seven "master" artists with young apprentices in ten-day studios (Contemporary And). KAB20 went online as a virtual 3-D exhibition because of COVID-19 (EU Delegation).
- No edition since 2020; the organisers say they remain open to future editions. There is nothing to visit now.

FUNDING
Kampala Art Biennale: project-funded: foreign cultural funds, the EU and Pro Helvetia (2020), TotalEnergies Foundation support for the 2018 mentoring programme
- KAB20 (2020): co-funded by the European Union and Pro Helvetia, the Swiss arts foundation (stated on the biennale's Partners page). The EU Delegation confirmed its support on 19 February 2021. No amount is stated.
- KAB18 (2018): the TotalEnergies Foundation supported the mentoring programme, in which seven African artists hosted 35 young artists in residence for ten days (stated by the foundation). The foundation states no amount and no years of support. No reporting found of TotalEnergies money for any later edition.
- Earlier editions: the Partners page shows only logos for KAB14, KAB16 and KAB18 funders, so none is named.
- Kampala Arts Trust and Afriart Gallery produce the biennale. No reporting found of accounts, annual reports or the amount of money either put in.
- No reporting found of Ugandan government funding. Start Journal (2019) says the organisers asked how the biennale could become sustainable and win government support for art.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2018 edition's curatorial model, not on money. The curator and one participant defended the 2018 model; no critique of the biennale's funders was found.
1. Contemporary And: Enos Nyamor, "From Missionaries to Artists" (2018)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/magazines/from-missionaries-to-artists/
- Argues the 2018 edition reflects European dominance, from the title "The Studio" to its Europe-based curator Simon Njami, who shaped its concept.
- Argues the master and apprentice model erases the young artists' contribution, since all works are credited to the masters, and that the show was detached from Kampala's political and economic realities.
- Calls the masters' interventions missionary-style. Names Njami and the masters; the article names no funders of the biennale.
2. Contemporary And: Simon Njami, "Simon Njami: 'The masters acted as mentors, big brothers, uncles'" (2018)
URL: https://www.contemporaryand.com/fr/c-and-magazine/texts/simon-njami-the-masters-acted-as-mentors-big-brothers-uncles
- Not criticism: the curator's answer to Nyamor. He says masters and apprentices are not exclusively Western concepts, and that his outsider position helps him avoid ethnocentrism.
3. Africanah: Matt Kayem, "The relevance of the Kampala Biennial 2018" (2018)
URL: https://africanah.org/the-relevance-of-the-kampala-biennial-2018
- A qualified defence. Records the criticism that the model was demeaning, echoed colonial patterns and had no Ugandan among the masters. Calls some of that criticism "egotistically driven."
- Kayem took part in the biennale as an apprentice. Names no funders.
4. Start Journal: Dominic Muwanguzi, "Curator: Friend or Foe of Art" (2019)
URL: https://startjournal.org/2019/04/curator-friend-or-foe-of-art/
- Reports local objections to Njami choosing masters from outside the continent and to a poster that used Courbet's "The Painter's Studio" rather than local masters' work. Says the model answered the organisers' question of how to win government support, an intention undermined by the criticism.
- Names no funders.
5. The Independent (Uganda): "Independent Reporter," on the 2016 edition (2016)
URL: https://www.independent.co.ug/kampala-biennale-makes-bold-moves
- Reports the 2016 decision to exclude painting for installations and performance; passers-by were puzzled by a 50-hour performance at the Uganda Museum. Reactions were mixed.
- News report with mild criticism of the programme, not of the funders; the byline reads only "Independent Reporter."

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the biennale is mostly foreign cultural funds: the EU and Pro Helvetia in 2020, and the TotalEnergies Foundation for the 2018 mentoring programme. That support is eight years old and no later TotalEnergies money was found, so it stays a funding fact. The weak points are the lack of published accounts and funder names for 2014 to 2018, and the argument over a Europe-based curator.
