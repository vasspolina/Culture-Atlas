STATE MUSEUM OF ARTS OF UZBEKISTAN: Tashkent, Uzbekistan
ID: state-museum-of-arts-of-uzbekistan-tashkent
Website: https://stateartmuseum.uz

OVERVIEW
- National art museum in central Tashkent, at 16 Amir Temur Avenue (Wikipedia). Its collection numbers more than 100,000 works (Designboom).
- Founded in 1918 as the Museum of People University, later renamed the Central Arts Museum; named the Tashkent Art Museum in 1924 and the Museum of Arts of Uzbekistan in 1935 (Wikipedia).
- Its first collection was a hundred works of art from Grand Duke Nicholas Konstantinovich of Russia and other private collections (Wikipedia).
- The current building, completed in 1974 and renovated in 1997, was designed by the Soviet architects I. Abdulov, A.K. Nikiforov and S.A. Rosenblum after the 1966 Tashkent earthquake destroyed the previous museum. It is cube-shaped, with aluminium-clad metal framing and a lower section of grey marble (Wikipedia).
- Four departments: national applied art, fine arts of Uzbekistan, Russian and Western art, and Far Eastern art (Wikipedia).
- In July 2026 UNESCO added the museum, with nine other modernist sites in Tashkent, to the World Heritage List as "Tashkent Modernist Architecture" (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia reports that in 2014 the chief curator Mirfayz Usmonov was caught selling artworks on the black market over 15 years and replacing them with copies; he was sentenced to nine years in prison and two other employees to eight years each.
- New building: the Art and Culture Development Foundation (ACDF) is the commissioner of a Tadao Ando project. Its own page describes the State Museum of Art reconstruction as a doubling of total area to about 20,000 square metres, with 7,000 square metres of exhibition space, storage for more than 100,000 archival objects and a 1.1-hectare public park around the building. Designboom and Fergana call the project the National Museum of Uzbekistan; Designboom gives it 40,038 square metres gross, with more than 8,500 square metres of exhibition halls, a library, conservation laboratories and a rooftop terrace, between the Abulkasym Madrasa, the Istiqlol Art Palace and the Alisher Navoi monument, with exhibition design by Atelier Brückner. Ground was broken on 27 August 2025 and opening is scheduled for March 2028 (Designboom).

FUNDING
State Museum of Arts of Uzbekistan: state: the new building is commissioned by the government-backed Art and Culture Development Foundation
- ACDF: established in 2017 by decree of President Shavkat Mirziyoyev and operating under the Cabinet of Ministers (Sharjah Architecture Triennial). Hyperallergic calls it state-funded. Gayane Umerova is its chairperson (ACDF website).
- ACDF lists the reconstruction of the State Museum of Art in Tashkent among its projects (ACDF website).
- The ground-breaking on 27 August 2025 was led by Mirziyoyev, according to the presidential press service as reported by Fergana. Design is by Tadao Ando Architect & Associates with Atelier Brückner; the general contractor is China's CSCEC International Construction (Fergana).
- The Spectator Australia (3 January 2026) says ACDF has commissioned two new national museums to open by 2028, one in Tashkent designed by Ando and one in Samarkand.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hyperallergic (Nevdon Jamgochian, "The Bukhara Biennial Will Break Your Heart", 27 October 2025) calls the Bukhara Biennial, supported by the state-funded Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation, a case of artwashing and says its existence "depends on a restrictive autocratic structure". It says Amnesty International continues to report widespread censorship and harassment of journalists in Uzbekistan. The piece does not name the Tashkent museum.
- The Spectator Australia (Alex Diggins, "Am I a useful idiot visiting Uzbekistan's first art biennial?", 3 January 2026) quotes an unnamed artist who said the biennial had "art-washy vibes" and felt "for the government, not the people". The author's trip was sponsored by ACDF, which the piece calls the show's organisers. The piece does not name the Tashkent museum.
- Connection: ACDF, which supports the Bukhara Biennial (Hyperallergic) and organises it (The Spectator Australia), is the body commissioning the new building (Designboom; ACDF website).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state institution, and its new building is commissioned by ACDF, a foundation created by presidential decree and chaired by Gayane Umerova. ACDF's other programmes, above all the Bukhara Biennial, draw artwashing criticism in the art press.
