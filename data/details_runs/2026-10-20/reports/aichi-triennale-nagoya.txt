AICHI TRIENNALE: Nagoya, Japan
ID: aichi-triennale-nagoya
Website: https://aichitriennale.jp/ (checked 2026-10-20; the record's website field points to an ArtAsiaPacific news page instead)

OVERVIEW
- International art festival held every three years across Aichi Prefecture, centred on the Aichi Arts Center in Nagoya; the archive lists editions from 2010 to 2025.
- Organised by the International Art Festival Aichi Organizing Committee, chaired in 2025 by Takeo Obayashi, chairman of Obayashi Corporation; its secretariat is staffed by Aichi Prefecture's culture department (2025 report).
- The 2025 edition, "A Time Between Ashes and Roses", ran from 13 September to 30 November 2025 under artistic director Hoor Al Qasimi, at the Aichi Arts Center, the Aichi Prefectural Ceramic Museum and the streets of Seto.
- 2025 showed 62 artists and groups and drew 524,069 visitors (2025 report).
- Shabbir Hussain Mustafa was named artistic director for Aichi 2028 in July 2026.
- In 2025 a one-day pass cost 2,100 yen general and 1,400 yen for students during the show, and a free pass 3,500 and 2,300 yen; booked school groups entered free (2025 report).

FUNDING
Aichi Triennale: mainly public: Aichi Prefecture, with ticket sales and a small share of sponsorship
- The 2025 budget was 1,287,851,000 yen, of which Aichi Prefecture paid 1,130,866,000 yen and Seto city 5,000,000 yen; tickets brought 125,185,000 yen and sponsorship, donations and grants 26,799,000 yen (2025 report, budget figures as of March 2026).
- Aichi Prefecture also paid about 42.5 million yen in 2023 and 137.3 million yen in 2024 for preparation (2025 report).
- 2025 grant-givers included the Agency for Cultural Affairs, the Sharjah Government, the UAE Ministry, the Obayashi Foundation and the British Council (2025 report).
- 2025 special partners were Aso, Elecom, Obayashi Corporation, Daimaru Matsuzakaya and Mori Building; platinum sponsors were Eugenio López and Fundación Jumex and Melco Group (2025 report).
- The 2025 budget lists no contribution from Nagoya city.

Red flags: state punishment over the 2019 censorship row
- The section "After 'Freedom of Expression?'" closed on 3 August 2019 after threats over Kim Seo-kyung and Kim Eun-sung's "Statue of a Girl of Peace"; police arrested a man over an arson threat (Hyperallergic).
- The Agency for Cultural Affairs refused the full 78 million yen grant, then paid 66 million yen, about 85% (ArtAsiaPacific, 2020).
- Nagoya under Mayor Takashi Kawamura withheld about 33.8 million yen of its 171 million yen share; the Supreme Court rejected Nagoya's final appeal on 6 March 2024 and ruled the refusal illegal (FCCJ).

Red flags: 2025 protest over the Aichi-Israel Matching Program
- Activists entered an opening event to demand an end to the prefecture's programme pairing Israeli startups with Aichi firms, which Aichi Prefecture funds alongside the triennale; the paper notes the programme has no direct ties to the triennale (The Art Newspaper).
- The letter named Hideyuki Tomita, committee vice chairman and president of Toho Technology, a programme participant; he resigned citing "personal reasons" (The Art Newspaper).
- Artists including Basel Abbas, Dala Nasser and Michael Rakowitz signed the open letter (The Art Newspaper). Nine of the 62 artists signed a letter saying the triennale "must not be used to artwash the systems of corporate greed and militarization" (e-flux).

Red flags: racecourse and gas utility sponsors in 2025
- Gold sponsor Nagoya Keiba owns Chukyo Racecourse and earns almost all its income renting it to the Japan Racing Association and the Aichi racing association (2025 report, Nagoya Keiba).
- Silver sponsor Toho Gas supplies city gas in Aichi, Gifu and Mie; Chubu Electric Power was a gold sponsor (2025 report).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Heavy coverage of the 2019 censorship and the 2025 Israel protest.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Artists Demand Removal of Their Works From Aichi Trienniale in Open Letter" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/513296/aichi-trienniale-artists-open-letter
- Ten artists, including Tania Bruguera and curator Pedro Reyes, asked to withdraw their works in solidarity and called the closure "an unacceptable act of censorship"; 72 of 90 artists signed an earlier protest.
2. Number 1 Shimbun (FCCJ): David McNeill, "State of the art" (2024)
URL: https://www.fccj.or.jp/number-1-shimbun-article/state-art-0
- Calls Kawamura "arguably" the political ringleader and reads the pressure on the triennale as part of a broader campaign to realign history with Japanese government objectives.
3. e-flux Criticism: Max Crosbie-Jones, "Aichi Triennale 2025, 'A Time Between Ashes and Roses'" (2025)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782330/aichi-triennale-2025-a-time-between-ashes-and-roses
- Questions whether the hopeful Adonis framing fits conditions in Gaza; says the opening solidarity gestures relate to optics.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival is mostly paid for by Aichi Prefecture (about 88% of the 2025 budget), and its sponsors give little by comparison. The weak points are its exposure to state punishment, shown in 2019, the 2025 protest over the prefecture's Israel programme, and current sponsorship from a racecourse owner and gas and power utilities.
