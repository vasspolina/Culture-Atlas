JOSLYN ART MUSEUM: Omaha, USA
ID: joslyn-art-museum-omaha
Website: https://joslyn.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 2200 Dodge Street, Omaha; Wikipedia calls it the largest in Nebraska (Joslyn).
- Sarah Joslyn gave it to Omaha in memory of her husband George A. Joslyn; it opened on 29 November 1931 (Joslyn, Wikipedia).
- John and Alan McDonald designed the Art Deco building; Norman Foster added a wing in 1994, and the Rhonda & Howard Hawks Pavilion, a 42,000 square foot addition by Snøhetta with Alley Poyner Macchietto, opened on 10 September 2024 after a two-year closure (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- The collection holds more than 12,000 objects, with American, European, Western American, Chinese, Japanese and contemporary art, and Karl Bodmer and Alfred Jacob Miller works at the Durham Center for Western Studies (Wikipedia).
- The sculpture grounds cover three acres with 22 works, including commissions by Alison Saar and Hank Willis Thomas (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting: general admission is free to all, always; hours are on a separate page that was not read. In 2025 it counted 206,546 visitors (Joslyn).

FUNDING
Joslyn Art Museum: private: Nebraska donors and foundations, a founder's endowment and gala income
- Stated by the museum: its 2025 budget was $9.8 million, from 55 percent contributions, 26 percent endowment, 12 percent earned income and 7 percent gala.
- The 2024 Form 990 shows revenue of $23,770,151, nearly all contributions ($23,377,079), expenses of $18,533,471 and net assets of $133,951,740 (ProPublica). The revenue figure includes campaign gifts for the expansion; the filing does not itemise donors in the summary opened.
- The Joslyns' fortune came from the ready-print newspaper business; the museum opened in 1931 with a $3 million bequest (The Art Newspaper). Wikipedia names the Western Newspaper Union as the source, and says Sarah Joslyn paid $2.6 million for the building and endowed its upkeep.
- Most of the money for the recent projects came from Nebraska donors and foundations, including Rhonda and Howard Hawks, for whom the pavilion is named. The Art Newspaper says the atrium is named for them; the museum's own pavilion page does not mention the Hawks. The expansion cost $100 million (The Art Newspaper). No reporting found of the Hawks gift amount.
- Free general admission is "generously provided by the Holland Foundation" (Joslyn).
- The Phillip G. Schrager Collection of 52 works was given and its atrium bears his name (Joslyn).
- The Mellon Foundation gave $400,000 in 2018 for a first curator of Native American art (The Art Newspaper).
- The museum offers the Bloomberg Connects digital guide, used by 4,116 people in 2025 (Joslyn); no reporting found of any campaign aimed at the Joslyn over it.
- The museum publishes no donor roster in the pages opened. Jack Becker, executive director, was paid $556,706 in 2024 (ProPublica).

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Hawks Pavilion
- Howard Hawks, a founder of the energy company Tenaska and a Joslyn board member, is the pavilion's namesake (The Art Newspaper, Tenaska, World-Herald).
- Tenaska markets natural gas and power and manages a 6,622 MW generating fleet; its page does not state the fleet's fuels (Tenaska).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is local Nebraska philanthropy, a founding endowment and gala income, with no corporate sponsor named. The weak point is the Tenaska energy fortune behind the Hawks Pavilion, whose gift amount is unreported. Donors are not itemised, and no protest was found.
