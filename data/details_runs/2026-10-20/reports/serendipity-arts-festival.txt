SERENDIPITY ARTS FESTIVAL: Panaji, India
ID: serendipity-arts-festival
Website: https://www.serendipityartsfestival.com (checked 2026-10-24; live, showing the 2026 edition, 13-20 December, Panjim, Goa)

OVERVIEW
- Annual multi-discipline arts festival held over about a week in late December in Panaji (Panjim), Goa, using heritage buildings and other venues across the city.
- Founded in 2016 by Sunil Kant Munjal, chairman of Hero Enterprise; organised by the non-profit Serendipity Arts Foundation (Wikipedia).
- The 2019 edition used more than 12 venues and over 95 projects (Wikipedia); the 2023 edition ran nine days across 18 locations and subvenues, with more than 150 events and over 1,500 artists (Harper's Bazaar India); the 2025 edition was the tenth, held 12 to 21 December (Homegrown).
- Each edition has a new curatorial team across visual arts, performance, music, theatre, crafts and culinary arts (Wikipedia).
- Venues named in a first-person account in The Goan include the Adil Shah Palace, Kala Academy and the Art Park opposite INOX; the 2024 craft display was in the Directorate of Accounts building (Garland).
- Visiting: a first-person account in The Goan describes the festival as free to enter. Venues are wheelchair accessible and descriptions are provided in braille, and the programme includes sessions for deaf, blind and learning-disabled visitors (Harper's Bazaar India, on the 2023 edition).

FUNDING
Serendipity Arts Festival: founder-led non-profit; Goa government, embassies and corporate partners support it; no budget published
- Sunil Kant Munjal is founder patron; Hero Enterprise and Munjal are listed as patrons (Wikipedia). Munjal said Hero avoids branding the festival so it does not become a corporate programme (Sakshi Post).
- 2025 festival partners page: "Supported By" the Government of Goa, the Sports Authority of Goa, the Directorate of Art and Culture, Kala Academy, Panjim Smart City, the Goa Forest Department and GTDC (stated by the festival).
- 2025 sponsors named by the festival: Milton (sustainability), Campa (official beverage), Sotheby's (dinner), IHCL (experience), Sun Estates (hospitality), Blue Tokai (coffee), AMD Maestro (celebration), Dainik Jagran and NDTV (media), Goa airports, plus beverage partners including Grover Zampa Vineyards and Sector Gin. Hero Insurance Broking, Hero Realty and Hero Homes appear under "Special Thanks" (stated by the festival).
- Institutional partners in 2025 include the Australian Government, the Japan Foundation, the British Council, the Danish Arts Foundation, Pro Helvetia, the Embassy of France and the City of Ghent (stated by the festival). The page gives no amounts.
- Munjal described the Goa government partnership as "excellent" (Sakshi Post).
- No reporting found of the festival's budget, of the amounts Hero or Munjal contribute, or of a full trustee list.

Red flags: closure of exhibits and arrest of performers during the 2019 edition
- Exhibits: on 18 December 2019, the fourth day, two works in the segment curated by Sudarshan Shetty were closed to viewers: a display of Miyah poetry, and a carbon-ink graffiti wall on which visitors had written slogans against the CAA and NRC (ETV Bharat).
- Reason given: Shetty said organisers told him the works were taken down for "technical issues"; organisers did not respond to reporters (ETV Bharat). IANS reported that officials cited a "technical glitch" (Morung Express).
- Response: poet Ashraful Hussain said "It is not a technical glitch, it is political suppression." Poet Hafiz Ahmed said it was "our fundamental right and it has been violated" (IANS, via Morung Express).
- Arrests: on the same day, four musicians of the band Dastaan Live were arrested after a complainant said their festival set hurt Hindu religious sentiments through references to the word "Om". They were later released on bail (ETV Bharat).
- Goa government: the same state government is a listed supporter of the 2025 festival (stated by the festival).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the festival as an institution. Coverage is mostly promotional or admiring; the critical pieces found concern programming and display, and none examines its funding or the 2019 closures.
1. Garland: Gopika Nath, "Serendipity Arts Festival: Re-viewing and re-thinking crafting methodologies" (2025)
URL: https://garlandmag.com/serendipity-arts-festival/
- The 2024 craft display in the Directorate of Accounts building was dim and crowded, so the embroidery and crafts were hard to see.
- Collaborations such as Bridge Bharat treated artisans as skilled labour and stripped the traditions of their history; a Kasuti piece used printing instead of counted-thread stitching.
- Criticises the programming and the display, not the funders.
2. Homegrown: Drishya, "Serendipity At 10: India's Biggest Arts Festival Was Exhilarating (& Exhausting)" (2026)
URL: https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-creators/serendipity-at-10-indias-biggest-arts-festival-was-exhilarating-exhausting
- Argues the festival's scale pushes visitors to treat it as a checklist, so they "come, see, post, and move on".
- Says the display of Caravaggio's Magdalene in Ecstasy in a blacked-out room "crystallised the festival's contradictions": an access achievement that can become a must-see trophy.
- Does not discuss funding or sponsorship.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival's funding is not documented in any budget or accounts, so the money behind it cannot be checked beyond the founder patron, Hero Enterprise, the Goa government and a long list of brand and embassy partners. The weak points are that undisclosed money, the 2019 closure of the CAA and NRC exhibits with a reason the poets disputed, and the Goa government's role as a listed supporter. Watch for any published accounts or trustee list.
