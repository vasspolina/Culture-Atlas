MANSUDAE OVERSEAS PROJECT GROUP OF COMPANIES (MANSUDAE OVERSEAS PROJECTS): Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: mansudae-overseas-project-group-of-companies-mansudae-overseas-projects-pyongyang
Website: none found (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Pyongyang construction company that is the overseas commercial arm of the Mansudae Art Studio. It builds monuments, memorials and public buildings, using North Korean artists, engineers and construction workers (Wikipedia).
- Set up in the 1970s (Wikipedia, Mansudae Art Studio article); projects are listed in 17 countries as of 2015, including Angola, Ethiopia, Germany, Namibia and Senegal (Wikipedia).
- Main works: the African Renaissance Monument in Dakar (2010); in Namibia, Heroes' Acre (2002), State House (2008) and the Independence Memorial Museum (2014); the Angkor Panorama Museum in Cambodia, closed indefinitely in 2020 (Wikipedia, Daily NK).
- Visiting: a contractor, not a venue.

FUNDING
Mansudae Overseas Projects: North Korean state-run company earning foreign currency from monument and building contracts abroad
- The UN listing says the group exported workers from North Korea for construction, generating revenue for the North Korean government or the Workers' Party of Korea (UN consolidated list via Lursoft; CBS News).
- Estimated earnings: $160 million from overseas projects as of August 2011 (Wikipedia); $260 million from Africa monuments as of 2016 (CBS News).
- Senegal: CBS News says the monument cost Senegal $27 million. A US embassy cable of 5 June 2008 called it a $30 million project and said Senegal would give a North Korean firm land in Almadies worth about $30 million; a cable of 6 March 2009 cited a $16 million price tag and called the financing murky. Neither names Mansudae; Wikipedia and Daily NK credit the monument to the group.
- Cambodia: Mansudae Art Studio designed, built and financed the $24 million Angkor Panorama Museum; profits go to the North Koreans for 10 years, then are split 50-50 with the Cambodian body Apsara, then the museum passes to the Cambodian government (Al Jazeera, 2016). Human rights groups worry the revenue could help circumvent sanctions.
- Namibia: No reporting found of the contract totals for Heroes' Acre, State House or the Independence Memorial Museum.
- No reporting found of accounts, owners or a board.

Red flags: UN, EU and US sanctions on the company
- 5 August 2017: the UN Security Council listed Mansudae Overseas Project Group of Companies (alias Mansudae Art Studio) under the DPRK programme (UN consolidated list, as mirrored by Lursoft).
- EU: Commission Implementing Regulation 2017/1457 of 10 August 2017 lists the company with designation date 5 August 2017 (legislation.gov.uk).
- US: on 2 December 2016 the Treasury (OFAC) designated the company under Executive Order 13722 for exporting workers from North Korea (US Treasury press release).
- OpenSanctions says the UN Panel of Experts (February 2017) recommended designation covering "all Mansudae operations and subsidiary branches world-wide".
- Namibia: in February 2016 the UN Panel of Experts reported on Namibia's dealings with Mansudae: a munitions factory at Leopard Valley, a military academy and the Ministry of Defence headquarters. Namibia said the factory was finished in 2005; the Panel found a 2010 Mansudae document advertising a research centre there and satellite images showing construction until 2014 (African Arguments, 2016).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is descriptive, not a full case against the company. Both pieces below concern the Mansudae Master Class project by the artist Onejoon Che.
1. The Art Newspaper: unsigned, "North Korea's dubious gifts to African public art" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/05/11/north-koreas-dubious-gifts-to-african-public-art
- Says the North Korean state has a "near monopoly" on "overbearing, blingtastic public monuments" and describes Heroes' Acre in Windhoek and the Dakar monument.
- Names the Mansudae Overseas Project Group of Companies as the builder; does not criticise its finances.
2. MoMA post: Onejoon Che, "Mansudae Master Class: The Monumental Gifts from North Korea" (2016)
URL: https://post.moma.org/?p=3483
- Says the group sent artists and labourers abroad for foreign currency after the mid-1990s, and that some projects honour African dictatorships, which raises suspicion of hidden links between North Korea and Africa, a continent with key materials for nuclear development.
- Argues the structures could be "a portal to greater understanding" of Africa and North Korea: a mixed assessment, not an attack.

WIDER CONTEXT
- US embassy cables from Dakar call President Wade's monument "a white elephant" (June 2008) and the land deal behind it "opaque", and refer to "relations with a pariah state" (March 2009). The cables do not name Mansudae.
- The group is the North Korean builder of that monument (Wikipedia, Daily NK).

BOTTOM LINE
The company raises foreign currency for the North Korean state and is on UN, EU and US sanctions lists. The weak points are the UN Panel's 2016 report on its Namibian military buildings and payments such as the Senegal land deal. Worth watching: Cambodia, where income is shared with a state body.
