NEON: Athens, Greece
ID: neon-athens
Website: https://neon.org.gr/en/

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art organisation in Athens that "does not restrict itself to just one space" (NEON website); Wikipedia describes projects on archaeological sites and in partner venues.
- Founded in 2013 by collector and entrepreneur Dimitris Daskalopoulos (NEON website, Wikipedia), and directed by Elina Kountouri (Wikipedia). NEON's April 2026 post is titled "NEON 2012 to 2026 | 14 years of contemporary art".
- NEON's site describes free exhibitions in public and private places in Athens and Greece, scholarships and grants for artists, educational programmes, live events and partnerships with cultural institutions.
- Projects included Antony Gormley's "SIGHT" on Delos (2019) and, with the Hellenic Parliament at the former Public Tobacco Factory, "Portals" (2021) and "Dream On" (2022) (Wikipedia).
- In 2016 NEON paid for the refurbishment of the basement of the Athens Conservatoire, which opened as an 1,800 square metre exhibition space on 24 September 2016 (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 19 September 2016).
- NEON announced on 20 April 2026 that it will conclude its activities with its final project, "Michael Rakowitz & Ancient Cultures", at the Old Acropolis Museum on Acropolis Hill (NEON website).

FUNDING
NEON: private non-profit: collector Dimitris Daskalopoulos, its founder, is the named funder of its projects
- The Art Newspaper's 2016 headline reads "Greek collector Dimitris Daskalopoulos funds new cultural centre in Athens"; NEON's partners page says NEON and Daskalopoulos exclusively funded the activities of Outset Greece for eight consecutive years.
- The 2016 Athens Conservatoire basement refurbishment cost 180,000 euros (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- NEON provided 1.4 million euros in 2020 for the renovation of the Lenorman Street Tobacco Factory, which reopened as a cultural centre in 2021 and was handed back to the Hellenic Parliament at the end of 2022; it also put 6 million euros into grants and scholarships (USA Art News, 28 April 2026).
- Wikipedia says NEON funded the renovation of the former Public Tobacco Factory in Athens, which was subsequently donated to the Greek state.
- The source of the founder's money: from 1983 to 2007 Daskalopoulos was principal owner, chairman and CEO of Delta Holdings/Vivartia, described by Wikipedia as Greece's largest food conglomerate. In 2007 Marfin Investment Group bought 30% of Vivartia from him and Spyridon Theodoropoulos for 550 million euros (Wikipedia). He now chairs DAMMA Holdings SA, a financial services and investment company (NEON website, Wikipedia).
- Partners named on NEON's partners page: Greek National Opera, Whitechapel Gallery (since 2012), Museum of Cycladic Art (since 2013), Hellenic Children's Museum ("Is this Art?" programme, from 2014) and the National Museum of Contemporary Art, EMST (supported since 2012).

Not related: Neon Parallax, based in Geneva, which began in 2006 with a call for artists to create neon signs (e-flux directory entry). It is a different organisation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money documented behind NEON is its founder's: The Art Newspaper and NEON's own pages name Daskalopoulos as the funder of its projects, and Wikipedia traces his wealth to Delta Holdings/Vivartia, Greece's largest food conglomerate. NEON announced on 20 April 2026 that it will conclude its activities with its final project at the Old Acropolis Museum.
