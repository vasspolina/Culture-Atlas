JAKARTA BIENNALE: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: jakarta-biennale
Website: https://jakartabiennale.id

OVERVIEW
- Recurring contemporary art exhibition held at Taman Ismail Marzuki (TIM), the Jakarta arts complex, and at collective spaces across the city. The 2024 edition, which ran from 1 October to 15 November, used the Emiria Soenassa and S. Sudjojono galleries at TIM (NOW! Jakarta, Sari Widiati, 31 October 2024).
- Its precursor was the Pameran Besar Seni Lukis Indonesia 1974 (Major Exhibition of Indonesian Painting) at TIM. Art school students answered it with the Desember Hitam statement, critical of decorative painting; the students were expelled and lecturers who defended them were dismissed (Elly Kent, Art and Australia, 2024).
- The Biennale is auspiced by Dewan Kesenian Jakarta, the Jakarta Arts Council (Art and Australia).
- Majelis Jakarta, an "open network of various art initiatives in Jakarta", has run it since 2024; the network began meeting in 2023 and its page lists 16 collectives (Jakarta Biennale website). NOW! Jakarta says 20 art collectives from Majelis Jakarta organised the 2024 edition.
- The 2026 edition, "KEBAL", is artist-initiated. Its main exhibition runs 4 to 28 October 2026 at Taman Ismail Marzuki, Gedung Trisno Soemardjo, and programmes run 7 October to 24 November 2026 at collective spaces. The theme concerns residents "displaced" from Jakarta by rising property and living costs (Jakarta Biennale website).
- Art and Australia says the collective ruangrupa implemented the Biennale for ten years.

FUNDING
Jakarta Biennale: collective-run, auspiced by the Jakarta Arts Council
- Organiser: Majelis Jakarta, initiated by Dewan Kesenian Jakarta, the Jakarta Arts Council (NOW! Jakarta).
- Art and Australia (Elly Kent, 2024) writes that publicly funded biennials depend on political will and key individuals rather than resilient institutions, and that government grant rounds failed to open in mid-2024.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two reviews of the 2024 edition criticise its curation and cohesion; they concern the exhibition, not its funders.
1. e-flux Criticism: Innas Tsuroiya, "Jakarta Biennale 2024, '50 Tahun Jakarta Biennale 1974-2024'" (5 December 2024)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/644742/jakarta-biennale-2024-50-tahun-jakarta-biennale-1974-2024
- The decentralised method amounts to "un-curation"; the main exhibition lacks cohesion and gives little information about its curatorial framework.
- Works are displayed without clear separation from other programmes, and many events were announced at short notice.
- Judges the show "no greater than the sum of its parts" yet with notable moments, and argues the lumbung-inspired model could help new movements grow outside the metropolitan centres.
2. Art and Australia: Elly Kent, "Contemporary Jakarta: A tale of two art events" (2024)
URL: https://artandaustralia.com/59_2/contemporary-jakarta-a-tale-of-two-art-events.html
- Criticises the 2024 Biennale for lacking cohesion: exhibition design gave visitors little help navigating the connections between works, and lighting, location and access undermined some strong works.
- Argues biennials depend on key individuals rather than resilient institutions and succession planning.

BOTTOM LINE
The Jakarta Biennale has been organised since 2024 by Majelis Jakarta, an artist-collective network initiated by the Jakarta Arts Council, which auspices the Biennale (Art and Australia). Art and Australia reports that government grant rounds failed to open in mid-2024.
