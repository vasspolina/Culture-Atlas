VICTORIOUS FATHERLAND LIBERATION WAR MUSEUM: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: victorious-fatherland-liberation-war-museum
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of the Korean War (조국해방전쟁승리기념관) in the Sosong District of Pyongyang, beside the Potong River (Koryo Tours).
- Set up in August 1953 in the Central District as the Fatherland Liberation War Museum (Koryo Tours); Korean Wikipedia gives 17 August 1953.
- Korean Wikipedia says it moved to its present site and reopened under its current name on 11 April 1974; English Wikipedia's text says the move to the Sosong District was in April 1963, while its infobox gives April 1974. Koryo Tours says the old building, which still stands next door, was completed in 1974.
- A new building opened next to the old one on 27 July 2013, the 60th anniversary of the armistice, and took in the old museum's panorama (Koryo Tours; Lonely Planet). Koryo Tours says the ceremony was Kim Jong Un's first appearance before the international press.
- KCNA quoted Kim Jong Un in July 2013 calling it "a center for anti-Japanese, anti-U.S. and class education".
- Inside: a statue of Kim Il Sung in the main lobby, rooms on each phase of the war and each branch of the armed forces, and a 360-degree panorama of the Battle of Taejon, upgraded in 2018 (Koryo Tours).
- Outdoors: a War Victory Monument, an Exhibition of Hero Equipment and an Exhibition of Captured Equipment with US armoured vehicles, artillery, downed aircraft and a seaborne drone (Koryo Tours).
- USS Pueblo, seized on 23 January 1968, was moved from the Taedong River to the Pothong River beside the museum in late November 2012 and opened to tourists in late July 2013 (Wikipedia). It remains a commissioned US Navy vessel (Wikipedia; Koryo Tours).
- A museum lecturer told KCNA in July 2014 that nearly 800,000 people had visited over the previous year (state claim).
- Visiting: tourists and locals visit with a guide in military uniform; a tour takes about two hours after a security check; photography is banned inside and allowed outside (Koryo Tours).

FUNDING
Victorious Fatherland Liberation War Museum: state-funded: rebuilt by the party and state on Kim Jong Un's initiative
- KCNA said on 27 July 2014 that the museum was remodelled in 2013 "under the care of" Kim Jong Un, who initiated the reconstruction and gave on-site guidance on 18 occasions.
- KCNA reported on 1 July 2013 that Kim visited the nearly finished museum and said "the party made a good decision to reconstruct the museum".
- North Korea announces its state budget as yearly percentage changes, not amounts. KCNA said on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's and 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the museum.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Former Pueblo crew members sued the North Korean government over their treatment in captivity. A US District Court in Washington awarded them $65 million in December 2008, and a US court awarded survivors and families $2.3 billion in February 2021; North Korea did not respond to the suit (Wikipedia).
- The ship at the centre of those cases is the museum's main foreign exhibit.

BOTTOM LINE
State money, with the 2013 rebuilding credited by state media to the leader personally. The sanctioned government is the funding risk; the open dispute over the captured Pueblo is the point to watch.
