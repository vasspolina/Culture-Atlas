ENGLISH NATIONAL BALLET: London, UK
ID: english-national-ballet-london
Website: https://www.ballet.org.uk (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Classical ballet company with its own orchestra, the English National Ballet Philharmonic, based at the Mulryan Centre for Dance on London City Island and touring the UK and abroad (Wikipedia, Howells).
- Founded in 1950 by Alicia Markova and Anton Dolin as Gala Performances of Ballet, with backing from the impresario Julian Braunsweg; later Festival Ballet and London Festival Ballet, renamed English National Ballet in June 1989 (Wikipedia).
- Aaron S. Watkin has been artistic director since 2023, after Tamara Rojo (2012 to 2022); Queen Camilla became royal patron in June 2024 (Wikipedia).
- The Mulryan Centre for Dance, by Glenn Howells Architects, was completed in 2019 in fair-faced concrete, timber and glass: seven rehearsal studios, a production studio with a 25-metre fly tower, a hydrotherapy pool, a costume atelier, a café, and teaching space for English National Ballet School (Howells). It is named for the family of Sean Mulryan, chairman of the developer Ballymore (thank-you page).
- Seasons at Sadler's Wells, where it is an associate company, the London Coliseum, the Royal Albert Hall and UK theatres, with Dance for Parkinson's and Dance for Dementia programmes.
- No reporting found of public opening hours for the building; performances include audio-described, relaxed and BSL-interpreted shows (2025-26 season page).

FUNDING
English National Ballet: mixed: Arts Council England, a property developer, trusts and private patrons
- Arts Council England supports it as a National Portfolio Organisation (season page); no reporting found of the grant amount from a page that could be opened.
- Ballymore is principal building partner; Cunard is UK tour week production sponsor for "The Sleeping Beauty" in Liverpool in the 2025-26 season, and Charles Holloway OBE was lead supporter of "Nutcracker" (season page).
- Trusts include Bloomberg Philanthropies, Garfield Weston Foundation, Linbury Trust, Backstage Trust, Leverhulme Trust and John Lyon's Charity; individual donors include Sir Lloyd and Lady Dorfman, Paul and Jacquie Gulbenkian and the Buffini Chao Foundation (thank-you page, undated).
- No reporting found of individual gift amounts or of the value of the Ballymore partnership.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism found is of working conditions under the previous director, reported in 2018; nothing found on its funders.
1. ArtsJournal: Matthew Westphal, "Dancers Are Quitting English National Ballet, Citing 'Hostile Work Environment' And Boss's Workplace Romance" (2018)
URL: https://artsjournal.org/?p=141172
- Summarises reports that the company lost about a third of its dancers in two years, with claims of verbal abuse and pressure to dance injured.
- Dancers blamed Tamara Rojo for the culture and cited her relationship with the dancer Isaac Hernández; the claims came from unnamed sources, and the original outlet is not named on the page.
2. The Stage: Giverny Masso, "Former English National Ballet principal dancer jailed for sexual assault" (2021)
URL: https://thestage.co.uk/news/former-english-national-ballet-principal-dancer-jailed-for-sexual-assault
- Reports that the former principal Yat-Sen Chang was jailed for nine years for sexual assault.
- A criminal case against an individual, not criticism of the company's funding.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Prince Andrew was the company's royal patron from 2001 until November 2019 (Wikipedia).
- After his BBC Newsnight interview about Jeffrey Epstein, the Times reported "real concern" among trustees and staff that the link could harm the company, and an unnamed trustee said "at some point it will require that discussion if he does not stand down" (the Telegraph, 20 November 2019, via Yahoo).
- On 22 November 2019 the company accepted his resignation "with immediate effect" and thanked him for "18 years of support" (The Independent, via Yahoo).
- The patron was a figurehead, not a donor or board leader, and no reporting found shows Epstein money reaching the company, so this is context, not a red flag.

BOTTOM LINE
The company runs on Arts Council money, ticket sales, a property developer's building partnership and a long list of trusts and private patrons. Its main weak point is that the value of the Ballymore partnership is not published. Its 18-year royal patron, Prince Andrew, left in 2019 after the Epstein interview; no Epstein money is reported. Worth watching: whether the company publishes the Ballymore terms.
