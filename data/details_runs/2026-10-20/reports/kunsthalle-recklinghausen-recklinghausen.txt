KUNSTHALLE RECKLINGHAUSEN: Recklinghausen, Germany
ID: kunsthalle-recklinghausen-recklinghausen
Website: https://kunsthalle-recklinghausen.de (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum at Große-Perdekamp-Straße 25 to 27 in Recklinghausen, directly opposite the main railway station (the museum's website; Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1950 during the fourth Ruhrfestspiele, with the exhibition "Deutsche und Französische Kunst der Gegenwart"; it was set up to complement the annual Ruhr festival with visual art (the museum; Wikipedia).
- Named for the city of Recklinghausen. The museum's art prize grew out of the artist group "junger westen", which Franz Große-Perdekamp encouraged to organise in 1947 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a former air-raid bunker (Hochbunker) converted into an exhibition house from 1949. No reporting found of an architect on the pages opened.
- On site: exhibitions, a municipal collection of about 5,000 works in storage that includes Emil Schumacher, Karl Otto Götz, Gerhard Richter and Timm Ulrichs, and the biennial "junger westen" art prize (Wikipedia; the museum). Nico Anklam has directed the museum since June 2021.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday and public holidays, 11:00 to 18:00; closed Monday. Admission €5, reduced €2.50, free for children under 14, and pay-what-you-want on Saturdays. The museum states it is barrier-free, with no further details.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Recklinghausen: mainly public: the City of Recklinghausen and the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, with regional foundation and bank support
- City of Recklinghausen: the museum is operated by the Museen der Stadt Recklinghausen, and the city awards the "junger westen" prize, which pays €20,000 to the winner (e-flux, 2025). The prize money rose from €10,000 in 2023 and the higher sum is set to continue through 2031.
- State of North Rhine-Westphalia: the museum's website says it is funded by the NRW Ministry of Culture and Science. For the 2026 Kjartansson show, e-flux lists the Ministry of Science and Culture of the State of North Rhine-Westphalia, Kunststiftung NRW, Sparkasse Vest Recklinghausen and the Deutscher Gewerkschaftsbund (the German trade union federation) as supporters, with Ruhrfestspielstadt Recklinghausen, the network of 21 RuhrKunstMuseen and WDR 3 as media partner. No amounts are given.
- Ruhrfestspiele: each year the museum stages the festival exhibition for the Ruhrfestspiele (Wikipedia; e-flux). Ruhrfestspielstadt Recklinghausen and the DGB are both listed as supporters of the 2026 show.
- Förderverein für Bildende Kunst: a non-profit association, chaired by Dieter Delius, that can finance acquisitions and exhibitions and helps pay for the website; it also arranges grants, donations and sponsorship. Its dues and totals are not given (the museum).
- "junger westen" prize 2025: additional funding came from the Cultural Foundation of the Stadtsparkasse Recklinghausen, the Rotary Club Recklinghausen and the Ulrike and Bernd Tönjes Foundation (e-flux).
- No reporting found of a board, annual report or accounts of its own: it is a city museum.
- No reporting found of fossil fuel, arms, gambling or tobacco funders among the names above.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kunsthalle Recklinghausen is public: the city and the state of North Rhine-Westphalia, with a regional savings bank, a state arts foundation, a union federation and local foundations on named projects. The weak points are that amounts are not published on the pages read and the museum has no board or report of its own. Worth watching: the city's budget for its museums, since the museum has no separate accounts.
