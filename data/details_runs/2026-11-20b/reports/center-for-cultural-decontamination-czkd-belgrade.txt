CENTER FOR CULTURAL DECONTAMINATION (CZKD): Belgrade, Serbia
ID: center-for-cultural-decontamination-czkd-belgrade
Website: https://www.czkd.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit cultural centre in the Veljković Pavilion at Birčaninova 21, Belgrade, known as "Kod Borke" after its founder (Radio Slobodna Evropa, 2010).
- Founded in 1994 by Borka Pavićević, formerly a principal of the Yugoslav Drama Theatre (Wikipedia); it began work at noon on 1 January 1995 with the "First Decontamination" (Vreme, 2025; CZKD).
- Vreme calls it the oldest anti-war centre in Serbia; the centre describes itself as an institution of critical thinking set up against nationalism, xenophobia and violence (CZKD).
- The pavilion was built in 1931 as Belgrade's first private art museum (CZKD; Sigrid Rausing Trust) and was the courtyard art pavilion of the Veljković family house until 1941 (Wikipedia).
- The pavilion hall covers 220 square metres, 5.8 metres high, with telescopic seating for 120, plus an upstairs room and a courtyard room for up to 40 each, a café and a cobbled courtyard (CZKD).
- Programmes include theatre, exhibitions, concerts, film, talks, workshops and publishing, with cycles such as CZKD Bioskop, FORA, Sokak and Svedočenja (CZKD); Pavićević counted four to five thousand programmes in its first 15 years (Radio Slobodna Evropa).
- The "Borka Pavićević" award for public gesture, set up in 2024 on the initiative of her admirers in Poland, is given by CZKD with the Polish Institute in Belgrade; its seven 2026 winners included University of Belgrade rector Vladan Đokić and the Arhiv javnih skupova protest-count project (021.rs, 2026).
- Visiting: office hours Monday to Friday, 11:00 to 18:00; events are listed one by one on the website (CZKD).

FUNDING
CZKD: grant-funded: foreign foundations, notably the Mott Foundation and the Sigrid Rausing Trust
- Mott Foundation: US$60,000 for 1 July 2013 to 30 June 2015 and a further US$60,000 for 1 July 2015 to 30 June 2017, for institutional capacity building under its Civil Society programme (Mott grant records).
- Sigrid Rausing Trust: lists CZKD as a grantee from 2020 to 2024, with £280,000 in previous grants (grantee profile).
- The centre's "Podrška" (Support) page lists, without amounts, the Open Society Foundation (Fondacija za otvoreno društvo), the Heinrich Böll Stiftung, the Danilo Kiš Foundation, YUCOM, the Helsinki Committee for Human Rights, Women in Black (Žene u crnom), BITEF and Peščanik (stated by the space).
- From February to June 2018 it was a partner in Trans-European Debates on the European Parliament (TEDEP), a project of Red House in Sofia carried out with the support of the European Parliament (Red House).
- The centre names its financial officer, Slavica Vučetić, who has worked there since its founding and specialises in legal and financial affairs (CZKD).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One attack on foreign-funded decontamination centres, made by a film critic on television in 2005.
1. B92, "Utisak nedelje": Olja Bećković, interview with Ranko Munitić (2005)
URL: https://web.archive.org/web/20140425143617/http://www.b92.net/info/emisije/utisak_nedelje.php?yyyy=2005&mm=10&nav_id=179537
- In the programme of 30 October 2005 the film critic Munitić attacked "ti centri za dekontaminaciju" ("these centres for decontamination", our translation) for "cinkarenju svoje sredine po svetu za debele pare" ("informing on their own community around the world for fat money", our translation), and stressed "za strane pare" ("for foreign money").
- Speaks of decontamination centres in the plural and does not name CZKD or Borka Pavićević; Wikipedia's article on CZKD cites the exchange as criticism of the centre's foreign funding.
- Read in an archive copy of 25 April 2014; the B92 page now redirects to a broken address.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: foreign foundation grants, with nothing under a flag.
