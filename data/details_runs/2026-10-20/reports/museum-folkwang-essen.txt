MUSEUM FOLKWANG: Essen, Germany
ID: museum-folkwang-essen
Website: https://www.museum-folkwang.de (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Essen with 19th- and 20th-century art, photography, prints, German posters, and ancient and non-European art (Wikipedia).
- Karl Ernst Osthaus opened it in Hagen in 1902. In 1922 Essen and Ruhr companies and private donors bought the collection and founded the Folkwang-Museumsverein, and it merged with Essen's municipal art museum. In 1937 the National Socialists confiscated more than 1,400 works as "degenerate art" (museum chronicle).
- David Chipperfield won the 2007 competition; his building added 16,000 square metres and opened in January 2010 (Wikipedia; museum chronicle).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday (Thursday and Friday until 20:00), closed Monday. The permanent collection is free; the Courbet exhibition costs €16, concessions €12.

FUNDING
Museum Folkwang: mixed: city of Essen and the Krupp foundation, with corporate and savings-bank sponsors
- The Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation decided in 2006 to finance the new building as sole funder (foundation page; museum chronicle); Wikipedia gives €55 million. It paid for free admission to the permanent collection from 2015 (its page says until 2021; the chronicle says it agreed with the city in 2020 to keep it free).
- The Folkwang-Museumsverein co-owns the collections with the city of Essen (Wikipedia). No city amounts found.
- Eleven sponsors on the museum's undated page: Allbau GmbH, Bankhaus Bauer, E.ON SE, Evonik Industries AG, GENO BANK, Merck Finck Privatbankiers AG, NATIONAL-BANK, RWE AG, Sparkasse Essen, Sparkassen-Kulturfonds and Stadtwerke Essen. No amounts are published.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (RWE and E.ON, sponsors)
- E.ON: on 13 November 2023 the museum and E.ON announced a three-year sponsorship to 2026 for two exhibitions, "Wir ist Zukunft" and "Grow It, Show It!", and outreach programmes (museum press release).
- RWE: listed on the museum's sponsors page (undated). The only dated RWE support found is a Simon Starling exhibition, reported by taz on 2 May 2007.
- RWE, based in Essen, made about 32% of its 2019 electricity from lignite and 9.3% from hard coal, and was Europe's largest carbon dioxide emitter in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- E.ON, also based in Essen, sells electricity and natural gas. It said in November 2014 that it would abstain from fossil energy but holds 50% of Enerjisa, which has coal power in Turkey (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against these sponsors at the museum.

Red flags: Krupp foundation money (forced labour and arms)
- The foundation began work on 1 January 1968 with Alfried Krupp's private fortune and the Fried. Krupp AG holdings. He had run Krupp's armament division from 1938 and became sole owner of the firm on 15 December 1943; he was sentenced at the 1947 to 1948 Krupp Trial to twelve years and forfeiture of his property for slave labour and plunder; the US High Commissioner commuted the sentence on 31 January 1951 and restored his property (Wikipedia).
- Money today: the foundation still funds photography scholarships with the museum's collection (2026 awards). Its income comes mainly from dividends on its 20.93% of thyssenkrupp AG, the largest shareholder; thyssenkrupp holds 51% of ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems, which makes frigates, corvettes and submarines (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Protest over a cancelled exhibition section (2023)
- On 13 November 2023 the museum cancelled the Afrofuturism section of "We Is Future", due to open on 24 November, after curator Anaïs Duplan's pro-Palestine posts. Director Peter Gorschlüter called some posts "unacceptable"; a spokesperson cited his 10 November post supporting BDS and said the decision was not about artistic content (Hyperallergic; Jüdische Allgemeine, 16 November 2023).
- Most artists in the section asked for their works back (Hyperallergic). No open letter or street protest was found; CIMAM, the international committee of modern art museums, published a statement on 15 January 2024 opposing the cancellation among others.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns the 2023 cancellation; none targets the sponsors or the Krupp foundation's money.

2. CIMAM: "Cancellation and censorship in times of war" (15 January 2024)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/cancellation-and-censorship-in-times-of-war/
- Argues museums should stay spaces for free expression and calls the Bundestag's 2019 BDS resolution "disputed and legally not binding".
- A museum-professionals' body, not an art outlet; takes its account from the Instagram account archive_of_silence.

BOTTOM LINE
The Krupp foundation paid for the building and free admission; its fortune came from an owner convicted of slave labour and it holds a fifth of thyssenkrupp, which owns a naval shipbuilder. The sponsor list includes E.ON, announced for 2023 to 2026, and RWE, with no dated support found.
