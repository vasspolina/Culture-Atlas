MUSEO DE ARTE MODERNO DE MEDELLÍN (MAMM): Medellín, Colombia
ID: museo-de-arte-moderno-de-medell-n-mamm-medell-n
Website: https://www.elmamm.org/

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum at Carrera 44 N° 19A-100 in Ciudad del Río, Medellín.
- Founded in 1978 and opened on 17 November 1978 by a group of artists of the "generación urbana", with architects and businesspeople; it followed the interest generated by the Coltejer Art Biennials of 1968, 1970 and 1972 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- In 2009 the Medellín city government assigned it the Talleres Robledo building, a steelworks dating from 1938 in the Ciudad del Río industrial zone; an expansion opened in 2015 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The museum's mission statement is to generate and promote spaces of knowledge and enjoyment for everyone through art and contemporary artistic practices (museum site).
- Programmes include exhibitions, Cine MAMM, the outdoor film nights of Noche Extendida on the last Friday of the month, the children's programme La ciudad de los niños y las niñas, a shop and rented event spaces (2023 management report).
- Attendance reached 163,072 in 2023 (2023 management report). Director María Mercedes González resigned after thirteen years in October 2025 and joined the board; the museum's site lists Rafael Tamayo Franco as director general.

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Moderno de Medellín: mixed: own earnings, corporate and foundation sponsors, and city and national public money
- Share of 2023 income: own income 54 percent, sponsorships 16 percent, Alcaldía de Medellín 12 percent, national government bodies 1 percent, international funds 5 percent (2023 management report). In 2022 own income was 44 percent, sponsorships 25 percent and the Alcaldía 12 percent.
- Own income in 2023 came from space rentals, the MAMM shop and tickets, which exceeded 700 million pesos. El Colombiano reported income of 7,100 million pesos for 2022 (Kirvin Larios, 9 January 2023).
- Pillars named on the museum's sponsors page: Alcaldía de Medellín, Ministerio de las Culturas, las Artes y los Saberes, Comfama, SURA, Fundación Fraternidad Medellín, Bancolombia, EPM and Fundación MUV. Allies are Celsa and Suma Legal; institutional allies are Creative Industries Fund NL, Fundación Montechico, El Boga Casa Taller and Minkalab. Celsia is credited for support of the website.
- Programme links in 2023: SURA and Fundación MUV paid for the Naufus Ramírez-Figueroa exhibition and public programme; Bancolombia has partnered on La ciudad de los niños y las niñas since 2008; EPM supports Cine MAMM; a 2023 celebration was paid for by SURA, Celsia, Codiscos, Celsa, Transmetano, Londoño Gómez, Davivienda and Autolarte (2023 management report).
- Board: Mauricio Ortega (president), Iván Darío Gómez, María Clara Gutiérrez, Jorge Alberto Jiménez, Leo Katz, Eduardo Macía, Lina Mejía, Julián Posada, Jimena Puyo, Paula Restrepo, Víctor Manuel Rodríguez and Ricardo Sierra (museum site). González called the museum "a private entity with a total vocation for public service" (translation, museum announcement, 15 October 2025).

WIDER CONTEXT
- El Espectador (Redacción Economía and Redacción Política, "Lo que viene tras fallo de responsabilidad fiscal por Hidroituango", 6 September 2021) reported that Colombia's Contraloría General de la República found 26 of 28 people and entities fiscally responsible, for gross negligence ("culpa grave"), in the Hidroituango dam project. The damage ruled on is 4.3 billones of pesos (4.3 trillion), in a first-instance decision open to appeal. The responsible parties named include Sergio Fajardo, Alonso Salazar, Luis Alfredo Ramos and the CCC Ituango consortium companies (Camargo Correa, Conconcreto and Coninsa Ramón H.). EPM is the company developing the project and is not named among those found responsible.
- The article does not mention MAMM. The connection is that EPM, the company developing the project, is listed as a pillar sponsor on the museum's sponsors page and supported Cine MAMM in 2023 (2023 management report).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum, which describes itself as a private entity, is paid for by its own earnings (54 percent of 2023 income), corporate and foundation sponsors, the Medellín city government and the national culture ministry, with 12 percent from the Alcaldía and 1 percent from national bodies in 2023. Its sponsor pillars include EPM and the bank Bancolombia, named on its own pages and in its 2023 management report.
