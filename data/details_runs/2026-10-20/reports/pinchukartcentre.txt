PINCHUKARTCENTRE: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: pinchukartcentre
Website: https://pinchukartcentre.org/en/about (checked 2026-10-24; the page showed "Closed" in its hours banner)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre in the Besarabka area of Kyiv, at Velyka Vasylkivska 1/3-2 (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Victor Pinchuk, who also founded the Victor Pinchuk Foundation in 2006; it opened on 16 September 2006.
- Occupies a historic complex renovated in the early 2000s, with interiors by Philippe Chiambaretta, on six floors with more than 3,000 sq m of exhibition space.
- On site: exhibition halls, a library, an education room and a video lounge. Programmes include the Research Platform (since 2016), the Future Generation Art Prize and the PinchukArtCentre Prize for artists aged 35 or younger.
- Closed for 143 days after Russia's full-scale invasion of 24 February 2022 and reopened on 17 July 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission and events are free; the FAQ gives Wednesday to Sunday, 12 pm to 9 pm, last entry 8:30 pm; the centre closes during air alerts. The page gives no accessibility details.

FUNDING
PinchukArtCentre: self-funded: founder Victor Pinchuk, through his foundation
- The centre lists itself among the Victor Pinchuk Foundation's projects and names Pinchuk as founder. No budget, donor list or board is published on the pages opened (the centre site; syg.ma says the annual budget is undisclosed).
- Pinchuk's fortune: he founded Interpipe in 1990, a producer of seamless pipes and railway wheels, and was the largest shareholder of StarLightMedia (Wikipedia). Forbes ranked him 1,515th in the world in July 2026 with US$2.8 billion (Wikipedia).
- The Future Generation Art Prize carries US$100,000 every two years and is funded by the Victor Pinchuk Foundation (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors or a state funder for the centre itself.

Red flags: protest after dismissal of guides
- 25 December 2019: guides who had formed a union in October 2019 protested near the centre; their fixed-term contracts were not renewed and management announced the posts cancelled on 9 February 2020 (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper (28 February 2020) reported 28 guides dismissed.
- The centre denies any link to the union and says an internal review in September 2019 led to the decision (The Art Newspaper).
- Two nominees for the 2020 PinchukArtCentre Prize, Valentina Petrova and Pavlo Grazhdanskij, withdrew in solidarity (The Art Newspaper; Kyiv Post).

Red flags: founder's ties to the Kuchma privatisations and US donations
- In 2004 Pinchuk and Rinat Akhmetov bought the Kryvorizhstal steel plant for about $800 million; a later government reversed the sale and the repeat auction raised about $4.8 billion (Wikipedia, whose page carries a neutrality warning). Pinchuk is married to Olena Pinchuk, daughter of former president Leonid Kuchma, who authorised the first sale.
- In September 2015 he gave $150,000 to the Trump Foundation in the year he hosted a video appearance by Trump at his Kyiv conference (Wikipedia).
- Unverified: Ihor Kolomoisky alleged in parliament in March 2015 that Pinchuk took bribes tied to Ukrnafta; the National Anti-Corruption Bureau opened proceedings on 18 March 2015 (Wikipedia). No charges followed, according to syg.ma.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in the established art press; the substantive critiques are a 2020 report on the guides dispute and a 2026 opinion essay on a community platform.
1. The Art Newspaper: Dorian Batycka, "PinchukArtCenter employees claim to have been sacked after attempting to form trade union" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/02/28/pinchukartcenter-employees-claim-to-have-been-sacked-after-attempting-to-form-trade-union
- 28 guides were dismissed months after trying to form a union; union head Anastasia Bondarenko alleges threats, denied sick leave and precarious contracts.
- The centre denies the allegations; two prize nominees withdrew.
2. Kyiv Post (reposted by ArtLeaks): Toma Istomina, "Pinchuk Art Center mediators lose jobs after forming union" (2020)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2020/02/26/pinchuk-art-center-mediators-lose-jobs-after-forming-union-kyiv-ukraine/
- Petrova called the centre "a PR project of oligarch Pinchuk" and questioned how a jury could assess twenty works in a day.
- Grazhdanskij said the centre avoided public discussion and raised questions about the prize's transparency.
3. syg.ma (community platform): artur.sumarokov, "The Pinchuk Art Centre: Two Decades of Cynical Art-Washing for Ukraine's Most Polished Oligarch" (2026)
URL: https://syg.ma/@artursumarokov/the-pinchuk-art-centre-two-decades-of-cynical-art-washing-for-ukraine-s-most-polished-oligarch
- Argues the centre launders reputation earned from Kuchma-era privatisations, and that Western institutions that took Pinchuk's money enabled it.
- Openly adversarial opinion essay by an individual author, not reporting; several claims are not documented.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre has no outside funder: Pinchuk's own money pays for it, and no budget or board is published. The weak points are the 2020 guides dismissal and prize withdrawals, and the founder's Kuchma-era privatisation and political donations. Worth watching: whether the centre publishes accounts or a board list.
