CHALE WOTE STREET ART FESTIVAL: Accra, Ghana
ID: chale-wote-street-art-festival-accra
Website: https://accradotaltradio.com

OVERVIEW
- Annual street art festival in Accra, organised by ACCRA [dot] ALT; Mantse Aryeequaye and Sionne Neely established the festival in 2011 (Nataal, 2018).
- Began as a conversation: in April 2011 Aryeequaye proposed a street art festival at an Accra [dot] Alt talk party, and the first festival in July 2011 drew about 1,000 people (TRUE Africa, Bianca Manu, 2015 interview).
- Held in Jamestown, which Aryeequaye called the oldest part of Accra, with colonial and pre-colonial history (TRUE Africa). In 2023 programming was limited to the Osu municipality, from 21 to 27 August at Black Star Square, with no activities in James Town (festival press release).
- By 2018 the festival ran for a week city-wide with street performances, processions, live music and installations (Nataal). Nataal counts upward of 20,000 visitors and over 200 artists from Ghana and abroad in 2018, and Contemporary And counts more than 30,000 Ghanaians and visitors at the seventh edition in August 2017.
- In 2017 the organisers secured access to James Fort and Ussher Fort, which were used as galleries and for installations (Contemporary And).
- The 2024 edition ran from 19 to 25 August, with the theme "And Now An End To The Empire Of Horrors", organised by Accra ALT with Redd Kat Pictures and Chale Wote Street Art Projekt (MyJoyOnline).

FUNDING
Chale Wote Street Art Festival: self-funded by the organisers, with State institutions and private partners from 2023
- Founders' account: the founders started with little money, believing the festival "would be transformative" (TRUE Africa, 2015). In 2023 the Ghana News Agency reported that the festival had been funded "independently" for the past 12 years, attributing this to Aryeequaye's earlier remarks, and that he had expressed reservations about inadequate corporate support in previous editions (23 August 2023).
- 2023: the Ghana News Agency reports a partnership with State institutions and private entities, and quotes Aryeequaye: "Our burden is lessened a bit, but it could be better". It names the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture, the Ghana Tourism Authority, the Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly and the MP Dr Zanetor Agyeman-Rawlings.
- The 2023 press release lists these supporters: the Ministry of Tourism, Arts & Culture, the Ghana Tourism Authority, Dr Zenator Agyeman Rawlings (MP for Korle Klottey, as spelled in the release), Accra Dot Alt, Citi FM/TV, Institut Français, the Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly, the Ghana Museums & Monuments Board and Lododo Art.
- 2024: the Multimedia Group was the official media partner for the second time (MyJoyOnline).
- Graphic Online, PaJohn Bentsifi Dadson, 4 September 2015: Chale Wote is "run on funding sourced privately".
- Al Jazeera (28 August 2015) describes it as a non-profit festival, free to the public, "functioning independently of state control".

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One art-press piece reports an objection to the festival, from religious critics of a procession; the article itself does not criticise the festival.
1. Contemporary And: Lineo Segoete, "Collaborating Against the Odds" (2017)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/magazines/collaborating-against-the-odds
- Reports that a procession honouring the goddess Mami Wata drew harsh criticism from religious fundamentalists in Accra, who "were quick to call the festival evil"; the article describes the procession as more a political call to action than a tribute to the deity.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Chale Wote is the organisers' own independently raised funds, supplemented from 2023 by supporters named in the festival's press release and the Ghana News Agency: the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture, the Ghana Tourism Authority, the Korle Klottey Municipal Assembly, Citi FM/TV, Institut Français and, as media partner in 2024, the Multimedia Group.
