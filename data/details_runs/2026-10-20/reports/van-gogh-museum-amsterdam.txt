VAN GOGH MUSEUM: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: van-gogh-museum-amsterdam
Website: https://www.vangoghmuseum.nl/ (checked 2026-10-24; the pages on the annual report and on founders and partners returned only an unfilled page template, so their content could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Vincent van Gogh's work at Paulus Potterstraat 7 on the Museumplein in Amsterdam-Zuid, between the Stedelijk Museum and the Rijksmuseum (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 2 June 1973. The government commissioned the building from Gerrit Rietveld in 1963; he died a year later and it was completed in 1973 (Wikipedia).
- The collection grew from the private collection of Johanna van Gogh-Bonger. Her son Vincent Willem van Gogh inherited it in 1925, and it was transferred in 1962 to the Vincent van Gogh Foundation, which the state initiated (Wikipedia).
- Martien van Goor renovated the building in 1998 to 1999 and Kisho Kurokawa added an exhibition wing for temporary shows. The museum closed for renovation from September 2012 and reopened in May 2013 (Wikipedia).
- It holds 200 paintings, 400 drawings and 700 letters (Wikipedia) and had 1.8 million visitors in 2024 (Art Newspaper report via USA Art News).
- Visiting: opening days and admission could not be read from the site. The Cultuurpers column puts a ticket at "20 euros or more".

FUNDING
Van Gogh Museum: mainly self-earned, with a state subsidy: tickets and commercial income, €8.5 million from the Dutch state, plus funds, foundations and sponsors
- Dutch state subsidy: €8.5 million a year. The museum says it needs €11 million a year for a €104 million renovation planned from 2028, and the government says the current subsidy is sufficient (USA Art News, 5 May 2026; ANP via Welingelichte Kringen, 27 August 2025).
- Own income: the museum says it earns 85% of its income itself (ANP).
- 2024: a profit of €2.9 million, and donations for art acquisitions from funds and foundations rose by €2.3 million (USA Art News). Cultuurpers reports equity above €53 million and a general reserve of €34 million.
- Dispute: the museum argues the state is breaking a 1962 agreement made when the collection was transferred to the foundation (ANP). By May 2026 the museum and the government were in confidential mediation and the court case was adjourned "for an indefinite period" (USA Art News).
- Shell, until August 2018: an 18-year sponsorship of technical research on the paintings, with the last five years on Van Gogh's work from 1888 to 1890. The article gives no amount (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found in pages I could open of the current corporate partners or their amounts. The museum's partner pages could not be read.

Red flags: Shell sponsorship, ended 2018
- Fossil fuels: Shell sponsored the museum for 18 years until August 2018; for the last five years the sponsorship focused on Van Gogh's paintings from 1888 to 1890 (The Art Newspaper).
- Campaign: the group Fossil Free Culture protested the sponsorship (The Art Newspaper).
- Outcome: director Axel Rüger thanked Shell for "an extremely rewarding collaboration". A Shell Nederland spokeswoman said the relationship ended through "mutual decisions". Fossil Free Culture said it "would like to think that the museums made ethical decisions" (The Art Newspaper).
- The article says technical research may be complete but the resulting catalogue is still some years away.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum in the art press. The Shell exit was reported mostly as news; the most direct criticism found is a Dutch opinion column on its subsidy request.
1. The Art Newspaper: Martin Bailey, on the Shell sponsorship ending (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/shell-sponsorship
- Reports that the sponsorship ended after 18 years and that Fossil Free Culture, which had protested, hoped the museums acted on ethics.
- Gives the museum's and Shell's accounts; makes no case against the museum.
2. Cultuurpers: Willem Jaap Zwart, "Het Van Gogh Museum vraagt om meer subsidie, maar de rest van Nederland kijkt hoofdschuddend toe" (2025)
URL: https://cultureelpersbureau.nl/en/2025/08/het-van-gogh-museum-vraagt-om-meer-subsidie-maar-de-rest-van-nederland-kijkt-hoofdschuddend-toe/
- Opinion column: a museum with a profit near €3 million and €34 million in reserve asks for more subsidy while regional institutions struggle; calls its stance "arrogance" in the author's words (translation).
- Criticises the subsidy request, not its funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's income is mostly its own, with a state subsidy and foundation gifts, and its one documented fossil-fuel funder, Shell, ended in 2018. The weak point is the missing current partner list: the pages that should name corporate patrons could not be read, so no current funder is confirmed here. Worth watching: the subsidy mediation and who funds the €104 million renovation.
