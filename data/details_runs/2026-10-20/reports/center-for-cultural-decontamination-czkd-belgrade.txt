CENTER FOR CULTURAL DECONTAMINATION (CZKD): Belgrade, Serbia
ID: center-for-cultural-decontamination-czkd-belgrade
Website: https://czkd.org

OVERVIEW
- Independent non-profit cultural centre in the Veljković Pavilion at Birčaninova 21, Belgrade, known as "Kod Borke" after its founder (Radio Slobodna Evropa, 2010).
- Founded by Borka Pavićević, formerly a principal of the Yugoslav Drama Theatre, in 1994 (Wikipedia); it began work at noon on 1 January 1995 with the "First Decontamination" (Vreme, 2025).
- Established, in Wikipedia's words, as a response to the political and cultural climate in Serbia, working against nationalism, xenophobia, intolerance, hatred and fear; Vreme calls it the oldest anti-war centre in Serbia.
- The pavilion was built in 1931 as Belgrade's first private museum (Sigrid Rausing Trust); Wikipedia says it was the courtyard art pavilion of the Veljković family house until 1941.
- On site: a flexible theatre, a library, a smaller room for talks and book launches and a courtyard (Sigrid Rausing Trust). Programme categories on the website include film, exhibitions, concerts, theatre, performances, lectures, workshops, discussions, fairs, seminars and publishing, with long-running cycles such as CZKD Bioskop, FORA, Sokak and Svedočenja.
- Pavićević said the centre hosted between four and five thousand programmes in its first 15 years; Shkelzen Maliqi recalled that a June 1997 exhibition of Albanian artists from Kosovo drew negative reactions from regime media in Belgrade and in Kosovo (Radio Slobodna Evropa, Branka Mihajlović, 10 February 2010).
- In 2024, with the Polish Institute in Belgrade, it set up the "Borka Pavićević" award for public gesture, started on the initiative of Pavićević's admirers and friends in Poland (021.rs, 2026).
- Visiting: office hours Monday to Friday, 11:00 to 18:00; events are listed individually on the website.

FUNDING
CZKD: grant-funded: foreign foundations, notably the Mott Foundation and the Sigrid Rausing Trust
- Mott Foundation: US$60,000 for 1 July 2013 to 30 June 2015 and a further US$60,000 for 1 July 2015 to 30 June 2017, both from its Civil Society programme for institutional and programmatic development (Mott Foundation grant records).
- Sigrid Rausing Trust: lists CZKD as a grantee, supported from 2020 to 2024, with a total of £280,000 in previous grants (Sigrid Rausing Trust grantee profile).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One criticism of the centre's foreign funding is recorded, by a film critic on television, and is known only from Wikipedia.
1. B92 television, "Utisak nedelje": Ranko Munitić (2005), as recorded by Wikipedia
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Cultural_Decontamination
- Unverified: Wikipedia, the only source, states that on 30 October 2005 the film critic Munitić criticised the centre's foreign funding on the B92 programme, arguing that the issue was reporting on their community abroad for large sums of money.

BOTTOM LINE
The money documented behind CZKD is grants from the Mott Foundation (2013 to 2017, US$120,000 in two grants) and the Sigrid Rausing Trust (supported 2020 to 2024, £280,000 in previous grants in total).
