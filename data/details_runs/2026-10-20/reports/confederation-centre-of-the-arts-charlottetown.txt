CONFEDERATION CENTRE OF THE ARTS: Charlottetown, Canada
ID: confederation-centre-of-the-arts-charlottetown
Website: https://confederationcentre.com/

OVERVIEW
- Arts complex at 145 Richmond Street in downtown Charlottetown, Prince Edward Island, on a block next to Province House.
- Built as "Canada's National Memorial to the Fathers of Confederation", funded by the ten provinces and the federal government; construction began in 1960 and Queen Elizabeth II opened it on 6 October 1964 (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Ray Affleck, Hazen Sise and Dimitri Dimakopoulos of the firm Arcop; at street level it reads as three buildings gathered around Memorial Hall (Wikipedia).
- On site: Confederation Centre Art Gallery, whose permanent collection held over 17,000 works as of June 2017; a 1,109-seat main stage and two studio theatres; and arts education programmes in dance and choral music (Wikipedia; 2023-24 annual report).
- The Charlottetown Festival has run every summer since 1965 except 2020, and its production Anne of Green Gables: The Musical holds a Guinness World Record for the longest-running annual musical theatre production, 1965 to 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Attendance in 2023-24: 55,931 patrons at the 2023 Charlottetown Festival and 31,124 visitors to the Gallery (annual report).

FUNDING
Confederation Centre of the Arts: public grants (C$15.1 million of C$20.7 million revenue in 2023-24), with Sobey and Webster foundations and corporate sponsors
- Revenue for the year to 31 March 2024 was C$20.733 million, of which public sector grants were C$15.109 million (operating C$6.533 million, capital C$8.576 million). Programming brought in C$3.033 million, commercial income C$0.940 million and fundraising C$1.624 million (2023-24 annual report).
- Sponsorship investment was C$402,250 and donations totalled C$1,187,905 from 620 annual donors (annual report).
- Founders' Circle donors ($10,000 and over) include The David & Faye Sobey Foundation, The Sobey Foundation and the Eric T. Webster Foundation; the Jackman Foundation and the R. Howard Webster Foundation give at the $5,000 to $9,999 level (annual report, 2023-24 donor list).
- Named sponsors: TD Ready Commitment (outdoor amphitheatre), CN (Heritage Players), the RBC Foundation (art gallery), Maritime Electric (choral music), BMO Financial Group and Hyndman & Company (arts education), CompassTech (LIVE @ the Centre title sponsor), and Cavendish Farms and Pepsico among performance sponsors (annual report).
- Capital project: the south pavilion revitalisation costs C$71.5 million. In March 2024 the federal and provincial governments announced C$47 million: C$25 million from Infrastructure Canada's Green and Inclusive Community Buildings programme, over C$2.3 million from ACOA's Innovative Communities Fund, and C$20 million from Prince Edward Island. The Centre is to raise C$16.5 million in donations, and the silent phase of the Shared Voices Capital Campaign had C$3.5 million in gifts and pledges (annual report).
- The board's liaisons include the Premier of Prince Edward Island and officials of Canadian Heritage (annual report).

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- Refusal: on 14 August 2025 CBC News (Tony Davis) reported that some downtown Charlottetown business owners were refusing to use promotional material, aimed at helping the Centre raise money for renovations, that showed John A. Macdonald's face on an orange background. CBC links orange to the Every Child Matters campaign on residential schools.
- Laura Noel, owner of The 5th Wave Espresso & Tea Bar, said she opened a package of promotional coffee sleeves, and that putting Macdonald and orange together "is going backwards in what we're trying to do in reconciliation". She also said she does not want to "cancel" the Centre.
- CEO Steve Bellamy said the Centre is Canada's national monument to the founding fathers, that the campaign was meant to acknowledge the past, and that the Centre has used orange in its promotional material since it opened in 1964. He said that if the pairing was an oversight the Centre apologised, and that he understood why some Islanders were concerned (CBC News).

BOTTOM LINE
The Centre is a national memorial; C$15.109 million of its C$20.733 million revenue in 2023-24 came from public sector grants. Private money comes from the Sobey and Webster foundations and from sponsors including TD, CN, RBC Foundation, BMO, Maritime Electric, Cavendish Farms and Pepsico, listed in its annual report. In August 2025 downtown businesses refused the Centre's Macdonald fundraising material, as CBC News reported.
