VICTORIA GALLERY: Samara, Russia
ID: victoria-gallery-samara
Website: https://gallery-victoria.ru/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art gallery on the Volga embankment in the historic centre of Samara (gallery website).
- Founded in 2005 by Leonid Mikhelson, a businessman from Novokuybyshevsk; the first director was Lyudmila Patrati (VK long read, Vse v Samare).
- Named for Mikhelson's daughter Victoria, a name shared with the V-A-C Foundation (Kommersant FM).
- The building closed for reconstruction in spring 2023. Since the reopening on 6 March 2026 the gallery occupies the whole three-storey building; before it held one third (Snob, Kommersant FM). The architect is not named in the pages read.
- On site: three exhibition halls, a lecture hall, a library, a bookshop, a cafe, studios, workshops and a roof exhibition space (VK long read).
- Visiting: exhibition halls open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 21:00. Ticket prices are not listed. The site lists tours for blind and visually impaired visitors and with Russian Sign Language interpretation.

FUNDING
Victoria Gallery: owner-funded: Leonid Mikhelson, whose wealth is in gas
- The gallery is private, founded and owned by Mikhelson (gallery website, Kommersant FM). Kommersant FM calls him the third-richest person on the Russian Forbes list.
- Mikhelson is chairman of the management board of Novatek (UK Sanctions List), described as Russia's second-largest gas company (The Art Newspaper, 2022).
- Neither the gallery's site nor the press pieces read give a budget, a partner list or other donors. No reporting found of a state or corporate funder of the gallery.

Red flags: owner's gas wealth and sanctions
- Fossil fuels: the owner's fortune comes from Novatek, a gas company (The Art Newspaper; Coda Story).
- UK: the UK Sanctions List (checked 24 October 2026) shows Mikhelson designated on 6 April 2022 with an asset freeze and travel ban, trust services sanctions from 21 March 2023 and a director disqualification from 9 April 2025. The ground is his directorships and shareholdings in Novatek and Sibur, in the energy and chemicals sectors.
- New Zealand: the Russia Sanctions Register of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade lists him as sanctioned on 12 October 2022.
- Ukraine: m24.ru reported on 20 October 2022 that President Zelensky enacted a National Security and Defence Council decision naming him. The decree itself was not opened.
- Australia: Ukraine's Defence Intelligence sanctions page gives 6 April 2022. The Australian government's list could not be opened.
- Coda Story reports that the US sanctioned Novatek over Russia's annexation of Crimea.
- Unverified: the Anti-Corruption Foundation (advocacy) says he controls companies that give financial and organisational help to the Russian Ministry of Defence, and the Dossier Center (investigative) lists him as suspected of giving a bribe, both as quoted by OpenSanctions.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Samara gallery itself found. Pieces criticise Mikhelson and V-A-C, without naming the gallery.

- Global Project occupied V-A-C's Palazzo Zattere in Venice in May 2022 with banners reading "Let's expropriate Russian oligarchs for peace and climate justice", and criticised the EU for not sanctioning Mikhelson.
- Does not name the Samara gallery.
2. Coda Story: "Putin's Oligarchs" series profile of Mikhelson (undated; events to March 2022)
URL: https://www.codastory.com/?p=40615
- Says Tate Modern, the Art Institute of Chicago and the New Museum kept accepting his money, and he served on their boards.
- Notes that V-A-C's 26 February 2022 statement, "cannot turn a blind eye", stopped short of condemning the war. Does not name the Samara gallery.

WIDER CONTEXT
- After the February 2022 invasion V-A-C left Instagram on 1 March 2022 and its artistic director Francesco Manacorda resigned in March 2022 (Coda Story). V-A-C's Venice palace was occupied by protesters in May 2022 and its activities suspended (The Art Newspaper, 19 May 2022).
- The Samara gallery is Mikhelson's earlier project and is linked by name and by staff to V-A-C (VK long read, Kommersant FM); campaigners targeted V-A-C in Venice, not Samara.
- The gallery's own news page says an exhibition, "Полный Сюр", closed early on 24 July 2026 because it could not meet fire safety requirements (stated by the space). No independent reporting found.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one person's: Mikhelson, chairman of Novatek's management board, who is on the UK and New Zealand sanctions lists and, per Ukrainian sources, Ukraine's and Australia's. The gallery posts no accounts, board or partner list, so nothing beyond his ownership can be confirmed. Worth watching: how the gallery treats the war and sanctions in its programme, and the early-closed July 2026 show.
