NATIONAL ART MUSEUM OF MOLDOVA: Chișinău, Moldova
ID: national-art-museum-of-moldova
Website: https://www.mnam.md (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum in central Chișinău, with its main building at 115 Strada 31 August 1989.
- Founded in 1939 (museum website). Wikipedia names the sculptor Alexandru Plămădeală and the painter Auguste Baillayre as founders and gives 26 November 1939 as the date. The original collection came mostly from donations by Bessarabian artists, with works from the Ministry of Culture and Arts in Bucharest. The whole collection was moved to Kharkiv at the start of the war and disappeared there (museum website).
- Building: the central Dadiani building was designed by Alexandru Bernardazzi and built in 1901 "with the funds of Princess Natalia Dadiani" as a girls' gymnasium. It later housed the Central Committee of the Communist Party of the Moldavian SSR and was handed to the museum in 1989; three earthquakes in 1990 forced the central section to close (museum website).
- The museum also uses the Hertza and Kligman houses and holds the Church of the Dormition of the Mother of God in Căușeni, dated 1763.
- Restoration: 1,783 square metres of the Dadiani building were restored between 2004 and 2016, with permanent exhibitions opening there in November 2016, and a further 1,485 square metres opened in September 2021, 3,268 in all (museum website). Europa Liberă (4 November 2016) says the museum had been closed for repairs for 13 years.
- Collection: national and universal art (museum website). The museum's 2024 report records 81,147 visitors and 8,500 at the Căușeni church that year.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. Ticket prices are on a separate ticket page that was not read. No accessibility information found.

FUNDING
National Art Museum of Moldova: mainly public: the Moldovan state through the Ministry of Culture, with one-off Romanian state aid for the building
- The Ministry of Culture lists the museum among its subordinate museums.
- Romania gave Moldova 2.8 million dollars under a 2014 memorandum for three cultural buildings; one million dollars went to the art museum and one million to the Organ Hall in Chișinău (Europa Liberă, 2016).
- Moldova's state budget funded restoration only unevenly: the then director, Tudor Zbârnea, said annual allocations ranged from 240,000 to 4 million, without naming the currency, and some were blocked (Europa Liberă, 2016).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, a friends group or a current budget figure; the 2024 activity report read gives visitor and collection numbers but no finances.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- A Court of Accounts audit of state budget spending in 2023 and 2024 found "significant deviations from the legal requirements" in how the Ministry of Culture and seven state museums managed money and cultural heritage, including unjustified expenditure and uncollected rent (Logos Press, 23 December 2025). Across the audited museums, only 0.38% of the 900,700 movable heritage units appear in accounting records.
- The National Museum of Art is one of the seven museums audited; the article gives no separate findings for each museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Moldovan state money plus one-off Romanian state aid for the building, with no private or flagged funder named in the sources read. The weak points are that no accounts, funder list or board are published, and that the museum sits inside a museum system whose 2023 and 2024 spending the Court of Accounts found deviated from legal requirements. Worth watching are the audit's findings for this museum and the ministry's funding of further restoration.
