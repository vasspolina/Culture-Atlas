MSHEIREB MUSEUMS: Doha, Qatar
ID: msheireb-museums
Website: https://msheirebmuseums.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Four museums in restored early 20th century houses in Msheireb Downtown Doha, the oldest part of the capital: Bin Jelmood House, Company House, Mohammed Bin Jassim House and Radwani House (stated by the museums; English Wikipedia).
- Sheikha Moza bint Nasser, then chairperson of Msheireb Properties, opened them on 20 October 2015; Msheireb Properties called them its flagship project and planned them as centres of research, exhibitions and education (Gulf Times, 20 October 2015).
- John McAslan was project architect for the restoration and additions, carried out from 2011 to 2015; the museums were on the Royal Institute of British Architects' 2018 list of the world's best new buildings (English Wikipedia).
- The houses cover Qatar's change after oil was found in 1939, Doha's architecture, family life in the 20th century, and in Bin Jelmood House the history of the slave trade (The Art Newspaper, 26 November 2015). Bin Jelmood House uses manumission records from the British political agent in Manama and oral histories from a few Qatari families (The National, 2016). It also holds "DNA: Journey to the Heart of Life", on genetics and Qatar's origins (stated by the museums).
- Hafiz Ali Ali was director of the museums in 2016 (The National); English Wikipedia names Abdulla Al Naama as general manager in 2024. Recent projects include a 2024 research initiative with Hamad Bin Khalifa University on digital citizenship and a 2025 tie-up with Qatar Foundation's Rasekh initiative (English Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free entry. Monday to Thursday and Saturday 9:00 to 17:00, Friday 15:00 to 21:00, closed Sunday; all houses are wheelchair accessible (stated by the museums).

FUNDING
Msheireb Museums: owned by a Qatari state-controlled developer: Msheireb Properties, held by Qatar Foundation and the Qatar Investment Authority
- English Wikipedia names Msheireb Properties as owner; the museums belong to the first phase of its 5.5 billion dollar (20 billion riyal) Msheireb Downtown Doha development.
- Msheireb Properties is 51 per cent owned by Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development; in June 2025 the Qatar Investment Authority, "the sovereign wealth fund of the State of Qatar", bought the other 49 per cent (Zawya, 4 June 2025, company press release).
- Saudi Gazette (7 October 2016) described Qatar Foundation as "a semi-private educational organization founded by the emir's father".
- The Art Newspaper (26 November 2015) said the redevelopment is backed by Sheikha Moza bint Nasser, second wife of Qatar's former emir.
- The museums offer corporate sponsorship packages and venue hire, and sell goods through the Sogha gift shop (stated by the museums).

Red flags: Gulf state body: owned by Qatar Foundation and, since 2025, the Qatar Investment Authority
- The developer that owns the museums is 51 per cent held by Qatar Foundation and 49 per cent by Qatar's sovereign wealth fund (Msheireb Properties and QIA statement on Zawya, company press release).

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper (2015) called the complex the state's "latest bid to rebrand itself as a culture hub" and noted human rights groups' criticism of conditions for migrant workers building stadiums and museums for the 2022 World Cup; it does not name the Msheireb builders.
- Ali said the museum was not a response to criticism over World Cup workers; Kristin Smith Diwan of the Arab Gulf States Institute said broadening the theme to exploitation shows Qatar as open to such challenges (The National, 2016).
- A consultant told The National that some Qataris, descendants of both slaves and slave owners, had concerns about the museum and many refused oral histories, so it does not explore in detail how that history shapes Qatari society today.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Msheireb Museums is Gulf state money.
