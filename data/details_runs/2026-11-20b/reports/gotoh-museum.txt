GOTOH MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: gotoh-museum
Website: https://www.gotoh-museum.or.jp/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum at 3-9-25 Kaminoge, Setagaya-ku, Tokyo, five minutes' walk from Kaminoge station on the Tokyu Oimachi line (museum website).
- It opened on 18 April 1960 as a private foundation museum, planned by Gotoh Keita (1882 to 1959), who laid the foundations of the Tokyu Group and died shortly before it opened (museum website).
- Collection: Japanese and East Asian art Gotoh collected over half his life, starting with Nara-period sutra copies, then Zen calligraphy and tea utensils; he bought the collection of Takanashi Nisaburo, including the Genji handscroll, and ancient mirrors from Moriya Kozo (museum website).
- The National Treasure "Genji Monogatari Emaki", the oldest surviving Japanese illustrated handscroll, made in the 12th century, is shown for about a week each year around Golden Week; the Murasaki Shikibu Diary scroll fragments appear each autumn (museum website; Conde House Japan, 4 September 2026).
- Library: the Daitokyu Memorial Library, opened on 20 April 1949 to mark the 1948 split of Tokyo Kyuko Electric Railway into five companies, moved into the museum in 1960 and merged with it on 1 March 2011 (museum website).
- Building: the main hall by Isoya Yoshida draws on Heian shinden-zukuri design; it was renovated in 2012 and, with two tea houses in the garden of about 6,000 tsubo, became a registered tangible cultural property on 27 October 2017 (museum website).
- Visitors: 62,589 in the year to March 2026 over 243 opening days, across seven exhibitions (FY2025 business report).
- Visiting: 10:00 to 17:00, closed Mondays; for the current exhibition, 1,400 yen, 1,100 yen for high school and university students, free for junior high age and under, 200 yen off for disabled visitors and one carer; garden only 300 yen (museum website).

FUNDING
Gotoh Museum: corporate foundation: mainly donations from Tokyu Corporation
- Status: a public interest incorporated foundation since 1 April 2012; it files its business reports and accounts with the Cabinet Office (museum website; FY2025 business report).
- Tokyu Corporation: gave two general donations of 90,000,000 yen each, on 30 May and 28 November 2025 (FY2025 business report). The 2026 plan expects 190,000,000 yen from Tokyu Corporation as part of its social contribution activities (FY2026 business plan).
- Tokyu Group: sponsors exhibitions, for example the current Muromachi show co-organised with the Nihon Keizai Shimbun; the 2026 plan expects 20,000,000 yen in sponsorship for six exhibitions (museum website; FY2026 business plan).
- Grants: the Mitsubishi Foundation paid 2,480,000 yen towards restoring the Ogura shikishi attributed to Fujiwara no Teika (FY2025 business report).
- Gifts of works in 2025 to 2026 were valued at 3,650,000 yen in all (FY2025 business report).
- Repairs: works paid from the facilities repair reserve came to 111,963,500 yen in FY2025, with building work by Tokyu Property Management and garden work by Tokyu Green System, both Tokyu Group firms by name (FY2025 business report).
- Friends: the "Bi no Tomo Kai" members' association charges 4,000 yen a year for free entry and monthly lectures (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money with no flagged funder. The weak point is dependence: one company carries almost the whole budget.
