TUOL SLENG GENOCIDE MUSEUM: Phnom Penh, Cambodia
ID: tuol-sleng-genocide-museum
Website: https://tuolsleng.gov.kh (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial museum on Street 113 in Boeung Keng Kang, Phnom Penh (museum website).
- The site began as two schools built in 1962, Tuol Sleng Primary School and Tuol Svay Prey High School. After the Khmer Rouge took Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975 it became S-21, the regime's main detention and interrogation centre, where about 15,000 to 20,000 prisoners were held and tortured before execution at Choeung Ek (Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts).
- The archive holds thousands of forced confessions, film negatives, biographies and cadre diaries, and is registered in UNESCO's Memory of the World (museum website).
- On 11 July 2025 UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage List in Paris, with the former M-13 prison and Choeung Ek, as the Cambodian Memorial Sites (Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts).
- In April 2021 the Ministry of Culture called on the artist Matt Loughrey and VICE to remove altered, colourised portraits of S-21 prisoners; VICE removed the article, saying it "did not meet the editorial standards of VICE" (Al Jazeera, citing Reuters).
- Visiting: open every day 8.00 to 17.00; foreign adults $5, ages 10 to 17 $3, children under 10 and Cambodians free; audio guide $5 for foreigners and $1 for Cambodians; cash only (museum website).

FUNDING
Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum: mainly public: Cambodia's Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts, with foreign aid for its archive and collections
- Operator: the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts, which lists the museum among its institutions (mcfa.gov.kh).
- Archive digitisation, phase II: the Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA) committed US$3 million over five years from 2024, through UNESCO; KOICA's Cambodia director Choi Moon Jung and UNESCO representative Alam Sardar Umar signed the grant, with Culture Minister Phoeurng Sackona signing the record of discussion. Phase I digitised over 745,494 pages (Cambodge Mag).
- Victims' clothing: the US State Department's Ambassadors Fund for Cultural Preservation gave $55,000 in December 2017 (VOA Khmer) and $75,000 for a second phase from September 2019 (Culture in Crisis).
- Earlier archive preservation partners: Cornell University (1992), the US National Archives (1994) and the Documentation Center of Cambodia (1995); museum archivists trained in Japan in 2009 and came back with acid-free storage materials (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in the art press; the main critique is a 2014 essay on how the museum has been framed politically since 1979.
1. New Mandala: David Hopkins, "Reflections on Tuol Sleng" (2014)
URL: https://www.newmandala.org/reflections-on-tuol-sleng/
- Vietnam set the museum up after its 1979 invasion and used its evidence to justify the intervention; exhibits such as a map of Khmer Rouge "acts of aggression" against Vietnam reflected Vietnamese aims.
- The uncensored display, with little explanatory text, aims to shock, and Western viewers risk reading the victim photographs as art.
- Under Hun Sen the museum serves an emotive purpose that casts the ruling Cambodian People's Party as the nation's saviour, letting elites avoid a deeper reckoning.
- Not an art outlet: an academic blog on Southeast Asian politics.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: state money and foreign conservation aid, none of it from a flagged source. The point to watch is political: how the government that runs the museum uses it to tell its own story.
