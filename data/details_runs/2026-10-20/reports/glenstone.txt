GLENSTONE: Potomac, United States
ID: glenstone
Website: https://www.glenstone.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Free private museum of modern and contemporary art at 12100 Glen Road, Potomac, Maryland, near Washington, DC, on a 230-acre campus that was formerly a fox hunting club (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Mitchell Rales and his wife Emily Wei Rales (The Art Newspaper, 2023); Rales bought the property in 1986 to build a home and the museum opened in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the original gallery was designed by Charles Gwathmey; the Pavilions, designed by Thomas Phifer and Partners at a cost of $200 million, opened in 2018 (The Art Newspaper, 2018). A Phifer building for Richard Serra's "Four Rounds" opened in 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is always free and scheduling a visit in advance is required (Glenstone). Wikipedia states children under 12 are not admitted.

FUNDING
Glenstone: self-funded: an endowment given by the founder Mitchell Rales
- Glenstone Foundation is a private foundation (Form 990-PF). Rales gave it $1.9 billion in 2021, made public in 2023; The Art Newspaper reported net assets of $4.6 billion afterwards. ProPublica's filings show contributions of $1,887,406,255 in fiscal 2021 and net assets of $2.70 billion in fiscal 2024.
- Rales co-founded the conglomerate Danaher Corp; Forbes lists his net worth at $5.6 billion (The Art Newspaper, 2023).
- The endowment pays operating costs, maintenance and acquisitions; The Art Newspaper (2018) reports an operating foundation with no acquisitions committee or board of trustees constraining purchases.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, public funding or a donor list.

Red flags: staff union drive (labour dispute)
- 8 May 2024: about 100 hourly staff, around half the workforce, gave Glenstone 24 hours to voluntarily recognise a union, with the International Brotherhood of Teamsters; the lowest-paid members of the bargaining unit earned $20.58 an hour against a $27.12 living wage for Montgomery County, calculated by MIT (Hyperallergic, Elaine Velie). Glenstone had not responded to Hyperallergic's request for comment.
- 7 June 2024: 89 workers voted to join Teamsters Local 639 (Teamsters press release).
- Unverified: that the owners ran an "intense union-busting campaign" and paid union avoidance consultants (the release names Proskauer Rose and the American Labor Group), from the Teamsters' press release; the union is a party to the dispute and a partisan source. No Glenstone response was found.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Most coverage is admiring. The criticism that exists concerns access, the tax status of private museums and staff pay, not the collection.
1. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, "Glenstone's giant $200m expansion is ready, but will visitors come?" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/09/21/glenstones-giant-dollar200m-expansion-is-ready-but-will-visitors-come
- Reports the Senate inquiry, the low visitor numbers and the daily cap of about 400 with all October and November slots already booked.
- Reports a $24 million lawsuit by contractor Hitt Contracting alleging disorganisation and changes (Glenstone declined to comment), and 2012 environmentalist criticism of its public sewer connection.
2. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Workers at Maryland's Glenstone Museum Move to Unionize" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/912801/workers-at-glenstone-museum-move-to-unionize/
- Staff seek living wages, healthcare for part-timers, safer conditions and transparency, set against the billionaire founders.
3. The Washington Post: Sebastian Smee, piece on Glenstone (2019, read in an ArtsJournal repost whose page does not show the headline)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=188533
- Mostly praise: calls Glenstone a 21st-century version of the Frick, the Phillips Collection or the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum.
- The excerpt includes the line that the founders "have designs on you".

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2015 to 2016: Senator Orrin Hatch, chairman of the Senate Finance Committee, examined 11 private art museums, Glenstone among them, and sent letters asking about visiting hours, donations, trustees, valuations and art loans (The Art Newspaper, 2018; Oregon Business).
- In a letter to the IRS Hatch wrote that some museums "are not readily accessible by the general public"; Rales said Glenstone "did not deserve to be in that category". The New York Times reported fewer than 10,000 visitors from 2006 to 2013 (The Art Newspaper, 2018).
- The inquiry concerned the tax status of private museums, not their funders, and no page read shows it was aimed at Glenstone alone. Glenstone was one of the museums that received letters.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Glenstone is one family's: Rales's Danaher wealth in a $1.9 billion endowment, with no public or corporate funder found. The weak points are low early access, the 2024 dispute over staff pay and recognition, and Glenstone's place in the 2015 to 2016 Senate inquiry into private museums' tax status, which was not aimed at it alone. Worth watching is the union's first contract and the foundation's future filings.
