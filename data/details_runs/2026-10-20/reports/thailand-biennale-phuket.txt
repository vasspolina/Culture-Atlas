THAILAND BIENNALE: Phuket, Thailand
ID: thailand-biennale-phuket
Website: https://www.thailandbiennale.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale organised by Thailand's Office of Contemporary Art and Culture (OCAC), Ministry of Culture (Tourism Authority of Thailand news item); the fourth edition, "Eternal [Kalpa]", runs in Phuket from 29 November 2025 to 30 April 2026 in partnership with the provincial government of Phuket (e-flux announcement, 19 August 2025).
- Earlier editions: Krabi in 2018, Korat in 2021 and Chiang Rai in 2023 (Biennial Foundation).
- Artistic directors Arin Rungjang and David Teh, with curators Hera Chan and Marisa Phandharakrajadej (e-flux).
- Shows about 65 artists and collectives from 25 countries (Tourism Authority of Thailand news item); works are dispersed across 19 venues in and around Phuket, from failed commercial buildings to Sino-Portuguese mansions (ArtReview, Max Crosbie-Jones, 13 April 2026).
- The organisers say the venues are "historic, post-industrial and civic" and that outdoor "Timepieces" are placed in everyday public spaces (e-flux).

FUNDING
Thailand Biennale: state-run: a division of the government's culture ministry, organised with the Phuket provincial government
- ArtReview states that the biennale "is funded by a division of the government's culture ministry" (Max Crosbie-Jones, 13 April 2026).
- OCAC, Ministry of Culture, organises it in partnership with the provincial government of Phuket (e-flux; Tourism Authority of Thailand).
- The 50 new commissions are realised in partnership with art institutions, government agencies and private foundations from around the world (e-flux; ArtAsiaPacific, 11 September 2025, a piece tagged "Sponsored").

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two reviews of the Phuket edition, both published on 13 April 2026: one mixed, one mostly positive. The criticism concerns scale, tourism, sustainability and delays.
1. Berlin Art Link: Adela Lovric, "Conflicting Ambitions: Thailand Biennale Phuket" (13 April 2026)
URL: https://www.berlinartlink.com/2026/04/13/thailand-biennale-phuket-review/
- The fourth edition aims to match Venice in scale; the review says the biennial is "riddled with some growing pains", and that across its many sites "meaningful encounters are sacrificed for fleeting impressions".
- Thailand's Ministry of Culture presents the biennial both as a driver of tourism and as a platform for reflecting on human-environment relations; the review says these aims "sit uneasily together".
- Mostly empty shuttle buses and energy-intensive cooling in previously inactive venues, the review argues, undermine its sustainability claims.
2. ArtReview: Max Crosbie-Jones, "Thailand Biennale 2025 Review: Beyond the Tropical Paradise" (13 April 2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/thailand-biennale-review-beyond-the-tropical-paradise/
- Mostly positive review with reservations: some artists were still installing work as the press tour arrived ("The delays were disheartening, and protracted"), and no artwork stands as the "entry point or opener".
- Says TOQA's "Like the sun, I love the sky" sits "between wistful melodrama and performative branding exercise", a connotation extended by the biennale's docents wearing TOQA's uniforms.
- Asks: "Had Phuket's 'island time' been slowed by Bangkok's 'bureaucratic time'?"

BOTTOM LINE
The Thailand Biennale is a state-run event, funded, according to ArtReview, by a division of the government's culture ministry, and organised by the Office of Contemporary Art and Culture with the Phuket provincial government. Its 50 new commissions are realised in partnership with art institutions, government agencies and private foundations.
