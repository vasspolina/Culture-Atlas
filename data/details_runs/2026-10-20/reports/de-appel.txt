DE APPEL: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: de-appel
Website: https://www.deappel.nl (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at Tolstraat 160, Amsterdam: an exhibition space, a curatorial and educational lab, and an art library and archive (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1975 by Wies Smals, a regular at the In-Out Center, to give performance and video art a stage (2016 annual report, Wikipedia).
- Saskia Bos set up the Curatorial Programme in 1994; past directors include Ann Demeester, Lorenzo Benedetti and Monika Szewczyk, and Lara Khaldi became director in 2023, with Eszter Szakács as interim director from October 2025 to March 2026 (Wikipedia, 2025 annual report).
- After losing national funding in 2016 it cut seven jobs and found new premises; in 2025 it moved most of its archive to the De Lutmaak studio complex nearby (2016 and 2025 annual reports).
- Programmes include exhibitions, an Embedded Art strand in schools and the neighbourhood, a market kiosk on the Albert Cuyp and Dappermarkt, and the archive; it marked its 50th year in 2025 (2025 annual report).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 14:00 to 20:00; adults €6, students, neighbours and Uitpas holders €3, under-18s free; Museumkaart, ICOM and Stadspas accepted (stated by the space).

FUNDING
De Appel: mainly public: Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst and Mondriaan Fonds, with project funds
- Total income in 2025 was €1,370,224 and spending €1,300,077, a surplus of €74,008 (2025 annual accounts).
- Four-year subsidy from the Amsterdams Fonds voor de Kunst: €509,000 in 2025 (€536,041 in 2024); multi-year subsidy from the Mondriaan Fonds: €450,000 in 2025 (€292,338 in 2024), both for the 2025 to 2028 arts plan period (2025 annual report).
- Incidental public subsidies brought €143,935; own income was €267,289, including €172,128 from private sources (2025 annual accounts).
- Ammodo funds the Curatorial Programme; project funders include Amsterdam's Zuid and Oost districts, the Fonds voor Cultuurparticipatie, Fonds21, Het Cultuurfonds, Stichting DOEN, the Vriendenloterijfonds and the ELJA Foundation (stated by the space).
- The supervisory board is chaired by Bart Beekman, a judge at the Amsterdam district court; Jelle Miedema, company secretary of Randstad, is treasurer (2025 annual report).

Red flags: 2015 dismissal of the director and protest
- In 2015 the board ended its work with director Lorenzo Benedetti, who had started on 1 June 2014; he sued, and the decision set off a heated public debate (2015 and 2016 annual reports).
- Open letters and petitions called on the board to resign, and the Curatorial Programme's tutors resigned publicly (Artribune).
- In May 2016 the national Raad voor Cultuur decided to stop funding De Appel through the Basisinfrastructuur; the board then resigned, and alderman Kajsa Ollongren and the institution's supporters decided to appoint an intendant, Maaike Lauwaert (2016 annual report).
- No reporting found of the Amsterdam inquiry into the board named in the record.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism on record concerns the 2015 to 2016 governance crisis; the Artnet report on it could not be opened, and current coverage is mostly admiring.
1. Artribune: Alessandra Troncone, "Lettera dal de Appel (in tempo di crisi)" (2016)
URL: https://www.artribune.com/attualita/2016/05/de-appel-amsterdam-curatorial-programme/
- Written by a 2015 to 2016 Curatorial Programme participant: the dismissal came as "una comunicazione secca, senza spiegazioni aggiuntive", and no explanation followed for months.
- The students had a strained, hard-to-reach relationship with the institution, and working without a director made decisions harder.
- Says the institution did not always handle the effects well, while crediting Amsterdam's art scene.

BOTTOM LINE
De Appel runs on Amsterdam and national arts fund money plus small project grants and donations; nothing in it comes from a flagged industry. The weak point is governance history: the 2015 dismissal, the protest and the 2016 loss of national funding nearly closed it. Worth watching: whether the stabilised finances of 2024 and 2025 hold through the 2025 to 2028 funding period.
