HOUSE OF TERROR (TERROR HÁZA): Budapest, Hungary
ID: house-of-terror-terror-haza
Website: https://www.terrorhaza.hu (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Hungary's fascist and communist dictatorships at Andrássy út 60, the Arrow Cross party's former House of Loyalty and later the ÁVH state security headquarters (444); half of its basement reconstructs the prison cells of the 1940s and 1950s (museum website).
- The Public Foundation for Research on Central and Eastern European History and Society bought the building in December 2000, and the museum opened on 24 February 2002, with Mária Schmidt as director-general (museum website).
- Architect Attila F. Kovács designed the interior, the exhibition and the black frame on the facade; Ákos Kovács composed the music (museum website).
- More than 8 million people visited in its first 24 years (Origo, 2026).
- Since 22 September 2026 it has been open with the exhibition unchanged while staff of ELTE's Institute of History and independent experts review it (444; Artincontext).
- Visiting: open every day except Monday, 10.00 to 18.00, cash desk closes at 17.30; full ticket 4,000 forints, reduced 2,000; tickets are sold only at the museum (museum website).

FUNDING
House of Terror: state-funded: a government-financed foundation from 2002 to 2026, now a culture ministry company
- Operator until 31 August 2026: the Foundation for Research on Central and Eastern European History and Society, set up in 1999 and led by Schmidt as director-general, which ran the museum alongside seven research institutes and a workshop (444; Hungarian Conservative).
- State money: the public foundation received 5.5 billion forints in 2019, one billion more than in 2018, according to its public benefit report as reported by Népszava; Schmidt was paid 64 million forints that year (444, 2020).
- Operator since 31 August 2026: Kulturális Vagyonkezelő Kft., a background company of the Ministry for Social Relations and Culture, named successor by government decree 134/2026 of 28 August (Artincontext), after Prime Minister Péter Magyar ordered non-university KEKVA foundations back into state ownership (Hungarian Conservative).
- The museum's supporters page names the Prime Minister's Office, Bethlen Gábor Alapkezelő Zrt. and the Petőfi Cultural Agency, with a long list of individual donors (museum website).

Red flags: 2026 ministerial order removing the director and closing the museum
- Culture Minister Zoltán Tarr ended Schmidt's appointment on 15 September 2026 and closed the museum until a review of its exhibition content was complete and the rights were settled (444; Hungarian Conservative).
- Schmidt said she received no reason for her dismissal and no reply to her letters (444).

Red flags: 2026 rally against the closure
- Right-wing commentators held a rally outside the museum on 20 September 2026 against its closure and Schmidt's removal; speakers included Fidesz politician Tamás Menczer, Zsolt Bayer and Schmidt herself (Infostart).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Historians have criticised the exhibition since it opened; the 2026 change of operator brought new critiques.
1. 444: Ádám Kolozsi, "A NER propagandagyárának látványkonyhájából kellene valódi múzeumot csinálni: a Terror Háza Schmidt Mária után" (2026)
URL: https://444.hu/2026/09/19/a-ner-propagandagyaranak-latvanykonyhajabol-kellene-valodi-muzeumot-csinalni-a-terror-haza-schmidt-maria-utan
- Calls the museum the show kitchen of the Orbán system's propaganda factory, built on a "double totalitarianism" story that equates communism with Nazism and pins the Holocaust on the Arrow Cross and the Germans, implicitly clearing the Horthy era.
- Quotes museologists Zsófia Frazon and Zsolt K. Horváth, who wrote that its approach amounted to "falsifying history" (translated from Hungarian), and historian Gábor Egry, who said its memory-politics and research functions had merged; notes the exhibition barely changed in 24 years.
2. The Baffler: Alexander Wells, "House of the Rising Hun: Manipulations of historical memory in Orbán's Hungary" (2025)
URL: https://thebaffler.com/salvos/house-of-the-rising-hun-wells
- Counts "three rooms dedicated to fascism and perhaps two dozen to communism" and argues the museum includes fascism to borrow the Holocaust's moral weight for an anticommunist project.
- Notes that executions never took place at 60 Andrássy Avenue and that the displays cast Hungary as a victim, although Horthy's government allied itself with the Axis; describes Schmidt as a professor and media mogul "whom Fidesz appointed as historian-in-chief".
3. Artincontext: Isabella Meyer, "Budapest's House of Terror Reopens While Exhibition Review Continues" (2026)
URL: https://artincontext.org/?p=127406
- Notes the state-ordered review had no published terms of reference, interim leadership or completion date.
- Cites a 2017 study by Zsófia Réti in The Hungarian Historical Review on criticism of the museum's emotional, multimedia style and its imbalance between the fascist and communist periods.

BOTTOM LINE
State money from start to finish, which alone is no red flag. The weak points are 24 years under a government-aligned foundation that controlled the narrative, and now a new government's direct intervention in the content; the outcome of the review and the choice of a new director are worth watching.
