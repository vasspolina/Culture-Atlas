CUE ART FOUNDATION: New York, United States
ID: cue-art-foundation-new-york
Website: https://cueartfoundation.org (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit gallery and artist programme at 137 West 25th Street, ground floor, between 6th and 7th Avenues, in Chelsea, Manhattan (CUE website).
- Established in 2003; the idea came after 9/11 from shipping executive Thomas K. Y. Hsu, with painter Gregory Amenoff as co-founder and titular head. Its name is a play on "curate" (Brooklyn Rail, April 2021).
- Its first gallery was at 511 West 25th Street. In 2012 rising Chelsea rents moved it to the current storefront, which an anonymous donor bought and renovated (Brooklyn Rail).
- Its model is artists choosing and mentoring other artists for solo shows. Other programmes: the Art Critic Mentorship Program (2006), the Open Call (2016), the CUE Teen Collective, Office Hours and the Evercore Artist Award (Brooklyn Rail; CUE website).
- It describes itself as W.A.G.E. certified (stated by the space).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday, 12 to 6pm. The gallery is wheelchair accessible with an all-gender, ADA-compliant restroom; large-print texts are available on request. The page lists no admission fee (CUE visitor information).

FUNDING
CUE Art Foundation: mainly private philanthropy: foundation grants, individual members and donors, with federal arts money
- Contributions made up 81.9% of 2024 revenue. Revenue was $297,378 in 2024, down from $599,792 in 2023 and $889,290 in 2021; 2024 expenses were $524,313, a deficit of $226,935, and net assets were $1,336,490 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, EIN 54-2064462).
- Foundation grants listed by the aggregator Granted AI: Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, $50,000 in 2020 and $50,000 in 2021, each for program support over two years; Hillman Family Foundations, $10,000 three times in 2021 and $3,000 in 2020 for the Teen Collective; Shelley & Donald Rubin Foundation, $10,000 in 2020 and $8,000 in 2021 for the Open Call; Milton and Sally Avery Arts Foundation, $10,000 in 2021; SRS Family Foundation, $10,000 in 2020 and $5,000 in 2021; Wilhelm Family Foundation, $5,000 in 2020; Kahn Charitable Foundation, $1,000 in 2021; Jedra Charitable Foundation, $1,000 in 2023.
- Federal: Granted AI lists eight National Endowment for the Arts awards totalling $200,000, reported for 2024.
- The Shelley & Donald Rubin Foundation named CUE one of 57 recipients of its 2019 art and social justice grant (CUE news post, 26 January 2019).
- Corporate: Aon launched an annual prize with CUE in 2018 with an honorarium and a year-long exhibition at its New York office (CUE news post, 16 November 2018). Evercore, Inc. is listed among CUE's funders on the Evercore Artist Award page, with the Arison Arts Foundation and ING Group, and the award exhibitions are held at Evercore's New York headquarters; no amounts are stated (CUE website).
- Membership runs from $120 a year to $5,000 a year at the Commissioner level (CUE website).
- Board: Thomas K.Y. Hsu (Chairman), Theodore S. Berger (President), Kate Buchanan, John S. Kiely, Kyle Sheahen, Lilly Wei, Amanda Adams-Louis, Marcy Cohen, Blake Horn, Steffani Jemison, Vivian Kuan, Aliza Nisenbaum, with Gregory Amenoff as Emeritus (CUE staff and board page). Executive Director Jinny Khanduja received $110,000 in reported compensation in 2024 (ProPublica).
- No reporting found of New York State Council on the Arts or New York City Department of Cultural Affairs grants to CUE on the pages opened; CUE's own Supporters page returned an error.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage is admiring: the Brooklyn Rail profile (Ann C. Collins, "ArTonic: CUE Art Foundation", April 2021, https://brooklynrail.org/2021/04/artonic/CUE-Art-Foundation/) draws almost entirely on founders, staff, board members and mentors, and offers no outside assessment. Reviews of individual CUE shows in Hyperallergic and the Brooklyn Rail judge the artists, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind CUE is clean: arts foundations, federal arts funding, members and two companies (Aon, Evercore) hosting prizes, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are thin published funder detail, a 2024 revenue drop to $297,378 and a $226,935 deficit, and a 2024 filing that reports conflict of interest transactions without detail on the page read. Worth watching is the 2025 filing and whether the NEA money continues.
