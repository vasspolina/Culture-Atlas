KAYSONE PHOMVIHANE MUSEUM: Vientiane, Laos
ID: kaysone-phomvihane-museum
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Memorial museum in Vientiane to the late president Kaysone Phomvihane, born on 13 December 1920 and died on 21 November 1992 (KPL, 2015).
- The Party Central Committee's Politburo decided to build it on 16 December 1992; construction began on 13 December 1997, and it opened on 13 December 2000, his 80th birthday (KPL, 2015).
- In March 2025 it held the exhibition "70 Years of the Birth and Growth of the Lao People's Revolutionary Party", nearly 300 images, documents and artefacts, attended by Party Secretary General and President Thongloun Sisoulith and Prime Minister Sonexay Siphandone and presented by Khamphanh Pheuyavong, head of the Party Central Committee's Propaganda and Training Board (KPL).
- On 28 November 2025 Thongloun presided over the opening of a 334-item photo exhibition in two parts, Kaysone's life from 1920 to 1992 and national development from 1975 to 2025, for the Party's 70th anniversary, the Lao PDR's 50th and Kaysone's 105th birth anniversary, with opening remarks by Khamphanh (KPL; Vientiane Times).
- Visiting groups include state staff: almost 50 women from the Lao News Agency toured it in March 2015 to learn about Kaysone's virtues (KPL).

FUNDING
Kaysone Phomvihane Museum: foreign state aid and the Lao party-state: built and refurbished with Vietnamese government grants
- Construction: paid for with non-refundable aid from the Vietnamese government (VOV, reprinted by the Vietnam National Museum of History, 2014).
- Refurbishment: on 24 March 2014 Vietnam agreed more than 14 billion dong, about 658,000 US dollars, in non-refundable aid for an 18-item upgrade of the museum's infrastructure and facilities (VietnamPlus). The work covered the main hall, painting, the Lao cultural symbol and Kaysone's funeral showroom (Vietnam National Museum of History).
- Implementer: VietnamPlus named the Ho Chi Minh City Museum as an investor in the project; the Vietnam National Museum of History named the Ho Chi Minh Museum.

BOTTOM LINE
State money, which is no red flag on the atlas. The weak point is its purpose as a ruling party's shrine to its own leader.
