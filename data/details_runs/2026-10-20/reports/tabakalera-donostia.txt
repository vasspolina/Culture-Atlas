TABAKALERA (INTERNATIONAL CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY CULTURE): San Sebastián, Spain
ID: tabakalera-donostia
Website: https://www.tabakalera.eus

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary culture centre in a former state tobacco factory in the Egia district of San Sebastián, next to Cristina Enea park and the bus and Norte stations (Spanish Wikipedia). The address is Plaza de las Cigarreras, 1 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The factory opened in 1913 and employed mostly women until it closed in 2003; the company that ran Spain's tobacco production was privatised into Altadis (Wikipedia). In 2004 the San Sebastián city council, the Gipuzkoa provincial council and the Basque Government bought the building to create the centre (Wikipedia).
- Refurbishment began in 2010 (Wikipedia) and the renovated building opened on 11 September 2015 (Wikipedia). The architects of the remodelling were Juan Manuel Montero Madariaga and Naiara Montero Viar; the floor area grew from 26,000 to 37,000 square metres (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Four strands: artistic practices, mediation, the digital culture laboratory Hirikilabs and the creation library Ubik; also exhibitions, a cinema, residencies, and the film festival Zabaltegi-Tabakalera since 2016 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The building also houses Kutxa Kultur, the Basque Film Archive, the San Sebastián International Film Festival, the Elías Querejeta Film School and the Etxepare Basque Institute (Wikipedia).
- Orain reported on 14 December 2016 that more than one million people visited in the first long year after opening, and that the Ubik library had 133,000 visitors after opening in March.
- Orain reported on 12 January 2018 that the board appointed Edurne Ormazabal to a new general director post, alongside the cultural director Ane Rodríguez.

FUNDING
Tabakalera: public: San Sebastián city council, Gipuzkoa provincial council and Basque Government (2017 budget)
- Ownership: the centre belongs to the three institutions, which agreed to recover the building and bought it in 2004 through a company called CICC (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Budget 2017: current expenditure of just over 5.8 million euros, to which each of the three institutions contributed 1,600,000 euros and Tabakalera's own income added 1,048,078 euros (Orain, "Tabakalera recibe más de un millón de visitas desde su apertura", 14 December 2016).
- The mayor of San Sebastián was president of the company CICC SA / Tabakalera in February 2019 (EH Bildu municipal motion).
- Tenants in the building include Kutxa Kultur, the Basque Film Archive, the San Sebastián film festival, the Etxepare Institute and the Elías Querejeta Film School (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ubik strike: the mediators who serve the public at the Ubik library, employed by the subcontractor Sedena, began an indefinite strike on 22 December 2018 over job insecurity, with pay under 1,000 euros a month and hard-to-reconcile schedules, per union ELA's spokesperson (Orain, "Los empleados de la biblioteca Ubik de Tabakalera comienzan una huelga indefinida", 22 December 2018). The strike ended on 8 August 2019 with an agreement for 2019 to 2022 that includes a 32 percent pay rise; the library had been closed during it (Orain, "Trabajadores de Ubik de Tabakalera logran un acuerdo que pone fin a la huelga", 8 August 2019). The pages describe a labour dispute with the subcontractor.
- EH Bildu, an opposition group, put a motion to the San Sebastián council on 15 February 2019 asking the city government to take part in the dispute. It said the mayor, as president of Tabakalera's company, held a single meeting, on 14 January, and attributed the whole responsibility to the company and the workers. This is the party's own account.
- Connection: the Ubik library is the creation library inside Tabakalera; the strikers worked for a subcontractor, and the EH Bildu motion is aimed at the mayor as president of Tabakalera's company.

BOTTOM LINE
Tabakalera is publicly owned and funded by the city council, the provincial council and the Basque Government, which each contributed 1.6 million euros to its 2017 budget. The building was a state tobacco factory until 2003. The Ubik library strike of 2018 and 2019 was a labour dispute with a subcontractor.
