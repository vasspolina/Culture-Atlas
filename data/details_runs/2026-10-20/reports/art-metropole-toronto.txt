ART METROPOLE: Toronto, Canada
ID: art-metropole-toronto
Website: https://artmetropole.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit artist-run centre and bookshop at 896 College Street, Toronto, that publishes, promotes, exhibits, archives and distributes artists' books, multiples and related media (Art Metropole website).
- Founded in 1974 by the artist collective General Idea as a division of Art-Official Inc. (established 1972), the non-profit publisher of File Megazine (1972 to 1989) (Art Metropole website). It opened first in "an abandoned space over a Greek restaurant in downtown Toronto" (Wikipedia).
- The site calls the shop the oldest and longest-running artist-run bookstore in the world.
- Archive: the early collection, over 13,000 items, is held at the National Gallery of Canada's Library and Archives; the centre's site dates the transfer to 1999 with support from Jay A. Smith, while Wikipedia dates it to 1997 (Art Metropole website; Wikipedia).
- Programmes: exhibitions and events, a shop, an institutional archive, library services and wholesale (Art Metropole website). The video distribution service began in 1975 and ended in 1987 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm. The shop entrance has a wheelchair ramp and the washroom is not wheelchair accessible at this time (Art Metropole website).

FUNDING
Art Metropole: mainly public arts funding: Canada Council for the Arts, Ontario Arts Council and Toronto Arts Council
- The site thanks the Canada Council for the Arts, the Ontario Arts Council and the Toronto Arts Council, and also lists the Ontario Trillium Foundation (Art Metropole website).
- Charity: registered charity number 12287 1270 RR0001, held by Art-Official Inc. (Art Metropole website).
- Fundraising: a 50th anniversary party with a Lucky Draw on 8 November 2024 where support goes towards the creation of a fund to sustain Art Metropole over the long term; event partners were East Room and Food Dudes, and sponsors were Burdock and Collective Arts (e-flux, 27 October 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Art Metropole is public arts funding with some donations; none of the named funders is flagged. Worth watching is the long-term sustaining fund started for its 50th anniversary.
