BARD COLLEGE: Annandale-on-Hudson, USA
ID: bard-college-annandale-on-hudson
Website: https://www.bard.edu/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private liberal arts college on about 1,260 acres above the Hudson River in Annandale-on-Hudson, New York, inside the Hudson River Historic District.
- Founded in March 1860 as St. Stephen's College on land John Bard had given to the Episcopal diocese; renamed Bard College for him in 1934.
- Its arts venues are the Frank Gehry-designed Richard B. Fisher Center for the Performing Arts, and the Center for Curatorial Studies (CCS Bard), whose Hessel Museum of Art opened on 12 November 2006 in a building by Goettsch Partners.
- The Hessel Museum shows the Marieluise Hessel Collection, more than 1,700 objects on permanent loan to Bard; Hessel founded CCS Bard, and the museum is named for her.
- Leon Botstein was president for 51 years until 30 June 2026; Jonathan Becker is acting president.
- Hessel Museum: open Wednesday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, closed Monday and Tuesday; free; a single-level building.

FUNDING
Bard College: private: donors and programme revenue, led by Open Society Foundations
- FY to June 2025: revenue $930,316,002, of which contributions $694,042,649; expenses $408,373,029; net assets $1,064,525,802; Botstein was paid $565,417 (Form 990 via ProPublica).
- Open Society Foundations gave $100 million in 2020, at $10 million a year for ten years, and George Soros gave a $500 million endowment challenge grant in 2021 (Wikipedia); Bard has wrapped up a $1 billion endowment campaign (Higher Ed Dive).
- Botstein said he helped raise nearly $3 billion during his tenure (Higher Ed Dive).
- No reporting found of a current corporate funder in a flagged industry.

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein
- Epstein gave $75,000 in 2011 and a second gift, including computers, in 2012 to Bard High School Early College through his entity Enhanced Education, years after his guilty pleas (WAMC; Higher Ed Dive).
- WilmerHale found Botstein contacted and visited Epstein often from 2012 to 2019, about 25 times at his townhouse and once for two days on his island, and knew of his crimes in 2012 without telling Bard the money came from Epstein (Higher Ed Dive).
- Epstein hired Botstein for consulting work, paid $150,000 in 2016, which Botstein says he gave to Bard; WilmerHale could not verify the arrangement, and the board did not know of it (Higher Ed Dive, citing the Times Union for the amount).
- Epstein attended concerts at the Fisher Center (Botstein's letter, via WAMC).
- WilmerHale found nothing illegal; Rep. Jamie Raskin called the probe insufficient. Unverified: Botstein was an accomplice in Epstein's trafficking of girls, from Rep. Jamie Raskin, as paraphrased by the reporter (WAMC).
- The board said on 1 May 2026 that Epstein-linked funds will go to groups supporting survivors of sexual harm.

Red flags: Leon Black gift
- About 18 months after Botstein's 2012 island visit, Leon Black gave $250,000 to Bard's arts programmes (Botstein's letter, via WAMC).
- A review by the law firm Dechert, announced by Apollo, found Black paid Epstein $158 million from 2012 to 2017 (Hyperallergic, 2021).

Red flags: staff protest at the Fisher Center
- Fisher Center performing arts staff wrote to the board asking it not to rehire Botstein, calling him "unfit to serve in a position of leadership over the young people on this campus" (OperaWire); nearly 200 people signed (WAMC).
- A staffer said the center risks losing staff and that ticket buyers asked whether he would be involved in upcoming performances (WAMC).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2021 Russia designated Bard an "undesirable organization," which Botstein linked to its Open Society funding (Wikipedia).
- Russia neither funds nor controls Bard, so this is context, not a flag.

BOTTOM LINE
Bard's money today is programme revenue and large philanthropy led by Open Society, with no flagged-industry funder found. The weak point is its president's long pursuit of Epstein and Epstein-linked donors such as Leon Black. Worth watching: whether Botstein keeps a paid role at the Fisher Center and how the donor vetting review is applied.
