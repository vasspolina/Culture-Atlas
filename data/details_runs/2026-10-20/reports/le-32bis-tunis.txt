LE 32BIS: Tunis, Tunisia
ID: le-32bis-tunis
Website: https://32bis.org

OVERVIEW
- Cultural association and art centre at 32 bis, rue Ben Ghedhahem, in the centre of Tunis, dedicated to research, creation, exhibition and the sharing of knowledge (32bis.org).
- Opened as a new contemporary art centre in February 2022 with the exhibition "WIRD" by the Franco-Algerian artist Bruno Hadjih, which ran from 18 February to 18 March 2022; Camille Lévy was artistic director at that time (Managers, 17 February 2022). In 2026 La Presse describes Hela Djebbi as curator and director.
- La Presse says the building was converted into an art centre by an association from 2016 (La Presse, Amel Bouslama, 2 January 2026). A gallery called Le 32 BIS opened on 5 June 2015 on rue Ali Ben Ghedahem in a three-storey former Philips building (Kapitalis); Kapitalis calls Yosr Ben Ammar and Mehdi Ben Cheikh the two gallery directors (Kapitalis, Fawz Ben Ali, 9 June 2015).
- Building: the former Philips headquarters, built in 1953, in two renovated buildings covering 4,000 m²; the first holds a specialised media library, a residency space with accommodation and a studio, and rooms for research and conferences, and the second, over 2,000 m² on three levels, is for exhibitions (32bis.org). Mamia Taktak handled the renovation (Managers).
- On site: exhibitions, artist residencies, events, a media library focused on Africa and the Middle East, performances, workshops and cultural education (32bis.org; Managers).
- Visiting: administrative hours are 9 am to 5 pm, with a summer closure from the end of July (32bis.org).

FUNDING
Le 32Bis: private patronage: an association funded by private patrons, according to its artistic director
- Camille Lévy said in 2022: "Nous sommes une association qui fonctionne grâce à l'aide du mécénat privé" ("We are an association that works thanks to private patronage") (Managers, 17 February 2022).
- Partners of the opening exhibition: Lalla Hadria Editions, the Lalla Hadria collection and the Djerba Explore museum (Managers).
- The centre partners with the African Culture Fund (ACF) for the ACF-Academy, a programme for young visual artists and cultural managers across Africa, announced for July 2022 (Le Journal des Arts, Anne-Cécile Sanchez, 14 June 2022; Managers).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Le 32Bis is private patronage channelled through a cultural association, as its artistic director stated in 2022. Its named programme partners are the African Culture Fund and the Lalla Hadria collection.
