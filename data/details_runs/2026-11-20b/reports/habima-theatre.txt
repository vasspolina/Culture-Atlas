HABIMA THEATRE: Tel Aviv, Israel
ID: habima-theatre
Website: https://www.habima.co.il (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Israel's national theatre, on Habima Square in central Tel Aviv (Wikipedia).
- Founded as an amateur Hebrew troupe by Nahum Zemach in Białystok in 1912; from 1918 it worked under the Moscow Art Theatre, premiered "The Dybbuk" in Moscow on 31 January 1922, left the Soviet Union in 1926 and settled in Palestine in 1931 (Wikipedia).
- It built its Tel Aviv house in 1945 and has been the official national theatre since 1958, when it received the Israel Prize (Wikipedia).
- Building: Ram Karmi rebuilt the house from 2007 to 2011, keeping its facades; it reopened on 22 January 2012 with four halls, Rovina (930 seats), Meskin (320), Bertonov (220) and Blanche Rapaport (170) (Hebrew Wikipedia; Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Dani Weiss has been general director since 1 September 2023 (Hebrew Wikipedia); on 15 June 2025 the board, chaired by Ziva Patir, appointed the director Noam Shmuel artistic director, while the theatre was closed by the war with Iran (Ice, 16 June 2025).
- Programme: the 2025 repertoire included the musical "Priscilla, Queen of the Desert", a stage version of Joseph Cedar's film "Footnote" and a new "Merchant of Venice" (Ice, 7 January 2025); it also runs a youth acting programme, Noar Habima (theatre website; Hebrew Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Habima Theatre: public: Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality and Israel's Culture Ministry
- Owner: Habima has been a municipal corporation of the Tel Aviv-Yafo municipality since 24 March 2022; the city appointed a new board chaired by Ziva Patir (N12, 24 March 2022).
- Rescue: with a deficit of more than NIS 100 million, the theatre was saved before liquidation by a joint initiative of the Culture Ministry, the municipality, the Histadrut labour federation and the actors' union; Culture Minister Hili Tropper asked Mayor Ron Huldai to take it on (Maariv, 18 April 2021).
- Before that, the district court suspended the general director and board on 25 November 2019 and appointed temporary trustees over debts of tens of millions of shekels, after the actors' union declared a labour dispute over late salaries (Hebrew Wikipedia).
- State funding: after a crisis in the late 1960s Habima became a national theatre funded from the state budget, with a board of trustees choosing its directors (Hebrew Wikipedia).
- Building works: the 2007 to 2011 rebuild cost about NIS 100 million, paid mainly by the Tel Aviv municipality with the Culture Ministry (Hebrew Wikipedia).
- Funding conditions: under Culture Minister Miri Regev, theatres that performed in settlements received a 10 percent bonus and those that refused could face a one-third cut in government funding (JTA, November 2016).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 10 November 2016 Habima staged Agnon's "A Simple Story" in the settlement of Kiryat Arba near Hebron, against opposition from supporters of the theatre and left-wing groups; one actor refused to perform for ideological reasons (JTA).
- Before the show Breaking the Silence took some actors on a tour of Hebron; Regev posted "Well done, Habima. Well done, Kiryat Arba" and promised shows in Bet El, Elkana and Ariel (JTA).
- In September 2010, as more than 30 artists declared a boycott of the new Ariel cultural centre, Habima co-manager Odelia Friedman told a Knesset committee that "residents of Ariel are residents of Israel and Habima will stage shows for them" (Arutz Sheva).
- On 29 March 2012 actors and directors including Emma Thompson, Mike Leigh and Jonathan Miller asked Shakespeare's Globe in a Guardian letter to withdraw Habima's invitation to its Globe to Globe festival, citing its "shameful record of involvement with illegal Israeli settlements" (letter reprinted by Mondoweiss).
- These disputes concern Habima's own performances under the funding rules of its state funder.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money: a city-owned theatre with state support, rescued from insolvency. The weak point is state influence over where it plays, shown by the settlement performances; worth watching is whether the city corporation keeps its books balanced.
