GARAGE MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Moscow, Russia
ID: garage-museum-moscow
Website: https://garagemca.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum at 9/32 Krymsky Val in Gorky Park, Moscow (stated by the space).
- Founded in 2008 by Dasha Zhukova and Roman Abramovich; it is a non-profit project of Zhukova's IRIS Foundation (stated by the space).
- Named for its first home, Konstantin Melnikov's former Bakhmetevsky Bus Garage on Obraztsova Street (2008 to 2011); from 2012 to 2015 it used a temporary Gorky Park pavilion by Shigeru Ban (stated by the space).
- Since 2015 it has occupied the former Vremena Goda restaurant (1968), rebuilt by Rem Koolhaas and OMA (stated by the space).
- It runs an archive of Russian contemporary art, a library of more than 55,000 books and journals, publishing, Garage Screen, studios and residencies at VDNKh opened in 2019 and, from 2024, its own collection shown in open storage (stated by the space).
- Daria Kotova, previously director of the endowment fund, replaced founding director Anton Belov in April 2025 (The Art Newspaper).
- Open daily, 11am to 10pm; open storage costs 600 roubles, free for students, schoolchildren, pensioners, visitors with disabilities and a companion, and several other groups; it publishes an accessibility plan (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Garage: founder-funded: Abramovich and Zhukova, plus an endowment of small donors
- Garage is a project of the IRIS Foundation, founded by Zhukova (stated by the space); no reporting found of its annual budget or accounts.
- The Garage Endowment Fund, set up in 2019 and described by Garage as Russia's third-largest museum endowment, invests donations and spends only the income (stated by the space).
- Its donor page names Roman Abramovich first among private donors for 2018 to 2023; donors named for 2024 to 2026 are individuals, including Anton Belov, and the company Leta; corporate donors for 2018 to 2023 include Cosmoscow and Cafe GARAGE (endowment fund donor page).
- No reporting found of state funding.

Red flags: co-founder Roman Abramovich under UK and EU sanctions
- Abramovich is subject to UK and EU sanctions (The Art Newspaper, 2025).
- The EU Council, in March 2022, called him an "oligarch close to Vladimir Putin", cited his stake in the steel group Evraz, and said he had benefited from Russian decision-makers responsible for annexing Crimea or destabilising Ukraine (MyKhel).

Red flags: 2018 petition against developer sponsorship
- In December 2018 Moscow residents petitioned director Anton Belov and chief curator Kate Fowle on Change.org to drop a partnership with developer PIK Group, saying it served PIK's image and "discredits" Garage; Garage declined to comment (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The press records pressure on Garage rather than critics' cases against it; the one campaign aimed at its money is the 2018 petition.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Moscow residents petition Garage Museum to reject sponsorship from property developer" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/12/19/moscow-residents-petition-garage-museum-to-reject-sponsorship-from-property-developer
- Reports the petition's case that PIK's high-rises are environmentally unsafe and that the tie-up served its commercial interests.
2. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Amid crackdowns on dissent, Russia's private museums are threatened" (2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/07/14/amid-cracks-down-on-dissent-russias-beleaguered-private-museums-fight-to-survive
- Not criticism: places Garage's 2022 pause, the 2024 raid and right-wing activists' visit among threats to Russia's private museums.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper (2025) describes crackdowns on dissent that threaten Russia's private museums, including New Holland in St Petersburg, which The Art Newspaper reports changed legal ownership in 2024 and whose former director resigned in March 2024 after her husband was charged with terrorism and extremism.
- In February 2022 Garage stopped work on all exhibitions "until the human and political tragedy that is convulsing Ukraine ends", postponing Lydia Masterkova and Anne Imhof shows (Finestre sull'Arte).
- On 26 April 2024 security services searched its office and its archive in the Education Center; Novaya Gazeta Europe linked the search to the case of Pussy Riot member Pyotr Verzilov.
- After it reopened with "Open Storage. Prologue" on 30 August 2024, pro-war activists came to the museum on 10 September, saying they wanted portraits of "heroes of the special military operation" (Meduza, our translation).
- The Russian state neither funds nor runs Garage; these events place it inside that crackdown rather than mark its money.

BOTTOM LINE
Garage is a project of its founders' IRIS Foundation, and co-founder Roman Abramovich is under UK and EU sanctions for his closeness to Putin and is the first private donor listed on its endowment page for 2018 to 2023. The other weak point is pressure from the Russian state. Worth watching: whether Abramovich money continues and whether the 2024 change of ownership The Art Newspaper reports at New Holland in St Petersburg reaches Garage.
