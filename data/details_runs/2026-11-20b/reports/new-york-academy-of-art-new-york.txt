NEW YORK ACADEMY OF ART: New York, USA
ID: new-york-academy-of-art-new-york
Website: https://nyaa.edu/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Graduate school for figurative and representational art at 111 Franklin Street in Tribeca, New York (school website).
- Instruction began in 1980 as the New York Drawing Association in a rented basement at the Middle Collegiate Church, with collector Stuart Pivar providing key financial support; later accounts list Andy Warhol as a key founder and funder (Wikipedia).
- Since 1990 it has occupied a five-storey, 42,000 square foot building designed by Benjamin Warner in 1861; a 2001 fire damaged half the interior, and TRA Studio renovated it (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: a two-year MFA in painting, drawing and sculpture, a one-year Certificate of Fine Arts, a four-week summer residency for undergraduates and continuing education workshops (school website). The MFA enrols about 100 students (Wikipedia).
- Paul R. Provost became president in April 2025, succeeding David Kratz, president from 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Public events: the Tribeca Ball, first held in 1994, and the Take Home a Nude auction at Sotheby's (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
New York Academy of Art: privately funded: tuition, donations and fundraising events
- Form 990 for the year to June 2025: revenue $8,845,834, of which program services (tuition) $6,337,108 and contributions $2,634,384; fundraising events ran at a net loss of $608,856; expenses $10,011,292; net assets $9,576,040 (ProPublica).
- Trustees in that filing include chair Eileen Guggenheim, Brooke Shields, Eric Fischl and David Walentas (ProPublica).
- Warhol left the school a bequest after his death in 1987 that let it buy the Franklin Street building, and the Warhol Foundation had given it more than $1 million by 2004 (Wikipedia).
- Van Cleef & Arpels was title sponsor of the Tribeca Ball on 15 April 2025 (Tribeca Citizen, a post sponsored by the Academy).

Red flags: Jeffrey Epstein on the board, 1987 to 1994, and his money after his 2008 plea
- Epstein came to the Academy through Pivar and served on the board from 1987 to 1994; both men left the board in 1994 (Academy statement, printed by Artforum, 2019).
- After his Florida prison sentence he bought tickets to one fundraising event in 2012 and two in 2014, and gave $30,000 in 2014 (same statement).
- In August 2020 the board said it would give $30,000, the amount Epstein gave in scholarship support, to a charity for victims of sexual assault (The Art Newspaper).
- In March 2026 the Academy transferred $65,900 in Epstein-linked donations to Girls Educational and Mentoring Services (GEMS) and said it had now redirected all money received from him after his 2008 conviction; board members agreed to match the sum (USA Art News, 16 March 2026).
- The board's email admitted "serious failures in judgment and governance" and announced an ethics committee to review fundraising and donor policies (Türkiye Today, citing The New York Times).
- Guggenheim retired as chair on 1 April 2026, a month early, and stays on the board (USA Art News).
- Documents in the Epstein files show the FBI interviewed Guggenheim in 2007 in a federal investigation into the sexual exploitation of minors (USA Art News, citing an FBI document).
- Unverified: alumna Maria Farmer alleges that Guggenheim, then dean of students, introduced her to Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell at her 1995 thesis show and pressed her to sell them a painting at a discount. Guggenheim denies introducing them and says she does not recall the conversation (Artforum; USA Art News).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In May 2020 a petition called for Guggenheim's resignation as chair (Wikipedia).
- A law-firm investigation released in June 2020 concluded that "critical aspects" of Farmer's allegations against Guggenheim were untrue, and the board backed its chair; after several board members resigned, among them Naomi Watts, the executive committee apologised to Farmer in August 2020 (The Art Newspaper).
- These are the earlier disputes over the same Epstein link.

BOTTOM LINE
Tuition and private gifts pay for the school. The Epstein link, through his board seat and his post-conviction money, is documented and admitted by the board; worth watching is what the promised ethics committee changes.
