MANSUDAE ART MUSEUM / MANSUDAE ART STUDIO GALLERY, 798: Beijing, China
ID: mansudae-art-museum-mansudae-art-studio-gallery-798-beijing
Website: https://798artdistrict.com (checked 2026-10-24; parked, redirects to a GoDaddy domain-for-sale page; it is the art district's domain, not the gallery's, and no website of the gallery itself was found)

OVERVIEW
- Gallery of North Korean art in the 798 Art District of Beijing, run under the name Mansudae Art Museum or Mansudae Art Gallery. The studio has had a space in 798 since 2009 and sells socialist-realist paintings, statues, posters, stamps and postcards (Wikipedia).
- Run by Ji Zhengtai, a Chinese-North Korean businessman who calls it Mansudae's official studio abroad. He says North Korea has no control over it and that he owns more than 1,000 works (CBS News, 2018). Reuters calls him head of the Mansudae Art Museum (2017).
- The Mansudae Art Studio of Pyongyang was founded on 17 November 1959 (Wikipedia). A Biennale official described the Beijing museum as the studio's own (Yonhap, 2018).
- Koryo Tours' guide, published 7 February 2019, says the North Korean art display in 798 is closed because of sanctions. No reporting found of its opening days, admission or accessibility, or of whether it has reopened.

FUNDING
Mansudae Art Gallery, 798: privately run sales outlet for North Korean studio art; whether money goes to North Korea is unconfirmed
- Ji Zhengtai runs the gallery. He mostly dodged questions about whether money goes back to North Korea (CBS News, 2018).
- The gallery says it is the studio's official overseas gallery and that sanctions do not apply to it or affect its business (Reuters, via Taipei Times, 2017).
- Mansudae Art Studio is a North Korean state art institution (Yonhap, via Korea JoongAng Daily, 2018). Reuters reports that dealers' mark-ups on North Korean paintings reach four or five times the purchase price, and that Dandong galleries sold some for up to $100,000.
- For the 2018 Gwangju Biennale, a Biennale official said works were selected from places like Mansudae Art Studio's Beijing art museum (Yonhap). The Korea Times reports that the works came from a collection held by the studio's Beijing branch and the US-based Yedo Arts Foundation.
- No reporting found of accounts, a funder list or a board for the gallery.

Red flags: sanctions on the studio whose work it sells
- 5 August 2017: the UN Security Council listed "Mansudae Overseas Project Group of Companies", alias Mansudae Art Studio, under the DPRK programme (UN consolidated list, as mirrored by Lursoft). Reuters reported an asset freeze and travel ban on the studio (Taipei Times, 2017).
- EU: Commission Implementing Regulation 2017/1457 of 10 August 2017 lists the same company and alias, designation date 5 August 2017 (legislation.gov.uk).
- US: on 2 December 2016 the Treasury (OFAC) designated Mansudae Overseas Project Group of Companies under Executive Order 13722 (US Treasury press release).
- No reporting found of a designation naming the Beijing gallery or Ji Zhengtai. He says sanctions do not apply to the gallery; Reuters says what the measures mean for existing Mansudae art "has yet to become clear". OpenSanctions says the UN Panel of Experts recommended covering all Mansudae operations and subsidiary branches worldwide, a recommendation, not a list entry.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Beijing gallery found in art media. Two pieces cover the studio's work or its Western trade.
1. Hyperallergic: Hayoung Chung, "Curators Strive to Transcend National Borders at the Gwangju Biennale" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/imagined-borders-gwangju-biennale-2018
- Covers the 22 North Korean works at the Biennale; wall labels and catalogue give only titles, artists' names and dates, leaving viewers little chance to understand them.
- Says the Biennale Foundation has not explained sufficiently how it sourced the works and that Korean media questioned the authenticity and provenance of some pieces.
- Names Mansudae Art Studio; does not mention the 798 gallery or sanctions.
2. Hyperallergic: Zachary Small, "The Italian Gallery Brokering an International Market for North Korean Art" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/442425/pier-luigi-cecioni-mansudae-studio/
- Says that, according to the UN, profits Mansudae earns from overseas art projects go to the North Korean government, and quotes dealer Pier Luigi Cecioni: money from his gallery "goes back into running the studio".
- Not criticism: it records Cecioni's own statements without independent checks. Does not mention the Beijing gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery presents itself as the outlet of a studio the UN, EU and US have sanctioned. Its director says sanctions do not apply and did not say where the proceeds go. The weak points are the unconfirmed ownership and money flows and the closure reported in 2019. Worth watching: whether it has reopened and whether any sanctions authority names it.
