LUMA ARLES: Arles, France
ID: luma-arles
Website: https://www.luma.org/arles

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre in the Parc des Ateliers, a former railway repair yard (Architectural Record) at 35 avenue Victor Hugo, Arles; the site covers 7 hectares and includes 19th-century buildings, a Frank Gehry tower and a park by Bas Smets (LUMA Arles).
- Created by the LUMA Foundation, headed by Maja Hoffmann (Wikipedia); the foundation was founded in Zurich in 2004 and LUMA Arles followed in 2013 (Wikipedia, Maja Hoffmann).
- The Gehry tower opened in summer 2021 (Wikipedia, Maja Hoffmann); Hoffmann first approached Gehry in 2008 (Architectural Record, Andrew Ayers, 29 June 2021).
- The 10-storey tower has galleries, a café, an auditorium, seminar rooms and a viewing terrace, with a podium of exhibition galleries and a library; its cladding combines about 11,000 stainless steel panels with concrete panels (Architectural Record).
- Other architects: Moatti Rivière converted the Grande Halle in 2007 and Selldorf Architects converted the remaining workshop buildings (Architectural Record).
- Artworks on site include a skate park by Koo Jeong A, a ceramic mural by Etel Adnan and slides by Carsten Höller (Architectural Record); several permanent works, including two slides and a skatepark, are free to visit (LUMA Arles).
- Open Wednesday to Monday, 10:00 to 18:00; a one-day ticket covers all exhibitions, and the site has an accessibility page for visitors with disabilities (LUMA Arles).

FUNDING
LUMA Arles: privately funded: the LUMA Foundation of Maja Hoffmann, a Roche heiress
- Architectural Record cites a project of $175 million for the LUMA Foundation and names Hoffmann, a Swiss pharmaceutical heiress, as the client who commissioned the tower (Andrew Ayers, 29 June 2021).
- Wikipedia states that Artnet reported the total cost "is understood to be 150 million euros" and that Hoffmann declined to comment on the figure.
- Wikipedia states that Hoffmann is the daughter of the pharmaceutical heir Luc Hoffmann and part of the shareholder pool of Roche descendants that controls Hoffmann-La Roche.
- Hoffmann owns two hotels (The Art Newspaper, Linda Yablonsky, 16 August 2018).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one piece cited is a defence of LUMA Arles against criticism of its scale, its design and Hoffmann's influence.
1. The Art Newspaper: Linda Yablonsky, "What's wrong with creating an art mega-complex in Arles?" (16 August 2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/08/16/whats-wrong-with-creating-an-art-mega-complex-in-arles
- A defence of LUMA Arles against its critics: Yablonsky rejects the complaint that the complex is not "authentically Arlesian" and calls an admission fee a more legitimate complaint.
- Records that critics, including Financial Times columnist John Gapper, say the complex is not Arlesian enough, and that opponents say Hoffmann is seeking a "grab for power that shouldn't be hers alone".
- Says LUMA collaborates with the Rencontres d'Arles photography festival while regarding it as a competitor, and that some describe Gehry's tower as "ugly".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind LUMA Arles is private: the LUMA Foundation of Maja Hoffmann, a Roche heiress. Architectural Record cites $175 million for the project, and Wikipedia cites Artnet at 150 million euros for the total cost.
