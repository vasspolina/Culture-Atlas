ART GALLERY OF NOVA SCOTIA: Halifax, Canada
ID: art-gallery-of-nova-scotia
Website: https://agns.ca (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Provincial art museum at 1723 Hollis Street in downtown Halifax, which describes itself as the largest art museum in Atlantic Canada and an agency of the Province of Nova Scotia (AGNS website).
- Founded in 1908 as the Nova Scotia Museum of Fine Arts to keep the Crown's 200-piece art collection; renamed in 1975 (AGNS website).
- It opened its downtown home in 1988, the former Dominion Building of 1867 by David Stirling and William Hay, and added two floors of the neighbouring Provincial Building in 1998, with room for the Maud Lewis House and Gallery (AGNS website; Wikipedia).
- The permanent collection holds over 19,000 works (AGNS website); Wikipedia states that the gallery held 55 works by Maud Lewis in June 2017 and acquired 2,070 images by Annie Leibovitz in June 2013.
- Sarah Moore Fillmore is chief executive and Grant Machum chairs the board (2025-26 annual report).
- The main gallery closed from February 2024 and reopened in June 2024 with a new 600,000 dollar sprinkler system; a 1.9 million dollar roof was due that year (CBC News, 10 June 2024).
- Visiting: closed Mondays; open 10am to 5pm Tuesday to Sunday and until 9pm on Thursdays. Admission is 15 dollars, 13 dollars for seniors and youth 7 to 17, and free for Nova Scotia residents under 25, children under 7 and Indigenous Peoples (AGNS website).

FUNDING
Art Gallery of Nova Scotia: mainly public: Province of Nova Scotia and federal grants, with bank and family foundation sponsors
- Status: a provincial agency reporting through the Ministry of Communities, Culture, Tourism and Heritage; the Province lets it use its premises at a nominal cost, and works it acquires become provincial property (audited financial statements, 2024-25).
- Revenue in 2024-25 was 5.41 million dollars: provincial grants 2.75 million, federal grants 435,800, endowment income 443,496, donations 412,214, admissions 212,518 and sponsorships 147,917 (financial statements, schedule 1).
- The gallery states that it raises more than 55% of its operating funds from donors, private partners, admissions and memberships (Reports and Plans page).
- Partners in 2025-26 include BMO Financial Group, Scotiabank, TD Bank Group, IG Wealth Management, Cabot Cape Breton, Benjamin Bridge, the Azrieli Foundation, the D.R. Sobey Foundation and The Sobey Foundation (2025-26 annual report).
- BMO pays for Free Access Thursday Nights, which it says let more than 50,000 visitors in free since 2017 (2025-26 annual report).
- TD Bank Group pays for a two-year TD Curatorial Fellowship begun in 2024 and sponsored the fellow's exhibitions (2025-26 annual report).

Red flags: TD Bank, sponsor of the curatorial fellowship, pleaded guilty to money laundering in 2024
- On 10 October 2024 TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering, paying 1.8 billion dollars to the Department of Justice, 1.3 billion to FinCEN and a Federal Reserve fine of over 124 million (NBC News, Dan Mangan of CNBC). The bank admitted failing to monitor 18.3 trillion dollars in customer activity over six years.
- TD is listed under "Current Supporters" for the TD Assistant Curatorial Fellowship on the gallery's business-support page (checked 2026-11-20).

Red flags: 2022 shelving of the waterfront building over rising costs
- The province put the planned waterfront gallery on hold in 2022; it was expected to cost about 137 million dollars when announced in 2019, and the premier's office later put the cost at more than 160 million (CBC News, 28 June 2023).
- The province paid the gallery a one-time 162,193 dollars to cover the costs of a 30 million dollar capital campaign that never launched; the gallery held signed pledges of 10 million dollars (CBC News, 28 June 2023).

Red flags: 2024 protests over closing the Yarmouth branch
- On 3 May 2024 the gallery announced it would not reopen its western branch in Yarmouth, opened in 2006, saying it lost more than 100,000 dollars a year (CBC News, Blair Rhodes, 3 May 2024).
- The decision prompted two protests in Yarmouth and a petition to reopen the branch (CBC News, 10 June 2024).
- Yarmouth's mayor Pam Mood and the Friends of the AGNS Western Branch Society said they had no warning (CBC News, Jean Laroche, 11 June 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage is thin; the one piece concerns the tax ruling on a donated collection, not the gallery's funders.
1. ArtsJournal: ArtsJournal2, "Once Again, A Canadian Panel Finds That An Annie Leibovitz Collection Is Not Fully 'Significant'" (2017)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=127358
- A Canadian panel again found the Leibovitz collection donated to the gallery in 2013 not fully significant, with major tax implications for the donor.
- The gallery said it disagreed. A short news item, not a critique of the gallery.

BOTTOM LINE
A provincial agency living mainly on provincial and federal grants. The weak points are TD Bank's sponsorship of its curatorial fellowship, the shelved waterfront building and the unconsulted Yarmouth closure.
