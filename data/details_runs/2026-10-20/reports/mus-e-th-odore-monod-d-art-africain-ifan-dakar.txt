MUSÉE THÉODORE MONOD D'ART AFRICAIN (IFAN): Dakar, Senegal
ID: mus-e-th-odore-monod-d-art-africain-ifan-dakar
Website: https://musee-monod.sn (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- University art museum at Place Soweto, near the National Assembly of Senegal (Wikipedia).
- Established in 1938 as the art museum of the Institut Fondamental d'Afrique Noire (IFAN), which was founded in 1936; Léopold Senghor promoted it (Wikipedia). IFAN moved to Cheikh Anta Diop University in 1960 and the building became a museum.
- Earlier names: IFAN/CAD African Art Museum, then IFAN Museum of African Arts. Renamed in December 2007 for the naturalist Théodore Monod, a former IFAN director (Wikipedia).
- The historic building dates from 1931 to 1932; a modern building with a gallery, a conference room and multipurpose spaces was added in 1991 to 1992. The gallery covers 620 m² (au-senegal.com). No reporting found of the architect.
- Holds nearly 10,000 art objects from more than twenty African countries, some from the 19th century (au-senegal.com). Also runs a "Création / Patrimoine" programme that pairs artists with the collection (museum site, Seneweb).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 17:00 (museum site); closed Mondays and public holidays. Listed admission: 5,000 FCFA for non-resident adults, 2,000 FCFA for resident adults, 1,000 FCFA for children (au-senegal.com, undated). No reporting found of accessibility information.

FUNDING
Musée Théodore Monod: university-run public museum with corporate, foreign-state and foundation partners, none of whom publish amounts
- The museum's partners page names 11 partners: the Senegalese arts directorate, Fondation SOCOCIM, the French embassy in Dakar, Grand Palais RMN, the Goethe-Institut, the Gerda Henkel Foundation, the Senegalese heritage directorate, Fondation TOTAL Sénégal, the Institut français, the Institut français du Sénégal and the Volkswagen Foundation. The page gives no years, amounts or projects and carries no date (stated by the museum).
- Fondation SOCOCIM: in 2010, before the World Festival of Black Arts, the museum was renovated with more than one million euros from the foundation (allAfrica, 2010). Curator El Hadji Malick Ndiaye said in March 2023 that it was among "quelques partenariats avec le privé national" and had renovated the museum in 2010 (Seneweb). Sococim has been 99.91 percent owned by the French group Vicat since 1999, and supplies about 70 percent of Senegal's cement (IDE-JETRO profile).
- le19M, the craftsmanship hub of Chanel (FashionUnited): the curator said in 2023 that le19M renovated one of the museum's two buildings, including wiring, lighting, flooring and security, and paid for research and the garden (Seneweb). It opened an off-site gallery at the museum with the exhibition "Sur le fil" from 12 January to 31 March 2023, and returned for a programme from 14 June to 14 July 2024 (Cécile Fakhoury Gallery).
- Gerda Henkel Foundation: funds the digitisation of part of the collection. The French embassy and Institut français fund staff training through the FSPI fund, run by RMN Grand Palais (Seneweb, 2023).
- Fondation TOTAL Sénégal is the eighth name on the same partners page, which carries no date. No page opened gives a year, project or amount for it, or states which company's foundation it is. Ndiaye's March 2023 interview, which named Sococim, le19M, Gerda Henkel and the French partners, does not mention Total (Seneweb). No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at this partner.
- No reporting found of annual accounts for the museum; it sits inside IFAN and posts no donor list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is public and university-run, and the named partners are foundations, embassies and one cement maker's foundation. The weak point is that no partner has a published amount, including Fondation TOTAL Sénégal, which appears only on an undated partners page. Worth watching: whether the museum publishes accounts or dates its partners.
