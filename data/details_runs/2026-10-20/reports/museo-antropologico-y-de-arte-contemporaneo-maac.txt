MUSEO ANTROPOLÓGICO Y DE ARTE CONTEMPORÁNEO (MAAC): Guayaquil, Ecuador
ID: museo-antropologico-y-de-arte-contemporaneo-maac
Website: no official MAAC site found (checked 2026-10-25; searches returned only press, Wikipedia and a petition page)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of Ecuadorian archaeology and modern and contemporary art on the Malecón Simón Bolívar in central Guayaquil, beside the Las Peñas neighbourhood and facing the Guayas River (Primicias, 2024). Wikipedia says it sits beside the Malecón 2000 riverwalk.
- Opened on 30 July 2003 according to Wikipedia; Primicias (2024) gives 2004 and a construction cost of about USD 9 million. The two sources disagree.
- The pages read do not say who founded it. A 2012 loan-for-use agreement was signed when the Banco Central del Ecuador ran the museum (Primicias, 2024).
- Not named for a person.
- Building: a petition cited by Primicias (2024) gives about 10,000 m2 over three floors. The city's figure of 13,526.73 m2 in the fee dispute is for exterior space (Expreso, 2025). No architect found.
- What is there: about 50,000 archaeological pieces and roughly 3,400 artworks (Primicias, 2024); a children's "Ludo" library was being built inside in 2024 (KCH Comunicación, citing the government communications office).
- Visiting: opening hours and admission were not found in any source read. 530,036 visits were counted in 2022 up to mid-June, about half of the visits to Ecuador's 16 national museums (Primicias, 2022).

FUNDING
MAAC: mainly public: Ecuador's Ministry of Culture and Heritage
- The Ministry of Culture and Heritage runs the museum (Primicias, 2024). In August 2025 reporting the ministry was to be absorbed by the Ministry of Education (Expreso).
- Budget: a little over USD 2.5 million a year (Primicias, 5 June 2024).
- The museum pays the Fundación Malecón 2000, a municipal entity, monthly fees of USD 37,872 plus VAT, nearly USD 500,000 a year and about 19 per cent of the budget (Primicias, 5 June 2024). The city says the foundation receives USD 37,872.80 a month for 13,526.73 m2 of exterior space and that payments have been late since April 2025; the minister acknowledged a delay (Expreso, 22 August 2025).
- Repairs: USD 700,000 estimated in 2022 for the facade, floors and climate control (Primicias, 2022); USD 1.2 million in 2017 works from an Inter-American Development Bank loan (Primicias, 2024); more than USD 451,000 invested by October 2024, including about USD 125,000 for research and the cultural agenda, according to the culture minister (KCH Comunicación, 8 October 2024, from the government communications office). The ministry planned USD 442,000 for waterproofing and roof repairs in 2024 (Primicias, 2024).
- Mayor Aquiles Álvarez said on X that the Fundación Malecón 2000 will not reform the agreement "in any way" (our translation of "bajo ningún concepto reformará el convenio, en ningún sentido", Primicias, 2024).
- No reporting found of private sponsors or donors.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Criticism of the museum's care comes from local press and a petition.
1. Primicias (not art media): Redacción Primicias, "S.O.S, un grito desesperado para salvar el Museo Antropológico en el Malecón" (2024)
URL: https://primicias.ec/noticias/guayaquil/grito-desesperado-salvar-museo-antropologico-malecon
- Reports a Change.org petition claiming the building is in critical condition because of the construction of the Aerovía cable car and years without maintenance, endangering more than 60,000 pre-Hispanic items.
- Unverified: the Change.org petition says the Fundación Malecón 2000 "has done absolutely nothing" (our translation) and that fees over nearly 20 years total almost USD 10 million; Primicias reports these claims without verifying them.
2. Primicias (not art media): Alexander García, "Ministra de Cultura sobre crisis del MAAC: 'El Alcalde no nos atiende, pido que nos escuche'" (5 June 2024)
URL: https://primicias.ec/noticias/guayaquil/crisis-maac-altas-alicuotas-municipio
- Minister Romina Muñoz says the fees hurt maintenance and that the Aerovía damaged the building; the mayor says the municipality would take over the museum if the ministry ended the agreement.
- Not criticism of the programme; a dispute between two levels of government.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Ecuadorian state, and no flagged funder, private sponsor or donor appears in the sources read. The weak points are the dispute between the ministry and the city over fees and upkeep, damage to the building that the ministry and a petition attribute to the Aerovía works, and the ministry's planned absorption into Education. Worth watching: whether the fee agreement is changed and how the repairs are paid.
