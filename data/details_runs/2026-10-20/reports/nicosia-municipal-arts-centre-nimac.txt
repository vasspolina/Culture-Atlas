NICOSIA MUNICIPAL ARTS CENTRE (NiMAC): Nicosia, Cyprus
ID: nicosia-municipal-arts-centre-nimac
Website: https://nimac.org.cy/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at 19 Palias Ilektrikis in the historic centre of Nicosia, in the renovated Old Powerhouse (NiMAC website).
- Inaugurated on 14 January 1994. It operates under a cooperation agreement between the Nicosia Municipality and the Pierides Foundation (Nicosia Municipality website).
- Building: the first power generating station in Nicosia, granted to the municipality by the Electricity Authority. It stood deserted for almost 20 years before restoration, and the centre received the Europa Nostra Award for it in 1994 (Nicosia Municipality website).
- On site: more than 70 exhibitions of contemporary art to date, a Children's Educational Centre opened in 2008, the D.Z. Pierides History of Art Library, the Cyprus Visual Arts Archive, a shop and the "Old Power House" restaurant (Nicosia Municipality website).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Saturday 10:00 to 20:00 and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00; closed Monday and Tuesday (NiMAC website). No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility details; parallel programme events for Fluid Persistence are free.

FUNDING
Nicosia Municipal Arts Centre (NiMAC): mainly public and foundation-backed: Nicosia Municipality and the Pierides Foundation, with ministry and foundation support per exhibition
- The Nicosia Municipality and the Pierides Foundation run the centre under their cooperation agreement (Nicosia Municipality website). No reporting found of the budget split.
- The exhibition Fluid Persistence (12 December 2025 to 31 May 2026) credits the Deputy Ministry of Culture's Department of Contemporary Culture, the A.G. Leventis Foundation and European University Cyprus (NiMAC website).
- For A Slight Indisposition (June to July 2026) no funder is named. The Deputy Ministry said it had no involvement in the show's organisation, funding or curation; the work in dispute was loaned from the State Collection (Politis, 29 June 2026).
- A NiMAC annual report for 2024 and 2025 is listed on its site (not read).

Red flags: row over Emin Cizenel in A Slight Indisposition, June 2026
- The group show, curated by Esra Plumer Bardak, includes Emin Cizenel's two-part work "A Lost Moment", which belongs to the Republic of Cyprus's State Collection. Cizenel designed the flag of the north; Greek Cypriots revile it as a symbol of the occupation (Cyprus Mail, 1 August 2026).
- Deputy Nicosia mayor Chrysanthos Fakas said: "If this information is correct, the exhibition should be immediately cancelled." Mayor Charalambos Prountzos said the municipality did not organise the event and asked NiMAC to examine the issue (Cyprus Mail, Katy Turner, 28 June 2026).
- A small protest outside the venue carried a banner reading "Stop Normalizing the Occupation, Art Washing" (SigmaLive, 28 June 2026).
- NiMAC said it has "complete artistic freedom", that the municipal council does not review its programme, and that its board would meet in extraordinary session (Cyprus Mail, James Morphakis, 29 June 2026; Politis).
- The Deputy Ministry of Culture said including a work in the State Collection "does not constitute political endorsement" (Politis).
- Outcome: the show ran to its end in late July with no withdrawal of the work reported (Cyprus Mail, Agnieszka Rakoczy, 1 August 2026).
- Unverified: journalist Giorgos Tattis alleges a Turkish Cypriot man accompanying the curator threw his phone down and stopped him filming; this is his claim, reported by SigmaLive, and no police complaint is reported.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
NiMAC's money is public and foundation money: a municipality and the Pierides Foundation run it, and exhibitions are backed by the Deputy Ministry of Culture and Cypriot foundations. No funder of concern was found. The weak points are that no budget or sponsor list is published and that in the June 2026 Cizenel row the centre, which says its programme is independent, was publicly opposed by the municipality's deputy mayor. Watch the outcome of the board meeting and the annual report.
