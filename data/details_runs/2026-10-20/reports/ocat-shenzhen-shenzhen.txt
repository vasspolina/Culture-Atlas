OCAT SHENZHEN: Shenzhen, China
ID: ocat-shenzhen-shenzhen
Website: https://www.octloft.cn/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads, but shows only the heading "华侨城创意文化园" (OCT Creative Culture Park) and no OCAT content)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the OCT-LOFT creative park, F2 Building, Enping Road, Nanshan District, Shenzhen; headquarters of the OCAT museums group (e-flux).
- Founded in January 2005 with sponsorship from OCT Group, a central state-owned enterprise; Huang Zhuan (1958 to 2016) led its academic work, and it registered as an independent non-profit art museum in April 2012 (TrueArt; OCAT's 18 March 2024 notice on Sohu).
- Building: No reporting found of an architect or site history.
- On site: exhibitions and the annual series OCAT Performs and OCAT Screens (e-flux).
- Branches: OCAT Shanghai opened on 29 September 2012 and announced a suspension of operations on 12 March 2023 (Artforum China); TrueArt describes a Beijing research centre (2015) and a Xi'an branch (2013).
- Visiting: e-flux lists Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:30; a Sohu notice (3 April 2025) lists a B10 exhibition open daily 10:00 to 17:30. No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
OCAT Shenzhen: state-owned corporate parent: OCT Group
- OCT Group, a central state-owned enterprise, has sponsored OCAT since its 2005 founding (TrueArt; OCAT's 18 March 2024 notice on Sohu).
- Unverified: a Southern Metropolis Daily article (oeeee.com, dated 21 March 2023 by its URL), seen only as a search extract in translation, reports that OCT Group funded OCAT for 18 years and that after OCT set up a new culture group in December 2022 it would cut funding sharply, leaving OCAT to earn its own income, with staff halved. The page returned HTTP 493 when opened.
- The Art Newspaper (Lisa Movius, 27 September 2023) says property-backed Chinese private museums often hold licences tied to a parent company that guarantees funding of around $275,000 a year. It does not mention OCT Group.
- No reporting found of other donors, annual reports, accounts or a board list. The official site was not readable.

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, OCAT Shanghai 2021
- In June 2021 a Weibo post about Song Ta's video "Uglier and Uglier" (2013), which shows 5,000 college women in secretly shot footage, went viral; the topic reached over a hundred million views. OCAT Shanghai said it had decided to withdraw the work and close the museum "for a period of time" (The Art Newspaper, 21 June 2021).
- Who acted: The Art Newspaper says the local Cultural Bureau instructed the museum to remove the work permanently and met nearby art spaces, telling them to be "particularly careful" about public sensitivities in the Communist Party's centenary year. The article does not say the Bureau ordered the closure; the closure was announced by OCAT Shanghai, and a sign said "closed temporarily" from 18 June.
- Connection: the article does not name OCT Group. OCAT is sponsored by OCT Group, a central state-owned enterprise (TrueArt; OCAT's 2024 notice), so a state body acted against a branch of an institution that a state-owned company funds.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of OCAT Shenzhen itself. The press coverage concerns the Shanghai branch's 2021 closure and the group's contraction after 2022.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "OCAT Shanghai closes after public outcry over Uglier and Uglier video that ranks women's attractiveness" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/06/21/ocat-shanghai-closes-after-public-outcry-over-uglier-and-uglier-video-that-ranks-womens-attractiveness
- Weibo commenters called the museum's response "belated" and said it should "hold legal responsibility" for the filmed women's privacy. OCAT said the process of making the work "suggests legal infringement."
- Curator Bao Dong, who co-organised the 2013 exhibition at UCCA Beijing, defended Song Ta and blamed the outcry on fake accounts and a US government conspiracy.
- Names OCAT Shanghai; does not name OCAT Shenzhen or OCT Group.
2. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "What's behind China's spate of museum closures and downsizings" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/09/27/whats-behind-chinas-spate-of-museum-closures-and-downsizings
- Says the OCAT chain in Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an and Shenzhen "abruptly closed in 2022." OCAT Shenzhen's own notice of 18 March 2024 (a two-day closure for renovation, 19 to 20 March) and a Sohu notice of 3 April 2025 (an exhibition open daily) show the Shenzhen venue open, so the two accounts conflict.
- An anonymous former director of private museums says "Private museums are never independent entities."
- Does not mention OCT Group or its funding decisions.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind OCAT Shenzhen is a central state-owned enterprise, with no flagged-industry funder found. The weak points are heavy dependence on that parent, which a Southern Metropolis Daily search extract says cut support sharply after December 2022 (unconfirmed), and the Cultural Bureau's 2021 instruction to remove the Shanghai branch's work. Worth watching how OCAT earns its own income.
