FÁBRICA DE ARTE CUBANO: Havana, Cuba
ID: fabrica-de-arte-cubano
Website: https://fabricadeartecubano.com/ (checked 2026-10-20; its footer carries gambling and spam links; the older address fac.cu did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Interdisciplinary cultural centre and night venue at Calle 26 and 11 in Vedado, Havana, mixing music, visual art, film, dance, design, fashion, theatre and food (stated by the space).
- Founded by the musician X Alfonso; after a run at the Pabexpo fairgrounds from mid-2010 it opened at its current site on 13 February 2014 with a concert by Silvio Rodríguez (CubaPLUS, 2026).
- Housed in the former El Cocinero cooking-oil factory, built in 1910 and recognisable by its brick chimney (CubaPLUS; Wikipedia; OnCuba).
- Galleries, concert and theatre spaces, film screenings, a bar, and free workshops for children and young people; FAC says it programmes more than 288 concerts a year and gives over 300 direct jobs (OnCuba, 2014; FAC communiqué via Periódico Cubano, 2026).
- Visiting: open Fridays and Saturdays, 8 pm to 2 am (stated by the space). In 2014 entry cost 50 Cuban pesos, with a consumption card paid on exit (OnCuba). Closed temporarily in June 2026 except for announced special events (Prensa Latina). No reporting found on accessibility.

FUNDING
Fábrica de Arte Cubano: self-funded: door and bar income, members and private partners, in a state-owned building
- In 2014 the Cuban Music Institute and the Ministry of Culture covered some costs until the project became self-financing; door income went to X Alfonso's business, with a share kept for the building's upkeep (OnCuba, 2014).
- In its 1 January 2026 communiqué FAC said it receives no government money; its finances depend on members, private partners, its director and the businesses that operate inside it (stated by the space, via Periódico Cubano).
- FAC says it has no premises of its own: a state cultural institution oversees the state-owned building and collects the box office (stated by the space). Spanish Wikipedia says a group of artists and musicians bought the factory in 2008; the two accounts do not agree.
- No reporting found of named private partners, accounts or amounts.

Red flags: state censorship of a Celia Cruz tribute
- The Centro Nacional de Música Popular, attached to the Ministry of Culture, cancelled Teatro El Público's Celia Cruz centenary tribute at FAC, set for 19 October 2025, with no reason given (Voz, an outlet openly critical of the Cuban government).
- Actor Daniel Triana called the show "censurado"; historian Rosa Marquetti said participants reported "órdenes inapelables y amenazas" (Voz).
- FAC said in January 2026 that the event "fue censurado a última hora" by the authorities (Periódico Cubano).

Red flags: official campaign over an admission dispute
- On 26 December 2025 FAC staff refused entry to Alejandro Bridón and two friends under the "right of admission"; he called it racial discrimination (Periódico Cubano).
- On 31 December 2025 the Instituto Cubano de la Música called the refusal "absurda" and announced disciplinary sanctions against the worker (Periódico Cubano).
- X Alfonso said security had mistaken the man for someone linked to thefts, apologised to him, and called the ministry "una espina en el zapato" since July 2018; FAC called the accusations a "campaña de descrédito" (Periódico Cubano).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism. Cuban outlets have criticised FAC for its prices, its pull toward tourists and its role as a business; none questions where its money comes from.
1. Havana Times: Daisy Valera, "Havana's 'Cuban Art Factory': Poetic Consumption for the New Middle Class" (2014)
URL: https://havanatimes.org/?p=104847
- Argues FAC offers leisure that is "exclusive" and built for an emerging middle class, not a broad public.
- Notes the entry fee and the 30 CUC charge for a lost card: "The management knows nothing of subsidies or freebies."
2. OnCuba: Carlos Ávila Villamar, "Fábrica tomada" (2017)
URL: https://oncubanews.com/cultura/artes-visuales/fabrica-tomada
- Argues FAC drifted into a large commercial venture filled by tourists as Havana residents stopped going.
- Says Havana's public shares the blame and that early critics who called it a business were unfair.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Prensa Latina blamed the US embargo, "impuesto a Cuba por más de seis décadas", for the June 2026 temporary closure; the report does not mention energy or fuel.
- FAC itself said only that its difficulties "son parte de un contexto más amplio" (Prensa Latina).

BOTTOM LINE
FAC's money is its own takings and private partners, with early state help in 2014, but it works in a state building whose box office a state institution collects. The weak point is state control: the Ministry of Culture's music centre cancelled a show there in October 2025 and the Music Institute moved against its staff in December 2025. Worth watching: whether it reopens after the June 2026 closure.
