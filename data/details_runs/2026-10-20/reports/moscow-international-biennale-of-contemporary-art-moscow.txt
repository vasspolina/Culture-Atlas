MOSCOW INTERNATIONAL BIENNALE OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Moscow, Russia
ID: moscow-international-biennale-of-contemporary-art-moscow

OVERVIEW
- Recurring contemporary art exhibition in Moscow, held in odd-numbered years since 2005; no biennale was held in 2021 (Meduza).
- The organiser is the Moscow Biennale of Contemporary Art Foundation, headed by Yulia Muzykantskaya, described by The Art Newspaper as its president.
- The eighth edition (2019) was held at an annex of the State Tretyakov Gallery and showed 34 artists from 11 countries (The Art Newspaper).
- The ninth edition was to open at the New Tretyakov on 7 November 2022. The Ministry of Culture announced its cancellation on 4 November 2022 (Meduza, The Russian Reader).

FUNDING
Moscow International Biennale of Contemporary Art: state grants and corporate partners: Ministry of Culture, Presidential Fund of Cultural Initiatives, Moscow city and sponsors
- 2022 edition: a Ministry of Culture subsidy of 8.5 million rubles and 10 million rubles from the Presidential Fund of Cultural Initiatives (RTVI, 8 November 2022).
- 2022 partners: Promsvyazbank and Russian Railways, whose contribution amounts are undisclosed under an agreement (RTVI). The Leonard Blavatnik Foundation stopped supporting projects in Russia after the war began (RTVI).
- 2013 edition: a budget of just over 3 million dollars, 55 percent from the Ministry of Culture and about 10 percent from the Moscow city authorities (Kommersant, 7 October 2013, reprinting Steven Lee Myers of the International Herald Tribune).

Red flags: Ministry of Culture cancelled the 2022 edition
- On 4 November 2022, three days before opening, the Ministry of Culture announced the cancellation of the ninth edition, citing an "absolute mismatch" between the level of several exhibits and the status of the venue, a federal museum. It also said the foundation had missed the installation deadlines and not provided required paperwork (Meduza; The Russian Reader).
- The ministry was a funder (8.5 million ruble subsidy; 10 million rubles from the Presidential Fund of Cultural Initiatives), and the Tretyakov Gallery reports directly to it (RTVI; Art Focus Now, Wagner).
- Culture Minister Olga Lyubimova said the content was controversial and the show could go ahead, but not at the Tretyakov (RTVI).
- Artist Sergei Bugaev says the gallery's leadership objected to a painted tank in his installation about demolished monuments (Meduza).

Red flags: Artists' open letter and boycott call in 2019
- On 6 June 2019 the Biennial Foundation published an open letter from artists of the 2017 edition, addressed to a "Partner of the 2019 Moscow Biennale" and aimed at the director, Muzykantskaya. It accused her of leaving production and labour fees unpaid and of abuse towards artists and staff, and urged the partner to withdraw from the 2019 edition.
- The Art Newspaper (George Nelson, 31 October 2019) counted 16 artists and two technicians among the signatories. A technical supplier, George Arzamasov, sued her for 23,500 dollars; she countersued for 50,000 dollars.
- Muzykantskaya said in a Facebook post that all debts were paid well before the letter (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Sanctioned state bank among 2022 partners
- RTVI (8 November 2022) names Promsvyazbank (PSB) as a partner of the 2022 edition.
- The US Treasury announced sanctions on PSB on 22 February 2022: "All assets under U.S. jurisdiction will be immediately frozen." It says the Russian government took control of the bank in 2018 to finance the defence industry. Wikipedia states that the United Kingdom sanctioned the bank the same day.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is mostly news of the 2019 payment dispute and the 2022 cancellation. The 2019 review criticises the show's organisation and title.
1. The Art Newspaper: George Nelson, "Moscow Biennale opening marred by 2017 controversy" (31 October 2019)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/moscow-biennale-opening-marred-by-2017-controversy
- Reports the open letter, the allegations against Muzykantskaya, and the lawsuit and countersuit. She declined to comment to the paper.
2. Art Focus Now: Simon Hewitt, "A scandalous Moscow Biennale" (1 November 2019)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/a-scandalous-moscow-biennale
- Criticises the 2019 edition's organisation and "monumentally uninformative" website, and calls its title, Orienteering & Positioning, meaningless.
- Finds the Russian artists stronger than the foreign works.
3. Art Focus Now: Ekaterina Wagner, "Closing time in Moscow's museums" (2022)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/closing-time-in-moscows-museums/
- Reports that the Ministry of Culture ordered the closure on the eve of opening. The Tretyakov's statement cited exhibit quality and a conflict with the show's 6+ age restriction.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the biennale is mainly state: a Ministry of Culture subsidy and a Presidential Fund grant in 2022, and 55 percent from the Ministry in 2013. Documented problems are the Ministry's cancellation of the 2022 edition, the 2019 unpaid-fees dispute with an open letter urging a partner to withdraw, and Promsvyazbank, sanctioned on 22 February 2022, among the 2022 partners.
