AFRICAN ARTISTS' FOUNDATION: Lagos, Nigeria
ID: african-artists-foundation-lagos
Website: https://africanartists.org (checked 2026-10-20; footer reads 2024 and the newest listed shows date from late 2024)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art organisation at 3B Isiola Oyekan Close, off Adeleke Adedoyin Street, Victoria Island, Lagos.
- Founded in 2007 by Azu Nwagbogu (Wikipedia).
- Runs the LagosPhoto festival, founded in 2009, and ran the National Art Competition, founded in 2008; the site lists a Magnum Photos collaboration, "Beyond the Silence", and "Dig Where You Stand" among past shows.
- In October 2020 AAF was evicted from the building it rented from the Umanah family, after Keystone Bank claimed the family's properties through a court order (ThisDay).
- The eviction halted most activity for about two years and put the 2021 LagosPhoto on hold; the board then decided to move to another African country, which ThisDay did not name.
- Open Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 18:00 and Saturday 11:00 to 18:00 (stated by the space). No admission or accessibility information is published.

FUNDING
African Artists' Foundation: mixed: private foundations, foreign cultural bodies and corporate sponsors
- Nigerian Breweries Plc and AAF jointly announced the 2016 National Art Competition; the prizes were 2 million naira for the grand winner and 1 million naira for each of two other awards (Nigerian Breweries).
- AAF's undated "Sponsors and Partners" logos include the Ford Foundation, Open Society Foundations, Mondriaan Fonds, Outset, Goethe-Institut, Alliance Française, the Spanish Embassy, Canon, National Geographic, Vontobel, Nigerian Breweries and Deutsche Bank (stated by the space; only the Goethe-Institut logo has alt text, the other names are read from image file names).
- No reporting found of amounts, years or published accounts for any of these funders, or of a board.
- No reporting found of a tobacco company among AAF's funders.

Red flags: Deutsche Bank on the sponsor list
- A Deutsche Bank logo appears under "Sponsors and Partners" on AAF's collaborators page, with no date and no alt text; the name is read from the image file name.
- No reporting found of what Deutsche Bank paid for, or of a protest aimed at AAF over it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. The only critique on record concerns LagosPhoto showing too few Nigerian artists, and Nwagbogu answered it.
1. Africa is a Country: Joe Penney, "LagosPhoto is Five Years Old" (2014)
URL: https://africasacountry.com/2014/10/lagosphoto-is-five-years-old
- Records the complaint that LagosPhoto does not show enough Nigerians and that some see AAF as elitist.
- Nwagbogu answers that few Nigerians apply and that "we will not show your work just because we have an obligation to show Nigerians."
2. ThisDay: Yinka Olatunbosun, "On Chokehold, LagosPhoto Festival Looks Elsewhere" (year not stated on the page)
URL: https://www.thisdaylive.com/index.php/2024/05/05/on-chokehold-lagosphoto-festival-looks-elsewhere
- Not criticism of AAF: reports the Keystone Bank eviction, AAF's claim of 600 million naira under an indemnity clause, and the bank's reply that the Umanah family, not the bank, caused the closure.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a mix of private foundations, European cultural agencies and a few corporate sponsors, none with published amounts. The weak points are the undated Deutsche Bank listing and the lack of any accounts or board list. The tobacco flag on the record is not supported by any source found.
