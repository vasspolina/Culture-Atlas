SLOVAK NATIONAL GALLERY: Bratislava, Slovakia
ID: slovak-national-gallery
Website: https://www.sng.sk/en (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- National art gallery with its headquarters at Rázusovo nábrežie 1, Bratislava (SNG website).
- Established by law on 29 July 1949 (Wikipedia).
- Bratislava buildings are the Esterházy Palace, reconstructed for the gallery in the 1950s, and the adjacent Water Barracks, with a modern extension added in the 1970s (Wikipedia).
- Other sites: Zvolen Castle, Strážky Manor, the Schaubmar Mill in Pezinok (open since August 2025) and the Ľudovít Fulla Gallery, which is temporarily closed (SNG website, TASR).
- Collections include Baroque and modern art (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Monday; Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00; Thursday 12:00 to 20:00. Free admission once a month, on a Sunday (SNG website). The 2024 open letter says admission was free since 2010 (letter, e-flux).
- Visitors in 2025: 116,304 across all sites, 67,338 of them in Bratislava (TASR).

FUNDING
Slovak National Gallery: state-funded: Ministry of Culture
- 2025 budget: EUR 13,229,248, later cut to EUR 9,609,327; admission revenue over EUR 330,700; year-end result a deficit of EUR 752,131 (TASR, 26 May 2026).
- The minister of culture appoints and dismisses the director: Martina Šimkovičová dismissed Alexandra Kusá on 7 August 2024 (The Art Newspaper).
- In 2024 the ministry said the gallery's Venice Biennale presentation was funded with EUR 300,000 and was wasteful (ministry, via TASR and noviny.sk).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, donors or a gallery council.

Red flags: dismissal of director Alexandra Kusá by the minister of culture (state punishment)
- On 7 August 2024 Culture Minister Martina Šimkovičová dismissed Kusá, a day after dismissing Matej Drlička, director of the Slovak National Theatre (The Art Newspaper, Richard Unwin).
- Ministry reasons, given by spokesperson Petra Bačinská: management failures in the building reconstruction, use of the gallery's premises, curatorial choices, the cost of the Venice presentation, and a conflict of interest because Kusá is a close relative of a co-owner of the original reconstruction contractor (noviny.sk, citing TASR).
- Kusá's reply: her father's architectural practice won the renovation contract in 2005, five years before she became director, and state authorities checked the arrangement and found no conflict (The Art Newspaper). She called the reasons "made-up" and the motive political (The Parliament, 2 September 2024). Unverified: the ministry's conflict-of-interest claim, which Kusá denies.
- Acting head: Anton Bittner, described by the ministry as a crisis manager (noviny.sk).

Red flags: protest
- An open letter to the Minister of Culture, dated 13 August 2024 on e-flux, said the dismissal undermined the independence of the cultural field. Signatory counts differ by outlet and date: e-flux lists signatories without a total; ArtReview counted over 230 as of 13 August; The Art Newspaper reported at least 360 arts professionals and organisations by 16 August 2024.
- Signatories include the board of ICOM Germany, Sebastian Cichocki of the Museum of Modern Art in Warsaw and Karola Kraus of mumok, Vienna (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns the minister's dismissal of the director, not the gallery's programme. It comes from open letters and news reports; no critic's review of the gallery's work was found.
1. The Art Newspaper: Richard Unwin, "International arts community calls for reinstatement of Slovak National Gallery director" (2024)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/08/16/international-arts-community-calls-for-reinstatement-of-slovak-national-gallery-director
- Reports that at least 360 international arts professionals and organisations signed a letter saying Kusá's removal "has undermined the independence" of culture in Slovakia.
- Gives Kusá's answer: the clash is "irreconcilable differences".
2. e-flux: open letter, "Open letter opposing the dismissal of the Director of the Slovak National Gallery" (2024)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/621698/open-letter-opposing-the-dismissal-of-the-director-of-the-slovak-national-gallery
- Argues the case should go through the courts and auditors, that the presumption of innocence applies, and that the ministry said a review was being prepared when none had started.
- Credits Kusá with free admission, better visitor services, digital access and Slovak shows abroad.
- Not a critical essay: a letter by signatories, first published on Change.org.
3. The Parliament Magazine (not art press): Sarah Schug, interview with Alexandra Kusá (2024)
URL: https://theparliamentmagazine.eu/news/article/slovak-government-crackdown-on-culture-qa-with-dismissed-national-gallery-director-alexandra-kus
- Kusá says the stated reasons are invented, that the government dislikes an open, transparent gallery, and that the actions are "pure destruction" rather than a coherent policy.
- These are her own claims.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is state-funded, with no private money disclosed, so funders are not the concern. The weak point is political control: the minister dismissed its director in August 2024 on grounds Kusá disputes, and the 2025 budget was cut by about EUR 3.6 million (from EUR 13.2 million to EUR 9.6 million). Watch for the review of the reconstruction contract amendments the ministry said it wants, and for the gallery's budget and leadership.
