VICTORIA MEMORIAL HALL: Kolkata, India
ID: victoria-memorial-hall
Website: https://victoriamemorial-cal.org (checked 2026-11-20; refused the automated check with a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Marble memorial and museum to Queen Victoria where the Maidan meets Queen's Way in central Kolkata, in 64 acres of gardens (Incredible India; Business Standard).
- Commissioned by the Viceroy Lord Curzon after Victoria's death in 1901 and opened on 28 December 1921 (Business Standard); the Prince of Wales laid the foundation stone on 4 January 1906 (Wikipedia).
- It was set up under the Victoria Memorial Act, 1903, and declared an institution of national importance in 1935 (CAG Report No. 10 of 2022).
- Building: white Makrana marble (Business Standard), designed by William Emerson in the Indo-Saracenic style and built by Martin & Co. of Calcutta, with gardens by Lord Redesdale and David Prain (Wikipedia).
- Collection: 33,497 objects in March 2020, of which 817, or 2.44 per cent, were on display (CAG); Incredible India lists 25 galleries, including works by Thomas and William Daniell, Indian National Army material and the Kolkata Gallery, which opened in 1992 (Wikipedia).
- Visitors: Incredible India gives 5 million a year; Live History India reports the highest footfall and biggest ticket revenue of any museum in India.

FUNDING
Victoria Memorial Hall: public: Government of India, through the Ministry of Culture, plus ticket income
- Status: under the Ministry of Culture, not the state government, and run by a board of trustees headed ex officio by the Governor of West Bengal (Business Standard, 2011); the ministry lists it among the autonomous bodies it funds (parliamentary question 1126).
- Ministry grant: Rs 33.77 crore budgeted for 2021-22 and Rs 30.29 crore in the revised estimate; Rs 30.99 crore and Rs 28.77 crore for 2022-23; Rs 31.34 crore for 2023-24 (Ministry of Culture answer to parliamentary question 1126, published by PIB in February 2025).
- Earlier grants: in 2010-11 the hall drew Rs 5.85 crore of an Rs 11 crore grant and left Rs 3.57 crore of it unspent, a ministry official said (Business Standard, 2011).
- Construction: the Rs 1.05 crore cost came entirely from donations by Indian well-wishers of the Raj, including many princes (Business Standard).
- Loans: under a 2007 agreement the hall was to pay the Rabindra Bharati Society Rs 10 lakh a year for 5,103 paintings lent in 2011, of which nine were shown (CAG).

Red flags: 2022 CAG audit of staffing, unspent grants and unsafe stores
- Vacancies rose from 30.1 to 53.7 per cent; 94 of 175 sanctioned posts were vacant for between three months and 32 years (CAG Report No. 10 of 2022, covering 2014-15 to 2020-21).
- From 2014-15 to 2019-20 the hall spent an average of Rs 27.37 crore a year against an average allocation of Rs 37.49 crore (CAG).
- Only 85 per cent of objects were accessioned and 18 per cent RFID-tagged; the accession register was in bad condition with no soft copy, and no report was written on the 2017-18 physical verification (CAG).
- The stores had no fire alarms, smoke detectors, sprinklers or firefighting equipment, no air-conditioning, and CCTV in only three of eight; the hall had one of two recommended baggage scanners and six of 13 hand-held metal detectors, and galleries were closed for up to 23 months (CAG).
- Unverified: unnamed former insiders told Live History India that unionisation is one reason museums keep permanent staff numbers low.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Heritage and business press criticise the hall's management and upkeep; none questions the source of its money.
1. Live History India: Deepanjan Ghosh, "The Woes of Victoria Memorial and the Indian Museum" (2022)
URL: https://www.livehistoryindia.com/story/heritage-matters/the-woes-of-victoria-memorial-and-the-indian-museum
- Argues from the CAG findings that a lack of money does not explain the empty posts.
- Says the 2021 Netaji exhibit showed few real objects; an anonymous former consultant says only visitor numbers are counted, with no measure of engagement, and that contract staff fill posts to avoid pension costs.
2. Business Standard: "Memorial service" (2011)
URL: https://www.business-standard.com/article/beyond-business/memorial-service-111121100007_1.html
- Describes poor upkeep, shabby display cases, two official guides for thousands of daily visitors and no metal detector at the entrance; the authorities declined to comment on a planned restoration.

BOTTOM LINE
The hall is paid for by the Government of India and its own tickets, with no private or flagged funder. Its weak point is how it manages that public money and its collection.
