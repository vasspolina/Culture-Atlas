MUSEO IXCHEL DEL TRAJE INDÍGENA: Guatemala City, Guatemala
ID: museo-ixchel-del-traje-ind-gena-guatemala-city
Website: https://museoixchel.org/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Guatemalan Indigenous textiles and dress, in the Centro Cultural UFM on the Universidad Francisco Marroquín campus, at the end of 6th Street, Zone 10, Guatemala City (museum site; Wikipedia).
- It grew from a committee of the Tikal Association that decided in the early 1970s to document Maya textile traditions. Carmen Pettersen painted watercolours of the costumes and her husband Leon Lind Pettersen published them, with the proceeds meant to fund a museum. The museum opened in 1976 in a rented house in Guatemala City's hotel district (Holly Nottebohm, Harvard DRCLAS ReVista, 4 December 2010).
- In 1993, after consulting textile curators at the Metropolitan Museum of New York, it moved into a new building on the UFM campus (DRCLAS). Wikipedia lists the architects as Victor Cohen, Peter Giesemann, Augusto de León Fajardo, Adolfo Lau and William Pemüeller, and says the exterior looks like a woven textile pattern.
- The collection holds over 6,000 woven pieces from more than 115 weaving villages (DRCLAS, 2010); Prensa Libre (Pablo Juárez, 12 March 2017) reported 7,801 textiles from 147 municipalities and 34 villages.
- The permanent exhibition is "Los hilos cuentan una historia" (museum site).
- Visiting: Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00, Saturday 9:00 to 13:00, closed Sundays and holidays (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo Ixchel del Traje Indígena: small private museum supported by memberships, a US friends charity and local companies
- Membership tiers are Estrella Q300 a quarter, Colibrí Q300 a year, Tecolote Q1,200, Águila Q5,000 and Quetzal Q10,000 a year, with individual and corporate categories (museum membership page).
- The museum describes itself as non-profit, independent and apolitical; Holly Nottebohm, who oversees research and fundraising and is a board member, wrote in 2010 that the museum "supports itself", that friends and local companies have supported it, and that the Friends of the Ixchel Museum is an American foundation that funds it (DRCLAS).
- Friends of the Ixchel Museum, established in 1984 (museum site), is based in Princeton, New Jersey (ProPublica); the museum says it supports renovations, field research and publications. It was granted tax exemption in August 2008. Its revenue was 29,130 dollars in 2024 and 45,638 dollars in 2025, with expenses of 47,402 and 74,131 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- The charity's 2025 president is Ana-maria V Zaugg; the treasurer is Loa P Traxler (ProPublica).
- La Hora (Diego Estuardo Guzmán, "Guatemala podrá recibir hasta US$500 mil del Fondo de Embajadores para la Preservación Cultural de EE. UU.", 18 November 2024) listed "la conservación de la colección de cortes textiles del Museo Ixchel" (conservation of the museum's textile cut collection; translated) among earlier support to Guatemala, in an article on the U.S. Ambassadors Fund for Cultural Preservation, a U.S. government programme.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a small private non-profit on the UFM campus, paid for by memberships, local companies, a New Jersey friends charity with annual revenue of 29,130 dollars in 2024 and 45,638 dollars in 2025, and project support such as the conservation work La Hora lists under the U.S. Ambassadors Fund article.
