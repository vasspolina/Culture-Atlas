NEVADA MUSEUM OF ART: Reno, USA
ID: nevada-museum-of-art-reno
Website: https://www.nevadaart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 160 West Liberty Street, Reno, Nevada, established in 1931 (Wikipedia).
- It moved into a new building by architect Will Bruder in 2003. A 50,000 square foot expansion began in 2024 and fully opened in August 2025 (Wikipedia). The expansion cost about $60 million (Our Town Reno).
- On site: a permanent collection of more than 5,000 works in four focus areas, the Altered Landscape photography collection, the E.L. Cord Museum School and the Institute for Art + Environment, formed in 2009, with more than 50 archives (Wikipedia).
- The expansion adds an education and research wing named the Charles and Stacie Mathewson Education and Research Center, with a new library and classroom (Our Town Reno).
- Visiting: open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10am to 6pm, Thursday 10am to 8pm, closed Mondays and national holidays. Adults $15, students and seniors $13, children 6 to 14 $3, children 5 and under and members free; free for students of UNR, TMCC and WNC and for Nevada high school students with ID. All public areas are wheelchair accessible (museum website).

FUNDING
Nevada Museum of Art: privately funded: trustees, foundations, members, gaming-linked and mining donors, with some public arts money
- The 2024 donor list places at $1,000,000+: Glenn Haldan Family Fund, Morby Family Charitable Foundation, Roswitha Kima Smale and Pauline and Thomas Tusher. At $500,000+: Carol Franc Buck Foundation, Jackie and Steve Kane, Linda and Alvaro Pascotto, Julie and Michael Teel. At $250,000+: the Henry Luce Foundation and the Nell J. Redfield Foundation. At $100,000+: The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, the Institute of Museum and Library Services and the E.L. Cord Foundation (2024 annual report donor list).
- Public bodies on the 2024 list: Nevada Arts Council ($50,000+), National Endowment for the Arts, Nevada Division of Museums and History and the Reno-Sparks Convention and Visitors Authority ($25,000+).
- Nevada Gold Mines, operated by Barrick, appears at $50,000+ and as a major sponsor of exhibitions and programmes in 2024. Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Ormat Technologies and the Nevada Mining Association also appear (2024 annual report).
- The museum raised the $60 million expansion privately, including $16 million from Charles and Stacie Mathewson (Our Town Reno; museum press release not readable).
- No reporting found of an itemised 2024 budget that could be opened.

Red flags: gambling-linked donors and trustee
- San Manuel Gaming and Hospitality Authority is on the 2024 donor list in the $25,000+ band. The list does not describe its business.
- The Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority and the Reno-Sparks Convention and Visitors Authority are also on the 2024 donor list at $25,000+. These are tourism bodies, not casinos.
- Charles N. Mathewson is an honorary life trustee and Stacie Mathewson a trustee (2024 annual report). Charles Mathewson led International Game Technology in the 1980s and 1990s and gave $16 million before he died in October 2021 aged 93 (Our Town Reno).
- No reporting found of a campaign or objection to any of these gifts.

Not related: the Marjorie Barrick Museum of Art at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas. It is named for the philanthropist Marjorie Barrick, and has no stated link to Barrick Gold or to the Nevada Museum of Art (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the museum found in art media. A July 2020 This Is Reno report on a backlash over the museum's response to Black Lives Matter could not be opened, so its content is not summarised here.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on private money from foundations, trustees and local business, with some public arts grants. The weak points are the gaming-linked entries on the 2024 donor list, the Mathewson family's role as trustee and lead donor, and Barrick's place as a major sponsor. Watch the next donor list and any change in the Barrick sponsorship.
