BALLROOM MARFA – Marfa, USA
Website: https://ballroommarfa.org (ok, checked 2026-10-20; the /about/support/ page returned a fetch error)

OVERVIEW
- A non-profit contemporary art space in a 1920s-era ballroom in Marfa, Texas. Virginia Lebermann and Fairfax Dorn founded it in 2003. It commissions site-specific projects, music and public programmes. Admission is free (stated by the space).
- Legal entity: Ballroom Cultural Arts Foundation, EIN 20-0126402 (ProPublica).
- Leadership changes reported by Glasstire (21 Dec 2025): Executive Director Holly Harrison, who joined in 2024 after Daisy Nam left, has gone. Vance Knowles is now Chief Creative Officer and Britney Bass is CFO. Fairfax Dorn stays Artistic Director. Owen Brainard, Dax Luksic and Alejandra Laviada joined the board.

FUNDING
Ballroom Marfa: mainly private individuals and foundations, with small public grants
- FY ending June 2023: revenue $818,119, of which $589,026 (72%) was contributions; expenses $1,130,988; net assets $738,510. Daisy Nam was paid $106,792 (ProPublica, from the Form 990).
- Texas Commission on the Arts: $9,000 Arts Create operational support in FY2025 (TCA legislative report).
- Stone Circle project acknowledgements (stated by the space). Major support: A.S. Gage Ranches Partnership, Freedom Solar Power, Nicholas Kirkwood, Lisson Gallery, Allison Sarofim. Project sponsors include the Andy Warhol Foundation, Arts Council England, British Council, The Brown Foundation (Houston), Scone Foundation, Fairfax Dorn & Marc Glimcher, and Bara & Alex Tisch. Further funding from the National Endowment for the Arts, the Texas Commission on the Arts, Suzanne Deal Booth, Lauren & Andres Santo Domingo, Virginia Lebermann and others.
- The About page says funding comes from "individuals and foundations" without naming them.
- No reporting found of a gift-acceptance policy barring fossil-fuel or defense money. The existing record's "watch" note says one exists; no source retrieved confirms it.

BOTTOM LINE
A small non-profit funded mostly by contributions, including its co-founders and Texas arts families, with modest NEA and Texas Commission on the Arts grants. No flagged funder was found. Worth watching: the 2023 deficit, staff turnover in 2024–25, and the unconfirmed claim about a fossil-fuel and defense exclusion policy.
