CRYSTAL BRIDGES MUSEUM OF AMERICAN ART – Bentonville, USA
Website: https://crystalbridges.org (ok, checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- American art museum founded by Alice Walton, daughter of Walmart founders Sam and Helen Walton; opened 11 November 2011 in a 120-plus-acre park with about 201,000 sq ft of galleries, library and offices, a 300-seat auditorium and trails (Talk Business, 2011). Satellite venue: the Momentary.
- Alice Walton chaired the board from 2011 until November 2021, when Olivia Walton became chair; Alice Walton stayed on as a board member (Talk Business, 2021).
- Open Mon, Wed 11-6; Thu-Fri 11-8; Sat-Sun 10-6; closed Tuesday. General admission free; temporary exhibitions ticketed but free for members, veterans, visitors 25 and under and SNAP recipients (museum site).

FUNDING
Crystal Bridges: endowed by the Walton family, with Walmart corporate sponsorship of free admission.
- Walton Family Foundation: $800M in three endowments announced May 2011 — $350M operations, $325M acquisitions, $125M capital (Talk Business, independent).
- Walmart: $20M in July 2011 to cover admission for five years, the first gift to the Next Generation Fund (stated by the space).
- Walmart Foundation: $7M grant reported in 2023 for free admission and Momentary operations (Corporate Philanthropy Report via EBSCO; Talk Business headline says "through 2028").
- Willard & Pat Walker Charitable Foundation: gift announced 30 September 2026 to widen free access to temporary exhibitions; amount not stated (museum newsroom listing).
- Form 990 figures for 2024: revenue $131,160,398, expenses $96,110,839, contributions $35,607,176, investment income $19,281,718 (Impala, from IRS filings).
- The money ultimately comes from the Walmart fortune, through the Walton family, its foundation and the company.

Red flags: Labour protests targeting the Walton family at the museum
- 11 November 2011, opening day: current and former Walmart workers held teach-ins at Occupy camps contrasting the museum's reported $1.2B cost with Walmart cuts to part-time health cover. Source is a Business Wire press release with the UFCW union as contact (partisan: union campaign "Making Change at Walmart").
- 7 June 2013: striking members of OUR Walmart, a union-backed group, walked through the museum and offered staff a Rosie the Riveter cut-out, which was declined (The Nation, a left-leaning outlet).
- Unverified: claims that Walmart retaliated against workers who organised are allegations made by OUR Walmart, as reported by The Nation.

BOTTOM LINE
Crystal Bridges is funded almost entirely by the Walton family and Walmart, which the museum and press state openly. Worth watching is that dependence on one fortune, and past union-backed protests that linked the museum to Walmart's labour record.
