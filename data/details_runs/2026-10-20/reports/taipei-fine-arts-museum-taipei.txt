TAIPEI FINE ARTS MUSEUM: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: taipei-fine-arts-museum-taipei
Website: https://www.tfam.museum/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art museum at No. 181, Section 3, Zhongshan North Road, Zhongshan District, in Taipei Expo Park (tfam.museum, Wikipedia).
- Taipei's municipal government began planning it in 1976. It opened on 8 August 1983 on the former site of the United States Taiwan Defense Command; Wikipedia calls it the first museum in Taiwan built for contemporary art exhibitions. The architect was Kao Er-pan.
- Programmes: the Taipei Biennial (international since 1998) and, since 1995, Taiwan's pavilion at the Venice Biennale (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 9:30 to 17:30, Saturdays until 20:30; closed Mondays (tfam.museum). No reporting found of ticket prices or physical accessibility.

FUNDING
Taipei Fine Arts Museum: mainly public: the Taipei City Government, plus sponsors
- The Taipei City Government logo appears in the site footer; no budget figure was found.
- Its sponsor directory (tfam.museum) lists for 2025: the CTBC Cultural and Educational Foundation (中國信託文教基金會; also listed for 2020, 2023 and 2024, Taipei Biennial), Chanel Ltd. (museum development plan), Japan's National Center for Art Research (NT$1,365,561, Biennial), Friends of TFAM Association (NT$1,050,000, Olafur Eliasson show) and two further foundations. Most amounts are withheld under contract confidentiality.
- Other entries: the Henry Luce Foundation (2024, NT$319,786, museum development plan) and the Goethe-Institut Taipei (2023, NT$208,000). The Ministry of Culture granted NT$4,012,379 in 2013 for the Venice pavilion.
- Economic Daily News (21 November 2025) says CTBC Bank, a subsidiary of CTBC Financial Holding, sponsored the Taipei Biennial; it names neither the foundation nor the museum. No page opened shows that the foundation belongs to the bank group.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

Red flags: Taipei City government reaction to a work (2020)
- Kuomintang city councillor Yu Shu-hui attacked Mei Dean-E's "I-DEN-TI-TY" (shown in the museum's "The Secret South" exhibition) in a Facebook post later deleted, calling it "an incitation of xenophobia, or a pure rage out of resentment". Mayor Ko Wen-je said that if a country complained, "we should give the director Lin Ping a demerit"; ArtAsiaPacific says he backed away after an outcry from the art community. Finestre sull'Arte says the city administration "sharply rejected" the work.
- Director Ping Lin's resignation became public on 22 November 2020 and took effect on 31 January 2021. The museum gave her nearing retirement age as the reason (ArtAsiaPacific, 4 December 2020; Finestre sull'Arte, 12 December 2020).
- Unverified: that the resignation was linked to the exhibition. ArtAsiaPacific calls this speculation and Finestre sull'Arte calls it rumours; neither names who holds the view.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes in episodes: the 2020 work dispute and the 2021 to 2022 Venice pavilion case. No sustained critique of the museum's funding found.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "Taiwan drops Venice Biennale pavilion artist over sexual assault accusations" (2022)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/01/12/taiwan-venice-biennale-pavilion-sexual-assault-accusations-sakuliu-pavavaljung
- On 12 January 2022 TFAM removed Sakuliu Pavavaljung from the 2022 Venice pavilion after allegations posted on 16 December 2021; his studio denied them on 23 December.
- A lawyer for the first accuser released recordings of calls in which Sakuliu's partner, per the article, pressed her to settle by invoking TFAM's influence.
- TFAM said the decision aimed to "maintain Taiwan's international diplomatic image and reputation".
2. ArtAsiaPacific: "Taiwan Pavilion Terminates Project with Artist Facing Sexual Assault Claims" (2022, no author listed)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/taiwan-pavilion-terminates-project-with-artist-facing-sexual-assault-claims
- On 24 December 2021 councillor Huang Yu-fen and the victim's lawyer released the recordings. Director Wang Jun-Jieh replied that the museum did not pressure the victim.
3. Finestre sull'Arte: editorial staff, "Strong criticism for controversial work, Taipei Fine Arts Museum director resigns" (2020)
URL: https://finestresullarte.info/en/news/strong-criticism-for-controversial-work-taipei-fine-arts-museum-director-resigns
- Reports the councillor's and mayor's comments on "I-DEN-TI-TY" and the director's resignation.

WIDER CONTEXT
- CTBC Bank was fined by Taiwan's regulator: NT$14 million on 30 December 2021 after sales agents stole NT$230 million from clients (Taipei Times, 30 December 2021); NT$20 million in May 2023 for staff helping fraud rings and NT$10 million in July 2023 for theft from clients (Taipei Times, 19 September 2023).
- Connection to the museum: the sponsor directory names the CTBC Cultural and Educational Foundation, and Economic Daily News names the bank as a Taipei Biennial sponsor. No page opened links the fines to the museum, the foundation or an objection at the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a Taipei City institution with foundation, corporate and foreign-agency sponsors; no flagged industry funder was found. The weak points are the city leadership's reaction to a work in 2020 and the 2022 pavilion case, in which the museum denied pressuring the accuser. Worth watching is whether sponsor amounts, now mostly withheld, are published.
