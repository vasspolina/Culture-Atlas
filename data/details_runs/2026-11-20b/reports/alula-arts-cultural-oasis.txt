ALULA ARTS & CULTURAL OASIS: AlUla, Saudi Arabia
ID: alula-arts-cultural-oasis
Website: https://www.experiencealula.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The arts and culture programme of AlUla, an oasis region of 22,561 square kilometres in north-western Saudi Arabia, 1,100 kilometres from Riyadh, home to the Hegra World Heritage Site (Arab News, 2021). The name "AlUla Arts & Cultural Oasis" does not appear on the visitor site.
- Run by the Royal Commission for AlUla (RCU), set up in July 2017 and chaired since then by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (RCU website); Arab News says the Ministry of Finance set it up.
- Its governor since 2017 is Prince Bader bin Abdullah bin Farhan Al Saud, Minister of Culture since June 2018; the board also includes the Governor of Madinah and the Minister of Tourism (RCU website).
- Arts strands: Wadi AlFann, permanent desert works by Ahmed Mater, Manal Al Dowayan, James Turrell, Agnes Denes and Michael Heizer, behind its first target of a 2024 opening; the Desert X AlUla biennial, first held in 2020; and the AlJadidah Arts District (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- The AlUla Contemporary Art Museum, designed by Lina Ghotmeh and directed by Candida Pestana, was named on 1 February 2026 at "Arduna", an 80-work show co-curated with the Centre Pompidou; it has no opening date (The Art Newspaper, 2026).
- AlUla had 265,000 visitors in 2024 and aims for one million by 2030 (The National, 2025).

FUNDING
AlUla Arts & Cultural Oasis: state-funded: the Saudi government through the Royal Commission for AlUla, with a budget secured by the Public Investment Fund
- Saudi Arabia put in $2 billion of seed funding; $3.2 billion for priority infrastructure was to come from public-private partnerships, within a $15 billion masterplan in three phases to 2035 (Arab News, 28 April 2021).
- RCU chief tourism officer Phillip Jones said AlUla's budget is 14 billion riyals for 2025 and a similar amount for 2026, secured by the Public Investment Fund until 2030, untouched by the kingdom's spending cuts (The National, 27 October 2025).
- The RCU is offering 21 projects worth 6 billion riyals to private investors and aims for private money to make up 40 per cent of funding (The National).
- Desert X AlUla is organised with the RCU (The Art Newspaper, 2022); Artforum reported in 2019 that the RCU funds it.
- The Centre Pompidou partnership, announced in 2023, sits within a France and Saudi Arabia intergovernmental agreement (The Art Newspaper, 2026).

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The RCU is a government body chaired by the Crown Prince, with ministers on its board (RCU website), and its budget comes from the state's Public Investment Fund (The National).

Overall: Criticism targets the Saudi state patronage behind AlUla's art, chiefly the 2019 Desert X partnership.

- RCU staff answer that the programme is "genuine and not cynical" and serves schools and families.
7. Al-Monitor: Robbie Corey-Boulet, "Saudi artists marvel at surprise patron: their own rulers" (2022, drawing on AFP)
URL: https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/06/saudi-artists-marvel-surprise-patron-their-own-rulers
- Critics call Wadi AlFann and similar projects "artwashing"; Mater recalls his friend, the poet Ashraf Fayadh, sentenced to death in 2015 and to eight years on appeal.
- Mater welcomes state backing; Al Dowayan says she has never been censored.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The October 2019 board resignations and donor withdrawal were protests inside Desert X, a Californian biennial, against its partnership with the RCU.
- The RCU runs AlUla's arts programme and paid for Desert X AlUla, which places the protest on AlUla's patron.

BOTTOM LINE
AlUla's art is Saudi state money, routed through a commission the Crown Prince chairs and funded by the sovereign wealth fund. Worth watching: which private investors and foreign institutions join, and whether Wadi AlFann and the museum open.
