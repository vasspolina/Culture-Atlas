PALAIS DE LOMÉ: Lomé, Togo
ID: palais-de-lomé-lomé
Website: https://palaisdelome.com/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads, but shows only section labels (about, history, funding, partners, patrons, opening hours, admission) with no text)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art and culture centre in a restored colonial-era palace in Lomé, standing in a botanical park of 11 hectares (The Art Newspaper) or ten hectares (French Wikipedia), with more than 40 bird species (The Art Newspaper).
- Opened to the public on 28 November 2019 as Togo's first major contemporary art and culture centre (The Art Newspaper, Julia Michalska, 2019); the director is Sonia Lawson.
- Earlier names and uses: Germany built the palace between 1898 and 1905 as the governor's residence; French governors later used it; after independence in 1960 it housed the presidency until 1970, briefly served as the prime minister's residence in September 1991, and was left abandoned for several years after damage in the political troubles, including an army attack in December 1991 (French Wikipedia).
- Building: restored by Segond-Guyon with Archipat and Sara Consult and landscape designer Frédéric Reynauld (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting: The Art Newspaper reported tickets under $2 and one free day a month at the 2019 opening. No reporting found of current opening days or accessibility.

FUNDING
Palais de Lomé: mainly public: the Togolese state, with corporate sponsors for events
- The government paid XOF 2.2bn ($3.7m) for the renovation (The Art Newspaper, 2019); Afrik.com (2019) gives 2.4 billion CFA francs. The Art Newspaper called it "the only contemporary art centre in Africa to be fully financed by the state."
- Lawson said she hoped to win corporate sponsorship for running costs: "We want to decrease the budget connected to the government to around half" (The Art Newspaper, 2019).
- President Faure Essozimna Gnassingbé, Prime Minister Komi Sélom Klassou and the artist Kehinde Wiley attended the inauguration (The Art Newspaper). Afrik.com says the project is "fortement soutenu par le chef de l'Etat" (strongly backed by the head of state; translation).
- The first exhibition of the Toyota Tsusho CFAO African Art Award, with five laureates, opened at the centre on 11 June 2026; CFAO is a subsidiary of Toyota Tsusho Corporation, and the show travels to Tokyo and Paris. The source is a press release carried by AllAfrica as Premium Partner content from 35Nord and gives no payment to the centre.
- No reporting found of accounts, a board, or any sponsor's payment to the centre itself.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. Coverage at the opening was admiring, with the state's role noted rather than attacked.
1. True Africa: Claude Grunitzky, "Why this new museum is putting Togo on the map" (2019)
URL: https://trueafrica.co/article/why-this-new-museum-is-putting-togo-on-the-map/
- Invokes the "Bilbao effect": the centre could revive Lomé as the Guggenheim did Bilbao, and concedes that critics could find the comparison contrived.
- Calls it "not a perfect museum," reports that some Togolese artists were more critical of state intervention than others, and describes it as a "gesture of goodwill" toward Togolese artists and writers who went into exile during political crises.
- Credits Gnassingbé with allocating public funds to the renovation.
2. The Art Newspaper: Julia Michalska, "Togo opens arts centre in former colonial palace" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/togo-opens-arts-centre-in-former-colonial-palace
- A news report, not criticism: Lawson stated there is no censorship of artists in Togo (stated by the director).
- Notes the state-only financing and the presidential attendance without comment on them.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ghanaian Times (citing the BBC, 1 July 2025): civil rights groups say at least seven people were killed in a crackdown on protesters who want Gnassingbé to step down; protests have been banned since 2022; the government called them a "campaign of disinformation and hatred" orchestrated from abroad.
- TimesLIVE (citing Reuters, 1 July 2025): twelve civil society groups accused security forces of "shocking violence"; the government attributed the bodies recovered from a lagoon and a lake to drowning; authorities arrested dozens of people on 5 and 6 June, which Amnesty International described as a crackdown on dissent.
- Gnassingbé became President of the Council of Ministers in May 2025, a post with no fixed term limit (TimesLIVE).
- Connection to the centre: the centre was built with state money and opened with Gnassingbé present. No report found of the centre taking part in the 2025 events.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the Togolese state's, with no flagged-industry funder found; state money alone is not a red flag. The weak points are that the centre depends on a government and president accused by civil society groups of violent repression, and that it publishes no accounts, board or funder list. Worth watching how much corporate sponsorship, such as CFAO and Toyota Tsusho, replaces the state budget as Lawson said she wanted.
