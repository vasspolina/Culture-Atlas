KATUAQ GREENLAND CULTURAL CENTRE: Nuuk, Greenland
ID: katuaq-greenland-cultural-centre-nuuk
Website: https://katuaq.gl (checked 2026-10-24; online)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre at Imaneq 21 in Nuuk, Greenland's capital, used for concerts, exhibitions, conferences and as a cinema (Wikipedia; the centre's website).
- Built as a joint project of the Greenland Home Rule Government, the Nuuk Municipal Council and the Nordic Council of Ministers; construction ran from 1994 to 1997 and it opened on 15 February 1997 (Wikipedia).
- Danish name: Grønlands Kulturhus. No reporting found of what the name Katuaq means on the pages opened.
- Building: designed by Schmidt Hammer Lassen, L-shaped, with a backward-leaning screen clad in golden larch wood, inspired by the northern lights (Wikipedia). The centre says the building resembles a grand piano or an iceberg from outside.
- On site: two auditoria, the 508-seat Hans Lynge Hall and an 80-seat hall, meeting rooms, offices and a cafe (Wikipedia). The centre also lists cinema, Cafétuaq, foyer and cafe concerts, bookable rooms and apprenticeships.
- Visiting: Cafétuaq is closed Monday, open 11:00 to 20:00 Tuesday to Friday, 10:00 to 20:00 Saturday and 10:00 to 14:00 Sunday. Some events are free and others ticketed; no prices are given on the pages opened. Step-free access with ramps and automatic doors at the main entrance, an accessible toilet, ground-floor meeting rooms and free parking behind the building (stated by the centre).

FUNDING
Katuaq: mainly public: the Greenland Self-Government and Kommuneqarfik Sermersooq, with bank and airline sponsors
- Self-Government and Kommuneqarfik Sermersooq: KNR (29 June 2026) reports an extra DKK 4,000,000 supplementary appropriation for Katuaq, to which the Self-Government and Kommuneqarfik Sermersooq each contributed DKK 2,000,000.
- Deficits: Katuaq ran deficits from 2019 except a surplus of DKK 3,000 in 2022. The 2023 deficit was DKK 1.1 million and the 2025 surplus just over DKK 4 million. KNR traces the problems to a 2019 office equipment lease signed by the former financial director without the then-director's approval; Katuaq lost the resulting 2023 lawsuit and had to pay compensation. A 2024 review found revenue below budget (KNR).
- Air Greenland: signed a new agreement in the week of 15 April 2024 as "Tuukkaq sponsor" with DKK 300,000, covering the period through 2026, plus access to its Ilik discount travel scheme (Air Greenland).
- GrønlandsBANKEN: described by the bank as a "long-time sponsor" of Katuaq. No amount or years are stated (banken.gl).
- Sponsor packages: Katuaq sells sponsorships at DKK 50,000 (Ulu), 100,000 (Tuukkaq), 150,000 (Qilaat) and 200,000 (Katuaq). The sponsor page shows logos with no names or years, so no further sponsor can be named from it (stated by the centre).
- No reporting found of fossil fuel, arms, gambling or tobacco money. No reporting found of the board's members on the pages opened; the 2023 management report is posted as a PDF that could not be read as text.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Katuaq is mainly public: the Greenland Self-Government and the Nuuk municipality, with a Greenlandic bank and the national airline as sponsors. The weak points are the finances, with deficits from 2019 and a 2023 lawsuit loss over a lease, and the lack of a named board or full accounts on the pages read. Worth watching: whether the 2026 top-up from both public funders turns into permanent funding.
