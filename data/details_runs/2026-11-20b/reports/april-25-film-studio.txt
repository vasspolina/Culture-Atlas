APRIL 25 FILM STUDIO: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: april-25-film-studio
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Film studio of the Korean People's Army at the southern edge of urban Pyongyang, just before the Monument to the Three Charters of National Reunification (Koryo Tours). Its full name is the Korean April 25 Film Studio (NK News).
- CNN says it was founded in the years after the Korean War; Wikipedia gives 1959, under the name February 8 Cinema Studio, and Koryo Tours says the site was built in the early 1960s.
- Named for 25 April, which North Korea marks as the army's foundation day, the founding date of the anti-Japanese guerrilla army; 8 February, the date the army itself was founded, was marked before (Koryo Tours).
- The main building has a large mosaic of soldiers on its front and a thousand-seat cinema; Han dynasty tombs lie behind the studio (Koryo Tours).
- Output: Koryo Tours calls its films propaganda glorifying the army, navy, air force and special forces, naming "Order 027" and "Wolmido". In April 2022 it premiered "A Day and a Night", which NK News called the country's first new feature film in more than five years. "Days and Nights of Confrontation", a thriller about a plot to bomb Kim Jong Il's train, filled cinemas in 2025, aired on state television in January 2026 and won awards at the Pyongyang film festival (CNN, Will Ripley, January 2026).
- Visiting: the complex is not usually open to visitors, though some tour groups have watched a film in its cinema (Koryo Tours).

FUNDING
April 25 Film Studio: state-run: the Korean People's Army's own studio, paid from the North Korean state budget
- North Korea announces its budget only as yearly percentage changes. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 spending would reach 105.8 percent of 2025, with 15.8 percent of spending going to national defence and the cultural sector's funds at 103.1 percent of the year before (KCNA, copied by GlobalSecurity).
- The American filmmaker Justin Martell told CNN that in recent years the government had put a lot of money into new film productions (CNN, January 2026).

Red flags: US sanctions on the state and army that own the studio (from 2016)
- Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea that is in the United States or in the control of US persons (Federal Register, 18 March 2016). The order does not name the studio.
- OFAC added the Korean People's Army, to which the studio belongs, to its sanctions list on 1 June 2017, in the same action as the Ministry of People's Armed Forces (OFAC). The notice names the army, not the studio.

Not related: the Korean Art Film Studio, the Korean Documentary Film Studio and the Korean Science and Educational Film Studio, which Wikipedia lists as separate state film studios.

BOTTOM LINE
The sanctions flag stands: the studio belongs to an army and a state that the US blocks. That ownership, not any single funder, is its weak point.
