NAIROBI NATIONAL MUSEUM: Nairobi, Kenya
ID: nairobi-national-museum
Website: https://www.museums.or.ke/nairobi-national-museum/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads; the museum is a page of the National Museums of Kenya site)

OVERVIEW
- Kenya's main national museum on Museum Hill, Nairobi, run by National Museums of Kenya, a state corporation (Wikipedia).
- Began in 1910 as a collection of the East African Natural History Society (now Nature Kenya); construction at the present site started in 1929 and it opened on 22 September 1930 as the Coryndon Museum, named for Sir Robert Coryndon (The Standard; museum site). Renamed after independence (The Standard says 1963; Wikipedia says 1964).
- Closed on 15 October 2005 for its first major rebuilding since 1930; reopened on 14 July 2008, opened by President Mwai Kibaki (The Standard). Triad Architects Limited designed a visitors' centre as part of the master plan.
- Permanent galleries: Cradle of Human Kind, Story of Mammals, History of Kenya, Cycles of Life and a numismatic exhibition (museum site). The grounds hold the Nairobi Snake Park, a botanic garden and a nature trail (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 8:30am to 5:30pm, including weekends and public holidays; night tours for booked groups of 10 or more (museum site). From 7 May 2026: Kenyan and East African adults KSh 350, children KSh 200; rest of Africa $9 and $6; outside Africa $18 and $9 (The Star). No reporting found of accessibility information.

FUNDING
Nairobi National Museum: mainly public: Kenya's state corporation, with a European Union-funded rebuild and German project funding
- Run by National Museums of Kenya, a state corporation (Wikipedia). No reporting found of its annual budget in sources opened.
- 2005 to 2008 renovation: Sh800 million from the European Union under the National Museums of Kenya Support Programme (The Standard, Wangeci Kanyeki, 2013).
- Planet Africa, a travelling exhibition that opened in Nairobi on 29 November 2024 and ran until 31 May 2025 (blog post dated 29 January 2025): funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft and the German Foreign Office; no amounts stated (German Archaeological Institute blog). Partners: University of Ghana, Museum fur Vor- und Fruhgeschichte Berlin and the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors or a donor list.

Red flags: state censorship, May 2017
- May 2017: management of National Museums of Kenya, the state corporation that runs the museum, pulled works by Michael Soi, Patrick Mukabi and Bertiers Mbatia from an exhibition due to open on International Museum Day, whose theme was "Speaking the Unspeakable in Museums". Business Daily (Margaretta wa Gacheru, 18 May 2017) reports that the museum judged the art "inappropriate" for its main audience, school children, and that the artists were told the day before the opening.
- Mukabi said the artists had been encouraged to make work in keeping with the theme. Curator Lydia Galavu said the art had been packed away (Business Daily).
- No reporting found that the museum later reversed the decision.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical report found, about the 2017 ban, in Kenyan press rather than the art press. No criticism of the building or the funders.

- Calls the museum's position "contradictory to say the least" given the day's theme, and says the works were "censored by the management of the National Museums of Kenya"; on social media the decision was called "hypocritical".
- Points to a breakdown in communication: the museum had asked all three artists to make work for the day.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Kenyan state funding, an EU-financed rebuild and German research and foreign office funds for one project, with no corporate sponsor named. The weak point is the 2017 removal of three artists' work on International Museum Day, with no reporting found of a reversal. Worth watching: whether National Museums of Kenya publishes recent accounts and a donor list.
