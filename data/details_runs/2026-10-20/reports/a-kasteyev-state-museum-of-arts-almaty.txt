A. KASTEYEV STATE MUSEUM OF ARTS: Almaty, Kazakhstan
ID: a-kasteyev-state-museum-of-arts-almaty
Website: https://nmirk.kz (checked 2026-10-25; the old address gmirk.kz redirects here, and the page text returned only the museum's name in Kazakh)

OVERVIEW
- National art museum in the Koktem-3 microdistrict of Almaty (22/1), now styled the Abylkhan Kasteyev National Art Museum after being granted national status.
- Opened on 16 September 1976 from the collections of the Shevchenko Kazakh State Gallery (founded 1935) and a museum of decorative and applied art (founded 1970); renamed in 1984 after the Kazakh artist Abilkhan Kasteev (1904 to 1973) (Wikipedia).
- Named for Abilkhan Kasteev, a Kazakh painter (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by architect G. Novikov; Wikipedia says it was listed in 1982 as a monument of republican significance (the entry has no citations). Tengrinews (2021) reports the Ministry of Culture and Sport dates the state monument listing to 1983.
- What is there: Kazakh decorative and applied art, Kazakh painting, sculpture and graphics, Russian, Western European, 20th-century foreign and Eastern art, a souvenir shop and a picture gallery. Sxodim.com gives more than 25,000 items; Wikipedia gives more than 23,000 works.
- Renovation: it reopened in January 2026 after work on halls, roof, floors, engineering systems and new climate control, timed to its 90th anniversary and the 50th anniversary of the building (Bizmedia, 17 January 2026; Forbes.kz, 18 January 2026).
- Visiting (Sxodim.com, undated): open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, ticket desk to 17:30, closed Monday and the last day of each month. Adults 2,000 tenge; students and pensioners 1,000; schoolchildren 500; foreign adults 5,000. The page gives no accessibility details, and says current prices should be checked by phone.

FUNDING
A. Kasteyev State Museum of Arts: public: Kazakhstan's Ministry of Culture and Information
- State museum; the Ministry of Culture and Sport (now Culture and Information) is the responsible ministry in the 2021 Tengrinews report.
- The 2026 renovation was carried out with the support of the President; Bizmedia (17 January 2026) says the project was implemented by the Ministry of Culture and Information together with the museum staff, Samruk-Kazyna and the public fund Senim Qogamy. The page says who did the work, not who paid, and gives no amounts.
- National status: a draft presidential decree on granting it to this museum and the Central State Museum was open for public comment until 12 December 2024. Bes.media reports the status opens access to additional financing, without amounts.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, a private donor list, published accounts or a board. No reporting found of a budget figure.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Noise dispute: Tengrinews reported on 14 July 2021 that the museum had been fined 230 MRP (about 670,000 tenge) over air-conditioner noise that residents of the Central Esentai Residence next door complained about, and ordered to fix the problem within 30 days. The museum says the housing complex was built illegally inside its protective zone. The Ministry says it never approved the construction and that the cooling system is needed to keep storage at 18 degrees Celsius plus or minus 1 under a 2015 order. The museum and the developer then negotiated.
- Unverified: an unnamed former director took about 1.544 million tenge from the museum, through a freight refund from a 2007 exhibition transport contract and a refund on a demolition job, from Express-K citing Almaty's financial police, reported by Vesti.kz on 13 March 2009. The article reports only the start of the trial and no verdict; no later reporting was found.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Kazakh state, with the 2026 renovation carried out by the Ministry, the museum staff, Samruk-Kazyna and the Senim Qogamy fund; no fossil fuel, tobacco, arms or gambling funder was found. The weak points are no published accounts or board, and no page says who paid for the renovation or how much. Worth watching: how national status changes its financing.
