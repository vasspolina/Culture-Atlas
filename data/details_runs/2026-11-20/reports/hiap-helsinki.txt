HIAP (HELSINKI INTERNATIONAL ARTIST PROGRAMME): Helsinki, Finland
ID: hiap-helsinki
Website: https://www.hiap.fi (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- International artist residency and exhibition programme with its office, Gallery Augusta, a project space and residency studios on Suomenlinna island, and further studios on the fourth floor of the Cable Factory (Tallberginkatu 1) in Helsinki (hiap.fi/about).
- Founded in 1998 by a coalition of Helsinki arts organisations. It runs as a non-profit association (hiap.fi/about).
- Members of the association include Uniarts Helsinki's Academy of Fine Arts, Kunsthalle Helsinki, the Cable Factory, the Finnish Museum of Photography, Pixelache Festival, Zodiak Center for New Dance, Artists' Association MUU, AV-arkki, Gallery Huuto and Pro Artibus, plus one individual (hiap.fi/about).
- Residencies last from 1 to 11 months for artists, curators and other cultural practitioners (hiap.fi/about).
- The director is Emma Beverley. The board for 2026 to 2027 is chaired by Vappu Renko of the Cupore Center for Cultural Policy Research, with members from HAM Helsinki Art Museum, Dance House Helsinki and the Sámi Museum Siida (hiap.fi/about).
- From the start of 2025 HIAP coordinates FAIRE, the Finnish Artist Residency Network, taken over from the Finnish Artists' Studio Foundation (stated by HIAP).
- In December 2025 it published a strategy for 2026 to 2030, drawn up with over 70 contributors (stated by HIAP).
- Visiting: a year-round ferry runs from the Market Square to Suomenlinna; HIAP asks residents and partners to travel to Helsinki by land or sea (hiap.fi/about).

FUNDING
HIAP: public funders: Taike (Arts Promotion Centre Finland), the Ministry of Education and Culture and the City of Helsinki
- HIAP names Taike Arts Promotion Centre Finland, the Ministry of Education and Culture and the City of Helsinki as its main funders (hiap.fi/about).
- Residency partners for 2026, which realise the residencies with HIAP: Creative Australia, BMWKMS Austria, Frame Contemporary Art Finland, the Finnish Cultural Foundation, the Finnish Institute in the UK and Ireland, Temple Bar Gallery + Studios, Tokyo Arts and Space, EMMA Museum of Modern Art, the University of the Arts Helsinki Foundation, New Art Exchange, Forum Box, Publics, Pot Viapori, Arabia Art Department, Saga Prefectural Government, Bioart Society, Comic Society, Ramallah Municipality and Sumud (HIAP partners page).
- Ukraine Solidarity Residencies: run with other Finnish art organisations and supported by Nordic Culture Point and the Ministry of Education and Culture; Goethe-Institut Finnland also supported the 2024 to 2025 round. The programme pays accommodation, travel and a working grant for stays of 3 to 6 months (stated by HIAP).
- Learning Materials residencies, run with Bioart Society, TUO TUO, Koynẽ Programme and Swamp Swap, are supported by Nordic Culture Point and Kone Foundation (HIAP partners page).
- Since autumn 2024 HIAP rejects funding and any sponsorship from the Israeli government and from Israeli institutions or lobby groups it counts as complicit (stated by HIAP).

Red flags: 2024 residents' withdrawal from the Open Studios over Palestine
- On 7 June 2024 the Spring 2024 residents collectively withdrew from the HIAP Open Studios, calling on HIAP to join the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions (BDS) movement and become an Apartheid Free Zone. The Open Studios were cancelled (stated by HIAP).
- HIAP met Sumud, the Finnish Palestine Network, on 19 June 2024 and agreed a decision process with its board in August, after a new director started (stated by HIAP).
- The Autumn 2024 members' meeting agreed that HIAP becomes an Apartheid Free Zone, after consultation with staff, board, member organisations, partners and funders (stated by HIAP).
- HIAP says the movement does not target individuals and that people of any nationality stay welcome (stated by HIAP).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind HIAP is Finnish public funding, with Nordic and foundation money for single programmes and partner organisations behind single residencies. The one dispute on record is the residents' 2024 boycott of the Open Studios, which ended with HIAP adopting the boycott's terms. Worth watching: whether partners or funders change their support after that decision.
