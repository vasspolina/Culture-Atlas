MUSÉE NATIONAL BOUBOU HAMA: Niamey, Niger
ID: mus-e-national-boubou-hama-niamey

OVERVIEW
- Niger's national museum, in a park in central Niamey with a cultural section, a scientific section and a zoo (Wikipedia).
- Founded on 18 December 1959 as the Musée National du Niger. Its first conservator, Pablo Toucet, designed it as part of the Culture Valley of Niamey, an idea proposed by Boubou Hama, after whom the museum is named (Wikipedia).
- Five traditional houses represent the Fulani, Hausa, Songhai, Tuareg and Zarma peoples, and the museum has a centre for arts and crafts and hosts temporary exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- The remains of the Arbre du Ténéré have been in the museum since 1974 and in their own mausoleum since 1979 (Wikipedia). The zoo has about fifty species (Wikipedia); AFP puts it at 111 (ADF Magazine, 14 October 2021).
- The Uranium Pavilion, which had existed for 30 years, was inaugurated after renovation on 30 May 2016, with AREVA's deputy general manager Olivier Wantz and Minister Assoumana Mallam Issa present (AREVA press release, 30 May 2016).
- The fossil hall shows resin replicas, including Jobaria; the original bones are kept in storage or abroad. The director in 2025 was Abdouramane Gabidan (Internazionale, 24 October 2025, from Shola Lawal and Hannane Ferdjani of Al Jazeera).
- The museum had 170,000 visitors a year as of 2013 (Wikipedia). Admission cost about 10 cents in 2021 (ADF Magazine).

FUNDING
Musée National Boubou Hama: state museum of Niger, with international donations planned for a renovation
- State museum of Niger. AFP reported in 2021 that its budget is a small fraction of what comparable museums receive, and that plans call for a renovation and expansion funded by international donations (ADF Magazine, "Le musée du Niger est son « miroir » national", 14 October 2021).
- Planned Museum of the River: the NigerHeritage project, whose contact is University of Chicago professor Paul Sereno, lists the Government of Niger, the City of Niamey, the Boubou Hama National Museum and the University of Chicago among its principal partners, with Stantec Architecture and Adji Mansour Architecture Aménagement as architects. The team has met in Chicago and Niamey since 2017, and its listed philanthropist is Bob Vladem of Santa Fe (NigerHeritage, stated by the project).
- In 2025 Sereno said he was working with Niamey and Agadez authorities to build two international-standard museums and training centres (Internazionale).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a State of Niger institution; AFP reported in 2021 a small budget and a renovation planned with international donations. The planned Museum of the River is a project of a University of Chicago led team with the Government of Niger, the City of Niamey and the museum as partners. AREVA's 2016 press release presents the renovated Uranium Pavilion as a symbol of its partnership with the State of Niger.
