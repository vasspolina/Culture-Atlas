TANK SHANGHAI: Shanghai, China
ID: tank-shanghai
Website: none listed in the record; no working site was found (checked 2026-10-25), so details come from third-party pages

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum and 60,000-square-metre art park on the West Bund waterfront of the Huangpu River, Xuhui District, at Longteng Avenue (Time Out gives 2350, the Chinese Art Foundations page gives 2380).
- Founded by the collector Qiao Zhibing, a karaoke club owner, who planned it for more than four years and was invited to create it by the government-backed West Bund Development Group. It opened on 23 March 2019 after a delay from November 2018 (The Art Newspaper, Lisa Movius, 22 March 2019).
- Building: five decommissioned aviation fuel tanks from the historic 1917 Longhua Airport, converted by Beijing-based OPEN Architecture. The Art Newspaper puts the buildings at roughly 10,000 square metres, about 60 percent exhibition space; three tanks are linked by a new building and two freestanding tanks serve as a clubhouse and event space.
- On site: exhibition spaces, plazas, gardens, a bookstore, an education centre and restaurants (Time Out). Opening shows: a teamLab installation, a solo show by Adrián Villar Rojas and the group show "Under Construction".
- Visiting (Chinese Art Foundations page): Tuesday to Friday 12:00 to 18:00, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. Time Out lists Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 6pm. At opening the teamLab installation cost RMB 120 (The Art Newspaper). No reporting found of accessibility details.

FUNDING
TANK Shanghai: founder-funded with a state-backed partner: Qiao Zhibing's money, with the West Bund Group inviting him and managing the park
- Qiao told The Art Newspaper (Lisa Movius, 22 March 2019) that the museum's funding came entirely from him: "That all comes from me. I don't want to say how much, but it was a lot of money." He said the government only managed the surrounding park and that "I have no other partners besides the government".
- Time Out calls the complex "An investment by Qiao himself and the West Bund Group". The accounts differ and no budget was found.
- The government-backed West Bund Development Group invited Qiao and manages the riverfront park of about 60,000 square metres around the museum (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of where Qiao's money came from beyond his karaoke club and collecting.
- No reporting found of an annual report, accounts, council or donor list.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of TANK itself; coverage is opening news and appreciative reviews.
1. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "Collector Qiao Zhibing's delayed Tank Shanghai museum opens on West Bund waterfront" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/03/22/collector-qiao-zhibings-delayed-tank-shanghai-museum-opens-on-west-bund-waterfront
- Reports the opening group show had one woman among 13 artists; Qiao said he "didn't deliberately seek out female artists."
- Reports the show's first curator, Li Bowen, left after being accused of sexual misconduct in 2018.
- A government AI conference in September 2018 halted construction for a month. The article reports no criticism of the museum itself.
2. Hyperallergic: Tina Shan, "In Shanghai, Carving Out a Space for Critiques of Consumerism" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/522437/in-shanghai-carving-out-a-space-for-critiques-of-consumerism/
- Largely positive review of the TANK Art Festival (12 to 15 September 2019, about ten thousand visitors); calls it "a landmark event" and says its Art Market made contemporary art affordable.
- Acknowledges one could question how meaningful its contemplation of consumption was. Criticises neither the funding nor the institution.

WIDER CONTEXT
- ArtAsiaPacific, Mioie Kwok, "Shanghai's Redevelopment of the West Bund Pushes Out Art Galleries" (19 July 2024): art spaces including ShanghArt, Don Gallery, Aike Gallery, Pond Society, Qiao Space and Ding Yi's studio were displaced in a large-scale redevelopment of the district, which the article describes as shifting toward technology and finance. Neither it nor ArtAsiaPacific's September 2024 report says who issued the eviction notices, and neither names the West Bund Group as the party that evicted tenants.
- The July article says aspirations for "a dynamic, enduring new cultural enclave" now appear "ever more improbable". It lists TANK Shanghai among the private museums the district attracted and does not report TANK as displaced.
- Connection: Qiao Space, Qiao's other gallery, shut on 15 June 2024 and was demolished; TANK stands on the same waterfront, which the West Bund Development Group manages (The Art Newspaper, 2019).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid for by its founder, a karaoke club owner, with a state-backed developer as inviting partner and park manager; the founder and Time Out disagree on whether the developer co-invested. No accounts, council or funder list were found. Worth watching is how the redevelopment of the West Bund, which has already closed Qiao Space, affects TANK.
