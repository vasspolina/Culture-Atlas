TALLINN ART HALL (TALLINNA KUNSTIHOONE): Tallinn, Estonia
ID: tallinna-kunstihoone-tallinn
Website: https://www.kunstihoone.ee

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art hall on Freedom Square (Vabaduse väljak), Tallinn, which opened in 1934 as one of Northern Europe's earliest purpose-built Kunsthallen and was founded to serve the Estonian Artists' Association (Artdependence, "Tallinn Art Hall to Reopen Following Landmark Five-Year Renovation", 2 July 2026; The Art Newspaper, Rachel Kubrick, "Historic Tallinn Art Hall to reopen this November following €13m renovation", 2 July 2026).
- Estonian Wikipedia reports that artists first proposed a building on Harjumägi in 1926, that Edgar Johan Kuusik produced a preliminary design, and that Anton Soans won a second competition in 1932 and had his final design approved that year. The hall opened on 15 September 1934 and its opening exhibition drew 13,493 visitors. The Art Newspaper's photo caption credits the building to Kuusik, while Estonian Wikipedia credits the executed design to Soans.
- Juhan Raudsepp's bronze figures Töö and Ilu were installed on the facade in 1937. The KuKu art club began operating there in January 1935 (Estonian Wikipedia).
- The state nationalised the building in 1940 and gave it to the Estonian SSR Art Foundation; a side building followed in 1953 and a superstructure in 1962; it was re-nationalised on 14 November 1993 and opened on 15 February 1995 (Estonian Wikipedia).
- It closed for renovation in March 2021 and reopens on 13 November 2026 with the exhibition "Estonian Art from Beginning to ..." (The Art Newspaper). The renovation was led by Juhan Rohtla of KUU Architects, with interior design by Tarmo Piirmets of Pink Sisearhitektid; it keeps the historic facade and adds new galleries, an underground Black Box gallery and a new restaurant (Artdependence); The Art Newspaper also reports the Black Box.
- During the closure it exhibited at the Lasnamäe Pavilion, Jaan Koorti 24, and the Tallinn City Gallery, Harju 13; it holds 10 to 12 exhibitions a year and describes itself as "not a museum" (Tallinn Art Hall website).
- Visiting: the Lasnamäe Pavilion is open Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00, closed Monday and Tuesday, with free admission and a ramp to the one-storey building (Tallinn Art Hall website).
- Paul Aguraiuja is the director (The Art Newspaper); Estonian Wikipedia says he was chosen as director on 8 June 2020.

FUNDING
Tallinn Art Hall: public: the Estonian government, through a foundation set up by the Estonian Artists' Union and the Ministry of Culture
- Legal form: SA Kunstihoone, a foundation registered on 12 December 2017 at Vabaduse väljak 6 (Estonian business register). Estonian Wikipedia says the Estonian Artists' Union and the Ministry of Culture set it up.
- Renovation: The Art Newspaper (2 July 2026) says the 13 million euro redevelopment was "fully funded by the Estonian government"; Artdependence gives the same cost and does not name a funder. Nordecon announced on 30 December 2024 a construction contract with SA Kunstihoone worth 7.99 million euros excluding VAT, with works to take 18 months (market announcement).
- Art commission: Estonia's Percent for Art scheme, which allocates 1 percent of publicly funded construction budgets to art, pays for the new commission Soup Kitchen by Dénes Farkas (The Art Newspaper; Artdependence).
- In 2019, heads of Estonian art organisations asked the culture minister to add the Tallinn Art Hall and four other art buildings to the list of nationally important cultural buildings funded by the Cultural Endowment (Kultuurkapital), proposing 5.45 million euros for the Tallinn Art Hall of a total 29.25 million euros (ERR, 29 October 2019).
- The Tallinn Art Hall website lists the Ministry of Culture, Kulka, AkzoNobel/Sadolin and Veinisober under Sponsors (stated by the space).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Tallinn Art Hall is public: the Estonian Artists' Union and the Ministry of Culture set up the foundation that runs it, and The Art Newspaper reports that the Estonian government fully funded the 13 million euro renovation, which reopens the building on 13 November 2026. The programme is produced by the foundation itself.
