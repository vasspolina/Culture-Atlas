OT301 (VERENIGING EHBK): Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: ot301-amsterdam
Website: https://www.ot301.nl

OVERVIEW
- Collectively run, not-for-profit venue at Overtoom 301, 1054 HW Amsterdam, combining housing, workspaces and public functions for arts, politics and subculture (OT301 website).
- Squatted in 1999 by a group of activists and artists called Eerste Hulp Bij Kunst (EHBK, "First Aid for Art"); the group later paid rent and bought the building in 2006 (The Amsterdammer, Elitsa Kaleva, 22 January 2023). Wikipedia says the building was the former Dutch film academy and that EHBK bought it from the council.
- Runs as the association Vereniging EHBK, organised as a "horizontal democracy" (The Amsterdammer, 2023).
- On site: Ventilator cinema and bar, Studio 301 for music, dance and performance, the 4bid Gallery, Café Gilde (a culture kitchen) and the AnaMorphic Rehearsal Studio (OT301 website). Wikipedia lists a non-profit print shop and about fifteen artists' workspaces, and says the vegan restaurant De Peper closed in 2023 and was replaced by Café Gilde.
- Green roof: installed shortly before April 2013, with the city funding half, doubling OT301's initial investment of 10,000 euros (IamExpat, Lily Heaton, 12 April 2013).
- Event times are listed on the OT301 website.

FUNDING
OT301: association-owned: a building bought by its users' association, with city money for a green roof
- The collective owns its building; EHBK bought it from the council in 2006 (Wikipedia); The Amsterdammer also dates the purchase to 2006.
- The Amsterdam Fonds voor de Kunst gave OT301 the Amsterdamprijs voor de Kunsten on 23 August 2007, presented by Mayor Job Cohen (Wikipedia).
- City money for the green roof: half of the investment, doubling OT301's 10,000 euros (IamExpat, Lily Heaton, 12 April 2013).
- The Amsterdammer (Elitsa Kaleva, 22 January 2023) quotes co-founder Ivo Schmetz calling OT301 non-commercial, with a goal of keeping spaces and events affordable.

BOTTOM LINE
OT301 is a squat turned association-owned building. The outside money documented is the city's half of the green roof and a 2007 arts prize from the Amsterdam Fonds voor de Kunst.
