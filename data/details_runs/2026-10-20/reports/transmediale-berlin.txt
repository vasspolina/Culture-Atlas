TRANSMEDIALE: Berlin, Germany
ID: transmediale-berlin
Website: https://transmediale.de

OVERVIEW
- Annual Berlin festival for art and digital culture. The 2026 edition, the 39th, ran from 28 January to 1 February 2026 at silent green Kulturquartier (Gerichtstraße 35) and CANK (Karl-Marx-Straße 95), curated by Neema Githere and Juan Pablo García Sossa (e-flux).
- Founded in 1988 as VideoFilmFest, a side programme of the Berlinale's International Forum of New Cinema, initiated by Hartmut Horst and Micky Kwella; renamed VideoFest in 1989, transmedia in 1997 and transmediale in 1998 (Wikipedia).
- Kulturprojekte Berlin organised the festival until 2020; since then the independent association transmediale e.V. has organised and managed it (Wikipedia; Kulturprojekte Berlin). The association's office is at silent green, Gerichtstraße 35 (Wikipedia).
- Artistic directors: Andreas Broeckmann (2001 to 2007), Stephen Kovats (from 2007), Kristoffer Gansing (from 2012) and Nóra Ó Murchú (from 2021, departure announced in July 2024) (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
transmediale: mainly public: the German Federal Cultural Foundation (Kulturstiftung des Bundes)
- The festival has been funded as a "cultural institution of excellence" by the Kulturstiftung des Bundes since 2004 (transmediale website; Kulturstiftung des Bundes project page for transmediale 2021 to 2022). Kulturprojekte Berlin says the federal government has funded it as a flagship project since 2004.

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- 2025: an open letter by !Mediengruppe Bitnik, Aram Bartholl, Constant Dullaart and Danja Vasiliev, sent to transmediale e.V. (Filippo Gianetta and Magdalena Ritter) and its advisory board on 1 February 2025 and forwarded to the nettime list by Andreas Broeckmann on 2 February 2025. It asked the leadership to make transparent the basis of its decisions, said Ó Murchú's departure in summer 2024 was "not transparently communicated", and objected to a 10-month lead-curator post with the exhibition element removed (nettime).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One essay on the festival's 2024 statement and Berlin's political climate, and one open letter on the 2025 leadership change and cancelled exhibition.
1. Spike Art Magazine: Adina Glickstein, "The Highs and Lows of transmediale 2024" (14 February 2024)
URL: https://www.spikeartmagazine.com/articles/essay-the-highs-and-lows-of-transmediale-2024
- Frames the festival as shaped by Berlin's political climate and the war in Gaza, including a Berlin Senate clause requiring state-funded artists to pledge opposition to antisemitism, which she argues conflated antisemitism with anti-Zionism. The Senate dropped the clause in January 2024.
- Reads the festival's statement, which condemned the clause and called for "safeguarding a free and respectful space for dialogue, openness and exchange", as a hedged appeal, noting that the statement says the festival depends on state funding.
- Says the #StrikeGermany boycott led to withdrawals, and that the panel "Anti-Colonial Tech through Resistance and Discomforts" was cancelled on the day; its panelists later said they were told they "could not be explicit about a certain solidarity".
2. nettime-l: Andreas Broeckmann forwarding an open letter to transmediale, "Re: <nettime> Transmedialle intel?" (2 February 2025)
URL: https://nettime.org/Lists-Archives/nettime-l-2502/msg00004.html
- Argues that a lead-curator post on a 10-month contract (April 2025 to February 2026) leaves little room for a multi-year vision and asks why no artistic director is being hired.
- Argues that the call, which removes the exhibition element, constrains the new post. In the forwarded thread, Ursula Damm writes that the festival has become a "media-theoretical conference".
- Addressed to transmediale e.V. (Filippo Gianetta and Magdalena Ritter) and the advisory board.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2024 withdrawals: the #StrikeGermany boycott, with over 1,600 signatories, asked artists to withdraw from publicly funded German cultural institutions and events "such as transmediale". Artists including Avril Corroon withdrew from transmediale 2024. The festival said "several artists and a curator" had withdrawn (Spike Art Magazine, Adina Glickstein, 14 February 2024). The boycott opposed the Berlin Senate clause and German state policy on the war in Gaza, not transmediale alone; the festival's own 19 January statement condemned the clause.
- The Berlin Senate's clause: culture senator Joe Chialo introduced it on 21 December 2023, requiring funding recipients to oppose antisemitism as defined by the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance. Almost 6,000 cultural workers and artists signed an open letter against it, and the Senate dropped it about a month later, in January 2024 (The Art Newspaper, Catherine Hickley, 22 January 2024).
- Connection: the clause applied to Berlin state-funded culture, and Spike ties the clause to the withdrawals from transmediale 2024.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind transmediale is German public arts funding: the Kulturstiftung des Bundes has funded it as a cultural institution of excellence since 2004. The documented controversy aimed at the festival is the 2025 open letter on its leadership change and the removed exhibition element; the 2024 artist withdrawals were a boycott of German state-funded institutions over the Berlin Senate clause.
