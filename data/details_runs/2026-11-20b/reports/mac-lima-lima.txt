MAC LIMA: Lima, Peru
ID: mac-lima-lima
Website: https://maclima.pe/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art at Av. Grau 1511 in Barranco, Lima, with three galleries of 100, 200 and 1,000 square metres in an industrial-style building surrounded by a terrace and a park (MAC Lima).
- It opened in late January 2013 on the site of the former Barranco Zoo and its artificial lake, which closed in 1970 (English Wikipedia); Spanish Wikipedia gives 23 January 2013.
- It is run by the Instituto de Arte Contemporáneo (IAC), founded in Lima on 11 June 1955, which decided in the late 1980s to build a museum for its collection (Spanish Wikipedia). The IAC opened in the former Galería de Lima premises, moved to Casa Mujica in 1966 and used the Museum of Italian Art building from 1969 to 1972 (English Wikipedia).
- Architect: Frederick Cooper Llosa (English Wikipedia).
- Board elected for 2022 to 2025: Meg de Romaña, president, the first woman in the post; Eduardo Hochschild Beeck, vice president; Manuel Ugarte Maggiolo, secretary; Carlos Heeren Ramos, treasurer (Arte al Día, 25 October 2022). The museum's magazine Cubo Abierto No. 6 (2023) lists the same officers. Hochschild has chaired Hochschild Mining plc since 2006 (Hochschild Mining).
- Pedro Pablo Alayza, a former director of the Museo de Arte de Lima and the Museo Pedro de Osma, took over as director in 2023 (Forbes Perú Brand Voice, paid content, August 2023). Cubo Abierto No. 6 names Nicolás Gómez Echeverri as executive director since 2018.
- Current shows: "Contemporáneo 1", a collection display of more than 100 Peruvian artists curated by Jorge Villacorta and Augusto Del Valle, from 1 December 2024 to 31 December 2026, built on collectors' loans and artists' donations (La República; MAC Lima), and the MAC Digital art festival from 20 November 2026 to 7 March 2027 (MAC Lima).
- Education programmes include Escuela Vecina, #aulamac and Domingos en el MAC (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 19:00, closed Monday; tickets are sold online (MAC Lima).

FUNDING
MAC Lima: private non-profit on municipal land: self-generated income, a friends' programme, a telecom foundation and a one-off Getty grant
- The museum is a private non-profit built on land donated by the Municipality of Barranco (English Wikipedia); Spanish Wikipedia calls it land ceded in concession by the district municipality.
- The building's first funds came from an auction in the 1990s, for which artists including Jesús Rafael Soto, Carlos Cruz-Diez and Antonio Seguí gave works to the IAC (Spanish Wikipedia).
- In 2023 Alayza described MAC Lima as running on self-managed resources and planned to grow the Amigos del MAC programme, venue hire for corporate events and donations (Forbes Perú Brand Voice, paid content). The Amigos page says members' contributions pay for exhibitions, acquisitions and building work (MAC Lima).
- Fundación Telefónica Movistar financed "Color. El conocimiento de lo invisible", a free show in the 1,000 square metre hall to March 2024 that drew more than 1,500 visitors a day (Forbes Perú Brand Voice, paid content).
- The Getty Foundation's Paper Project gave a one-off grant of 64,000 US dollars in its 2022 round for an exhibition and publication on MAC Lima's prints and drawings, led by Gómez Echeverri and Giuliana Vidarte Basurco (Getty Foundation).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In February 2018 the museum showed "Canon: Vírgenes de la Puerta" by Juan José Barboza-Gubo and Andrew Mroczek, to 1 April. A CitizenGo petition addressed to the museum's then director Enrique Stiglich, to the head of the Spanish aid agency and to Spain's ambassador demanded the closing of this show and a parallel one at the Cultural Center of Spain; it had more than 20,000 signatures by 27 February. The Advocate names Tradición y Acción por un Perú Mayor, Revuelta Contra el Arte Moderno and the Asociación Antonin Scalia as the groups behind the attack (The Advocate).
- Conservative groups and congressman Carlos Tubino called the show blasphemous, which led to bill 2450 to criminalise religious defamation; the museum defended the works in public (Spanish Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind MAC Lima is clean: none of its documented funders falls under a flag. The point to watch is the board's tie to Hochschild Mining.
