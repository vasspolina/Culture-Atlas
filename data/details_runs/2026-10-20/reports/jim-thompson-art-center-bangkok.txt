JIM THOMPSON ART CENTER: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: jim-thompson-art-center-bangkok
Website: https://www.jimthompsonartcenter.org (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at No. 6 Soi Kasemsan 2, Rama 1 Road, Bangkok, about a 3-minute walk from the National Stadium BTS station (exit 1) and beside the Jim Thompson House Museum (Jim Thompson Art Center).
- Run by the James H.W. Thompson Foundation, which was legally established in 1975 and also operates the Jim Thompson House Museum; the centre reopened on 27 November 2021 after about three years of construction on a new building and renovation of the house museum (ArtAsiaPacific, Pamela Wong, 29 November 2021; James H.W. Thompson Foundation). No reporting found of the date the centre first opened.
- Named for Jim Thompson, the American who founded the Thai Silk Company in 1951 and disappeared in Malaysia's Cameron Highlands on 27 March 1967; his nephew Henry B. Thompson III inherited the estate and donated the house and antiques to the foundation (James H.W. Thompson Foundation).
- Building: a four-storey structure of about 3,000 square metres designed by the Bangkok studio Design Qua (ArtAsiaPacific); ArchDaily gives 3,800 square metres, lists steel, concrete and brick, and says the site was an outdoor car park serving the house museum, with an L-shaped new structure and an existing building forming a courtyard.
- On site: two exhibition galleries, a rooftop space, a library, a cafe and a museum shop (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Visiting: open every day 10am to 6pm; admission 50 baht for the art centre alone, 250 baht with the Jim Thompson House Museum; members and children under 10 enter free (Jim Thompson Art Center). No reporting found of an accessibility statement.

FUNDING
Jim Thompson Art Center: self-funded by its foundation: stated by the foundation
- The James H.W. Thompson Foundation says it is a non-profit that "relies entirely on its own funding to carry out its activities". This is stated by the foundation; no source opened shows where that money comes from.
- No reporting found that house-museum ticket income pays for the art centre, and none of a state, corporate or private donor.
- No reporting found of annual reports or accounts. The foundation states its board has seven members and lists six current trustees: Vudthi Nathamethikul, Pinaree Sanpitak, Pawapun Bunnag Booth, Punthumadee Katawandee, Rachaporn Choochuey and Devid Teh. The seventh is not named.
- No reporting found of a current link between the foundation and the Thai Silk Company.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is reporting on the 2021 reopening and listings.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is stated by its foundation to be its own, with no named outside funder, so it cannot be traced further. The weak points are that the source of the foundation's income, its accounts and its seventh trustee are not published. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes accounts that show how far house-museum admission pays for the art centre.
