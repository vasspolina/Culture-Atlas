NEW HOLLAND ISLAND: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: new-holland-island-st-petersburg
Website: https://newhollandsp.ru/en/information (checked 2026-10-24; working; the visit page returned HTTP 404)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural and public space on a man-made island in central St Petersburg between the Moika river and the Admiralty canals, run by the non-profit ANO New Holland (project website; Wikipedia).
- Peter the Great founded the island in 1719. It served the Russian Navy as a warehouse and administrative site, a ship-model testing site, a naval prison and a radio station (project website).
- The Ministry of Defence left the buildings in 2004. A redevelopment plan by Foster + Partners that followed stalled (Wikipedia).
- New Holland Development won the bid to restore and redevelop the island in November 2010 (project website). The island partly reopened to the public in July 2011 (Prospect).
- Dasha Zhukova chose the design by the New York firm WORKac (Prospect). Her Iris Foundation organises the summer programme (project website).
- On site: a park, Garden Walk, Herb Garden, beach, playgrounds, and restored buildings including the Foundry, the Commandant's House, the Bottle House and House 12 (project website). House 17, a former warehouse on the Moika, was due to open in June 2026 with an exhibition space, bookshop, concert hall and restaurant (Business Petersburg, 14 May 2026).
- No reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility details: the visit page was not available.

FUNDING
New Holland Island: privately funded: Roman Abramovich's company group and the Iris Foundation
- Millhouse LLC, founded by Roman Abramovich, won the 2010 tender to restore the buildings and reorganise the island (Fontanka, 8 June 2022). The Iris Foundation, created by Abramovich and Dasha Zhukova, produced the public space concept (Fontanka).
- Abramovich committed "at least" 12 billion roubles for a seven-year redevelopment and received a long-term lease; his spokesman called the figure a "preliminary estimate" (Prospect, 2011).
- By February 2024, more than 12 billion roubles had been invested and three of five construction stages were complete. New Holland Development, with 5.86 billion roubles of charter capital, was fully owned by Protonius Investments Ltd, which the paper describes as generally considered controlled by Abramovich; its participants were then hidden in the Russian company register (Business Petersburg, Evgenia Ivanova, 28 February 2024).
- The Art Newspaper reported in April 2024 that Russian media said the company was still controlled by Abramovich, and that New Holland had become an independent non-profit.
- No reporting found of public money paid into the project, or of a published sponsor list.

Red flags: sanctions on Roman Abramovich and work suspended
- The UK sanctioned Abramovich a week before the EU did on 15 March 2022. The EU cited his "privileged access" to Vladimir Putin and his stake in the steel group Evraz (click2houston, 15 March 2022).
- In June 2022 the project's press service said restoration and construction work had stopped. Unverified: Fontanka links the stop to the freezing of Abramovich's assets in mid-March 2022; the project would not say, and the city government did not answer (Fontanka, Alina Ampelonskaya, 8 June 2022).
- In August 2023 the management company told Fontanka the project was still "frozen" with no start date (Fontanka, Galina Boyarkova).
- Business Petersburg, which reports that three of five stages were complete, does not mention sanctions against the project.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of New Holland found in art media. Coverage is news reporting; the one critical essay is from 2011 and predates the sanctions.
1. Prospect: Shaun Walker, "Rebuilding Russia" (2011)
URL: https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/essays/49432/rebuilding-russia
- Asks why Abramovich committed funds before a design competition or feasibility studies, and notes the payback period could be optimistic.
- Suggests Russia's wealthy are expected to invest in national projects and stay out of politics, but says the terms of any deal are not public.
- General magazine, not art press; written before the sanctions.
2. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky (2024)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/04/26/head-of-new-holland-art-centre-in-st-petersburg-founded-by-dasha-zhukova-and-roman-abramovich-resigns-after-husbands-social-post
- News report, not criticism: general director Roxana Shatunovskaya resigned on 25 March 2024 after her husband, Nikolay Konashenok, posted online about the Crocus City Hall attack; he was detained and on 17 April 2024 listed by the Russian government as a "terrorist and extremist".
- Does not mention sanctions.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Prospect essay says it is unclear how much room Russia's wealthy have to refuse investing in state-backed projects (Shaun Walker, 2011).
- New Holland is connected to this through Abramovich's long-term lease and 12 billion rouble commitment.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind New Holland is private and comes from Abramovich's companies; no public funder is reported. The weak points are the 2022 EU and UK sanctions on Abramovich, the 2022 work stoppage that Fontanka linked to them, the hidden ownership register, and the Russian state's action against a director's husband in 2024. Watch whether House 17 opens and how the project's ownership is disclosed.
