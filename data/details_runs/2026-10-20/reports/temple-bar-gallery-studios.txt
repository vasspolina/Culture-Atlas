TEMPLE BAR GALLERY + STUDIOS: Dublin, Ireland
ID: temple-bar-gallery-studios
Website: https://www.templebargallery.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and artists' studio complex at 5 to 9 Temple Bar, Dublin 2, in the city centre (templebargallery.com).
- Founded by artists in 1983, "one of the first DIY artist-led initiatives in Ireland" (stated by the space). It began in a former shirt factory (Wikipedia).
- The name comes from the Temple Bar district; the pages read give no other naming history.
- Building: overhauled by McCullough Mulvin Architects and completed in October 1994; it now holds a gallery and thirty artist studios (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: exhibitions, studio membership, international residencies including an exchange with the Helsinki International Artist Programme since 2007, and talks and events. In 2022 it curated Niamh O'Malley's Irish presentation at the 59th Venice Biennale (Wikipedia).
- It is a registered charity (20034375) and a company limited by guarantee. Its board is elected by artist members (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: gallery open Tuesday to Saturday, 11am to 6pm, and Sunday, 12pm to 4pm (templebargallery.com). No admission fee is listed in the pages read. No reporting found of accessibility details; the site links an Accessibility page that was not read.

FUNDING
Temple Bar Gallery + Studios: mainly public: the Arts Council of Ireland, plus city and culture grants and corporate partners
- The Arts Council is its principal funder. Grant funders: Dublin City Council, Culture Ireland, the Finnish Institute in the UK and Ireland, Dublin UNESCO City of Literature and Project Ireland 2040. Corporate partners: Henry J Lyons, Arthur Cox and Dublin Port. RTÉ Supporting the Arts also supports it (stated by the space, no amounts or years).
- Wikipedia says it relies heavily on Arts Council funding. In 2010 the gallery dismissed its director Marian Lovett and said an Arts Council budget cut made her post redundant; in 2012 the Employment Appeal Tribunal upheld her unfair dismissal claim and awarded €30,000 (Wikipedia, single secondary source).
- Artists pay for studio memberships of three or six years, or one-year associate studios (Wikipedia). Corporate and supporter memberships and donations are also offered (stated by the space).
- Audited accounts for 2025 are posted on the site but the PDF was not read, so no income figures were verified.
- Board (templebargallery.com): Richard Lyons (chair since January 2024), Joe Prendergast (vice chair), Michelle Darmody, Joe Hanly, Austin Hearne, Léann Herlihy, Anne Mathews, Paul McAree, Peter McGovern, Isabel Nolan, Lye Ogunsanya, Geraldine Shanley and Laura Redmond.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Village magazine (credited to Village, no named author), "Temple Bar - Dublin's crony quarter" (22 August 2013): a polemic about the Temple Bar Cultural Trust (TBCT), which it calls a private company managing a Dublin City Council property portfolio, whose sole shareholder is the Dublin City Manager. It cites a Dublin City Council audit "released in March" with 59 recommendations "while not imputing any wrongdoing".
- It lists "Temple Bar Galleries" among organisations that "availed of lucrative arrangements and loans", with no amounts or dates for that entry. It does not use the name Temple Bar Gallery + Studios, so the article does not show that the two are the same body. Village is a news magazine, not art press, and the piece is one publication's allegation.
- Connection: the gallery lists the Temple Bar Cultural Trust among its partners.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public arts funding, mainly from the Arts Council, with a few Irish corporate partners and no flagged funder found. The weak points are the thin published detail on amounts and the unverified 2013 allegation about the wider Temple Bar arts network, which does not name the gallery. Worth watching are the 2025 accounts, which were not read here.
