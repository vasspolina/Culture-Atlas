NY CARLSBERG GLYPTOTEK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: ny-carlsberg-glyptotek
Website: https://www.glyptoteket.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Art and antiquities museum at Dantes Plads 7, Copenhagen (museum website).
- Built from the private collection of the brewer Carl Jacobsen, first shown in 1882 at his villa; he donated it in 1888 to the Danish State and the City of Copenhagen, and the purpose-built museum opened on 1 May 1897 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Vilhelm Dahlerup's 1897 red-brick building, a 1906 wing by Hack Kampmann, a 1996 addition by Henning Larsen, and a central Winter Garden with a copper-and-wrought-iron dome (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Greek, Roman and Etruscan antiquities, more than 1,900 Egyptian pieces, French 19th-century art including all of Degas's bronzes, and Danish Golden Age painting; more than 10,000 works (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Mondays; Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday until 21:00. Adults DKK 150 at the museum (135 online), under 27 and students DKK 120, under 18 free, free entry on the last Wednesday of each month. The visit page gives no accessibility details (museum website).

FUNDING
Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek: foundation-funded: the Carlsberg Foundation and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation, plus a Møller family foundation
- Carlsberg Foundation and Ny Carlsberg Foundation: announced on 5 March 2026 DKK 1.5 billion, DKK 750 million each, to restore the listed buildings; the project budget is DKK 2 billion. The Ny Carlsberg Foundation gave a further DKK 80 million in 2016 to 2025 for design studies (Carlsberg Foundation).
- Origin of the money: the Carlsberg Foundation was established in 1876 by J.C. Jacobsen and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation in 1902 by Carl and Ottilia Jacobsen (Carlsberg Foundation). Carl Jacobsen transferred his Ny Carlsberg brewery to the Carlsberg Foundation in 1902 (lex.dk).
- A.P. Møller og Hustru Chastine Mc-Kinney Møllers Fond til almene Formaal: DKK 150 million for the dome and Winter Garden. It owns A.P. Møller Holding, which holds a large stake in Mærsk (TV 2 Kosmopol). The museum closes to visitors from 2028 for the work.
- Augustinus Fonden: supported the 2022 purchase of Gauguin's "La neige à Copenhague" with the Ny Carlsberg Foundation and Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond; no amount given (Ny Carlsberg Foundation). No reporting found of a 2025 Augustinus Fonden grant of DKK 1.53 million.
- No reporting found of the state operating grant or ticket income.

Red flags: tobacco money (Augustinus Fonden)
- Augustinus Fonden owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (Wikipedia), which held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group on 1 May 2025; the investor page lists no other holder above 5% except C.W. Obel with more than 10% (Scandinavian Tobacco Group).

Red flags: antiquities bought from dealers tied to looting
- Hecht: the museum bought Etruscan and Greco-Roman objects from the dealer Robert Hecht (ArtsJournal, 2009).
- Italy: on 5 July 2016 the museum and Italy announced an agreement to return objects; a Politiken list named 501 objects and fragments (Chasing Aphrodite).
- Türkiye: on 26 November 2024 the museum decided to return a bronze head of Septimius Severus, bought in 1970, saying it came from Bubon (The Local Denmark).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No review criticises the museum's programme or funders. The criticism found concerns how it acquired antiquities, in a blog on the antiquities trade and in news outlets.
1. Chasing Aphrodite: no byline shown, "The Danish Connection: Holding on to Loot at the Ny Carlsberg Glypotech of Copenhagen" (2013, updated 2016)
URL: https://chasingaphrodite.com/2013/03/14/the-danish-connection-holding-on-to-loot-at-the-ny-carlsberg-glypotech-of-copenhagen/
- Says Giacomo Medici's sentence and seized Polaroids linked Glyptotek objects to looting, and that the museum first denied buying from Medici or associates after 1970, then shifted as documents emerged.

- Italy found the Glyptotek harder to resolve matters with than American museums, over objects bought from Hecht. Archive excerpt only.
3. The Local Denmark: "Denmark to return severed head of ancient statue to Türkiye" (2024)
URL: https://www.thelocal.dk/20241127/denmark-to-return-severed-head-of-ancient-statue-to-turkiye
- Director of collections Rune Frederiksen said in May 2023 "we are not as sure as we perhaps were 25-30 years ago." News report, not criticism by a critic.

BOTTOM LINE
The restoration money comes from the foundations the Jacobsen family created and from a Møller family foundation. The weak points are Augustinus Fonden's 2022 gift, whose parent holds a large share of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, and acquisitions from dealers later tied to looting, settled with Italy in 2016 and Türkiye in 2024.
