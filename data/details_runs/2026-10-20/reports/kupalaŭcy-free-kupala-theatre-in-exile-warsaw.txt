KUPALAŬCY (FREE KUPALA THEATRE IN EXILE): Warsaw, Poland
ID: kupalaŭcy-free-kupala-theatre-in-exile-warsaw
Website: https://reform.news/ad-kupala-skaga-da-kupala-ca-shljah-volnaj-trupy-da-zhynjoj-u-shjesc-sezona

OVERVIEW
- Belarusian-language theatre troupe in exile; the question of a stationary venue was never resolved, and its seventh season (2026) has premieres in Kraków, Wrocław and Warsaw (Reform.news, 31 January 2026; Reform.news, 30 July 2026).
- Formed in 2020 after dismissals at the Yanka Kupala National Academic Theatre in Minsk in August; Wikipedia gives 26 August 2020 as the founding date, and the troupe debuted on 6 October 2020 with "Tutejshyya" at the OK16 venue, without sets or costumes (Reform.news, 31 January 2026).
- Wikipedia says director-general Pavel Latushko was fired on 17 August 2020 and 58 actors and staff were dismissed on 26 August 2020.
- Plans to start in Lviv in March 2022 ended when the full-scale war began a month earlier, and the troupe moved to Poland (Reform.news, 31 January 2026).
- Programme: repertory shows in Belarusian with Polish subtitles; a theatre week in Warsaw in February 2025 presented four productions at the Scena Przodownik of the Dramatic Theatre (Nasha Niva, 1 February 2025). "Minsk Sea" is staged in Kraków on 15 September 2026, and "Next Stop" in Wrocław on 25 September and in Warsaw on 26 and 27 September (Reform.news, 30 July 2026).
- Belarus declared the troupe an "extremist formation" at the end of 2024 (Reform.news, 31 January 2026); its YouTube channel, social networks and logo were declared extremist earlier, as reported on 19 February 2024 (Belarus-NAU).

FUNDING
Kupalaŭcy: ticket sales, diaspora tax donations and project support
- Ticket sales: actor Oleg Garbuz said "we live entirely on tickets, our financing is zero, simply none at all" (translation); the fee is 500 zloty (about 125 dollars) per performance (Reform.news, Dzianis Martsinovich, 31 January 2026).
- Diaspora tax donations: the February 2025 Warsaw theatre week was partly funded by Belarusians in Poland who directed their 1.5% income tax contribution to the troupe through a designated organisation (Nasha Niva).
- Polish Ministry of Culture: an honorary award granted through the Culture Promotion Fund; Pavel Latushka, deputy head of the United Transitional Cabinet and former director of the Kupala Theatre, sent the request to the minister Hanna Wróblewska in October 2024 and she approved it on 17 March 2025 (Reform.news, 18 March 2025).
- Düsseldorf, February 2024: the page "The Kupalaucy in Düsseldorf" says the event was organised with the financial support of Pavel Latushka's team, the National Anti-Crisis Management (NAM), and the Free Belarus Museum; it was posted by "Admin of the NAM" on 19 February 2024 (Belarus-NAU).
- Rada Kultury: its help was rather an exception, and the funding in question was European money received through it (Reform.news, 31 January 2026).
- The Polish Theatre Institute residency programme for Belarusians, run since 2022, ended in 2024; the production "Order of the White Mouse" was funded by crowdfunding from Belarusians (Reform.news, 22 December 2024, titles translated).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the troupe is small and mixed: ticket sales, diaspora tax donations, a Polish Ministry of Culture honorary award and support from Pavel Latushka's team, the National Anti-Crisis Management, for the Düsseldorf event. Belarus declared the troupe an extremist formation at the end of 2024.
