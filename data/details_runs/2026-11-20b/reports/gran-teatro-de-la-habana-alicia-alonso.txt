GRAN TEATRO DE LA HABANA ALICIA ALONSO: Havana, Cuba
ID: gran-teatro-de-la-habana-alicia-alonso
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Theatre complex on the block bounded by San Rafael, San José, Consulado and the Paseo del Prado in Havana (EcuRed, state encyclopedia, archive copy of 20 February 2026).
- The Teatro Tacón, designed by Antonio Mayo and officially opened on 15 April 1838, stood on the site before (EcuRed).
- The Centro Gallego bought the site in 1906 for its social palace, a neo-baroque building by the Belgian architect Paul Beleu that opened in 1915, with marble groups by Giuseppe Moretti on the main façade (EcuRed).
- From 1985, on Alicia Alonso's initiative, the whole building took the name Gran Teatro de La Habana, keeping "García Lorca" for the main hall (EcuRed).
- It houses the Ballet Nacional de Cuba, the Teatro Lírico Nacional and the Ballet Español de Cuba, and has been the main stage of the Havana International Ballet Festival since 1960 (EcuRed).
- The Council of State renamed it for Alonso in September 2015 (EcuRed; EFE in Diario las Américas).
- After a capital repair from 2013 to 2015 it reopened on 1 January 2016 with President Raúl Castro and Alonso present. EFE reported a fully renovated hall seating 1,300, an enlarged lobby, a new café and a small performance space.
- In February 2023 it closed again for termite damage in the main hall and loose sections of the upper slab, according to its management (CiberCuba).
- In October 2025 Canal Habana's Hola Habana reported that the main hall and other spaces were in "un proceso de inversión constructiva" (a building investment process, our translation) (Periódico Cubano; CiberCuba).

FUNDING
Gran Teatro de La Habana Alicia Alonso: state-funded: Ministry of Culture through the Consejo Nacional de las Artes Escénicas
- EcuRed lists the theatre among the institutions of the Consejo Nacional de las Artes Escénicas (CNAE), a Ministry of Culture council created on 1 April 1989 with its own legal personality. Its president, Lillitsy Hernández Oliva, explained the 2025 repair on Canal Habana's Hola Habana (Periódico Cubano; CiberCuba).
- 2013 to 2015 repair: façades, lobbies, boxes, roof and stage floor were restored, with new seating, curtains, air conditioning, acoustics, stage machinery, rehearsal rooms, a recording studio and more than 20 dressing rooms (EcuRed). Culture Minister Julián González described the restoration at the reopening (EFE). Periódico Cubano calls it a multimillion investment; CiberCuba says it cost millions of pesos.
- 2025 repair: Hernández named air-conditioning leaks, a deteriorated third-floor roof and damage from sound vibrations (Periódico Cubano; CiberCuba).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two reports from independent outlets describe repeated decay and repairs within a decade of a costly restoration. None is art criticism.
1. Periódico Cubano: "Cubanos tras reparación del Gran Teatro de La Habana: '¿Otra vez? Se robaron los recursos'" (2025)
URL: https://www.periodicocubano.com/cubanos-estallan-por-reparacion-del-gran-teatro-de-la-habana-otra-vez-se-robaron-los-recursos/?amp=1
- Says the work has drawn criticism of management and the use of public money, and calls for more transparent management.
- Unverified: social media users quoted by the paper say money and materials from the earlier repair were stolen.
2. CiberCuba: "The Gran Teatro de La Habana is being repaired once again after a failed million-dollar investment" (2025)
URL: https://en.cibercuba.com/node/312823
- Calls the 2013 to 2015 investment failed and describes rubbish, urine stains and people sleeping at the entrance.
3. Havana Times: Juan Diego Rodriguez (14ymedio), "Electrocution Risk from Leaks at Havana's National Theater" (2023)
URL: https://havanatimes.org/features/electrocution-risk-from-leaks-at-havanas-national-theater/
- Quotes the bassoonist Osmany Hernández, who wrote in March 2023 that the Gran Teatro and the Karl Marx Theater were closed because of termites. Mostly about the Teatro Nacional.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and raises no funder flag. The weak point is how that public money is spent and accounted for. Worth watching: the cost and reopening date of the current works.
