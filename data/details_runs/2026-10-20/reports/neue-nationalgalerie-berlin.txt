NEUE NATIONALGALERIE: Berlin, Germany
ID: neue-nationalgalerie-berlin
Website: https://www.smb.museum/museen-einrichtungen/neue-nationalgalerie (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of 20th-century modern art at the Kulturforum in Berlin, south of the Tiergarten park (Wikipedia). It is one of the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin.
- Opened on 15 September 1968 in West Berlin (Wikipedia).
- Architect Ludwig Mies van der Rohe designed the building and its sculpture garden: a black-painted pre-stressed steel roof plate about 1.8 m thick on eight cruciform steel columns, a glazed facade and a granite floor (Wikipedia).
- David Chipperfield Architects renovated it; the museum closed in 2015 and reopened in August 2021 with a Calder exhibition. The budget was €101 million and the project ended at €140 million (Wikipedia).
- The collection focuses on 20th-century art, with strengths in Cubism, Expressionism, the Bauhaus and Surrealism; only a small part is on display at a time (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Monday, open Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday 10:00 to 20:00, Friday 10:00 to 18:00; described as fully wheelchair accessible, with the step-free entrance on the left side of the building at Reichpietschufer (museum website). Admission is free on the first Thursday of the month from 16:00 to 20:00 under the Art4All programme (museum website). No reporting found of current ticket prices on the museum's pages.

FUNDING
Neue Nationalgalerie: mainly public: federal government and the German states through the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation
- The Stiftung Preußischer Kulturbesitz receives annual federal allocations set in the federal budget law; the states' grants are set in financing agreements between the federal government and the states (§ 3 StiftPKG). No reporting found of a budget line for this museum alone.
- Volkswagen Group sponsors the Art4All free-admission programme, and the Mercedes-Benz Art Collection provides a long-term loan of works (museum website).
- No reporting found of any other corporate sponsor or donor list.

Red flags: 2024 dispute over a Nan Goldin slide
- The Goldin retrospective "This Will Not End Well" opened on 22 November 2024 and ran until 6 April 2025. At the opening Goldin criticised Germany's stance on Israel and Gaza; protesters shouted down director Klaus Biesenbach, and Culture Minister Claudia Roth said she was "appalled" (taz, dpa report; translation).
- Goldin said she was told to remove a credit slide in her slideshow that expressed solidarity with Gaza, the West Bank and Lebanon, and that the museum warned including it could jeopardise its funding (Hyperallergic, Hanno Hauenstein, 11 December 2024). Unverified: Goldin's claim that the museum threatened its funding; no document in the piece shows a funder's instruction.
- The museum said on 6 December 2024 that the slide was inserted without consultation, did not originally name Israeli victims, and was later removed by Goldin's studio team (Hyperallergic).
- Leaked internal emails show Biesenbach consulting senior staff at the culture ministry and the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation about whether to include the revised slide; on 4 December the museum confirmed to Goldin it would be included (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2024 Goldin affair; most coverage reports the dispute rather than argues a case against the museum, and one review praises the show.
1. Hyperallergic: Hanno Hauenstein, interview with Nan Goldin (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/nan-goldin-speaks-out-on-censorship-of-berlin-show
- Goldin says the museum wanted no sign of her politics in the show; emails with Biesenbach, including leaked internal ones, contradict parts of the museum's account.
- Names Biesenbach, the culture ministry and the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation.
2. Flash Art: Gea Politi, review of "This Will Not End Well" (2024)
URL: https://flash---art.com/2024/11/nan-goldin/
- Praises the show as the first comprehensive Goldin survey in Germany and a fitting venue given her ties to Berlin.
- Reports that Goldin, Hito Steyerl, Candice Breitz and Eyal Weizman withdrew from a planned symposium, and that Biesenbach defended it as independently planned.
3. The Left Berlin: "The Fabrication of a Scandal: Nan Goldin at Berlin's Neue Nationalgalerie" (2024)
URL: https://theleftberlin.com/the-fabrication-of-a-scandal-nan-goldin-at-berlins-neue-nationalgalerie/
- Argues German institutions and media enforce a state-backed narrative (Staatsräson) that silences critics of Israel, and calls the museum's symposium a "prophylactic" (a claim it attributes to Goldin).
- Portrays Biesenbach as yielding to political pressure. Openly pro-Palestinian advocacy outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Roth, the federal culture minister, and the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation sided with the museum against Goldin's statements; Roth rejected her "intolerably one-sided views" (taz; translation).
- The museum belongs to the foundation that receives the federal allocations, and Roth said it was right that the museum and the foundation contradicted Goldin (taz; translation).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public, with Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz named only for a free-admission programme and a loan. The weak point is the Goldin dispute, where the leaked emails show officials consulted on a slide that the museum says Goldin inserted unannounced; Goldin calls this coercion and the museum disputes it. Watch for any published account of the ministry's role.
