LOUVRE ABU DHABI: Abu Dhabi, UAE
ID: louvre-abu-dhabi-abu-dhabi
Website: https://www.louvreabudhabi.ae/ (checked 2026-10-24; blocked automated access with HTTP 403, so opening hours and prices were not read)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum on Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, overseen by the Abu Dhabi Department of Culture and Tourism (Wikipedia).
- Created under a UAE-France agreement signed on 6 March 2007 that allows use of the Louvre name. Inaugurated on 8 November 2017 by Emmanuel Macron, Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan and Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, and open to the public three days later (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Jean Nouvel; its dome is made of 7,850 aluminium stars, with engineering by BuroHappold (Wikipedia).
- Collections are mainly modern and contemporary European art; the museum has been criticised for its European focus (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia reports over one million visitors in its first full year and about 2 million by 2019.
- No reporting found of opening days, admission or accessibility, because the site could not be read.

FUNDING
Louvre Abu Dhabi: state-owned: the government of Abu Dhabi pays France for the Louvre name, loans and management advice
- Under the 2007 agreement Abu Dhabi agreed to pay €400M for the Louvre name, €190M for art loans, €75M for special exhibitions, €165M for management advice and €25M for a Zayed-named space in Paris. A 2017 French Senate estimate put total inflation-adjusted payments at €974.5M (Wikipedia).
- The agreement runs for 30 years (The Art Newspaper). Wikipedia says the term was extended in 2023 from 2037 to 2047.
- The developer of Saadiyat Island, the Tourism Development and Investment Company (TDIC), is government-owned (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors in the sources opened.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Overseen by the Abu Dhabi Department of Culture and Tourism, a government body of an Emirati state (Wikipedia).

Red flags: forced or exploited labour on Saadiyat Island
- HRW documented unsafe conditions causing accidents and deaths, passport confiscation, poor housing, very low wages and sometimes unpaid wages in its February 2015 report on Saadiyat Island (Bénédicte Jeannerod, HRW commentary, 8 November 2017).
- A 2016 International Labour Organization review found passports still confiscated and wages withheld despite laws banning it (HRW commentary).
- In 2013 several hundred migrant workers who struck were deported and banned from re-entering (HRW commentary).
- TDIC says it prioritises workers' welfare, citing a worker village and an Employment Practices Policy from 2009 and PricewaterhouseCoopers monitoring since 2011. PwC's 2015 survey, published in January 2016, noted tougher rules on recruitment-fee reimbursement but flagged missing internal compliance staff and inconsistent penalties (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- HRW's Sarah Leah Whitson said the Louvre was the least responsive of the institutions she had dealt with on these issues, and said of the UAE: "You have the bizarre outcome of one of the richest countries in the world paying workers $200 a month" (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism, mostly about migrant labour on the construction site and the sale of the Louvre name. Coverage dates from 2014 to 2017.
1. The Art Newspaper: Hannah McGivern, Aimee Dawson and Anna Somers Cocks, "Louvre Abu Dhabi opens but questions over welfare of migrant workers persist" (2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/11/15/louvre-abu-dhabi-opens-but-questions-over-welfare-of-migrant-workers-persist
- Reports that rights groups say abuses persisted on the site and that two journalists filming Pakistani workers were held for more than 50 hours.
- Sets TDIC's welfare claims against HRW's view; Gulf Labor renewed its boycott of Saadiyat institutions.
2. Hyperallergic: Claire Voon, "Gulf Labor Criticizes Guggenheim's Silence on Migrant Workers' Rights" (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/gulf-labor-criticizes-guggenheims-silence-on-migrant-workers-rights
- Gulf Labor urges supporters to keep criticising the Guggenheim, the Louvre, NYU and any institution that does not respect workers' rights.
- Centres on the Guggenheim; names the Louvre Abu Dhabi as part of the Saadiyat Island campaign and in the coalition's letter.
3. Apollo: Caroline Rossiter, "Problem child? The Louvre Abu Dhabi is previewed in Paris" (2014)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/?p=103141
- Finds the preview show a disjointed mix of objects and notes that the French press has been largely hostile.
- Cites Andrew Ross on harsh conditions for workers; some French critics feel the Louvre is selling its name.
- Argues that the Emiratis pay a fair price for the art and expertise.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a state payment: Abu Dhabi pays France for the Louvre name, loans and advice, and the museum sits inside a Gulf government department. The weak points are the documented exploitation of migrant workers on Saadiyat Island and the dispute between TDIC and HRW over how far reforms went. Watch for any independent labour reporting since 2017 and the 2023 extension of the name licence.
