LALIT KALA AKADEMI: New Delhi, India
ID: lalit-kala-akademi
Website: https://lalitkala.gov.in (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- India's national academy of fine arts, headquartered at Rabindra Bhavan, Ferozshah Road, New Delhi, in a building shared with the Sangeet Natak Akademi and the Sahitya Akademi (Wikipedia).
- Set up by the Government of India and inaugurated on 5 August 1954; registered under the Societies Registration Act 1860 on 11 March 1957 (Akademi website). Maulana Azad, then Minister of Education, set it up (Wikipedia).
- The name is Hindi for "fine arts academy"; it is not named for a person.
- Rabindra Bhavan holds its art galleries, which the Akademi lets artists book for exhibitions; the Akademi runs regional centres in Chennai, Lucknow, Kolkata, Bhubaneswar and Garhi (Delhi), with sub-centres at Shimla, Ahmedabad, Agartala and Patna. A new Kolkata centre building was inaugurated on 15 January 2026 (Akademi website).
- Programmes: the annual National Exhibition of Art, the Triennale-India (run since 1968), scholarships and fellowships, a bilingual journal and the Garhi studios (Wikipedia). Its permanent collection holds over seven thousand artworks, including works by M.F. Husain, S.H. Raza and Somnath Hore (Akademi website).
- Visiting: the reference library is open Monday to Friday, 9.30 a.m. to 6.00 p.m. The Akademi site does not state gallery hours, admission or accessibility on the pages read.

FUNDING
Lalit Kala Akademi: public: the Union Ministry of Culture
- The Ministry of Culture is its parent body and funder (Wikipedia). The Akademi site links a financial assistance page but gives no budget figures.
- The collection was built from the Annual Exhibition, the Kala Mela art fair, workshops and camps, the Artists Aid Fund and the Chairman's Discretionary Funds (Akademi website).
- No reporting found of any private, corporate or foreign funder.
- No reporting found of published audited accounts; the Akademi site links an Annual Reports page.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little critique from art-press critics. The criticism found is about government control of the Akademi, from a news and politics outlet, and it rests mostly on anonymous artists and insiders.
1. The AIDEM, republished by SabrangIndia: Apurva Roy Chatterjee, "Murder(s) at and of Lalit Kala Akademi" (2024)
URL: https://sabrangindia.in/murders-at-and-of-lalit-kala-akademi/
- Argues the Ministry of Culture eroded the Akademi's autonomy: a January 2024 order signed by Under Secretary Suman Bara curtailed Chairman V. Nagdas's powers, contract staff were dismissed in February 2024 (29 per The AIDEM) and the files for a full-time Secretary were held up.
- Says the 63rd National Exhibition, opened 28 August 2023, was cut short so the galleries could show 2,500 photographs for the Prime Minister's birthday, and Culture Minister Arjun Meghwal led that event.
- Artists who spoke privately told the author the ministry wants to do away with the Akademi's autonomy; the piece also links the dispute to Sanskar Bharti, of which Nagdas had been a member, and to a called-off Ayodhya art project. Much rests on anonymous artists and insiders, and the piece presents it as their view.
2. Wikipedia (not art press): "Lalit Kala Akademi", History and Chairman sections
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalit_Kala_Akademi
- Says the government took over management in April 2015, citing complaints of alleged administrative and financial irregularities; that after the administration was charged with misappropriation of funds in mid-2017 the ministry set up a three-member inquiry committee, whose order was withdrawn on 16 August 2017; and that a Delhi High Court public interest petition alleging misappropriation led the court on 24 August 2017 to ask the ministry for an explanation, to which the article says it gave no reply.
- Records Vivan Sundaram's 1970s protest against the Triennale's international emphasis and Geeta Kapur's criticism that it extended international exhibitions with commercial interests.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Ministry of Culture notice of 6 April 2015, released by the Press Information Bureau (reproduced by Shabdankan): the Central Government invoked provisions of the Akademi's Memorandum of Association and took over management from 1 April 2015, appointing K.K. Mittal, IAS, Additional Secretary in the Ministry, as Administrator until further orders. It gave as reasons complaints of financial and administrative irregularities, a General Council and Executive Board that had not functioned since 2013 because of court cases, and a suspended Secretary.
- The Akademi is a government-funded body, and both the 2015 takeover and the 2024 curbs on its chairman came from its own ministry.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public and ultimately ministry money; no private funder or flagged industry is named. The weak points are the ministry's repeated takeover of management (2015) and curbs on the chairman (2024), and the lack of published accounts. Worth watching: who now runs the Akademi and whether the National Exhibition runs without interference.
