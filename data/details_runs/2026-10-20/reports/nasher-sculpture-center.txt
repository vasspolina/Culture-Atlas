NASHER SCULPTURE CENTER: Dallas, USA
ID: nasher-sculpture-center
Website: https://www.nashersculpturecenter.org

OVERVIEW
- Sculpture museum and garden at 2001 Flora Street in the Dallas Arts District, across from the Dallas Museum of Art (Wikipedia, the museum's own site).
- Founded from the collection of Raymond and Patsy Nasher, who began collecting sculpture in the 1950s; Raymond Nasher bought the land in 1997 and the Center opened in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Nasher family. The museum also awards the Nasher Prize, established in April 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Building by Renzo Piano, 55,000 square feet, on a 2.4-acre site with a garden of about 2 acres designed with landscape architect Peter Walker; the site was part of the old Caruth family farm (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, an outdoor sculpture garden terracing down to an open-air auditorium, and James Turrell's skyspace Tending (Blue), which the museum closed to the public at the artist's request after the nearby Museum Tower, built in 2012, intruded on its view (Wikipedia).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. Admission: adults $10, DART riders $8, seniors $7, students and educators $5; free for under 12s, military and first responders, SNAP EBT holders and members. Free on the first Saturday of each month. The museum aims to be a sensory-friendly environment, with assistive resources to reserve and Walk and Talk tours in English and Farsi (the museum's visit page).

FUNDING
Nasher Sculpture Center: built with the Nasher Foundation's $70 million; contributions were 99.8% of revenue in the year to June 2025
- Construction: the Nasher Foundation paid the entire $70 million cost of designing and building the museum (Wikipedia).
- Contributions were 99.8% of revenue in the fiscal year ending June 2025: revenue $13,986,319, expenses $13,988,918 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, from the Form 990).
- Foundations and government bodies the museum lists as partners: Abe Zale Foundation, The Buddy Taub Foundation, Burnham Foundation, Carl B. and Florence E. King Foundation, City of Dallas Office of Cultural Affairs, Communities Foundation of Texas, The Dorothea L. Leonhardt Fund, The Eugene McDermott Foundation, Fundación Almine y Bernard Ruiz-Picasso para el Arte, The Goss-Michael Foundation, Henry Luce Foundation, Hoblitzelle Foundation, Llagas Foundation, Lyda Hill Foundation, Meadows Foundation, National Endowment for the Arts, Pro Helvetia, TACA, Texas Commission on the Arts and Texas Instruments Foundation.
- Hoblitzelle Foundation and Lyda Hill Foundation gave capital grants for security, HVAC, IT, flooring and garden infrastructure; the Dallas Foundation is presenting sponsor of Nasher Prize Celebration Month, programming around the 2017 laureate Pierre Huyghe (museum's foundation and government support page).
- Corporate partners the museum lists: Aston Martin of Dallas, Ben E. Keith Beverage, Christie's, Dallas Art Fair, Dallas Convention & Visitors Bureau, The Dallas Morning News, DFW International Airport, D Weddings, KXT, Target and Trammell Crow Center.
- 2013 citywide exhibition Nasher XChange raised more than $3 million. The Eugene McDermott Foundation and the Nasher Foundation were presenting sponsors; Hoblitzelle Foundation gave $225,000; donors of $100,000 or more were the Dallas Foundation, The Joule Hotel + Shops, Texas Monthly, the Donna M. Wilhelm Family and an anonymous donor. Among donors of $25,000 or more were Frost, J.P. Morgan and Wells Fargo (museum press release, 20 October 2013).
- 2026 exhibition Rauschenberg Sculpture: leading support from the Robert Rauschenberg Foundation; further support from Frost Bank, an anonymous donor, Gladstone, Gianfranco D'Amato, Leo Katz, Katherine Sachs, Thaddeus Ropac and the Dallas Tourism Public Improvement District (museum event page).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hyperallergic (Samuel Rowlett, "How to Destroy a James Turrell", 18 June 2013) criticises Museum Tower, a 40-story condominium in the Dallas Arts District: Turrell declared Tending (Blue) destroyed because the tower blocks the sky view from the skyspace, and the tower's reflected light affects the Nasher's gallery conditions, keeps works on paper from being shown and harms trees in the Peter Walker garden.
- Unverified: Raymond Nasher had a "gentleman's agreement" with the Dallas Police and Fire Pension System, whose real estate arm wanted to build the tower, to keep it under 20 stories, and the design changed after he died in 2007, from an insider account that Rowlett relays and does not confirm.
- Connection to the museum: Museum Tower stands near the Nasher and its glare falls on the museum and its garden; the article criticises the tower's developer, not the Nasher.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum was built with a $70 million gift from the Nasher Foundation, and contributions were 99.8% of its revenue in the year to June 2025 (ProPublica). The museum names foundations, government arts bodies including the National Endowment for the Arts and the Texas Commission on the Arts, and corporate sponsors on its support pages.
