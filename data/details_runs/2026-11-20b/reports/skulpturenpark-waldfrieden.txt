SKULPTURENPARK WALDFRIEDEN: Wuppertal, Germany
ID: skulpturenpark-waldfrieden
Website: https://skulpturenpark-waldfrieden.de/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Sculpture park at Hirschstraße 12 in Wuppertal, in a wooded area in the hills above the Elberfeld and Barmen districts (EGHN); German Wikipedia puts it at 14 hectares in the Hesselnberg district of Barmen.
- Founded on the private initiative of the British sculptor Tony Cragg and his wife, the artist Tatjana Valsang; Cragg bought the abandoned Waldfrieden estate in 2006 and the park opened in 2008 under the Cragg family's non-profit foundation (park's site), in September 2008 (German Wikipedia).
- Named for the Villa Waldfrieden on the estate, also called "Villa Herberts", where the chemicals entrepreneur Kurt Herberts lived from the late 1940s until his death in 1989 (German Wikipedia).
- The lacquer manufacturer Herberts bought the estate in 1940; its house of 1894 was destroyed in air raids in 1943, and the artist and architect Franz Krause built the listed two-storey villa and its grounds on the old basement between 1947 and 1950; it now holds the foundation's archive and offices (park's villa page).
- In 2012 Cragg acquired about 5 hectares of forest south of the park from the City of Wuppertal in exchange for a wooded plot beyond Buschstraße; the plot was fenced and the Wupperweg and Residenzenweg footpaths through it were rerouted (German Wikipedia).
- On site: about 40 outdoor sculptures, three exhibition halls (the second opened on 20 September 2013, a round third hall in October 2017), the Café Podest, and the concert series KlangArt and Tonleiter, both running since 2009 (German Wikipedia).
- The permanent collection includes works by Cragg, Joseph Beuys, Joan Miró, Thomas Schütte, Henry Moore and Eva Hild (park's site); EGHN names permanent loans of works by Richard Deacon, Markus Lüpertz, Wilhelm Mundt, Jaume Plensa and Norbert Kricke.
- Current season: "Tony Cragg: Recent Works" and "Farbe, Farbe! Willi Baumeister, Franz Krause, Oskar Schlemmer", both 12 September 2026 to 3 January 2027 (park's site).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 18:00 from 14 March to 31 October and 11:00 to 17:00 from 1 November to 3 January; closed 24 and 31 December and from 4 January to 28 February 2027. Day ticket EUR 15, EUR 9 for full-time students and severely disabled visitors, EUR 10 each in groups of 10 or more, season ticket EUR 50 or EUR 40 reduced, free for ages 18 and under. The park says its hilly terrain gives only limited access for disabled visitors; pets are not allowed (park's site).

FUNDING
Skulpturenpark Waldfrieden: privately funded: Tony Cragg and the Cragg family's foundation, plus tickets, tours, venue hire and donations
- Ownership: the EGHN page lists "Owner: Tony Cragg" and "Management: Cragg Foundation".
- The Cragg Foundation is a non-profit foundation in Wuppertal, set up by the Cragg family in 2005 and legally recognised on 13 December 2005; its board is Tatjana Cragg (chair), Tony Cragg (deputy chair) and Michael Brämer, and it runs the park as a private museum (German Wikipedia). The park's legal notice names the foundation as publisher, with VAT number DE814982444.
- Building and site: Cragg bought the estate in 2006 and began redesigning the grounds and renovating the long-empty buildings that year (park's site); the 2012 extension came through the land exchange with the City of Wuppertal (German Wikipedia).
- Earned income: day and season tickets, group tours at EUR 90 and EUR 120 for foreign-language tours, public tours at EUR 5 plus entry (park's site), and hire of the Café Podest and the villa for celebrations, conferences and events (park's renting page).
- Donations: the foundation's page asks visitors to support its work with a donation, through PayPal (park's site).

BOTTOM LINE
The park rests on one family's money: Tony Cragg owns the land and his family's foundation runs it, with tickets, hire and small donations on top. The foundation publishes no accounts or funder list, so how far it depends on Cragg's own art income is not shown. The 2012 land exchange with the city is the one public deal on record.
