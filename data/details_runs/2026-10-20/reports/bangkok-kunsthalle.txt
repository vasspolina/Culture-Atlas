BANGKOK KUNSTHALLE: Bangkok, Thailand
ID: bangkok-kunsthalle
Website: https://khaoyaiart.org/ (checked 2026-10-20; the old khaoyaiart.com page redirects here, and the page shows only headings and images, no text)

OVERVIEW
- Art centre at 599 Soi Phantha Chit (Pantachit Alley), Pom Prap Sattru Phai, in Bangkok's Yaowarat (Chinatown) district.
- Founded by the art patron Marisa Chearavanont through her organisation Khao Yai Art; opened in January 2024 with Michel Auder's "Nine Plus Five" (12 January to 11 February 2024).
- Occupies the former headquarters of the printing house Thai Watana Panich: three connected buildings, the oldest over seven decades old, abandoned after a fire gutted the upper floors (ArtAsiaPacific; Wikipedia gives 2001 for the fire).
- The brutalist complex covers nearly 65,000 square feet; in 2024 only the ground floor was in use, with plans to expand upward (Wikipedia; Art Basel).
- Led by director and curator Stefano Rabolli Pansera, formerly director of Hauser & Wirth St. Moritz; it runs about four shows a year, residencies, talks and screenings, and is the urban counterpart of Khao Yai Art Forest, about 150 km east of Bangkok.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday 2pm to 8pm, closed Monday and Tuesday; entry free (Time Out Bangkok). No accessibility details found.

FUNDING
Bangkok Kunsthalle: private: the founder's philanthropy, from the Chearavanont family of CP Group
- Marisa Chearavanont is married to Soopakij Chearavanont, chairman of the Charoen Pokphand (CP) Group, which ArtReview calls Thailand's largest agribusiness (ArtAsiaPacific; ArtReview).
- The art press describes the project as philanthropic; no source found gives an amount, a budget or a separate foundation's accounts.
- No reporting found of public grants, sponsors or partners; the website shows no text.

Red flags: protest by artn't at the opening show
- During Korakrit Arunanondchai's "nostalgia for unity" in 2024, two people from the Chiang Mai collective artn't appeared in the show in T-shirts reading "WORLD CLASS ARTIST, WORLD CLASS GALLERY, WORLD CLASS CORPORATION, WORLD CLASS EXPLOITATION" and "THIS COUNTRY IS YOUR GALLERY FOR EXHIBITING THE ART OF MONOPOLY" (ArtReview).
- A member told ArtReview "the origins of art funding deserve critical examination."
- CP Foods, controlled by CP Group, was named in a 2015 US class action against Costco over prawns raised on fishmeal from slave-crewed boats, following a 2014 Guardian investigation (Taipei Times, from The Guardian); a US court later dismissed it, and CP Foods said it does not own the boats named (Wikipedia).

Not related: the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC), the city-backed centre in Pathum Wan whose 2025 censorship under Chinese embassy pressure has nothing to do with the Kunsthalle.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical piece; other coverage is admiring features on the founder and director.
1. ArtReview: Pearamon Tulavardhana, "Will the New Bangkok Kunsthalle Fire up the Art Scene?" (2024)
URL: https://artreview.com/will-the-new-bangkok-kunsthalle-fire-up-the-art-scene/
- "Philanthropy is crucial in a country where government funding for the arts is limited," but "concerns must be raised when an act of philanthropy may end up doing something else entirely."
- Asks whether the space can start "critical conversations about the issues of exploitation and environment" around it, and notes rising rents in Chinatown and its disconnect from the neighbourhood.
- Links the show's smoke to real haze from burning by contract corn farmers, without naming CP Group.
2. Art Basel: Nick Hackworth, "Can a kunsthalle turn Bangkok into Southeast Asia's premier art hub?" (2024)
URL: https://www.artbasel.com/stories/kunsthalle-art-forest-turn-thailand-into-southeast-asia-premier-art-hub
- Not criticism: an admiring feature that does not mention CP Group.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the founder's own, and behind it sits the Chearavanont family's CP Group fortune; nothing is published on amounts or structure. The weak points are the undisclosed amounts and the CP Group connection, which artn't targeted at the opening show. Worth watching: whether Khao Yai Art publishes accounts or names funders.
