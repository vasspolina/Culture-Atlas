MUSEO DE ARTE CONTEMPORÁNEO DE SANTIAGO: Santiago, Chile
ID: museo-de-arte-contemporáneo-de-santiago-santiago
Website: https://mac.uchile.cl/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum of the University of Chile, run through the university's Faculty of Arts, with two sites: Parque Forestal and Quinta Normal (Diario Financiero, 31 July 2021; University of Chile news, 17 May 2021).
- Operating since 1947 (Francisco Brugnoli, El Mostrador, 22 December 2014).
- Both sites together cover more than 5,000 m², with 32 staff in 2021 (Diario Financiero, 31 July 2021).
- Daniel Cruz Valenzuela, a visual artist and University of Chile academic, became director on 15 May 2021, succeeding Francisco Brugnoli, who led the museum for 23 years (University of Chile news, 17 May 2021).
- The Quinta Normal site was damaged in the earthquake of 27 February 2010 and its programme was suspended until structural reports were issued; the Parque Forestal facade and portico were also damaged (Radio UChile, 3 March 2010).
- In 2021 the museum was closed for 14 months; Diario Financiero reported that Parque Forestal was set to reopen on 19 August and Quinta Normal on 25 August 2021, with a capacity of 25 people per hour.

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Santiago: public: the University of Chile
- Brugnoli described the MAC as a public institution under the University of Chile, outside the national DIBAM museum network (El Mostrador, "Opinión: El MAC no puede ser gratis", 22 December 2014).
- Budget in 2014: about 200 million pesos a year, around 140 million from the Faculty of Arts and an extra 60 million from the university's Rectoría the last year, covering salaries and basic costs for both sites. Because it belongs to the university and not the DIBAM, it could not receive funds from the Consejo de la Cultura. Admission was 600 pesos, or 400 for students and older adults (Radio UChile, Rodrigo Alarcón, 23 December 2014).
- Director Daniel Cruz called the museum's financing "terrible" in 2021 and said repairing the roofs costs about 180 million pesos. He hoped to secure support from the government and private donors, and planned a "Círculo MAC" of external supporters (Diario Financiero, Sofía García-Huidobro, 31 July 2021).
- The virtual MAC project, announced with Cruz's appointment, is funded by a Fondart grant (University of Chile news, 17 May 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the University of Chile, which in 2014 gave about 200 million pesos a year through its Faculty of Arts and Rectoría. A Fondart grant funds the virtual MAC project.
