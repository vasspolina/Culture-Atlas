FONDATION VINCENT VAN GOGH ARLES – Arles, France
Website: https://www.fondation-vincentvangogh-arles.org (in search results, live pages indexed; direct fetch not permitted in this run, 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition foundation at 20 rue de la Liberté, Arles, in the Hôtel Léautaud de Donines. Pairs Van Gogh works (often lent by the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam) with contemporary artists.
- Roots in a 1983 association started by curator Yolande Clergue; Luc Hoffmann relaunched the project in 2008; established under its current name in 2010; opened 2014 (Wikipedia; the space's own corporate brochure). "Reconnue d'utilité publique".
- Artistic director Bice Curiger (2014 to 2025) was succeeded by Jean de Loisy from April 2025; Curiger joins the board after her last show closes 26 October 2025 (The Art Newspaper France, 19 March 2025). Maja Hoffmann is board president.

FUNDING
Fondation Vincent van Gogh Arles: Hoffmann family–backed, with small corporate memberships.
- Founding backing from Luc Hoffmann (Wikipedia). Where the money ultimately comes from: the Hoffmann family's Roche fortune; Maja Hoffmann belongs to the Roche founding-family shareholder pool (Wikipedia).
- Corporate membership: "Ami" €500 and "Partenaire" €1,500, with a 60% French corporate tax reduction under article 238 bis CGI (stated by the space, brochure).
- The partners page (Hiscox, Blackwall Green, Christie's in the existing record) could not be retrieved in this run, so those names are not re-confirmed.
- No reporting found of annual accounts, budget figures or public subsidy amounts.

Red flags: Roche founding-family money
- Roche pleaded guilty in 1999 to a worldwide vitamin price-fixing conspiracy and paid a $500M US criminal fine (existing Museum Tinguely record; Roche Wikipedia). This concerns the company, not the foundation.
- The Hoffmann family's role in Arles (LUMA, hotels, restaurants) prompted the local magazine L'Arlésienne to question the democratic legitimacy of private actors shaping the city (Le Temps, Swiss daily). Commentary, not an allegation of wrongdoing.

Not related: Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam — a separate Dutch museum that lends works to the foundation.
Not related: Fondation Van Gogh (Vincent van Gogh Foundation, Amsterdam) — the Dutch foundation that owns the Van Gogh family collection.

BOTTOM LINE
No reporting found of wrongdoing by the foundation itself. It is backed by the Hoffmann family's Roche-derived fortune and relies on French tax-deductible patronage; it publishes no accounts. Worth watching is the concentration of Hoffmann influence over Arles cultural life.
