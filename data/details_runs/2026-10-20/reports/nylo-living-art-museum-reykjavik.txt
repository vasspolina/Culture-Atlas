NÝLÓ (THE LIVING ART MUSEUM / NÝLISTASAFNIÐ): Reykjavík, Iceland
ID: nylo-living-art-museum-reykjavik
Website: https://www.nylo.is

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run museum and exhibition space for contemporary art in Marshall House (Marshallhúsið), Grandagarður 20, Reykjavík; the Strætó 14 bus stops at Grandi (museum website).
- Founded in 1978 by a group of artists, mainly linked to Fluxus and conceptual art, with a gallery added in 1981 (Wikipedia). Reykjavík City's 2016 notice says 27 artists founded it; Wikipedia says 26.
- The collection holds over 2,200 works from the 1960s to the present, donated by artists, collectors and people connected to the museum (museum website); Giulia Nestola writes that the museum is not allowed to buy artworks (museum website, 22 July 2026).
- Run by a five-member board and a three-member secondary board, elected yearly and mostly voluntary (Wikipedia).
- The museum moved to Breiðholt in 2014 and to Marshall House in 2017, sharing it with the artist-run space Kling & Bang and Ólafur Elíasson's studio (Wikipedia); the museum website also lists the restaurant La Primavera.
- Marshall House was built in 1948 as the Faxi herring factory, named for Marshall Plan aid from the United States, which helped finance construction; it stood empty after the factory closed until it opened as an arts venue in 2017 (museum website).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00, closed between exhibitions (museum website).

FUNDING
Nýló: supported by the Icelandic state, the City of Reykjavík, and private and voluntary donations
- The museum is "supported through collaboration between the Icelandic State, the City of Reykjavík, and private and voluntary donations" (essay by Giulia Nestola on the museum website, 22 July 2026).
- The Reykjavík Culture and Tourism Council listed Nýló for 9.5 million ISK under its cooperation agreements for 2016; the long-term agreements are subject to City Council approval and the annual budget (Reykjavík City, 19 January 2016).
- Marshall House: Reykjavík City leases the building from its owner HB Grandi for 15 years and sublets it to Nýló, Kling & Bang and Ólafur Elíasson. The city guarantees the lease; HB Grandi plans the renovation (Reykjavík City, 29 January 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Nýló comes from the Icelandic state, the City of Reykjavík, and private and voluntary donations, according to the museum; the city listed it for 9.5 million ISK in 2016. Its home since 2017, Marshall House, is owned by HB Grandi and was leased through the city under a 2016 agreement.
