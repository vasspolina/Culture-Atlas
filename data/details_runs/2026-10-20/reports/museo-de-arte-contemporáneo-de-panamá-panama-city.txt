MUSEO DE ARTE CONTEMPORÁNEO DE PANAMÁ (MAC PANAMÁ): Panama City, Panama
ID: museo-de-arte-contemporáneo-de-panamá-panama-city
Website: https://macpanama.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on Calle San Blas in Ancón, Panama City, next to the Instituto Panameño de Habilitación Especial and the family courts (museum site).
- Founded in 1962 as the Panamanian Institute of Art (Panarte), a non-profit with no building of its own; it became the Museo de Arte Contemporáneo de Panamá in 1983 (Wikipedia).
- In 1983 the board bought and remodelled the old Masonic Temple in Ancón; Coqui Calderón and Graciela de Eleta led the work (Wikipedia). No reporting found of an architect.
- The collection holds Panamanian and Latin American work of the 20th and 21st centuries; Wikipedia gives both "more than 700" and "around 1,200" works.
- Programmes: temporary exhibitions, guided tours for groups and schools, workshops, an art appreciation course and the free monthly "Abierto Para Todos" day, supported by Bi Bank (museum site).
- A new headquarters is planned in the San Francisco district; an international architecture competition ran from 13 January to 29 March 2026, with the winner expected in May 2026 (News in America).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 17:00, closed Monday. Admission: B/.5.00 for Panamanians and residents, B/.2.50 for their students and retirees, B/.10.00 for foreigners, B/.5.00 for foreign students and retirees; free for children to 12 (stated by the museum). No reporting found of accessibility information.

FUNDING
MAC Panamá: private non-profit: corporate sponsors and fundraising events
- Wikipedia describes the 1983 purchase as funded by a campaign of contributions from many people and companies, plus a loan from the Panama Savings Bank.
- Humberto Vélez wrote in La Estrella de Panamá (2024) that the museum is a non-profit civil association run by private individuals who seek private sponsorship, and that the State delegated the economic and programme responsibility of the two main museums to private hands.
- Naturgy: on 13 April 2023, Naturgy country manager José Luis Lloret and MAC legal representative Antonio Murzi signed a sponsorship agreement covering the upkeep and operation of the museum's facilities, community relations, staff training and other projects set by the board. Amount and duration are not stated (paid press release in La Prensa).
- Global Bank is named as one of the main sponsors (News in America, 2026). Bi Bank supports the monthly free day (museum site).
- The museum runs a gala and an auction for fundraising; the page names no donors or amounts.
- No reporting found of annual accounts or a named board.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Naturgy, a natural gas and electricity group that distributes gas in Spain, Italy and Latin America (Wikipedia), sponsors the museum's maintenance and operation under the 2023 agreement.
- The press release states no end date. No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at this sponsorship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. One opinion piece in a newspaper criticises how Panama's two main museums are governed.
1. La Estrella de Panamá: Humberto Vélez, "¿De quiénes son los museos?" (2024)
URL: https://www.laestrella.com.pa/vida-y-cultura/de-quienes-son-los-museos-HB7399678
- Argues the State has handed the economic and programme responsibility of the two main museums, the MAC and the Museo del Canal, to private hands, which he sets in a history of "poco compromiso y empatía" toward museums.
- Newspaper opinion by an artist, not art press; it criticises the governance model, not any sponsor.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is privately run and sponsor-funded, and the only energy company named is Naturgy, with an open-ended 2023 deal and no published amount. The weak points are the lack of named donors, accounts or a board in the site text, and the dependence on corporate sponsors. Worth watching: who funds the new headquarters.
