MUSEO NACIONAL DE BELLAS ARTES DE LA HABANA: Havana, Cuba
ID: museo-nacional-de-bellas-artes-de-la-habana-havana
Website: https://www.bellasartes.co.cu (checked 2026-11-20; did not load)

OVERVIEW
- Cuba's national fine arts museum, a public museum under the Ministry of Culture, in two buildings near the Paseo del Prado: the Palacio de Bellas Artes for Cuban art and the Palacio del Centro Asturiano for international art (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Founded in February 1913 under President José Miguel Gómez and opened on 28 April 1913 as the Museo Nacional de la República, an encyclopedic museum; it specialised in fine arts in 1964 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Palacio de Bellas Artes: designed by Alfonso Rodríguez Pichardo and opened in 1954 on the site of the former Mercado de Colón (Spanish Wikipedia).
- After the 1959 revolution it took in all or part of the collections of Julio Lobo, Oscar Cintas, José Gómez Mena and other collectors (Spanish Wikipedia).
- From 1996 the Council of State backed an expansion into three buildings, led by Jorge Candelaria and the museum designer José Linares; Fidel Castro opened the new spaces on 18 and 19 July 2001 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- In February 2014 the Consejo Nacional de Patrimonio Cultural confirmed a theft of works from the museum's Antonio Rodríguez Morey administrative building; the canvases had been cut from their frames (OnCuba).
- The director is Jorge Fernández Torres (Spanish Wikipedia; OnCuba, 2021).
- For the 15th Havana Biennial (2024 to 2025) it showed exhibitions in both buildings; Fernández Torres and José Manuel Noceda curated the show in the international art building (Two Coats of Paint).
- Visiting: Spanish Wikipedia lists 09:00 to 17:00 Wednesday to Saturday and 09:00 to 14:00 on Sundays.

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes de La Habana: mainly public: the Cuban state, under the Ministry of Culture, with foreign donors of works
- It depends on the Ministry of Culture (Spanish Wikipedia).
- 1996 to 2001 expansion: Spanish Wikipedia gives an estimated investment of 14.5 million dollars. Cuba Internacional, a Prensa Latina magazine, put the original budget for the three buildings at 31 million pesos, about 40 percent of it in foreign currency (IPS, July 2001).
- January 2010: the Paris-based Brownstone Foundation, run by the collector Gilbert Brownstone, gave nine drawings and prints by artists including Picasso, Warhol and Miró, the first of 120 works it planned to give. Brownstone dedicated the gift to the "Cinco Héroes" (Cuban Five), Cuban officials held in the United States on espionage charges (Europa Press in La Vanguardia).
- Spanish Wikipedia notes that Brownstone later put the gift at 130 works and a museum statement of 2021 at 108.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself. The main case is the 2021 artists' request; other pieces criticise state cultural policy around the museum.
1. The Art Newspaper: Daniel Cassady, "Cuban artists ask Museum of Fine Arts to remove their work from display while Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara remains in government custody" (2021)

- According to 27N's letter, the official response was that Otero Alcántara "is neither detained nor prosecuted".
2. The New York Review of Books: Esther Allen, "The Art of the Possible at Havana's Bienal" (2019)
URL: https://www.nybooks.com/online/2019/06/15/the-art-of-the-possible-at-havanas-bienal/
- Praises the two shows at the museum, one curated by Fernández Torres; criticises the Biennial's setting and Decree 349 more than the museum.
3. e-flux: Solveig Font, Coco Fusco and others, "Can a Biennial Respect Difference in a Country that Represses Dissidence? On the Havana Biennial" (2024)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/627502/can-a-biennial-respect-difference-in-a-country-that-represses-dissidence-on-the-havana-biennial
- Links the 2014 theft to the commercialisation of Cuban art under the Ministry of Culture.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 24 May 2021: Tania Bruguera, Tomás Sánchez, Marco Castillo, Jorge Luis Marrero, Sandra Ceballos, Celia-Yunior and Reynier Leyva Novo handed director Fernández Torres a letter asking that their works on show be covered and their other works removed from the museum's website until Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara was freed and the police siege on him ended (OnCuba; The Art Newspaper).
- In 2019 Bruguera stayed away from the 13th Biennial, whose two central exhibitions were at the museum, saying the Ministry of Culture was diverting resources to "whitewash its international image" after Decree 349 (NYRB).
- Esther Allen (NYRB, 2019) calls Decree 349, signed by Miguel Díaz-Canel in April 2018 and requiring government approval before artists exhibit, the legal basis for the arrest of Otero Alcántara and other artists.
- Unverified: six Cuban arts professionals claim in e-flux (2024) that the 2014 theft of 71 works and their appearance for sale in Miami led to the ouster of Culture Minister Rafael Bernal Alemany.
- The connection: the museum received the 2021 protest letter, and it hosts the state-run Biennial's main shows.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is Cuban state money and raises no funder flag. Its weak points are the security of its collection and its place at the centre of state cultural policy that artists contest. Worth watching: whether its website returns with reports or accounts.
