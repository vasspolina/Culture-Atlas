CITÉ INTERNATIONALE DES ARTS: Paris, France
ID: cit-internationale-des-arts-paris
Website: https://www.citeinternationaledesarts.fr

OVERVIEW
- Artist residence in Paris with 310 live-in studios on two sites: the Marais, at 18 rue de l'Hôtel de Ville, about 280 artists at a time, and Montmartre, at 24 rue Norvins, around thirty studios (stated by the Cité).
- Officially inaugurated on 20 May 1965 in the Marais; the idea dates to 1937, when the architect Félix Brunau and the Finnish artist Eero Snellman proposed it; the Montmartre site opened in 1971 (stated by the Cité).
- A nonprofit private foundation recognised as a public-benefit body under French law, by decree of 14 September 1957 (stated by the Cité).
- The City of Paris signed a long-term lease for the Marais site in 1950 and owns the Montmartre site, which it made available to the Cité (stated by the Cité).
- The Marais site has the Galerie, opened in 1995, which hosts two group exhibitions per year, and a Vitrine for installations; the Montmartre site combines studios, apartments and wooded grounds on the Butte Montmartre, near the Sacré-Coeur (stated by the Cité).
- Visiting: Open Studios at the Marais every Wednesday from 18:00 to 21:00; at Montmartre twice a year, in February and June 2027 (stated by the Cité).

FUNDING
Cité internationale des arts: partner-funded: a cooperative model of public and private residency partners
- The Cité describes a cooperative model: public and private, French and foreign partner organisations give financial support to artists and to the Cité through a single contribution, and more than 135 partners have been long-standing supporters (About page). The Partners page says the Cité runs residency programmes co-constructed with over 300 partners in France and worldwide.
- Residency partners named on the Partners page include the Ministère de la Culture, the Académie des beaux-arts, the Direction des affaires culturelles of the City of Paris, the Ministry of Culture and Science of the State of North Rhine-Westphalia, the City of Reykjavík, the City of Zürich (Cultural Affairs), the Province of Salzburg, the Samsung Foundation of Culture, the Icatu Group, the Neuflize OBC Corporate Foundation, Sacem, the Fondation Daniel and Nina Carasso, the Fondation Antoine de Galbert and the Patiño Foundation.
- The About page states that André Malraux, then Minister of Cultural Affairs, played a decisive role in the Cité's development.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One item, a 2007 dispute over expulsion orders at the Norvins site.
1. paris-art.com: La rédaction, "Remous à la Cité internationale des arts" (23 February 2007)
URL: https://www.paris-art.com/23-02-07-remous-a-la-cite-internationale-des-arts/
- Resident artists published a statement on 21 January 2007 denouncing expulsion orders issued without notice; on 25 January the Cité's president called it "désinformation totale et gratuite" (total and gratuitous disinformation, my translation) and said the artists had finished their residencies and must vacate their studios for electrical and boiler repairs at 24 rue Norvins.
- On 31 January 2007 the City of Paris said the Cité was ending temporary residencies in part of the Cité Norvins and planned works in the vacated studios.
- Unverified: the artists' statement, which paris-art.com did not publish, is known from the article only through Simone Brunau's reply; she denies visiting studios when residents were absent and reserving studios for privileged individuals, and says she directed the Cité for 41 years on a voluntary basis.
- paris-art.com removed the artists' statement at Brunau's request and published her reply.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Cité is partner funding: the Cité describes a cooperative model in which more than 135 partners have been long-standing supporters, and residency partners include the Ministère de la Culture, the Académie des beaux-arts, the Samsung Foundation of Culture, the Icatu Group and the Neuflize OBC Corporate Foundation. The City of Paris signed a long-term lease for the Marais site in 1950 and owns the Montmartre site.
