NATIONAL MUSEUM OF PAKISTAN: Karachi, Pakistan
ID: national-museum-of-pakistan-karachi
Website: none on record (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Pakistan's national museum in Burns Garden, on Dr. Ziauddin Ahmed Road, Karachi.
- Sources differ on its start: Wikipedia gives 1950 for its establishment and 1951 for the first display in Frere Hall, where it replaced the Victoria Museum; Dawn dates the museum to 1969.
- It moved to the present building in 1970.
- Eleven galleries cover the Indus Valley, Gandhara sculpture, Islamic art, miniature painting, coins and manuscripts, and ethnology; a Quran Gallery holds over 300 copies of the Quran.
- The collection includes about 58,000 old coins and some 70,000 publications of the Archaeology and Museums Department.

FUNDING
National Museum of Pakistan: public: the federal government, in a standing dispute with Sindh province
- The federal government ran the museum until devolution under the 18th Amendment passed control to Sindh.
- The federal culture ministry took the matter to the Supreme Court; then Chief Justice Saqib Nisar ruled for the ministry and control returned to the federal government on 17 April 2019 (Dawn, 25 February 2023).
- Sindh filed a review petition. In February 2023 Sindh culture minister Syed Sardar Ali Shah told the provincial assembly that control would pass to the province soon.
- While Sindh held the museum it started two repair and maintenance schemes and raised its funds (The News, 2013).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 24 June 2024 Sindh culture minister Zulfiqar Ali Shah said the museum was "in a dilapidated condition" and that the Centre should hand it to the province for better upkeep (Express Tribune).
- Sindh's antiquities department called the earlier federal takeover unconstitutional (The News, 2013).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is in federal government hands and no funder is objected to. The weak point is the tug of war between Islamabad and Sindh since devolution, during which a provincial minister described the building as dilapidated. Worth watching: the review petition and any transfer to Sindh.
