SERRALVES MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Porto, Portugal
ID: serralves-museum-of-contemporary-art
Website: https://www.serralves.pt (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum inside the Serralves estate in Porto, run by the Fundação de Serralves (Wikipedia).
- The Portuguese State bought the estate in 1986; the park and villa opened to the public on 29 May 1987, the foundation was incorporated in 1989 and the museum opened on 6 June 1999 (Wikipedia).
- The foundation's stated aim is "to raise the general public's awareness concerning contemporary art and the environment" (Wikipedia). Wikipedia says there are currently 181 founders, including companies, individuals and institutions.
- The Casa de Serralves, built between 1925 and 1944 for the second Count of Vizela and designed by José Marques da Silva, is an exhibition space; Álvaro Siza Vieira designed the museum building, with 4,500 square metres of exhibition space in 14 galleries; the park of about 18 hectares, landscaped by João Gomes da Silva, holds sculptures by Richard Serra and Claes Oldenburg (Wikipedia).
- Serralves projected about 1.24 million visitors for 2024, against about 1.1 million in 2023 (Notícias ao Minuto, 17 December 2024, title translated).

FUNDING
Serralves Museum of Contemporary Art: mixed: Portuguese State subsidy, plus corporate founders and patrons
- Serralves says the sponsorship contributions of the Founders, alongside the subsidy support provided by the State, play a key role in the foundation's financial equilibrium (Serralves, Maecenas and Sponsors page).
- The 2024 Patron Founders list names 59 contributors to the Annual Fund, including Amorim Investimentos e Participações, Bankinter, Bial, Brisa, Caixa Geral de Depósitos, CTT, El Corte Inglés, Fidelidade, GALP Energia, Inditex, Mota-Engil, REN, Sonae and Vodafone Portugal, and the municipalities of Aveiro, Braga, Chaves, Faro, Guarda, Matosinhos, Torres Vedras and Vila Nova de Gaia (Serralves).
- Corporate sponsors commit generally for several years as Institutional Sponsor, Sponsor of Activities or Sponsor of the Collection, and receive Portuguese tax benefits: the donation counts as an expense increased by 130% for annual contracts and 140% for multi-year contracts (Serralves).

Red flags: fossil fuel funder, GALP Energia
- GALP Energia, SGPS, S.A. is listed as a Patron Founder of the foundation in the 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024 lists, each list naming the founders who contributed to the Annual Fund that year (Serralves).
- Wikipedia describes Galp as a Portuguese multinational energy company whose activities cover oil and gas exploration and production, refining and retail, with co-generation and renewables, and calls it the largest oil and gas group in Portugal as of 2020.

Red flags: Mapplethorpe exhibition, 2018
- Twenty of the 179 planned works were left out of the Robert Mapplethorpe exhibition at Serralves, and two rooms were restricted to visitors under 18, who could enter only with an adult. A Serralves spokesman told Público the works were removed by the curator, João Ribas (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris and José da Silva, 24 September 2018).
- Ribas, appointed director in January 2018, stepped down; the article quotes him as saying he "is no longer able to lead the institution" (The Art Newspaper, 24 September 2018).
- More than 150 artists and arts professionals, including Wolfgang Tillmans, Tania Bruguera and Stuart Comer, signed an open letter to the foundation's president Ana Pinho condemning the removals and age restrictions (The Art Newspaper).
- A Serralves Foundation statement of 28 September, added to the article, said the 20 works were left out "at the sole decision of the curator" and do not "correspond to any form of censorship", and that its board "agreed and continues to agree" with Ribas's proposal for a reserved area at the end of the exhibition for the most sensitive works. Serralves itself, through its curator, removed the works.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One report in the art press records the 2018 censorship row and the open letter against the foundation.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris and José da Silva, "Wolfgang Tillmans and Tania Bruguera pledge support for censored Serralves Museum director" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/09/24/wolfgang-tillmans-and-tania-bruguera-pledge-support-for-censored-serralves-museum-director
- The open letter says that invoking the Portuguese law on the classification of "pornographic" material to restrict access is "disingenuous and insulting".
- Includes the foundation's statement that the removal was the curator's sole decision and not censorship.
- Names the Serralves Foundation and its president Ana Pinho.

BOTTOM LINE
Serralves is a foundation financed by Portuguese State subsidy support and by a corporate founders' and patrons' base whose contributions carry Portuguese tax benefits. The documented weak points are GALP Energia, an oil and gas company that was a Patron Founder from 2021 to 2024, and the 2018 Mapplethorpe row, in which 20 works were left out at the curator's decision, the director stepped down and the foundation denied censorship.
