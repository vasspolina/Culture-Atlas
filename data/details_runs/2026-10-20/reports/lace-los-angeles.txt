LACE (LOS ANGELES CONTEMPORARY EXHIBITIONS): Los Angeles, USA
ID: lace-los-angeles
Website: https://welcometolace.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit exhibition space and archive on Hollywood Boulevard (6522 Hollywood Blvd, per its site); it describes a 3,500 sq. ft. gallery storefront and a 350 sq. ft. Project Room.
- Founded in 1978 by thirteen artists; Joy Silverman became the first executive director in 1982 and Sarah Russin is the current executive director (Wikipedia). It began downtown (Getty Research Institute) and reached Hollywood in 1993 with help from the CRA (stated by the space; Wikipedia gives 1994).
- Named for what it does: LACE is short for Los Angeles Contemporary Exhibitions.
- Building: the gallery and two adjacent spaces sit in a 1919 building owned by the City of Los Angeles. It is being rebuilt as the arts building of the Hollywood Arts Collective, with a redesigned street front, a skylit gallery and mezzanine offices.
- On site after the rebuild: LACE, the under-99-seat Glorya Kaufman Theatre, a courtyard and nonprofit arts offices. The Getty Research Institute holds the LACE records, 1978 to 2018.
- Visiting: the site says LACE is temporarily closed for renovation; its temporary offices are at 6464 Sunset Blvd, Suite 1070. No admission, hours or accessibility details are posted.

FUNDING
LACE: mixed: private arts foundations and a small state grant
- Andy Warhol Foundation: $80,000 over two years from 2015 for the Emerging Curators series, the Project Room and a summer residency (LACE, 30 July 2015). It also makes LACE's Lightning Fund regrants possible; in the 2026 round LACE ran the open call and the fund gave $6,000 each to nine artists and $7,500 to one collective (Warhol Foundation, 9 February 2026).
- Ruth Foundation for the Arts: $150,000 over three years, 2025 to 2028 core grant (LACE).
- Teiger Foundation: a 2025 grant for three years of programming led by Selene Preciado, Curator and Director of Programming (LACE; amount not stated).
- Getty Foundation: support for LACE's project in Pacific Standard Time (PST ART) 2024, the exhibition "(Un)disciplinary Tactics" by Beatriz da Costa, shown at the Los Angeles Municipal Art Gallery from 7 September 2024 to 5 January 2025. LACE says it was one of 45 institutions the Getty Foundation supported; no amount is stated. LACE's about page names the Getty and Warhol Foundations as major funders.
- California Arts Council: $14,400 Artists in Communities grant, 2018 to 2019.
- Accounts (ProPublica, EIN 95-3397305): fiscal year to September 2025, revenue $858,218, expenses $780,290, net assets $1,762,740.
- No reporting found of a Mike Kelley Foundation grant, a corporate sponsor or a list of individual donors.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Getty)
- Getty Foundation support: LACE's PST ART 2024 exhibition ran from 7 September 2024 to 5 January 2025 with Getty Foundation support (LACE press release and exhibition page). The amount is not stated.
- Endowment source: J. Paul Getty left nearly $660 million of Getty Oil stock to the trust, which received the bequest in 1982 (Wikipedia). In 1984 the trust received a portion of the $10 billion proceeds from the sale of Getty Oil to Texaco (InfluenceWatch).
- The Getty Foundation is one of the J. Paul Getty Trust's programs (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of LACE found in art media. Coverage found is admiring or descriptive.
1. Hyperallergic: Abe Ahn, "The Getty Acquires the Archive of LACE, a Grassroots Alternative Art Space in Los Angeles" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/lace-los-angeles-contemporary-exhibitions-the-getty
- Not criticism: traces how the venue endured economic and cultural shifts in Los Angeles and presents the archive as a record of the city's art scene.
- Quotes Russin: "Right now we have four staff members and lots of interns"; the piece does not disclose the terms of the sale.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Knock LA, Farah Abouzeid, "'Affordable' Artist Living: The Plight of the Hollywood Arts Collective" (23 June 2025): tenants were told in September 2024 of a 7% rent increase, changed to 4% after tenant pressure, and 43 of 151 households had received eviction notices. The piece says the city-owned land was leased to the developer, Thomas Safran & Associates, for $1, and that promised artist amenities were never added.
- Connection: LACE is the arts-building tenant of the same Hollywood Arts Collective complex. The article does not mention LACE.

BOTTOM LINE
LACE's money is mostly arts foundation grants with small public support, and its tax filings are public through ProPublica. The one flagged source is the Getty Foundation, whose endowment comes from Getty Oil stock. Worth watching: how the Hollywood Arts Collective rebuild is paid for (no cost found) and the tenant complaints about the housing next door.
