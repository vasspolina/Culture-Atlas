OVERVIEW
- Museum and sculpture garden of the work of Isamu Noguchi at 32-37 Vernon Boulevard, Long Island City, Queens (Wikipedia).
- Chartered as The Isamu Noguchi Foundation and Garden Museum. It opened to the public in 1985, initially on a seasonal basis, and was the first museum of its kind established in America by a living artist (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder, the sculptor Isamu Noguchi, who bought a former photogravure plant and gas station across the street from his studio in 1974 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a two-storey building of about 24,000 square feet with a sculpture garden. A $13.5 million capital master plan was approved in 1999, and the museum reopened in June 2004 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of current opening hours, admission or accessibility terms: the museum's site blocked access.

FUNDING
Noguchi Museum: mainly self-generated: its own assets, merchandise sales and some city capital grants
- Form 990 for 2024: revenue $19,394,466, of which contributions were $1,822,009, sales of assets $7,985,372 and net inventory sales $6,191,425; expenses $11,800,874; net assets $79,074,060 (ProPublica).
- In 2022 the museum received $4.5 million in capital funding to restore Noguchi's 1959 studio and build a collection and archive building: about $1.5 million from Mayor Eric Adams and the rest from Queens Borough President Donovan Richards (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a published donor list, corporate sponsor list or annual report that could be opened.

Red flags: 2024 keffiyeh ban, firings and boycott
- ArtReview reported the museum introduced a dress code on 14 August 2024 barring political messages on staff clothing and emailed its 72 employees; The Art Newspaper said the policy was updated in August.
- A petition against it was signed by about two thirds of the 72-person staff (The Art Newspaper, Anni Irish, 11 September 2024) or by 54 staff (ArtReview). On 21 August, 14 staff walked off the job according to The Art Newspaper; ArtReview counted eight.
- Three gallery attendants were fired on 4 September 2024 after refusing to follow the dress code; Hyperallergic says they were fired for wearing keffiyehs (Valentina Di Liscia, 26 September 2024). A museum spokesperson told The Art Newspaper, "Given this pertains to personnel matters, we are unable to provide further details" (The Art Newspaper).
- On 8 September supporters rallied outside the museum, calling for director Amy Hau to step down (The Art Newspaper).
- Author Jhumpa Lahiri declined the 2024 Isamu Noguchi Award in support of the fired workers; a spokesperson said: "We respect her perspective and understand that this policy may or may not align with everyone's views" (Hyperallergic, 26 September 2024).
- Staff petitioned for a union vote with UAW Local 2110 (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan, 16 December 2024) and won a unanimous National Labor Relations Board election, announced on 13 January 2025. The museum said it "acknowledges and respects our staff's decision to unionize" (Hyperallergic, Isa Farfan, 15 January 2025).
- No reporting found of later developments such as reinstatement or a settlement.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum looks clean: it rests on its own assets, merchandise sales and city capital grants, with no sponsor of concern found. The weak point is governance of staff: the 2024 political dress ban, the three firings and the boycott by Jhumpa Lahiri. Watch whether the museum publishes a donor list and how its dealings with the new union develop.
