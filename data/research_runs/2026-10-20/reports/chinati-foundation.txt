CHINATI FOUNDATION – Marfa, USA
Website: https://chinati.org (pages loaded 2026-10-20; /about/support/ could not be fetched)

OVERVIEW
- Donald Judd established the foundation in 1986 on 340 acres of former Fort D.A. Russell in Marfa, Texas. It opened to the public in 1987. The Dia Art Foundation funded Judd's renovation of the fort buildings from 1978 (stated by the space).
- It holds permanent installations by Judd (100 mill-aluminum works in two artillery sheds, plus 15 outdoor concrete works), Dan Flavin (six barracks), John Chamberlain (a wool warehouse) and others, including Oldenburg/van Bruggen, Roni Horn, Ilya Kabakov, Carl Andre and Robert Irwin.
- Admission prices and accessibility details were not found in the sources retrieved. The capital campaign includes accessibility work at the Artillery Sheds.

FUNDING
Chinati Foundation: private foundations and individual donors, under 5% government money
- 2024 (Impala, drawn from IRS filings): revenue $6.4M, expenses $3.7M, assets $25.5M, net assets $25.3M. Government grants were under 5% of revenue. Top funders: Fidelity Investments Charitable Gift Fund ($2.2M, a donor-advised fund that does not identify the donor), The Brown Foundation ($2M) and The Ruth Stanton Foundation ($1.3M).
- "Art as Intended" capital campaign, begun in 2017 with a $16M goal; donor list dated 2 Oct 2026 (stated by the space). It includes Sarofim Foundation $2.5M; two anonymous donors, The Brown Foundation and The Chadwick-Loher Foundation at $1M each; and the Greenberg Fund, Ruth Stanton Foundation, Behnke and Cohn families and others at $500K–999K. The National Trust for Historic Preservation, the Texas Historical Commission and the Frankenthaler Climate Initiative gave $25K–99K.
- Start Small, the Covid-19 relief fund launched by Jack Dorsey, granted $1.25M in 2021 for preservation and job training for young women in West Texas (The Art Newspaper).
- Lannan Foundation gave general operating support in 2003 and an endowment challenge grant (from search results).
- The existing record says it "names no donors". The campaign page does name donors.

BOTTOM LINE
Funded by Texas family foundations (Brown, Sarofim), arts foundations and individual collectors, with very little public money. No flagged funder was found. Worth watching: large gifts through donor-advised funds and anonymous donors at the $1M level, which hide where the money comes from.
