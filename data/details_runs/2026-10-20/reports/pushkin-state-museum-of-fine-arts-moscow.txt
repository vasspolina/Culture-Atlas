PUSHKIN STATE MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS: Moscow, Russia
ID: pushkin-state-museum-of-fine-arts-moscow
Website: https://pushkinmuseum.art/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Federal state museum of European art at Volkhonka 12, Moscow, with a second building, the Gallery of 19th and 20th Century European and American Art, and branches in Nizhny Novgorod and Tomsk (museum site).
- Founded in 1912 by Ivan Tsvetaev, with the support of the philanthropist Yuriy Nechaev-Maltsov and the architect Roman Klein; first named the Alexander III Museum, renamed for Pushkin in 1937 (Wikipedia).
- Roman Klein and Vladimir Shukhov designed the main building, built from 1898 to early 1912 (Wikipedia).
- About 700,000 items, from Byzantine mosaics and icons to modern art (Wikipedia).
- Ekaterina Pronicheva was named director on 14 January 2026, announced by Culture Minister Olga Lyubimova; she previously ran the Vladimir-Suzdal Museum-Reserve (BFM). Marina Loshak, director since 2013, resigned on 20 March 2023 (Moscow Times).
- Visiting: Main Building ticket 700 roubles, 500 discounted; free for children 6 and under, disabled adults, wheelchair users and ICOM members; free on Thursdays for children 7 to 17 and Russian students. Fast-track entry for people with disabilities and over-70s. Opening hours were not on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Pushkin State Museum of Fine Arts: mainly public, with corporate sponsors and a board of state-linked companies
- The museum is a state museum; its board is chaired by a Deputy Prime Minister and includes the Culture Minister and a Moscow Deputy Mayor. No budget amounts were read.
- Sponsors named on the museum site, with no years or amounts: VTB (general sponsor), LG (partner), PSB (partner of the "Diamond Chariot: Buddhist Art of Russia" exhibition) and Yandex Eda (partner for developing the collection).
- Board of trustees, chaired by Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin: Culture Minister Olga Lyubimova; German Gref of Sberbank; Gazprombank; Vladimir Kalanda of Transneft; Mikhail Karisalov of Sibur; Russian Railways; Pyotr Fradkov, chairman of Promsvyazbank; Natalia Sergunina, Deputy Mayor of Moscow; and others.
- No reporting found of foreign or private-donor funding in the pages opened.

Red flags: Sanctions, sponsors and trustees
- VTB, the general sponsor, was placed on the US SDN list on 24 February 2022, a full blocking sanction (Kelley Drye).
- Gazprombank, a trustee, was placed on the US SDN list on 21 November 2024 to curtail Russia's use of the financial system for its war against Ukraine (Baker McKenzie).
- Sberbank, a trustee, faced US correspondent-account restrictions from 26 March 2022 and is not on the SDN list; Transneft, a trustee, is subject to restrictions on new equity and debt (Kelley Drye).
- Ukraine placed the museum itself on its sanctions list on 12 May 2023 for holding Russian Ministry of Culture permits for archaeological work in occupied Crimea from 2014 to 2023 (Ukrainian Main Intelligence Directorate register).

Red flags: Arms and defence, Promsvyazbank
- PSB is a state-owned bank that became a "captive bank for the Russian defense sector"; a December 2019 law made it the supporting bank of the military-industrial complex (Wikipedia). The UK sanctioned it on 22 February 2022 (Wikipedia).
- PSB's chairman Pyotr Fradkov sits on the museum's board of trustees (museum site).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's money; the coverage is about the 2023 change of director and the pressure on Russian cultural leaders.
1. usaartnews.com: "Marina Loshak steps down as director of Moscow's Pushkin Museum in latest high-level Russian departure" (2023)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/marina-loshak-steps-down-as-director-of-moscows-pushkin-museum-in-latest-high-level-russian-departure
- Links the wave of museum leadership changes to ideological repression after the invasion of Ukraine; Loshak's daughter and nephew are journalists labelled "foreign agents".
- Reports, citing Agentstvo, that her successor Elizaveta Likhacheva once worked for a pro-Kremlin youth movement; the article says this is not independently verified.
- Does not discuss the museum's sponsors or trustees.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Moscow Times (21 March 2023) presented Loshak's exit and the February 2023 change at the Tretyakov Gallery as a pattern of security-linked appointments in Russian cultural institutions.
- Connection to the space: Loshak's resignation and Likhacheva's appointment as director of the Pushkin Museum were one of the two changes the Moscow Times describes.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a Russian state museum, which alone is not a flag, but its general sponsor VTB and trustee Gazprombank are US-sanctioned banks, and PSB, a defence-sector bank, sponsors an exhibition and its chairman sits on the board. Ukraine sanctions the museum itself over Crimea digs. Worth watching: sponsor amounts, which are unpublished on the pages opened, and any change to the board.
