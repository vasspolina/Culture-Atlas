UCCA CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Beijing, China
ID: ucca-center-for-contemporary-art
Website: https://ucca.org.cn/en (checked 2026-10-24; the About and Visit pages loaded, the Support and Gala pages loaded on a second try, and the 2025 annual report PDF timed out)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the 798 art district, Beijing, with two further sites: UCCA Dune in Beidaihe, buried under a sand dune by the Bohai Sea, and UCCA Clay in Yixing, Jiangsu.
- Founded in 2007 as the Ullens Center for Contemporary Art by collectors Guy and Myriam Ullens. Renamed UCCA after ownership passed in 2017 to a group of Chinese and international patrons and shareholders (UCCA website).
- Beijing building: 1950s factory halls designed by East German architects of the Dessau Design Institute, renovated by Jean-Michel Wilmotte and Qingyun Ma in 2007 and by OMA in 2019. UCCA Dune was designed by OPEN Architecture and UCCA Clay by Kengo Kuma and Associates.
- The state-run Seven Star Group owns the Beijing buildings, with a long-term partnership with UCCA (The Art Newspaper, 2018). The centre has no permanent collection; the Ullens Collection "was never owned by UCCA" (The Art Newspaper).
- Visiting, UCCA Beijing: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 19:00, last entry 18:30. Discounts for students, over-60s, disabled guests, minors over 1.2 metres and groups of 20 or more; children under 1.2 metres free; members pay CNY 300 a year. The site lists no step-free access details.

FUNDING
UCCA Center for Contemporary Art: private non-profit foundation: patrons, an annual gala, corporate sponsors, with Chinese investors as owners since 2017
- 2017 sale: investors led by Jason Jiang, founder of Focus Media, and the education company Future Edutainment bought the centre (The Art Newspaper). ArtReview also names Jerry Mao of Lunar Capital among the investors; the price was not disclosed.
- Structure: the Beijing centre became a non-profit, tax-exempt foundation, with a board registered in Hong Kong in 2017 (ArtReview). The UCCA Foundation for Art and Education is accredited by the Beijing Bureau of Civil Affairs. Commercial activity sits in UCCA Enterprises, which includes retail and children's education (The Art Newspaper).
- Patrons: the Patrons Council began in 2012. The Foundation Council lists dozens of names or couples, including Jason Jiang, Jerry Mao and Laura Shi, Wendy Yu and the Bao Foundation (UCCA website, undated). Amounts are not given.
- Gala: the 2024 gala raised nearly RMB 8.4 million from auction and fundraising, with over 400 guests (stated by the space).
- Sponsors: the Support page describes Lead, Presenting and Supporting Partner tiers and sponsors from finance, technology, luxury brands, embassies, foundations and hotels, and names none. UCCA Dune is supported by Aranya (stated by the space). The 2025 annual report could not be opened, so no sponsor list is confirmed here.
- Visitors: more than 900,000 in 2017 (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Chinese state censorship of exhibitions
- 2011: the Ullens Center cancelled an Ai Weiwei exhibition after officials asked for a later opening; the museum's communications director said it "did not want to upset authorities" (Hyperallergic, Kyle Chayka).
- 2019: Beijing officials denied permission to import works for a Hung Liu solo show due to open on 6 December. The Bureau gave no explanation. Director Phillip Tinari wrote on 12 November that "There has been a tightening of the civil sphere in China in recent months" (Art Asia Pacific).
- 2022: Li Songsong's painting "Six Men" (2008) was removed from the group show "Common Ground" after a visitor complaint and the show closed a week early on 7 August. Police investigated the work (Art Asia Pacific).
- The Beijing buildings are owned by the state-run Seven Star Group (The Art Newspaper, 2018).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on censorship and compliance with officials, with older pieces on management and money. No recent critique of the sponsors was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Chinese Authorities Cancel Exhibition, Signifying Increased Censorship of Political Art" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/hung-liu-beijing/
- Reports that officials objected to nine of Liu's works and withheld import permits for the rest, even after she withdrew the nine.
- Frames the cancellation as growing censorship of political art; does not fault UCCA's conduct.
2. Hyperallergic: Kyle Chayka, on the cancelled Ai Weiwei show (2011)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/18750/ai-exhibition-ullens-center/
- Argues the cancellation showed the museum could not withstand political pressure and lost credibility by yielding.
- Notes it depended on Guy Ullens as its only major funder and was criticised for favouring European over Chinese staff, and that a handover to Chinese partners raised fears of a pay-for-play space.
3. The Art Newspaper: Chris Gill, on upheaval at the Ullens Center (2008)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/archive/what-is-going-on-at-the-ullens-center-in-beijing
- Reports that most senior staff left within about six months and that, according to insiders, Ullens moved the break-even target from 2013 to 2010.
- Reports Beijing art-world objections to foreign-run management. Does not concern the current owners.
4. Art Asia Pacific: coverage of the Li Songsong removal (2022)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/painting-of-kamikaze-removed-at-ucca-beijing
- Reports the removal; states the reason it was seen as politically sensitive remains unclear. UCCA's spokesperson confirmed the removal after visitor complaints.

BOTTOM LINE
The funding is private and non-profit, with no fossil-fuel, arms or Gulf funder found, though the sponsor list could not be read. The documented weakness is censorship: three exhibitions cancelled or cut short in 2011, 2019 and 2022, and a state-run landlord. Worth watching: who the 2025 partners are, and whether UCCA publishes accounts.
