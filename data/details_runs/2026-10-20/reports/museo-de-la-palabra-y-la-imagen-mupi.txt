MUSEO DE LA PALABRA Y LA IMAGEN (MUPI): San Salvador, El Salvador
ID: museo-de-la-palabra-y-la-imagen-mupi
Website: https://www.museo.com.sv (checked 2026-10-25; the address is compromised: the home page loaded but carried unrelated gambling advertisements in several languages, and the About page has an online casino link in its navigation menu)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and archive of Salvadoran memory at 27 Avenida Norte #1140, Urbanización La Esperanza, San Salvador (museum website).
- Founded by the Venezuelan journalist Carlos Henríquez Consalvi, who is also its director; Wikipedia says the late 1990s, the museum's own timeline shows it appearing publicly in 1996 with the book "Luciérnagas en El Mozote" and the campaign "Contra el caos de la desmemoria," and El Salvador's press marked its 30th anniversary in January 2026.
- The name means Museum of the Word and the Image.
- Building: the museum owns a building in the capital with several exhibition halls, a cinema hall and educational space (Wikipedia). It bought an adjoining annex with a mortgage loan to hold the archive, because the second floor where it was kept is a vulnerable zone in an earthquake area (elsalvador.com, 2024 and 2026).
- What is there: a film archive, a photo archive of more than 35,000 images from 1872, an audio archive that includes Radio Venceremos transmissions, and a social science library (Wikipedia). The museum also runs the Escuelas de Paz programme in schools (ContraPunto, 2021).
- Visiting: Monday to Friday 8am to noon and 2pm to 4:30pm, Saturday 8am to noon; Saturday afternoon, Sunday and holidays only by prior reservation (museum website). Admission and accessibility were not found on the pages read.

FUNDING
MUPI: donor-funded: private non-profit supported by foreign development funders and public donations
- On 11 January 2026 elsalvador.com reported that the museum bought the adjacent building with a mortgage loan, backed by international cooperation from AGIAMONDO and the Ford Foundation, and moved its archive there. Director Carlos Henríquez Consalvi asked businesses and citizens to cover the loan instalments; donations for this are tax-deductible from income tax.
- On 17 December 2024 (Óscar Orellana, elsalvador.com) the museum launched the campaign "Un techo para la cultura" to raise money; the annex mortgage payment is a significant monthly cost, and donations go to accounts at Banco Cuscatlán and BAC Credomatic, by PayPal and other routes. The article states no goal or amounts.
- For the Escuelas de Paz cycle in 2021, ContraPunto (Tania Primavera, 15 July 2021) listed the funders as TDH Alemania, BMZ and AGIAMONDO, within the regional INTERPAZ project.
- No reporting found of amounts from any funder, or of annual accounts, a budget or a donor list.
- No reporting found of Salvadoran state funding. The museum website has a donation link that leads to PayPal.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is donations plus named foreign funders (AGIAMONDO, Ford Foundation, TDH Alemania, BMZ), with no flagged funder found and no state funding found. The weak points are that no accounts, budget, donor list or board are published on the pages read, and that a mortgage on the annex depends on continuing donations. Worth watching: how the mortgage is repaid.
