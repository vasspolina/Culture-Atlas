THE BROAD: Los Angeles, USA
ID: the-broad
Website: https://www.thebroad.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 221 South Grand Avenue in downtown Los Angeles, holding the Broads' collection of more than 2,000 works by nearly 200 artists (stated by the museum).
- Founded by Eli and Edythe Broad; opened on 20 September 2015. Joanne Heyler is the director (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Broads.
- Designed by Diller Scofidio + Renfro with Gensler; about 120,000 square feet (Wikipedia). The site was leased by Broad on Grand Avenue (see Funding).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday 11am to 5pm, Thursday 11am to 8pm, Saturday and Sunday 10am to 6pm; closed Monday. General admission is free; special exhibitions may be charged (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).

FUNDING
The Broad: self-funded: the founders' money, a $200M endowment and the Broad foundation
- The Broads paid for the building, about $140M, and set up a $200M endowment (The Art Newspaper, 2015). Eli Broad's fortune came from insurance and home construction (The Art Newspaper).
- Form 990-PF, 2024: it reported revenue of $27.8M, expenses of $29.6M and total assets of $538M (ProPublica).
- Land: Wikipedia says Broad agreed to pay $7.7M for a 99-year lease on the Grand Avenue site. An aide to Councilwoman Jan Perry told Reason (partisan) in August 2010 that the $7.7M was not the lease but money the Broad foundation paid for affordable housing under a 2004 agreement, and that the lease rate is $1 a year.
- No reporting found of a corporate sponsor or state grant paying for the museum's running costs.

Red flags: board figure with an SEC settlement (Bruce Karatz)
- Bruce Karatz, who retired as chief executive of KB Home on 12 November 2006, is named in Wikipedia as a member of the Broad's Board of Governors. His own Wikipedia page lists a Broad board role with no dates; the museum's About page lists no board, so current membership is not confirmed.
- On 15 September 2008 he settled civil charges brought by the Securities and Exchange Commission over stock option backdating at KB Home.
- On 10 November 2010 a federal judge sentenced him to five years' probation in the Central District of California; a federal judge ended the probation early, effective 27 April 2012 (Wikipedia, which does not name the judge).

Red flags: Eli Broad's move from the Grand Avenue Committee to a museum site on public land
- Consumer Watchdog (an advocacy source, 5 February 2010) states that Broad chaired the Grand Avenue Committee from 2003 to 2007 and was co-chairman from 2007, and resigned on 30 November. The piece gives no year. Weeks later he began talks with Grand Avenue Authority members, a body the piece says he formed, to put his museum on part of the public land.
- A Broad Foundations spokeswoman, Karen Denne, said he stepped down from the committee so he would not have a conflict of interest (Consumer Watchdog).
- Unverified: Consumer Watchdog states that the developer, Related Companies, received more than $100 million in city, county and state subsidies and tax breaks for Grand Avenue before the 2008 crash, during Broad's chairmanship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Mixed reviews at the 2015 opening, aimed at the building, the collection's taste and Broad's control. No art critic found makes a case against the museum's funding.
1. The Art Newspaper: Julia Halperin, "The Broad: as big as its founders' influence" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/10/01/the-broad-as-big-as-its-founders-influence
- Broad has been "criticised for demanding too much control" and clashed repeatedly with museum directors and board members.
- Broad declined to give his collection to LACMA, where he had funded a building in 2008.
- Critics faulted the opening show for "the splashy and the monumental".
2. Hyperallergic: Matt Stromberg, "The New Broad Museum Brings LA Lots of Blue-Chip Art and a Few Surprises" (2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/the-new-broad-museum-brings-la-lots-of-blue-chip-art-and-a-few-surprises
- Notes a 15-month delay, rising costs and legal disputes, and calls the $140 million cost "a steep price tag for intrigue".
- Says relationships with earlier institutions, including LACMA, sometimes "eventually soured".

WIDER CONTEXT
- Consumer Watchdog (advocacy source) criticised the $3.2 billion Grand Avenue project, which it called stalled, over public subsidies to its developer. Reason (partisan) criticised the lease terms: $7.7M for 99 years reported, against the $1 a year an aide described.
- The museum sits on public land Broad leased in that project.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Broads' own, with a $200M endowment, and no reporting found of a corporate or state funder. The weak points are the public-land lease Broad negotiated after chairing the Grand Avenue committee, his documented control over LA institutions, and Bruce Karatz's 2008 SEC settlement and 2010 probation, with his board seat unconfirmed.
