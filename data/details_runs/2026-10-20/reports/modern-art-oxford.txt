MODERN ART OXFORD: Oxford, UK
ID: modern-art-oxford
Website: https://www.modernartoxford.org.uk (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at 30 Pembroke Street, Oxford, run by a registered charity.
- Founded in late 1965 by Trevor Green and a small group of modern art enthusiasts as the Museum of Modern Art Oxford; its first shows were in a warehouse on King Edward Street, and in September 1966 it moved to Pembroke Street. It took the name Modern Art Oxford in 2002 (stated by the gallery).
- Not named for a person.
- Building: a former Victorian brewery (The Art Newspaper). David Kohn Architects led a £2m redevelopment of the ground and lower-ground floors, and the artist Emma Hart designed the basement café; the gallery reopened on 2 November 2024 (The Art Newspaper).
- On site: exhibition galleries, a ground-floor gallery for Oxford-based artists, a learning studio, a basement café, a shop and event space.
- Visiting: closed Monday; Tuesday to Saturday 11am to 6pm; Sunday 11am to 4pm. The ground-floor gallery is free; the current exhibition costs £9.50 or £7 concessions. The site offers a sensory map and a visual story. The Museums Journal found no level access at the front entrance, with a flat entrance around the corner.

FUNDING
Modern Art Oxford: mainly public and trust-funded: Arts Council England, Oxford City Council, charitable trusts, individuals and corporate partners
- Income of £3,148,137 in the year to 31 March 2025 (£2,545,561 the year before), of which £1,164,425 was capital project income and £575,712 was activities for generating funds (accounts).
- Arts Council England: a National Portfolio grant of £2,726,298 over 2023/24 to 2025/26, paid in three instalments of £908,766, extended to March 2028; it also gave capital investment funding for the building project (accounts).
- Oxford City Council: annual grant cut from £70,000 to £50,000 from 2022/23, with a further 50 per cent reduction notified from 2025/26 (accounts).
- Capital project donors in 2024/25: CHK Foundation, Charina Endowment Fund, Fidelity UK Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Gresswell Environment Trust, The Lovington Foundation, Staples Trust, Wolfson Foundation and the Art Happens public appeal (accounts). The Art Newspaper also names the donor Dasha Shenkman.
- Exhibitions: The Ampersand Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, The Tavolozza Foundation (£10,000 for Belkis Ayon). Learning: The Headley Trust, Doris Field Charitable Trust, The Arts Society Oxford, Michael and Shirley Hunt Charitable Trust, 29th May 1961 Charitable Trust, Al Tajir Trust, Oxford City Council Community Impact Fund.
- Corporate: Edel Assanti, HMG Law, Lavazza, Oxford Brookes University. Individual giving was 35 per cent of fundraised income (accounts).
- Trustees: chair Anna Yang, treasurer Antony Perring, Rebecca Ajulu-Bushell, Heidi Baravalle, Christina Cree, Jenny Garcia, Patrick Holmes, Beatrice Lupton and Elizabeth McCarthy; Paul Hobson is the director.
- Cash at bank was £23,548 and the trustees call reserves low; the auditor reported no going-concern uncertainty (accounts).
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism. Coverage of the 2024 reopening is favourable, with practical caveats about the building project.
1. Museums Journal: Simon Stephens, "Open minded | Modern Art Oxford" (2025)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/reviews/2025/03/open-minded-modern-art-oxford/
- Praises the open entrance, the café and the ground-floor gallery.
- Says the four-month building schedule was "a little too ambitious" and the reopening slipped by four weeks.
- Says the raised ground floor leaves no level access at the front and the upper galleries were untouched.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Modern Art Oxford gallery gets a £2m revamp" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/10/28/modern-art-oxford-gallery-gets-a-%C2%A32m-revamp
- Not criticism: reports the revamp, its funders and the gallery's environmental targets, drawn largely from the gallery's own statements.
- Reports no critical voices.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is mostly public grants and named UK charitable trusts, and no flagged funder appears in the accounts. The weak points are thin cash reserves and the city council grant, cut from £70,000 to £50,000 and notified for a further 50 per cent reduction from 2025/26. Worth watching: how the gallery covers the council cut.
