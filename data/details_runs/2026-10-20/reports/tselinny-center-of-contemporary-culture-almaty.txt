TSELINNY CENTER OF CONTEMPORARY CULTURE: Almaty, Kazakhstan
ID: tselinny-center-of-contemporary-culture-almaty
Website: https://tselinny.org/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the About, Visit and Partnership pages carried mostly navigation text)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre in a rebuilt Soviet cinema in Almaty, Kazakhstan.
- Founded by businessman Kairat Boranbayev, who bought the former cinema in 2015 (Orda; Kunstkritikk dates the purchase to 2016). It opened to the public in September 2025 after a seven-year reconstruction (Vlast). It is directed by Jamilya Nurkalieva; the artistic director is Boranbayev's daughter, Alima Kairat (Kunstkritikk).
- The cinema opened in 1964 with about 1,500 seats, was split in two in 2000, later became a hookah lounge and nightclub, and closed in 2013 (Orda, Vlast).
- Architect Asif Khan led the redesign with Zaure Aitayeva. The main hall became a central gallery, the original foyer was rebuilt, and the sgraffito by Yevgeniy Sidorkin was reinstalled in fragments (Brooklyn Rail). The entrance was raised by 1.5 metres for accessibility (Orda).
- There is no permanent collection; the building holds exhibitions, performances, film programmes and courses. An advisory board of five includes Bart de Baere and Natasha Ginwala (Tselinny website).
- Visiting: exhibitions are free for people with disabilities, WWII veterans, children under 7 and CIMAM members; students and seniors get discounts on some events (Tselinny website). Opening days, hours and prices were not found.

FUNDING
Tselinny Center: self-funded: the founder's money, from a fortune built in oil and gas, real estate, retail and pharmaceuticals
- The project was fully funded by Boranbayev, who declined to discuss finances. Ticket sales will not cover costs soon, so the cafe is part of the revenue plan (Orda).
- Boranbayev's fortune comes from investments in "oil and gas, real estate, retail and pharmaceuticals" (The Art Newspaper). The Art Newspaper describes the centre as "financed by Kazakh entrepreneurs".
- The centre runs a Patrons' Club with 25 named patrons in Platinum, Gold and Silver tiers. The page gives no dates, amounts or use of funds (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of a state funder or a corporate sponsor of the centre.

Red flags: founder's oil and gas wealth and conviction
- Oil and gas: the founder's fortune includes oil and gas (The Art Newspaper). The assets he later gave the state included the Karazhanbas Severny oil field and the KazTurboRemont Service Center plant (Orda).
- Conviction: in March 2023 Boranbayev was sentenced to eight years for aiding embezzlement of state property tied to a 14.5 billion tenge purchase of liquefied petroleum gas through the Aziyagazchunja company (Orda).
- Appeal: the sentence was cut to six years in September 2023 and he was released in November (Orda).
- Assets returned: he paid 14.5 billion tenge in compensation, returned assets worth over 90 billion tenge and transferred 66 million dollars to the Education Infrastructure Support Fund. Orda gives the total as 140.7 billion tenge.
- Boranbayev told the court the charges stemmed from his ties to the Nazarbayev family (Orda). The Art Newspaper quotes director Nurkalieva: "The situation has been fully resolved, and currently there are no implications for the museum and its funding."

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism. Most coverage is admiring or a partner piece; one essay and one magazine feature raise the founder's record and the dependence on one owner.
1. Kunstkritikk: Anders Kreuger, "Can the Subaltern Build a Museum?" (2025)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/can-the-subaltern-build-a-museum/
- Tests whether a formerly colonised country can build an institution not judged as derivative; finds Tselinny the more distinct, decolonially oriented of two new Almaty museums.
- Calls Boranbayev "another Kazakh oligarch" imprisoned on embezzlement charges tied to gas sales after the January 2022 unrest, and notes the tension in the centre's position.
- Judges the Sidorkin sgraffito panels "not in themselves compelling or important".
2. Trebuchet Magazine: Veronica Simpson, "Kazakhstan: Close Encounters With Central Asian Culture, Contemporary Art And Post-Soviet Capitalism" (2025)
URL: https://www.trebuchet-magazine.com/?p=529496
- Names Boranbayev's fortune and his 2023 sentence, citing The Art Newspaper.
- Notes Tselinny has no collection to leverage, unlike Alma. Descriptive, not a case against the centre.
3. The Art Newspaper: feature on new Kazakh art venues, author not identified on the page read (2025)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2025/04/30/as-kazakhstan-cautiously-strengthens-ties-with-western-europe-new-art-venues-herald-a-change-of-direction
- Reports the conviction, the asset handover and the director's reply. No criticism of the centre itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is one man's: Boranbayev's fortune includes oil and gas, and he was convicted in 2023 of aiding embezzlement and gave the state assets worth more than 140 billion tenge in total. The centre has no state or corporate funder on record. Worth watching: how the Patrons' Club money is used, and whether the centre publishes accounts or a governing board; neither was found.
