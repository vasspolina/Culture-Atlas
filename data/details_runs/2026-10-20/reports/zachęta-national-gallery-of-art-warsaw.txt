ZACHĘTA NATIONAL GALLERY OF ART: Warsaw, Poland
ID: zachęta-national-gallery-of-art-warsaw
Website: https://zacheta.art.pl/en (checked 2026-10-24; the gallery's own site, which loads normally. The address in the record is ORLEN's sponsorship page.)

OVERVIEW
- State contemporary art gallery at pl. Małachowskiego 3 in central Warsaw, showing Polish and foreign art of the 20th and 21st centuries, with a Project Room for young artists at Gałczyńskiego 3.
- The name comes from the Society for the Encouragement of Fine Arts (Towarzystwo Zachęty do Sztuk Pięknych), whose first meeting was on 13 December 1860. After the war the building housed the Central Bureau for Art Exhibitions from 1949; it became the Zachęta State Gallery and was renamed National Gallery of Art in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Stefan Szyller won an 1894 competition for a neo-Renaissance design; construction began in 1898 and the front building opened in December 1900. It was damaged in the 1944 Warsaw Uprising and reconstructed in 1991 to 1993 (Wikipedia).
- On site: a collection of almost 3,700 works, exhibitions, the Project Room, education programmes, a library and documentation archive, an art bookshop, and the organisation of Poland's pavilion at the Venice Biennale for over 70 years (stated by the gallery).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 12 to 8pm. Adults pay 30 zł, reduced 20 zł, students up to 26 pay 5 zł, ages 7 to 18 pay 2 zł, under-7s are free. Entry to the main gallery is free on Thursdays, and the Project Room is always free. Guided tours in Polish Sign Language are offered; other accessibility details sit on pages that could not be read.

FUNDING
Zachęta National Gallery of Art: mainly public: Poland's Ministry of Culture and National Heritage, with ORLEN as named patron
- The gallery's partners page lists the Ministry of Culture and National Heritage as the body that financed the gallery. The size of the subsidy was not found.
- ORLEN: listed as patron of the gallery on the gallery's partners page and on ORLEN's own sponsorship page ("The Gallery, whose patron is ORLEN, is a fully accessible and inclusive space"). Neither page gives a start year or an amount.
- Other partners named on the partners page: the Śródmieście District of the City of Warsaw (supports the Project Room), Ingart (partner of the collection), IMM, BenQ (sponsor of the Project Room), Tubadzin and Grohe (bathroom renovation), Kultura Dostępna (co-financed the Art Encyclopedia in Polish Sign Language), the Museum of History of Photography in Cracow, and the Gessel Foundation (partner of the scholarship programme). The page gives no dates.
- Annual reports are published, but the 2024 report PDF could not be read as text, so revenue and ticket income figures were not verified.

Red flags: ORLEN, a fossil-fuel patron
- ORLEN is a multinational oil refiner, petrol retailer and natural gas trader based in Płock; the Polish State Treasury holds 49.9% of its shares (Wikipedia, as of October 2026).
- ORLEN is the gallery's current patron, per both the gallery and ORLEN.
- No reporting found of a protest or campaign aimed at ORLEN's patronage of Zachęta.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism found concerns the gallery's leadership and programme under director Janusz Janowski, ending with his dismissal in December 2023. No criticism of Zachęta's funding or of ORLEN's patronage was found.
1. OKO.press: Roman Pawłowski-Felberg, article on the dismissal of Janusz Janowski (22 December 2023)
URL: https://oko.press/zacheta-dyrektor-odwolany-janusz-janowski
- Reports that Hanna Wróblewska, of the ministry's national institutions department, handed Janowski his dismissal letter. The ministry's stated reasons: he dropped EU funds and a planned partnership with the Kunstmuseum in Trondheim, and curators did not help build the exhibition programme. Wróblewska also faulted him for not filing the mandatory 2022 activity report.
- Reports that the November 2023 show "Pejzaż malarstwa polskiego," curated by Janowski, drew protests from ten artists, including Wilhelm Sasnal and Leon Tarasewicz, who said their works were used without consent.
- Reports that a jury Janowski led chose Ignacy Czwartos for the Polish pavilion at Venice, and that some jury members distanced themselves from the decision.
- Does not mention sponsors or ORLEN.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is publicly financed by Poland's culture ministry, with ORLEN, an oil and gas company whose largest shareholder is the Polish state, as its named patron. The weak points are the fossil-fuel patron, undisclosed sponsorship amounts, and political swings in its leadership: the director was dismissed in December 2023, after ten artists protested one of his exhibitions. Watch whether the 2024 and 2025 financial statements show how much ORLEN pays.
