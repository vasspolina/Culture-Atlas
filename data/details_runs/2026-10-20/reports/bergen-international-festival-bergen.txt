BERGEN INTERNATIONAL FESTIVAL: Bergen, Norway
ID: bergen-international-festival-bergen
Website: https://www.fib.no/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual international music, theatre and arts festival, Festspillene i Bergen, run over about two weeks from late May into June in venues and squares across Bergen.
- Founded by Frank Meidell Falch and Christen Gran Bøgh, modelled on the Salzburg Festival; the first festival opened on 1 June 1953 (Wikipedia).
- Run by Stiftelsen Festspillene i Bergen, a foundation; Lars Petter Hagen has been festival director since 2022 and leaves after the 2027 festival to run Den Norske Opera & Ballett (stated by the space).
- Venues include Grieghallen, Haakon's Hall, Edvard Grieg's home Troldhaugen, Bergen Kunsthall, and a festival tent on Festplassen; the 2025 festival had over 70,000 visitors (stated by the space).
- Visiting: the 2027 festival runs 26 May to 12 June. Under-40s pay at most NOK 250, students and under-18s at most NOK 130, over-67s get 10% off; free companion tickets with a companion card. Step-free access varies by venue: Grieghallen has lifts, Haakon's Hall has none.

FUNDING
Bergen International Festival: mainly public with corporate and foundation sponsors: Ministry of Culture, Bergen, Vestland, DNB and Equinor
- Public operating grants come from the Ministry of Culture and Equality, Bergen municipality and Vestland county council (stated by the space).
- Main partner: DNB, which with its forerunners has backed the festival since the start (stated by the space).
- Festival partners: Equinor, DNV, KPMG, Bergens Tidende and Galleriet (stated by the space, no amounts).
- Foundations: Stiftelsen Kristian Gerhard Jebsen, Grieg Foundation, the Westfal-Larsen fund, Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Hilmar Rekstens Allmennyttige Fond, Kavlifondet, GC Rieber Fondene, Talent Norge, Signatur, Krefting-stiftelsen, Bergesenstiftelsen and Fritt Ord (stated by the space).
- Equinor renewed in 2025 as a local sponsorship with its Bergen office; no amount or term was given (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of current grant or sponsor amounts; the reports page links only a 2024 report on Canva.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Equinor)
- Equinor, as Statoil, has worked with the festival since 1990; it paid NOK 10 million for 2006 to 2009 (Equinor, 2006).
- In recent years the Equinor partnership centred on the talent programme Morgendagens helter; from 2025 the agreement continues as a local sponsorship (stated by the space).
- Equinor is listed first among the festival partners on the current sponsor page (stated by the space).

Red flags: protest over Equinor sponsorship
- On 24 May 2023, at the opening ceremony, Greenpeace activist and climate researcher Christine van der Horst sat on the catwalk with a banner reading "Equinor ut av Festspillene" (Greenpeace Norge, an advocacy group).
- Folk mot Fossilmakta, an Oslo climate campaign, runs a petition asking the festival to phase out fossil sponsorship, starting with Equinor; it had 387 signatures when checked (Action Network, advocacy source).
- In 2025 Folk mot Fossilmakta carried out "peaceful but disruptive actions" during the festival (stated by the space).
- The festival held a 2025 debate on oil sponsorship with Equinor's information director Sissel Rinde, a Folk mot Fossilmakta spokesperson and its own board vice chair; it says sponsors have no influence on artistic content (stated by the space).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The objections to Equinor come from campaign groups, not from art critics.

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the festival's money is public, from the state, the city and the county, with a long list of Norwegian foundations. The weak point is Equinor, a sponsor since 1990 and renewed in 2025, which has drawn a 2023 protest, a petition and 2025 actions. Worth watching: whether the festival publishes the size of the Equinor deal.
