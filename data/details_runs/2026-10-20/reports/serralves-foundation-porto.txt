SERRALVES FOUNDATION: Porto, Portugal
ID: serralves-foundation-porto
Website: https://www.serralves.pt/en/ (checked 2026-10-24; live, listing Jenny Holzer "Wrong Answers" from 18 June 2026 and Lee Ufan "Fragments of Infinity" from 16 October 2026)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art, a villa and about 18 hectares of park in Porto, run by the Fundação de Serralves (Wikipedia).
- The State bought the Serralves estate in 1986; the park and villa opened to the public on 29 May 1987; the Foundation was created in 1989 by Decree-Law 240-A/89 as a partnership between the State and about 51 public and private bodies (Wikipedia).
- The villa, Casa de Serralves, was built between 1925 and 1944 for Carlos Alberto Cabral, second Count of Vizela, to designs by José Marques da Silva (Wikipedia).
- The museum was designed by Álvaro Siza Vieira, contracted in March 1991, and opened on 6 June 1999: 13,000 m2 with 14 galleries (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: general ticket EUR 24; park only EUR 15; free on the first Sunday of each month, for children under 12 and for visitors with a certified disability rating of 60 percent or more plus one companion. 50 percent off for ages 12 to 17, students and over-65s (Serralves website).

FUNDING
Serralves Foundation: mixed: State co-financing plus founders from Portuguese business and municipalities
- Founders: Wikipedia counts 181 founders. The Foundation's page of "Patron Founders", those who paid into the Annual Fund, lists for 2021 to 2024 (amounts not published on the page): Galp Energia, Amorim Investimentos e Participações, Mota-Engil, Sonae, Inditex, El Corte Inglés, Caixa Geral de Depósitos, REN, Brisa, NOS, Vodafone, Auchan, Bial, Sogrape and others, and 15 municipal councils including Matosinhos and Braga. Tabaqueira appears in 2021 and 2022.
- State: the page shows the Portuguese Republic under "Co-financing". The Bloco de Esquerda said in October 2024 that Serralves receives EUR 6.4 million of state funding that year (Lusa, via Observador).
- No reporting found of a full budget breakdown by funder.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Galp Energia is a Patron Founder in each year from 2021 to 2024 (Serralves website). Galp's units include oil and gas exploration and production and crude oil refining; Amorim Energia holds 35.8 percent (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Tobacco
- Tabaqueira, S.A. is a Patron Founder in 2021 and 2022 (Serralves website). Wikipedia describes Tabaqueira as a subsidiary of Philip Morris International.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concentrates on one episode, the 2018 Mapplethorpe exhibition, when the Foundation's board and its artistic director disagreed in public. No piece found examines the funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris and José da Silva, "Wolfgang Tillmans and Tania Bruguera pledge support for censored Serralves museum director" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/09/24/wolfgang-tillmans-and-tania-bruguera-pledge-support-for-censored-serralves-museum-director
- Artistic director João Ribas resigned after 20 works were removed from the Mapplethorpe show and two galleries were limited to visitors aged 18 and over.
- More than 150 artists and arts professionals signed a letter to Foundation president Ana Pinho; it called the use of Portuguese law on "pornographic" material "disingenuous and insulting".
- The Foundation said the 20 works were left out by the curator alone and was not censorship.
2. ArtReview: news desk, "Serralves Museum removes Mapplethorpe works, artistic director resigns" (2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-24-sep-2018-serralves-museum-removes-mapplethorpe-works-artistic-director-resigns
- Reports the removals, the age-restricted rooms, the resignation and protesters outside the museum.
- The museum said it receives nearly one million visitors a year, many of them children and school groups, and that the public should be alerted.
3. Arte al Día: no author named, "Censorship and controversy at the Serralves Contemporary Art Museum of Porto" (2018)
URL: https://www.artealdia.com/News/CENSORSHIP-AND-CONTROVERSY-AT-THE-SERRALVES-CONTEMPORARY-ART-MUSEUM-OF-PORTO
- Argues the Foundation's action echoes the US culture wars of the 1980s and 1990s, calling it a retreat into moral conservatism.
- Reports that collector Luiz Teixeira de Freitas wrote in solidarity with Ribas and asked for the return of 700 drawings he had lent.
- Reports that the Foundation's leaders deny censorship.
4. Observador (news, not art press): Rita Cipriano and Gonçalo Correia, "João Ribas: demiti-me porque não podia tolerar a censura em Serralves" (2018)
URL: https://observador.pt/2018/10/16/joao-ribas-demiti-me-porque-nao-podia-tolerar-a-censura-em-serralves/
- Ribas told the parliamentary culture committee on 16 October 2018 that he resigned over censorship by the board, and that two works were removed an hour before the press conference.
- Ana Pinho told the committee there was no censorship and that Ribas's accusations lacked basis. Board member José Pacheco Pereira said it was false that two works were removed.

BOTTOM LINE
The Foundation is funded by the State and by Portuguese business founders, and the Annual Fund list is public by name though not by amount. The weak points are two named patron founders, Galp Energia (every year 2021 to 2024) and Tabaqueira (2021 and 2022), and the 2018 dispute over the Mapplethorpe show, where the board and Ribas gave opposing accounts. Watch for amounts per founder and for the free-access dispute raised in 2024.
