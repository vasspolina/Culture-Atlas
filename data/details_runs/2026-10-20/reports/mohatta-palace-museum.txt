MOHATTA PALACE MUSEUM: Karachi, Pakistan
ID: mohatta-palace-museum
Website: https://www.mohattapalacemuseum.com (checked 2026-10-25; the home page lists its latest exhibition as running from 10 December 2019, the About page link returned an error, and the Support section carries unrelated outside links, one to a promo-code site, so the address is compromised)

OVERVIEW
- Museum in a pink stone palace in Old Clifton, Karachi (Associated Press, 2024). It was formally inaugurated on 15 September 1999; its first exhibition was "Treasures of the Talpurs" (Dawn, 2020).
- The palace was built in the 1920s, completed in 1927 according to Wikipedia, by the Hindu businessman Shivratan Mohatta as a seaside home (Associated Press). Wikipedia names the architect as Ahmed Hussain Agha.
- Named for Mohatta. It was renamed Qasr-e-Fatima after Fatima Jinnah, sister of Muhammad Ali Jinnah, who moved in in 1964; her sister Shireen Jinnah lived there until her death in 1980 and the building was then sealed (Wikipedia, Associated Press).
- Building: pink Jodhpur stone, stained glass windows, a sweeping staircase and domes (Associated Press).
- On site: exhibitions on Pakistani and regional arts and culture, more than 20 since opening (Dawn, 2020). Nasreen Askari has been the museum's curator since 1998; Dawn reported on 14 December 2025 that she was stepping down after 28 years.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm, closed on public holidays; admission Rs30, free for students, children and seniors (Associated Press, September 2024).

FUNDING
Mohatta Palace Museum: a palace bought by the Government of Sindh and run by a trust, supported by donations, event hire and ticket income (stated by the museum and the Associated Press)
- Sources conflict on the purchase. Wikipedia says the Government of Sindh bought the palace in 1995 and appointed an independent board of trustees headed by the Governor. In court the Sindh government said it bought the property in 1993 at a price the official assignee set as its market value (Dawn, 2021). Dawn (2020) says that after 1980 the federal government bought the palace and handed it to the Sindh government, and gives no year.
- The Mohatta Palace Gallery Trust runs the museum and sought to intervene in the lawsuit below (Dawn, 2021; The Friday Times, 2023).
- Admission fees do not cover maintenance costs. The palace earns income by renting itself out for corporate and charity events (Associated Press).
- Stated by the museum: its Support page says it depends mainly on public donations, corporate and individual, and has had past government support for specific projects, and it lists an Endowment Fund and a Collections Fund. The page names Citibank, Standard Chartered Bank, Shell Pakistan, Engro, Unilever and Telenor as clients that held events at the museum, not as donors. The site is compromised (see Website line).
- No reporting found of a budget or a donor list.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The palace is the subject of a long-running lawsuit over the estate of the Jinnah family; the respondents are the legal heirs of a plaintiff from 1971, and their counsel said the dispute had lasted more than half a century (Dawn, 2021; Associated Press).
- On 13 October 2021 a single judge of the Sindh High Court ordered the official assignee to take over the building and set up a medical college or hospital. The Sindh government appealed, and on 8 November 2021 a division bench restrained the official assignee from taking possession until 18 November; the appeal was not decided that day (Dawn, 2021).
- In August 2023 (The Friday Times, 25 August 2023) a single bench rejected applications by old Clifton residents and the Mohatta Palace Gallery Trust to join the suit as parties, finding they were not essential parties. The verdict under challenge would convert the palace into a girls' medical and dental college.
- In September 2024 the Associated Press reported that the trustees had fended off a plan to turn the palace into a dental college, while the lawsuit by heirs of a former owner seeking control of the land continued. No reporting found of a final ruling.
- The museum is connected to this dispute: the trust that runs it sits on the disputed property and sought to join the case.

BOTTOM LINE
The palace was bought by government (Sindh, per Wikipedia and court reporting) and a trust runs the museum, which says it depends mainly on donations and also earns from tickets and event hire; no flagged funder appears. The weak points are the open ownership lawsuit that has threatened to turn the building into a medical college, and the absence of any published budget or donor list in the sources read. Worth watching: the court outcome and who succeeds Nasreen Askari as curator.
