SONIC ACTS: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: sonic-acts-amsterdam
Website: https://sonicacts.com

OVERVIEW
- Interdisciplinary arts organisation in Amsterdam, founded in 1994 to support new developments in electronic and digital art; it runs a biennial international festival combining art, theory and technology, and the Sonic Acts Academy for artistic research (Sonic Acts).
- Wikipedia says it was founded by Paradiso and the ArtScience Interfaculty, which included the Royal Academy of Art and the Royal Conservatory of The Hague, and that the festival was originally held each year at Paradiso.
- The 2026 Biennial, "Melted for Love", ran from February to March 2026 with 80 events at 20 venues in Amsterdam, including Muziekgebouw, Paradiso, the Stedelijk Museum, W139, Framer Framed, Arti et Amicitiae, Rozenstraat and Zone2Source in Amstelpark (Subbacultcha; Re-Imagine Europe).
- Other activities: artist residencies, commissions and co-productions, publications, exhibitions, club nights, screenings, workshops and symposia; recent projects include Re-Imagine Europe, a four-year project organised with ten cultural organisations across Europe, and the Progress Bar series on radical sound and club culture (Sonic Acts).
- Vertical Cinema, a series of ten 35mm films projected vertically, premiered in 2013; in 2022 it changed from an annual festival to a two-month biennial that drew about 20,000 visitors (Wikipedia).
- Sonic Acts Press began publishing the magazine Ecoes in 2021 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Sonic Acts: supported by an EU programme, a Dutch arts fund, the Amsterdam arts fund and Paradiso
- Its own site says it is generously supported by the Creative Europe Programme of the European Union, Creative Industries Fund NL, the Amsterdam Fund for the Arts (AFK) and Paradiso (Sonic Acts).
- Re-Imagine Europe, in which Sonic Acts is one of 14 partner organisations alongside Paradiso, is co-funded by the European Union (Re-Imagine Europe, 29 January 2026).
- Earlier projects: Dark Ecology was funded by BarentsKult, Public Art Norway (KORO), Arts Council Norway, Creative Industries Fund NL, PNEK, the Mondriaan Fund and Finnmark County Municipality (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Sonic Acts is public and institutional: its own site names the EU Creative Europe Programme, Creative Industries Fund NL, the Amsterdam Fund for the Arts and Paradiso as its supporters, and Re-Imagine Europe is co-funded by the European Union.
