MUSEUM FOLKWANG: Essen, Germany
ID: museum-folkwang-essen
Website: https://www.museum-folkwang.de (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Essen with 19th- and 20th-century art, photography, prints, German posters, and ancient and non-European art; its key work is Caspar David Friedrich's "Mountain Landscape with Rainbow" (Wikipedia).
- Karl Ernst Osthaus opened the Folkwang Museum in Hagen in 1902. In 1922 a consortium of Essen and Ruhr companies and private individuals acquired the collection, the Folkwang-Museumsverein was founded, and the collection merged with Essen's municipal art museum (museum chronicle).
- In 1937 the National Socialists confiscated more than 1,400 works as "degenerate art" (museum chronicle).
- David Chipperfield won the architecture competition in 2007; his building added 16,000 square metres and opened in January 2010 (Wikipedia; museum chronicle).
- The Deutsche Plakat Museum holds about 340,000 posters (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday and Friday 10:00 to 20:00, closed Monday. The permanent collection is free; the Courbet exhibition costs €16, concessions €12. The pages read give no accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museum Folkwang: mixed: city of Essen and the Krupp foundation, with corporate and savings-bank sponsors
- The Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation decided in 2006 to finance the new building as sole funder (foundation culture page; museum chronicle). Wikipedia's article on the foundation gives the amount as €55 million.
- The foundation offered free admission to the permanent collection from 2015; in 2020 the City of Essen and the foundation agreed to keep it free permanently (museum chronicle).
- The Folkwang-Museumsverein, with about 400 members, co-owns the collections with the city of Essen (Wikipedia). No amounts for the city's share were found.
- Eleven sponsors named on the museum's page: Allbau GmbH, Bankhaus Bauer, E.ON SE, Evonik Industries AG, GENO BANK, Merck Finck Privatbankiers AG, NATIONAL-BANK, RWE AG, Sparkasse Essen, Sparkassen-Kulturfonds des Deutschen Sparkassen- und Giroverbandes and Stadtwerke Essen ("since 2018"). No amounts are published.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (RWE and E.ON, sponsors)
- RWE, headquartered in Essen, generated about 32% of its 2019 electricity from lignite and 9% from hard coal, and in 2018 was reported as Europe's largest carbon dioxide emitter. Protests have targeted its Hambach surface mine since 2012 (Wikipedia).
- E.ON, also headquartered in Essen, sells electricity and natural gas and announced in 2014 that it would exit fossil generation. It still holds 50% of Enerjisa, which owns coal power in Turkey (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against these sponsors at the museum.

Red flags: Krupp foundation money (forced labour and arms)
- The foundation was created from the fortune of Alfried Krupp, convicted at the 1947 to 1948 Krupp Trial of using forced labourers and plundering economic assets in occupied countries. He was sentenced to twelve years and forfeiture of his assets; the US High Commissioner commuted the sentence in 1951 and restored his property (Wikipedia).
- The foundation holds 20.93% of thyssenkrupp AG, its largest shareholder (Wikipedia). thyssenkrupp holds 51% of ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems, which makes frigates, corvettes and submarines for the German and other navies (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Protest over a cancelled exhibition section (2023)
- The museum cancelled the Afrofuturism section of "We Is Future", due to open on 24 November 2023, after curator Anaïs Duplan's pro-Palestine posts. Director Peter Gorschlüter called some posts "unacceptable"; a spokesperson cited a 10 November post supporting the BDS movement, which the Bundestag has labelled antisemitic. The museum said the decision was not about artistic content (Hyperallergic).
- CIMAM, the international committee of modern art museums, published a statement on 15 January 2024 opposing the cancellation as part of wider censorship of artists who support Palestinians.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns the 2023 cancellation. No criticism found in art media of the sponsors or the Krupp foundation's money.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "German Museum Cancels Afrofuturism Show Over Curator's Pro-Palestine Posts" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/856774/german-museum-cancels-afrofuturism-show-over-curators-pro-palestine-posts/
- The cancellation came a day after a member of Duplan's team travelled to Essen to help install the section.
- Duplan said the museum had not started a real conversation before cancelling and called its approach "really repressive". Most of the artists in the section asked for their works back.
2. CIMAM: "Cancellation and censorship in times of war" (2024)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/cancellation-and-censorship-in-times-of-war/
- Argues museums should stay spaces for free artistic expression and calls the Bundestag resolution "disputed and legally not binding".
- A museum-professionals' body, not an art outlet; takes its details from an Instagram account, archive_of_silence.

BOTTOM LINE
The building and free admission are paid by the Krupp foundation, whose fortune came from a company whose owner was convicted of using forced labour and which still holds a fifth of thyssenkrupp, a maker of naval vessels. The sponsor list includes RWE and E.ON.
