JAPAN FOLK CRAFTS MUSEUM (NIHON MINGEIKAN): Tokyo, Japan
ID: japan-folk-crafts-museum-nihon-mingeikan-tokyo
Website: https://mingeikan.or.jp

OVERVIEW
- Folk crafts museum at 4-3-33 Komaba, Meguro-ku, Tokyo, near Komaba-Todaimae Station on the Keio Inokashira Line (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Planned in 1926 by Yanagi Soetsu (1889 to 1961) and others, and opened in 1936 in Komaba as the base of the Mingei (folk crafts) movement. Yanagi was its founding director (museum website).
- Yanagi had earlier planned a private museum, the Shirakaba Museum, in 1917, opened the Korean Folk Art Museum in Seoul in 1924, and opened a Nihon Mingeikan in Hamamatsu in 1931 (museum website).
- The main building is a two-storey wooden structure with a tile roof. A long gate-residence was moved from Tochigi Prefecture and rebuilt in front of it, and a reinforced concrete New Building was completed in 1982 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds about 17,000 Japanese and foreign crafts old and new: ceramics, textiles, lacquerware, paintings, metalwork and basketry (museum website).
- The West Wing (Nishikan) is Yanagi's former residence (museum website).
- Directors: Yanagi Soetsu, Hamada Shoji, Yanagi Sori, Kobayashi Yotaro and, currently, the product designer Fukasawa Naoto (museum website).
- Visiting: main building open 10:00 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30), closed Mondays. Admission is 1,500 yen for adults and 800 yen for university and high school students; middle school students and younger enter free. Disabled visitors and one caregiver pay 750 yen. The West Wing opens on the 2nd and 3rd Wednesdays and the 2nd and 3rd Saturdays, 10:00 to 16:30 (museum admission page).

FUNDING
Japan Folk Crafts Museum: public-interest foundation: admissions, membership dues and donations
- Legal form: a public interest incorporated foundation, Koeki Zaidan Hojin Nihon Mingeikan (museum foundation outline page). Its chairman is Naoto Fukasawa (museum membership page).
- Founding patron: the businessman Ohara Magosaburo offered 100,000 yen in March 1935 to help fund the museum. He founded Kuraray, the Ohara Museum of Art, the Ohara Institute for Social Research and Hosei University (Wikipedia). The museum's own history page names him among the many supporters who helped establish it.
- Membership: individual members pay 10,000 yen per unit, with a minimum of 1 unit, for one year. Corporate members pay 100,000 yen per unit, with a minimum of 1 unit, from April to March. The membership page says dues support exhibitions, research, publications, lectures and training of craftspeople (museum membership page).
- Donations start at 1,000 yen a unit and fund restoration of the collection, repair of the building and new projects such as online courses (museum donation page).
- The foundation page links business reports for FY2018 to FY2025, income and expenditure reports and budgets for the same years, balance sheets for FY2017 to FY2025, and a list of officers uploaded in July 2025 (museum foundation outline page).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by a public interest foundation that charges admission and takes individual and corporate membership dues from 10,000 and 100,000 yen a unit, plus donations. Wikipedia reports that the businessman Ohara Magosaburo offered 100,000 yen in March 1935 to help fund its founding. The foundation publishes its business reports and income and expenditure statements.
