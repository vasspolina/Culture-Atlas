BIRMINGHAM MUSEUM OF ART: Birmingham, USA
ID: birmingham-museum-of-art-birmingham
Website: https://www.artsbma.org/

OVERVIEW
- City-owned art museum at 2000 Rev. Abraham Woods, Jr. Blvd, Birmingham, Alabama (Wikipedia, museum website).
- Its roots are the Birmingham Art Club, founded in 1908. A museum board formed in September 1950 and the museum opened in April 1951 in five unused rooms in city hall; Richard Foster Howard was the first director (Wikipedia).
- Graham Boettcher is the current director (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Oscar Wells Memorial Building opened on 3 May 1959, designed by Warren, Knight & Davis and paid for by a 1954 bequest. Edward Larrabee Barnes of New York led a renovation and expansion completed in 1993. It covers 180,000 square feet on 3.9 acres and includes a sculpture garden (Wikipedia).
- Collection of more than 24,000 objects, including American art, Asian art, African art, European decorative arts and the Wedgwood collection (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free to the public throughout its history (Encyclopedia of Alabama).

FUNDING
Birmingham Museum of Art: mainly public with corporate sponsors: owned by the City of Birmingham, with a corporate circle that includes Alabama Power
- The museum is a city department. The Encyclopedia of Alabama (undated figures) says the city provided about half of a $6.2 million budget, with individuals, corporations and foundations providing the rest. An 18-member Board of Trustees approved by the city council governs it.
- The museum's sponsorship page (archived 5 March 2026) says corporate support funds the operating budget, education programmes, special exhibitions and museum-wide programmes. Under the heading Founders Circle it shows the logos of Alabama Power, PNC, Protective, Regions and EBSCO Industries; under Sustainers Circle it shows the logos of Blue Cross and Blue Shield of Alabama, Barber Companies and Vulcan Materials.
- The same page names Medical Properties Trust, Stewart Perry Construction and Encompass Health Corporation in the Chairman's Circle, the Chalker Group in the Director's Circle, Arlington Properties and Dobbins Group in the Curators' Circle, and Four Corners Custom Framing Gallery, National Cement Company of Alabama, William Blackstock Architects, Kassouf & Co, Storyteller Overland and Sinclair Law Firm in the Benefactor's Circle.
- The Birmingham Museum of Art Endowment Trust (ProPublica, IRS filings) had net assets of $33,760,976 and expenses of $1,334,203 in the year to June 2025. Its revenue was $1,064,453 that year and $3,637,520 in the year to June 2024, of which $2,862,025 was contributions.
- The Samuel H. Kress Foundation lent Italian Renaissance paintings in 1952 that formed the core of the European collection (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Alabama Power)
- Alabama Power's logo appears under Founders Circle on the museum's list of current corporate supporters (sponsorship page, archived 5 March 2026). The page shows the name as a logo.
- Alabama Power's own 2026 fact card gives its 2025 generation as 37% natural gas, 34% coal, 21% nuclear, 5% hydro and 3% other. Its energy page says about 30% of the electricity generated for all customers comes from clean sources (Alabama Power, updated February 2026).
- Plant Miller emitted 20,834,019 metric tons of carbon dioxide in 2021, the most of any US facility, and was the largest coal-fired plant in the US by generation (Phil McKenna, Inside Climate News, 21 November 2022).
- Plant Miller, operated by Alabama Power, was the top US greenhouse gas emitter for 2023 for the ninth year in a row, per Dennis Pillion (Inside Climate News, 16 October 2024). The article names Southern Company as Alabama Power's parent and says Alabama Power has announced no plans to retire any of Plant Miller's four units.

BOTTOM LINE
The City of Birmingham owns the museum and, in the Encyclopedia of Alabama's undated account, paid about half of its budget, with individuals, corporations and foundations paying the rest; its endowment trust holds about $33.8 million. The weak point is the Alabama Power logo in the Founders Circle: the utility's own figures put 71% of its 2025 generation in natural gas and coal, and it operates Plant Miller, the top US greenhouse gas emitter in 2023.
