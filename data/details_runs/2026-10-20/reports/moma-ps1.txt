MOMA PS1: New York, USA
ID: moma-ps1
Website: https://www.momaps1.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 22-25 Jackson Avenue in Long Island City, Queens, an affiliate of the Museum of Modern Art (museum website).
- Founded in 1971 by Alanna Heiss; opened in 1976 as the P.S.1 Contemporary Art Center in an 1892 public school building; affiliated with MoMA since January 2000 and renamed MoMA PS1 in 2010 (Wikipedia).
- Open 12 to 6 pm, Saturday from 10 am; closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays. Admission is free. Wheelchair accessible, with wheelchairs at the entrance (museum website).

FUNDING
MoMA PS1: private: MoMA's trustees and donors, with city support
- Affiliate of the Museum of Modern Art since 2000; MoMA's board chair, Marie-Josée Kravis, and president, Ronnie F. Heyman, sit on the PS1 board ex officio, with the Mayor of New York City and other city officials (MoMA Annual Report, Donors 2023-2024).
- The City of New York provided about 8 percent of the operating budget in 2019 (Wikipedia). PS1 has been a member of the City's Cultural Institutions Group, with the Department of Cultural Affairs, since 1982 (museum website).
- MoMA's 2023-2024 report names MoMA PS1 donors. Champions include the Mellon Foundation and Agnes Gund; Leaders include Allianz, Bloomberg Philanthropies and the Luma Foundation; Benefactors include Paula H. Crown, Eva and Glenn Dubin and Pace Gallery.

Red flags: protests at the museum over MoMA trustees, 2019 to 2020
- Phil Collins withdrew his work from "Theater of Operations: The Gulf Wars 1991-2011" at PS1. Michael Rakowitz asked PS1 to divest from MoMA board members Larry Fink and Leon Black or, if MoMA would not, from its parent. He asked three times to pause his video "Return" and post his protest statement; after a 3 November 2019 meeting the curators said the two museums have separate boards, and the museum refused (The Art Newspaper, Zachary Small, 2 December 2019).
- On 11 January 2020 Rakowitz paused the video himself; the museum later restarted it and took down his plaque against his stated wishes. Thirty-seven artists in the show signed an open letter asking MoMA to separate itself from trustees tied to private prison companies and a defence contractor involved in the Iraq wars (Hyperallergic, Jasmine Weber, 17 January 2020).
- On 1 March 2020 more than 70 MoMA Divest activists gathered at PS1 for the closing of the exhibition. They said Fink's firm BlackRock is invested in the two largest US private prison companies and Black's company in Constellis Holdings, formerly Blackwater Worldwide. PS1 removed works by Ali Yass before the action (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 2 March 2020).
- Connection: the protests were aimed at MoMA trustees and held at PS1. MoMA's 2023-2024 report lists Laurence D. Fink and Leon D. Black among MoMA's trustees.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper (Carlie Porterfield, "US Senator calls for investigation into MoMA trustee Leon Black's business ties to Jeffrey Epstein", 19 August 2025): Black paid Epstein at least $158 million between 2012 and 2017. Senator Ron Wyden wrote to the IRS on 31 July 2025 accusing it of failing to properly audit suspicious transactions tied to Epstein's tax and estate planning services. Black's spokesperson said an independent review found he had not engaged in any wrongdoing.
- Same article: more than 150 artists signed an open letter demanding Black's removal as MoMA board chair. He remains a trustee along with Glenn Dubin, whom the article calls another billionaire client of Epstein's.
- Connection: PS1 has been affiliated with MoMA since January 2000. MoMA's 2023-2024 report lists Black and Dubin among MoMA's trustees and Eva and Glenn Dubin among MoMA PS1 Benefactors.

BOTTOM LINE
MoMA PS1 is paid for by private donors and MoMA's circle, and the City of New York supplied about 8 percent of the operating budget in 2019 (Wikipedia). Artists and activists protested at PS1 in 2019 and 2020 over MoMA trustees Larry Fink and Leon Black. Black, a MoMA trustee, paid Epstein at least $158 million between 2012 and 2017, according to The Art Newspaper.
