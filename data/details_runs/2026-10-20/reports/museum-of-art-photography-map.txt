MUSEUM OF ART & PHOTOGRAPHY (MAP): Bengaluru, India
ID: museum-of-art-photography-map
Website: https://map-india.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum at 22 Kasturba Road in central Bengaluru, within walking distance of Cubbon Park, MG Road and Vidhana Soudha metro stations (MAP website).
- Founded in 2016 by the businessman and collector Abhishek Poddar (MAP website); it launched as a digital museum in 2020 and opened to the public on 18 February 2023 (Wikipedia).
- A unit and project of the Art & Photography Foundation, a not-for-profit (MAP website).
- The five-floor, 44,000 sq ft building was designed by Mathew & Ghosh Architects and includes five galleries, an auditorium, a research library, a classroom, a restoration lab and a sculpture garden (Wikipedia). The land was bought through a donation from the Poddar family (USA Art News).
- Holds some 60,000 works, most of them donated by Poddar (USA Art News).
- Open Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 18:30, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 19:30, closed Monday; last entry 30 minutes before closing. Entry to all exhibitions is free on Tuesdays, other days by "Access Pass" sold online. The pages read give no ticket prices and no accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museum of Art & Photography: private philanthropy: the Poddar family, founding patrons and corporate donors
- MAP states that it receives no government funding and is governed by the foundation's trustees, an advisory board and an international advisory panel.
- The Poddar family donated the land and, with other philanthropists and companies, partly funded the building (USA Art News, July 2022).
- Collection: Poddar donated more than 7,000 artworks in 2017, and Christie's auctioned a large part of the family's personal collection in December 2016, with the proceeds used to establish the museum (Wikipedia, an article carrying a promotional-content banner).
- Founding patrons named on MAP's support page, undated: Avanee Foundation, Axis Bank, Bluespring, Citi, HBSF, HT Parekh Foundation, Infosys Foundation, Manipal Foundation, Mphasis F1 Foundation, RIST, Sasken Technologies, Tata Trusts, Torrent Group UNM Foundation and Wipro Foundation.
- Other building donors named by USA Art News: Kiran Mazumdar Shaw, Sunil Munjal, Citi and Tata.
- Poddar is the son of the industrialist Bimal Poddar and a trustee of the foundation; Wikipedia links him to Matheson Bosanquet, where he became managing director in 1997, and to Sua Explosives & Accessories, which makes mining explosives.
- No reporting found of donation amounts or of the foundation's trustees named on the museum's own governance page (it links to a separate foundation site).

Red flags: Protest over the Venkatappa Art Gallery deal (2015 to 2016)
- In July 2015 (The News Minute gives June 2015) Karnataka's tourism and archaeology, museums and heritage departments signed a memorandum of understanding handing management of the government-owned Venkatappa Art Gallery to MAP, then a division of the Tasveer Foundation. Local artists learned of it in early 2016 (Hyperallergic).
- Artists formed the VAG Forum and held near-weekly actions over the summer of 2016, including a sit-in at Town Hall, a human chain and a day of painting and sculpting at the gallery. A Change.org petition against the deal had over 1,000 signatures (Hyperallergic).
- Poddar replied that the initiative "is not in the realm of privatization nor is it a takeover" and that existing collections would remain a key attraction (Hyperallergic).
- On 12 May 2016 the artist Suresh Kumar said the government had not changed its stance (The News Minute). No reporting found of the final outcome of the memorandum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is a single episode, the 2016 fight over the Venkatappa Art Gallery. No criticism found of the donors or of the museum since it opened in 2023.
1. Hyperallergic: Deepa Bhasthi, "Artists Protest Collector's Takeover of Indian Museum" (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/artists-protest-collectors-takeover-of-indian-museum/
- Artists object that the memorandum gives the foundation and its founder, a collector and dealer, near-total control over programming and the collection, and fear it could be used to show his collection and business interests and make the space less available to emerging artists.
- Notes the Department of Kannada and Culture, which oversees the gallery, was not consulted, and that the Tasveer Foundation had no public website or listed trustees.
- Quotes the artist Pushpamala N, writing as "The Phantom Lady", calling the deal a "sinister new precedent". The VAG Forum wanted the memorandum scrapped and welcomed MAP in Bengaluru but not at the gallery.
2. The News Minute: Sarayu Srinivasan, on the VAG dispute (12 May 2016)
URL: https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/no-answer-sight-bengalurus-artists-keep-questions-vag-43129
- Reports the government had not met any of the Forum's demands and artists wanted written assurances about access to the space.
- MAP associate director Nathaniel Gaskell said rents would be raised only to affordable levels and deserving artists could use the space free on some days. Kumar attributes to Poddar a remark that Tasveer should have a say in what is shown if it has invested money; this is the artists' account.
- News outlet, not art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private and mostly named: the Poddar family's land and collection plus a list of founding patrons, though amounts are not published. The weak points are the 2016 deal to take over a public art gallery, which artists opposed, and the lack of named trustees on the museum's own page. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes donor amounts.
