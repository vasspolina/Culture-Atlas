ASIA ART ARCHIVE (AAA): Hong Kong
ID: asia-art-archive-hong-kong
Website: https://aaa.org.hk

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit research library and archive documenting recent art histories in Asia, at 233 Hollywood Road in Sheung Wan, Hong Kong, with independently run offices in New York and New Delhi (Wikipedia; AAA America).
- Founded in 2000 by Claire Hsu, Johnson Chang Tsong-zung and Ronald Arculli; Hsu was the first Executive Director and Christopher K. Ho succeeded her in September 2021 (Wikipedia). It is a registered charity in Hong Kong (Wikipedia).
- The library was renovated in 2022, increasing shelving capacity by fifty percent (Wikipedia). In July 2025 AAA launched an archiving facility with a digitisation lab, a processing room and archival storage (Artomity).
- Over 120,000 records were in the online catalogue as of January 2023; about 70% of acquisitions are donations (Wikipedia). The CIMAM award profile puts donations at over 85% of the library collection.
- Programmes: exhibitions, public and educational programmes, artist residencies, publishing, archival workshops and itinerant library projects; a post-custodial model digitises personal archives and returns the originals to their owners (CIMAM).
- Governance: a board of directors and a rotating Advisory Board; Wikipedia says Jane DeBevoise chaired the board from 2003, and that in 2021 Hsu became co-chair with Benjamin Cha.

FUNDING
Asia Art Archive: mainly private: an annual donated-art auction and donors, with a small share of public funding
- CIMAM's award profile says income comes from private donations, annual fundraisers featuring donated artworks and public funding, which makes up less than 10% of the budget. An endowment was established in 2010.
- The annual fundraiser is "a major source of support for its operations, programmes and endowment" (e-flux, 2012). The 2012 endowment campaign had raised USD 4.3 million of a USD 6.5 million goal since 2007. The 2012 auction was previewed at Sotheby's Hong Kong, with works donated by artists and galleries including Gagosian Gallery, Pearl Lam Fine Arts and ShanghART Gallery.
- 2014: Van Cleef & Arpels was lead sponsor for the second consecutive year; the auction offered 80 donated works, previewed at Christie's Hong Kong offices (e-flux). AAA states it relies on government agencies, corporations and individuals.
- 2025 (25th anniversary): over 65 donated works; sponsors Altaya Wines, Christie's Hong Kong, Chubb, Jebsen, The Peninsula Hong Kong, SALONI and WTW | Willis; venue partner The Henderson (Artomity).
- 2026: more than 50 donated works; supporters Altaya Wines, Christie's Hong Kong, Chubb, Jebsen and WTW | Willis; venue sponsor H Queen's (ArtReview). The two-year "New Archive" digital archiving project is supported by the Arts Capacity Development Funding Scheme.
- The New York office, Asia Art Archive in America, is funded by the New York State Council on the Arts, the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs in Partnership with the City Council, and the Ruth Foundation for the Arts (AAA America).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One news piece, not from the art press, reports a criticism by an artists' group whose trip AAA sponsored.
1. Hong Kong Free Press: Holmes Chan, "Interview: 'A little bit of espionage' - The Guerrilla Girls attack gender and class bias at Art Basel Hong Kong" (2018)
URL: https://hongkongfp.com/2018/03/31/interview-little-bit-espionage-guerrilla-girls-attack-gender-class-bias-art-basel-hong-kong/
- Reports that the Guerrilla Girls "criticise Asia Art Archive, one of the sponsors for their trip, for inadequate diversity in its archives".
- Names the Hong Kong Museum of Art and M+ as having less than 20% women in their collections; the Hong Kong Museum of Art replied that this reflects the historical situation of the time and M+ did not respond.
- The Guerrilla Girls' Zubeida Agha said the group works with organisations "willing to be self-critical".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind AAA is private and public-arts money: donated works sold at an annual auction, corporate sponsors, an endowment and, per CIMAM, under 10% public funding. The sponsors named are corporate and change with each auction; the 2018 criticism concerned gender diversity in its archive, not its funders.
