INSTITUTO CUBANO DEL ARTE E INDUSTRIA CINEMATOGRÁFICOS (ICAIC): Havana, Cuba
ID: instituto-cubano-del-arte-e-industria-cinematograficos-icaic
Website: http://www.cubacine.cult.cu (checked 2026-11-20; returned a 502 error)

OVERVIEW
- State film body at Calle 23 No. 1155, between 10 and 12, Vedado, Havana (EcuRed, state encyclopedia).
- Created by Law No. 169 of the Council of Ministers, made public on 24 March 1959 over the signatures of Prime Minister Fidel Castro and Education Minister Armando Hart (EcuRed).
- EcuRed calls it the national film authority and main producer of features, documentaries and animation; it runs the Cinemateca de Cuba, film archives, animation studios and the national distributor.
- It created the Cinemateca in 1960, the Grupo de Experimentación Sonora in 1969, the Havana film festival in 1979 and the Muestra Joven in 2000 (EcuRed).
- Ramón Samada left the presidency at his own request in 2023 and Susana Molina became acting president (OnCuba, July 2023). Alexis Triana was president in March 2025 (Prensa Latina).

FUNDING
ICAIC: state-funded through the Ministry of Culture, with co-productions and foreign restoration partners
- EcuRed lists ICAIC among the institutes of the Ministry of Culture's system.
- From the 1990s, to face scarce resources, ICAIC took on co-productions and sold technical services to foreign productions (EcuRed).
- Fondo de Fomento del Cine Cubano: created under Decree-Law 373 of 2019 to finance independent films, separate from ICAIC's own budget. The state funds it, and it can also take income from Cuban or foreign people and companies (OnCuba, May 2020). Its seventh round, announced in October 2025, received 53 short-film projects; Prensa Latina, a state agency, says it has backed about a hundred projects.
- Restoration: Memorias del subdesarrollo was restored by the Cineteca di Bologna's L'Immagine Ritrovata laboratory, financed by The Film Foundation's World Cinema Project, and presented at Cannes in 2016 by ICAIC (Festival de Cannes).
- Triana credited the Fundación del Patrimonio Fílmico Colombiano with helping restore Cuban classics (Prensa Latina, March 2025).

Red flags: 2023 broadcast of La Habana de Fito and the filmmakers' assembly
- La Habana de Fito, by Juan Pin Vilar, was shown on state television in an unfinished version without its director's permission; an open letter to the Ministry of Culture drew more than 600 signatures and the Asamblea de Cineastas Cubanos formed in June 2023 (EFE in El Universal).
- July 2023: the Asamblea said "el cine cubano no es el Icaic" ("Cuban cinema is not ICAIC", own translation) after the culture minister said Cuban cinema is the cinema made by ICAIC (OnCuba).
- December 2023: it said the forces controlling ICAIC pressed the festival to exclude La Habana de Fito and Llamadas desde Moscú (EFE in El Universal).

Red flags: 2020 removal of Sueños al pairo from the Muestra Joven
- The Muestra's board said the ICAIC presidency censored the documentary on Mike Porcel for political and ideological differences and denied its makers the archive images; co-director José Luis Aparicio said ICAIC objected to its treatment of the Mariel exodus (OnCuba, 27 February 2020).
- Seven directors withdrew their films and ICAIC postponed the 19th Muestra Joven (OnCuba).

Red flags: 2018 dispute over Quiero hacer una película
- ICAIC moved Yimit Ramírez's unfinished film to a 24-seat room, Ramírez withdrew it, and ICAIC's leadership defended the institution's right to make its own decisions (OnCuba, 2020).

Red flags: 2016 veto of Santa y Andrés
- ICAIC dropped Carlos Lechuga's film after it was preselected for the 38th Havana festival; Fernando Pérez called the veto an error and a policy of exclusion unsustainable (OnCuba, 8 December 2016).

Red flags: 2014 withdrawal of Regreso a Ítaca
- Twelve filmmakers said the highest authorities of the Ministry of Culture and ICAIC forced the festival to withdraw Laurent Cantet's film; all the National Film Prize winners later joined their statement (OnCuba, 13 April 2015).

Overall: Criticism comes from Cuban filmmakers through OnCuba and EFE; it attacks ICAIC's decisions on what can be shown, not its films.

BOTTOM LINE
ICAIC is a state body paid through the Ministry of Culture, with foreign foundations helping restore its films; no flagged funder is named. Its weak point is a run of films pulled or vetoed from 2014 to 2023 and the filmmakers' assembly formed against it. Worth watching: who sits on the Fondo de Fomento juries and whether the Asamblea's demands are met.
