MAIIAM – Chiang Mai, Thailand
Website: https://maiiam.com/en (ok, checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- MAIIAM Contemporary Art Museum, 122 Moo 7 Tonpao, San Kamphaeng District, Chiang Mai. Opened July 2016 with the retrospective "Apichatpong Weerasethakul: The Serenity of Madness" (Bangkok Post, Larry's List, ArtAsiaPacific).
- Founded by Jean Michel Beurdeley, his late wife Patsri Bunnag and Eric Bunnag Booth to show the family's Thai contemporary art collection, built since the early 1990s. Named after Chao Chom Iam, a royal consort of King Rama V and a relative of Booth (stated by the space; Bangkok Post).
- Sources differ on the relationship: the museum and Bangkok Post call Booth the couple's son; ArtAsiaPacific calls Beurdeley his stepfather.
- Building: a converted 3,000 m² warehouse with a mirrored façade, designed by the firm all(zone) (Bangkok Post).
- Programme: permanent collection display, changing exhibitions, workshops and outreach to students, notably from Chiang Mai University (Chiang Mai CityLife, 2019).
- Open Friday to Monday, 10am–6pm (stated by the space). No admission prices, discounts or accessibility information found.

FUNDING
MAIIAM: appears self-funded by the founding family.
- Privately owned by the Beurdeley-Bunnag family (Bangkok Post); "a private museum funded by Booth" (Chiang Mai CityLife, 2019).
- South Korea's Yanghyun Foundation supported the opening Apichatpong retrospective (Bangkok Post).
- No reporting found on the source of the family's wealth, on corporate sponsors, or on state support.
- The museum publishes no accounts, funder list or board.

BOTTOM LINE
MAIIAM is a private family museum that, according to press reports, is paid for by its founders, mainly Eric Bunnag Booth. No red flags found. The only things worth watching are that it publishes no financial information and that no source explains where the family's money comes from.
