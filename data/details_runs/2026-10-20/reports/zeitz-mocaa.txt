ZEITZ MOCAA: Cape Town, South Africa
ID: zeitz-mocaa
Website: https://zeitzmocaa.museum

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art from Africa and its diaspora in the Silo District, South Arm Road, V&A Waterfront, Cape Town (stated by the museum). It describes itself as a public, not-for-profit museum.
- Opened on 22 September 2017; Wikipedia lists the V&A Waterfront and Jochen Zeitz as founders and Mark Coetzee as founding executive director and chief curator.
- Named for Jochen Zeitz, a German businessman who built the collection from 2008 with Coetzee and received naming rights (Daily Maverick, Rebecca Davis, 26 September 2017). Zeitz loaned the collection and later donated it (The Art Newspaper, John Kampfner, 22 April 2024).
- Building: a grain silo built from 1921 to 1924, converted from 2014 by Heatherwick Studio (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, the BMW Centre for Art Education and the Atelier studio space (stated by the museum). Koyo Kouoh was executive director and chief curator from May 2019 until her death in May 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:30 (stated by the museum). At opening in 2017 the adult fee was R180 (Daily Maverick).

FUNDING
Zeitz MOCAA: public-private: the V&A Waterfront and its investors built it; Zeitz, corporate sponsors and foundations support it
- The conversion cost more than R500 million, borne by the V&A Waterfront and its investors, including the Public Investment Corporation (Daily Maverick, 2017).
- The museum's supporters page lists four founders: Growthpoint, Jochen Zeitz, the Public Investment Corporation and the V&A Waterfront. It lists Allan Gray and Bloomberg as corporate members and the Mellon Foundation, Hazendal Wine Estate and BMW Group South Africa as sponsors.
- BMW Group South Africa agreed a three-year naming-rights partnership for 2024 to 2026 that renamed its Centre for Art Education and supports curatorial and education programming (BMW Group press release, 12 September 2024).
- The Mellon Foundation funds much of a fellowship scheme placing about six young people a year at the museum for 12 months; Gucci and BMW are named as sponsors. Kouoh enlarged the board of trustees, made it a paying board and set up a Global Council and an American Friends organisation (The Art Newspaper, John Kampfner, "How Koyo Kouoh is getting Zeitz Mocaa museum back on track", 22 April 2024).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Four pieces from 2017 to 2019 name the museum. Two criticise its governance, financial openness and public access; one reports allegations against its founding director; one is a mostly favourable feature that records criticism.
1. The Art Newspaper: Matthew Blackman, "Zeitz Mocaa: Africa's private 'Tate Modern' must do more for its public" (2018)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/comment/africa-s-private-tate-modern-must-do-more-for-its-public
- Criticises the museum: argues its curatorial, educational and governance record falls short of its promise a year after opening, and says the museum "has steadfastly declined to reveal details about its finances".
- Notes only 192 schoolchildren took part in one programme in the first year, against 9,000 students engaged overall, and says the museum has yet to live up to its "public benefit" status.
2. Daily Maverick: Rebecca Davis, "Zeitz Mocaa: Cape Town's new art museum stuns and provokes" (2017)
URL: https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2017-09-26-zeitz-mocaa-cape-towns-new-art-museum-stuns-and-provokes/
- General-press feature, not art press, and favourable overall; it closes by wishing the project well. It says the R180 fee puts the museum beyond the budget of most South Africans, and raises a conflict of interest because Coetzee handled acquisitions for both the museum and Zeitz's personal collection.
- Records gallerist Matthew Blackman's 2015 open letter arguing that the museum's buying habits were skewing South African art markets, which it calls "currently inadequately substantiated".
3. Flash Art: Kirsty Cockerill, "The Main Complaint" (2019)
URL: https://flash---art.com/2019/03/the-main-complaint-zeitz-mocaa-cape-town/
- A review of the exhibition The Main Complaint (Flash Art, 11 March 2019) that praises the show; it criticises the museum, saying it shirked the ethics expected of a public museum and describing "a governance structure unable to meet its responsibilities".
4. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Zeitz Mocaa director Mark Coetzee embroiled in #MeToo scandal" (2018)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/05/21/zeitz-mocaa-director-mark-coetzee-embroiled-in-metoo-scandal
- News report (21 May 2018), not criticism of the museum: City Press reported that assistant curators alleged Coetzee behaved inappropriately and abusively; Coetzee was suspended on 16 May and then resigned according to the museum, and the trustees said an inquiry into his professional conduct had begun.

BOTTOM LINE
Zeitz MOCAA was built with more than R500 million from the V&A Waterfront and its investors, including the Public Investment Corporation (Daily Maverick), and houses the collection of its namesake Jochen Zeitz. Its supporters page lists Growthpoint, Zeitz, the Public Investment Corporation and the V&A Waterfront as founders, and BMW Group South Africa, the Mellon Foundation and Hazendal Wine Estate as sponsors; BMW's naming partnership covers 2024 to 2026.
