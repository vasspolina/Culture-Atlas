NATIONAL MUSEUM OF BOSNIA AND HERZEGOVINA: Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina
ID: national-museum-of-bosnia-and-herzegovina
Website: https://zemaljskimuzej.ba/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of archaeology, ethnology and natural sciences at Zmaja od Bosne 3 in Sarajevo; in Bosnian, Zemaljski muzej Bosne i Hercegovine.
- Founded on 1 February 1888; the National Government was the founder and Kosta Hörmann the first director (museum history page).
- The complex of four pavilions and a botanical garden was begun in 1909 and opened in 1913.
- It keeps the Sarajevo Haggadah in a dedicated room.
- Departments: archaeology, ethnology, natural sciences, conservation and restoration, and common services with the library.
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 19:00, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday; adults BAM 20 (citizens of Bosnia and Herzegovina BAM 10), international students BAM 10, ages 6 to 18 BAM 2, preschool children free.

FUNDING
National Museum of Bosnia and Herzegovina: patchy public money: the University of Sarajevo pays salaries, small ministry and municipal grants, ticket income
- Yearly needs are about KM 1.75 million for salaries, utilities and running costs (museum's 2025 results).
- The University of Sarajevo covers about KM 1.085 million of salaries, paid straight to staff accounts and not booked as museum income.
- The Federal Ministry of Education and Science and Novi Grad Municipality gave KM 274,950 in 2025.
- The museum's own income was KM 524,010 to 30 September 2025; it sold 45,719 tickets in 2024.
- 58 of 105 planned posts were filled in 2025.
- The Government of Japan and JICA are partners in a project for IT network and archive equipment.
- In January 2022 Sarajevo Canton prime minister Edin Forto and University of Sarajevo rector Rifat Škrijelj announced a plan to make the museum a teaching and research base of the university (University of Sarajevo).

Red flags: protests over the Sarajevo Haggadah proceeds
- The museum decided to give the proceeds of its book on the Sarajevo Haggadah, and of tickets to the Haggadah room, to the Palestinian people (RFE/RL, 6 August 2025).
- The Jewish Community of Mostar, led by Amir Kabiri, wrote to the museum and called the decision political and antisemitic.
- B'nai B'rith International condemned the decision and the Anti-Defamation League said it was "appalled" (translated from Bosnian); Pinchas Goldschmidt, president of the Conference of European Rabbis, called it shameful.
- Milorad Dodik called it hostility toward a people. The museum said it would go on, and that staff had received threatening emails.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The museum is one of seven culture institutions whose state status the Parliament of Bosnia and Herzegovina never confirmed, so no government budget lists them (RFE/RL, 2025).
- On 21 November 2024 High Representative Christian Schmidt ordered the state to pass a law taking on their funding within 60 days; the Council of Ministers never sent a law to Parliament and the deadline lapsed.
- Republika Srpska officials dispute the institutions' state status. Finance Minister Srđan Amidžić said a Presidency request to fund them was impossible because they are not budget beneficiaries.
- The seven institutions need about 7 million convertible marks a year between them.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public and carries no objection, but it is thin and improvised: a university pays the salaries and the state pays nothing. The weak points are the unresolved legal status and the 2025 dispute with Jewish organisations over the Haggadah proceeds. Worth watching: a state funding law, and what the museum does with the Haggadah money.
