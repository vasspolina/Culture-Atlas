GALERI NASIONAL INDONESIA: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: galeri-nasional-indonesia
Website: https://galeri-nasional.or.id (checked 2026-11-20; down: the domain did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- State art gallery at Jalan Medan Merdeka Timur 14, Central Jakarta, opposite Gambir Station (Wikipedia; Popmama, May 2026).
- Dedicated as the national gallery on 8 May 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Gedung A, the main building, was built in 1817 as an Indies residence with materials from Kasteel Batavia; it housed the Carpentier Alting Stichting school from 1900 to 1961 and an army brigade after 1965, before returning to the education department in 1981 and 1982 (Wikipedia).
- It holds 1,770 works, including Raden Saleh, Affandi and Basuki Abdullah, and foreign artists such as Wassily Kandinsky, Sonia Delaunay and Zao Wou Ki (Wikipedia).
- Jarot Mahendra spoke as the person in charge of the gallery unit in January 2025 (Antara).
- The whole site closed on 12 October 2026 for revitalisation, until further notice (iNews, 11 October 2026).
- Before the closure it opened Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 19:00, with free entry after online registration (Popmama, May 2026).

FUNDING
Galeri Nasional Indonesia: public: Indonesia's Ministry of Culture
- Owner: the Ministry of Culture through the Indonesian Heritage Agency (Wikipedia). The ministry is one of President Prabowo Subianto's new ministries (The Jakarta Post, 3 January 2025).
- Operating model: the Indonesian Heritage Agency became a public service agency (BLU) running 18 museums, including the national gallery, and 34 heritage sites; as a BLU it keeps ticket income and investor support instead of paying all revenue to the treasury as non-tax state revenue (Fortune Indonesia, April 2024).
- In April 2026 it still shared one BLU with the national museum; agency head Indira Esti Nurjadin said units must meet rising non-tax revenue targets, and Culture Minister Fadli Zon said the gallery should ideally run as its own BLU (RRI, 24 April 2026).
- Revitalisation: architect Jasin Tedjasukmana discussed the first design with Fadli Zon on 31 August 2026, restoring Gedung A and the auditorium and adding a rooftop restaurant (RRI).
- The contractor PT Nindya Karya broke ground on 23 October 2026 on a design-and-build contract covering the landscape, Gedung A, C and D and the building services (Kontan, 23 October 2026).

Red flags: 2024 cancellation of Yos Suprapto's exhibition
- The show "Kebangkitan: Tanah Untuk Kedaulatan Pangan", due to run from 20 December 2024 to 19 January 2025, was cancelled ten minutes before its opening on 19 December 2024 after Yos refused to conceal three more paintings; five of the works were said to portray former president Joko Widodo (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Curator Suwarno Wisetrotomo had called the works "too vulgar" and resigned; the gallery's notice blamed "unavoidable technical constraints", and Fadli Zon withdrew from opening the show (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Zamrud Sayta Negara, head of the museum and gallery team, said the gallery offered a postponement, not a ban; Fadli Zon said the five works had no link to food sovereignty and added "mungkin juga ada motif politik" (perhaps there is also a political motive, our translation) (Mongabay, 23 December 2024).
- LBH Jakarta called the request repression of free expression, and Komnas HAM wrote to the ministry and the gallery for an explanation (Mongabay).
- On 24 January 2025 Megawati Soekarnoputri criticised the cancellation at a PDI Perjuangan event, saying "ekspresi seni itu harus dihormati" (artistic expression must be respected, our translation) (CNN Indonesia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism centres on the 2024 cancellation; a 2025 essay also criticises the rehung permanent display.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Annette Meier, "Indonesian Artist's Exhibition Canceled Over Political Paintings" (2025)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/indonesian-artist-s-exhibition-canceled-over-political-paintings
- Reports the requests to hide the works and notes speculation of political pressure.
- Quotes Yos comparing it to censorship and calling it "an alarm for freedom of expression in Indonesia".
2. CIMAM Museum Watch: "Postponing Censorship? Contentious Positions that Undermine the Museum as a Space for Artistic Freedom" (1 April 2025)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/postponing-censorship-contentious-positions-that-undermine-the-museum-as-a-space-for-artistic-freedom/
- Argues that calling the show "postponed" can become a route to cancellation, a form of censorship; says the gallery did not answer repeated requests for comment.
3. The Jakarta Post: editorial board, "Authoritarian alarm" (2025)
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/01/03/authoritarian-alarm
- Argues the closure should alarm the country about free expression and that the gallery "refused to live in the present". A newspaper editorial, not art media.
4. Indonesia at Melbourne: Laurel MacLaren, "Indonesia's National Gallery rewrites history as Prabowo's revisionism takes hold" (21 October 2025)
URL: https://indonesiaatmelbourne.unimelb.edu.au/indonesias-national-gallery-rewrites-history-as-prabowos-revisionism-takes-hold/
- Says the permanent display relaunched in July 2025 by Fadli Zon, a co-founder of Prabowo's Gerindra party, removed the 2023 gallery on the Lekra movement and the persecution of artists after 1965.
- Objects to a wall text calling the Soeharto era one of "individual freedom" and to post-1998 works shown without context. A university blog essay, not art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: the Indonesian state pays and runs it, with no private sponsor on record. The weak point is political control under the Culture Ministry, shown by the 2024 cancellation and the criticised 2025 rehang; worth watching is how the 2026 rebuild is paid for and who programmes the reopened gallery.
