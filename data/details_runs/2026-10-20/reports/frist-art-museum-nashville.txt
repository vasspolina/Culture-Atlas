FRIST ART MUSEUM: Nashville, USA
ID: frist-art-museum-nashville
Website: https://fristartmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting art museum at 919 Broadway in downtown Nashville, with no permanent collection; it makes its own exhibitions and takes touring shows (Wikipedia).
- Opened in April 2001 as the Frist Center for the Visual Arts; renamed the Frist Art Museum in 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the Frist family: Thomas F. Frist Jr. and his family, through the Frist Foundation, proposed the building for a downtown art museum in the early 1990s; the Frist family contributed an endowment (Wikipedia).
- Housed in Nashville's former main Post Office, a white marble Art Deco building by Marr & Holman completed in 1934; the Post Office still uses about 15,000 square feet of the lower floor (Wikipedia; 2024 audited statements).
- Galleries, the free Conte Community Arts Gallery, the Gordon CAP Gallery and the hands-on Martin ArtQuest gallery (2025 community report; Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Monday, Thursday (to 8 pm), Friday and Saturday from 10 am, Sunday from 1 pm, closed Tuesday and Wednesday; adults $20, seniors, military and college students $16; free for members, guests 18 and younger, and up to four adults with SNAP, EBT or WIC cards; accessible parking and a barrier-free entrance (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Frist Art Museum: philanthropic: its own Frist Art Museum Foundation, the Frist Foundation and HCA's foundation, in a city-owned building
- 2024 total support and revenue $20.27 million: contributions and grants $12.30 million; support from the Frist Art Museum Foundation $2.65 million; memberships $1.30 million; admissions $1.11 million; investment income $1.30 million (2024 audited statements, Baker Tilly).
- The Frist Art Museum Foundation, set up in 1998, is the museum's sole member, appoints its board and pays it 4% a year of its average portfolio value; the museum expects to depend on it "for the foreseeable future" (2024 audited statements).
- In 1998 the Metropolitan Government of Nashville gave $19.9 million to its housing agency MDHA to buy the Post Office for $4.4 million and renovate it for $15.5 million; MDHA owns the building and leases it to the museum for $1 a year until 2097 (2024 audited statements).
- Donors of $100,000 or more in 2025: the Frist Foundation, HCA Healthcare Foundation, HCA Healthcare/TriStar Health, Pinnacle Financial Partners, the Dettwiller Foundation, E.A. Michelson Philanthropy, the Sandra Schatten Foundation, Neil Krugman and Lee Pratt, and the Tennessee Arts Commission (2025 community report).
- Board chair and president Billy Frist; Thomas F. Frist Jr. is chair emeritus; executive director Seth Feman (2025 community report).

Red flags: HCA, the Frist family's hospital company
- Thomas F. Frist Jr. co-founded the Hospital Corporation of America in 1968 with his father and Jack C. Massey and was its chairman until January 2002; his son Billy Frist joined the HCA board in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- In December 2000 HCA subsidiaries pleaded guilty and paid more than $840 million in fines, restitution and penalties; in June 2003 HCA agreed to pay $631 million in civil penalties and damages over false claims. The Justice Department called the case the "largest health care fraud case in U.S. history", with a total recovery of $1.7 billion (US Department of Justice, 2003).
- HCA Healthcare Foundation and HCA Healthcare/TriStar Health are current top-tier donors (2025 community report).

Red flags: nurses' protests at the Frist Gala
- National Nurses United has protested outside the Frist Gala each year since 2022; nearly 100 nurses protested on 27 April 2024 over HCA hospital cuts (National Nurses United, the union).
- About 100 health care workers protested at the 2025 gala; HCA called it "a predictable tactic" and said its staffing is "safe and appropriate" (WKRN, 2025).
- The union called a protest for 18 April 2026 over HCA's use of Palantir software in staffing (National Nurses United).
- Unverified: the Frist family "fund galas and art museums by pillaging and plundering the hospitals that HCA buys up", from nurse Molly Zenker in a National Nurses United release, a union in contract talks with HCA.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum sits on public money (a city-bought building at $1 a year) and runs on its own foundation's endowment, the Frist Foundation, HCA's foundation and local donors. The weak point is HCA: a company whose subsidiaries pleaded guilty to health care fraud in 2000, and which nurses picket at the museum's gala every spring. Worth watching: how much of the $12.3 million in gifts comes from HCA and the Frists.
