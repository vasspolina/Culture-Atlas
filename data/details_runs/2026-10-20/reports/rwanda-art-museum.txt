RWANDA ART MUSEUM: Kigali, Rwanda
ID: rwanda-art-museum
Website: https://www.rwandaheritage.gov.rw (checked 2026-10-25; live; this is the page of the parent body, the museum has no site of its own)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in Kanombe, in the Nyarugunga sector of Kicukiro district, about four kilometres east of Kigali airport (Wikipedia, KT Press, The New Times).
- The villa opened to the public in February 2009 as the Presidential Palace Museum. It was renamed the Rwanda Art Museum on 18 May 2018, International Museum Day (Wikipedia, The New Times).
- Not named for a person: the building was the residence of President Juvénal Habyarimana until 1994 and later of President Pasteur Bizimungu (The New Times, KT Press).
- Building and site: a 1970s villa. A 2008 report listed a swimming pool, a snake house, a tennis club and a bar and nightclub (The New Times, 18 October 2008). The wreckage of Habyarimana's plane, shot down on 6 April 1994, lies outside the garden walls (Foreign Policy).
- What is there: a permanent exhibition and temporary exhibitions; at the 2018 opening, 127 artworks by 51 artists under the theme "Art For Peace", a children's studio and a rule that only members of the Rwanda Art Council could exhibit (The New Times 2018).
- Visiting: open daily 9:00 to 17:00, from 12:00 on Umuganda days (last Saturday of the month); closed 7 April, 25 December and 1 January. Exhibition tickets in Rwandan francs: residents 1,000 (students) and 4,000 (adults); Africans 4,000 and 8,000; internationals 8,000 and 15,000 (Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy). Admission was free at the 2018 opening. No reporting found of accessibility arrangements.

FUNDING
Rwanda Art Museum: public: Rwandan state body
- Owned by the Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy, a "non commercial Public Institution" (Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy), set up by Presidential Decree No. 082/01 of 28 August 2020 as a merger of the Institute of National Museums of Rwanda, the Rwanda Academy of Language and Culture and Rwanda Archives and Library Services (Wikipedia). The Academy runs the national museums and sets their fees (Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy).
- Director General: Robert Masozera, appointed 11 November 2020 (Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy).
- No reporting found of the museum's or the Academy's budget, funders, donors or board. The 2008 conversion report said only that "the government plans" to develop facilities and did not say who paid (The New Times). The 2018 opening report also did not say who funded the museum or the art.
- Income from fees and event hire: the Academy lists wedding and event packages from 100,000 to 600,000 Rwandan francs.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the art museum in art media was found. The criticism that exists is of the same house in its earlier form as a political museum, from a political magazine and travel writing.
1. Foreign Policy: Michela Wrong, "The False Idols of Rwanda's Genocide" (2016)
URL: https://foreignpolicy.com/2016/04/27/the-false-idols-of-rwandas-genocide-war-paul-kagame-museums-culture/
- Argues the new government-run sites suggest a regime "anchoring its legacy in brick and mortar", with a selective view of Rwandan history.
- Says the former presidential villa and the crash debris serve as "implicit warnings to anyone contemplating a change in Rwanda's leadership." The ruling RPF officially denies involvement in Habyarimana's assassination.
- Names the Presidential Palace Museum, the museum's earlier name. A political essay, not art criticism.
2. Roads & Kingdoms: Jonathan W. Rosen, "In Rwanda, a Tour of Disaster" (2014)
URL: https://roadsandkingdoms.com/2014/in-rwanda-a-tour-of-disaster/
- Says the tour "stays far from politics" and focuses on the former president's eccentricities, even though no one has identified who brought down the plane.
- Names the museum under its earlier name; travel writing, not art criticism.
3. The New Times: Moses Opobo, opening report (2018)
URL: https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/152634/News/rwanda-art-museum-opens-to-public/amp
- Notes the show includes "a few other random well produced art works, even when they tell a different story" from the peace theme.
- A mild remark inside a news report, about the selection of works.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Wrong argues the government-run museums anchor the regime's legacy "in brick and mortar" and present a selective view of Rwandan history (Foreign Policy, 2016).
- The Academy that runs the Rwanda Art Museum runs the national museums, and the building is the former presidential villa, then the Presidential Palace Museum, that Wrong describes.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is state-owned and state-run, so its money is public and there is no corporate or private funder on record. The weak points are that no budget, donor or board information was found, and that the site is a former presidential residence beside the wreckage of the 1994 plane. Worth watching: how the Academy presents the site's history now that it holds art.
