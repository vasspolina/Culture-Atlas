NEW ORLEANS MUSEUM OF ART: New Orleans, United States
ID: new-orleans-museum-of-art-new-orleans
Website: https://noma.org/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The oldest fine arts museum in New Orleans, in City Park (Wikipedia).
- Founded with a grant from the sugar broker and collector Isaac Delgado, who asked the City Park Board for the site; it opened on 11 December 1911 as the Delgado Museum of Art (Wikipedia).
- The building was partly designed by the city's former chief engineer Benjamin Morgan Harrod; a $23 million expansion opened in 1993 (Wikipedia).
- The museum describes a collection of nearly 50,000 works, strongest in African art, photography, decorative arts and Japanese art. The 12-acre Sydney and Walda Besthoff Sculpture Garden opened in 2003 and grew by 6.5 acres in 2019; it holds nearly 100 works (museum website).
- Susan M. Taylor is the Montine McDaniel Freeman Director (museum website; Form 990). The Victoria and Albert Museum's "Marie Antoinette Style" opens there on 14 November 2027 (museum website).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 5pm, Wednesdays noon to 7pm. Adults $18 for Louisiana residents, $23 for others; under-20s free. The sculpture garden is free and open daily. Ramped entrances and accessible parking at the front (museum website).

FUNDING
New Orleans Museum of Art: privately funded: trustees, foundations and donors
- Finances: in 2024 the museum took in $9,981,201 and spent $13,087,681; contributions were $6,189,419, or 62 per cent of revenue. Net assets were $101,181,856. Taylor was paid $509,414 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- The Helis Foundation, "established and funded by the William Helis Family in 1955", pays for free admission for Louisiana residents on Wednesdays and for visitors aged 13 to 19 through its Art for All programme, launched in 2007 (foundation website; museum website).
- The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation gave $1 million in January 2020 to plan and staff a conservation centre, with posts for object and photography conservation (museum news release).
- Gifts: the Besthoff family gave Isamu Noguchi's 40-ton Mississippi Fountain, and a new donor group, the Collectors' Circle, backs contemporary programming (museum director's letter, Fall 2026).

Red flags: Helis Foundation oil money behind free admission, 2007 to now
- The foundation's post of 8 March 2021 calls William George Helis, whose family set up the foundation five years after his death in 1950, an oil magnate and a "hot oil" man. It describes its leader David Kerstein as "a soft-spoken lawyer, oil-company executive and philanthropist".
- The open letter of 12 October 2020 supporting #DismantleNOMA says the museum is sustained by "oil money and other harmful private funders" and cites the Helis Foundation (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara). Unverified: the letter's description of the foundation as the philanthropic arm of the Helis Oil & Gas Energy Project; no document in the piece shows it.

Red flags: 2020 open letters alleging racism at the museum
- In June 2020 five former workers published an open letter saying 30 employees resigned in two years because of a "toxic work environment and institutional racism". It alleged slurs, wage discrimination against Black workers, surveillance and a plantation parlor installed against the advice of Black staff; NOMA declined to comment on specifics, citing "privacy restrictions" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 25 June 2020).
- A second letter on 12 October 2020, signed by more than 150 people including the Guerrilla Girls and Michael Rakowitz, demanded Taylor's removal and an independent investigation and urged artists to withhold labour; it also said Delgado's wealth "derives from a sugar plantation". NOMA said it was "committed to being an anti-racist institution" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara).

Red flags: 2023 backlash over the hiring of a curator of African art
- On 29 June 2023 NOMA announced Amanda Maples, a white woman, as curator of African art, and more than 850 social media comments criticised the hire. A spokesperson said the museum was "listening closely to feedback" (The Art Newspaper, Torey Akers, 3 July 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
A private museum that ran a $3.1 million deficit in 2024 and relies on contributions for most of its income. The clearest funding weak point is the Helis Foundation, built on an oil fortune and led by an oil-company executive, which pays for its free days. Worth watching: a current published donor list.
