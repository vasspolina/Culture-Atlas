BAMPFA: Berkeley, United States
ID: bampfa-berkeley
Website: https://bampfa.org

OVERVIEW
- University art museum and film archive at 2155 Center Street, on the edge of the UC Berkeley campus and downtown Berkeley; part of the University of California, Berkeley (BAMPFA site). It holds more than 25,000 artworks and 18,000 films and videos (BAMPFA site).
- Founded in 1963 after the artist Hans Hofmann donated 45 paintings and $250,000; Sheldon Renan founded the Pacific Film Archive in 1967 (Wikipedia). Peter Selz was founding director; Lawrence Rinder directed from 2008 to 2020, and Julie Rodrigues Widholm has been executive director since August 2020 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Diller Scofidio + Renfro rebuilt and enlarged a 1939 Art Deco printing plant, after the Mario Ciampi building of 1970 was judged seismically unsafe in 1997 and a Toyo Ito design was cancelled in 2009. The museum reopened on 31 January 2016 (Wikipedia). Wikipedia puts the cost at about $100 million; Architectural Record puts it at about $112 million.
- On site: galleries, a film theatre, a shop and the Kopi Bar and Bakery (BAMPFA site).
- Visiting: galleries and shop Wednesday to Sunday, 11 am to 7 pm. Gallery admission is $18, $12 for over-65s, visitors with disabilities, non-UC college students, and UC alumni and retirees, $5 for ages 14 to 18, and free for members, UC Berkeley students, faculty and staff, children 13 and under and SNAP participants. Galleries are free on the first Thursday of each month (BAMPFA site).

FUNDING
BAMPFA: university-run, with foundation and public grants
- It is a unit of UC Berkeley (BAMPFA site).
- Top tier ($100,000 and above) on its supporters page, undated: The Eucalyptus Foundation, Walter & Elise Haas Fund, the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation, the Renate, Hans & Maria Hofmann Trust, the Institute of Museum and Library Services, the National Endowment for the Humanities, the Rotasa Foundation, the Terra Foundation for American Art, the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, the Mellon Foundation and the Henry Luce Foundation.
- $50,000 to $99,999: William K. Bowes Jr. Foundation, Frederick Hammersley Foundation, Helzel Family Foundation, Koret Foundation, the Joseph and Vera Long Foundation, the National Endowment for the Arts, the Bernard Osher Foundation, Van Doren Waxter and the Korea Foundation.
- $20,000 to $49,999: includes the U.S. Bank Foundation, the Louis B. Mayer Foundation and the Hollywood Foreign Press Association. Lower tiers include the City of Berkeley Civic Arts Program ($10,000 to $19,999), and Google Inc. and the Alameda County Arts Commission ($4,999 and below).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece criticises the 2016 building and the university's decisions about it.
1. Architectural Record: Josephine Minutillo, "Berkeley Art Museum and Pacific Film Archive by Diller Scofidio + Renfro" (1 March 2016)
URL: https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/11545-berkeley-art-museum-and-pacific-film-archive-by-diller-scofidio-renfro
- Argues the building is a compromise shaped by a nearly 20-year dispute over the site, and leaves open whether the university's choices were right given the loss of Ciampi's building and Ito's abandoned proposal.
- Finds the galleries too grand for small, subtle works, which "get lost" in the space, and says light-sensitive pieces needed skylight blackout shades at the opening.
- Criticises the building and the university's decisions.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hollywood Foreign Press Association: BAMPFA's undated supporters page lists it in the $20,000 to $49,999 tier. Wikipedia reports that on 21 February 2021 the Los Angeles Times found none of its members was Black, and that an LA Times investigation found it regularly paid members over $1 million a year for committee service. In May 2021 Netflix and Amazon Studios paused work with it, WarnerMedia later joined them, and on 10 May 2021 NBC said it would not air the 2022 Golden Globes. Wikipedia says the association was rebranded the Golden Globe Foundation on 12 June 2023, when Dick Clark Productions and Eldridge Industries bought the Golden Globes assets.
- Wikipedia reports that in 2025 about $230,000 in conservation funds from an Institute of Museum and Library Services grant was revoked by executive order, just before the "Routed West" quilt exhibition opened. The IMLS is also listed among BAMPFA's top-tier supporters.

BOTTOM LINE
BAMPFA is a University of California unit backed by large US foundations and federal arts agencies (supporters page, undated). The supporters page lists the Hollywood Foreign Press Association in the $20,000 to $49,999 tier, and about $230,000 in federal conservation funds was revoked in 2025.
