NATIONAL GALLERY OF ARTS TIRANA: Tirana, Albania
ID: national-gallery-of-arts-tirana
Website: https://galeriakombetare.gov.al (checked 2026-10-25; down, the domain does not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Albania's national art gallery on the "Martyrs of the Nation" Boulevard in central Tirana (Wikipedia). It closed for reconstruction in October 2021 (Wikipedia, whose infobox says until December 2023); Shqiptarja reported on 15 October 2025 that the work was still going on.
- Traces its origin to a group of Albanian artists and the Arts Committee of 1946; the Gallery of Arts opened to the public on 11 January 1954 (Wikipedia). The article gives 29 November 1974 for the new venue's opening but its infobox gives 1956, so the dates conflict.
- Names: it was the National Gallery of Arts. In July 2018 Culture Minister Mirela Kumbaro said it would be renamed the National Museum of Fine Arts (BalkanWeb). Shqiptarja (15 October 2025) calls Adela Demetja the new director and the first woman in the post; she told Report TV that the idea is to return it to a Museum of Fine Arts but "this is still an open discussion" (translation). Wikipedia's infobox lists Erzen Shkololli as director.
- Building: a cultural monument; Kumbaro said in 2018 that by law it would be rehabilitated, not demolished (BalkanWeb, translation). In March 2021 Culture Minister Elva Margariti presented a design by Atelier 4 that restores the building and adds a doubled volume at the rear with an underground section (Shqiptarja).
- Collection: 340 works at the 1974 opening; Shqiptarja (2025) gives about 4,800 works, and Wikipedia says over 5,000, with a permanent collection focused on Socialist Realism. Shqiptarja reported in January 2023 that the fund is kept whole in a museum while the gallery is closed.
- Visiting: closed. No opening hours or admission found.

FUNDING
National Gallery of Arts Tirana: mainly public: the Albanian state, under the Ministry of Economy, Culture and Innovation
- Wikipedia says the gallery operates under the Ministry of Economy, Culture and Innovation.
- The reconstruction was priced at about 1.365 billion lek over three years (Margariti, March 2021, Shqiptarja; the Ministry of Culture figure, Citizens.al, March 2024). Acting director Entela Çipa put it at 1 billion lek in January 2023 and called it a government investment (Shqiptarja, from her Report TV interview).
- Çipa said in January 2023 that groundwater problems appeared after the Lana river was diverted and a nearby building was built, and that the original deadline was October 2023 (Shqiptarja).
- Çipa also said the gallery has no board and seven staff, including three restoration specialists.
- No reporting found of private, corporate or foreign funders, of annual accounts or of an operating budget.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the gallery itself in art media. One 2020 open letter names a plan to demolish it, and a 2024 news analysis criticises the lack of explanation for the closure.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Open Letter Condemns the 'Artwashing' of Albanian Prime Minister's Politics" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/565114/open-letter-condemns-artwashing-albania/
- Reports an open letter by Jonida Gashi and Vincent W.J. van Gerven Oei, with 36 signatories at publication, asking the international art community to stop promoting Edi Rama's work.
- The letter says money goes to government "vanity projects" while other national institutions are underfunded, and that "plans are currently underway to demolish the National Gallery of Arts". It treats the 17 May 2020 demolition of the National Theatre as a "point of no return".
- Aimed at the prime minister, not at the gallery's management. The ministry later described the work as rehabilitation (see above).
2. Citizens.al: Hana Vranac, "Albania and Bosnia closed cultural institutions for a better future?" (2024)
URL: https://www.citizens.al/en/2024/10/31/Albania-and-Bosnia-close-cultural-institutions-for-a-better-future/
- Argues the closure is unexplained: media reported three years of reconstruction, the ministry site no longer gives a deadline, and three years had passed.
- Says she found no document describing the reconstruction plan and was told by the gallery to contact the ministry.
- A news outlet, not an art-press title.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is Albanian state money, with no private or foreign funder named. The weak points are the long closure since October 2021, two cost figures, 1.365 billion lek (2021) and 1 billion lek (2023), with no published accounts, and the absence of a board. Worth watching are the reopening date, the final cost and whether the institution becomes a museum.
