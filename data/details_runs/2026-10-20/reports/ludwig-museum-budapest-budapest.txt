LUDWIG MUSEUM BUDAPEST: Budapest, Hungary
ID: ludwig-museum-budapest-budapest
Website: https://www.ludwigmuseum.hu

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art in the Müpa Budapest building (formerly the Palace of the Arts), in the wing facing the Danube, with 3,300 square metres of exhibition space (Ludwig Museum).
- Founded by the German collectors Peter and Irene Ludwig: the contract to establish it was signed in Budapest on 21 March 1989, and the Ludwig Foundation donated 70 artworks to the state of Hungary to establish the collection (Ludwig Museum).
- The first permanent exhibition opened on 4 June 1991 in Building A of the Buda Castle Palace; from 1996 to 2005 the museum was housed in the A wing of the Hungarian National Gallery, and in 2005 it moved to the Müpa (Ludwig Museum).
- Peter Ludwig, born in Koblenz in 1925, worked in the family chocolate company and began collecting in 1957; he died on 22 July 1996 (Ludwig Museum).
- The collection has a Central-Eastern European character, with Hungarian art of the 1960s to 1980s shown through selected works; the museum organises an average of 8 to 10 temporary exhibitions a year (Ludwig Museum).
- The museum has run the Hungarian Pavilion at the Venice Biennale since 2015 (Ludwig Museum).
- The director is Júlia Fabényi (Ludwig Museum website; Emerging Europe, 2019).

FUNDING
Ludwig Museum Budapest: public: a central government-funded body, with the Ludwig Foundation financing acquisitions
- Under a 2005 deed of foundation the museum is a central government-funded body under the Treasury, with financial independence; its supervisory body is the Ministry of the National Cultural Heritage (Ludwig Museum history page).
- The museum states that its international acquisitions and professional programmes are still regularly financed by the Ludwig Foundation based in Aachen (Ludwig Museum).

Red flags: protest over the 2013 choice of director
- On 9 May 2013 more than 30 artists occupied the museum. The group United for Contemporary Art demanded a new, transparent tender for the director's post; it said the Ludwig Foundation had endorsed Barnabás Bencsik, who was competing with Júlia Fabényi, whom it described as "Fidesz-backed" (ArtLeaks, 12 May 2013, press release of the artists' group).
- The occupiers left the museum on 22 May and continued the protest in front of the Ministry of Human Resources (ArtLeaks).

Red flags: the museum excluded a portrait of the prime minister
- In May 2019 director Júlia Fabényi decided to exclude János Brückner's work "Here and Now", a mural of Viktor Orbán that visitors built piece by piece for the museum's 30th anniversary exhibition. Emerging Europe reports that she expected a negative public reaction and calls the decision self-censorship (Claudia Patricolo, 18 May 2019).
- Emerging Europe cites no instruction from a ministry or government body; the museum itself acted.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The two pieces concern the 2013 director appointment and the 2019 exclusion of one work.
1. ArtLeaks: United for Contemporary Art, "Enough is enough! (Ludwig Museum, Budapest, Hungary)" (12 May 2013)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2013/05/12/enough-is-enough-ludwig-museum-budapest-hungary/
- Demands a new, transparent tender for the museum's director, and transparency in cultural governance, institutional and professional autonomy, and an end to withheld funding.
- An artists' protest statement, partisan in its stance; the page names no individual author.
2. Emerging Europe: Claudia Patricolo, "Why did a Hungarian museum self-censor a portrait of Viktor Orbán?" (18 May 2019)
URL: https://emerging-europe.com/?p=39912
- Reports that the director excluded Brückner's work from the anniversary exhibition and that the artist and Péter Bencze, founder of Everybody Needs Art, were called in to discuss it.
- Quotes Brückner saying he refuses to "play the self-censorship game".
- Not an art-press outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
The Ludwig Museum is a central government-funded body under the Treasury, with the Ludwig Foundation based in Aachen financing acquisitions and professional programmes. The documented issues are the 2013 artists' occupation over the director's appointment and the director's 2019 decision to exclude a portrait of Orbán.
