TAI KWUN: Hong Kong, China
ID: tai-kwun-hong-kong
Website: https://www.taikwun.hk (checked 2026-10-24; the pages other than the Jockey Club funding page could not be read, so opening hours and admission are not stated here)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for heritage and arts in the former Central Police Station compound in Central, Hong Kong: 16 historic buildings dating from 1841 to 1925, including three declared monuments, the former Central Police Station, the Former Central Magistracy and Victoria Prison (Wikipedia).
- Opened to the public in part on 29 May 2018 (Wikipedia). "Tai Kwun" is the name of the revitalised compound.
- Revitalised in a partnership between the Hong Kong government and the Hong Kong Jockey Club; operated by The Jockey Club CPS Limited, a not-for-profit set up by the Club's Charities Trust (stated by the space).
- Herzog & de Meuron did the masterplan, Rocco Design Architects the executive design, and Purcell the heritage conservation; two new buildings were added (Wikipedia).
- New buildings include JC Contemporary, whose stairs are a popular visitor spot (Wikipedia).
- Construction began in 2011; a wall and roof collapse in 2016 halted work (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Tai Kwun: privately funded by a gambling operator's charity: the Hong Kong Jockey Club Charities Trust
- The Hong Kong Jockey Club paid for the revitalisation and its Charities Trust set up the operator; the government is the other named partner (stated by the space).
- Wikipedia gives different construction costs: HK$1.8 billion in one place, HK$3.7 and HK$3.8 billion in others. The space publishes no figure.
- The Jockey Club holds a government-granted monopoly on horse racing betting, the Mark Six lottery and fixed-odds overseas football betting. About 90 percent of its annual surplus after tax goes to its Charities Trust, which gave HK$7.3 billion to approved charities in 2022/2023 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of any other funder of Tai Kwun's operations; no annual report or accounts were found.

Red flags: Gambling
- The funder is the Hong Kong Jockey Club, whose betting monopoly pays the Charities Trust that funds Tai Kwun (Wikipedia, Tai Kwun).

Red flags: Protest over the Ma Jian cancellation
- November 2018: Tai Kwun cancelled two literary festival events with the exiled writer Ma Jian. Director Timothy Calnin said, "We do not want Tai Kwun to become a platform to promote the political interests of any individual" (Hong Kong Free Press, 8 November 2018).
- PEN Hong Kong's president called it "at the very least an act of self-censorship" (Hong Kong Free Press).
- The decision was reversed two days later amid public outcry. On 18 November 2018 twelve artists marched silently with blank placards inside the compound and handed in a letter with five demands: a public explanation, a definition of "political interests", the decision criteria, the names of the decision-makers, and a complaints system (Hong Kong Free Press, 20 November 2018).
- No reporting found of a response to the five demands.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Tai Kwun is the Jockey Club's betting profit, channelled through its Charities Trust, so the funder is a gambling operator by nature. The documented weak point is the 2018 Ma Jian cancellation, reversed after protest. Worth watching: the missing accounts and the lack of any answer to the artists' questions on decision-making.
