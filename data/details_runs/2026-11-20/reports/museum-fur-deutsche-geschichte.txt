MUSEUM FÜR DEUTSCHE GESCHICHTE: Berlin, Germany
ID: museum-fur-deutsche-geschichte
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- The central history museum of the German Democratic Republic, seated in the Zeughaus on Unter den Linden, East Berlin, set up to convey the Marxist-Leninist view of history (Deutsches Historisches Museum).
- The SED Politburo decided in February 1950 to found it (Karen Pfundt, Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte, 1994); the DHM dates the SED Central Committee's decision to August 1950. It was officially founded on 18 January 1952 (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).
- Its first permanent exhibition opened in mid-1952 at a provisional site, Clara-Zetkin-Straße 26, and it moved into the rebuilt Zeughaus in the early 1960s (DHM). The Zeughaus, the Prussian army's arsenal, was built from 1695 to 1730 (DHM).
- It was organised in eight departments, from prehistory to the present and a "Lenin-Stalin" department. The historian Alfred Meusel was its director and also president of its 32-member academic council (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).
- Its exhibitions included "20 Jahre SED" (1967), "Karl Marx - Leben und Werk" (1968) and "Der Leninismus und die deutsche Arbeiterbewegung" (1970) (DHM).
- At its founding its only collection was the surviving military holdings of the Prussian armoury; it grew through donations, purchases and part of the Soviet war booty returned by the USSR in 1958 (DHM).
- After the Wall fell, its management publicly appealed in late 1989 to "[get] the GDR into the museum" (DHM).
- The Council of Ministers of the last GDR government decided on 29 August 1990 to dissolve it with effect from 15 September 1990; its property and holdings, and the Zeughaus, passed to the Deutsches Historisches Museum (DHM).

FUNDING
Museum für Deutsche Geschichte: state-owned: the GDR state under SED party control (closed 1990)
- The museum was a GDR state institution founded by decision of the ruling SED; on the state side its coordination fell to the history officer of the State Secretariat for Higher Education, Herwig Förder (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).
- On the party side it answered to Kurt Hager, head of the SED Central Committee's propaganda department, and to the secretariat member Fred Oelßner (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).

Red flags: 1952 party control and censorship of the founding exhibition
- The SED leadership steered and checked the exhibition preparations in detail; most non-Marxist historians soon withdrew, and the Marxist historians often submitted unwillingly to the party leadership's censorship (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).
- On 13 June 1952 the Politburo, led by Walter Ulbricht and Otto Grotewohl, inspected the preparations and sharply criticised the departments from 1848 onwards (Aus Politik und Zeitgeschichte).

Red flags: GDR-era land-reform expropriations in the collection
- Part of the collection came from expropriations of former private property under the East German land reform (DHM).

BOTTOM LINE
A party-state propaganda museum, wholly funded and controlled by the GDR and closed in 1990. The open question today is the provenance of the expropriated objects now held by its successor.
