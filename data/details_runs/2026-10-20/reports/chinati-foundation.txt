CHINATI FOUNDATION: Marfa, USA
ID: chinati-foundation
Website: https://chinati.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in Marfa, Texas, based on the ideas of the artist Donald Judd, preserving and presenting permanent large-scale installations by a limited number of artists (Wikipedia).
- Judd first visited Marfa in 1971 and moved there full time in 1977; construction began in 1979 with initial assistance from the Dia Art Foundation in New York, and the museum opened to the public in 1986 (Wikipedia).
- The foundation occupies 340 acres on the former site of Fort D. A. Russell, plus buildings in downtown Marfa, at 1 Cavalry Row (Wikipedia).
- It was conceived to show work by Judd, John Chamberlain and Dan Flavin; the collection later grew to include Carl Andre, Roni Horn, Ilya Kabakov, Richard Long, Claes Oldenburg and Coosje van Bruggen, and Robert Irwin, whose "untitled (dawn to dusk)" opened in July 2016 in the former fort hospital (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Marianne Stockebrand (1994 to 2010), Thomas Kellein (2011 to 2012), Jenny Moore (2013 to 2022), Caitlin Murray (2023 onward) (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include an artist residency started by Judd in 1989, an internship programme and summer art classes for local students (Wikipedia). In 2022 the foundation and the Central Marfa Historic District were added to the National Register of Historic Places (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:00; guided tours are the only way to see most of the collection, and Judd's 15 untitled works in concrete are free to view during museum hours (Chinati website).
- Accessibility can be limited in some areas because of the buildings' "landmark status" and the uneven grounds; wheelchairs are available at the front desk, and visitors can email the visitor services team before arriving (Chinati website).

FUNDING
Chinati Foundation: private philanthropy: foundations and individual donors, with Dia's early support
- Dia Art Foundation gave initial assistance when construction began in 1979 (Wikipedia).
- Revenue was $6,370,230 in 2024, of which contributions were $5,037,551; expenses were $3,707,604 and total assets $25,489,139 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Art as Intended capital campaign, $16 million goal: gifts listed as of 23 October 2026 include $2,500,000 from the Sarofim Foundation; $1,000,000 each from The Brown Foundation, Inc. of Houston, The Chadwick-Loher Foundation and two anonymous donors; $500,000 tier from Shari and John Behnke, Lee and Mike Cohn, Miguel and Rodrigo Fernandez, Franck Giraud and Gonzague Luze with The Ruth Stanton Foundation, and the Lenore S. & Bernard A. Greenberg Fund (Chinati campaign page).
- Gifts of $250,000 to $499,999 come from Mack and Cece Fowler, George and Linda Kelly, Anthony and Celeste Meier, The Still Water Foundation, The Susan Vaughan Foundation, and Annabel Thompson and Jed Walentas; the Texas Historical Commission and the National Trust for Historic Preservation are listed in the $25,000 to $99,999 tier (Chinati campaign page).
- $1.25 million Start Small grant, reported on 12 May 2021, from the Covid-19 relief fund of Jack Dorsey, for repairing the John Chamberlain Building courtyard wall and training young women from West Texas in adobe restoration and native grassland restoration work (Jacoba Urist, The Art Newspaper).
- Mellon Foundation: $525,000 awarded on 6 September 2018 for the Chinati Foundation Archives (Mellon Foundation).
- Lannan Foundation: a challenge grant in December 1996 to establish a bridge fund toward an operating endowment, with further grants to the bridge fund in October 1997, October 1998 and December 1999 (Lannan Foundation).
- Wikipedia's account of the 1986 opening calls Chinati an "independent, non-profit, publicly funded institution".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Chinati is private philanthropy: Dia's early support, then foundation and individual gifts led by the Sarofim Foundation, the Brown Foundation of Houston and the Chadwick-Loher Foundation, plus grants from Mellon, Lannan and the Dorsey relief fund. Its 2024 revenue was $6.4 million and its audited statements and Form 990s are posted for 2016 to 2024.
