MITSUBISHI ICHIGOKAN MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: mitsubishi-ichigokan-museum
Website: https://mimt.jp/ (checked 2026-11-20; the older address mitsubishi-museum.jp did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Corporate art museum at 2-6-2 Marunouchi, Chiyoda-ku, about five minutes on foot from JR Tokyo Station (Tokyo Art Beat; museum website).
- Opened on 6 April 2010 with the exhibition "Manet and Modern Paris", held in cooperation with the Musée d'Orsay (Wikipedia).
- The building is a reconstruction of Mitsubishi Ichigokan, Mitsubishi's first Western-style office building, designed by the British architect Josiah Conder and completed in 1894. It was demolished in 1968 and rebuilt on the same site from the Meiji-era drawings and surveys; the new building was completed on 30 April 2009 (museum website; Wikipedia).
- Red brick, three storeys above ground and two below, with about 800 square metres of exhibition space in 20 rooms (Wikipedia).
- The collection centres on late 19th-century Western art, with Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, Odilon Redon and Félix Vallotton; it holds over 200 Lautrec lithographs and posters from the Maurice Joyant collection (museum website; Japanese Wikipedia). The Musée Toulouse-Lautrec in Albi has been its sister museum since April 2009 (museum website).
- The director is Yuko Ikeda, in post since 1 April 2024, after Keiji Kimura (2020 to 2024) and Akiya Takahashi (museum website).
- The museum closed for repair work on 10 April 2023 and reopened on 23 November 2024 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Current season: "Fontanesi: Light of Italy, Landscapes of the Heart", 17 October 2026 to 24 January 2027, organised with the Turin Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art (GAM) and the Fondazione Torino Musei; an exhibition on Montmartre follows from 20 February to 23 May 2027 (museum website).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 18:00, to 20:00 on Fridays, closed on Mondays. The current exhibition costs 2,300 yen, 1,300 yen for university students and 1,000 yen for high school students; holders of disability certificates pay half and one companion enters free (museum website).

FUNDING
Mitsubishi Ichigokan Museum: corporate: run by Mitsubishi Estate, with corporate members led by Mitsubishi group companies
- Operator: Mitsubishi Estate Co., Ltd., a real estate company, runs the museum as a corporate museum (museum website; Japanese Wikipedia). The same company demolished the original building in March 1968 (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Corporate members: the museum says its activities are supported by the corporate members it lists. The four Premium Support Members are MUFG Bank, Mitsubishi Corporation, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries and Mitsubishi UFJ NICOS (museum website, corporate members page).
- Support Members named on the same page include Kirin Holdings, Mitsubishi Electric, Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking, Meiji Yasuda Life Insurance, AGC, ENEOS Holdings, Tokio Marine & Nichido Fire Insurance and Mitsubishi Chemical; the full list runs to well over 100 companies.
- Exhibition sponsors: the Fontanesi exhibition is sponsored by DNP Dai Nippon Printing and CRIF Japan, with the support of the Embassy of Italy in Japan (museum website).

Red flags: Arms and defence: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries among the top-tier corporate members
- Mitsubishi Heavy Industries is one of the museum's four Premium Support Members (museum website, checked 20 November 2026).
- Wikipedia describes the company as "the world's 32nd-largest defense contractor measured by 2024 arms revenues and the largest based in Japan"; it makes fighter jets, missiles, torpedoes and the Type 10 main battle tank.

Red flags: Fossil fuels: Mitsubishi Corporation and ENEOS Holdings among the corporate members
- Mitsubishi Corporation, a Premium Support Member, trades and invests in crude oil and liquefied natural gas, holds 50 percent of the BHP Mitsubishi Alliance, which mines coking coal in Queensland, and in July 2026 completed the purchase of shale-gas assets from Aethon Energy Management, a deal valued at 7.53 billion US dollars including debt (Wikipedia).
- ENEOS Holdings, a Support Member, is "a Japanese global petroleum and metals conglomerate"; its company ENEOS Corporation does petroleum refining and marketing (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum belongs to one company, Mitsubishi Estate, and is backed by a corporate membership that the Mitsubishi group leads. Among the current members are Japan's largest defence contractor and two companies in oil, gas and coal. Watch the corporate members page and the sponsors named on each exhibition.
