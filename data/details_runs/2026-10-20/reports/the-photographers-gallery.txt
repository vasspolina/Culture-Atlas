THE PHOTOGRAPHERS' GALLERY: London, UK
ID: the-photographers-gallery
Website: https://thephotographersgallery.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public gallery for photography at 16-18 Ramillies Street, W1, near Oxford Circus on the border of Soho and Oxford Street (the gallery).
- Founded by Sue Davies, who opened it on 14 January 1971 in a converted Lyons Tea Bar at 8 Great Newport Street, Covent Garden, as the first public gallery in the UK devoted solely to photography (Wikipedia).
- Directors since: Sue Grayson Ford, Paul Wombell (from 1994), Brett Rogers (from 2005) and Shoair Mavlian (from January 2023) (Wikipedia).
- It reopened in May 2012 in a former textiles warehouse in the West End, redesigned by Irish architects O'Donnell & Tuomey; a £3.5M Arts Council grant helped buy the venue (Wikipedia).
- Three exhibition spaces, a print sales gallery, an education studio, a digital media screen, a bookshop and a cafe (Wikipedia); the Soho Photography Quarter in Ramillies Place has free open-air exhibitions (the gallery).
- Open Monday to Wednesday 10:30 to 18:00, Thursday and Friday 10:30 to 20:00, Saturday 10:30 to 18:00, Sunday and bank holidays 11:00 to 18:00. Admission prices and physical access details were not on the pages opened.

FUNDING
The Photographers' Gallery: mixed: public grants, trusts, a prize sponsor and corporate partners
- Charity Commission register, year to 31 March 2025: income £4,262,114, expenditure £4,565,686, with £1,040,879 from three government grants. The year to March 2024 showed income of £4,329,871 and £955,535 from five government grants.
- The Art Newspaper (2022) says the gallery is a registered charity supported by Arts Council England and also backed by the Deutsche Börse Foundation and the Wolfson Foundation. Deutsche Börse has sponsored the photography prize since 2005, with a £30,000 award (Wikipedia).
- Corporate partners: Leica Camera and Polaroid. In-kind supporters: Pol Roger, Voyager Estate. Thanked: Lazari Investments Limited, New West End Company, Omni, Pontegadea. No amounts or years are given.
- Kusuma Trust gave £31,648.50 for the Develop programme for young people, with commissions through 2025/26, and £9,900 for the Teen Tours programme in 2022 (Kusuma Trust).
- No reporting found, on pages I could open, of amounts from Westminster City Council, MPB, the Speroni Foundation or John Lyon's Charity.

Red flags: Kusuma Trust, funded by online gambling proceeds
- Anurag Dikshit co-founded PartyPoker; in December 2008 he pleaded guilty in New York to one count of online gambling in violation of the Federal Wire Act and agreed to forfeit $300 million (Wikipedia).
- Forbes reported he gave the proceeds of selling his PartyGaming stake to the Kusuma Trust (Wikipedia). He remained a trustee in 2023.
- The Kusuma Trust's gifts to the gallery are for youth education programmes and are current (2022 to 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the gallery's funding. The one dispute in the art press was political, about a 2019 project.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anny Shaw, "Leave campaigners call for Photographers' Gallery to be stripped of charitable status over Jonas Lund's project to 'reverse Brexit'" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/01/15/leave-campaigners-call-for-photographers-gallery-to-be-stripped-of-charitable-status-over-jonas-lunds-project-to-reverse-brexit
- Leave Means Leave, a pro-Brexit campaign group, called on 9 January for the gallery to lose charitable status and for Arts Council England funding to be withheld, calling Lund's "Operation Earnest Voice" "blatant political campaigning".
- The gallery said it had followed Charity Commission guidelines, consulted Arts Council England and taken legal advice, and did not change the project.
- Criticises the project, not the funders; Leave Means Leave is a campaign group.
2. The Art Newspaper: Diane Smyth, "The Photographers' Gallery in London appoints Shoair Mavlian as new director" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/10/10/the-photographers-gallery-in-london-appoints-a-new-director
- Not criticism: reports a loss of just over £200,000 in 2019 to 2020 and a return to surplus by March 2021, and says the new director faces pressure on income.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery runs on Arts Council England and other public grants, trusts and partners. The weak point is the Kusuma Trust, whose gifts to its youth programmes come from the online poker fortune of Anurag Dikshit, who pleaded guilty in 2008. Watch the Kusuma and Deutsche Börse commitments and the gallery's next accounts.
