PERSPEKTIVET MUSEUM: Tromsø, Norway
ID: perspektivet-museum-tromso
Website: https://perspektivet.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- The museum for the Tromsø area, with its exhibitions and offices at Storgata 95 in central Tromsø, a protected wooden merchant's house built in 1838 for the Mack trading family and used as Folkets Hus from 1911 to 2001 (Perspektivet, strategy 2023 to 2027).
- The writer and painter Sara Fabricius, known as Cora Sandel, lived in a flat in the house as a young woman; the current exhibitions on the second and third floors start from her life and the house's history, with photography shown on the ground floor (Perspektivet).
- Established in 1996, it took over the collections of Tromsø bymuseum and Troms folkemuseum (Lokalhistoriewiki); it is part of the national museum network, and documentary photography is one of its main fields (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- It also runs the open-air museum Folkeparken on Tromsøya, the former Troms Folkemuseum, and the coastal farm Straumen gård on Kvaløya; it manages 25 historic buildings, about 500,000 photographs and about 16,000 objects (Lokalhistoriewiki).
- Its strategy for 2023 to 2027 puts an extension on the neighbouring plot, Havnegata 10, ahead of every other goal, to be pursued with Tromsø Municipality, because developing the 1838 house needs universal design, purpose-built galleries and workshop space (strategy 2023 to 2027).
- Director Marianne A. Olsen (Perspektivet).
- Visiting: Storgata 95 opens Tuesday to Friday 10 to 16 and weekends 11 to 17, closed Mondays; adults NOK 95, concessions NOK 65, students NOK 50, under 18 free, annual card NOK 300 (Perspektivet).

FUNDING
Perspektivet Museum: mainly public: Tromsø Municipality, Troms County and the Ministry of Culture
- A foundation supported by Tromsø Municipality, Troms County and the Ministry of Culture (Lokalhistoriewiki).
- Tromsø Municipality appoints ten of its twelve board members, including the chair, Rebekka Brox Liabø; staff elect the other two (Perspektivet).
- When Storgata 95 was put up for sale in 2001, Tromsø Municipality stepped in as tenant; the foundation bought the property in 2005 (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- Accounts for 2025: operating income NOK 11.86M, of which NOK 464,097 sales, and a surplus of NOK 141,277; 2023 closed with a loss of NOK 781,000 (Regnskapsregisteret).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: local and state public money under municipal control, with almost no earned income. Worth watching: how the planned extension will be paid for.
