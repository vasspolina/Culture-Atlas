SIIDA SÁMI MUSEUM (SAAMELAISMUSEO SIIDA): Inari, Finland
ID: siida-s-mi-museum-saamelaismuseo-siida-inari
Website: https://siida.fi (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Sámi culture and regional museum for cultural environment tasks in the Sámi area, at Inarintie 46, Inari, Finnish Lapland, with an open-air museum of about 50 historic buildings (siida.fi, museot.fi).
- Founded in 1959 by the Sámi association Sámi Litto as the Sámi Museum, with the first open-air museum buildings moved to the site in 1960; it opened to the public in summer 1963 as the first independent Sámi museum in the Nordic countries (siida.fi).
- The Sámi Museum Foundation, established in 1986, owns the collections and the open-air museums and runs the museum. Its board has eight members elected for four-year terms, and at least four must be Sámi-born citizens of Finland (siida.fi).
- Building: the Siida building opened in 1998 on the original open-air museum site and is shared with the Northern Lapland Nature Centre. It was extended and renovated for the new main exhibition, with new parts for the Sámi Museum collections unit and a restaurant (siida.fi, Metsähallitus release). No reporting found of the architect.
- What is there: the main exhibition "Enâmeh láá mii párnááh, These lands are our children", planned to open on 1 June 2022 (siida.fi), with sections on culture and on northern nature; a shop, a restaurant and the Skolt Sámi Heritage House in Sevettijärvi (siida.fi, museot.fi). The Finnish National Museum agreed to return Sámi objects to Siida; Yle reported about 2,600 items in April 2017, and Scan Magazine (2022) said it "has returned 2,200 objects".
- Visiting: Monday to Saturday, 10:00 to 17:00 from 5 October 2026 to 31 May 2027, closed Sundays. Adults EUR 18, discount EUR 15, children 7 to 17 EUR 7, children 0 to 6 free (siida.fi). museot.fi lists a wheelchair for loan, an induction loop and disabled parking, and says the open-air museum is not fully accessible.

FUNDING
Siida Sámi Museum: mainly public: Finnish ministry money, with Finnish foundations for the exhibition renewal
- The Ministry of Education and Culture provides most of the funding, and Inari Municipality gives an annual grant (siida.fi). No reporting found of the amounts.
- Main exhibition, cultural section: the Kone Foundation gave EUR 300,000 and the Finnish Cultural Foundation (Museum Vision) EUR 400,000 (per Siida's releases). The Ministry of Education and Culture and the Jenny and Antti Wihuri Foundation also paid, amounts not stated (siida.fi).
- Main exhibition, nature section: Metsähallitus, the state forestry body, received funding from the government's amending budget (siida.fi).
- Siida applied for EUR 17.5 million in state funds for the expansion (Yle, 2017). No reporting found of the amount granted.
- No reporting found of accounts, annual reports or named board members.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is descriptive: a trade magazine feature and the museum's own releases.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Siida is clean on what is published: Finnish state money, a municipal grant and named Finnish foundations, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no accounts, amounts or board names are published, and that the museum states its own funding. Worth watching: how the returned National Museum objects are handled and shown.
