WIDER CONTEXT
- The city of Icheon in South Korea set up a committee in 2008 to seek the return of the Icheon five-story stone pagoda, an early Goryeo pagoda that Japan moved to Gyeongbokgung Palace in 1915 and that Okura Kihachiro took to Japan in 1918; it stands in the garden behind the Okura Hotel in Tokyo (Kyeonggi Ilbo, Lee Yeon-seop, 15 February 2021). A Yonhap photo caption places it in the rear garden of the Okura Shukokan (Seoul Shinmun, 29 October 2010).
- On 29 October 2010 Icheon's return committee said that after talks in Tokyo the Okura Cultural Foundation had said it could return the pagoda if the two governments consulted and the Japanese government allowed it; the foundation said the pagoda is foundation property affected by national cultural property policy (Seoul Shinmun, citing Yonhap, 29 October 2010).
- Icheon citizens raised 151 million won and unveiled a replica of the pagoda on 16 October 2020; the foundation's stated position remained that it could return the pagoda only if the Japanese government allowed it (Kyeonggi Ilbo, 15 February 2021).
