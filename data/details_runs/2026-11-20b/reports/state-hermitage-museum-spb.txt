THE STATE HERMITAGE MUSEUM: Saint Petersburg, Russia
ID: state-hermitage-museum-spb
Website: https://hermitagemuseum.org (checked 2026-11-20; its security certificate failed standard verification)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of art and culture in six historic buildings on Palace Embankment, including the Winter Palace, with modern art in the General Staff Building (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1764, when Catherine the Great bought paintings from the Berlin merchant Johann Ernst Gotzkowsky (Wikipedia).
- A 1996 decree placed it under the patronage of the President of the Russian Federation (Decree No. 862 of 12 June 1996).
- Mikhail Piotrovsky has directed it since 1992; Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin reappointed him for five years (Expert).
- A government-appointed museum president is to chair the board of trustees, the fundraising council and the international advisory board (Sobaka.ru); in 2003 the trustees elected Vladimir Potanin of Interros as their first chairman (Delovoy Peterburg, 25 April 2003).
- Visiting: foreigners pay more than Russian and Belarusian citizens; entry is free on the third Thursday of each month and daily for students; 2,812,913 visitors in 2022 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
The State Hermitage Museum: mainly public: the Russian federal budget, with Gazprom, Magnit and Sber sponsoring galleries and shows
- The 1996 decree ordered the government to fund its upkeep from a separate federal budget line from 1997 (Decree No. 862).
- In 2017 it received the largest state subsidy of ten leading museums, more than 5 billion rubles, and spent over 2 billion rubles on salaries (News.ru via Rambler, 2018).
- For 2026 the federal budget gives it a capital investment subsidy of 929 million rubles, 6.2 times the previous year's (Delovoy Peterburg, 27 May 2026).
- The retailer Magnit has been a strategic partner since late 2023, paying for a restoration in 2023, backing a Snyders show in 2024 and an art deco show in 2025 (Magnit release on Retail.ru).
- Sber was general partner of a show of contemporary Indian art opened on 4 June 2026 (Lenta.ru).

Red flags: Gazprom sponsorship, 2022
- Gazprom was general sponsor of the "Peter the Great Gallery", opened on 14 January 2022 by Gazprom's management board chairman Alexey Miller, and helped restore Peter's ivory chandelier (Komsomolskaya Pravda); two more halls opened with its support on 21 June 2022 (AK&M, Gazprom release).

Red flags: EU sanctions on the director, 2026
- The EU listed Piotrovsky on 23 April 2026 as a "close associate" of Putin who "actively supported and justified" the war, citing his backing of the absorption of Ukrainian museum items and of digs in occupied Crimea (The Art Newspaper, 29 April 2026).
- Canada sanctioned him in August 2023 (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: 2022 suspension of ties by foreign partners
- Hermitage Amsterdam returned a loan show and cut ties, the UK and Italian Hermitage foundations suspended support, the International Advisory Board suspended its work on 15 March 2022 and the Bizot group suspended Piotrovsky (The Art Newspaper, 4 April 2022).

Red flags: 2026 Accounts Chamber finding on the storage complex
- Reviewing 2025 federal culture spending, the Accounts Chamber found shortcomings in building the Hermitage's production base and storage complex, a risk of ineffective use of 25.5 million rubles, and 358.1 million rubles unspent on 1 January 2026 (Delovoy Peterburg).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism since 2022 concerns the director's support for the war and the museum's closeness to the state.
1. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Isolated by Russia boycotts, Hermitage director calls for 'cultural bridges between the nations'" (2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/04/04/russia-boycott-hermitage-museum
- Piotrovsky had not condemned the invasion; in 2021 he told the paper "Putin's been my person since the 1990s".
- Ukrainian cultural figures condemned Russian museum directors' silence.
2. USA Art News: "State Hermitage Museum curator quits over Russia's war in Ukraine" (2022)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/state-hermitage-museum-curator-quits-over-russias-war-in-ukraine
- Head of contemporary art Dmitry Ozerkov resigned on 2 October 2022: "I don't intend to have anything in common with today's Russia".
- Quotes Piotrovsky in Rossiiskaya Gazeta: "We are all militarists and imperials."
3. Novaya Gazeta Europe: Anna Matveeva, "The emperor" (2025)
URL: https://novayagazeta.eu/articles/2025/10/20/the-emperor-en
- Traces Piotrovsky from reformer to war supporter who backed Putin in the 2012, 2018 and 2024 elections.
- Cites a 2021 Proekt report that businesses tied to the Hermitage belonged to friends of the Piotrovsky family.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013 the Accounts Chamber found inflated Hermitage restoration contracts with damage above six billion rubles; ministry official Boris Mazo was later jailed for eight years (Novaya Gazeta Europe).
- Hermitage archaeologist Alexander Butyagin, arrested in Poland in December 2025 at Ukraine's request over digs in occupied Crimea, was freed in a prisoner exchange on 28 April 2026 (The Art Newspaper); Ukraine's charge is violations of the laws and customs of war (United24 Media).
- Unverified: Ukraine's Defence Intelligence says his Myrmekion expedition removed 14 artefacts (United24 Media, Ukrainian state outlet).

BOTTOM LINE
A state museum paid for mainly from the federal budget, with the state gas company among its sponsors. Its weak points are a director under EU sanctions for backing the war, a fresh audit finding on its building programme and its isolation from former partners abroad.
