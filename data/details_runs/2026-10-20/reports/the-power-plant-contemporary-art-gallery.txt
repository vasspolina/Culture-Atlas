THE POWER PLANT CONTEMPORARY ART GALLERY: Toronto, Canada
ID: the-power-plant-contemporary-art-gallery
Website: https://www.thepowerplant.org

OVERVIEW
- Public gallery of contemporary visual art in a red brick building with a smokestack at 231 Queens Quay West, on Toronto's waterfront within Harbourfront Centre (the gallery's about page).
- Opened in 1976 and at its current location since 1987 (The Art Newspaper, 23 September 2022).
- It is a registered charity under the legal name The Art Gallery at Harbourfront, operating as The Power Plant, run by a board of directors (annual report).
- Harbourfront Centre is its landlord and, in the words of The Art Newspaper, its primary funder; Harbourfront employs the gallery's staff and appoints 13 of the board's 27 seats.
- Leadership: CEO Carolyn Vesely and artistic director Adelina Vlas as of 31 March 2026 (annual report 2025-26).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm. Admission is free. Wheelchair accessible.

FUNDING
The Power Plant Contemporary Art Gallery: mainly public through Harbourfront Centre and arts councils, with bank sponsors and donors
- Harbourfront Centre makes three contributions (occupancy, facilities rental and operating) that the gallery's accounts call "a material source of operating funds." The 2025-26 accounts say the gallery's ability to continue operations depends on Harbourfront, which depends on the Government of Canada, the City of Toronto and its own sponsorship, parking, concession and ticket revenue.
- Federal, provincial and municipal grants in the year to 31 March 2024 totalled 1,004,712 dollars: Canada Council for the Arts 518,800, Ontario Arts Council 226,612, Toronto Arts Council 206,000 and Ontario Trillium Foundation 53,300 (annual report 2023-24, note 13).
- BMO Financial Group sponsors free admission all year (the gallery's visit and join and support pages).
- TD Bank Group and its TD Ready Commitment sponsored touring exhibitions by Manuel Mathieu, Sasha Huber, Shona Illingworth and Nathan Eugene Carson (annual report 2023-24); the 2025-26 report lists a TD Curatorial Fellow for 2025-27.
- RBC supports an artist programme for members: the RBC Emerging Artist Membership launched in 2023-24 and the 2025-26 RBC Artist Program, which delivered seven programmes in 2025-26.
- Foundations and donors named in the annual reports: the Vega Foundation (partner on the Sharon Lockhart exhibition), the Hal Jackman Foundation, the Goring Family Foundation, the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts and the Jack Weinbaum Family Foundation (2023-24); Lead Donors Paul M. Butler and Chris Black, James D. Fleck, and Bill Morneau and Nancy McCain (2025-26).
- Board: Katrina Rempel of BMO Capital Markets and Julia Park of CIBC (treasurer) sit on the board of 14 directors (the gallery's leadership page).

Red flags: TD Bank Group, 2024 money-laundering guilty plea
- TD Bank N.A. pleaded guilty on 10 October 2024 to conspiring to operate without a Bank Secrecy Act compliant anti-money laundering program, to file inaccurate Currency Transaction Reports and to launder monetary instruments, and agreed to pay $1.8 billion in penalties (US Department of Justice, Criminal Division).
- The Power Plant's annual reports name TD Bank Group as sponsor of the Nathan Eugene Carson touring exhibition (2023-24) and list a TD Curatorial Fellow for 2025-27 (2025-26).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One governance dispute in 2022, between the gallery's board and Harbourfront Centre, reported by two art outlets.
1. The Art Newspaper: Hadani Ditmars, "Toronto's revered Power Plant art centre in peril after board members' mass resignation" (23 September 2022)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2022/09/23/power-plant-toronto-board-resignations-harbourfront
- Twenty-four board members resigned. Harbourfront tried on 2 June to remove 12 independent directors and replace them with its own appointees; former members say no compelling rationale was given.
- Harbourfront CEO Marah Braye called the allegations of inappropriate interference "incorrect." Harbourfront sued the gallery on 21 June; a ruling on the resolution's validity was pending when the article was published.
2. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Liu, "Collective Resignation of Board Members Shakes Toronto's Power Plant Gallery" (23 September 2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/collective-resignation-of-board-members-shakes-torontos-power-plant-gallery/
- Twenty-four members resigned on 21 September; the 15 former members who signed the letter say the institution's existence is "jeopardized by the actions of Harbourfront Centre."
- Former member Richard Lee said there was "no democratic process" and voiced concern that the proposed replacements were affiliated with Harbourfront. Braye cited "governance and operational concerns" and said Harbourfront directors were appointed to the board "on an interim basis."

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind The Power Plant is public funding routed mainly through Harbourfront Centre, plus Canada Council, Ontario Arts Council and Toronto Arts Council grants, with bank sponsorship from BMO, TD and RBC and donations from foundations and individuals. The documented weak points are the 2022 dispute over Harbourfront's control of the gallery's board and TD Bank Group's 2024 guilty plea, with TD named as a sponsor in the annual reports.
