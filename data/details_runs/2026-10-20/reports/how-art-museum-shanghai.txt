HOW ART MUSEUM: Shanghai, China
ID: how-art-museum-shanghai
Website: http://www.howartmuseum.org.cn/ (checked 2026-10-24; redirects to /portal/, which returned a page with almost no text and no visitor information)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum in the Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park, Shanghai, described as a free-trade zone dedicated to scientific research and development (The Art Newspaper, 22 September 2017, Lisa Movius).
- Founded by the hotelier-collector Zheng Hao; it opened on 20 September 2017 as a sister venue to his museum in Wenzhou, which opened in 2013. An earlier plan to open in Waigaoqiao was cancelled in 2014 (The Art Newspaper).
- The name is HOW Art Museum; no person or earlier name is given in the sources opened.
- Three-storey building of 7,000 square metres in the same park as Zheng's Onehome Art Hotel, which launched in 2016 and whose 300-plus rooms double as storage for the collection; the architect is not named in the sources opened (The Art Newspaper).
- On site: exhibition galleries, a collection of about 2,000 works by artists including Damien Hirst, Anish Kapoor, Yayoi Kusama, Xu Bing, Lin Tianmiao and Ai Weiwei, and a curatorial residency that houses participants at the hotel next door (The Art Newspaper; e-flux, 16 March 2018).
- Visiting: in 2018 the museum listed Tuesday to Friday 1 pm to 10 pm and Saturday and Sunday 10 am to 10 pm (e-flux); Jing Daily reported in 2019 that it stays open to 10 pm six days a week and drew about 150,000 visitors a year. No reporting found of current admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
HOW Art Museum: self-funded: the private wealth of founder Zheng Hao, described as a hotelier and a real estate figure
- The Art Newspaper (2017) calls Zheng a hotelier-collector; Jing Daily (2019) calls him a "real estate mogul with a passion for art". Neither gives a figure for the cost of the museum or its running costs.
- The museum sits in the same park as the founder's Onehome Art Hotel, and its director Yun Cheagab said of the arrangement: "What the museum shows, the hotel collects" (The Art Newspaper).
- Jing Daily (Richard Whiddington, 10 October 2019) does not discuss funding sources; it says only that Zheng had "the means" to build the museum.
- No reporting found of a corporate, foundation or state funder, or of published accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece of reporting on a dispute between an artist and the museum; no critique of the programme or building. Other coverage is descriptive or admiring.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Pamela Wong, "Artist Demands Compensation from Shanghai Private Museum for Unreturned Artworks" (5 November 2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/artist-demands-compensation-from-shanghai-private-museum-for-unreturned-artworks
- Unverified: artist Gordon Cheung says he consigned 20 works to Zheng's Other Gallery in Shanghai for a solo show, that the gallery closed in 2017, and that the works not sold or returned ended up with HOW Art Museum. He demands their return and compensation, or USD 569,450 if the museum keeps them. This is the artist's allegation, reported by ArtAsiaPacific; no ruling is reported.
- Unverified: Cheung says Zheng offered GBP 65,450 for the remaining 11 paintings in 2012, paid GBP 21,500 in 2013 and then stopped paying, and that copies of his works hang in Zheng's hotels.
- The museum's spokesperson replied that it "was established in 2017" and "has never worked with any artist called Zhang Yibin" (the name given in the article, not Cheung's), called Cheung's Instagram posts "malicious and false accusation against the Museum", and said it had contacted its lawyers.
- No reporting found of a settlement or ruling after November 2020.
2. Jing Daily: Richard Whiddington, "How The New HOW Art Museum Is Beating All Expectations With A New Museum Model" (2019)
URL: https://jingdailyculture.com/?p=94200
- Not criticism: describes the museum as a leading cultural attraction with international contemporary shows.
- Does not discuss funding.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper (Lisa Movius, 27 September 2023) reported that many Chinese private museums depend on parent companies to pay their bills, and named the collapsing property market and tightening censorship among the pressures behind closures and downsizings.
- The same article lists HOW Art Museum among the boom-era private museums "plodding away through these lean years", not as closed or downsized.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a private venture of its founder with no named outside funder, so the money behind it cannot be traced beyond Zheng Hao's hotel and property interests. The weak point is the unresolved dispute with Gordon Cheung, which is an artist's allegation answered by a denial. Worth watching: published accounts, which none of the sources show, and the museum's reliance on one owner's business.
