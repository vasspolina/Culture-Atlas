ID: sydney-symphony-orchestra

Red flags: 2021 dismissal of the chief executive and her claim against the orchestra
- In December 2021 chairman Geoff Wilson announced that chief executive Emma Dunch had left her role two years before her contract was due to end, and gave no reason; Dunch said she was the victim of "a politically architected 'hit job'" (Limelight, 11 December 2021).
- An Australian Financial Review story of 25 November 2021 reported that Create NSW had commissioned two reviews that raised serious concerns about the orchestra's leadership (Limelight, 11 December 2021).
- In April 2022 Dunch took legal action against the orchestra over her dismissal, seeking $74,560 more than the A$471,302 she received on termination and aggravated damages for "anxiety, shock and distress"; the orchestra said it "will vigorously contest her claim" (Slipped Disc, 8 April 2022).
- Unverified: Dunch alleges she was fired after she began investigating claims of sexual assault and harassment against male musicians, in a review led by former Sex Discrimination Commissioner Elizabeth Broderick, from court documents reported by The Australian and cited by Limelight (8 April 2022).
- Dunch discontinued her unfair dismissal case (Lawyerly, 28 March 2023; paywalled, only excerpts were read).
