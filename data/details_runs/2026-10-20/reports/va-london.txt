V&A: London, UK
ID: va-london
Website: https://www.vam.ac.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of art, design and performance. The main site, V&A South Kensington, is on Cromwell Road, London SW7.
- Traces its origins to the Government School of Design of 1832, with the museum listed as established in 1852; earlier names include the South Kensington Museum. The Victoria and Albert Museum name was made public at the 1899 foundation-stone ceremony (Wikipedia).
- Other sites: V&A East Museum and Storehouse, Young V&A, V&A Dundee and the V&A Wedgwood Collection (V&A website).
- Visiting: general admission is free; some exhibitions and events charge. South Kensington is open daily 10:00 to 17:45, Fridays until 22:00 (V&A website).

FUNDING
V&A: mainly public with large private giving: government grant from DCMS, donations, exhibition tickets
- The V&A is a non-departmental public body sponsored by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (Wikipedia).
- Year to March 2023: total income fell from £131.7m to £121.4m. The DCMS grant was £67.5m (down from £78.3m) and donations and legacies £14.8m (up from £14.5m) (Civil Society). The 2024-25 annual report could not be read as text, so no later split is given.
- Blavatnik Family Foundation and Warner Music Group: £10 million in 2023 for the David Bowie Centre at V&A East (Blavatnik Family Foundation).
- Other named donors on completed projects (V&A website, no amounts): Europe 1600-1815 (2015): Heritage Lottery Fund, The Wolfson Foundation, Würth Group. Exhibition Road Quarter (2017): The Monument Trust, The Headley Trust, Garfield Weston Foundation. Cast Courts (2018): Garfield Weston Foundation, American Express.

Red flags: Sackler name and opioid money
- The Dr Mortimer and Theresa Sackler Foundation is listed as a donor to the Exhibition Road Quarter, completed in 2017 (V&A website). The V&A's Exhibition Road courtyard and Centre for Arts Education carried the Sackler name (The Art Newspaper). Members of the Sackler family owned Purdue Pharma, which marketed OxyContin and filed for bankruptcy in 2019 (The Art Newspaper).
- Theresa Sackler was a V&A trustee from 2011 to 2019 (The Art Newspaper).
- July 2019: director Tristram Hunt said the V&A received "very generous support from the Sackler family" and "we remain grateful for that" (The Art Newspaper).
- 16 November 2019: about 30 PAIN activists led by Nan Goldin held a die-in in the courtyard. Hunt then said "We are not going to be taking names down or denying the past" (Hyperallergic, 18 November 2019).
- 3 October 2022: the V&A said it and the family of Mortimer Sackler had "mutually agreed" the courtyard and centre "will no longer carry the Sackler name"; the V&A said the decision was made in May 2022, and the signs have been taken down (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Blavatnik family money
- Wikipedia says Len Blavatnik made his initial fortune in the privatisation of state-owned aluminium and oil assets, that AAR bought 40% of Tyumen Oil (TNK), and that he controls 21% of LyondellBasell. Ukraine's president imposed personal sanctions on him in December 2023; he denies connections to Putin.
- The Blavatnik Family Foundation is listed as a donor to the Exhibition Road Quarter, completed in 2017 (V&A website), and in 2023 gave £10 million with Warner Music Group for the David Bowie Centre at V&A East (Blavatnik Family Foundation).

BOTTOM LINE
The V&A is mainly publicly funded, with private gifts for capital projects. The documented weak points are the Sackler gift, which the museum defended in 2019 and ended in 2022, and Blavatnik family money, including 2023 funding for V&A East. Worth watching: the V&A East donor lists and the 2024-25 accounts, which could not be read.
