LUGAR A DUDAS: Cali, Colombia
ID: lugar-a-dudas-cali
Website: https://www.lugaradudas.org (checked 2026-10-25; live, with a bot-check step)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run, self-managed non-profit art space in the Granada neighbourhood of Cali (Helguera, 2024; MoMA's Post, 2017). Its programme: a library specialised in contemporary art, residencies, a cinema club, exhibitions, talks and the online project Secuela.
- Founded in 2005 by the artist Oscar Muñoz, who still directs it (MoMA's Post, 2017; Pablo Helguera, 2024). A directory lists 2006; the space's own history page says it has worked since 2005. The history page does not name the founders.
- The name is Spanish and means "place for doubts" (my translation).
- Building: Muñoz used a 2003 grant to buy a historic house in the Granada neighbourhood (Helguera). MoMA's Post (2017) describes a street-facing exhibition space and an artist residency programme.
- On site: a Fotocopioteca listed under its publications, an open-shelf library, publications and prints, school group programmes. From 2021 to 2022 only the online project Secuela ran; in-person programming resumed in 2023 (Lugar a Dudas history page).
- Visiting: no opening hours or address were stated on the pages read.

FUNDING
Lugar a Dudas: foundation-funded, now running on savings and its own resources
- Past funders credited by the space: the Prince Claus Fund, Hivos (Netherlands), Doen (Netherlands), Mondrian Fonds (Netherlands), Avina (Switzerland), Daros Latin America, Smart Arts for Development (Switzerland), Resó (Italy), Proartes, the Secretaría de Cultura de Cali and the Ministerio de Cultura de Colombia (stated by the space). It also thanks Universidad del Valle, Universidad Javeriana, Centro Colombo Americano and Museo La Tertulia as partners. Years and amounts are not given.
- Daros supported the launch in 2005 (Helguera).
- In 2023 the space operated with savings from past funding from Arts Collaboratory plus its own resources; the page does not give the full breakdown of current financing.
- Muñoz bought the founding house with a 2003 grant (Helguera). No reporting found of how the house is owned today.
- No reporting found of a board, annual accounts or a current corporate sponsor.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is admiring or descriptive.
1. Beautiful Eccentrics (Substack): Pablo Helguera, "La Gran Vitrina / The Great Vitrine" (2024)
URL: https://pablohelguera.substack.com/p/la-gran-vitrina-the-great-vitrine
- Not criticism: calls it "one of the most exciting artist-run art organizations in Latin America".
- Observes that it began with exhibitions but soon emphasised sociability and conversation, and that only the window project La Vitrina remains of its exhibiting.
2. MoMA's Post: Marta Dansie, "Growing Seeds of Thought: 10 Days in Colombia" (2017)
URL: https://post.moma.org/?p=12017
- Not criticism: describes the space, its programmes and Muñoz's aim to correct an imbalance in Cali, a city with many art schools but few galleries.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper (Melanie Gerlis, 31 May 2013): a Turin appeals court sentenced Stephan Schmidheiny, head of the Eternit group, to 18 years over the deaths of 3,000 people exposed to asbestos since 1976; he denied the charges and was appealing. It also reported that Schmidheiny's estranged wife owned 70% of the autonomous Daros LatinAmerica collection. The piece does not mention Lugar a Dudas.
- Connection: Daros Latin America supported the launch of Lugar a Dudas in 2005 and is credited among past funders; no reporting found of Daros money reaching the space today. Wikipedia reports the Daros Latinamerica Collection was acquired in its entirety by MALBA, announced in December 2025.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is Dutch and Swiss arts funds and Colombian public bodies, with Muñoz's own grant behind the house; it now runs on savings. The weak points are the lack of any board, accounts or current funder list, and a past funder, Daros Latin America, 70% of which belonged in 2013 to the estranged wife of Stephan Schmidheiny, who was sentenced that year over asbestos deaths and was appealing. No reporting found of Daros money reaching the space today. Worth watching: how the space is funded now that older grants are spent.
