NY CARLSBERG GLYPTOTEK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: ny-carlsberg-glyptotek
Website: https://www.glyptoteket.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Art and antiquities museum at Dantes Plads 7, Copenhagen (museum website).
- Built from the collection of the brewer Carl Jacobsen, first shown in 1882 at his villa; he donated it in 1888 to the Danish State and the City of Copenhagen, and the museum opened on 1 May 1897 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Vilhelm Dahlerup's original building, an expansion by Hack Kampmann inaugurated in 1906, a 1996 addition by Henning Larsen, and a Winter Garden with a copper and wrought-iron dome (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Greek, Roman and Etruscan antiquities, Egyptian pieces, all of Degas's bronzes and Danish Golden Age painting; more than 10,000 works (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Mondays; 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday until 21:00. Adults DKK 150, under 27 and students DKK 120, under 18 free, free on the last Wednesday of each month (museum website).

FUNDING
Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek: foundation-funded: the Carlsberg Foundation and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation, plus a Møller foundation
- Carlsberg Foundation and Ny Carlsberg Foundation: on 5 March 2026 they announced DKK 750 million each for restoration and technical upgrades of the listed complex; project budget DKK 2 billion. The Ny Carlsberg Foundation gave DKK 80 million in 2016 to 2025 for studies and design (Carlsberg Foundation).
- Origin of the money: the brewer Carl Jacobsen founded the Ny Carlsberg Foundation in 1902 and transferred his brewery to the Carlsberg Foundation, founded by his father J.C. Jacobsen in 1876 (lex.dk).
- A.P. Møller og Hustru Chastine Mc-Kinney Møllers Fond til almene Formaal: DKK 150 million for the restoration of the dome and the indoor Winter Garden (TV 2 Kosmopol, undated page). The article states that A.P. Møller Fonden owns A.P. Møller Holding, which owns a large part of the shipping company Mærsk, and that the museum closes to visitors when the work starts in 2028.
- Augustinus Fonden: supported the purchase of Gauguin's "La neige à Copenhague", together with the Ny Carlsberg Foundation and Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond; the page is tagged 2022 and gives no amounts (Ny Carlsberg Foundation). No 2025 grant found: no page opened shows an Augustinus Fonden grant of DKK 1.53 million to the Glyptotek.
- No reporting found of a state grant or ticket income.

Red flags: tobacco money (Augustinus Fonden)
- Augustinus Fonden owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (Wikipedia), which held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group on 1 May 2025; C.W. Obel held more than 10% (Scandinavian Tobacco Group investor page).

Red flags: antiquities from dealers, as reported
- Hecht: ArtsJournal (2009) reported that Italy's dispute concerned Etruscan and Greco-Roman objects the Glyptotek bought from the dealer Robert Hecht, then on trial in Rome.
- Medici: Chasing Aphrodite, an antiquities-trade blog, reports that the sentence of the dealer Giacomo Medici identified museum objects shown in Polaroids seized in his warehouse, and that the museum denied wrongdoing until 2007.
- Italy: on 5 July 2016 the museum and Italy announced an agreement to return objects; Politiken listed 501 objects and fragments (as reported by Chasing Aphrodite).
- Türkiye: the museum announced on 26 November 2024 that it would return a bronze head of Septimius Severus, acquired in 1970, which it concluded came from Bubon (The Local Denmark).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No review criticises the museum's programme or funders. The criticism found concerns how it acquired antiquities, in a blog and news outlets.
1. Chasing Aphrodite: no byline shown, "The Danish Connection: Holding on to Loot at the Ny Carlsberg Glypotech of Copenhagen" (2013, updated 2016)
URL: https://chasingaphrodite.com/2013/03/14/the-danish-connection-holding-on-to-loot-at-the-ny-carlsberg-glypotech-of-copenhagen/
- Says the museum denied wrongdoing until 2007, then sent Italy documents on objects bought from Medici and Hecht in the 1970s after political pressure; updated in July 2016 with the agreement and Politiken's list of 501 objects. Credits Politiken's reporting (journalist Camilla Stockmann).
2. ArtsJournal: Laura Collins Hughes, "Danish Museum Resists Italy's Bid For Artifacts' Return" (2009)
URL: https://artsjournal.org/?p=44471
- Italy found the Glyptotek harder to deal with than American museums, over objects bought from Hecht. Archive excerpt only.
3. The Local Denmark: "Denmark to return severed head of ancient statue to Türkiye" (2024)
URL: https://www.thelocal.dk/20241127/denmark-to-return-severed-head-of-ancient-statue-to-turkiye
- Reports a Turkish embassy request in May 2023 and the museum's agreement on 26 November 2024; director of collections Rune Frederiksen told AFP it was "not as sure as we perhaps were 25-30 years ago." News report, not criticism.

BOTTOM LINE
The restoration money comes from foundations the Jacobsen family created and from a Møller foundation. The weak points are Augustinus Fonden's support for the Gauguin purchase, as its parent holds more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, and reported antiquities purchases from dealers, which led to returns to Italy in 2016 and Türkiye in 2024.
