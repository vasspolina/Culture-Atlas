MUSEO DE LA MEMORIA Y LOS DERECHOS HUMANOS: Santiago, Chile
ID: museo-de-la-memoria-y-los-derechos-humanos
Website: https://mmdh.cl (checked 2026-10-25; the home page loaded but shows no visit details, board or accounts)

OVERVIEW
- Museum at Avenida Matucana 501, Santiago, on the human rights violations of the military dictatorship of 1973 to 1990 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- President Michelle Bachelet laid the first stone on 10 December 2008 and inaugurated the museum on 11 January 2010 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Not named for a person.
- Building: a 2007 public competition was won by a team of Brazilian architects; construction ran from 2008 to 2009. The building is about 10,900 m2, a volume raised above two water fountains (Spanish Wikipedia).
- What is there: a permanent exhibition across several levels and eleven rooms, from the 1973 coup through repression, resistance, the 1988 plebiscite and the transition to democracy; Alfredo Jaar's "La Geometría de la Conciencia" in the plaza's underground (Spanish Wikipedia).
- María Fernanda García has been executive director since 2022 (Spanish Wikipedia); Francisco Estévez was director in 2018 (BioBioChile).
- Visiting: the main exhibition is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00 (2024 annual report). In 2024 it received 176,846 people: 152,753 free visits, 24,073 people in 1,458 mediated visits and 20 in one online visit; in 2023 it received 209,364 (annual reports). Admission and accessibility were not found on the pages read.

FUNDING
Museo de la Memoria y los Derechos Humanos: mainly public: Government of Chile, through the national heritage service, paid to a private-law foundation that runs the museum
- A private-law foundation runs it; its board includes academics and human rights organisations such as the Vicaría de la Solidaridad and Casa de la Memoria (Spanish Wikipedia). In 2018 the board president was María Luisa Sepúlveda (BioBioChile).
- The foundation's 2024 annual report says it is financed by state transfers set in successive budget laws: CLP 2,152,606,000 in 2024 and CLP 2,079,813,000 in 2023 (museum annual reports, read as extracted text). The report says the museum has the Government of Chile's funding through the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural.
- Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural funding was at issue in Congress: in November 2022 the Senate restored its funding after the Chamber of Deputies rejected it. The Chamber's objections came from Renovación Nacional and Partido Republicano members (El Mostrador, 23 November 2022).
- 2026: The Clinic (Felipe Betancour, 15 May 2026) reports that the Fundación Museo de la Memoria y los Derechos Humanos loses $79,429,000 (Chilean pesos) under the heritage access and support for heritage organisations programme, as part of the Culture ministry's cut of $51,750 million (about 9.81 per cent of its 2026 budget), after the Finance Ministry asked each ministry to cut 3 per cent.
- The 2024 annual report lists CLP 2,142,448,743 in resources accounted for to the state. No reporting found, in the pages read, of private donors, corporate sponsors or audited accounts. The report describes donations of objects to the collection, not of money, and names no corporate sponsor in the passages read.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The criticism found is political and comes from Chilean press and politicians who say the museum shows only part of the history.
1. El Mostrador (not art media): newsroom desk, "Senado revierte negativa de la Cámara y repone recursos para instituciones de DD.HH." (2022)
URL: https://www.elmostrador.cl/destacado/2022/11/23/senado-revierte-negativa-de-la-camara-y-repone-recursos-para-instituciones-de-dd-hh-entre-ellas-el-museo-de-la-memoria/
- Reports Republican Senator Rojo Edwards saying the museum "takes resources comparable to all the other museums added together" (our translation), and that "there has to be reasonableness in what is being spent" (our translation).
- Senators Daniel Núñez and Claudia Pascual defended the funding. Criticises the spending, not the art.
2. BioBioChile (not art media): Leonardo Casas, "Museo de la Memoria tiene área sobre atentado a Pinochet" (2018)
URL: https://www.biobiochile.cl/noticias/nacional/chile/2018/08/13/museo-de-la-memoria-tiene-area-sobre-atentado-a-pinochet-invitan-a-criticos-a-conocerlo.shtml
- Context: Mauricio Rojas lost his post as Culture Minister after calling the museum "a staging" ("un montaje") in a book (our translation).
- Senator Jacqueline van Rysselberghe said the museum shows "only part of the history" and admitted she had not visited; Mario Desbordes of RN asked it to broaden its view to other victims.
- The board president and the director answered that the museum covers the attack on Pinochet and invited critics to visit.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Chilean state, and no flagged funder or private donor appears in the sources read. The weak points are dependence on yearly budget votes in Congress, where deputies rejected its funding in 2022 and the Senate restored it, a 2026 budget cut, and recurring attacks from the political right on its narrative. Worth watching: the 2026 budget and any further cuts under the current government.
