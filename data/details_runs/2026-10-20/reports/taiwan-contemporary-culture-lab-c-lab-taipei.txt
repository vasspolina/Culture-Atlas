TAIWAN CONTEMPORARY CULTURE LAB (C-LAB): Taipei, Taiwan
ID: taiwan-contemporary-culture-lab-c-lab-taipei
Website: https://clab.org.tw

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary culture centre on the 7.15-hectare site of the former Republic of China Air Force Headquarters in Da'an District, Taipei, west of Zhongxiao Fuxing station (C-LAB website; Wikipedia). It lies under the Jianguo Elevated Highway, between Zhongxiao East Road, Renai Road and Jianguo South Road (Bnext, 2020).
- Launched in 2018 and operated by the Taiwan Living Arts Foundation under the Ministry of Culture (C-LAB website). Wikipedia reports that Culture Minister Cheng Li-chun announced the plan in April 2018 and that it opened to the public in August 2018.
- Site history: the Taiwan Governor-General's Industrial Research Institute was set up in 1939; Number 2 Hall, now the Heritage Building, was completed between 1941 and 1943; the Ministry of National Defense set up the Air Force Command Headquarters there in 1950; from 2012 to 2015, after the headquarters moved, the site became the Taiwan Air Force Innovation Base under the Ministry of Economic Affairs; a preparatory group began planning the culture lab in 2017 (C-LAB website).
- In 2024 the former office building, the war-command building and 15 auxiliary facilities were designated national monuments; the C-LAB website states that restoration of the Historic Building and war-command building was completed in 2026 (C-LAB website). CNA reported on 7 July 2026 that the ministry unveiled the restoration, that the 70,000 square metre site would open in full for the first time in August, and that the National Culture and Arts Foundation would move in before the end of the year.
- Programmes: creative residencies for cultural professionals, technology teams and the animation industry, spanning sound, visual arts, cultural technology, animation, performance, education and community services. Taiwan Sound Lab was founded in 2019 with France's IRCAM; a technology media platform followed in 2020 and an animation creators' base in 2022 (C-LAB website).
- Main development areas are contemporary art, technology media and social innovation; the CREATORS programme supports creators in art and research (Bnext, 2020).

FUNDING
Taiwan Contemporary Culture Lab (C-LAB): state: Ministry of Culture, through the Taiwan Living Arts Foundation
- Bnext (25 March 2020, an article labelled as sponsored by C-LAB) describes C-LAB as a government-led public infrastructure project led by the Ministry of Culture, with the Ministry of Science and Technology and the Ministry of Economic Affairs cooperating.
- Operator: the Taiwan Living Arts Foundation runs C-LAB. Its chairperson is Sue Wang, Deputy Minister of the Ministry of Culture. Trustees: Peng Chun-Heng, Lin Ping, Chien Hsiu-Chih, Kung Shu-Chang, Lee Yu-Lin, Wang Chun-Hsiung, Huang Hsin-Chien, Liu Hui-Chih, Joe Hsueh, Yen Ming-Hong, Chiang Ching-Sung and Yami Chou. Standing supervisor Huang Shih-Chung; supervisors Grace Shao and Wu Mei-Shao (C-LAB website, governance page).
- The foundation's public information page carries its 2027 budget document (C-LAB website).

BOTTOM LINE
C-LAB is a state project: the Ministry of Culture took over the former Air Force Headquarters site in 2018 and runs it through the Taiwan Living Arts Foundation, whose chairperson, Sue Wang, is the Ministry of Culture's Deputy Minister. The ministry unveiled the restoration of the monument buildings on 7 July 2026 (CNA).
