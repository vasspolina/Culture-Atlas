GALERI NASIONAL INDONESIA: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: galeri-nasional-indonesia
Website: https://galeri-nasional.or.id (checked 2026-10-20; down: the domain does not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- State art gallery at Jalan Medan Merdeka Timur 14, Central Jakarta, opposite Gambir Station (Wikipedia; Popmama).
- Dedicated as the national gallery on 8 May 1999 (Wikipedia).
- The main building, Gedung A, was built in 1817 as a Dutch Indies residence with materials from Kasteel Batavia; from 1900 it housed the Carpentier Alting Stichting school, abolished in 1961; after 1965 it served as a student group's and then an army brigade's headquarters, before returning to the education and culture department in 1981 to 1982 (Wikipedia).
- Holds about 1,770 works, including Raden Saleh, Affandi and Basuki Abdullah, and foreign artists such as Wassily Kandinsky, Victor Vasarely and Zao Wou-Ki (Wikipedia).
- Jarot Mahendra was named in January 2025 as the person in charge of the gallery unit (Antara).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday, 9am to 7pm; free entry with online registration (Popmama, May 2026). No reporting found of accessibility provisions.

FUNDING
Galeri Nasional Indonesia: public: Indonesia's Culture Ministry
- Owned by the Ministry of Culture through the Indonesian Heritage Agency (Wikipedia), one of the new ministries created in President Prabowo Subianto's cabinet expansion (The Jakarta Post).
- It sits inside one public service agency (BLU) with other state museums; in April 2026 Culture Minister Fadli Zon said the gallery should ideally be run as its own BLU, and the head of Museums and Cultural Heritage, Indira Esti Nurjadin, said the units must keep raising non-tax state revenue (RRI).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, a budget figure or an annual report.

Red flags: Yos Suprapto exhibition cancelled
- The gallery asked Yogyakarta artist Yos Suprapto to remove five of 30 paintings from "Kebangkitan: Tanah Untuk Kedaulatan Pangan", works that press reports link to former president Joko Widodo; he covered two, "Konoha I" and "Konoha II", with black cloth and refused to hide three more (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Curator Suwarno Wisetrotomo resigned, calling the works "too vulgar"; the gallery called off the show ten minutes before its 19 December 2024 opening, citing "unavoidable technical constraints" (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Zamrud Sayta Negara, head of the gallery team, denied any intent to ban the show; Fadli Zon said the five works had no link to food sovereignty and raised a possible political motive (Mongabay).
- LBH Jakarta and Komnas HAM criticised the cancellation as a restriction on freedom of expression (Mongabay); on 24 January 2025 Megawati criticised it at a PDI Perjuangan event, saying "ekspresi seni itu harus dihormati" (artistic expression must be respected, our translation) (CNN Indonesia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism centres on the December 2024 cancellation, covered by the art press and as a press-freedom issue by national media.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Annette Meier, "Indonesian Artist's Exhibition Canceled Over Political Paintings" (2025)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/indonesian-artist-s-exhibition-canceled-over-political-paintings
- Reports the requests to hide the works, the curator's resignation and the cancellation.
- Quotes Yos comparing it to censorship and calling it "an alarm for freedom of expression in Indonesia"; notes Fadli Zon withdrew from opening the show.
2. The Jakarta Post: editorial board, "Authoritarian alarm" (2025)
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/01/03/authoritarian-alarm
- Argues the closure should alarm the country about free expression and that the gallery "refused to live in the present"; the images spread online anyway.
- Sets it beside a broadcasting bill critics said would widen censorship powers.
- A national newspaper editorial, not art media; subscription prompts appear, but the full text was readable.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is paid for and run by the state, with no private sponsors on record. The weak point is political control: in 2024 it cancelled a show over paintings that press reports link to Jokowi, and the Culture Minister who oversees it suggested a political motive. Worth watching: how the planned move to its own public service agency changes its independence.
