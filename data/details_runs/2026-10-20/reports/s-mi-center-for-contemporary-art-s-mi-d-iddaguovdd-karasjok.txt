SÁMI CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART (SÁMI DÁIDDAGUOVDDÁŠ): Karasjok, Norway
ID: s-mi-center-for-contemporary-art-s-mi-d-iddaguovdd-karasjok
Website: https://samidaiddaguovddas.no

OVERVIEW
- Sámi contemporary art centre at Suomageaidnu 14, Karasjok. It calls itself the leading international center for contemporary Sámi artists, with a main focus on international Sámi contemporary art with local anchoring in Sápmi (stated by the centre).
- Established in 1986 (stated by the centre). The Stiftelsen Sámi Dáiddaguovddáš foundation was established on 23 August 2013 and registered on 9 January 2014 (Proff, from the Norwegian business register).
- Its purpose, in its own words, is supporting, communicating and visualizing Sámi contemporary art in Europe, to indigenous areas and to the largest art arenas in the world, while providing room for new and experimental artistic practices.
- A gallery shop sells books, art, handicrafts, jewellery, gifts and Sámi music.
- Free entry for everyone. Open Thursday and Friday 10:00 to 16:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 16:00.
- Board per the centre's website: chair Geir Tore Holm (appointed by SDS); deputy chair Maja Kristine Jåma and deputy Piera Heaika Muotka (appointed by the Sámi Parliament); member Svein Atle Somby and deputy Mathis Nilsen Eira (appointed by Karasjok Municipality); two further members and two further deputies appointed by SDS.
- In May 2025 the board appointed Line Kalak, a lawyer and former assistant professor of law at the Sámi University of Applied Sciences, as managing director, "the very first Sámi leader of the institution since SDG was transformed into a foundation over ten years ago" (press release, 13 May 2025). The board appointed Marija Griniuk as director for a four-year term, due to start on 25 July 2022 (centre's announcement, 19 May 2022).

FUNDING
Sámi Dáiddaguovddáš (Karasjok): Sámi Parliament project funding, with board seats held by the Sámi Parliament and Karasjok Municipality
- The Sámi Parliament (Sametinget) funded the centre's participation at the ArtVilnius art fair in Lithuania, 4 to 6 October 2024 (stated by the centre).
- Operating revenue for 2025 was NOK 7,921 thousand, with an operating result of minus NOK 229 thousand, total assets of NOK 2,051 thousand and equity of NOK 1,611 thousand at December 2025 (Proff).

BOTTOM LINE
The documented money behind the centre, a Sámi foundation, is the Sámi Parliament's funding of its ArtVilnius 2024 participation. The Sámi Parliament and Karasjok Municipality also appoint board members. Proff reports 2025 revenue of NOK 7.9 million.
