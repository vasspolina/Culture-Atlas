DE LA WARR PAVILION: Bexhill-on-Sea, United Kingdom
ID: de-la-warr-pavilion
Website: https://www.dlwp.com (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary arts centre on the seafront at Marina, Bexhill-on-Sea, East Sussex, in a Grade I listed building (DLWP website; Wikipedia).
- Built in 1935: the 9th Earl De La Warr, a socialist and Mayor of Bexhill, started the architectural competition and persuaded the council to develop the seafront site as a public building. The Duke and Duchess of York opened it on 12 December 1935 (Wikipedia).
- Named for the 9th Earl De La Warr (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Erich Mendelsohn and Serge Chermayeff; listed Grade I in 1986 (Wikipedia).
- After the war Bexhill Corporation, later Rother District Council, managed it; ownership passed to the De La Warr Pavilion Charitable Trust from 2004, and £6 million from the Heritage Lottery Fund and Arts Council England in 2002 paid for a restoration that reopened it on 15 October 2005 as an arts centre (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, a theatre for concerts, comedy, film and family events, a Café Bar & Kitchen, a shop and workshops (DLWP website).
- Visiting: open 10am to 5pm; the first exhibition listed is free, and screenings and live events are ticketed from £6. It stays open during the current staircase window works (DLWP website). No accessibility details were read.

FUNDING
De La Warr Pavilion: mainly public: Arts Council England, Rother District Council and UK Government capital money, with trusts and foundations
- Arts Council England: £517,785 a year as a National Portfolio Organisation for 2023 to 2026, plus a £400,000 capital grant in 2022 for auditorium technical systems (DLWP funders page).
- Rother District Council: £477,000 a year from April 2021 to March 2024 (DLWP funders page).
- Levelling Up Fund: Rother District Council announced £19,192,000 on 26 January 2023, and said the Pavilion project totals £23.4m, with other funding still to be found (Rother District Council). The Pavilion's capital project page says £17m of the £19m goes to the Pavilion and £2m to a community hub in Sidley, and that the Pavilion has £17m secured from the UK Government.
- National Lottery Heritage Fund: the project page records an award of £7,875,683 dated 27 March 2024 to Rother District Council, delivered with the Trust, for the first phase: auditorium and foyer roof repairs, window replacement, access, low-carbon heating and community programmes. Its article dated 12 December 2025 speaks of support of "over £7.6million", so the two figures differ (Heritage Fund). The capital project page says a full application was due in August 2025 with a decision in December 2025, and gives no amount.
- Capital project supporters named without amounts: East Sussex County Council, The Chalk Cliff Trust, Foyle Foundation and Suzy Eddie Izzard. The Trust is raising £3m to begin the masterplan and sells seat plaques for £250 to £1,000 (DLWP capital project page).
- Programme funders now: Art Fund, The Chalk Cliff Trust, Esmée Fairbairn Foundation, John Thaw Foundation, McLay Dementia Trust, The Isabel Blackman Foundation, Grow Wild Community Grants and The Red Hill Trust (DLWP funders page).
- Past programme funders listed include Garfield Weston Foundation, Paul Hamlyn Foundation and Henry Moore Foundation (DLWP funders page).
- No reporting found of accounts figures on the pages read.

Red flags: Gulf state body listed as a building funder
- The Pavilion's funders page lists "United Arab Emirates" as the last of 15 names under "Building funders", a list introduced as thanks for "current and previous support for the building". The page gives no year, amount, purpose or named UAE body (DLWP funders page).
- The capital project page and Wikipedia do not mention the UAE, so no source opened says what the money paid for or when.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is admiring or descriptive: The London Magazine (Maria Mendes, review of "Group Hat" and "How Chicago! Imagists 1960s & 70s", undated on the page) calls the Pavilion "a pioneering centre for art, culture and recreation" and criticises nothing. Local newspaper pages on the council grant could not be opened and are not cited.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Pavilion is public and charitable: Arts Council England, the local council, the UK government and lottery capital grants, and trusts, with no corporate sponsor of concern found. The weak point is the "United Arab Emirates" entry on its own building funders list, with no year, amount or named body. Worth watching are the outcome of the Arts Council Creative Foundations Fund application, a decision on which the Pavilion expected in March 2026, and the three-phase masterplan whose total cost the Pavilion has not stated.
