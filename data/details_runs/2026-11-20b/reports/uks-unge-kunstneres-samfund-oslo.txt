UKS (UNGE KUNSTNERES SAMFUND / YOUNG ARTISTS' SOCIETY): Oslo, Norway
ID: uks-unge-kunstneres-samfund-oslo
Website: https://www.uks.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Membership organisation for professional artists and an exhibition space for contemporary art at Keysers gate 1, Tullinløkka, in central Oslo (UKS website; Store norske leksikon).
- Founded by artists for artists in 1921 (UKS website); its 2026 e-flux announcement says the founders were the Oslo avant-garde.
- Membership of UKS includes membership of Norske Billedkunstnere (NBK); in 2024 it had about 700 members and was NBK's largest member organisation (Store norske leksikon).
- Besides exhibitions it holds artist talks and public events, administers artist grants and residency places (Store norske leksikon), and does political work through hearings and consultations; its main projects under the 2022 to 2026 strategic plan are climate and sustainability, Palestine, the studio situation and the national artist survey (UKS website).
- The 2026 programme, "Lessons in Love", presents new commissions by artists chosen through an artist-juried open call (e-flux, 2026).
- Board: chair Ragnhild Aamås and six members, with two deputies; director Miriam Wistreich (UKS website).
- Its archive of nearly 100 years is held by the National Archives (Riksarkivet) in Oslo (Store norske leksikon).
- Visiting: during exhibitions open Wednesday 12 to 5 pm, Thursday 12 to 7 pm, Friday to Sunday 12 to 5 pm; no entrance fee (UKS website).

FUNDING
UKS: mainly public: the Norwegian Ministry of Culture, with an Oslo municipal grant
- UKS describes itself as a non-profit organisation with the Ministry of Culture as its principal funder (UKS website). Store norske leksikon says it is mainly financed by the Ministry of Culture and Equality.
- The exhibition space receives an annual operating grant from the Ministry of Culture and the City of Oslo; the board's political work is paid for by members' fees (UKS annual report 2023).
- Accounts: operating income of NOK 7.80 million in 2023, NOK 7.31 million in 2024 and NOK 8.49 million in 2025, with a loss of about NOK 20,000 in 2025 (Brønnøysund accounts register).
- KORO (Public Art Norway) supports its 2026 social-practice commission, Afrang Nordlöf Malekian's "Dance Dance Revolution: Oslo" (e-flux, 2026).
- Fritt Ord supported a UKS seminar on artists' studios on 24 April 2023 (UKS annual report 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean, public money in an artist-run organisation, with no corporate sponsor in view and an elected board answerable to its members.
