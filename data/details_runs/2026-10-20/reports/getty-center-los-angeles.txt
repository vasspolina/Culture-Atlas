GETTY CENTER: Los Angeles, USA
ID: getty-center-los-angeles
Website: https://www.getty.edu/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and research campus at 1200 Getty Center Drive in Brentwood, Los Angeles, on a hilltop in the Santa Monica Mountains above Interstate 405 (Wikipedia, Getty).
- Built by the J. Paul Getty Trust, which J. Paul Getty set up in 1953; the Center opened on 16 December 1997 (Getty, Wikipedia).
- Named for J. Paul Getty (1892 to 1976), the oilman and collector whose bequest endowed the Trust (Getty).
- Richard Meier, chosen in 1984, designed the campus in concrete and steel clad in travertine or aluminium; the cost was later put at $1.3 billion (Wikipedia).
- On a 24-acre campus within a 110-acre site: the Getty Museum, Research Institute, Conservation Institute and Foundation, and Robert Irwin's Central Garden (Wikipedia, Getty).
- Open Tuesday to Friday and Sunday 10am to 6:30pm, Saturday 10am to 9pm, closed Monday; admission is free with a timed reservation, parking $25; ADA accessible, with a wheelchair-accessible tram and free wheelchair rental (stated by the space).
- Galleries in the South, East and North Pavilions are closed for renovation; the whole Center closes on 15 March 2027 and reopens in spring 2028 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Getty Center: endowment-funded: the J. Paul Getty Trust's investments, which began as Getty Oil stock
- Getty's will left the Trust nearly $700 million (stated by the space).
- The annual budget is 5% of the endowment's 36-month average (stated by the space).
- Audited statements for the year to 30 June 2025: investments of $9.32 billion, net investment income of $864.7 million, expenses of $442.7 million and donor contributions of $11.1 million (KPMG audit).
- In May 2025 the Trust issued $500 million in taxable bonds for facilities work (audited statements). Press puts the renovation at $600 to $800 million (Blooloop, citing the Los Angeles Times).
- No reporting found of named corporate sponsors or of current fossil fuel holdings in the endowment.

Red flags: Getty Oil fortune behind the endowment
- J. Paul Getty's will left the bulk of his estate, including $660 million in Getty Oil stock, to the Trust, which received the full bequest in 1982 (Getty timeline, Wikipedia).
- In January 1984 the Getty Museum, which held 11.8% of Getty Oil, agreed to sell its shares to Texaco at $125 a share (Texaco v. Pennzoil case brief). The Christian Science Monitor reported in 1987 that the move to Texaco nearly doubled the endowment. No reporting found of a Getty Oil holding since.
- The atlas counts the endowment as Fossil fuels money, although the shares were sold in 1984. It still pays for the Center through the 5% budget rule above.

Red flags: 2006 Attorney General findings and the antiquities cases
- In October 2006 California Attorney General Bill Lockyer reported that president Barry Munitz overspent on travel and meals and had staff run personal errands, and that the Trust spent over $21,500 on artwork gifts for trustees (Chronicle of Philanthropy).
- Munitz resigned in 2006, repaid $250,000 and gave up over $2 million in benefits; an independent monitor oversaw the Trust until 7 May 2008 (Chronicle of Philanthropy, Wikipedia).
- Antiquities curator Marion True was indicted in Italy on 1 April 2005 for conspiracy to traffic in illicit antiquities and resigned on 1 October 2005; an Italian court dismissed the remaining charges in 2010 (Wikipedia).
- NPR reported on 1 August 2007 that the Getty would return 40 objects Italy said were looted, keeping a statue of Aphrodite until 2010.

Not related: the Climate Emergency Fund, co-founded in 2019 by Aileen Getty, J. Paul Getty's granddaughter, which funds climate protest groups. It is a separate nonprofit.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little current criticism found; the main critical writing dates from the 2005 to 2007 crisis.
1. The New York Review of Books: Martin Filler, "The Getty: For Better and Worse" (2006)
URL: https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2006/11/16/the-getty-for-better-and-worse/
- Says the bequest made the Getty "the world's richest art institution" and calls its troubles of 2005 to 2006 an "epic of hubris and retribution".
- Calls the sums museums squander on mediocre buildings "scandalous" and applies that to Meier's Getty Center.
- Reports a widespread feeling in museum circles that True was made a scapegoat for the Trust's acquisition practices, and says its "severely compromised ethics" kept the Getty from defending her.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Getty Center is the Getty Trust's own endowment, which began as Getty Oil stock; the atlas counts it as Fossil fuels money although the shares were sold in 1984. The other weak points are the 2006 Attorney General findings and the 2007 antiquities returns. Worth watching: the $500 million bond issue and the closure from 2027.
