FUNDAÇÃO SINDIKA DOKOLO: Luanda, Angola
ID: fundação-sindika-dokolo-luanda
Website: http://www.sindikadokolofondation.org (checked 2026-10-20; serves spam: it redirects through fondation-sindikadokolo.com to the gambling site 1-win.net.br; fundacaosindikadokolo.com does not respond)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural foundation headquartered in Luanda that looks after Sindika Dokolo's contemporary African art collection and ran the Luanda Triennial (Wikipedia).
- Dokolo, a Congolese collector, began the collection in 2004 with the Angolan artist and curator Fernando Alvim and set up the foundation in 2006 (The Art Newspaper, 2017); Alvim was vice-president and Simon Njami a consultant (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder, the husband of Isabel dos Santos, daughter of former president José Eduardo dos Santos; he died free-diving off Dubai on 29 October 2020, aged 48, and Dubai Police found no criminal suspicion (Al Jazeera).
- The collection began with the purchase of Hans Bogatzke's collection (Wikipedia) and held about 3,000 works by artists including William Kentridge, Zanele Muholi and Edson Chagas (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- Programmes: the Luanda Triennial, the Check List Luanda Pop side project at the 2007 Venice Biennale, a 2018 show with Documenta 14 artists it helped fund, and buying back looted works, including three Chokwe pieces returned to the Dundo Museum in 2015 (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of a current building, opening hours or public programme since 2020.

FUNDING
Fundação Sindika Dokolo: founder-funded: the Dokolo and dos Santos fortune
- The foundation paid for the Chokwe returns itself, buying from collectors in Belgium and France at undisclosed prices (The Art Newspaper, 2015).
- No reporting found of any other funder, accounts or register entry.
- In early January 2020 Angola froze the couple's assets and bank accounts; President João Lourenço sought to recover $1bn in state loans (The Art Newspaper). The Art Newspaper said it was not known whether the collection was targeted.

Red flags: founders' fortune from Angolan state deals
- The ICIJ's Luanda Leaks, from more than 715,000 documents, accused dos Santos of making her $2.2bn fortune by exploiting Angola; she denies it (The Art Newspaper, January 2020).
- Her father appointed her head of the state oil company Sonangol in 2016; Lourenço fired her in 2017 (The Art Newspaper).
- In July 2020 the Provincial Court of Luanda called the deal through which state diamond company Sodiam invested in the jeweller de Grisogono "fraudulent", saying it gave Dokolo and dos Santos the chance to control the company; Sodiam says it lost its $120 million investment (ICIJ). Dokolo's lawyers said he invested $115 million and more.

Red flags: US corruption designation
- On 9 December 2021 the US State Department designated Isabel dos Santos for "significant corruption" and barred her and her immediate family from the United States (ICIJ).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism; most art-press coverage praised Dokolo's restitution work, and the one sustained attack is an opinion essay.
1. esferapública: Carlos Salazar, "Alfredo Jaar, Muxima y los Dokolo de Angola, Resucitados" (2009)
URL: https://esferapublica.org/?p=17314
- Argues the collection's claim to dignify Africans is contradicted by the family's money: Dokolo's father's bank and its collapse in 1986, and diamond activity the author ties to abuses in the Lundas.
- Faults Alfredo Jaar for showing in the 2007 African pavilion without comment, and quotes Barthélémy Toguo refusing to have his name associated with Dokolo.
- An openly polemical opinion piece; its allegations rest on press sources it cites.
2. The Art Newspaper: Cristina Ruiz, "Africa's top collector returns ancestral works to Angola" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/africa-s-top-collector-returns-ancestral-works-to-angola
- Not criticism: reports Dokolo's offer to repay collectors of stolen works only what they paid, and notes his marriage to the president's daughter.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation ran on the private fortune of Dokolo and dos Santos, which Angolan courts, the ICIJ and the US government tie to state oil and diamond deals. No reporting found of activity since Dokolo's death in 2020, and its old domain now serves a gambling site. Worth watching: what Angolan or Portuguese courts decide about the collection.
