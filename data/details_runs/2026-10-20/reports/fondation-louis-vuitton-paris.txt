FONDATION LOUIS VUITTON: Paris, France
ID: fondation-louis-vuitton-paris
Website: https://www.fondationlouisvuitton.fr/en (checked 2026-10-20; it refused automated reading, so visit details come from listings)

OVERVIEW
- Corporate art foundation and museum at 8 avenue du Mahatma-Gandhi, in the Jardin d'Acclimatation in the Bois de Boulogne, 16th arrondissement (Wikipedia).
- A fondation d'entreprise of the LVMH group, backed by Bernard Arnault; the building was inaugurated on 20 October 2014, with Suzanne Pagé as artistic director (Wikipedia).
- Building by Frank Gehry: glass sails of about 3,600 panels over white forms clad in about 19,000 concrete panels (Wikipedia).
- The land is held from the City of Paris under a 55-year agreement from 1 January 2007 at 100,000 euros a year (French Wikipedia).
- Exhibition galleries and a collection including works by Basquiat and Jeff Koons; 1,570,000 visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: during exhibitions open daily except Tuesday, 11 am to 8 pm; full price €18, reduced €5 or €10; wheelchair access listed (Offi).

FUNDING
Fondation Louis Vuitton: corporate: LVMH companies, much of the building cost returned as tax breaks
- LVMH companies paid for the building. The estimate was 100 million euros in 2006; Marianne reported a final cost of 780 million euros in May 2017 (English Wikipedia; French Wikipedia says nearly 800 million).
- Under the 2003 Aillagon law, companies deduct 60% of cultural gifts from corporate tax. The Cour des comptes reported in November 2018 that the exemption cost 518.1 million euros over the foundation's first eleven fiscal years, about 47.1 million euros a year (Anticor).
- The foundation's cost alone came to about 8% of the country's patronage tax breaks, and parliament then limited the law for the first time (The Conversation, 2025).
- The Conseil d'État ruled on 7 October 2022 that corporate foundations' accounts cannot be released, as they reveal their "situation financière" (Anticor). No reporting found of a budget or annual accounts.

Red flags: tax breaks and secret accounts
- The Cour des comptes report of November 2018 spoke of "le coût exorbitant pour les contribuables" of the LVMH exemption (Anticor).
- In November 2018 the anti-corruption group FRICC filed a complaint for fraud and tax evasion; it was dismissed in September 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Anticor went to court in 2020 to see the accounts; the Conseil d'État's 2022 ruling kept them closed (Anticor).

Red flags: LVMH spying settlement (2021)
- On 17 December 2021 a Paris court approved LVMH's payment of €10 million to end an inquiry, opened in 2011, into claims that LVMH hired former intelligence chief Bernard Squarcini to spy on critics including the filmmaker François Ruffin, who sued in 2019 (The Local).
- LVMH's legal director said the group accepted responsibility "including for the failings"; Ruffin asked: "Can justice be bought so cheaply?" (The Local).

Red flags: labour exploitation at an LVMH unit
- In 2024 a Milan judge placed Manufacturers Dior SRL under court administration with an external director after police inspections of four Chinese-owned suppliers found workers in conditions below minimum requirements; data mapping showed "seamless day-night production cycles" (FashionUnited). Reuters, via Storyboard18 (11 June 2024), reported a one-year administration of LVMH's Italian unit; the court said the unit did not take "appropriate measures to check the actual working conditions" of its contractors.
- A March 2022 Mediapart investigation reported that the foundation's security agents stood still on 12-hour shifts and that reception hosts felt pressured and unprotected by their employers (French Wikipedia, citing Mediapart, which was not read).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is about money, not art: the tax cost to the public and the closed accounts. It comes from an academic outlet, a newspaper investigation and a watchdog group, not the art press.
1. The Conversation: Tanguy Gatay, "The group Louis Vuitton (LVMH), a gift to the Musée d'Orsay and a huge tax incentive: business as usual?" (2025)
URL: https://theconversation.com/the-group-louis-vuitton-lvmh-a-gift-to-the-musee-dorsay-and-a-huge-tax-incentive-business-as-usual-265403
- Argues LVMH's patronage is largely paid by the public through tax deductions, with a €518 million rebate on the foundation.
- Argues the gifts raise LVMH's image as a "cultural benefactor" and warns of diffuse influence over museums and an implicit sense of indebtedness.
2. USA Art News: Claire Bennett, on Le Monde's investigation (2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/bernard-arnault-defended-his-use-of-tax-breaks-on-arts-patronage/
- Reports Le Monde's case that Arnault's arts spending brought LVMH tax and marketing gains.
- Arnault answered that he funded the foundation under the 2003 law: "I gave, and that seems suspicious." Le Monde's series was not read.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation is paid for by LVMH companies, whose tax bill fell by 518.1 million euros through the exemption, per the Cour des comptes. Its accounts are closed by court ruling. Worth watching: LVMH's legal record (the 2021 spying settlement, the 2024 Dior labour case) and any change to the Aillagon tax rules.
