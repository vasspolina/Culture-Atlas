ID: kunstmuseum-bern

Red flags: 2021 decision to give up 29 works from the Gurlitt bequest
- On 10 December 2021 the Kunstmuseum Bern announced that it would give up ownership of works of unclear provenance from the Cornelius Gurlitt bequest (ORF, 10 December 2021). The Art Newspaper (Hannah McGivern, 17 December 2021) puts the number at 29 works with incomplete wartime provenance that are suspected of being Nazi-looted art.
- The Kunstmuseum Bern Foundation decided on 5 November 2021 to transfer two 1922 Otto Dix watercolours, "Dame in der Loge" and "Dompteuse", jointly to the descendants of Ismar Littmann and Paul Schaefer, pending an agreement between the parties; the Littmann heirs had a long-standing legal claim to the works (The Art Newspaper, 17 December 2021).
- The museum was considering a restitution claim from the heirs of Fritz Salo Glaser to 13 of the 29 works; five were to go to the German federal authorities and nine were to be researched further (The Art Newspaper, 17 December 2021).
- The museum kept close to 1,100 works whose ownership history from 1933 to 1945 remained unclear (The Art Newspaper, 17 December 2021).
- Director Nina Zimmer said: "We felt it was time to act even in these more difficult cases" (The Art Newspaper, 17 December 2021).

Red flags: 2025 Munich court ruling on a fee claim by Gurlitt's former lawyer
- Hannes Hartung, a former lawyer of Cornelius Gurlitt, sued the Kunstmuseum Bern for legal remuneration of about EUR 1.36 million; in a judgment of 14 May 2025 the Munich Higher Regional Court (Oberlandesgericht München) awarded him EUR 106,815 (CMS, the law firm that represented the museum, 22 May 2025).
- At the first hearing before Munich Regional Court I on 26 January 2016, Hartung claimed a partial bill of 6,000 euros from Gurlitt's unknown heirs; neither the Kunstmuseum Bern nor the estate administrator had paid it (Legal Tribune Online, Tanja Podolski, 26 January 2016).
