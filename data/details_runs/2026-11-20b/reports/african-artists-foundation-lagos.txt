AFRICAN ARTISTS' FOUNDATION: Lagos, Nigeria
ID: african-artists-foundation-lagos
Website: https://africanartists.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit art space at 3B Isiola Oyekan Close, off Adeleke Adedoyin Street, Victoria Island, Lagos (stated by the space).
- Founded in 2007 by the curator Azu Nwagbogu (Wikipedia; stated by the space).
- It runs the LagosPhoto festival, founded in 2009, and ran the National Art Competition, founded in 2008 (Wikipedia). Its touring residency and exhibition series "Dig Where You Stand" has run in Benin, Togo (Palais de Lomé), Portugal and Dakar (stated by the space).
- In October 2020 AAF was evicted from the building it rented from the Umanah family after Keystone Bank claimed the family's properties through a court order; the closure halted most activity for two years and put the 2021 LagosPhoto on hold, and after the bank refused a refund AAF's board decided to relocate to another African country (ThisDay). By 2026 the space was showing exhibitions in Victoria Island again (stated by the space).
- Programme: "Nsi: After the Harvest", curated by Nkhensani Mkhari with the Congolese plantation workers' collective CATPC and Human Activities, opened in Lagos on 5 October 2026 and stays on view until January 2027, with a Dakar chapter from 22 November 2026 (stated by the space).
- In November 2026 AAF and the ZILART Museum in Moscow launched an online colloquium, "African Art: In Search of Meanings", tied to ZILART's exhibition "African Art: Gods, Ancestors, Life" (stated by the space).

FUNDING
African Artists' Foundation: mixed: private foundations, foreign cultural bodies and corporate sponsors
- National Art Competition: Nigerian Breweries Plc sponsored it and AAF organised it; for the 2016 edition the overall winner received 2 million naira and two other winners 1 million naira each (BusinessDay).
- LagosPhoto: the festival's supporters page, headed "Festival 2025" with dates of 27 October to 23 January 2025, so the edition it covers is uncertain, names the Open Society Foundations, the Ford Foundation, Tecno Mobile and the Mike Adenuga Center as principal sponsors, Canon Central and North Africa as imaging partner, the Institut Français and Alliance Française among partners, and Vontobel, Tiger Lager Beer and Hennessy as supporters (LagosPhoto).
- The Mike Adenuga Alliance Française Centre in Ikoyi, inaugurated in July 2018, was delivered on a pledge by Globacom chairman Mike Adenuga (ThisDay, 2019).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. The one critique on record concerns LagosPhoto showing too few Nigerian artists, which Nwagbogu answered.
1. Africa is a Country: Joe Penney, "LagosPhoto is Five Years Old" (2014)
URL: https://africasacountry.com/2014/10/lagosphoto-is-five-years-old
- Interview recording complaints that LagosPhoto does not show enough Nigerians and that some see AAF as elitist and exclusive.
- Nwagbogu answers that few Nigerians apply to World Press Photo and that "we will not show your work just because we have an obligation to show Nigerians."
2. ThisDay: Yinka Olatunbosun, "On Chokehold, LagosPhoto Festival Looks Elsewhere" (2024, dated by the page address)
URL: https://www.thisdaylive.com/index.php/2024/05/05/on-chokehold-lagosphoto-festival-looks-elsewhere
- Not criticism of AAF: reports the Keystone Bank eviction, AAF's 600 million naira claim, and the bank's reply that the Umanah family, not the bank, caused the closure.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind AAF is clean, and no named funder belongs to a flagged industry. The record carries no red flag.
