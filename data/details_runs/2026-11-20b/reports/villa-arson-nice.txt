VILLA ARSON: Nice, France
ID: villa-arson-nice
Website: https://villa-arson.fr (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- National higher school of art and contemporary art centre at 20 avenue Stephen Liégeard, on the Saint-Barthélemy hill in northern Nice, overlooking the city and the coast (Villa Arson website; Wikipedia).
- The site has the school, the art centre, artist residencies, a publishing shop, a specialist library and Radio Arson (Villa Arson website).
- The estate was an eighteenth-century villa bought in 1812 by the Avignon merchant Pierre-Joseph Arson, later the Grand Hôtel Saint-Barthélemy and then the Clinique de Cyrnos; in 1964 the City of Nice ceded it to the State for an international art school wanted by André Malraux (French Wikipedia).
- Building: Michel Marot designed the concrete buildings, commissioned in 1964 and completed in 1972, with roof terraces and patios that keep century-old trees; the site covers nearly 15,000 m² of buildings and holds the "Architecture contemporaine remarquable" label since 2000 (DDA Architectes; French Wikipedia).
- The conference, cinema and theatre hall has stood unused since completion because of structural and water problems; DDA Architectes made a feasibility study for it in 2021 and a heritage diagnosis and master plan for the whole site in 2023 (DDA Architectes).
- In 2002 the Ministry of Culture designated it a grand établissement for contemporary art (Wikipedia); in 2003 it became an établissement public administratif, and since 2020 it is a component of Université Côte d'Azur (French Wikipedia; Villa Arson website).
- Staff: 67, of whom 24 teach (Villa Arson website).
- Directors: Sylvain Lizon from 2019 to 2025 (French Wikipedia); then Ulrika Byttner, formerly head of the École supérieure d'art et de design Le Havre-Rouen, named by Minister of Culture Rachida Dati after the board's opinion (Ministry of Culture press release, 19 January 2026).
- Current season: an exhibition on Édouard Glissant and "L'atelier Serge Hélénon", both 12 November 2026 to 4 April 2027 (Villa Arson website).
- Visiting: the art centre opens daily except Tuesday, 2 pm to 6 pm, and the gardens every afternoon; entry is free and needs no booking; the library is free to all from October to June; exhibition rooms and gardens are largely accessible to wheelchair users (Villa Arson website).

FUNDING
Villa Arson: public: a national public establishment under the French Ministry of Culture
- The Villa Arson is an établissement public administratif under the supervision of the Ministry of Culture; its board votes the budget and the allocation of funds (Villa Arson website). The Minister of Culture appoints the director (Ministry of Culture, 2026).
- The State owns the site, which the City of Nice ceded to it in 1964 (French Wikipedia).
- Apprenticeship tax: companies can assign their apprenticeship tax balance to the school through the state SOLTéA platform; the school also seeks corporate and private patronage for exhibitions, equipment and restoration of the Bosco garden, the concrete of the labelled buildings and the Grand théâtre (Villa Arson website).
- The Francis Bacon MB Art Foundation of Monaco, founded by Majid Boustany, has been a partner since 2017 and awards a 10,000 euro scholarship every two years to a Villa Arson graduate with a DNSEP master's in painting or drawing (MB Art Foundation).
- The Marguerite et Méthode Keskar foundation, sheltered by the Fondation de France, gives a 4,000 euro prize to a Villa Arson graduate under 40; the 2026 winner is Magdalena Galus (Villa Arson website, 16 November 2026).
- With the Cité internationale des arts in Paris it runs three-month residencies for recent graduates from March 2027 (Villa Arson website).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: a State school paid for by the Ministry of Culture, with small private prizes from two foundations. Worth watching is the cost of repairing the Marot buildings and the unused hall.
