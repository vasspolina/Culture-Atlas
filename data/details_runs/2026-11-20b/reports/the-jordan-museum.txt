THE JORDAN MUSEUM: Amman, Jordan
ID: the-jordan-museum
Website: https://www.jordanmuseum.jo (checked 2026-11-20; the site says it is being redeveloped and points visitors to its social media)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Jordan's archaeology and history in Amman's Ras Al Ain area, in a building of about 10,000 square metres (The Jordan Times, 12 February 2017; The National, 13 March 2014).
- Director general Ihab Amarin said a Jordan Museum had been a national dream since the 1960s; its first phase, from the Palaeolithic to the end of the Classical period, opened in 2013 (The Jordan Times, 2017). The National reported a soft opening in January 2013, with Sharifa Nofa Bint Nasser as director.
- English Wikipedia says a committee headed by Queen Rania was set up to develop the museum, that construction began in 2009 and that it opened officially in 2014.
- Collection: animal bones 1.5 million years old, the 'Ain Ghazal plaster statues of about 7500 BC, the Tulaylat Al Ghassul wall painting, the Tal Siran bottle, the Dead Sea Copper Scroll and a replica of the Mesha Stele (The National; The Jordan Times, 2017).
- Leadership: Princess Sumaya bint El Hassan is vice-chair of the board of trustees; she showed King Abdullah II and Oman's Sultan Haitham bin Tarik round the museum on 23 May 2024 (Royal Hashemite Court). Ihab Amarin, still director general, spoke at a UNESCO museums forum in China in April 2025 (The Jordan Times, 28 April 2025).
- Visitors: about 24,000 in 2016, some 45 percent of them school students (The Jordan Times, 2017).

FUNDING
The Jordan Museum: public: the Jordanian state, with a building planned under a Japanese aid loan
- The state: the museum displays pieces on loan from the Department of Antiquities (The National, 2014).
- Japan: a 2000 JICA design study for Japan's yen-loan Tourism Sector Development Project, financed by the Japan Bank for International Cooperation with Jordan's tourism ministry as counterpart, included a new National Museum in Amman, to be built by the Greater Amman Municipality and the ministry and run by a newly established body (JICA study report).
- The same study judged that admission and other income would only slightly exceed running costs and could not repay the building, and urged hiring out halls and galleries for events (JICA study report).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: a state museum with Japanese development finance behind its building. Worth watching is how little the museum publishes about its running budget and board while its website is offline.
