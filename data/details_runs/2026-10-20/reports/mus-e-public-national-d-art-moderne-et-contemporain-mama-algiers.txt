MUSÉE PUBLIC NATIONAL D'ART MODERNE ET CONTEMPORAIN (MAMA): Algiers, Algeria
ID: mus-e-public-national-d-art-moderne-et-contemporain-mama-algiers

OVERVIEW
- National museum of modern and contemporary art at 25 rue Larbi Ben M'Hidi in central Algiers (Wikipedia), facing the Cinémathèque (Gradhiva, 2022).
- Created by executive decree no. 06-263 of 8 August 2006 and inaugurated on 1 December 2007, the year Algiers was Capital of Arab Culture; its decree covers works from 1905 and Algerian modern art from 1945 (Émilie Goudal, Gradhiva, 2022).
- Housed in a neo-Moorish former department store, the Galeries de France, later the Galeries algériennes, which went bankrupt in 1988 (Gradhiva). English Wikipedia dates the building to 1901 to 1909; French Wikipedia says it was built in 1914 by architect Henri Petit.
- Architect Halim Faidi renovated it for the museum in 2006, and it was listed as a historic monument in 2008 (French Wikipedia). The interior was repainted in a specially made paint, "blanc Mama", giving a white cube (Gradhiva).
- Curators: Mohamed Djehiche (2007 to 2016), Nadira Aklouche Laggoune (2016 to 2020) and Kamel Righi (2020 to August 2023) (French Wikipedia).
- Nearly 40 exhibitions since 2007 (Gradhiva), including retrospectives of Olivier Debré (2010) and Ahmed Malek (2019) (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Musée Public National d'Art Moderne et Contemporain (MAMA): public: a state museum created by decree and supported by the Ministry of Culture, with a collection built largely on private donations
- State: the museum was created by government decree and its opening was supported by the Ministry of Culture under minister Khalida Toumi (Gradhiva; English Wikipedia). Acquisitions came through endowments tied to Algiers Capital of Arab Culture and the 50th anniversary of independence (Gradhiva).
- Budget: Gradhiva states the museum had no acquisition budget and no substantial budget beyond the inauguration period, and hopes a future budget will allow more regular exhibitions.
- Donations: about 70 works from Mustapha and Fatiha Orif (2016) and 335 works from collectors Zoulikha Benzine Inal and Djaâfar Inal, plus gifts from artists including Bernard Rancillac, Erró and Sergio Silva Cajahuaringa (Gradhiva). A 1964 "Art et révolution" donation stayed at the Musée national des beaux-arts and was never transferred to MAMA (Gradhiva).
- Oversight: art sociologist Saadi Leray Farid wrote in 2018 that the museum sits under a "triple tutelle" of the Ministry of Culture, the Ministry of Moudjahidine and the Ministry of Religious Affairs (Le Matin d'Algérie).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Le Matin d'Algérie and Gradhiva criticise the museum's programme and its dependence on the state; Ibraaz quotes an artist calling for more independence and financial support for the museum.
1. Le Matin d'Algérie: Saadi Leray Farid, "Les options, contraintes et enjeux du Musée d'art moderne d'Alger" (6 March 2018)
URL: https://lematindalgerie.com/les-options-contraintes-et-enjeux-du-musee-dart-moderne-dalger/
- Opinion piece arguing that the exhibition "Dessinez vos desseins" (opened 27 January 2018) copies one that Hélène Poquet introduced in Toulouse in November 2012.
- Argues that the director, Nadira Laggoune, uses the label "scène alternative" as a stopgap, and that diaspora artists make up for what is missing locally.
- Argues that the triple ministerial supervision keeps the museum from breaking free of "national constants", and cites a 2014 project, "Drapeau Ostentatoire", which presidential advisers at El-Mouradia did not envisage in the sixtieth anniversary year.
2. Gradhiva: Émilie Goudal, "Émanciper les récits, ériger un musée d'art moderne en postcolonie ? Ancrages du musée d'art moderne d'Alger" (no. 34, 2022)
URL: https://journals.openedition.org/gradhiva/6494
- Notes that no permanent display of the collections had been set up, and that the civil war period (1992 to 1999) is not treated in the collections.
- Criticises an emphasis on the national dimension and state policies, and argues that the museum should take the risk of internal disorientation by accepting alternative and critical artistic and curatorial voices.
3. Ibraaz: Alice Planel, "Productive Contexts and Contemporary Restraints: The Practice of Contemporary Art in Algeria Today" (2 May 2012)
URL: https://archive.ibraaz.org/essays/35
- Calls MAMA "an institution in its infancy" and quotes artist Amina Menia saying the institution needs more independence and financial support.
- Notes that the museum's catalogues are laboriously produced and hard to obtain, not available outside Algeria and sometimes not even within it.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Alice Planel (Ibraaz, 2 May 2012) wrote that Algerian government investment in the arts remains limited and that there is no dedicated, sustainable private or corporate patronage for the arts.
- The connection to MAMA: it is a state museum supported by the Ministry of Culture.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Algerian state, through the Ministry of Culture, with a collection that grew mostly from private and artist donations. The weak points are the absence of an acquisition budget and the supervision by three ministries, as described by Gradhiva and Le Matin d'Algérie.
