TRAMWAY: Glasgow, United Kingdom
ID: tramway-glasgow
Website: https://www.tramway.org

OVERVIEW
- International arts venue for contemporary visual art and performance on Glasgow's south side at 25 Albert Drive, G41 2PE, presenting dance, live art, festivals and free exhibitions (Tramway website).
- Managed by Glasgow Life, the charity that delivers culture and sport for Glasgow City Council (Tramway website).
- The building began life in 1893 as the Coplawhill tram shed and was the city's main tram terminus, depot and factory in the early 1900s. After trams ended in the 1960s it became the Museum of Transport; the museum moved to Kelvin Hall in 1986 and the empty building faced demolition (Tramway's History page).
- Tramway began after a search for a venue to host the 1988 UK performances of Peter Brook's Mahabharata. In 1989 Andy Goldsworthy staged Snowballs in Summer in the space that became the main gallery (Tramway's History page).
- The building has been Category B listed since 1990. It has two performance spaces and two galleries, and in 2009 Scottish Ballet moved into a new extension. In 2003 plans to close the arts centre for Scottish Ballet use drew opposition, and the compromise kept the arts centre (Wikipedia).
- Programming has included Robert Lepage and Douglas Gordon; Tate announced in January 2013 that Tramway would host the 2015 Turner Prize (Wikipedia). Glasgow Life's 2025/26 arts and music review says Tramway worked with more than 60 creative companies, programme partners and artists and staged solo exhibitions by Rae-Yen Song and Solange Pessoa.

FUNDING
Tramway: mainly public: Glasgow City Council through Glasgow Life, plus Creative Scotland
- Creative Scotland: Tramway is a Creative Scotland Regularly Funded Organisation (Tramway website). On 31 January 2025 Glasgow Life announced that all seven of its applications to Creative Scotland's Multi-Year Funding 2025 to 2028 had succeeded, with £3.8 million over three years shared among Tramway, Celtic Connections, Glasgow International, Aye Write, Merchant City Festival, the Glasgow Mela and the Creative Communities programme.
- Glasgow City Council: Glasgow Life delivers culture and sport on its behalf (Tramway website), and the council appears in the 2025/26 "With thanks" list of Glasgow Life partners and funders (Glasgow Life annual review).
- National Lottery: in 1998 Tramway was awarded Scottish Arts Council National Lottery funding to upgrade its theatre and exhibition spaces (Tramway's History page).
- Donations to Glasgow Life support its work (Tramway website).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Tramway is public: Glasgow City Council through Glasgow Life, Creative Scotland multi-year funding for 2025 to 2028 and past National Lottery money. Creative Scotland's £3.8 million award was shared among seven Glasgow Life programmes, Tramway among them.
