MUSÉE BORIBANA: Dakar, Senegal
ID: musee-boribana

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary African art in Dakar, at the Rond-point Place ONU, Boulevard Dial Diop, corner Boulevard du Canal 4 (Senegalese culture ministry page).
- Founded by the Senegalese anthropologist Boubacar Koné and his wife, the actress CCH Pounder (DakArtNews, 6 July 2026). Sources give different founding years: 1990 (culture ministry), 1992 (DakArtNews) and 1998 (Enquête+, 2015).
- "Boribana" is Mandingo for "the race is over" (culture ministry page).
- Designed as a grand piano, with four exhibition rooms on two levels, artists' studios for residencies, storage and a documentation room (culture ministry page). Enquête+ gives the land title as 1,000 m². DakArtNews adds a library and an outdoor cinema among later additions.
- It opened with 34 works (DakArtNews, 6 July 2026); Enquête+ reports at least 400 artworks by artists of several nationalities in 2015.
- The collection holds sculpture, ceramics, paintings, tapestries, photographs and video (culture ministry page).
- Since 12 December 2014 it belongs to the Senegalese state. Enquête+ (25 March 2015) reports that Koné gave the museum to the state and also left the state the museum's land and artistic holdings. The same article announced that a conservator, Hawa Mara Ba, formerly a cultural adviser at the Ministry of Culture and Communication, would take up her post that week.

FUNDING
Musée Boribana: founders' private money until the December 2014 gift to the Senegalese state
- Private origin: French Wikipedia states it was founded with private funds. DakArtNews (Remy Mallet, "She Helped Build Senegal's First Private Museum of Contemporary Art. Today, CCH Pounder Collects 'for the Future'", 6 July 2026) says Koné wanted Dakar to see the museum was built by Senegalese people, with the phrase "we had built it ourselves", and says the couple could not afford the provenance research and authentication that older African works require, so they collected contemporary art.
- State since December 2014: Koné donated the museum to the Senegalese state on 12 December 2014; the culture ministry page says national authorities, including President Macky Sall, highly praised the gift.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum was the founders' own, from Koné and Pounder, until they gave it to the Senegalese state in December 2014; it now belongs to the state.
