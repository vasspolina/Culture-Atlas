CENTRE D'ART LA PANERA: Lleida, Spain
ID: centre-dart-la-panera-lleida
Website: https://www.lapanera.cat

OVERVIEW
- Public contemporary art centre at Plaça de la Panera 2, Lleida, in a medieval building; it describes itself as a platform for producing and spreading contemporary visual arts, with research, education and public programmes, and projects in schools, health centres and community services.
- The centre's own page says it was inaugurated in 2003, taking up the Biennal d'Art Leandre Cristòfol that the city of Lleida began in 1997, and that the centre's collection grew from the biennial. English Wikipedia gives 1997 as the year the centre opened, with the introduction of the biennial.
- Christian Alonso won the competition for its direction in July 2022 (Bonart) and has directed it since then (Wikipedia).
- Building: known as the Almodí, built between the 12th and 13th centuries as the city's trading hall for grain, oil and grapes; 21 medieval stone columns, each 5.70 metres high, survive. In 1606 the cathedral canons bought it and added a floor, giving it the name La Panera dels Canonges. After the 1835 Desamortització it passed to the Paeria (city council), which ceded it to the army; it became a cavalry barracks in 1860 and later a national police barracks until the late 1980s, when it returned to the Paeria and was chosen as the site for an art centre.
- Programme: exhibitions on two floors, a children's space (MiniPanera), a documentation centre with a fund of special editions and artists' books, a publishing programme and a typography residency, school and secondary-school artist residencies, and public programmes on art and technology, functional diversity and mental health.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 10:00 to 14:00 and 17:00 to 19:00, Sundays and public holidays 11:00 to 14:00, closed Mondays.

FUNDING
Centre d'Art La Panera: public, by the centre's own account: Ajuntament de Lleida and the Generalitat de Catalunya
- Stated by the centre: it is a public facility, the result of cooperation between the Ajuntament de Lleida and the Department of Culture of the Generalitat de Catalunya.
- It belongs to the Network of Territorial Visual Arts Centres of Catalonia, part of the Generalitat's public visual arts system, with ACVic, Bòlit (Girona), Centre d'Art Tarragona, Tecla Sala (L'Hospitalet), Fabra i Coats (Barcelona), Mataró Art Contemporani and Lo Pati (Amposta). The network's centres are municipally owned and promoted by the Generalitat.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre calls itself a public facility created through cooperation between the Ajuntament de Lleida and the Department of Culture of the Generalitat de Catalunya, within the Generalitat's network of territorial visual arts centres. The building returned to the Paeria (city council) in the late 1980s.
