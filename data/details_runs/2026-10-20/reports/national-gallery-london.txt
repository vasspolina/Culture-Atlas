NATIONAL GALLERY: London, UK
ID: national-gallery-london
Website: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National collection of European painting on the north side of Trafalgar Square, London.
- Founded in 1824, when the British government bought 38 paintings from the heirs of John Julius Angerstein for 57,000 pounds (Wikipedia).
- The Trafalgar Square building is by William Wilkins, built from 1832 and opened in 1838. The Sainsbury Wing (1991), by Robert Venturi and Denise Scott Brown, was paid for by almost 50 million pounds from Lord Sainsbury and his brothers Simon and Tim (Wikipedia).
- An exempt charity and a non-departmental public body of the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (Wikipedia).
- Open daily 10:00 to 18:00, Friday until 21:00. Admission to the collection is free. The Sainsbury Wing has step-free access.

FUNDING
National Gallery: public body with heavy private support: government grant, individual donors, corporate partners and members
- Gallery Partners (corporate support page, checked 24 October 2026): Bloomberg Philanthropies, LG and Bank of America.
- Corporate Supporters include Iberdrola Scottish Power, BBVA and Rothschild & Co. Benefactor Members include HSBC, JP Morgan and The Citadel. No amounts are given.
- Project Domani, announced in September 2025: a 750 million pound programme with a new wing by Kengo Kuma. Of 375 million pounds secured, Crankstart (the foundation of Sir Michael Moritz and Harriet Heyman) gave 150 million, the Julia Rausing Trust 150 million, and 75 million came from Chair of Trustees John Booth, the National Gallery Trust and anonymous donors (Gallery).
- A 50 million pound endowment from Sir Paul Getty in 1985 funded purchases (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the annual grant-in-aid figure, or of a campaign against Citadel or Bloomberg at the Gallery.

Red flags: Shell sponsorship (ended January 2018)
- Shell was a Gallery partner for 12 years and sponsored "Rembrandt: The Late Works" in 2014-15 (ArtReview, Culture Unstained).
- Documented objections (Culture Unstained, an advocacy source): a Greenpeace UK banner on the portico in 2012 during a Shell reception; a theatre group at the Rembrandt launch in 2014; the choir Shell Out Sounds outside in December 2014; a fake Shell presentation by Reclaim the Power in 2015.
- Freedom of Information papers showed the partnership ended in January 2018; Shell did not renew. ArtReview, citing The Guardian, reported it was worth about 20,000 to 35,000 pounds. Shell said it would focus on inspiring the next generation of engineers.

Red flags: Finmeccanica (arms) sponsorship ended 2012
- The Italian arms maker Finmeccanica was a corporate benefactor from 2006 and paid 30,000 pounds a year to use the Gallery's rooms for corporate entertaining; the deal was due to run to 1 October 2013 (Campaign Against Arms Trade, 30 March and 10 October 2012, an advocacy source).
- The Gallery hosted the DSEI arms fair reception in September 2011 and Farnborough International receptions from 2006 to 2010 (CAAT).
- Documented objection: CAAT and Stop the Arms Fair ran the "Disarm the National Gallery" campaign, with an action on 31 March 2012 and letters to the director (CAAT).
- CAAT announced on 10 October 2012 that the deal ended a year early. The Gallery said Finmeccanica "exercised their right to terminate".

Red flags: HSBC, a Benefactor Member
- HSBC is listed as a Benefactor Member on the Gallery's corporate support page (checked 24 October 2026); no start date or amount is given.
- On 11 December 2012 HSBC Holdings and HSBC Bank USA admitted anti-money laundering and sanctions violations and agreed to forfeit 1.256 billion dollars under a deferred prosecution agreement with the US Department of Justice (US Department of Justice).
- No reporting found of a campaign, petition or open letter against HSBC's membership at the Gallery.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little critical commentary on the funders in the art press; the Shell and arms objections come from campaign groups, with ArtReview reporting the outcome.
1. Apollo: Michael Prodger, "A new dawn for the National Gallery?" (2025)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/national-gallery-collection-after-1900-contemporary-art-tate-dispute/
- Welcomes Project Domani but says the 375 million pounds "will only go so far" given design and construction costs.
- Says the Gallery and Tate will court the same donors. Does not criticise any named funder.
2. ArtReview: staff, "News 19 October 2018: Shell's partnership with London's National Gallery ends" (2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-19-october-2018-shells-partnership-with-londons-national-gallery-ends
- A news report, not a critique: Shell's sponsorship ended, and BP still partnered with the British Museum and the National Portrait Gallery.
3. openDemocracy: Chris Garrard, "Unexpected partnerships in protest at National Gallery" (2014)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/unexpected-partnerships-in-protest-at-national-gallery/
- Calls Shell "an extremely unethical choice of sponsor" and links oil sponsorship to the Gallery's plan to privatise up to two-thirds of its workforce (PCS union's Clara Paillard: two sides of the same coin).
- Names Shell and Finmeccanica. Garrard belongs to Art Not Oil; openDemocracy is not an art-press outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- ArtReview notes campaigners had also protested BP's partnership with Tate, which ended in 2016.
- The Gallery held oil and arms sponsors in the same years as these institutions.

BOTTOM LINE
The Gallery's operating money is public and its large current gifts come from named philanthropic foundations. The weak points are the Shell and Finmeccanica deals, which ended after campaigns, and HSBC's 2012 deferred prosecution agreement while the bank is a current member.
