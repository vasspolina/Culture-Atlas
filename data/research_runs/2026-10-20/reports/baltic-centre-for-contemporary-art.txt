BALTIC CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART – Gateshead, UK
Website: https://baltic.art (loads normally, checked 2026-10-20; the support and corporate-supporters pages could not be fetched this run)

OVERVIEW
- A contemporary art centre in a converted 1950 Rank flour mill on the south bank of the Tyne, redesigned by Ellis Williams Architects. It opened on 13 July 2002 (Wikipedia).
- It is run by the charity Baltic Flour Mills Visual Arts Trust (no. 1076251, registered 1999). Sarah Munro has been director since November 2015, and Kirsty Lang chairs a board of 16 trustees, two of them councillors (Charity Commission).
- Entry is free. The ten millionth visitor came on 9 April 2025 (Baltic).

FUNDING
BALTIC: mainly public, through Arts Council England, central government and Gateshead Council, with growing private fundraising.
- Capital: £50M in total, including £33.4M from the Arts Council Lottery Fund. Gateshead Council bought the silo (Wikipedia).
- Year to 31 March 2024: income was £6.28M, of which £3.21M came from two government grants. Donations and legacies were £3.75M and trading £2.07M. It has 78 staff (Charity Commission).
- Apollo (2015) called Baltic "the most generously funded visual arts organisation outside of London".
- April 2026: £3,649,800 from the government's Arts Everywhere Creative Foundations Fund for building maintenance (Artlyst; Baltic).
- June 2025: Baltic launched a £10M endowment with a major donation from Sting (amount not disclosed). Damien Hirst, Anish Kapoor and Cornelia Parker gave works for an October 2025 gala auction (Baltic).
- Corporate supporters: the existing record lists Tommee Tippee and NorthStandard. These could not be checked this run because the corporate page was blocked, and no other reporting was found.

Red flags: Great Exhibition of the North arms sponsor (2018)
- On 1 March 2018 BAE Systems was announced as a premier partner of the Great Exhibition of the North, where Baltic was one of three hub venues. BAE withdrew on 7 March after the artist-led Art Not Arms campaign (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- In a joint statement with Sage Gateshead, Baltic said: "We are not in receipt of funds from BAE Systems" (Museums Association, 6 March 2018).
- No reporting found of a Nissan sponsorship.

BOTTOM LINE
A publicly funded charity whose money comes mostly from Arts Council England, central government and local government, plus a new endowment campaign. No reporting found of controversial funders of its own. Baltic said it took no BAE money in 2018. The corporate supporters list is worth re-checking.
