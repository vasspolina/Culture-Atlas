FRANCO-NAMIBIAN CULTURAL CENTRE (FNCC): Windhoek, Namibia
ID: franco-namibian-cultural-centre-fncc
Website: https://www.fncc.org.na (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- French-Namibian cultural centre at 118 Robert Mugabe Avenue, in Windhoek's city centre, with a gallery, cinema theatre, classrooms and a restaurant.
- The centre's about page says it was inaugurated on 30 January 1991; a memorial page for Sam Nujoma on the same site says Nujoma signed the creation of the centre on 31 July 1991.
- Presidents Sam Nujoma and Jacques Chirac laid the foundation stone of the current building on 25 June 1998; Nujoma attended its official opening on 1 December 2000 (FNCC memorial page).
- Building: seven classrooms, a large multipurpose gallery, a cinema theatre, a conference room and a house dating from 1908, classified as a historical monument, now a restaurant (stated by the centre). No architect is named on the pages read.
- Programme: French language courses, lectures, film screenings, theatre, concerts, exhibitions, and support for Namibian artists and cultural projects with equipment and expertise.
- Opening hours listed: Monday to Thursday 8:00 to 18:00, Friday 8:00 to 13:00. No admission prices or accessibility details are listed.

FUNDING
Franco-Namibian Cultural Centre: state-funded by France: a binational body under Namibian law paid for by the French government
- Per its about page, the centre has a "Bi-national structure, ruled under Namibian legislation and funded by the French government" (stated by the centre).
- Namibia gave the land and France funded the construction of the 1998 to 2000 building (stated by the centre).
- Events are co-presented with the French Embassy in Namibia (the MOVEMENT dance festival and La Belle et la Bête), with Ombetja Yehinga Organisation, Trilast Entertainment and IFcinéma also named as partners.
- Ambassador Cécile Vigneau thanked the centre's team and board at the July 2026 Bastille Day event (The Namibian). The centre's team page names Martin Beyer as director but no board.
- No reporting found of an annual budget, grant amounts, accounts or a list of board members.
- No reporting found of a corporate funder, a Gulf state body or any other named donor.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is French state money, which is an ordinary public funder for a cultural centre, and no corporate or flagged funder was found. The weak points are transparency: the centre publishes no accounts, budget, funder list or board, and the founding date differs between two pages of its own site. Worth watching are the centre's budget and board, neither of which is public.
