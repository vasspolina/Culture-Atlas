OAKLAND MUSEUM OF CALIFORNIA: Oakland, USA
ID: oakland-museum-of-california-oakland
Website: https://museumca.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of California art, history and natural sciences at 1000 Oak Street, Oakland, on a campus beside Lake Merritt (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1969 by merging the Oakland Public Museum, the Oakland Art Gallery and the Snow Museum of Natural History; Oakland voters had approved a $6.6 million bond in 1961 to start the campus (Wikipedia).
- Building: a concrete mid-century modernist complex by Kevin Roche John Dinkeloo and Associates, with landscape design by Dan Kiley; a renovation in 2009 to 2013 was led by Mark Cavagnero Associates (Wikipedia).
- On site: three tiers of art, history and natural science galleries, temporary exhibition galleries, an auditorium, a restaurant and roof gardens; collections of over 70,000 California art objects, more than 1.8 million history items and over 100,000 natural science specimens (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00. Adults $25, seniors 65 and over $22, youth 13 to 17, students and educators $18, children 12 and under and members free; $1 for Museums for All (EBT) cardholders; $15 for visitors who self-identify as having a disability; free general admission on First Sundays (museum website).
- Accessibility: three entrance ramps and an elevator serving Levels 1 to 3, but not the roof gardens; service animals are allowed in public areas (museum website).

FUNDING
Oakland Museum of California: mixed private: donations, corporate supporters and earned income, after the city's operating subsidy ended
- Finances: for the fiscal year ending June 2025 it reported revenue of $17.4 million, expenses of $20.4 million, contributions of $13.7 million and total assets of $84.4 million (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- City of Oakland: since 1 July 2011 the nonprofit Oakland Museum of California Foundation has operated the museum; the city kept ownership of the building and collection. Oakland agreed to pay the foundation $5 million a year for nine years, with a final $3 million payment due in 2022 (Nonprofit Quarterly, 2011). No reporting found of a current city subsidy. A 2002 city bond, Measure G ($23.6 million), supported the renovation (Wikipedia).
- Corporate supporters, listed by tier with no years (museum website): Presenter, Kaiser Permanente. Sustainer, Bank of America and Pacific Gas and Electric Company. Benefactor, Bloomberg Philanthropies, Chevron, Dodge & Cox, Donahue Fitzgerald LLP, Levi Strauss & Co. and The PG&E Corporation Foundation. Director tier, EBMUD, Port of Oakland, Orrick, Morrison & Foerster Foundation and others.
- Chevron gave $1 million in 2006 to broaden the museum's learning programmes (Chevron press release of 6 February 2006).
- Bernard Osher Foundation: provides free admission for children (stated by the museum). Bank of America and the museum's Women's Board support the Friday Nights series (museum press release of 14 July 2026, in Spanish; the Women's Board name is my translation).
- Bloomberg Connects: the museum launched its first Bloomberg Connects digital guide in August 2026. The release names no funder for the guide.
- No reporting found of the size of any individual corporate gift beyond the 2006 Chevron gift.

Red flags: fossil fuels (Chevron)
- Chevron is a current Benefactor-level corporate supporter on the museum's supporters page.
- Chevron's earlier $1 million gift in 2006 is the only amount found.

Red flags: PG&E criminal plea
- PG&E is a current Sustainer, and The PG&E Corporation Foundation a current Benefactor, on the museum's supporters page.
- On 23 March 2020 PG&E announced it would plead guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter and one count of unlawfully starting a fire in the 2018 Camp Fire, with $4 million in fines (KTVU). The plea needed approval from the Butte County Superior Court.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bloomberg Philanthropies: in January 2025 protesters at London's Institute of Contemporary Arts demanded that the ICA "cut ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies," which sponsors the New Contemporaries exhibition, and said it is "directly implicated in facilitating settlement infrastructure in the West Bank." The ICA called the protest "misguided" and said much of the information was "factually untrue" (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 16 January 2025).
- Connection: Bloomberg Philanthropies is a Benefactor-level supporter of the Oakland Museum of California. No reporting found of a campaign aimed at the Oakland museum over it.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a privately run nonprofit with a long list of named corporate supporters, and the city subsidy has ended. The weak points are two current corporate names: Chevron, and PG&E after its 2020 guilty plea over the Camp Fire. Watch Bloomberg Philanthropies, which sponsors the museum and has been the target of a protest at another arts venue.
