DALLAS MUSEUM OF ART: Dallas, USA
ID: dallas-museum-of-art-dallas
Website: https://dma.org/

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1717 North Harwood Street in the Dallas Arts District (Annual Report FY2025; Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1903 as the Dallas Art Association, which first showed paintings in the Dallas Public Library; renamed the Dallas Museum of Fine Arts in 1932 and moved to Fair Park in 1936 (Wikipedia).
- Current building by Edward Larrabee Barnes, opened in January 1984, with 159,000 square feet of exhibition space. The $54 million facility was financed by a 1979 City bond election and private donations (Wikipedia).
- Collection of more than 24,000 objects from the third millennium BC to the present (Wikipedia), including the Reves Collection of French Impressionists (The Art Newspaper).
- A renovation led by Nieto Sobejano Arquitectos, estimated at $150 million to $175 million, was chosen in 2023 (Wikipedia). The annual report names Brian Ferriso as the next Eugene McDermott Director, starting 1 December 2025 (Annual Report FY2025).
- Flood: a rainstorm in August 2022 closed the Reves Collection galleries and the Center for Creative Connections; the City Council approved $6 million on 8 January 2025 from its 2022 Severe Weather and Flood Fund for repairs, expected to finish by January 2026 (The Art Newspaper, Torey Akers, 14 January 2025).
- Visiting: general admission is free (free since 2013 per Wikipedia); the museum served more than 400,000 guests through free general admission in FY2025, and held its first year of FREE First Sundays in January 2025 (Annual Report FY2025).

FUNDING
Dallas Museum of Art: mixed: City of Dallas, individual donors, foundations and corporate donors
- The museum says it is supported in part by members and donors, the Texas Commission on the Arts, and the citizens of Dallas through the City of Dallas Office of Arts and Culture (Annual Report FY2025). The City of Dallas is listed in the $20,000,000 and above lifetime giving tier.
- The City's bond package allocates $20 million for maintenance; the $6 million flood repair money comes from the City's 2022 Severe Weather and Flood Fund (The Art Newspaper, 14 January 2025).
- Foundations in the FY2025 report: The Meadows Foundation (lifetime giving $10,000,000 to $19,999,999), The Dallas Foundation, Lilly Endowment Inc., Stephen M. Seay Foundation and Harold Simmons Foundation. Restricted funds in FY2025: The Getty Foundation $100,000 to $249,999; CM Bass Foundation $50,000 to $99,999.
- Corporate support of $1,000 and up as of 30 June 2025 includes Hunt Consolidated, Inc., Kosmos Energy, Citi, Truist Bank, S&P Global Inc. and Fidelity Investments.
- Officers for 2024 to 2025: Jeffrey S. Ellerman, Chairman; Gowri N. Sharma, President (Annual Report FY2025).

Red flags: fossil fuels
- Kosmos Energy is a corporate donor in the FY2025 annual report, with cash support of $1,000 and up as of 30 June 2025. Wikipedia describes Kosmos Energy as an American upstream oil company founded and based in Dallas, with production operations offshore Ghana, Equatorial Guinea and in the Gulf of Mexico.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece, from 2011, reports a federal lawsuit over the museum's Reves collection.
1. The Art Newspaper: Charlotte Burns and Helen Stoilas, "Wendy Reves's son claims that his mother was taken advantage of" (31 March 2011)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2011/04/01/wendy-revess-son-claims-that-his-mother-was-taken-advantage-of
- Unverified: Arnold Leon Schroeder Jr., Wendy Reves's son from her first marriage and sole heir, claims in a federal lawsuit filed in February 2011 that the museum and five trustees conspired to defraud him of a $400m collection, used "Texas charm and flattery" on his mother to move it from France to Dallas, and flew it out on a private jet to avoid inheritance laws.
- The museum says the case is "without merit".

BOTTOM LINE
The Dallas Museum of Art is funded by the City of Dallas, individual donors, foundations such as Meadows and corporate donors, with free general admission. The documented flag is Kosmos Energy, an oil and gas producer, as a corporate donor in the FY2025 report.
