TEATRO ESPAÑOL: Madrid, Spain
ID: teatro-espanol
Website: https://www.teatroespanol.es (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public theatre owned by Madrid City Council, at Calle del Príncipe 25 on the Plaza de Santa Ana (Spanish Wikipedia; theatre website; El Español).
- Origins: a decree of Philip II in 1565 set up the Cofradía de la Sagrada Pasión in Madrid with a space for staging plays; the brotherhood bought the Calle del Príncipe site in 1582, opened its playhouse on 21 September 1583 and sold it to the City Council 32 years later (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Earlier names: the Corral del Príncipe became the Teatro del Príncipe with a new building of 1735 to 1745 by Juan Bautista Sachetti and Ventura Rodríguez, and took the name Teatro Español by royal decree in 1849 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Building: after fire in 1802 it reopened under works by Juan de Villanueva; Román Guerrero rebuilt it from 1887 to 1894, works paid for by Guerrero and his daughter, the actress María Guerrero; another fire on 19 October 1975 closed it until 16 April 1980. The hall seats 763, and the former café became a small stage in 2005 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Management: the State ran it from 1939; after a year of joint City Council and Ministry of Culture management it returned to the City Council alone on 16 October 1981 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Direction: in December 2023 the City Council's Culture, Tourism and Sport department, led by Marta Rivera de la Cruz, named the stage director Eduardo Vasco artistic director on a two-year contract extendable to four. The Teatro Español and the Matadero stages, run together until then, were separated (El Español, 12 December 2023).
- Programme: Madrid Destino's budget describes a repertory of classic and contemporary texts with in-house productions in the main hall, smaller work in the Sala Pequeña and a new space, the Salón de los Balcones (Madrid Destino, budget 2025). The current season includes Lorca's Yerma in the main hall from 30 October to 27 December 2026 and Romancero gitano in the Salón de los Balcones (theatre website).

FUNDING
Teatro Español: public: Madrid City Council through its company Madrid Destino
- Owner: Madrid City Council owns the theatre (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Operator: Madrid Destino Cultura Turismo y Negocio, S.A., the City Council's company, holds the copyright on the theatre's website and sells its tickets (theatre website).
- Running costs: Madrid Destino's 2025 budget plans a City Council operating subsidy of 98,992,097 euros, after an estimated 90,063,224 euros in 2024 that included unspent 2023 funds and EU money; the company's own turnover was estimated at 24,802,115 euros for 2024 (Madrid Destino, budget 2025). These are figures for the whole company, which also runs Nave 10 Matadero and other city venues, not for the Teatro Español alone.
- Building history: the 1887 to 1894 rebuilding was paid for by Román Guerrero and María Guerrero, who then ran the theatre under a City Council concession until 1909 (Spanish Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: the theatre runs on Madrid City Council money. No funder carries a flag.
