JEHANGIR ART GALLERY: Mumbai, India
ID: jehangir-art-gallery-mumbai
Website: https://jehangirartgallery.com/about (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition gallery at 161 Kala Ghoda, South Mumbai, behind the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya museum (Wikipedia, gallery site).
- Built on a gift from Sir Cowasji Jehangir, 2nd Baronet, in memory of his late son Jehangir; B. G. Kher, then Chief Minister of Bombay, inaugurated it on 21 January 1952 (gallery site).
- Wikipedia says Cowasji Jehangir founded it at the urging of K. K. Hebbar and Homi J. Bhabha and paid the entire construction cost.
- The gallery site credits the architect Durga Bajpai; Wikipedia's introduction credits G. M. Bhuta, tagged "citation needed", so the sources conflict.
- The space has an auditorium hall, four exhibition galleries, the Hirji Jehangir Gallery and a terrace gallery for photography and visual art (gallery site). The complex housed the Samovar cafe, and the antique dealer Natesans is a tenant (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: entry is free and hours of 11:00 to 19:00 are given by Incredible India and Outlook Traveller, not by the gallery's own page.

FUNDING
Jehangir Art Gallery: private trust and committee: the Jehangir family's founding gift, with no sponsors named
- Cowasji Jehangir's gift paid the construction cost; a Committee of Management runs the gallery (Wikipedia).
- The committee chairman is Adi H. Jehangir and the secretary is K. G. Menon; members include Dilip De, Shirin K. Bharucha, Yogesh Kamdar, Vishvanath D. Sabale, Sushma Gaikwad, Narendra D. Vichare, Prof. Anil Naik and Nitin Gadre, I.A.S. (gallery site).
- No reporting found of ongoing funders, sponsors, accounts or annual reports, and no reporting found of what artists pay to hire a gallery.

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, two works removed, April 2013
- Mumbai police and the gallery told artist Eleena Banik to take two goddess paintings out of public view after complaints; Varsha Thakar of the Hindu Janajagruti Samiti was among the complainants (AFP via The National).
- One painting showed Kali without her garland of skulls; the other showed a goddess-like woman with strawberries on her body, which Banik said protested the fatal gang rape of a student in New Delhi (AFP via The National).
- Banik said she received threats, including a death threat, and was given police protection. Gallery director K. G. Menon confirmed the request but gave no details (AFP via The National).

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, police visit and a covered sculpture, 2006 and 2007
- In 2006 the gallery asked a student to cover the sensitive parts of his nude sculpture (Subir Ghosh, blog).
- In December 2007 two policemen visited Jatin Das's show "Body & Line" and suggested a notice barring under-18s; Joint Police Commissioner K. L. Prasad said it was only a suggestion, and Das put up a "Not Under 18" sign (Subir Ghosh, blog).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The censorship cases above were reported by a news agency and a personal blog.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is a family founding gift run through a committee, with no sponsor, flagged or otherwise, on record. The weak points are opacity (no reports, accounts or booking terms) and a record of removing or covering work after complaints, in 2006 and 2013, with police involved in 2007 and 2013. Worth watching: who sits on the committee and how exhibition slots are allocated.
