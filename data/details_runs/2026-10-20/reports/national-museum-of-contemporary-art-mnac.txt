NATIONAL MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART (MNAC): Bucharest, Romania
ID: national-museum-of-contemporary-art-mnac
Website: https://www.mnac.ro (checked 2026-10-25; loads, but only the museum's name was readable; its About and Partners pages gave no text)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in a glass wing of the Palace of the Parliament, Calea 13 Septembrie 1-3, Bucharest (Wikipedia, e-flux).
- Opened in 2004. Augustin Ioan wrote in ARTMargins (2008) that Prime Minister Adrian Nastase financed the museum's move into the building "in deep secret".
- The Palace was built in the 1980s as the House of the Republic; its upper floors remain unfinished (ARTMargins).
- The Ministry of Culture ran an open competition for general director, which Călin Dan won (e-flux, 16 February 2015).
- Visiting: Wednesday to Sunday, 11:00 to 18:30 (2025 e-flux announcement). No admission prices or accessibility information found.

FUNDING
National Museum of Contemporary Art (MNAC): mainly public: the Romanian Ministry of Culture, with company sponsors
- On 24 April 2024 Culture Minister Raluca Turcan announced 1,500,000 lei for acquisitions, 500,000 lei each for MNAC, the Brukenthal National Museum in Sibiu and the Moldova National Museum Complex in Iași (Bursa.ro).
- Sponsors listed in October 2023: AQUA Carpatica, BRD Groupe Société Générale, Greentek and DARA Lighting, Crama Oprișor, LG Electronics România, Policolor România, Grup Transilvae, Molotow, ProfiArt, UniCredit Bank, and Vitamin Aqua and Pop Cola (e-flux).
- The 2025 Malevich show listed most of the same sponsors, BRD included, with "the support of" Dental Clinic Herăstrău (e-flux).
- No reporting found of annual accounts or a board.

Red flags: sponsor linked to the lender of disputed Malevich paintings, 2025
- Konstantin Akinsha wrote that the exhibition is sponsored by a dental clinic owned by the collector who holds the three paintings, and that their provenance is "incomplete and unverifiable" (e-flux Notes, 30 June 2025).
- In a July 2025 statement MNAC said it lacks the expertise to determine authenticity, rejected the conflict-of-interest charge and said the sponsorship paid for security. The lender, Yaniv Cohen, says he gave 24,000 euros for security (HVG, August 2025).
- No one has proven the works are forgeries; the doubts concern provenance (HVG). Cohen's lawyers demanded that e-flux remove Akinsha's article or face legal action (HVG).

Red flags: allegation against the director, 2017
- Unverified: the researcher Mihaela Varzari says in an open letter (22 October 2017, republished by the activist platform ArtLeaks on 13 December 2017) that director Călin Dan's museum cancelled her residency two weeks before it began, citing budget cuts, without compensation, and calls it an abuse of power. MNAC's unsigned reply of 23 October 2017 says talks on housing, transport and dates failed and budget cuts forced it to suspend the residency.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concentrates on the 2025 Malevich show and the 1980s-built site.
1. e-flux Notes: Konstantin Akinsha, "Do We Need the 'Alternative' Malevich?" (2025)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/notes/6783369/do-we-need-the-alternative-malevich
- Says MNAC's account of Malevich's life is inaccurate and the attribution rests on one Ukrainian art historian, Dmytro Horbachev.
- Says the works lack documentation from the artist's lifetime and consensus from internationally recognised experts, and names the sponsor conflict.
2. HVG: Emőd Péter, "Malevich in the crosshairs: forgery suspicion overshadows the Bucharest avant-garde exhibition" (2025; translated title)
URL: https://hvg.hu/360/20250828_A-mu-Kazimir-Malevics-Bukarest-avantgard-kiallitas-hamisitvanygyanu
- Reports Akinsha's doubts and MNAC's July statement, which differs from an earlier one calling the provenance and expert opinion a solid basis for display.
- Hungarian news weekly, not art press.
3. ARTMargins: Augustin Ioan, "Bucharest's National Museum of Contemporary Art in the Big House" (2008)
URL: https://artmargins.com/bucharests-national-museum-of-contemporary-art-in-the-big-house/
- Reports that the "national" label was questioned as contradictory to "contemporary", that the fenced, guarded site makes access difficult, and that provincial and experimental-zone artists objected to the purchase policy.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Société Générale S.A. entered a three-year deferred prosecution agreement on 5 June 2018 over bribery to win Libyan investments, with over $860 million in penalties to resolve bribery and interest-rate manipulation charges in the US and France (Paul Weiss). UniCredit Bank AG, a Munich-based UniCredit bank, agreed to plead guilty on 15 April 2019 to conspiring to violate US sanctions law, with about $1.3 billion in penalties across the group (Mondo Visione, reproducing the Justice Department release).
- The pages read do not show that the Romanian subsidiaries BRD or UniCredit Bank were party to these cases. BRD and UniCredit Bank appear on MNAC's 2023 sponsor list, and BRD on its 2025 list.

BOTTOM LINE
The core money is Romanian Ministry of Culture money with company sponsors and no fossil fuel, arms or tobacco funder named. The weak points are the 2025 Malevich loan sponsored by the lender's company, an unresolved provenance dispute and, per Augustin Ioan, the secret financing of the 2004 move into the building. Worth watching is the Malevich dispute.
