HANGAR.ORG: Barcelona, Spain
ID: hangar-barcelona
Website: https://hangar.org (checked 2026-10-24; the site refused automated access with HTTP 403, so its own pages were not opened)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for artistic production and research at c/ Emilia Coranty 16 (formerly Passatge Marquès de Sta. Isabel 40), in the Can Ricart complex in Poblenou, Barcelona (Barcelona City Council, Fàbriques de Creació).
- Founded in 1997 by the Associació d'Artistes Visuals de Catalunya (AAVC); it has had private foundation status since 2003 (Barcelona City Council).
- Building: an industrial warehouse in Can Ricart, a textile factory dating to 1853 by the architects Josep Oriol i Bernadet and Josep Fontserè i Mestres; the page credits Yaiza Alonso and Arantxa Manrique with the architecture project. It covers 2,600 square metres.
- Fillip (2008) describes Hangar as the only site still active at Can Ricart after more than thirty small industries were pushed out; 3Cat (2016) reports that the city ceded 7,000 square metres to the University of Barcelona, which is developing a humanities and social sciences park there.
- On site: material workshops (carpentry, bio-hacking), audiovisual and multimedia labs, a 150-square-metre film set, coworking space, a classroom and multi-purpose rooms (Barcelona City Council); 3Cat reports 19 artists' studios and an artist residence (2016).
- Visiting: Monday to Friday, 10:00 to 14:00 and 15:00 to 19:30. No admission prices or accessibility details found.

FUNDING
Hangar: public and private support, with no funder list published: city hall paid its early rent and renovated its buildings, and a bank foundation co-ran a 2020 grant
- Barcelona City Council rehabilitated the new halls Hangar uses at Can Ricart (3Cat, 2016); its factories programme page does not say how Hangar is funded.
- Hangar describes itself as "a private foundation established by an association of artists" (Barcelona tourism listing).
- Fundació Banc Sabadell and Hangar jointly offered a research grant for 2020: fees of 9,750 euros gross, a production grant of up to 4,000 euros gross, travel up to 250 euros, and accommodation and work spaces at Hangar, with the application deadline on 31 October 2019 (Barcelona City Council grants page). The page does not say which party paid which item.
- Jeffrey Swartz (Fillip, 2008) wrote that Hangar was originally funded entirely by Barcelona city hall, which also paid the rent on its building, and that its association had since secured broader funding.
- No reporting found on the pages opened of a current Generalitat de Catalunya or European grant, of annual amounts, of accounts or of a board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of Hangar found in art media. One art essay uses Hangar as a case study in a city conflict.
1. Fillip: Jeffrey Swartz, "Space-Run Artists: Art Activism and Urban Conflict in Contemporary Barcelona" (2008)
URL: https://fillip.ca/content/art-activism-and-urban-conflict-in-contemporary-barcelona
- Describes Hangar as the only site still active at Can Ricart after more than thirty small industries and some artist and design groups were pushed out; the city agreed to save the factory from demolition.
- Links the "soft" political stance of the association that runs Hangar to its financial dependence on city hall, and says it became more outspoken about the shortage of studio space once it had secured broader funding.
- Criticises the city's contradictory policy, not Hangar.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Hangar is a mix of city support and a bank-foundation grant in 2020; no flagged funder was found. The weak points are that the pages opened give no current funder amounts or accounts and that its own site could not be read here. Worth watching is its relationship with the city council, which paid its early rent and renovated its buildings.
