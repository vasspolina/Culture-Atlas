SOLOMON R. GUGGENHEIM MUSEUM: New York, USA
ID: solomon-r-guggenheim-museum
Website: https://www.guggenheim.org (checked 2026-10-24; live, but the pages opened showed only a cookie notice, so no site text was read)

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum on the Upper East Side of Manhattan, operated and owned by the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation (Wikipedia).
- Solomon R. Guggenheim began collecting in the 1890s; the Foundation was set up in 1937 and its first venue, the Museum of Non-Objective Painting, opened in 1939 under Hilla Rebay. The museum took Guggenheim's name in 1952 (Wikipedia).
- Rebay and Guggenheim commissioned Frank Lloyd Wright in 1943; the building opened in 1959, six months after Wright died (Wikipedia).
- About 861,000 people visited in 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Mariët Westermann became director in June 2024; Wikipedia names Melissa Chiu as director from September 2026. J. Tomilson Hill has chaired the board since 2021 (Wikipedia; Form 990).
- Visiting: No reporting found of current hours or ticket prices in the pages opened.

FUNDING
Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation: private non-profit: gifts and earned income
- Fiscal 2024 (Form 990 data on ProPublica): revenue $88.2 million, of which contributions $40.1 million and program service revenue $34.5 million; expenses $84.9 million; net assets $122.8 million.
- Sackler family: the Mortimer D. Sackler family gave $7 million between 1995 and 2006 to create the arts education center and $2 million more between 1999 and 2015 for general support (The Art Newspaper).
- Abu Dhabi: the Foundation is a partner of the Department of Culture and Tourism Abu Dhabi in Guggenheim Abu Dhabi (see Red flags). No reporting found of what the Abu Dhabi side pays the Foundation.
- No reporting found of a current corporate partner list in the pages opened.

Red flags: Opioids (Sackler money)
- On 25 March 2019 the museum said it had received no Sackler contributions since 2015 and would not accept gifts from the Mortimer D. Sackler branch of the family, named in lawsuits over OxyContin marketing (The Art Newspaper, Victoria Stapley-Brown).
- On 9 February 2019 Nan Goldin and P.A.I.N. protested at the museum, calling for it to stop taking Sackler money (Wikipedia, P.A.I.N.).
- In 2022 the Sackler Center for Arts Education was renamed the Gail May Engelberg Center for Arts Education for trustee Gail May Engelberg, who with her husband gave $15 million (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Gulf state partner (Guggenheim Abu Dhabi)
- The Department of Culture and Tourism Abu Dhabi announced on 28 July 2026 that Guggenheim Abu Dhabi, designed by Frank Gehry, opens on 11 December 2026; it calls the museum a collaboration with the Guggenheim Foundation (DCT Abu Dhabi release).
- The project, announced in 2006, sits on Saadiyat Island under the government-owned Tourism Development & Investment Company (The Art Newspaper, 2019).
- Protest: in April 2019 the Gulf Labor Coalition asked artists to withhold participation, saying it represents several thousand signatories; it says the museum is "built on the back of exploited, indebted, and abused workers" (Hyperallergic, 2019, quoting the Coalition). The Foundation ended negotiations with the Coalition in April 2016 and has said it made measurable progress on workers' welfare with its UAE partner (Hyperallergic, 2016).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism over two issues, Gulf Labor and the Abu Dhabi branch, and staff layoffs; the Sackler episode was reported mainly as news.
1. Hyperallergic: Carey Dunne, "Protesters Shame Guggenheim and Its Trustees with Light Projections" (2016)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/protesters-shame-guggenheim-and-its-trustees-with-light-projections/
- Artist groups G.U.L.F. and the Illuminator projected "YOU BROKE TRUST" and trustees' faces on the facade after the board ended talks with Gulf Labor.
- Names board chairman William L. Mack; the Guggenheim said the demands were beyond its direct line of influence.
2. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Gulf Labor Coalition Calls on Artists to Boycott Guggenheim Abu Dhabi" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/gulf-labor-coalition-calls-on-artists-to-boycott-guggenheim-abu-dhabi
- The Coalition asks for fair pay, no recruitment-fee debt, decent housing and an independent monitor; it says only the monitor request was met, not satisfactorily.
3. The Art Newspaper: Victoria Stapley-Brown, "Guggenheim Museum says it does not plan to accept any gifts from the Sackler family" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/03/25/guggenheim-museum-says-it-does-not-plan-to-accept-any-gifts-from-the-sackler-family
- News report of the decision and the family's earlier gifts, not a critique.
4. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Workers Protest Abrupt Layoffs at Guggenheim Museum" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1004134/workers-protest-abrupt-layoffs-at-guggenheim-museum/
- More than 50 workers rallied on 16 April 2025 over the end of 20 staff positions, the third round of layoffs in five years; 14 of those laid off belonged to UAW Local 2110.
- The museum said its "financial picture required us to make the difficult decision to reduce staffing". Concerns the institution's labour practice, not its funders.

WIDER CONTEXT
- US diplomatic cables published by WikiLeaks mention the Guggenheim in passing: a July 2009 cable from Abu Dhabi notes the UAE government rejected Human Rights Watch's allegations of labour abuses at Saadiyat Island, "future home of the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi"; a January 2010 cable from Baku says the Pashayev family were associated with an effort to open a Guggenheim branch on Baku's waterfront. The cables mention no payments.

BOTTOM LINE
The New York museum's money is mostly gifts and earned income, with filings public. The weak points are the Sackler gifts it later refused and renamed, and its partnership with the Abu Dhabi government on a museum built amid migrant-labour protests that opens in December 2026. Watch the terms of the Abu Dhabi deal and the Gulf Labor response at the opening.
