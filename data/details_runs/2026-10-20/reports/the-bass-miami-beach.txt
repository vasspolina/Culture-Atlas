THE BASS: Miami Beach, USA
ID: the-bass-miami-beach
Website: https://thebass.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 2100 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, in a 1930s Art Deco building listed on the National Register in 1978 (stated by the museum).
- Opened on 7 April 1964 in the former Miami Beach Public Library, after John Bass and Johanna Redlich bequeathed more than 500 works to the City of Miami Beach in 1963, on condition that a museum stay open to the public (Wikipedia).
- Named for John and Johanna Bass; formerly the Bass Museum of Art, it dropped "Museum of Art" in the 2017 rebrand (Wikipedia).
- Arata Isozaki designed a wing added in 2001 to 2002; the $12 million expansion that reopened on 29 October 2017 was designed by David Gauld with Isozaki as consultant, adding four galleries, a store, a cafe and an education space (Wikipedia, the museum).
- The 2017 reopening added four galleries, a museum store and cafe, and an education facility; the museum describes itself as Miami Beach's contemporary art museum.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 5pm. Admission $15; $8 for seniors 65+, college students and youth 7 to 18; free for children 6 and under, members and military, on the third Thursday evening, and on the last Sunday of each month. Miami Beach residents and city employees enter free with ID. Accessibility details are on a separate page not read.

FUNDING
The Bass: mainly public plus private donors: City of Miami Beach, county and state, with a board-led donor base
- The donor page for 2025 to 2026 lists the City of Miami Beach at $20,000,000+. Wikipedia reports a $7.5M city grant in 2013 and a 2022 voter-approved city bond of $20.1M for a new wing.
- Other public funders listed: Miami-Dade County Department of Cultural Affairs ($500,000 to $999,999), State of Florida Division of Cultural Affairs and Miami Beach Cultural Arts Council ($100,000 to $499,999 each), Bal Harbour Village and Town of Surfside ($5,000 to $9,999 each).
- Private tiers: $1M to $4.99M: Ambassador Paul and Trudy Cejas, the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation, Diane and Alan Liebermann, the George Lindemann Family. $500,000 to $999,999: Tatyana and Riccardo Silva. $100,000 to $499,999: Art Bridges Foundation, The Henri and Flore Lesieur Foundation, the Paresky and Gould Families.
- Corporate names on the donor page include Citadel ($20,000 to $49,999 tier), Morgan Stanley, Raymond James, J.P. Morgan Private Bank, BNP Paribas, Bank of America Charitable Foundation, Bulgari, Givenchy, Tory Burch, Walgreens, Gagosian, Sotheby's, Phillips and Art Basel Miami Beach.
- Ken Griffin and Bloomberg are not on the donor list. No reporting found of a campaign, protest or open letter aimed at Citadel at The Bass.
- George Lindemann chairs the board, per the museum.
- Wikipedia gives an annual operating budget of about $4.5M.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution. Hyperallergic coverage is mostly admiring or aimed at exhibitions, and no piece makes a case against the museum or its funders.
1. Hyperallergic: Benjamin Sutton, "Hardware and Soft Concepts at the Bass Museum" (2014)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/hardware-and-soft-concepts-at-the-bass-museum/
- Calls the "GOLD" group show conceptually thin, and the Peter Marino show self-promotional: a walk-through portfolio of Marino's luxury interiors, with a wax figure of him.
- Sarcastically hopes Marino gave the museum "bucket-loads of gold" in exchange for the galleries.
- Mostly criticises the art and curation; the institution is criticised only in passing.
2. Hyperallergic: Nathaniel Sandler, "Miami Beach's Bass Museum Cranks It Up After Renovation" (2017)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/miami-beachs-bass-museum-cranks-it-up-after-renovation/
- Not criticism: presents the renovation as a success built around visitor experience. Contains no criticism of the museum, its funding or the city.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Early years: after published reports questioned the authenticity of many bequeathed paintings, citizens asked for an independent appraisal and the Miami Beach City Council closed the museum temporarily in 1973; it reopened in January 1980 (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind The Bass is mostly public: the City of Miami Beach, Miami-Dade County and the State of Florida, with private donors above them. The one flagged name is Citadel, a mid-tier corporate donor, with no documented objection aimed at the museum. Watch the new-wing bond spending and the next donor list.
