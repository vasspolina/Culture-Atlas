NASJONALMUSEET (NATIONAL MUSEUM OF NORWAY): Oslo, Norway
ID: nasjonalmuseet-national-museum-of-norway
Website: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Norway's museum of art, architecture and design, at Brynjulf Bulls plass 3 on the Vestbanen site in central Oslo (museum site).
- Created on 28 April 2003 by merging the National Gallery, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Museum of Architecture and two design museums; it is state-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia). It has 449 employees (Brønnøysund Register Centre).
- The new building by Kleihues + Schuwerk, winners of the 2010 competition, opened in June 2022; the Storting set a cost frame of NOK 5.327 billion on 6 June 2013 (Wikipedia).
- It holds over 400,000 works, including the first version of Edvard Munch's "The Scream" (1893), and also runs the architecture museum at Bankplassen 3 and Villa Stenersen (Wikipedia; annual report 2024).
- Visitors: 946,000 in 2024; 46 percent lived outside Norway (annual report 2024).
- Director Ingrid Røynesdal, appointed in 2023 after Karin Hindsbo (2017 to 2023); board chair Maria Moræus Hanssen (Wikipedia; annual report 2024).

FUNDING
Nasjonalmuseet: mainly public: a state foundation funded by the Ministry of Culture, with private partners led by the Fredriksen family
- Operating income was NOK 1,058.9 million in 2024, including own income of NOK 110.5 million and gifts of NOK 12.5 million (annual report 2024), and NOK 1,084.7 million in 2025, with an operating loss of NOK 34.9 million (Brønnøysund accounts register).
- Government grants covered 90 percent of the 2022 operating budget (Kunstkritikk, 2022).
- Fredriksen Family Art Company: lends the Fredriksen Family Collection, shown in the Søylerommet, under an agreement that runs initially for ten years, and funds four doctoral fellowships from 2023 to 2027 (museum site; annual report 2024). Cecilie and Kathrine Fredriksen, daughters of John Fredriksen, have loaned more than 100 works and funded acquisitions, exhibitions and research (Apollo, 2025). Kunstkritikk put the 2022 contribution at up to NOK 20 million. Dagbladet reported in 2019 that the first agreement was signed in June 2017.
- Sparebankstiftelsen DNB: long-term art loans, exhibitions and NOK 6 million over three years for buying Norwegian contemporary art (annual report 2024); NOK 12 million in 2022 (Kunstkritikk).
- AKO Foundation: a contributor since 2022, paying in 2024 towards the Anna-Eva Bergman and Else Hagen exhibitions (annual report 2024).
- Stiftelsen Scheibler: NOK 1 million a year from 2024 for design, crafts and fashion; Lise M. Stolt-Nielsen and her children: a multi-year performance programme agreed in summer 2024 (annual report 2024). The museum also lists Viking as a partner.

Red flags: 2022 artists' appeal against the Fredriksen agreement
- 12 March 2022: Jérémie McGowan, former director of Nordnorsk Kunstmuseum, wrote in Klassekampen that Seadrill, Fredriksen's drilling company, signed a major agreement with Rosneft just before Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, and called the museum "naïve or complicit" (Kunstkritikk).
- Kunstkritikk added that Putin gave John Fredriksen Russia's Order of Friendship in 2015, that Fredriksen was fined for oil fraud in the United States in 2014, and that the art company is registered in Cyprus.
- The museum's communications director Eirik Kydland replied that no Fredriksen company had been hit by sanctions over the war in Ukraine and that the funds come from legitimate business; Hindsbo did not respond (Kunstkritikk).
- 10 June 2022: artists Nikhil Vettukattil, Knut Åsdam, Kajsa Dahlberg and Sille Storihle, then showing at the museum, issued the appeal "A Gift we can Afford to Refuse", calling the deal artwashing and asking for it to be ended; it had well over 100 signatures (Kunstkritikk).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Kunstkritikk has covered the Fredriksen agreement critically since 2022, and the museum's director has answered in its pages.
1. Kunstkritikk: Live Drønen, "The National Museum of Norway Linked to Russia" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/the-national-museum-of-norway-linked-to-russia/
- Argues the invasion of Ukraine renewed the case against the deal; Ruben Steinum of the Association of Norwegian Visual Artists asked how the museum assesses partners whose wealth was partly made in areas of conflict and war.
2. Kunstkritikk: Tiril Flom, "Artists Fight Sponsorship Agreement" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/artists-fight-the-fredriksen-collaboration
- Dahlberg: the deal gives too much influence to one powerful family; Åsdam: it "kills the professional integrity of a national public institution".
3. Kunstkritikk: Live Drønen, "Money Problems" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/money-problems
- Hindsbo answered that if Norwegian museums could not take fossil fuel money they would also have to refuse state funds, since Norway earns heavily from oil and gas.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2019 law professor Jan Frithjof Bernt criticised the museum and the ministry in Klassekampen for keeping the 2017 agreement from the public until a June 2019 addendum (Dagbladet, 26 August 2019).
- The secrecy concerned the same Fredriksen agreement.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money carries the museum; the private share is small but rests on shipping and drilling fortunes, and the Fredriksen agreement is the one under sustained protest. Watch whether it is renewed when the first ten-year term ends.
