ISTANBUL ARCHAEOLOGY MUSEUMS: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: istanbul-archaeology-museums
Website: https://muze.gov.tr/muze-detay?SectionId=IAR01&DistId=IAR (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Complex of three museums in one garden on the Osman Hamdi Bey slope between Gülhane Park and Topkapı Palace, Sultanahmet, Fatih: the Archaeological Museum, the Museum of the Ancient Orient and the Tiled Kiosk (Turkish Museums, Ministry of Culture and Tourism; muze.gen.tr).
- Founded in 1869 as the Müze-i Hümayun (Imperial Museum) from antiquities gathered in Hagia Irene; Osman Hamdi Bey became director in 1881 and brought the Sidon royal necropolis sarcophagi to Istanbul after his 1887 to 1888 excavations (Turkish Museums).
- Buildings: the Tiled Kiosk of 1472, built under Mehmed II and a museum since 1880; Alexandre Vallaury's 1883 Academy of Fine Arts building, the Museum of the Ancient Orient since 1917; and Vallaury's neo-classical Archaeological Museum, opened in 1891 (Turkish Museums).
- Collection: nearly one million objects, including the Alexander Sarcophagus, the Sarcophagus of the Weeping Women and the Tabnit and Lycian sarcophagi (Turkish Museums; Hürriyet Daily News, 2019).
- Visitors: 530,065 in 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Current show: "No Escape: Boubon", bronzes looted from Boubon in Burdur in the 1960s, opened by Culture Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy on 2 November 2026 alongside 100 objects returned from the United States (Hürriyet Daily News, 3 November 2026).
- Visiting: 9am to 6:30pm, box office closes 5:30pm (Turkish Museums). The main entrance moved to a new visitor centre on Gülhane Square in 2026 (Hürriyet Daily News, 19 September 2026).

FUNDING
Istanbul Archaeology Museums: state: Turkey's Ministry of Culture and Tourism
- Operator: a directorate of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, which runs it as a state museum (Turkish Museums).
- Restoration, phase one: work on the 1891 building and its south wing began in 2011; Deputy Minister Nadir Alpaslan reopened halls 8 to 15, with the Sidon sarcophagi, on 27 September 2019 after nearly eight years of reinforcement and restoration (Hürriyet Daily News, 2019; DHA, 2026).
- Phase two: reinforcement and display projects from 2017; the whole Classical building reopened in 2021 (Ersoy, quoted by DHA and Hürriyet Daily News, September 2026).
- Phase three: from January 2023 the Museum of the Ancient Orient and the Tiled Kiosk were reinforced against earthquakes and redisplayed; the Ancient Orient museum, closed since 2022, reopened on 6 July 2026, and about 54,000 exterior tiles of the Tiled Kiosk were restored one by one (Hürriyet Daily News; Aydınlık, August 2026).
- Income: admission fees at 15 euros for foreign visitors (muze.gen.tr).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Turkish state money only. Worth watching is a public account of what the restoration cost.
