ART-BELARUS GALLERY (BELGAZPROMBANK CORPORATE COLLECTION): Minsk, Belarus
ID: art-belarus-gallery-belgazprombank-corporate-collection-minsk
Website: https://artbelarus.by/ru/about.html (checked 2026-10-20; loaded normally)

OVERVIEW
- Gallery in the Palace of Arts at Kozlova Street 3, Minsk, entered from the left end of the building, showing Belgazprombank's corporate collection (stated by the space).
- The bank began the collection in 2011 and 2012 by buying works by Belarus-born artists of the Paris School, among them Marc Chagall and Chaim Soutine; the first major show opened in Minsk on 21 September 2012 (stated by the space).
- Run as the "Art-Belarus" project, a public-private partnership under a 2015 agreement with the Ministry of Culture, working with the National Art Museum, the National Commission for UNESCO and the Foreign Ministry (stated by the space).
- The collection holds over 100 works from the early 16th century on; 35 are on the State Registry of Historical and Cultural Values (stated by the space; eclat.org).
- The project claims more than 20 exhibitions in eight cities (Minsk, Gomel, Vitebsk, Brest, Grodno, Riga, Vilnius, Paris) and more than 300,000 visitors.
- Tickets cost 10 and 5 Belarusian rubles. No reporting found of opening hours or accessibility on the pages read.

FUNDING
Art-Belarus Gallery: corporate: Belgazprombank, owned by Gazprom and Gazprombank
- The bank buys the works and runs the gallery and its shows (stated by the space).
- Gazprom and Gazprombank each hold 49.8% of Belgazprombank (Wikipedia).
- Viktar Babaryka chaired the bank's board from July 2000 until 12 May 2020 and helped return works by Chagall, Soutine and Léon Bakst to Belarus (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of acquisition budgets or gallery running costs.

Red flags: owned by Gazprom and Gazprombank
- Belgazprombank's two near-equal shareholders are Russia's Gazprom and Gazprombank (Wikipedia); the gallery depends entirely on the bank.
- In 2023 Canada and Ukraine added Belgazprombank to their sanctions lists (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2020 state seizure of the collection and the Babaryka case
- Babaryka resigned to run for president and was detained in June 2020; the arrest was "believed to be politically motivated" (Wikipedia). He was sentenced to 14 years on 6 July 2021 and released and deported in December 2025.
- The National Bank put Belgazprombank under temporary administration; Gazprom and Gazprombank refused to cooperate with it (Wikipedia).
- On 13 June 2020 the collection was confiscated as evidence; from 16 June the gallery showed QR codes in place of the works (eclat.org).
- Belarus state TV reported that 150 paintings worth $20 million were added to the case; curator Alexandr Zimenko was barred by court order from saying where they were (The Art Newspaper).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is wholly paid for by a bank owned by Gazprom and Gazprombank, so its money comes from Gazprom and Gazprombank. The weak points are that ownership, the 2023 sanctions on the bank, and the 2020 state seizure of the collection in the case against its former chairman, who had stood against Lukashenko. Whether all seized works are back in the bank's hands is not documented on the pages read.
