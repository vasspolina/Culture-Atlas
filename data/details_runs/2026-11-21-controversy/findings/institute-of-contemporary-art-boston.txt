ID: institute-of-contemporary-art-boston

WIDER CONTEXT
- July 2017: eight Boston artists and activists distributed an open letter calling on the ICA to "please pull the show", its Dana Schutz exhibition, the day before it opened on 26 July; they objected to her painting "Open Casket", based on a 1955 photograph of Emmett Till's body (WBUR, 10 August 2017).
- The painting was not in the Boston show. Chief curator Eva Respini said: "The 'Open Casket' painting was never in our show." The ICA kept the exhibition open, scheduled to run through 26 November 2017 (WBUR).
- 20 March 2021: tenants of Fineberg Companies, with Greater Boston Tenants Union demonstrators, protested outside the ICA. The Daily Free Press reported that Gerald Fineberg, the company's founder and owner, was a member of the ICA's Board of Trustees and a major donor, and an organiser said the protest was held there because Fineberg is a prominent patron and board member with a wall gallery in his name in the museum's lobby (Daily Free Press, 21 March 2021).
- The tenants' union wanted to meet Fineberg to negotiate lower rents, better living conditions and no evictions during the pandemic (Daily Free Press).
- Hyperallergic reported that the Fineberg Tenant Union gathered at the ICA with a pop-up exhibit "documenting squalid conditions and evictions at Fineberg properties" (Hyperallergic, Sam Lefebvre, 23 March 2021; paywalled, only excerpts were read).
