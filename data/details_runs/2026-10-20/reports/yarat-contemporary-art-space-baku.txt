YARAT CONTEMPORARY ART SPACE: Baku, Azerbaijan
ID: yarat-contemporary-art-space-baku
Website: https://yarat.az/ (checked 2026-10-24; working, but it lists no hours, admission, funders or board on the page opened)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in a former Soviet naval building on the Baku seafront by the Caspian Sea, about 1.5 miles along the waterfront (The Art Newspaper, 2015).
- Founded in 2011 by the artist Aida Mahmudova; the building opened in March 2015, replacing a temporary venue in an abandoned air-conditioning factory used for a 2013 show (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper).
- The name is Azerbaijani for "to create" (Hyperallergic, 2020).
- The building is a converted naval maintenance base with about 2,000 square metres of exhibition space (Wikipedia).
- On site and elsewhere: the Painting Museum, ARTIM Project Space in the Old City (opened October 2015), YAY Gallery and YARAT Studios; programmes cover exhibitions, talks, a film club, an art school, workshops, a residency and a Friends membership (Wikipedia, YARAT website).
- No reporting found of opening days or admission.

FUNDING
YARAT Contemporary Art Space: undisclosed: no funder list or accounts published; the founder says private, government help acknowledged
- Mahmudova said in a 2015 Forbes interview that the government provides "technical support", such as renting spaces and permits, but does not fund YARAT (Hyperallergic, 2020). She credited the government with helping obtain and convert the site (The Art Newspaper).
- Hyperallergic (2021) says YARAT has no public records, and that Mahmudova claimed an unnamed bank backs it, which the article did not find listed.
- Unverified: Meydan TV (2017) says YARAT receives large contributions from the telecom companies Azercell and Azerfon, whose beneficiaries include the daughters of President Aliyev, from the outlet's reporting; it gives no financial records. Meydan TV describes itself as independent and is critical of the government.
- March 2026: YARAT was one of the partners of the "Beyond the Frame" exhibition at the Stone Chronicle Museum, with bp, SOCAR Midstream Gas Operations (initiator and supporter), PASHA Holding and the Regional Development Public Union of the Heydar Aliyev Foundation (APA).

Red flags: ties to the Aliyev family and state
- Family: Hyperallergic (2020) calls Mahmudova the niece of Ilham Aliyev; The Art Newspaper (2015) says First Lady Mehriban Aliyeva, who leads the Heydar Aliyev Foundation, is her aunt.
- State help: Mahmudova credits the government with helping obtain and convert the building (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: Hyperallergic (2021) argues the state funds YARAT, a conclusion it draws from the absence of records; the outlet is critical of the Aliyev government.
Red flags: protest by an exhibiting artist
- On 25 October 2020 YARAT's Instagram showed its facade with an Azerbaijani flag and the hashtag #KarabakhIsAzerbaijan. The Turkish artist Ahmet Ogut objected that it used his work as a "propaganda tool" and demanded his exhibition close. YARAT agreed to end it on 28 October, three weeks early, but kept the image up (Hyperallergic, ArtReview).
Red flags: fossil fuel partners
- bp and SOCAR Midstream Gas Operations were named with YARAT as partners of that March 2026 exhibition (APA); no amount or payment to YARAT is stated.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism is recurring and centres on state ties and the 2020 Karabakh episode. Most of it is from advocacy-leaning or opinion outlets; one reports the artist's own statement.
1. Hyperallergic: Dorian Batycka, "Artist Accuses Azerbaijani Art Center of Using His Work as 'Propaganda Tool'" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/598624/yarat-contemporary-art-space-propaganda-tool
- Argues YARAT is politically aligned with the government despite claiming independence.
- Cites the 2017 exhibition "Neither War, Nor Peace", whose press statement said Azerbaijan's position was used to "strengthen patriotic moods", and the Ogut dispute.
2. Hyperallergic: Nevdon Jamgochian, "Artwashing a Dictatorship" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/615519/artwashing-a-dictatorship/
- Argues it is impossible to see how YARAT is funded "in any way but through the Aliyevs"; YARAT did not respond.
- Concedes parts of its case rest on inference rather than documents.
3. ArtReview: "Baku art centre pulls exhibition amid political controversy" (2020)
URL: https://artreview.com/baku-art-centre-pulls-exhibition-amid-political-controversy/
- Reports Ogut's withdrawal and notes some in the art world worry the centre is funded through the founder's family link to President Aliyev.
- No statement from YARAT in the article.
4. Meydan TV: "When Authoritarianism and Contemporary Art Cross Paths" (2017)
URL: https://www.meydan.tv/en/article/when-authoritarianism-and-contemporary-art-cross-paths/
- Argues artists at YARAT self-censor, quoting a former assistant curator. Not an art-press outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
YARAT publishes no funder list or accounts, so its money cannot be checked. The documented weak points are the founder's family tie to the Aliyev family, government help with the building, the 2020 Karabakh episode and bp and SOCAR as 2026 partners. Worth watching: any published accounts, and whether the telecom and state funding claims are ever documented.
