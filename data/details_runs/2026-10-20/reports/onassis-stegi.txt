ONASSIS STEGI: Athens, Greece
ID: onassis-stegi
Website: https://www.onassis.org (checked 2026-10-24; the page returned no text, so facts below come from other pages)

OVERVIEW
- Performing arts and culture venue on Syngrou Avenue, Athens, owned by the Onassis Foundation (Wikipedia, "Onassis Stegi").
- Built by the Alexander S. Onassis Public Benefit Foundation; construction began in 2004, the opening ceremony was on 7 December 2010 with President Karolos Papoulias, and it opened to the public on 11 December 2010. It was formerly called the Onassis Cultural Centre (Wikipedia).
- The Foundation is named for Alexander Onassis, who died in 1973; Aristotle Onassis's will directed half his estate to it and his executors established it in 1975 (Wikipedia, "Alexander S. Onassis Foundation").
- Building: 18,000 square metres on a three-acre plot, designed by the French firm Architecture-Studio, chosen through a competition; two main halls of about 900 and 200 seats, an exhibition space and a restaurant (Wikipedia).
- Programme: theatre, dance, concerts, film, lectures and conferences; an estimated 300,000 visitors a year (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening days, admission or accessibility in the pages read.

FUNDING
Onassis Stegi: foundation-funded: the Alexander S. Onassis Public Benefit Foundation, paid for by shipping and real estate profits
- The Public Benefit Foundation's activities "are funded exclusively by the profits of the Business Foundation," which operates mainly in shipping and real estate (Wikipedia, "Alexander S. Onassis Foundation").
- The Foundation funded the construction of the Stegi (Wikipedia).
- Tanker income: on 31 January 2023 the Indian Transport & Logistics Network described Olympic Shipping and Management as "Onassis Foundation-controlled" and said its fleet "comprises 18 crude oil tankers". No page read says how much of the Business Foundation's profit comes from the tankers, or that the Foundation produces fossil fuels.
- Origin of the founder's money: Aristotle Onassis "amassed the world's largest privately owned shipping fleet"; Wikipedia says oil companies including Mobil, Socony and Texaco signed time charters with him ("Aristotle Onassis").
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors of the Stegi, its budget or its board; the Wikipedia entry names no sponsors.

Red flags: teachers' strike over the Foundation's Onassis Public Schools
- Onassis Public Schools: the Foundation committed 160 million euros over 12 years for 22 Onassis Public Schools, 11 middle and 11 high schools (To Vima, 28 January 2025). The Federation of Secondary Education Officials held a three-hour work stoppage from 11:00 to 14:00 on Monday 17 February 2025, with a rally at Syntagma Square, arguing the change threatens neighbourhood schools and could increase privatisation (To Vima, 15 February 2025).
- Teachers at the 3rd General Lyceum of Kozani said no consultation was held with teachers or parents; Deputy Minister Zetta Makri replied that the state will have a majority on the steering committee (To Vima, 28 January 2025).
- To Vima reports no direct response from the Foundation to the strike.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism of the Stegi found in art media. The one critical analysis found is an academic article on private foundations in Athens in general.
1. International Journal of Urban and Regional Research: Penny Koutrolikou, Antonis Papangelopoulos and Christos Georgakopoulos, "Athens's philanthrocapitalist landscape within the crisis-austerity conjuncture" (2025)
URL: https://www.ijurr.org/article/athenss-philanthrocapitalist-landscape-within-the-crisis-austerity-conjuncture/
- Argues that philanthrocapitalist foundations have grown in influence over Athens during about 15 years of crises, changing the city's governance.
- The journal's editorial note names the Onassis and Niarchos Foundations among the entities shaping urban governance and cultural projects. The abstract does not name them.
- Academic journal, not art press; only the abstract and editorial note were read. It does not name the Stegi.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Stills, 2015: the Stegi's Fast Forward Festival 2 projected Kris Verdonck's video work "Stills", which shows nudes, in Klafthmonos Square. After a priest filed a complaint that went to the Acropolis police station, the Stegi stopped the projection on 26 May 2015. It said it did so because of complaints about the content, rejected the view that nudity in art is obscene and called for discussion of artistic freedom in public space (in.gr, 29 May 2015, translated from Greek).
- Connection: the Stegi is funded by the Onassis Foundation, not the Greek state (Wikipedia), so this is context about artistic freedom in Greece, not a state-funding issue.

BOTTOM LINE
The Stegi is paid for by the Onassis Foundation, whose income comes from shipping and real estate; its shipping arm ran 18 crude oil tankers in January 2023. The tanker income is a fact about the Foundation's money, not a documented link to fossil fuel production. The weak points are the unpublished budget and sponsors, and the 2025 teachers' strike against the Foundation's school programme.
