CASTELLO DI RIVOLI: Rivoli (Turin), Italy
ID: castello-di-rivoli-turin
Website: https://www.castellodirivoli.org/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in the former Savoy castle at Piazza Mafalda di Savoia, Rivoli, Turin. From Turin, visitors take the metro to Paradiso, then bus 36 and a shuttle bus to the museum (Castello di Rivoli).
- The castle reopened in 1984 as the Museo d'Arte Contemporanea, described by Wikipedia as the first contemporary art museum in Italy. Rudi Fuchs and Johannes Gachnang directed it from 1984 to 1991; Wikipedia states Francesco Manacorda has been director since 2024. The museum lists Marcella Beccaria as deputy director (Castello di Rivoli).
- Building: first mentioned in 1159; sacked by French troops; Filippo Juvarra intervened from 1716 but left the facade unfinished. The comune of Rivoli used it as barracks after 1863, and restoration began in 1979 (Wikipedia). The Manica Lunga is the only 17th-century part still visible (Wikipedia).
- On site: a library open to the public since 1999 (Wikipedia) and the Cerruti Collection (Castello di Rivoli).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Friday 10 am to 5 pm, Saturday, Sunday and public holidays 11 am to 6 pm; the Cerruti Collection opens Saturday and Sunday 10:30 am to 6 pm. Tickets: €15 full, €6.50 reduced (over 65s, teachers, students and teenagers 11 to 18); free categories include minors and visitors with disabilities and their caregivers. Two ramps give access, a wheelchair is available and guide dogs are allowed.

FUNDING
Castello di Rivoli: Regione Piemonte, the Ministry of Culture, Compagnia di San Paolo, Fondazione CRC, Fondazione CRT and Intesa Sanpaolo
- 2023 income was €5,628,736 (accounts, approved 20 March 2024). Public grants were €3,249,636: Regione Piemonte €510,000 as membership fee and €1,979,000 institutional contribution (together about €2.49 million); Ministry of Culture project grants (PNRR €316,317 and €183,645, PAC €98,212, Italian Council €41,000, library €8,462); City of Turin €75,000; City of Rivoli €15,000.
- Private grants were €1,279,281, from Compagnia di San Paolo (several lines, including €95,585, €59,415, €35,000 and €20,000), Fondazione CRC (€30,000 for Coltivarte and exhibition grants of €98,361, €34,592 and €12,307), Fondazione CRT per l'Arte (€20,000), Fondazione Magnetto (€13,000) and Fondazione TBA21 (€25,000). Fondazione CRT also made an institutional grant in 2023 (accounts). The accounts record a €90,000 contribution for the "Artisti in guerra" exhibition.
- Intesa Sanpaolo gave €23,000 in sponsorship and €5,000 for Lunch Talks in 2023; the museum names it, with Gallerie d'Italia, as its partner (accounts; Castello di Rivoli). The accounts also record two loans from Intesa Sanpaolo, for fire-system maintenance and for refrigeration in the Manica Lunga.
- Membership and donations were €168,000: benefactors €30,000, supporters €27,000, friends €41,000, donations €40,000 and Art Bonus €30,000. Ticket sales were €214,380.
- The museum lists 11 benefactors, including Francesca Lavazza, Miuccia Prada, Patrizia Sandretto Re Rebaudengo and Andrea Zegna (Castello di Rivoli). The accounts credit Lavazza Group as main partner of the Olafur Eliasson exhibition.
- Governance: Francesca Lavazza chairs the board (Wikipedia gives her as chair since 2021) and Paola Volpe is vice president; auditors are led by Alessandro Cian (Castello di Rivoli).

BOTTOM LINE
Castello di Rivoli's 2023 income was €5.63 million. Public grants were €3.25 million (Regione Piemonte about €2.49 million, the Ministry of Culture, the City of Turin and the City of Rivoli) and private grants €1.28 million (Compagnia di San Paolo, Fondazione CRC, Fondazione CRT, Fondazione Magnetto and Fondazione TBA21). Intesa Sanpaolo is a named partner and sponsor, and individual benefactors and Friends give a further €168,000.
