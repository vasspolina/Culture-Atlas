NASJONALMUSEET (NATIONAL MUSEUM OF NORWAY): Oslo, Norway
ID: nasjonalmuseet-national-museum-of-norway
Website: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no (checked 2026-10-24; loads; the visit and ticket pages showed no hours or prices to the reader used)

OVERVIEW
- Norway's national museum of art, architecture and design, at Brynjulf Bulls plass 3 on the Vestbanen site in central Oslo (museum site; Wikipedia).
- Created on 28 April 2003 by merging the National Gallery, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Museum of Architecture and two design museums; it is state-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia).
- The new building opened in June 2022. Kleihues + Schuwerk won the 2010 competition; the government presented the project at about NOK 5.3 billion in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Holds over 400,000 works, including the first version of Edvard Munch's "The Scream" (1893) (Wikipedia).
- Critics derided the building; one called it the "national prison". The first director Sune Nordgren resigned amid staff conflict (Wikipedia).
- The director is Ingrid Røynesdal, appointed in 2023, after Karin Hindsbo (2017 to 2023) (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening hours, admission prices or accessibility details; the museum's visit pages showed none to the reader used.

FUNDING
Nasjonalmuseet: mainly public: a state-owned foundation, with private partners including the Fredriksen family
- State-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia). The 2024 annual report PDF exceeded the reading tool's size limit, so no income figures could be confirmed.
- The museum lists six partners: AKO Foundation, the Fredriksen Family Collection, Lise M Stolt-Nielsen and family, Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Stiftelsen Scheibler and Viking. The page states no amounts or years.
- Fredriksen Family Art Company: lends the Fredriksen Family Collection on a long-term basis, initial term ten years, confirmed in 2017; funds a doctoral programme with four fellowships planned for 2023 to 2027, and a separate doctoral project on Frida Hansen. No financial figures are given (museum site). Cecilie and Kathrine Fredriksen, daughters of the shipping magnate John Fredriksen, announced the partnership in 2019 and have lent more than 100 works (Apollo, 2025).

Red flags: protest against the Fredriksen partnership
- 10 June 2022: artists Nikhil Vettukattil, Knut Asdam, Kajsa Dahlberg and Sille Storihle launched an appeal, "A Gift we can Afford to Refuse", calling for the deal to be ended; it had well over 100 signatures by publication (Kunstkritikk).
- The appeal argues the deal gives one wealthy family too much influence, calls it artwashing, and cites Fredriksen's business ties to Russia, including the Order of Friendship awarded to him in 2015 (Kunstkritikk).
- 18 March 2022: former Nordnorsk Kunstmuseum director Jeremie McGowan wrote in Klassekampen that Fredriksen's drilling company Seadrill signed a major deal with Rosneft, mainly owned by the Russian state, just before Russia annexed Crimea in 2014. Ruben Steinum, chair of the Association of Norwegian Visual Artists, said the museum should explain how it assesses such partners (Kunstkritikk, Live Dronen).
- Museum response: communications director Eirik Kydland said the museum sees no new circumstances that call for review, that no Fredriksen company has been hit by sanctions, and that the money comes from legitimate business (Kunstkritikk).
- McGowan called the museum "naive or complicit", his opinion as quoted by Kunstkritikk.

URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/artists-fight-the-fredriksen-collaboration
- Argues the museum is giving up authority and credibility, and that public trust should outweigh the financial benefit.
- Notes that the Astrup Fearnley Museum cut ties with Lundin Oil and that the Met and Serpentine ended Sackler sponsorship. No direct statement from the museum is included.

BOTTOM LINE
Nasjonalmuseet is mostly state-funded, with a named private partner, the Fredriksen family, whose loan and doctoral funding come from a shipping and drilling fortune. The weak points are the artists' 2022 appeal, the Russia links raised by McGowan, and income figures that could not be confirmed from the 2024 report. Worth watching: whether the Fredriksen agreement is renewed when its ten-year term ends.
