OVERVIEW
- Non-profit that commissions, presents and keeps art projects; its main museum, Dia Beacon, is at 3 Beekman Street on the banks of the Hudson River in Beacon, New York, about a five-minute walk from the Metro-North station (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1974 by Philippa de Menil, Heiner Friedrich and Helen Winkler; renamed Dia Center for the Arts in 1987, the name still on its tax filings. Jessica Morgan has been director since 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Dia Beacon opened in 2003 in a former Nabisco box-printing plant built in 1929, converted by the artist Robert Irwin with the architects OpenOffice; its galleries are named the Riggio Galleries (Wikipedia).
- About 240,000 square feet of exhibition space on a 31-acre site, showing artists of the 1960s and 1970s such as Andy Warhol, Dan Flavin, Richard Serra, Michael Heizer and Agnes Martin. Dia also runs Dia Chelsea and keeps sites including Walter De Maria's "The Lightning Field" and Robert Smithson's "Spiral Jetty" (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Dia Art Foundation: private: donors, an endowment and investment income, built on Schlumberger oil wealth
- Schlumberger oil wealth paid for much of Dia's early work; when Schlumberger stock fell in the early 1980s Dia made deep cuts and sold works (Wikipedia).
- Leonard Riggio, of Barnes & Noble, gave at least $35 million of the roughly $50 million cost of Dia Beacon (Wikipedia). He chaired Dia's board while it was built, paid cost overruns and later resigned as chairman (ArtsJournal, 2007).
- In the year to June 2023 revenue was $37,212,649, of which contributions were $30,083,580, investment income $4,228,957 and programme revenue $2,074,096; expenses were $20,191,928 and net assets $225,431,839 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- In 2013 Dia sold works at Sotheby's for $38.4 million to fund acquisitions (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a current list of corporate sponsors or of named donors' amounts; Dia's corporate giving page was blocked.

Red flags: Sackler gift and naming, dropped in 2019
- In 2016 Dia created the Sackler Institute for programming, research and education and removed the name in 2019; ARTnews's 2016 headline said Dia created it with a "generous" gift (Wikipedia list, citing ARTnews).
- Hyperallergic wrote in 2021 that several institutions, including New York University and Dia, had removed the Sackler name, in part due to PAIN's advocacy and increasing public awareness; it does not tie that advocacy to Dia in particular.
- No reporting found of a current Sackler gift to Dia.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution, on staff pay and the Sackler name. Reviews of the collection are mostly admiring.
1. Hyperallergic: Elaine Velie, "Dia Art Foundation Workers Seek a Union" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/748337/dia-art-foundation-workers-seek-a-union/
- A UAW press release said gallery attendants at Dia Beacon earned at most $15.30 an hour until July 2022 (Dia said the minimum had been $15); a worker said the raise to $16 left staff "below a livable wage for a single person in Dutchess county".
- A worker said Dia's Beacon development raised local housing costs; Dia said it supported staff weighing union representation.
2. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Weber, "Sacklers Temporarily Banned From Slapping Name on Art Institutions" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/661915/sacklers-temporarily-banned-from-slapping-name-on-art-institutions/
- Names Dia among institutions that removed the Sackler name, in part due to PAIN's advocacy.
- Mentions Dia only in passing.
3. ArtsJournal: Douglas McLennan, "One Pissed-Off Donor" (2007)

- On Riggio's angry resignation as chairman after paying for Dia Beacon's overruns.

BOTTOM LINE
Dia runs on private gifts and a large endowment, with roots in Schlumberger oil money and Dia Beacon built largely by Leonard Riggio. The weak point is the 2016 Sackler gift and naming, dropped in 2019. Worth watching: donor disclosure, which its blocked site does not allow to be checked.
