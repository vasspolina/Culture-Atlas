KUNSTMUSEUM BERN: Bern, Switzerland
ID: kunstmuseum-bern
Website: https://www.kunstmuseumbern.ch/de

OVERVIEW
- Fine art museum at Hodlerstrasse 8 to 12 in Bern, about 10 minutes on foot from Bern station (museum website).
- Established in 1879 (Wikipedia). Its holdings run from the Middle Ages to the present and include works by Paul Klee, Pablo Picasso and Ferdinand Hodler.
- In May 2014 the museum was named sole heir of the collector Cornelius Gurlitt, whose homes held more than 1,400 artworks, many suspected of being stolen from European Jews by the Nazis. The bequest required provenance research, and the board voted to accept it in November 2014 (Wikipedia).
- Nina Zimmer, the director, took over the Kunstmuseum Bern in August 2016 and is also director of the Zentrum Paul Klee (Wikipedia).
- The museum says its building lacks up-to-date infrastructure, including climate and cooling systems and fire protection. The winning design "Eiger" by Schmidlin Architekten would add a new contemporary building and a public square (museum website). On 14 June 2026 Canton Bern voters rejected the CHF 15.7 million planning credit with 51.8 percent No (SRF).
- Visiting: closed Monday; open Tuesday 10:00 to 20:00 and Wednesday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00. Admission was CHF 24 on 25 October 2026.

FUNDING
Kunstmuseum Bern: a public funding body and foundations, with UBS as main sponsor
- The museum's partner page lists UBS as Hauptsponsor (main sponsor) and, under Subventionsgeber (funding body), a logo linking to be.ch, the Canton Bern site.
- Exhibition sponsors on the page are three logos linking to bak.admin.ch (Federal Office of Culture), mobiliar.ch and art-uniqa.ch. Media partners are Der Bund and SonntagsZeitung.
- Funding foundations on the page: Dr. Georg und Josi Guggenheim Stiftung, Minerva Kunststiftung, Museumsstiftung für Kunst der Burgergemeinde Bern, Rudolf und Ursula Streit-Stiftung, Ruth & Arthur Scherbarth Stiftung, Stiftung GegenwART of Dr. h. c. Hansjörg Wyss, Susann Häusler-Stiftung and Ursula Wirz-Stiftung.
- Private individuals named: Pierre Kottelat and Susanne and Franz Portmann.
- The planned renovation and new building cost CHF 147 million, of which the canton would pay CHF 81 million and the rest would come from private donors, the "Bernburger" and the lottery fund (SRF, 12 June 2026).

WIDER CONTEXT
- FinCEN announced on 3 August 2026 a $125 million penalty against UBS Financial Services Inc. for willful violations of the Bank Secrecy Act. The release concerns anti-money laundering failures, including more than 50,000 foreign currency wires worth more than $10 billion that were inadequately monitored, not sanctions violations. The penalty is against the US subsidiary, not UBS, the sponsor the museum names.
- The Federal Reserve announced on 10 May 2004 that UBS AG of Zurich consented to a $100 million civil money penalty "in connection with U.S. dollar banknote transactions with counterparties in jurisdictions subject to sanctions under U.S. law", naming Cuba, Libya, Iran and Yugoslavia. The order is against UBS AG; the museum's partner page lists UBS.
- UBS is the museum's main sponsor (museum partner page).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded through a public funding body and a set of Bern foundations, with UBS as main sponsor and the Federal Office of Culture, Mobiliar and UNIQA Art linked as exhibition sponsors. The 2026 FinCEN penalty is against UBS Financial Services Inc. and the 2004 Federal Reserve penalty against UBS AG; both are listed as wider context. Bern voters rejected the canton's share of the rebuilding costs in June 2026.
