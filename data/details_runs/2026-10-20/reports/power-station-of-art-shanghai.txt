POWER STATION OF ART: Shanghai, China
ID: power-station-of-art-shanghai
Website: https://www.powerstationofart.com/

OVERVIEW
- State-owned contemporary art museum at 678 Miaojiang Road, Huangpu district, Shanghai (Shanghai government guide; Wikipedia).
- Opened on 1 October 2012 with the 9th Shanghai Biennale, which it has hosted since (Wikipedia).
- It is directly under the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism (Wikipedia).
- Building and site: the Nanshi Electric Plant opened in 1897 and moved to this riverside site in 1935; it ceased operation in 2007 and its building served as the Pavilion of the Future at Expo 2010 (Wikipedia).
- The conversion took about nine months and kept the 165-metre chimney, the rooftop generators and the general layout; it was led by TJAD Original Design Studio, part of Tongji University's Architectural Design and Research Institute (Wikipedia).
- The museum also holds design exhibitions under the name Power Station of Design and has hosted the Emerging Curators Project since 2014; an Academic Committee was set up in 2013 (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia lists Gong Yan as director and gives about 700,000 visitors in 2019.
- In 2015 it was designated a Shanghai Municipal Heritage Architecture (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 11:00 to 19:00, last entry 18:00, closed Monday except national holidays (Shanghai government guide). Wikipedia describes exhibitions as free to the public.

FUNDING
Power Station of Art: government-run: the Shanghai municipal government paid for the conversion
- Wikipedia states the conversion into a museum cost the Shanghai municipal government $64 million.
- The museum sits directly under the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism (Wikipedia); Hyperallergic calls it "China's first government-run contemporary art museum".
- The 9th Shanghai Biennale, held at the museum, was directed by the local government (The Miami Rail, Jacob Dreyer).

Red flags: state censorship, Shanghai cultural officials removed Ai Weiwei's work from a 2014 show at the museum
- Hyperallergic (Jillian Steinhauer, 30 April 2014), citing the New York Times, reported that local cultural officials in Shanghai censored Ai Weiwei's name and work from the exhibition "15 Years Chinese Contemporary Art Award" at the museum. Two works, a wooden stool and porcelain sunflower seeds, were pulled.
- The works were to be shown until a few days before the opening the previous Saturday, when Shanghai officials told Sigg they could not be shown (Hyperallergic). Ai's name stayed on the show's webpage; Hyperallergic wrote that it "has apparently been removed from the on-site wall text".
- The museum is state-owned and sits directly under the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism, and the Shanghai municipal government paid $64 million for its conversion (Wikipedia). Hyperallergic names the actors as Shanghai officials and local cultural officials, not as a named body.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism dates from 2012 to 2014 and concerns state control of programming, the execution of the opening and the 2012 biennale.
1. Hyperallergic: Jillian Steinhauer, "Chinese Officials Remove Ai Weiwei Work from Exhibition" (30 April 2014)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/chinese-officials-remove-ai-weiwei-work-from-exhibition
- Reports the removal of Ai Weiwei's work and name from the "15 Years Chinese Contemporary Art Award" exhibition and describes the museum as China's first government-run contemporary art museum.
2. The Miami Rail: Jacob Dreyer, "The 9th Shanghai Biennale"
URL: https://miamirail.org/visual-arts/the-9th-shanghai-biennale
- Names the opening of the Power Station of Art among opportunities that could have been spectacular and says "a sense of incompetence in the details of execution cheapened a potentially amazing venue for cultural production"; says the echoes of Tate Modern went from seeming like a subtle tribute to a mediocre copy.
- Says the 9th Shanghai Biennale, "directed by the local government", betrayed the problems of its approach, and that state-run exhibitions like the biennale "seem to appropriate this sort of outsider vision for their own".
- Cites prominent spelling errors in exhibits, such as "Hong Knog".
3. Border Crossings: Jesi Khadivi, "Shanghai Biennial 2012" (March 2013)
URL: https://bordercrossingsmag.com/article/shanghai-biennial-2012
- Says construction finished in the immediate lead-up to the official opening and the exhibition seemed "half-empty" despite large-scale works in its main venue.
- Describes an "elastic theme with oblique references to contemporaneity and social engagement" and reports that the Vancouver Art Gallery silently withdrew Brian Jungen's contribution to the Vancouver pavilion, citing poor gallery maintenance.
- Describes the state-backed China Art Palace, opened alongside the Power Station of Art, as "dazzling, though questionable"; this concerns the neighbouring building, not the Power Station.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum sits directly under the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism, and Wikipedia says the Shanghai municipal government paid $64 million to convert the power plant. The documented weak point is the 2014 removal of Ai Weiwei's work from a show at the museum by local Shanghai cultural officials, as reported by Hyperallergic.
