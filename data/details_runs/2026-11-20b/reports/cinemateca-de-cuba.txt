CINEMATECA DE CUBA: Havana, Cuba
ID: cinemateca-de-cuba
Website: http://www.cinematecadecuba.cult.cu (checked 2026-11-20; did not load)

OVERVIEW
- Film archive and screening programme of ICAIC, housed since 20 October 2020 in Alfredo Guevara's former home at Calle 11 No. 806, between 2 and 4, Vedado, Havana (EcuRed, state encyclopedia, archive copy of 12 November 2024; OnCuba).
- Its own chronology and EcuRed date its creation to 6 February 1960, as a cultural department of ICAIC; OnCuba names Héctor García Mesa as founder.
- Spanish Wikipedia instead says Germán Puig founded a Cinemateca de Cuba in 1951 at the FIAF congress in Cambridge, citing a 2004 paper by Emmanuel Vincenot. EcuRed says an earlier film club used the name without holding films and calls crediting it with the founding an error.
- For many years it occupied the first floor of the ICAIC building on Avenida 23 (EcuRed).
- In 1989 ICAIC's film archive was merged into it, partly to remove the archive's cost from the Estudios del ICAIC (EcuRed).
- Holdings: Cuban and foreign films, scripts, production photographs, posters, press cuttings and a specialised library and newspaper archive; it publishes catalogues and books (EcuRed).
- The new house has a small hall, El Mégano, for screenings and talks, and a large patio (OnCuba, quoting director Luciano Castillo; EcuRed).
- Its usual screening venue is the Cine 23 y 12; its 65th anniversary programme ran from 6 to 9 February 2025 (Prensa Latina, a state agency).
- Luciano Castillo, director since 2013 (EcuRed), announced the programme of ICAIC's restored-film meeting in March 2025, held in four Havana cinemas with free entry (Prensa Latina).
- It is a member of the International Federation of Film Archives (FIAF) and of CLAIM, the Latin American coordinator of moving image archives (EcuRed).

FUNDING
Cinemateca de Cuba: state-funded: Ministry of Culture through ICAIC
- It is a department of ICAIC (its own chronology; EcuRed), and EcuRed lists ICAIC in the Ministry of Culture's system.
- The government and the Ministry of Culture assigned it the new house in 2020; President Miguel Díaz-Canel and ICAIC president Ramón Samada attended the opening (EcuRed; OnCuba).
- EcuRed says that with ICAIC's contribution it restored all the pre-1960 Cuban films it could recover, and that it keeps duplication materials and exhibition prints of ICAIC's films.
- Private collections, the assets of film distributors and producers, and purchases and donations of foreign films added to the holdings (EcuRed).
- Restoration: at ICAIC's 2025 restored-film meeting, ICAIC's president Alexis Triana credited the Fundación del Patrimonio Fílmico Colombiano with helping Cuba restore classics of its cinema (Prensa Latina).

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2014: a protest statement by Cuban filmmakers said the Ministry of Culture and ICAIC, the Cinemateca's parent, forced the Havana festival to withdraw Regreso a Ítaca (OnCuba, 2015).
- 2016: Fernando Pérez publicly rejected ICAIC's exclusion of Santa y Andrés from the Havana festival (OnCuba, 2016).
- 2020: ICAIC removed Sueños al pairo from the Muestra Joven, and other young filmmakers withdrew their films in solidarity (OnCuba, 2020).
- 2023: the Asamblea de Cineastas Cubanos, which met at the Cine 23 y 12, the Cinemateca's usual venue, condemned the handling of the documentary La Habana de Fito and declared that Cuban cinema is not ICAIC (OnCuba, July 2023).
- The Cinemateca is a department of ICAIC.

BOTTOM LINE
The Cinemateca's money is clean in the atlas's terms: state money through ICAIC and no flagged funder. Its exposure is its parent's censorship record.
