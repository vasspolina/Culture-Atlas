BLAXTARLINES KUMASI: Kumasi, Ghana
ID: blaxtarlines-kumasi

OVERVIEW
- Artist collective, organisational network and art incubator based on the campus of Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) in Kumasi. It "works in association with, yet independent from, KNUST" (e-flux, Doreen Mende, 6 October 2026), and KNUST places it at the Department of Painting and Sculpture, Faculty of Art.
- Formally instituted in 2015 to reform the KNUST College of Art's curriculum (The State of the Arts). Its ethos draws on kąrî'kạchä seid'ou's Emancipatory Art Teaching project, started at KNUST in 2003 (The State of the Arts), and e-flux names seid'ou as a cofounder.
- Its name draws on Kwame Nkrumah and Marcus Garvey's idea of the Black Star (Brooklyn Rail, Jesse Weaver Shipley, 2026).
- Members include Ibrahim Mahama and Bernard Akoi-Jackson (Brooklyn Rail); e-flux also names Tracy Naa Koshie Thompson, Adjoa Daiki Apodey Kisser, Kwaku Boafo Kissiedu and Kwasi Ohene-Ayeh as key figures.
- Programme: four end-of-year exhibitions from 2015 to 2017 and "Spectacles. Speculations…" in 2018, with a network extending internationally (The State of the Arts).
- Ranked No. 69 in ArtReview's 2025 Power 100, up from No. 81 the year before (KNUST). Selected for the main exhibition of the 2026 Venice Biennale, "In Minor Keys", where it presented "SLOW DOWN: CATTLE BATTLE AHEAD" at the Arsenale, with a video archive, a library, a Kente-weaving loom and a map (e-flux; Brooklyn Rail).
- Visiting: its exhibitions are free (The State of the Arts).

FUNDING
blaxTARLINES KUMASI: artist-run network working from the KNUST campus
- The collective is based at KNUST's Department of Painting and Sculpture (KNUST news release) and two of its cofounders, kąrî'kạchä seid'ou and Ediwn Bodjawah, live in the campus atelier houses (e-flux).
- e-flux describes the group's economy as value passed on rather than accumulated, "transforming art from commodity to gift".

BOTTOM LINE
The documented backing for blaxTARLINES is its base on a university campus, from which an artist-run network runs free exhibitions and has built an international profile through ArtReview's Power 100 and the 2026 Venice Biennale.
