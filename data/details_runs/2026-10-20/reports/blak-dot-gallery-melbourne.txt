BLAK DOT GALLERY: Melbourne, Australia
ID: blak-dot-gallery-melbourne
Website: https://blakdot.com.au (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- First Nations artist-run gallery at 33 Saxon Street, Brunswick, in Naarm (Melbourne), reached by Dawson Street; it describes itself as "Australia's leading gallery for contemporary First Nations art".
- Founded in 2011 as an artist-run space; its 2014 address was 413 Lygon Street, East Brunswick (TextaQueen), and it moved to its current premises behind the Brunswick baths in 2016 (Time Out). The founders are not named on the pages read.
- Director Kimba Thompson leads the gallery with help from volunteers and artists (Time Out). It is a non-profit governed by a board; no board members are named on its About page.
- The gallery's support page says it moved to a new home at Balam Balam Place, 33 Saxon Street, in April 2025.
- Programme: solo, group and community exhibitions, workshops, performances, panels, lectures and artist markets, plus Blakademy, a learning space for First Nations creatives. In April 2014 it hosted "18C: Artists' Responses to the Impending Amendment of the Racial Discrimination Act 1975", a group show of Indigenous and non-Indigenous artists responding to the government's plan to remove sections 18B to 18E, with documentation to be sent to the Attorney General's office (TextaQueen).
- Visiting: Time Out lists Thursday to Saturday noon to 5pm and Sunday noon to 4pm; the gallery's own page lists no hours. Time Out does not mention an entry fee. The venue is wheelchair accessible, has accessible toilets and a reserved accessible car park at the rear, and welcomes service animals (Melbourne Fringe).

FUNDING
Blak Dot Gallery: mainly public: Australian Government, Victorian Government and Merri-bek Council grants
- The About page names three funders: Australian Government Indigenous Language and Arts, Creative Victoria and Merri-bek Council. No amounts or years are given. ACCA's page on its offsite project at the gallery also names support from the Australian Government Indigenous Language and Arts and Creative Victoria.
- The gallery is a registered charity and a Deductible Gift Recipient; gifts over $2 are tax deductible. Its support page asks for help to fit out the new gallery with accessible infrastructure and to fund artist development, residencies and community events, and names no donors.
- Sales of artwork through the gallery and its online shop support the artists directly (support page).
- No reporting found in opened sources of grant amounts, annual reports or accounts.
- No reporting found of a corporate, fossil fuel, arms or other flagged funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is the gallery's own announcements, a Time Out listing and curatorial pages.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is government grants for a small First Nations artist-run space, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no amounts or accounts could be confirmed from the pages opened, and that the About page names no founders or board. Worth watching are its ACNC filings for the share of government income.
