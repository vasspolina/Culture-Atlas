YERBA BUENA CENTER FOR THE ARTS: San Francisco, USA
ID: yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-san-francisco
Website: https://ybca.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art, performance and film centre at 701 Mission Street, in the Yerba Buena Gardens area of South of Market, San Francisco.
- Opened in 1993; it was conceived in a deal Mayor George Moscone made with developers to set aside land and funds for cultural institutions in the South of Market redevelopment (Wikipedia).
- Two buildings: the Gallery and Forum building by Fumihiko Maki, and the Blue Shield of California Theater by James Polshek and Todd Schliemann (stated by the centre).
- Programmes: exhibitions, public art, performances, education, an archive and venue rental. It ran a 2021 guaranteed income pilot that paid $1,000 a month for six months to 130 artists (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday 11am to 8pm and Thursday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. The centre's about page gives no admission prices and no accessibility information.

FUNDING
Yerba Buena Center for the Arts: mixed public and private: city arts money, state and federal grants, foundations and individual donors
- City money: Proposition E (2018) directs 1.5% of the city's hotel tax to arts and cultural services, and the centre's founding deal set aside land and funds for it (Wikipedia). The size of the centre's own city grants was not found.
- Revenue for 2024-25 was $9,315,946 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- The centre's own funder line says its programmes are made possible in part by MacKenzie Scott and Dan Jewett, the California Arts Council and #StartSmall; the full sentence continues with further foundations and donors, including the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation, the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation and the San Francisco Arts Commission (YBCA 100 page, as shown in search results).
- Foundation grants listed by the grants database Granted AI from IRS filings include Hewlett (described as the largest recurring funder), Koret, Mellon, Doris Duke, Kenneth Rainin, Bernard Osher, Helen Frankenthaler, Rasmuson, William Penn and William G. Irwin Charity Foundation. No amounts per funder were verified.
- Granted AI also lists federal awards from the Institute of Museum and Library Services and the National Endowment for the Arts.
- The 2021 artist income pilot ran with the San Francisco Arts Commission and Grants for the Arts.
- No donor roster with amounts was readable in the sources opened; the 2023-24 annual report PDF could not be read as text.

Red flags: boycott and protest by exhibiting artists, aimed at funders and board
- Eight artists in the Bay Area Now 9 triennial altered their own works on 15 February 2024 during the "Love Letter to Gaza" action, organised with Jewish Voice for Peace Bay Area and other groups (KQED, Hyperallergic).
- Their demands included an end to censorship of artists, a ceasefire in Gaza, and the removal of "Zionist" board members and funders; Hyperallergic named the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation and the Koret Foundation as the funders the protesters targeted.
- On 21 February 2024 the centre's board rejected the demands as discrimination on religion, ethnic background or national origin and said the altered work would be removed and stored (YBCA statement).
- The artists then called for a boycott of the centre until they met its leadership, and asked it to join the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel (KQED, 26 February 2024).
- The galleries stayed closed for about a month. Interim CEO Sara Fenske Bahat resigned on 3 March 2024, citing "the vitriolic and antisemitic backlash directed at me personally" (KQED, Hyperallergic).
- Supervisor Hillary Ronen called a special hearing on the publicly funded centre and called its actions censorship (KQED).
- The dispute began in December 2023, when the centre blocked "Free Palestine" on its outdoor marquee for artist Lukaza Branfman-Verissimo; artist Jeffrey Cheung said he was barred from using Palestinian flag colours in a mural, which the CEO denied (KQED).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the 2024 protest is detailed and comes mostly from news outlets; the one art-press piece found supports the artists. No criticism of the centre's funding or programme before 2024 was found in art media.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Artists Alter Their Work, Protesting San Francisco Institution's 'Silence' on Gaza" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/872425/artists-deface-their-work-protesting-san-francisco-yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-silence-on-gaza/
- Reports the artists' view that the centre restricted pro-Palestine advocacy in their art and text, and lists their demands, including the removal of the Hewlett and Koret Foundations.
- Gives the centre's reply: the CEO said a mural design arrived after a deadline needed to coordinate with the city and funders, and that the marquee request was declined.
- Names Hewlett and Koret; names no board member.
2. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "Yerba Buena Center for the Arts CEO Resigns Over Pro-Palestine Action" (2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/yerba-buena-center-for-the-arts-ceo-resigns-over-pro-palestine-action
- Reports the resignation and both sides: artists say the centre blocked their advocacy; the CEO cites antisemitic backlash.
- The artists say their boycott targets institutions, not individuals.
- Names no funders or board members.
3. Variable West: Ella Ray, "Editor's Note: YBCA's censorship of artists for Palestinian liberation" (2024)
URL: https://variablewest.com/2024/03/10/editors-note-ybcas-censorship-of-artists-for-palestinian-liberation/
- Argues the centre censored artists, removed their work into storage and avoided addressing its ties to Israel.
- Calls on the centre to cut ties with "Zionist" donors and join the boycott campaign; names the Hewlett and Koret Foundations as funders. The piece states that Variable West "unequivocally supports" the artists, so it is an advocacy position.
- The piece does not specify the nature or size of the funding ties.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre's money is mostly public and foundation money, and nothing opened ties it to a flagged industry or donor. The weak points are thin donor disclosure, the 2024 dispute over censorship claims and the demand that two named foundations be dropped, and a leadership change that followed. Watch the board's response to the boycott call and whether donor lists with amounts are published.
