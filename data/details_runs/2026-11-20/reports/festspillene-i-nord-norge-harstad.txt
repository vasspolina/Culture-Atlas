FESTSPILLENE I NORD-NORGE (ARCTIC ARTS FESTIVAL): Harstad, Norway
ID: festspillene-i-nord-norge-harstad
Website: https://festspillnn.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- An eight-day international festival of music, theatre, dance and visual art held in Harstad each June around the summer solstice; it was first held in 1965 (festival). The 2026 edition ran from 20 to 27 June; the next runs from 19 to 26 June 2027 (festival).
- The idea came from Jan Kristian Nilsen, a journalist at Harstad Tidende; a founding meeting on 22 June 1964 set its programme, and children's events have been part of it since 1966 (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- It is organised by the foundation Festspillene i Nord-Norge, which has eight employees (Brønnøysund Register).
- Festival director is Susanne Næss Nielsen (festival, March 2026); Karin Eriksen, previously chief executive of Lavangen Municipality, was hired in June 2026 as administrative head, the first new holder of the post in 28 years (festival).
- It runs the Ungkunst grants, NOK 170,000 each to two young northern artists for new work premiered at the 2027 festival, a NOK 100,000 call for UiT art students, and the youth festival NUK, where participants meet across abilities (festival).
- It held national nodal festival status from 1995 to 2015 (festival).

FUNDING
Festspillene i Nord-Norge: mainly public: the state, the three northern counties and Harstad, with Aker BP among its business partners
- The festival names Arts Council Norway, Nordland, Troms and Finnmark counties and Harstad Municipality as its funders; international work is funded by the Nordic Council, Creative Europe and international cultural institutions (festival).
- Its home page lists as partners, without amounts: the Ministry of Culture, Troms, Nordland and Finnmark counties, Harstad Municipality, SpareBank 1 Nord-Norge, UiT The Arctic University of Norway, Chili, Harstad Tidende, Polarkonsult, Hinnstein, Kupa, HRS, Quality Hotel Harstad, Aker BP and Ramsalt (festival, checked 2026-11-20).
- In March 2026 HRS (Hålogaland Ressursselskap), a waste and recycling company owned by eight municipalities, signed a three-year agreement as sustainability partner and handles the festival's waste (festival).
- Accounts for 2025: operating income NOK 30.2M, of which NOK 7.3M sales; a loss of NOK 623,387, after a loss of NOK 580,756 in 2024 (Regnskapsregisteret).

Red flags: Aker BP, an oil company, among the business partners in 2026
- Aker BP is a Norwegian oil company that explores, develops and operates fields on the Norwegian continental shelf, producing about 480,000 barrels of oil a day (Store norske leksikon).
- Its logo sits among the festival's partners on the home page; the festival gives no amount (festival, checked 2026-11-20).

Red flags: 2017 loss of 82,400 euros to fraud
- The festival reported a financial fraud linked to the 2017 festival to the police; its loss came to 82,400 euros, about NOK 770,000 (Fagforbundet, 29 September 2017).

BOTTOM LINE
Mostly public money, with one current fossil fuel partner whose contribution is undisclosed. Worth watching: what the oil partnership pays for.
