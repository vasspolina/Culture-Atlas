ARKTIKUM (REGIONAL MUSEUM OF LAPLAND AND ARCTIC CENTRE): Rovaniemi, Finland
ID: arktikum-rovaniemi
Website: https://arktikum.fi (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Science centre and museum at Pohjoisranta 4 on the bank of the Ounasjoki river, on the edge of Rovaniemi city centre along highway E4 (arktikum.fi).
- It houses two exhibition producers under one roof: the Regional Museum of Lapland and the University of Lapland's Arctic Centre, a research institute and science centre founded in 1989 (arktikum.fi, Arctic Centre).
- The idea began in the late 1970s with the Finnish section of the European Cultural Foundation, which proposed a Finnish Arctic Museum; the city joined because the provincial museum needed new premises, and the project was made part of Finland's 75th anniversary (Arctic Centre).
- The Danish firm Birch-Bonderup & Thorup-Waade won the architectural competition; the visible part is a 172 metre glass tube of about a thousand panes; the exhibition halls lie underground, with floors of Perttaus granite and Lapland pine (arktikum.fi).
- It opened to the public on Finland's Independence Day, 6 December 1992; the extension, an office building, followed in 1997 (arktikum.fi, Arctic Centre).
- The Regional Museum of Lapland collects and researches the cultural history and natural science of Lapland and gives official statements on archaeology and built heritage for most of the province (arktikum.fi).
- The Arctic Centre employs more than 60 people in four research programmes, its science centre exhibition reopened renewed on 6 December 2024, and a library on northern issues is open to all (Arctic Centre, arktikum.fi).
- The seven-hectare Arctic Garden in front of the building is maintained by the University of Lapland; its rock exhibition belongs to the Geological Survey of Finland and was made possible by the Arctic Centre and Senate Properties (arktikum.fi).
- The museum counted 172,661 visits in 2025, of them 146,084 paid and 26,577 free (Finnish Heritage Agency, museum statistics 2025).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10 to 18, closed Monday, from 1 March to 30 November; adults EUR 22, students, pensioners and unemployed EUR 17, children 7 to 17 EUR 10, under 7 free; the Finnish Museum Card is accepted and assistants enter free; a threshold at the main entrance and heavy doors to the exhibitions limit wheelchair access; lifts serve both levels (arktikum.fi).

FUNDING
Arktikum: mainly public: the City of Rovaniemi, the Finnish state and the University of Lapland, plus ticket income
- The Regional Museum of Lapland is a municipal museum of the City of Rovaniemi (Finnish Heritage Agency); the State Audit Office described it in 2003 as owned and mainly financed by the city.
- Museum spending in 2025 was EUR 1,754,571: EUR 762,767 on staff, EUR 513,164 on property costs and EUR 51,934 on exhibitions (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- Museum income in 2025 was EUR 1,561,410: admissions EUR 1,002,575, the state's statutory museum funding under the Museums Act EUR 507,621 and state grants EUR 34,046 (Finnish Heritage Agency).
- The Arctic Centre is funded partly through the University of Lapland and partly by external funding it competes for (Arctic Centre).
- The building's owner in 2003 was the property company Kiinteistö Oy Arctic Centre (State Audit Office).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2003 the State Audit Office (report 64/2003) found that Arktikum-palvelu Oy, a private company set up when the building opened, sold the tickets to both exhibitions and kept the receipts, while public funds covered most of the cost of producing and running them.
- The audit found no documented agreement between the Arctic Centre and the museum on their joint exhibitions, and none between either of them and the company on buying its services.
- It called the handling of the ticket money "the most problematic thing in the audit" and asked the Arctic Centre, the museum, Kiinteistö Oy Arctic Centre and the businesses in the building to arrange their relations as good administration requires.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Arktikum is Finnish public money and visitor income, and carries no funder flag. Worth watching: who owns the building today.
