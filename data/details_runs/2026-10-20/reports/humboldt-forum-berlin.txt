HUMBOLDT FORUM: Berlin, Germany
ID: humboldt-forum-berlin
Website: https://www.humboldtforum.org

OVERVIEW
- Museum and cultural venue in the reconstructed Berlin Palace on Schlossplatz, next to Berlin Cathedral on the Spree, on Museum Island (Humboldt Forum website; Wikipedia).
- Operated and built by the Stiftung Humboldt Forum im Berliner Schloss, a foundation set up in 2009 by the federal government on a Bundestag resolution (Humboldt Forum website, in German).
- Named for Wilhelm and Alexander von Humboldt. It houses the non-European collections of the Berlin State Museums, the Ethnological Museum and the Museum of Asian Art, plus temporary exhibitions and events (Wikipedia).
- Building: the palace foundation stone was laid on 12 June 2013 and Franco Stella was the architect. The forum opened digitally on 16 December 2020, to visitors on 20 July 2021, and its eastern wing opened on 16 September 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Neil MacGregor was founding director until 2018; Hartmut Dorgerloh has been general director since June 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: since 3 October 2025 a single Humboldt Forum ticket covers the collection displays, temporary exhibitions, BERLIN GLOBAL and the current special exhibition. Children and young people up to 19 enter free, as do areas such as the Humboldt Lab and the staircase hall (Annual report 2025, in German).

FUNDING
Humboldt Forum: mainly public: federal government
- The Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media (BKM) funds the foundation, and a federal building ministry is also named as a funder (Humboldt Forum foundation page, in German).
- In 2025 the foundation received €57,016,068 from federal funds and other public funds: €53,510,258 in institutional grants and project funding and €3,505,810 in project funding for the construction (Annual report 2025, income statement).
- The same statement shows €3,999,155 in sales revenue and €265,993 from other third parties in 2025. In 2024 the figures were €64,695,990 from public funds, €4,777,489 in sales and €1,305,329 from third parties.
- Admission charges began on 3 October 2025. The annual report says public funds alone do not cover rising operating and programme costs, and that the foundation drew up its first fundraising and sponsorship strategy after reconstruction ended.
- The palace facade was funded by private donors. Medallions in the portals honour donors who gave more than €1 million (The Art Newspaper, Catherine Hickley, 5 November 2021).
- Hyperallergic gave the project's cost in December 2020 as €677 million.

Red flags: protest over colonial ties and cost
- December 2020: the Coalition of Cultural Workers Against Humboldt Forum protested on the opening day and demanded that the project be defunded and the money redirected to decolonisation work in Berlin's cultural institutions (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 16 December 2020).
- The article also cites the €100 million overrun on the 2013 reconstruction and an earlier protest in July 2020 against the gilded cross on top of the palace (Hyperallergic).
- Bénédicte Savoy resigned from the forum's advisory board in 2017 (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism in the art press centres on the forum's colonial links, its cost and its donors, and is aimed at the institution.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Artists and Cultural Workers Oppose Humboldt Forum Opening, Citing Colonial Ties" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/609318/humboldt-forum/
- Reports a protest by artists and cultural workers who call the museum a costly project with colonial roots and want its funding cut.
- The coalition's statement says engagement with the forum is "an injury to the world we wish to build" until the funding is redirected to decolonisation.
- Names Savoy, Nigeria's ambassador Yusuf Tuggar and the Benin Bronzes dispute.
2. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Humboldt Forum to remove medallion honouring far-right donor" (2021)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/11/05/humboldt-forum-to-remove-medallion-honouring-far-right-donor
- Reports that the forum will remove the medallion of the donor Ehrhardt Bödecker after the architectural theorist Philipp Oswalt set out some of his far-right and antisemitic statements in the Tagesspiegel in October 2021.
- The forum's statement called his views "militaristic, anti-democratic and in some respects, radically right-wing and antisemitic" and said it had not known them before; his son and daughter-in-law asked for the removal. Bödecker, a banker and historian who died in 2016, is honoured by one of the medallions for donors of more than €1 million toward the palace facade.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Humboldt Forum is public: the federal government funds the foundation, with €57 million in federal and other public funds in 2025 against about €4 million in sales. Private money paid for the palace facade, including Ehrhardt Bödecker, whose medallion the forum announced in November 2021 it would remove. The protests aimed at the forum concern its colonial links and its cost.
