SCHOOL OF VISUAL ARTS: New York, USA
ID: school-of-visual-arts-new-york
Website: https://sva.edu/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- Private art and design college in Manhattan, with its main address at 209 East 23rd Street, buildings in Gramercy Park and Chelsea, and a residence hall on Ludlow Street (SVA).
- Founded in 1947 by Silas H. Rhodes and Burne Hogarth as the Cartoonists and Illustrators School, with three teachers and 35 students, most of them veterans; renamed the School of Visual Arts in 1956 (Wikipedia).
- On 1 September 2025 ownership passed from the Rhodes family to the nonprofit SVA Alumni Society, part of a plan begun in 2019 to convert the school to a nonprofit (Wikipedia).
- On site: four galleries, among them the Chelsea Gallery and the Flatiron Gallery, plus the SVA Theatre at 333 West 23rd Street and a library with the Milton Glaser Design Study Center and Archives.
- Visiting: gallery hours are not listed on the homepage; the school directs visitors to its contact page.

FUNDING
School of Visual Arts: tuition-funded: student fees, with no sponsor list published
- Annual budget of about 200 million dollars, heavily dependent on tuition; full-time tuition for the coming year is 55,270 dollars (USA Art News, 11 August 2026).
- The school had been for-profit and owned by the Rhodes family for nearly eight decades before the 2025 transfer to the SVA Alumni Society (USA Art News).
- Structural deficit of 22 million dollars, which interim president Christopher J. Cyphers called the largest in the school's history; enrolment is down about 34% from its 2019 peak (USA Art News).
- No reporting found of a sale price or terms for the transfer, of a donor list, or of annual accounts under the school's own name.
- No reporting found of any donation from Jeffrey Epstein to the school.

Red flags: David A. Ross and Jeffrey Epstein, Epstein-linked
- David A. Ross chaired the MFA Art Practice department from 2009 and resigned effective 3 February 2026, after the US Justice Department released his emails with Epstein (The Art Newspaper, 10 February 2026; Hyperallergic, 3 February 2026).
- October 2009: Epstein proposed an exhibition involving images of minors, which he suggested titling "statutory", and Ross replied enthusiastically (Hyperallergic, Inside Higher Ed). Ross says he was naive and sought Epstein's funding for post-museum projects, which Epstein never gave.
- A July 2009 email says Ross had also been hired as special assistant to the school's president (Hyperallergic). Ross says he had no fundraising role at SVA. No page opened shows an Epstein donation to the school.
- SVA said it was aware of the correspondence and accepted his resignation; students and alumni had called for him to go (The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).

Red flags: student sit-in over divestment
- 2 May 2024: about 20 students led by the SVA chapter of Students for Justice in Palestine occupied the ground-floor administrative offices at 344 East 24th Street, demanding the school divest from Israeli weapons manufacturers and end its partnership with Hillel International. By 5 pm four remained, and administrators let them stay until Monday 6 May (Hyperallergic, 2 May 2024).
- A school spokesperson said SVA is "committed to supporting free speech and engaged citizenry"; two police officers came for a "wellness check" (Hyperallergic).

BOTTOM LINE
The school's money is tuition, and no corporate or donor list is published. The weak points are the Epstein emails of a department chair who left in February 2026, the 2 May 2024 divestment sit-in, and a 22 million dollar deficit. Watch the nonprofit's first filings for donors and trustees.
