WORDIE HOUSE (BASE F): Winter Island, Antarctica
ID: wordie-house-winter-island
Website: https://ukaht.org/places/wordie-house/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Small British base hut on Winter Island in the Argentine Islands, Wilhelm Archipelago, off the west coast of Graham Land (UKAHT).
- The first hut, built in 1935 for the British Graham Land Expedition, was destroyed in 1946 by a suspected tsunami. The present hut went up in 1947 from repurposed corrugated tin, timber and packing crates, was used until 1954 and was briefly reoccupied in the winter of 1960 (UKAHT).
- Named after the Scottish geologist James Wordie, chief scientist on Shackleton's Endurance expedition of 1914-17 and president of the Royal Geographical Society from 1951 to 1954 (UKAHT).
- It started one of Antarctica's longest climate records. When the hut closed in 1954 the work moved to the Faraday base on nearby Galindez Island (UKAHT), which the UK sold to Ukraine for the symbolic sum of one pound in February 1996 and which now runs as Vernadsky station (Wikipedia).
- Historic Site and Monument no. 62 since 1995. Around 500 original artefacts remain, including a stove and period food in the kitchen, a workshop, a darkroom and the base leader's office (UKAHT).
- In the 2026-27 season the Trust is sending a team to the hut for the first time: specialists led by former Port Lockroy base leader Lisa Ford, with conservator Sophie Rowe and engineer Maggie Cook, will make the first full survey of its condition and structure and map it digitally. Chief executive Camilla Nichol is joining them (PA in The Irish News, 12 October 2026).

FUNDING
Wordie House: charity-run, paid from the UK Antarctic Heritage Trust's general funds, which come mostly from its shops
- Operator: the UK Antarctic Heritage Trust (charity no. 1160847). The Trust's own pages differ on the start date: its page for the hut says it has managed Wordie House since 2014, its 2020 history of the hut says the hut has been its responsibility since 2009 (UKAHT).
- Trust income in the year to 30 April 2025 was £1,645,353, 56% of it trading (shop turnover of £927,850) and the rest donations, membership, grants and events. Of £1,689,840 spent, 24% went on conservation of its Peninsula sites, which include this hut (2024-25 accounts).
- Its major partners are the British Antarctic Survey, the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, the Natural Environment Research Council and the Government of the British Antarctic Territory (2024-25 report).
- As statutory heritage adviser to the Foreign Office and the territory's government, the Trust received £50,000 in 2024-25 as the first instalment of a three-year agreement, starting with a conservation plan for the wreck of Endurance (2024-25 accounts).
- Staff of Ukraine's Vernadsky station help preserve the hut (UKAHT). The station is run by the National Antarctic Scientific Center of Ukraine (Wikipedia).
- Initial conservation and weatherproofing of the hut are complete (UKAHT, 2020).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Wordie House is clean. The weak point is that the hut earns nothing itself and depends on what the Trust raises elsewhere. Worth watching: the repair bill that follows the survey.
