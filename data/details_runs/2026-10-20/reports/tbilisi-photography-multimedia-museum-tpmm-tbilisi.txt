TBILISI PHOTOGRAPHY & MULTIMEDIA MUSEUM (TPMM): Tbilisi, Georgia
ID: tbilisi-photography-multimedia-museum-tpmm-tbilisi
Website: https://tpmm.ge/en/museum/

OVERVIEW
- Non-governmental, not-for-profit museum dedicated to the contemporary image, at 14 M. Kostava Street, Tbilisi; it calls itself the first institution in Georgia of that kind (stated by the space).
- Nestan Nijaradze co-founded the non-profit in 2017 with a group of women professionals (Freunde von Freunden, Ann-Christin Schubert, undated); it opened in 2019 (Equal Times, Clément Girardot, 24 October 2024).
- Housed at Stamba, a former Soviet-era publishing house that became a boutique hotel in 2018 (stated by the space). Freunde von Freunden describes a 950 square metre industrial-style exhibition space that grew out of the Tbilisi Photo Festival.
- Programmes: "exhibitions, discussions and educational programmes", including the South Caucasus Hub for Education and Innovation, its leading project (stated by the space).
- Visiting: open Monday to Friday 12:00 to 19:00 and weekends 14:00 to 20:00; entrance is free (stated by the space).

FUNDING
TPMM: donor-funded non-profit: Swiss development agency, with space provided by Adjara Group
- Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation (SDC) funds the South Caucasus Hub for Education and Innovation, the museum's leading project (stated by the space). Freunde von Freunden says the Swiss cooperation agency of the South Caucasus covers the museum's main activities.
- Adjara Group, which includes Stamba Hotel, provides the space (Freunde von Freunden). The museum's site also names Stamba and Adjara Group among its donors and partners.
- The site's donors and partners list also names the Prince Claus Fund, OSGF, MG Law and MagiC Carpets (stated by the space).
- Adjara Group is described by Investor.ge (Hans Gutbrod, 14 July 2026) as Temur Ugulava's corporate entity. The same article says his "success was built on wealth earned through Adjarabet" and that "He sold his stake in Adjarabet in 2019." Georgia Today reported on 22 June 2026 that he had died at 56 and that he founded Adjara Group, under which Rooms Hotels and Stamba Hotel were created.
- Equal Times describes the museum's building, a disused former Soviet building in central Tbilisi, as "refurbished by a patron" and does not name the patron.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Georgia's Law on Transparency of Foreign Influence passed its third reading on 14 May 2024, and parliament overrode the president's veto on 28 May 2024; it requires civil society and media organisations that receive more than 20 per cent of their annual revenue from abroad to register as "implementing organisations of foreign power interests" (ECNL, 24 May 2024).
- Equal Times (Clément Girardot, 24 October 2024) says the government introduced the measure, which it calls a law requiring NGOs to declare themselves "foreign agents" if they receive funding from outside Georgia, after several weeks of mass protests, and that it was inspired by a similar Russian law.
- Connection to TPMM: Nijaradze told Equal Times the law "has created administrative difficulties" for the museum and that it set up a new NGO in Lithuania "in a more secure legal environment". She said she fears "a return to censorship", linked in the article to a 17 September law banning "LGBT propaganda".

BOTTOM LINE
TPMM is funded by international donors, with the Swiss development agency SDC as its main named funder, and it occupies space provided by Adjara Group at Stamba. Adjara Group's founder Temur Ugulava earned wealth through Adjarabet, a stake he sold in 2019 (Investor.ge).
