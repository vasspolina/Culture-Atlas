KUINDZHI ART MUSEUM: Mariupol, Ukraine
ID: kuindzhi-art-museum
Website: No site of the museum found (checked 2026-10-24; the Atlas record lists none, and searches returned only Wikipedia, Bellingcat, Hyperallergic, Moscow Times and Justice Info pages)

OVERVIEW
- Museum dedicated to the painter Arkhip Kuindzhi, born in Mariupol; Bellingcat places it a couple of blocks from the destroyed Drama Theatre.
- Opened on 30 October 2010 as a branch of the Mariupol Museum of Local Lore; the idea was first proposed in 1914, the premises were donated in 1997 and renovation began in 2008 (Wikipedia). Tatiana Buli is listed as director (Wikipedia).
- Housed in a building put up in 1902 as a wedding gift for Valentina Gadzinova, wife of the founder of the Real School (Wikipedia).
- Collection: about 650 paintings, 960 graphic works, 150 sculptures and over 300 decorative objects, with works by Aivazovsky, Vereshchagin and Shishkin (Wikipedia).
- Damage: on 23 March 2022 Mariupol's city council said a Russian bomb had destroyed the museum; Bellingcat's satellite imagery of 24 April shows the building damaged, not razed, and Bellingcat could not identify the weapon or establish who was responsible. Wikipedia says the exhibition had been moved to the basement and lists the museum as dissolved in 2022.
- Bellingcat saw a handwritten sign at the door placing the building under the "Ministry of Culture" of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic (DNR).
- No reporting found of any visiting information since 2022.

FUNDING
Kuindzhi Art Museum: state-run: a municipal branch museum until 2022, then under the DNR culture ministry
- Until 2022 it was a branch of the city's Museum of Local Lore (Wikipedia). No reporting found of its budget or donors.
- Under occupation, Bellingcat reports only the sign naming the DNR "Ministry of Culture"; no reporting found of a budget, accounts or board.
- Occupation authorities set up an open-air exhibition in a central Mariupol park showing digital replicas of the stolen works (Justice Info, 2025).

Red flags: sanctions on the occupying body, DNR
- The DNR is on the sanctions lists of the United States (under Executive Orders 13660 and 14065), the EU (Council Regulation 269/2014), the UK and Canada, all taking effect in July 2014 (Black Sea News sanctions database).
- The Atlas record says the museum is run by the DNR culture ministry; the only source opened for that is the sign Bellingcat saw at the door in 2022.

Red flags: removal of the paintings to Donetsk
- Lina Garmash of the Donetsk History Museum said 25 paintings from Mariupol, including three Kuindzhis, are in storage there (The Moscow Times).
- Alexander Mozgovoi, a DNR television anchor, said the works were taken to Donetsk for safekeeping and "Nothing has been stolen" (The Moscow Times).
- Unverified: that Natalia Kapustnikova of the Museum of Local Lore handed the paintings to the occupiers, from Mariupol mayoral adviser Petro Andriushchenko, a Ukrainian official (Bellingcat, Hyperallergic). In June 2023 the EU sanctioned her for handing over the Kuindzhi museum's works (Justice Info); Kapustnikova told Izvestiya the paintings were safe (Bellingcat).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Justice Info (Shahida Tulaganova, 2025) reports that over 2,000 artefacts were removed from Mariupol within days to temporary storage in Donetsk, and that the EU sanctioned four museum directors in June 2023 for roles in transfers of Ukrainian collections.
- The Kuindzhi museum's paintings are among the works the EU decision lists as transferred to the Local History Museum of Donetsk.

BOTTOM LINE
There is no private funder to assess: the museum was a city branch until 2022 and now stands, damaged, under a body the US, EU, UK and Canada sanction. The weak points are the removal of its Kuindzhi paintings to Donetsk and the lack of any published budget or governance. Worth watching: whether the paintings return to Ukraine.
