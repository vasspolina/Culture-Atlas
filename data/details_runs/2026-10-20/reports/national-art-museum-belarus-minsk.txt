NATIONAL ART MUSEUM OF THE REPUBLIC OF BELARUS: Minsk, Belarus

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in Minsk, the largest art museum in Belarus, with more than 30,000 works in about 20 collections, mainly Belarusian art (Wikipedia).
- Founded as the State Art Gallery on 24 January 1939 by resolution of the Council of People's Commissars of Belarus, in 15 halls of a former girls' gymnasium; nearly all of its roughly 2,711 early works were lost in the Second World War. Renamed the National Art Museum in 1993 (Wikipedia).
- Early holdings came partly from the Vitebsk Museum of Modern Art (1919 to 1925) (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Uladzimir Prakaptsou (1998 to 2023), then Ganna Konanava (2023 to present) (Wikipedia).
- From April 2026 it shows Marc Chagall's "Stroll" ("Progulka") on loan from the State Russian Museum, St Petersburg, extended to October 2026 after talks between the Belarusian and Russian prime ministers (Nasha Niva, 7 July 2026).
- No reporting found of the building's architect, opening hours, admission or accessibility in sources that could be opened.

FUNDING
National Art Museum of the Republic of Belarus: public: a state museum, with a past partnership with Belgazprombank
- No reporting found of the museum's budget, accounts or a sponsor list.
- Chagall exhibition: no source opened names a sponsor. The Nasha Niva article names none, and searches found no report of Sber Bank as sponsor.
- Belgazprombank: its Art-Belarus project, launched under a public-private agreement with the Ministry of Culture signed on 17 June 2015, works with the museum and hosted "Ten Centuries of Belarusian Art" there from 27 March to 10 July 2014 (Art-Belarus site). In July 2012 the bank gave the museum two Chagall prints, "Vision of the Apocalypse" and "The Way of the Cross" (myfin.by). The bank's "Autumn Salon" is a sales show for young artists run with the Belarusian Union of Artists (Art-Belarus site); a search result places recent editions at the Palace of Arts, not this museum.
- Belgazprombank ownership in 2020: Gazprom and Gazprombank held 49.66% each (search excerpt of Interfax and Prime Press, not opened).

Red flags: staff dismissed over a political poem, 2020
- July 2020: guide Nikita Monich left the museum after publishing a poem addressed to Lukashenko about the seizure of about 150 paintings from Belgazprombank's collection; the director asked him to remove it so as not to "embarrass the museum" before the election, he refused and was dismissed (Current Time, 13 July 2020).
- Context: Viktor Babariko, who led Belgazprombank, was detained on 18 June 2020 on charges he called politically motivated (search excerpt, not opened).
- Zerkalo reports 18 people were dismissed from the museum on 23 December 2022 (search excerpt; page returned 403). No reasons are stated in the excerpt.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is Belarusian state money, and no source opened shows a sponsor for the current Chagall show. The weak points are the museum's place in a state where staff were dismissed for political speech in 2020, its earlier partnership with the Gazprom-owned Belgazprombank, and the lack of any published budget or sponsor list. Worth watching: who sponsors the Chagall loan and whether the museum publishes accounts.
