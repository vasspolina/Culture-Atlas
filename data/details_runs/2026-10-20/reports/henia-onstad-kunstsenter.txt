HENIE ONSTAD KUNSTSENTER: Høvikodden, Norway
ID: henia-onstad-kunstsenter
Website: https://hok.no

OVERVIEW
- Art centre on a headland at Høvikodden in Bærum, on the Oslofjord, about 10 km southwest of Oslo (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the figure skater Sonja Henie and the shipping owner and collector Niels Onstad, who set up foundations in 1961 and gave their collection together with funds for a building and its running; it opened to the public on 23 August 1968 (Store norske leksikon, Norwegian Wikipedia).
- Named for its two founders.
- Jon Eikvar and Svein Erik Engebretsen won the 1964 architectural competition for the building, their first commission; it uses glass, stone, exposed concrete and copper. The building was expanded in 1994 (Store norske leksikon, Norwegian Wikipedia).
- On site: permanent exhibitions including Yayoi Kusama's "Hymn of Life", "Merz! Flux! Pop!" on Kurt Schwitters and later avant-garde artists, and a room on Sonja Henie; temporary exhibitions; a sculpture park of about 30 works; and a café. The collection holds about 8,000 works (hok.no, Norwegian Wikipedia, Store norske leksikon).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday to Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, Thursday 11:00 to 20:00, closed Monday. Wheelchair access is good throughout the building (hok.no).
- Director: Anne Hilde Neset (Wikipedia, Store norske leksikon).

FUNDING
Henie Onstad Kunstsenter: founders' gift with public and bank-foundation money: Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Bærum municipality, Akershus County Municipality, the Norwegian state and the Wilhelmsen family
- The founders' 1961 gift of their collection and of funds for construction and operation created the centre (Store norske leksikon).
- A workshop building due to open in summer 2026 is fully funded by Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Bærum municipality, Akershus County Municipality and the Norwegian state budget (Wikipedia).
- Sparebankstiftelsen DNB has deposited the Kurt Schwitters collection at the centre since 2009 (Store norske leksikon).
- The Lise Wilhelmsen Art Award, a prize of $100,000, is a partnership between the Arne Wilhelmsen family and the centre. It comes with an exhibition, a catalogue and an acquisition budget for the Henie Onstad Collection. Recipients: Otobong Nkanga (2019), Guadalupe Maravilla (2021), Alia Farid (2023), Lap-See Lam (2025). Lise Wilhelmsen (1936 to 2019) bought art for ships of the Wilhelmsen group (hok.no).

BOTTOM LINE
The centre began as a private gift from Sonja Henie and Niels Onstad. Its documented money is the 2026 workshop funding from Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Bærum municipality, Akershus County Municipality and the Norwegian state, and the $100,000 Lise Wilhelmsen Art Award in partnership with the Wilhelmsen family.
