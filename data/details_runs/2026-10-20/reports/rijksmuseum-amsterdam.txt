RIJKSMUSEUM: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: rijksmuseum-amsterdam
Website: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/ (checked 2026-10-24; working)

OVERVIEW
- The Netherlands' national art and history museum, at Museumstraat 1 in Amsterdam.
- Founded in The Hague on 19 November 1798 on the proposal of Finance Minister Isaac Gogel, opened as the National Art Gallery in 1800 and moved to Amsterdam in 1808 (Wikipedia).
- Its collection was built through purchase and donation, not from a royal collection (Wikipedia).
- Building: Pierre Cuypers won the 1876 design contest and the building opened on 13 July 1885; the 2003 to 2013 renovation by Spanish architects Antonio Cruz and Antonio Ortiz cost 375 million euros, and the same firm designed the Asian pavilion (Wikipedia).
- On site: the main galleries, the Rijks restaurant (one Michelin star since 2017), a research library, gardens with annual sculpture exhibitions, and a satellite branch at Schiphol airport.
- Visiting: open daily 9 to 17h, including holidays; adults pay 25 euros, under-18s are free, CJP and EYCA holders pay 12.50 euros, and Museumkaart and Friends holders enter free; tickets are sold online with a booked time. Wheelchair and mobility scooter accessible, with lifts on all floors.

FUNDING
Rijksmuseum: mainly public with corporate and private partners: Dutch state, KPN, ING, VriendenLoterij and many funds
- The Ministry of Education, Culture and Science is listed as the museum's founder; no figure for the state grant was found in the sources opened.
- Hart van Nederland (2023) states that the museum depends on partners, funds and private donations for a third of its income.
- ING: main sponsor since 2005 of the museum and Rijksmuseum Schiphol; it has funded an online programme since 2020, plus a podcast series and digital courses and tours (stated by the museum). No amount is published. Bénédicte Ficq, handing over a petition, estimated ING's share at under 1% of the museum's funding (Banken.nl, 2024); XR put it at under 0.6% of income (NL Times, 2024).
- KPN: main sponsor since the 2013 reopening, giving annual funding plus telecom, data and ICT services and the museum app technology (stated by the museum); no amount published.
- VriendenLoterij: partner since 1998; with the Mauritshuis, Van Gogh Museum and Kröller-Müller it gives more than 8 million euros a year combined for acquisitions (stated by the museum).
- Anonymous donor: 12.5 million euros in 2023 for the annual garden sculpture exhibitions for ten years; this was the largest private monetary gift the museum had received (Hart van Nederland).
- Project funders named by the museum include AkzoNobel (Night Watch varnish removal), Ammodo, Bagri Foundation, Bank of America Foundation, Mellon Foundation, Getty Foundation, Mondriaan Fonds, Fonds 21, Bloomberg Philanthropies (Collection Online), CHANEL and Pon (Lee Ufan exhibition).
- No reporting found of a published breakdown of the museum's income by source.

Red flags: ING sponsorship and fossil fuel finance
- Extinction Rebellion (XR) calls ING the largest fossil-fuel financier in the Netherlands and demands the Rijksmuseum end the sponsorship (XR press release, 14 March 2024; XR is the campaigner).
- 16 February 2024: XR activists disrupted the opening of the Frans Hals exhibition, placing altered replicas beside the paintings (NL Times).
- 17 May 2024: Fossielvrij NL announced it would hand the museum a petition, "Rijks zegt NEE", with more than 5,400 signatures (Banken.nl).
- 7 September 2024: XR chained itself to the fences of the tunnel leading to the entrance and the museum closed (NL Times).
- ING's reply: the economy still needs oil and gas, the activists' approach toward the museum is "unacceptable" (translation), and they should contact the bank directly (Banken.nl).
- No reporting found of a response from the Rijksmuseum in the pieces opened.

Red flags: Saudi Aramco, a Gulf state body
- Fossil Free Culture Netherlands states that the Rijksmuseum ended a partnership with Saudi Aramco in 2017 (Museums Association, 2020). The museum did not respond to the Museums Association and the article does not confirm the claim.
- No reporting found of a protest at the museum over Aramco, or of the reason the partnership ended.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's funding in art media; the sponsorship protest was covered in Dutch general news. The one art-press piece found criticises the museum's treatment of its own history.
1. The Art Newspaper: Carine Zaayman, "The Big Review: Slavery at the Rijksmuseum" (2021)
URL: https://theartnewspaper.com/2021/07/09/the-big-review-slavery-at-the-rijksmuseum
- Praises the show's structure around ten people's stories, and calls it a first step.
- Argues it does not examine the conventions of museum display and largely ignores the Rijksmuseum's own role in maintaining imperialism, treating the institution as "made new, unsullied".
- Does not mention funders or sponsors.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is mostly state-funded, with about a third of its income from partners, funds and private gifts. The weak points are the ING sponsorship, which XR and Fossielvrij NL have protested since 2024, and the 2017 end of Saudi Aramco's partnership, a claim from an activist group that the museum has not answered. Watch whether ING stays a main partner and whether the museum publishes its income by source.
