Red flags: 2024 to 2025 court challenge over demolishing 142 Free Street
- Greater Portland Landmarks' complaint alleged that city councilors failed to consider Historic Preservation Land Use standards, such as the building's architectural significance, when they voted in May 2024 to declassify 142 Free Street as non-contributing, and asked the Superior Court to vacate the order and send the matter back to the City Council (Maine Public, Carol Bousquet, 20 June 2024).
- The museum argued that alterations over time had diminished the building's historical significance. Director Mark Bessire said: "This appeal questions the judgment of our elected officials and will also impose a significant financial and human resource burden on the City of Portland" (Maine Public, 20 June 2024).
- A Cumberland County Superior Court judge rejected the appeal in late March 2025, siding with the museum (Maine Public, Nicole Ogrysko and Madi Smith, 31 March 2025).
- Kate Lemos McHale, executive director of Greater Portland Landmarks, said she supports the expansion and said: "It doesn't need to come down to a choice between new and old" (Maine Public, 31 March 2025).
