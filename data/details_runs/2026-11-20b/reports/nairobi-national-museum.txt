NAIROBI NATIONAL MUSEUM: Nairobi, Kenya
ID: nairobi-national-museum
Website: https://www.museums.or.ke/nairobi-national-museum/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Kenya's national museum on Museum Hill, 20 minutes from the city centre, run by the state corporation National Museums of Kenya (NMK) (museum site; Daily Nation).
- Initiated in 1910 by naturalists of the East Africa and Uganda Natural History Society to keep their specimens; it first stood on the site of today's Nyayo House and, from 1922, where the Nairobi Serena Hotel now stands (museum site).
- The colonial government set aside land on Museum Hill in 1929; the building opened on 22 September 1930 as the Coryndon Museum, named for Sir Robert Coryndon, a governor of Kenya, and was renamed after independence in 1963 (museum site; The Standard).
- Closed on 15 October 2005 for refurbishment and expansion; President Mwai Kibaki reopened it as the Nairobi National Museum on 14 July 2008. Triad Architects Limited designed a visitors' centre with a ticket office, cafés and shops as part of the master plan (The Standard, Wangeci Kanyeki, 2013).
- Permanent galleries: Cradle of Humankind, Story of Mammals, History of Kenya, Cycles of Life, a numismatic exhibition and an Asian African Heritage exhibition, plus temporary galleries, shops, restaurants and botanical gardens (museum site). In 2024 the director general also listed Contemporary Art Galleries (The Standard).
- Visiting: open daily 8:30am to 5:30pm, including weekends and public holidays; booked night tours for groups of 10 or more run 6pm to 10pm (museum site). From 7 May 2026 Kenyan and East African adults pay KSh 350 and children KSh 200; residents of the rest of Africa $9 and $6; visitors from outside Africa $18 and $9 (The Star).

FUNDING
Nairobi National Museum: mainly public: Kenya's state corporation for museums, with an EU-financed rebuild and German money for one exhibition
- NMK, the state corporation that runs it, depends on creditors and government grants for its day-to-day operations (Daily Nation, 12 December 2022, on the Auditor-General's 2020/21 audit).
- 2005 to 2008 refurbishment and expansion: Sh800 million from the European Union under the National Museums of Kenya Support Programme (The Standard, 2013).
- Ticket income: NMK raised entry fees across its sites on 7 May 2026; visitors from outside East Africa now pay in US dollars at most of them (The Star).
- Night tours: launched on 13 September 2024 in partnership with CityBlue Hotels, whose founder Jameel Verjee said the company was honoured to support NMK's mission; NMK also hires out the museum for concerts, conferences, corporate launches and weddings (The Standard, 13 September 2024). No amounts were stated.
- Planet Africa, a German archaeology exhibition, opened at the museum in November 2024 and ran until the end of May 2025. It was funded by Germany's Federal Foreign Office, the German Research Foundation (DFG) and the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, and built on the DFG-funded research programme Entangled Africa of the German Archaeological Institute; no amounts were stated (IDW, German Archaeological Institute release; deutschland.de).

Red flags: Auditor-General's 2020/21 audit of National Museums of Kenya
- Auditor-General Nancy Gathungu found that NMK could not meet its current obligations as they fell due: current liabilities of Sh276.85 million against assets of Sh137.68 million, a negative working capital of Sh139.17 million (Daily Nation, 12 December 2022).
- NMK recorded a deficit of Sh434.66 million, which took its revenue reserves from Sh12.94 million to negative Sh421.72 million; the audit said this position had not been disclosed in the financial statements (Daily Nation).
- The same audit questioned value for money on two NMK works at Fort Jesus in Mombasa: a Sh226.02 million project that had stalled after Sh108.37 million was paid to the contractor, and a Sh59.35 million seawall repair whose pavement had cracked (Daily Nation).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critical piece, in the Kenyan business press rather than the art press, about the 2017 removal of three artists' work.
1. Business Daily (Nation Media Group): Margaretta wa Gacheru, "Museum bans works by three of Kenya's finest artists" (2017)
URL: https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/lifestyle/art/museum-bans-works-by-three-of-kenya-s-finest-artists-2152656
- Says the works of Patrick Mukabi, Michael Soi and Bertiers Mbatia were "censored by the management of the National Museums of Kenya" the day before the opening, and calls the decision "contradictory to say the least" given the day's theme; on social media it was called "hypocritical".
- Points to a breakdown in communication: the museum had asked all three artists to make work for the day.

WIDER CONTEXT
- May 2017: NMK management pulled works by Michael Soi, Patrick Mukabi and Bertiers Mbatia from an exhibition due to open on International Museum Day, whose theme was "Speaking the Unspeakable in Museums", judging the art "inappropriate" for children; curator Lydia Galavu said the art had been packed away (Business Daily, 18 May 2017).
- The removal took place at this museum, by the state corporation that runs it.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public, Kenyan and European, with no flagged funder. The weak points are the state corporation's precarious finances and its readiness to remove artists' work; whether NMK publishes recent audited accounts is worth watching.
