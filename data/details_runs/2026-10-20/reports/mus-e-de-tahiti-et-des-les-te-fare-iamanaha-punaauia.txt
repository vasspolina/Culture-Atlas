MUSÉE DE TAHITI ET DES ÎLES, TE FARE IAMANAHA: Punaauia, French Polynesia
ID: mus-e-de-tahiti-et-des-les-te-fare-iamanaha-punaauia
Website: https://www.museetahiti.pf

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the natural and cultural heritage of Oceania, especially Polynesia, at Nu'uroa point in Punaauia, in a park of about three hectares with an ethnobotanical garden (museum website).
- French Wikipedia puts it about 15 km southwest of Papeete, on a plot facing the Tahiti lagoon.
- A museum created in Papeete in 1917 by the Société des Études océaniennes was merged into it. The government bought the site in 1967, the Territorial Assembly voted the creation in 1974, and the museum opened in the 1970s; French Wikipedia gives 30 May 1977 for the inauguration.
- The Nu'uroa headland was a major ancient ritual site, and London Missionary Society missionaries settled at Burder's Point in 1819 (museum website).
- It became a public administrative establishment (établissement public à caractère administratif) in November 2000 (French Wikipedia).
- The permanent hall was rebuilt from 2020: Edouard Fritch laid the first stone, exhibition space grew from 900 m2 to 1,400 m2, and the architects were a group led by Pierre-Jean Picart with scenographer Adrien Gardère (Tahiti Infos, 21 August 2020).
- The renovated museum reopened on 4 March 2023 under the name Te Fare Iamanaha, with loans from the Musée du Quai Branly, the British Museum and Cambridge's Museum of Archaeology and Anthropology (French Wikipedia).
- The British Museum lent the sandalwood sculpture A'a from Rurutu for three years, along with five other items (usaartnews, Romario, 12 April 2023).
- Collections hold about 30,000 objects, including about 13,000 herbarium sheets, 11,500 ethnographic objects and 5,500 art objects (French Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 9:00 to 17:00; Tuesday 8:00 to 16:00 is listed as open to schools (museum website). A friends' association, Amis du Musée, is linked from the museum website.

FUNDING
Musée de Tahiti et des Îles: mainly public: the government of French Polynesia, with State and Pays money for the renovation
- The museum is a public establishment of French Polynesia, created by a vote of the Territorial Assembly (French Wikipedia).
- The renovation cost 844 million XPF, financed through the Contrat de projets, with project management delegated to Grands Projets de Polynésie (Tahiti Infos, "La rénovation du musée de Tahiti et des îles est lancée", 21 August 2020).
- In May 2021 a State and Pays steering committee approved 16 projects worth 5.5 billion XPF (about 46.09 million euros), funded half by the State and half by the Pays, including the museum's extension with a gallery for contemporary art, under the contrat de développement et de transformation 2021 to 2023 (Outremers360, Jean-Tenahe Faatau, 2 May 2021).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: it is a French Polynesian public establishment whose renovation, announced in 2020 at 844 million XPF, was financed through the Contrat de projets, and whose extension with a contemporary art gallery was approved in 2021 for State and Pays funding in equal halves.
