MERCER UNION: Toronto, Canada
ID: mercer-union-toronto
Website: https://www.mercerunion.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit, artist-run centre for contemporary art at 1286 Bloor Street West, Toronto, one block east of Lansdowne subway station (Mercer Union).
- Founded in 1979 by twelve artists who each put in $1,500 to buy the lease on an 1,800 square foot space at 29 Mercer Street, which gave the centre its name (Mercer Union).
- Moves: 333 Adelaide Street West in 1981, opening to the public in January 1982, 439 King Street West in 1994, 37 Lisgar Street in 1999 and Bloor Street West in 2008 (Mercer Union).
- Its stated mission is to commission and produce new works of contemporary art (Mercer Union).
- Wikipedia reports that in December 1993 the Morality Bureau of the Metropolitan Toronto Police raided the gallery and seized works by Eli Langer; Langer and the gallery's director were first charged under the Criminal Code's child pornography provisions, the Crown withdrew those charges and prosecuted the paintings instead, and the works were later ruled legal.
- Open Tuesday to Saturday, 11am to 6pm. The gallery is at street level with unobstructed pathways and an accessible ground-floor washroom; the entrance and washroom have no automatic doors, and service animals and support persons are welcome (Mercer Union).

FUNDING
Mercer Union: public arts councils, with TD Bank Group, a brewery and named individual donors
- Government funders listed: the Canada Council for the Arts, the Ontario Arts Council and the Government of Ontario, and the City of Toronto through the Toronto Arts Council (Mercer Union support page).
- TD Bank Group: "Leading Support" for the TD Communications & Engagement Assistant post (Mercer Union support page).
- Burdock Brewery is the beverage sponsor; the Ontario Trillium Foundation supported the website through its Resilient Communities Fund in 2020 (Mercer Union support page).
- Individual supporters listed by name include Femi and Aminat Adeyinka, Paul and Kaye Beeston, Pascal Desjardins and Roxanne Arsenault, Liza Mauer and Andrew Sheiner, David and Erica Neligan, and Jad and Roula Shimaly. Donors acknowledged as Legacy Donors gave "substantial support" tied to the 40th anniversary campaign (Mercer Union support page).
- Board of eleven: Marina Shepaksov (President), Emma Steen (Vice President), Voke Odibo (Treasurer), Amanda Pajak (Secretary), Femi Adeyinka, Sarah Heim, Christie Kirchner, Kahira Ngige, Philip Leonard Ocampo, Erica Stocking and Yue Yin (Mercer Union).

Red flags: TD Bank Group, a bank that pleaded guilty to money laundering
- On 10 October 2024 TD Bank N.A. pleaded guilty to conspiring to fail to maintain an anti-money laundering programme that complies with the Bank Secrecy Act, to fail to file accurate Currency Transaction Reports and to launder money; TD Bank US Holding Company pleaded guilty to causing the failures (US Justice Department release, reproduced by the IRS).
- The Justice Department resolution totalled $1,886,945,780.40, made up of $452,432,302.00 in forfeiture and a $1,434,513,478.40 criminal fine, and the bank must retain an independent compliance monitor for three years (Justice Department release, via IRS).
- American Banker (Catherine Leffert, 10 October 2024, updated 18 October 2024) reported that TD agreed to pay $3.09 billion in fines in total across the Justice Department and FinCEN, and that its US assets were limited to about $434 billion under the agreement with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency.
- Connection: Mercer Union's support page names TD Bank Group as giving "Leading Support" for the TD Communications & Engagement Assistant post. The pleas were entered by TD's US bank and its US holding company.

BOTTOM LINE
Mercer Union is an artist-run centre funded by the Canada Council, the Ontario Arts Council and the Toronto Arts Council, with named individual donors and two sponsors. The flagged name is TD Bank Group, which gives Leading Support for a staff post at the centre and whose US bank and holding company pleaded guilty on 10 October 2024, the bank to money laundering conspiracy.
