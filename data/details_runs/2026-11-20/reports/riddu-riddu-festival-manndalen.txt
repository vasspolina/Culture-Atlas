RIDDU RIĐĐU: Manndalen, Norway
ID: riddu-riddu-festival-manndalen
Website: https://riddu.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- An annual international Indigenous music and culture festival in Olmmáivággi (Manndalen) in Gáivuotna (Kåfjord) Municipality, Nord-Troms; the North Sámi name means "small storm on the coast" (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- The Sámi youth group Gáivuona Sámenuorat started it in 1991 to make Sámi and other Indigenous culture and identity visible (Norwegian Wikipedia), at a time when almost all of Manndalen's formerly Sámi majority considered themselves Norwegian (English Wikipedia).
- Since 1 January 2010 it has been organised by the company Riddu Riđđu Festivála AS, before that by the association Riddu Riđđu searvi; the company had 11 employees in November 2025 (Norwegian Wikipedia). Its address is the Centre for Northern Peoples in Manndalen (Riddu Riđđu).
- The programme mixes concerts, theatre, seminars, courses, a children's festival, art, film, youth and literature events (Norwegian Wikipedia); each year it honours a "Northern People of the Year" (Kritiikin Uutiset).
- The 2025 festival drew 6,149 visitors and included a debate between the two leading candidates in that year's Sámi Parliament election and the commissioned work "The Arctic is not for sale" (Riddu Riđđu). Festival director then was Sajje Solbakk (Riddu Riđđu); Erle Bårdsdatter Sæther holds the post for 2026 to 2030 (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- The next festival runs from 7 to 11 July 2027 (Riddu Riđđu).

FUNDING
Riddu Riđđu: mainly public: the Sámi Parliament, Arts Council Norway, the Ministry of Culture, Troms County and Kåfjord Municipality
- The company is owned by about 120 year-round crew members, who work alongside about 200 festival volunteers (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- The festival's site lists as partners, without amounts: Kåfjord Municipality, Troms County, the Sámi Parliament, Arts Council Norway, Talent Norge, the Ministry of Culture, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, UiT The Arctic University of Norway, Coop Nord, Lásságámmi, the Centre for Northern Peoples, Visit Lyngenfjord, Noereh, Suoma Sámi Nuorat and Ramsalt (Riddu Riđđu, checked 2026-11-20).
- The Sámi Parliament's direct festival grant was NOK 1,667,000 for 2025, raised by a permanent NOK 56,000 to NOK 1,723,000 in its revised budget of March 2025 (Sámi Parliament).
- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs granted NOK 1M a year for three years for an international delegate programme, announced in December 2020 (Riddu Riđđu).
- It became a national nodal festival in 2009 with a state grant of NOK 1.5M a year; after that scheme ended in 2015 it has applied for three-year support from Arts Council Norway (Norwegian Wikipedia).
- Accounts for 2025: operating income NOK 15.93M, of which NOK 3.36M sales, and a profit of NOK 527,261 (Regnskapsregisteret).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public Norwegian and Sámi money, with nothing in the sources read tying it to a flagged funder.
