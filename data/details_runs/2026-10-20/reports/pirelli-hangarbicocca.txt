PIRELLI HANGARBICOCCA: Milan, Italy
ID: pirelli-hangarbicocca
Website: https://pirellihangarbicocca.org/en/pirelli-hangarbicocca/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit foundation for contemporary art in a former industrial building in the Bicocca district of Milan, about 15,000 square metres, with the Shed and the Navate used for temporary exhibitions (foundation site).
- Set up in 2004 and made an official foundation in 2008; the building was converted into exhibition galleries in 2012 (Wikipedia). Pirelli is listed as founder.
- Named for its founder and funder, the tyre maker Pirelli.
- The building recalls the Ansaldo Group, founded in 1886 by Ernesto Breda, which made railway carriages and locomotives; rails used to test locomotives are still visible in the room with the Kiefer installation (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions, a bookshop and a bistrot. The permanent display is Anselm Kiefer's "The Seven Heavenly Palaces" (2004 to 2015). Vicente Todolí has been artistic director since 2012.
- Visiting: free admission; exhibitions open Thursday to Sunday, 10:30 am to 8:30 pm. The site links to an accessibility statement but does not describe physical access.

FUNDING
Pirelli HangarBicocca: corporate-funded: Pirelli, founder and supporter
- The foundation was conceived and is supported by Pirelli (stated by the foundation). No amounts are published.
- Technical sponsors listed on the foundation site: Mainstreaming, M&C Saatchi and K-Way. Their roles are not described.
- Board: chairman Marco Tronchetti Provera, with Maurizio Abet, Federica Barbaro, Andrea Casaluci and Ilaria Tronchetti Provera as directors; Alessandro Bianchi is general manager.
- Tronchetti Provera's consortium (Marco Polo Industrial Holding, with Camfin) is Pirelli's largest shareholder at 26.2%; Sinochem holds 20.1% after selling 14% to Lumina Crown, a vehicle of Czech businessman Michal Strnad, in July 2026 (just-auto).
- Pirelli Tyre Russia, Pirelli's Russian subsidiary, which controls the plants in Kirov and Voronezh, is 25.005% owned by Rostec (The Insider, 4 June 2026). The US Treasury designated Rostec on 28 June 2022 under Executive Order 14024 as a Russian defence-sector entity, and its release says Rostec is also sanctioned by Australia, Canada, the EU, New Zealand, Switzerland and the UK. No page opened shows Rostec controlling Pirelli or the foundation, so the Sanctions flag does not apply.
- No reporting found of public funding or accounts for the foundation itself.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Pirelli and Yokohama Rubber have been the only major foreign tyre makers to keep operating in Russia since 2023; neither was legally required to leave (European Rubber Journal, 5 June 2026).
- Unverified: Grizzly Research, a US investment research firm, alleged that Pirelli's Kirov plant shares territory and infrastructure with a neighbouring state-owned enterprise managed by a rubber research institute that Grizzly says Rostec fully owns (The Insider, 4 June 2026); the European Rubber Journal says the note covered tyre production for military use. No independent investigation in the sources opened confirms this.
- Pirelli's statement of 4 June 2026 said the note "does not reflect the truth", that Pirelli "does not produce tires for military use", and that it had engaged a legal firm to act in all jurisdictions against those who spread it (European Rubber Journal).
- Connection to the foundation: Pirelli founded and funds it.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation is paid for by Pirelli, and no source opened ties the foundation's own money to a flagged industry. The weak point is the parent company's Russian subsidiary, 25.005% owned by Rostec, a sanctioned defence group, with an unverified allegation from Grizzly Research that Pirelli denies. Worth watching: any regulator or court finding on the Kirov plant, and whether the foundation publishes accounts.
