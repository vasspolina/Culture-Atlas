BUNDANON: Shoalhaven, Australia
ID: bundanon
Website: https://www.bundanon.com.au

OVERVIEW
- Art museum, heritage homestead and artist residency site in the Shoalhaven region of the New South Wales South Coast, set across 1,000 hectares of wildlife refuge; the site is about 25 km by car from Nowra (Bundanon).
- Painter Arthur Boyd and his wife Yvonne Boyd, gave the property and their art collection to the Australian people; Prime Minister Paul Keating announced the government's acceptance in January 1993, and the government set up trustees to run the Bundanon Trust (Wikipedia).
- The Boyds bought Bundanon in 1979; the homestead was completed in 1866 (Wikipedia).
- The Education Centre, designed by Glenn Murcutt with Wendy Lewin and Reg Larkin, was completed in 1999 (Wikipedia).
- The Art Museum and the Bridge for Creative Learning, designed by Kerstin Thompson Architects, are partially buried in the landscape on the Riversdale property; the museum opened on 5 March 2022. The Bridge holds a creative learning centre, visitor information, accommodation and a cafe (Wikipedia).
- Artworks were moved to Sydney during the 2019 to 2020 bushfires and returned at the end of 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Art Museum open Wednesday to Sunday, 10am to 4pm; Homestead and Arthur Boyd's Studio open Saturday and Sunday, 10am to 4pm. Adults A$18, concession A$12, family A$40; free for members and children under 12. Certified assistance animals are accepted.

FUNDING
Bundanon: mainly public: Australian Government, NSW Government, with a patron program of individuals and family foundations
- Bundanon calls itself one of Australia's nine National Collecting Institutions.
- The Art Museum and Bridge project totals A$34 million: A$22.5 million from the Australian Government and A$10.3 million from the NSW Government, with philanthropic support also noted (Art Collector, Charlotte Middleton, originally published 18 January 2021). The article adds that the Federal Government later announced uplift funding of A$6 million over two years.
- John McDonald (Sydney Morning Herald, 7 May 2022) writes that the redevelopment was funded by federal and state governments and the private sector.
- Patrons listed on Bundanon's supporters page, with tiers and no years or amounts: Founding Patrons Warwick Smith and Ezekiel Solomon; Emerald Patrons Philip Bacon, the Oranges & Sardines Foundation and Ezekiel Solomon; Platinum Patrons including the Nick & Caroline Minogue Foundation. The Benefactors tier includes the Gordon Darling Foundation, David Gonski and the late Paul Ramsay.
- Bequests named: the late Jim Birkett and Roy Weatherhead, and the late Eileen Kramer.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one critical piece is a 2022 review of the opening exhibition.
1. Sydney Morning Herald: John McDonald, "Bundanon: Impulse to Action" (7 May 2022; read on McDonald's own site)
URL: https://www.johnmcdonald.net.au/?p=19572
- Calls the changes at the redeveloped site "impressive and far-reaching" and says the first exhibition at the museum, "From Impulse to Action," "possibly bites off more than it can chew"; he writes that coherence was sacrificed in an effort to cover every base.
- Criticises the exhibition, not the institution or its funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Bundanon is Australian federal and New South Wales government funding, with a patron program of individuals and family foundations. The site began as a gift of the Boyds' land and collection to the nation.
