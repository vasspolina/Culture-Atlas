AMOS REX: Helsinki, Finland
ID: amos-rex
Website: https://amosrex.fi (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at Mannerheimintie 22-24 in central Helsinki, with galleries under Lasipalatsi Square and a restored 1930s cinema, Bio Rex, in the Lasipalatsi building above.
- Run by the foundation Konstsamfundet, founded in 1940 by the publisher Amos Anderson; the foundation was renamed the Amos Anderson Fund in autumn 2025. Amos Rex opened on 30 August 2018 and replaced the Amos Anderson Art Museum, which opened in 1965 on Yrjönkatu and closed on 3 September 2017 (Wikipedia).
- Named for Amos Anderson (1878-1961), a Swedish-speaking Finnish businessman, newspaper publisher and art patron who owned 438 artworks when he died (the museum's Amos Anderson page).
- Building: JKMM Architects, chief architect Asmo Jaaksi, built the galleries underground; concrete domes with round skylights rise from the square. The largest hall spans 30 metres without supports and the gross area is 13,000 square metres. The 1936 Lasipalatsi was designed by Niilo Kokko, Viljo Revell and Heimo Riihimäki, with shops and a restaurant intended to serve visitors to the planned 1940 Helsinki Olympics. Construction began in January 2016 (Finnish Architecture Navigator).
- On site: exhibition halls, Bio Rex, a museum shop, and learning programmes including Amos Rex for kids and a Gen Z research project.
- Visiting: open Monday and Wednesday to Friday 11:00 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00, closed Tuesday. Adult ticket EUR 22 at the museum, EUR 20 online; EUR 5 for students and everyone aged 18 to 29; free for under-18s. The accessibility page was not readable in the sources opened.

FUNDING
Amos Rex: privately funded: an endowed foundation built on Amos Anderson's estate, plus three named partners
- Owner: the Amos Anderson Fund (Amos Andersons fond), which says Anderson's will named Konstsamfundet "his only legatee" (amosanderson.fi).
- Anderson built his fortune in real estate during the First World War; his two most important companies were the newspaper Hufvudstadsbladet and the Tilgmann printing house (Amos Rex, Amos Anderson page).
- The building project cost EUR 50 million and was privately financed. Director Kai Kartio said it was done "without recourse to taxpayers" (Museums Journal, 2019).
- Partners named on the museum's site: Helsingin Sanomat (main partner), Genelec and Bauer Media Outdoor. No amounts or years are published.
- The foundation also gives grants for Swedish-language arts, journalism and vocational education (amosanderson.fi).
- No reporting found of the foundation's assets, accounts or board in the pages opened.
- No reporting found of any fossil fuel, arms, gambling or other flagged funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage found is mostly admiring and concerns the opening in 2018. One piece carries a limited criticism of the programme; none criticises the funding or the foundation.
1. Yle: Jonni Aromaa, "Researcher: Amos Rex shows Guggenheim isn't needed in Helsinki" (2018, title translated from Finnish)
URL: https://yle.fi/a/3-10375914
- Art critic Veikko Halmetoja says the darkened teamLab opening show makes it hard to grasp the building's architecture, and calls drawing teenagers to the planned Magritte and Carlstedt shows somewhat challenging.
- Criticises the programme, not the funding. Urban researcher Kaarin Taipale praises the museum.
- A general news outlet, not an art-press title.
2. Museums Journal: Miles Rowland, "International opening | Amos Rex, Helsinki" (2019)
URL: https://www.museumsassociation.org/museums-journal/features/01022019-international-opening
- Not criticism: a feature built mainly on an interview with the director, who gave the cost, the private financing and the visitor figures (about 210,000 by mid-December 2018).
- Gives little detail on how the EUR 50 million was raised.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Amos Rex is a bequest foundation built on a newspaper and real estate fortune, with three commercial partners and no flagged funder found. The weak points are transparency: the sources opened show no accounts, board list or partner amounts. The partner list is worth watching for amounts and for any change in who the main partner is.
