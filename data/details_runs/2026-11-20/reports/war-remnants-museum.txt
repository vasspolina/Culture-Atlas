WAR REMNANTS MUSEUM: Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
ID: war-remnants-museum
Website: https://baotangchungtichchientranh.vn (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the evidence and consequences of war at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street, Xuân Hòa ward (Sài Gòn Giải Phóng).
- Founded on 4 September 1975 under a policy of the city's Party organisation as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes; renamed the Exhibition House for Crimes of War and Aggression in 1990 and the War Remnants Museum on 4 July 1995 (Báo Văn Hóa; English names from Wikipedia). Wikipedia links the 1995 name to the normalisation of relations with the United States.
- More than 20,000 documents, objects and films, over 1,500 of them shown in eight exhibitions (Viet Nam News, 2023).
- More than 25 million visitors in 50 years; one in six international tourists to the city visits it (Báo Văn Hóa, 2025). It received more than 1.4 million visitors in 2025 (Sài Gòn Giải Phóng).
- Ranked a first-class museum in Vietnam's national system in 2023; member of ICOM and the International Network of Museums for Peace (Báo Văn Hóa).
- Trần Xuân Thảo was director in 2023 (Viet Nam News); The Washington Spectator described him as shortly to retire in 2024.
- Visiting: open daily 7:30 to 17:30, ticket desk closes at 17:00; 40,000 dong, free for children under 6, half price for pupils and students; online tickets since 10 February 2026 (Sài Gòn Giải Phóng).

FUNDING
War Remnants Museum: self-financing public museum of the Ho Chi Minh City government
- Operator: one of nine museums under Ho Chi Minh City's Department of Culture and Sports (Vietbao, 2026; Báo Văn Hóa).
- Running costs: Báo Văn Hóa, the Ministry of Culture's newspaper, reported in September 2025 that since 2014 it has been the first museum in the city to cover its operating costs entirely from its own income.
- US aid project: on 10 April 2023 USAID Vietnam director Aler Grubbs and museum director Trần Xuân Thảo signed a memorandum under which USAID would help build a modern gallery on mine clearance, environmental clean-up and support for people with disabilities, due to open by 2025 (Viet Nam News). In May 2025 CSIS reported the funds frozen and the USAID and USIP officials in charge no longer allowed to work, leaving the project on indefinite hold (CSIS).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Writers focus on the museum's display of US war crimes and on the US-backed gallery that was meant to add the story of reconciliation.
1. The Washington Spectator: George Black, "Of Memory and Forgetting" (2025)
URL: https://washingtonspectator.org/of-memory-and-forgetting/
- Describes the 1975 exhibition house as a "house of horrors" aimed at a purely Vietnamese audience, with "angry, strident" iconography.
- Reports that well over a million people a year visit and 90 percent are foreigners.
- Says the director had cautioned that the US gallery needed approval from the city People's Committee, the culture ministry and the army's Office 701.
2. CSIS: Lauren Mai, "Reconciliation at Risk: 50 Years After the Vietnam War" (2025)
URL: https://www.csis.org/blogs/new-perspectives-asia/reconciliation-risk-50-years-after-vietnam-war
- Argues an exhibit on US reconciliation in "a museum full of 'American war crimes'" could have begun to shift decades of stigma, and would have been the first time the US government shaped how the war is shown in Vietnam.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: a city museum that pays its own way. The only open funding question is the frozen US-backed gallery.
