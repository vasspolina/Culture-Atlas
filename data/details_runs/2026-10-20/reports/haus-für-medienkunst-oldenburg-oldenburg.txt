HAUS FÜR MEDIENKUNST OLDENBURG: Oldenburg, Germany
ID: haus-für-medienkunst-oldenburg-oldenburg
Website: https://hausmedienkunst.de (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition house for contemporary media art run by the City of Oldenburg, at Katharinenstrasse 23, near the Peter-Friedrich-Ludwig-Hospital, a former hospital now a cultural centre (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 2000 as the Edith-Russ-Haus für Medienkunst; the city council renamed it Haus für Medienkunst Oldenburg on 24 February 2025. Edit Molnár and Marcel Schwierin have directed it since 2015.
- Paid for by a bequest from the journalist and teacher Edith Ruß (1919 to 1993); a bequest from the educator Elisabeth Brand (1898 to 1993) furnished it.
- Two buildings in a Bauhaus-influenced style that Ruß wanted modelled on Mies van der Rohe's National Gallery: an exhibition hall of 291.68 m² and a guest house of 88.77 m² with apartments for invited artists. No architect is named on the site.
- No permanent collection: changing exhibitions, artist talks, workshops and several new commissions a year, plus three Stiftung Niedersachsen media-art grants.
- Open during exhibitions Tuesday to Friday 2 pm to 6 pm, weekends and public holidays 11 am to 6 pm, closed Monday. No admission prices or accessibility details are given on the pages checked.

FUNDING
Haus für Medienkunst Oldenburg: mainly public: the City of Oldenburg, on a building paid for by the Ruß bequest
- The city runs the house as one of its own exhibition houses (stated by the space).
- Stiftung Niedersachsen pays three media-art grants of €12,500 each a year; awards are listed from 2001 (stated by the space).
- The house was founded through Ruß's endowment (Wikipedia). taz reported that the origin of her "enormes Vermögen" is unclear, possibly an uncle's inheritance, and that she had evaded tax, so her estate first had to pay back taxes (taz, 2026).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors. Mayor Jürgen Krogmann said in 2024 that the name was causing distance from artists, sponsors and partners, without naming them (taz).

Red flags: the founding donors' Nazi pasts and the removed name
- A study for the city by the historians Mareike Witkowski and Joachim Tautz found that Ruß joined the NSDAP on 1 January 1941, which she had denied, including in her denazification (taz, 1 October 2024).
- She was features editor of the NSDAP paper Oldenburgische Staatszeitung and spread propaganda about "Lebensraum" and "Endsieg" until 1945; in 1948 she and her former boss Herbert Heitz, an antisemite, vouched for each other in denazification (taz, 2026).
- The historians found völkisch and nationalist ideas in her articles but no antisemitic or racist statements (taz, 2024).
- Brand was an NSDAP member from 1933 and held local propaganda and treasurer roles in the Nazi women's organisation (stated by the space).
- taz wrote that the city had long ignored the issue: a 2000 biography by the current head of the city's Kulturbüro described Ruß as independent.
- The council removed the name on 24 February 2025 after Krogmann called it no longer tenable for a municipal institution (taz).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press coverage; the critique comes from taz, whose reporting started the renaming. e-flux carried only the house's own announcement.
1. taz: Aljoscha Hoepfner, "Oldenburger Museum erforscht Stifterin: Edith Ruß war „überzeugte Nationalsozialistin“" (2026)
URL: https://taz.de/Oldenburger-Museum-erforscht-Stifterin/!6149464/
- Reviews the house's exhibition "Die Stifterin, der Nationalsozialismus und das Haus" (28 January to 1 March 2026) and criticises what it leaves out, such as the publisher Isensee printing the Staatszeitung from 1929.
- The city-commissioned report was partly revised after taz pointed out errors; Tautz calls Ruß an "überzeugte Nationalsozialistin" (convinced National Socialist, our translation), Witkowski did not reach that conclusion.
- The city's cultural councillor praised the handling without saying the city already held Ruß's press pass; the author reads the show as the city's closing word.
- The author gave a lecture at the exhibition (disclosed by taz).
2. taz: Alexander Diehl, "Oldenburger Edith-Russ-Haus: Bürgermeister will Entnazifizierung" (2024)
URL: https://taz.de/Oldenburger-Edith-Russ-Haus/!6036688/
- Reports the study and argues the city had long ignored Ruß's past; names the house and the city.

BOTTOM LINE
The running money is the city's own and the grants come from a Lower Saxony foundation, so current funding is clean. The weak point is the founding bequest: both donors were NSDAP members, Ruß a Nazi newspaper editor, and the house was renamed in 2025. Worth watching: whether the origin of Ruß's fortune is ever established.
