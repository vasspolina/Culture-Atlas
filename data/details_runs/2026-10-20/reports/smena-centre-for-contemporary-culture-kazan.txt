SMENA CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY CULTURE: Kazan, Russia
ID: smena-centre-for-contemporary-culture-kazan

OVERVIEW
- Independent contemporary culture centre on Burkhana Shakhidi Street in Kazan, Tatarstan, with a bookshop, gallery, lecture hall, publishing house and festivals (RBC Style, 30 May 2025; Russian Art Archive).
- Launched in 2013 as a do-it-yourself project by a group of young people aged 20 to 25; the co-founder Kirill Maevsky is a publisher and curator, and the artist Robert Khasanov was an early contributor (RBC Style, 2025). It grew out of a 2013 book fair (The Art Newspaper Russia, 2023).
- Building: an early 20th-century building that was a stable block with hay storage, later a Soviet warehouse; the owner is Yakov Margulis, a car wash occupied the first floor until the 2015 renovation, and Smena rents space there (RBC Style, 2025). Business Online (April 2016) says the original ceilings are Siberian larch.
- Timeline: bookshop, gallery and lecture space in 2013; its own publishing programme from 2015, whose first book was "Mythology of the Kazan Tatars" (title translated); a ground-floor vinyl corner, coffee shop and larger exhibition space in 2016; a second bookshop in the National Library of Tatarstan from 2020 (RBC Style, 2025).
- In April 2023 Smena merged with the Bulat Galeev "Prometheus" Foundation, named for the physicist and visionary Bulat Galeev, who explored combining music and light. The Prometheus archive sits in the building's attic, and Galeev's rare books are in a reading room (The Art Newspaper Russia, 2023).
- Programmes: a summer book fair at Chernoe Ozero park since 2017, winter and summer book festivals, over 1,000 lectures and 500 screenings and concerts in its first decade, and book festivals commissioned in Almetyevsk, Nizhnekamsk, Vyksa, Svobodny and Chusovoy (RBC Style, 2025).
- Co-organises the exhibition "На ножках" with the Zarya Center for Contemporary Art in Vladivostok, on show there from 26 September 2026 to 11 April 2027, curated by Aleksandra Paperno and Kirill Maevsky (Zarya).

FUNDING
Smena: self-generated and patron-backed: commissioned festivals and a building owner who charges minimal rent; Smena says it has no permanent state or private funding
- Smena describes itself as independent and says it has no permanent private or state funding (RBC Style, 2025; The Art Newspaper Russia, 15 November 2023). Its income comes partly from commissioned projects, including book festivals run since 2023 at the invitation of regional authorities and businesses, and from showing Kazan artists at art fairs (RBC Style, 2025).
- Building owner: Yakov Margulis, a businessman who previously led the pharmaceutical company Tattekhmedpharm, owns the building and was investing in its reconstruction in April 2016; Khasanov said Smena pays minimal rent and that on commercial terms Smena would have closed long ago (Business Online, April 2016).
- Rinat Mardanov invested in Smena early and later became a patron; Khasanov covers periodic losses with income from his other projects (Agency for Social Information, 22 December 2017).
- In December 2017 the Ministry of Culture of the Republic of Tatarstan supported the Summer Book Festival, the Goethe-Institut was the main partner for the Unsound festival, which ran at a loss of over 500,000 rubles, and the Living City fund was a close collaborator; Smena registered an autonomous non-profit organisation to ease applying for grants and state support (Agency for Social Information, 22 December 2017). Khasanov said in April 2016 that the centre relies on state grants for large events and that the Kazan city government supports the winter book festival (Business Online).
- Smena also supports the Prometheus foundation financially (RBC Style, 2025).
- Co-founder Kirill Maevsky says the centre has never had an annual budget (The Art Newspaper Russia, 15 November 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Smena is mixed: festival commissions from regional authorities and businesses, a building owner's low rent, an early patron, and Tatarstan state grants for large events, according to Smena's founders.
