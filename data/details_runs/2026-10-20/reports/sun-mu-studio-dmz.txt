SUN MU STUDIO & NORTH KOREAN DISSIDENT ART ARCHIVE: Paju, South Korea
ID: sun-mu-studio-dmz
Website: https://sunmuart.com (checked 2026-10-25; loads; an artist's website with artworks, exhibitions, press and a shop, not an institution's site)

OVERVIEW
- The working base and website of the pseudonymous North Korean defector artist Sun Mu, who lives in South Korea. No reporting found of a public archive or of a studio in Paju; a Korean article reproduced on his site places his studio near Haengju Fortress by the Han River in Goyang, in an area bordered by a military fence.
- Sun Mu is a pseudonym meaning "no borders" (Wende Museum). He was trained as a propaganda-poster artist, left North Korea in 1998 according to the Wende Museum, and still works in that style, turning its messages upside down. A 2008 Weekly Kyunghyang profile says he left in 2002 and entered Hongik University in 2003; the sources disagree on the year.
- His site names his work as portraits of life in North Korea and "images of hope" (stated by the space).
- Shows: Wende Museum, Culver City, 10 February to 2 June 2019, the first US museum exhibition of his work; a solo show "Naui Gil" (나의 길) at Art Forum Ri in Bucheon, 4 to 30 July (Kyeonggi Ilbo, 23 June 2022).
- Visiting: no opening hours or public visits are listed on the site; inquiries go by email.

FUNDING
Sun Mu Studio: self-funded as an individual artist's practice, with project support from outside groups
- No reporting found of how the studio itself is financed, or of any institutional funder of the studio or an archive.
- Liberty in North Korea, a US advocacy group, has been "great in supporting him", per the filmmaker Adam Sjöberg in the Frontline Club recap. No amounts are given.
- Kyeonggi Ilbo (23 June 2022) says the Bucheon solo show was supported through a "North-South Integrated Culture Content Creation Support" project of the Ministry of Unification's North-South Integration Center and the Korea Hana Foundation (남북하나재단). The article gives no amount and does not say who paid the artist. That supported an exhibition, not the studio.
- The Wende Museum page names no funders for the 2019 show; it says two Wende board members, Blaine Vess and Fiona Chalom, worked to retrieve his paintings from China and bring them to Southern California.
- A 2008 profile says his wife worked in skin care to support the household (Weekly Kyunghyang, 2008). No later reporting found of his income.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism of the studio. Coverage is favourable and mostly profiles of the artist; the only critical notes concern how his images are received.
1. GARAGE (via VICE, reposted on sunmuart.com): "North Korean Defector Artist Sun Mu's Lost Utopias" (undated, author not named; image file names point to February 2019)
URL: https://sunmuart.com/author/suadmin/page/2/
- Argues the work revisits lost childhood and unification utopias and uses propaganda style ambiguously, not as simple anti-North Korean criticism, and that the paintings carry the totalitarian pull of their own style.
- Notes the work has drawn criticism for being too harsh on North Korea and for not being harsh enough, and records Sun Mu's own reflection that he used children as propaganda.
- Mostly favourable; a repost on the artist's own site.
2. Frontline Club: event recap, "Sun Mu: From North Korean Propagandist to Pop Art Defector" (undated)
URL: https://www.frontlineclub.com/sun-mu-from-north-korean-propagandist-to-pop-art-defector/
- The filmmaker Adam Sjöberg says Sun Mu's images are "offensive to South Koreans as well."
- Criticises the art's reception, not the studio. Author not named.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Beijing, 2014: Chinese police blocked entry to Sun Mu's show "Red White Blue" at Yuan Dian gallery and removed the paintings shortly before its 27 July opening, and officials gave no reason (The Verge, 30 July 2014, citing Yonhap, as reproduced on sunmuart.com). A Korean article reproduced on the same page (JoongAng Ilbo) says Chinese security officers raided the gallery after a North Korean embassy report and seized about 80 works and 1,000 catalogues.
- China neither funds nor controls the studio; the action fell on an exhibition in China.

BOTTOM LINE
No institutional money, corporate or state, sits behind the studio itself in anything found, and the one South Korean state grant paid for a single exhibition. The weak points are that no archive and no Paju studio could be confirmed, and that almost all coverage comes from the artist's own site or friendly profiles. Worth watching is whether an archive with named supporters is ever published.
