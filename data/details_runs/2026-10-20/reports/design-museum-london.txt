DESIGN MUSEUM: London, UK
ID: design-museum-london
Website: https://designmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of design and architecture at 224 to 238 Kensington High Street, London W8.
- Founded in 1989 by Sir Terence Conran, with Stephen Bayley as first chief executive, in a former banana warehouse at Shad Thames; it reopened in Kensington on 24 November 2016. Tim Marlow has been director since 2019.
- Housed in the former Commonwealth Institute building, refitted by a team led by John Pawson; Conran gave £17.5 million toward the move in June 2011 (Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions, the free collection display "Designer Maker User", the Future Observatory research programme, learning programmes, an auditorium, shops and cafés.
- Open daily, 10:00 to 17:00 Monday to Thursday and to 18:00 Friday to Sunday. The collection is free; exhibitions are ticketed, with a concession for disabled visitors and free entry for carers. Step-free by lift, with bookable wheelchairs.

FUNDING
Design Museum: mostly self-generated: tickets, trading, donations and corporate partners, with a small government grant
- In 2018 the museum said two per cent of its funding came from public money (Dezeen).
- The museum lost £2,026,462 in the year to March 2019 and the Conran Foundation lent it £3 million; the annual report, written by the then chair Lord Mandelson, called its finances "not sustainable in the medium term" (Dezeen, 2019).
- Corporate partners named on its site include Bloomberg Connects, Canva, W Hotels, NOT A HOTEL, Nyetimber, Mailchimp (2023), Van Cleef & Arpels (2022), Harvey Nichols (2024), Snap Inc (2025 to 2026), Nike and Greggs (2026).
- Trustees include Sebastian Conran and Lady Victoria Conran; Stuart Roden has chaired the board since July 2023 (trustees page).
- No reporting found of a current Deutsche Bank sponsorship; neither the museum's partner page nor Deutsche Bank's UK corporate memberships page names the other.
- No reporting found of amounts from individual corporate partners.

Red flags: artists withdrew work over an arms company event
- On 17 July 2018 the museum hosted a private event for Leonardo, the Italian aerospace and defence company, timed to the Farnborough airshow, during the exhibition "Hope to Nope" (The Art Newspaper).
- Over 30 artists and designers signed an open letter on the Campaign Against Arms Trade website (Dezeen). More than 40 works, a third of the show, were removed, including Peter Kennard's and Shepard Fairey's (The Art Newspaper, Dezeen).
- The directors Deyan Sudjic and Alice Black wrote that "professional activists whose work didn't feature in the exhibition took the view that the museum had acted wrongfully and were quick to exploit the situation",, and committed to no such private hires during a policy review (The Art Newspaper).
- In 2019 the museum said it would judge funding proposals "on a case by case basis". The artists, organised as Nope to Arms, asked it to rule out arms, oil and tobacco funding and raised concerns about Mandelson's business relationships with arms and oil companies, which the piece does not describe (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2018 Leonardo event and the museum's handling of it; none found on its programme.

URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/meet-your-new-chairman-the-prince-of-darkness/
- Satirical column on Mandelson's appointment; it mocks him, not the museum.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Lord Mandelson was named chair in December 2016 (Apollo) and served until Stuart Roden took over in July 2023 (trustees page).
- In September 2025 Prime Minister Keir Starmer dismissed him as UK ambassador to the US after supportive emails he sent Jeffrey Epstein after Epstein's 2008 conviction were revealed (Cherwell). Cherwell does not mention the museum.
- No source opened ties this link to his time as chair or to the museum's money.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on tickets, trading, donations and brand partners, backed by the Conran family. The weak points are the 2018 arms company hire and the vague policy after it. Worth watching: whether it publishes a funding policy.
