MUSEUM FÜNF KONTINENTE: Munich, Germany
ID: museum-funf-kontinente
Website: https://www.museum-fuenf-kontinente.de/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Bavaria's state museum of world cultures at Maximilianstrasse 42, Munich, in a building that Eduard Riedel built from 1859 to 1865 for the Bavarian National Museum (German Lost Art Foundation; museum history page, which elsewhere gives 1858 to 1867); the museum moved there in 1925 and 1926 (museum website).
- Moritz Wagner was appointed the first curator in 1862, when the collection belonged to the Kingdom of Bavaria; it opened to the public in 1868 in the Hofgarten arcades, was renamed Museum für Völkerkunde in 1917 and Museum Fünf Kontinente in 2014 (museum website).
- The building has about 12,500 square metres and holds about 160,000 objects, 135,000 photos and films, and over 100,000 books and periodicals (German Lost Art Foundation).
- Holdings came from Wittelsbach court collections, expeditions and colonial agents; director Max Buchner, who took part in colonial wars in Cameroon in 1884 and 1885, gave objects from the people he fought and brought over 4,000 artefacts from an 1888 to 1890 trip; Princess Therese of Bavaria bequeathed almost 3,000 objects (museum website).
- The public reading library holds over 100,000 volumes and 120 periodicals (museum website; German Wikipedia).
- Uta Werlich is director, with Anahita Mittertrainer and Hilke Thode-Arora as deputies (museum website).
- Current show: "Kabuki. Schauspiel, Drucke, Fankultur", 23 October 2026 to 2 May 2027, with 13 prints by Tōshūsai Sharaku (museum website).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 9:30 to 17:30; permanent galleries 5 euros, reduced 4 euros, 1 euro on Sundays, free under 18; step-free entrance on Knöbelstrasse (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum Fünf Kontinente: public: a state museum of the Free State of Bavaria
- A state museum: its imprint lists it under "Staatliche Museen in Bayern" (state museums in Bavaria), and its superior authority is the Bavarian Ministry of Science and Arts (museum imprint; Hilke Thode-Arora, Abendzeitung, 12 March 2019).
- The Freundeskreis Museum Fünf Kontinente e.V. supports the museum; members get free entry and invitations (museum website).
- The Preetorius-Stiftung funds science and art connected with the Preetorius collection held at the museum, Chinese and Japanese painting and prints, Tibetan thangkas and Persian and Indian book illustration; it goes back to the collector Emil Preetorius (1883 to 1973) (museum website).
- Ticket income and donations, project funding and gifts (museum website).
- In the 19th century the Kingdom of Bavaria and its monarchs promoted exploration and collecting, and rewarded large gifts of objects with orders and honours (museum website).

Red flags: Benin holdings researched in 2021 and 2022, with no return
- About 50 objects in the Africa collection are assigned to the Benin Empire or marked Benin City in the entry books; for most, the provenance had not been fully clarified (German Lost Art Foundation).
- A provenance project ran from 1 December 2021, planned for four months and extended to six, to test links to the British punitive expedition of 1897 and other violent appropriation (German Lost Art Foundation).
- Werlich said experts hold that Benin court objects almost certainly came from that expedition, a well-documented context of injustice: "Diese Objekte müssen zurückgehen" (translated: "These objects must go back") (LMU, 4 July 2022).
- In May 2023 Bavaria and Saxony were the only German states that had not concluded return agreements with Nigeria (Berliner Zeitung, 17 May 2023).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2019 Werlich said the only return claim on the museum was for a ship's prow from Cameroon sought by Prince Kum'a Ndumbe III; in 2016 the museum asked him to prove he was entitled to receive it, and there had been no contact since (Abendzeitung, 12 March 2019).
- In January 2024 the museum was one of eleven German museums that declared, with delegates of Cameroon's interministerial committee, their intent to work together for returns of cultural goods taken in the colonial era (Stuttgarter Communiqué, 24 January 2024).
- The museum states that in 1938 it supported a trip by its Africa curator to Ethiopia, then occupied by fascist Italy, that a large Tibet collection from the Nazi perpetrators Ernst Schäfer and Bruno Beger came to it in 1974, and that in 1949 it returned a Chinese work to the Jewish Bernheimer family (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The state money behind the museum is clean. The weak point is the colonial collection, the Benin holdings above all.
