NASJONALMUSEET (NATIONAL MUSEUM OF NORWAY): Oslo, Norway
ID: nasjonalmuseet-national-museum-of-norway
Website: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Norway's museum of art, architecture and design, at Brynjulf Bulls plass 3 on the Vestbanen site in central Oslo (museum site).
- Created on 28 April 2003 by merging the National Gallery, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Museum of Architecture and two design museums; it is state-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia).
- The new building by Kleihues + Schuwerk opened in June 2022; the Storting set a cost frame of NOK 5.327 billion on 6 June 2013 (Wikipedia).
- It holds over 400,000 works, including the first version of Edvard Munch's "The Scream" (Wikipedia).
- Visitors: 946,000 in 2024. Director Ingrid Røynesdal, appointed in 2023 after Karin Hindsbo (2017 to 2023); board chair Maria Moræus Hanssen (Wikipedia; annual report 2024).

FUNDING
Nasjonalmuseet: mainly public: a state foundation funded by the Ministry of Culture, with private partners led by the Fredriksen family
- Operating income was NOK 1,058.9 million in 2024, including gifts of NOK 12.5 million (annual report 2024), and NOK 1,084.7 million in 2025, with an operating loss of NOK 34.9 million (Brønnøysund accounts register).
- Government grants covered 90 percent of the 2022 operating budget (Kunstkritikk).
- Fredriksen Family Art Company: lends the Fredriksen Family Collection under an agreement confirmed in 2017 that runs initially for ten years, and funds four doctoral fellowships, the first two begun in 2024 (museum site; annual report 2024). Cecilie and Kathrine Fredriksen, daughters of John Fredriksen, have loaned more than 100 works and funded acquisitions, exhibitions and research (Apollo, 2025). The museum put the 2022 contribution at up to NOK 20 million (Kunstkritikk).
- Sparebankstiftelsen DNB: long-term art loans, exhibitions and NOK 6 million over three years for buying Norwegian contemporary art (annual report 2024); NOK 12 million in 2022 (Kunstkritikk).
- AKO Foundation: a contributor since 2022, paying in 2024 towards the Anna-Eva Bergman and Else Hagen exhibitions (annual report 2024).
- Stiftelsen Scheibler: NOK 1 million a year from 2024 for design, crafts and fashion; Lise M. Stolt-Nielsen and her children: a multi-year performance programme agreed in summer 2024 (annual report 2024). The museum also lists Viking as a partner.

Red flags: 2022 artists' appeal against the Fredriksen agreement
- 12 March 2022: Jérémie McGowan, former director of Nordnorsk Kunstmuseum, wrote in Klassekampen that Seadrill, Fredriksen's drilling company, signed a major agreement with Rosneft just before Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, and called the museum "naïve or complicit" (Kunstkritikk).
- Kunstkritikk added that Putin gave John Fredriksen Russia's Order of Friendship in 2015 and that he was fined for oil fraud in the United States in 2014. The case: on 4 August 2014 a New York federal court ordered Fredriksen's oil trader Arcadia, its US unit Parnon and two traders to pay a $13 million civil penalty for manipulating crude oil futures in 2008, by consent and without admission of wrongdoing (CFTC; Reuters).
- Communications director Eirik Kydland replied that no Fredriksen company had been hit by sanctions and that the funds come from legitimate business; Hindsbo did not respond (Kunstkritikk).
- 10 June 2022: artists Nikhil Vettukattil, Knut Åsdam, Kajsa Dahlberg and Sille Storihle, then showing at the museum, issued the appeal "A Gift we can Afford to Refuse", calling the deal artwashing and asking for it to be ended; it had well over 100 signatures (Kunstkritikk).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Kunstkritikk has covered the agreement critically since 2022; the director answered in its pages.
1. Kunstkritikk: Live Drønen, "The National Museum of Norway Linked to Russia" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/the-national-museum-of-norway-linked-to-russia/
- Argues the invasion of Ukraine renewed the case against the deal; Ruben Steinum of the Association of Norwegian Visual Artists asked how the museum assesses partners whose wealth was partly made in war zones.
2. Kunstkritikk: Tiril Flom, "Artists Fight Sponsorship Agreement" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/artists-fight-the-fredriksen-collaboration
- Dahlberg: the deal gives too much influence to one powerful family; Åsdam: it "kills the professional integrity of a national public institution".
3. Kunstkritikk: Live Drønen, "Money Problems" (2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/money-problems
- Hindsbo answered that if Norwegian museums could not take fossil fuel money they would also have to refuse state funds, since Norway earns heavily from oil and gas.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Dagbladet (NTB) reported on 26 August 2019 that the first Fredriksen agreement was signed in June 2017 and made public only with a June 2019 addendum; law professor Jan Frithjof Bernt criticised the museum and the ministry in Klassekampen for the secrecy.
- The secrecy concerned the agreement the 2022 appeal targets.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money carries the museum. The private share is small but rests on shipping and drilling fortunes, and the Fredriksen agreement draws sustained protest; its renewal is the point to watch.
