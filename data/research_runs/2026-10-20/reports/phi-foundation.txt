PHI FOUNDATION – Montréal, Canada
Website: https://phi.ca (ok, checked 2026-10-20; the About page could not be opened in this run)

OVERVIEW
- Founded in 2007 by Phoebe Greenberg as DHC/ART, a non-collecting, kunsthalle-style contemporary art space in Old Montreal, free to the public; renamed PHI Foundation for Contemporary Art in March 2019. Sister bodies: PHI Centre (2012) and PHI Studio (2019) (Wikipedia).
- PHI Contemporary will merge PHI's programmes in four heritage buildings and an adjacent lot at Bonsecours and Saint-Paul East streets, designed by Kuehn Malvezzi, Pelletier de Fontenay and Jodoin Lamarre Pratte (Canadian Architect, July 2022). PHI now gives groundbreaking in 2026 and opening in 2029 (stated by the space); earlier reporting gave 2026 as the opening.

FUNDING
PHI Foundation: founder-funded, with public capital for the new building.
- Phoebe Greenberg's family: her father Irving Greenberg co-founded the Minto Group in 1955 (Wikipedia). No source read in this run states the amount or route of family funding.
- Federal and Quebec governments announced C$26,645,740 on 29 April 2022, more than C$13.3M each, for PHI Contemporary (federal money through the Investing in Canada Infrastructure Program) (Government of Canada). PHI's own contribution is not stated.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsorship beyond the Partners Circle cited in the existing record; it was not re-checked.
- No annual report or accounts found; the CRA charity listing could not be opened in this run.

BOTTOM LINE
PHI looks clean on available sources: a founder-funded free art space with documented public capital grants for its new building. Worth watching is how the new institution's operating costs will be funded once it opens, since no accounts are published.
