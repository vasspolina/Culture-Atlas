MADISON MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Madison, USA
ID: madison-museum-of-contemporary-art-madison
Website: https://www.mmoca.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Independent nonprofit art museum at 227 State Street in downtown Madison, Wisconsin, next to the Overture Center for the Arts.
- Founded in 1901 as the Madison Art Association; it became the Madison Art Center in 1964, moved into the Madison Civic Center in 1980 and took its present name in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- Opened in its current building on 23 April 2006, after 105 years of showing art in borrowed and refurbished spaces (museum website).
- Building designed by César Pelli, who also designed the Overture Center; 51,500 square feet inside plus a 7,100-square-foot Rooftop Sculpture Garden. W. Jerome Frautschi made the facility possible (museum website).
- On site: galleries, a 230-seat lecture hall, a children's classroom, a new-media gallery and a study center; the collection holds nearly 6,000 objects (museum website).
- Open Wednesday, Friday and Sunday 12 to 6 pm, Thursday 12 to 9 pm, Saturday 10 am to 6 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday. Admission to exhibitions is free. The main entrance has an automatic door opener, one elevator serves three floors, and a manual wheelchair is free to borrow; there is no on-site parking.

FUNDING
Madison Museum of Contemporary Art: mainly private giving: individuals, foundations, members and event sponsors, with free admission
- The museum states that it relies on individuals, corporations, private event rentals, foundations, memberships, fundraisers such as Art Fair on the Square, and private gifts (Wikipedia, summarising the museum).
- Form 990 data (ProPublica): in the year to April 2025 revenue was $5,254,356, of which $4,953,866 was contributions; expenses were $3,767,099 and net assets $10,021,335. Revenue was about $2.0 million a year from FY2022 to FY2024, with deficits in each year.
- Art Fair on the Square sponsors, listed on the museum's page headed 2026 (its thank-you note refers to the 2025 fair): Lake Ridge Bank (Major), Wisconsin Lottery (Gold), Ho Chunk Gaming Madison and Wildwood Pro Acquisitions (Silver), DoubleTree by Hilton (Official Hotel), plus media sponsors Madison Magazine, Isthmus and WKOW. The museum says sponsors help it offer free admission to nearly 100,000 visitors a year.
- The Ho-Chunk Nation owns and operates Ho-Chunk Gaming Madison, a casino on trust land inside Madison city limits (GGB magazine, 2019).
- No reporting found of a published list of the museum's major donors.

Red flags: gambling sponsors
- Ho Chunk Gaming Madison, a tribal casino, is a Silver sponsor of Art Fair on the Square, a museum fundraiser (museum sponsorship page).
- Wisconsin Lottery is a Gold sponsor of the same event (museum sponsorship page).
- No reporting found of an objection to either sponsor.

Red flags: 2022 Wisconsin Triennial open letter
- In August 2022, artists in the Triennial "Ain't I A Woman?", curated by Fatima Laster and showing Black women artists, published an open letter alleging "shameful mistreatment" of Black artists, contractors and staff and demanding the resignation of director Christina Brungardt (ArtReview).
- Eleven of 23 artists withdrew by 23 August (Hyperallergic); the Daily Cardinal counted at least 16 of 23 by October 2022.
- The trigger was damage to Lilada Gee's unfinished installation in June, and an earlier verbal attack on Gee by a staff member of the Overture Center, which shares the building but is independent (Hyperallergic).
- The museum's executive committee denied institutional racism (Wikipedia). In a September 2022 statement it apologised for the damage to Gee's work and announced a "truth and reconciliation" project led by three board members (Daily Cardinal, October 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One episode draws sustained criticism in art media, the 2022 Triennial row; the pieces below report the artists' allegations and the museum's denial. No criticism of the museum's funders found in art media.
1. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Liu, "Citing 'Institutional Racist Violence,' Half of the Wisconsin Triennial Artists Withdraw Their Work" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/755393/citing-institutional-racist-violence-half-of-the-wisconsin-triennial-artists-withdraw-their-work/
- At least 11 of 23 artists withdrew after a March verbal attack on a Black artist at the shared building, slow leadership responses, and the June defacement of Gee's work.
- Artists also cite poor installation and no high-resolution images for press; they seek an apology, restitution, the director's removal and an independent audit.
- Names Brungardt, Gee, Laster and the Overture Center. The museum did not respond to repeated requests for comment.
2. ArtReview: unsigned, "Wisconsin Triennial artists pull work from show dedicated to Black women artists; cite 'shameful mistreatment'" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/wisconsin-triennial-artists-pull-work-from-show-dedicated-to-black-women-artists-cite-shameful-mistreatment
- Reports that 11 of 23 artists withdrew and relays the open letter calling for Brungardt to resign, and its claim that artists and the curator were underpaid.
- Names Brungardt, Gee and Laster. Reports no response from the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is mostly private giving, and its revenue was about $5 million in FY2025. The weak points are the current gambling sponsors of its main fundraiser, a tribal casino and the state lottery, and the unresolved 2022 Triennial dispute over its treatment of Black artists. Worth watching: whether the sponsor list changes and whether the museum publishes the findings of its reconciliation project.
