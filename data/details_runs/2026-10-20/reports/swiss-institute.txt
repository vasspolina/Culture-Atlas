SWISS INSTITUTE: New York, USA
ID: swiss-institute
Website: https://www.swissinstitute.net (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art institute at 38 St. Marks Place at Second Avenue in the East Village, Manhattan (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1986 by "a group of Swiss expats looking to highlight their country's artists and culture"; the article does not name them (Wikipedia). It began in the Swiss Townhouse at 35 West 67th Street, then moved to 495 Broadway in 1994, 18 Wooster Street from 2011, a temporary space at 102 Franklin Street from 2016, and St. Marks Place from 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Building: a former bank of about 700 square metres, redesigned by Selldorf Architects as a four-storey building with exhibition space, an education and public programmes floor, a library and a rooftop. Printed Matter's St. Mark's bookstore occupies the ground floor (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Ziba Ardalan de Weck (1987 to 1992), Carin Kuoni, Annette Schindler, Marc-Olivier Wahler, Gianni Jetzer, Simon Castets (2013 to 2021) and Stefanie Hessler (2022 to the present). Board chairs: Fabienne Abrecht (2002 to 2016) and Maja Hoffmann (2016 to the present) (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is free (Wikipedia). No reporting found of current opening hours or accessibility in the pages read.

Wikipedia says Len Blavatnik made his initial fortune in the privatisation of Soviet-era aluminium and oil assets, including an AAR purchase of 40 percent of Tyumen Oil, and that his business controls 21 percent of LyondellBasell. Neither foundation is listed after 2021.
- The board chair Maja Hoffmann's LUMA Foundation appears as a partner in 2008 to 2010 and again in 2025 and 2026 (stated by the space).
- Finances (Form 990, ProPublica): revenue $4,198,232 in 2025, $4,894,615 in 2024, $6,224,196 in 2023; expenses exceeded revenue in 2024 and 2025, and net assets were negative $248,650 at the end of 2025. Contributions were about 99.9 percent of 2025 revenue. The director's pay was $209,156 in 2025.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One substantial case against the institute, in 2019, about how it handled a show including an artist facing criminal charges; no later criticism of its funders or programme found.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Open Letter Asks Swiss Institute to Address Claims Against Artist" (9 October 2019, updated 2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/open-letter-swiss-institute-claims-against-artist
- More than 50 artists, writers and curators signed a "Letter of Concern" to the Swiss Institute's staff and board, to the show's curator Anna-Sophie Berger and to the artists in it, citing charges against an artist in "Life and Limbs" (assault with intent to cause injury, criminal obstruction of breath or circulation, attempted assault and harassment, from a 1 December 2018 incident; arrest in March 2019).
- The institute said it could not comment on an ongoing legal case and would defer to the courts.
- Update: all criminal charges were dismissed and the case sealed in January 2021; the artist pleaded guilty only to a violation.

BOTTOM LINE
The institute is funded by many foundations and public arts bodies, and none of the current partners listed is a flagged industry funder. The weak points are two former partners, Bloomberg Philanthropies (2018 and 2019) and the Blavatnik Family Foundation (2020 and 2021), and a finance picture of falling revenue and negative net assets in 2025. Worth watching is whether its revenue recovers and whether the Blavatnik or Bloomberg money returns.
