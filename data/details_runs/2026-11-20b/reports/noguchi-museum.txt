NOGUCHI MUSEUM: New York, USA
ID: noguchi-museum
Website: https://www.noguchi.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and sculpture garden of Isamu Noguchi's work in Long Island City, Queens, entered at 9-01 33rd Road (museum website).
- Noguchi set up his studio there in 1961, bought a factory and lot across the street by 1974 and opened the museum on 11 May 1985, the first US museum established, designed and installed by a living artist to show their own work (museum website).
- Open year-round since renovations ended in 2004, when foundation and museum merged as The Isamu Noguchi Foundation and Garden Museum, a 501(c)(3) public charity (museum website).
- Two storeys and 12 galleries, after a $13.5 million capital plan approved in 1999 and an $8 million stabilisation begun in 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Amy Hau has been director since 2024 (Wikipedia; Form 990).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 10 am to 5 pm; free on first Fridays and for visitors with disabilities, SNAP and WIC recipients and military families (museum website).

FUNDING
Noguchi Museum: mainly self-generated: sales, its own assets and investments, with city and state grants and foundation gifts
- Form 990 for 2024: revenue $19,394,466, of which sales of assets $7,985,372, net inventory sales $6,191,425, contributions $1,822,009 and investment income $1,823,964; expenses $11,800,874 (ProPublica).
- Public money: the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs with the City Council, and the New York State Council on the Arts (museum website).
- In 2022 the city awarded $4.5 million in capital funding, about $1.5 million from Mayor Eric Adams and the rest from Queens Borough President Donovan Richards, to restore Noguchi's 1959 studio and build a 6,000 square foot collection and archive building (Wikipedia).
- Foundations: the Windgate Foundation Art Education Fund pays for education; lead support comes from Bloomberg Philanthropies, the Henry Luce Foundation, the J.M. Kaplan Fund, the Leon Levy Foundation, the Terra Foundation for American Art and an anonymous donor (museum website).
- Its annual benefit on 6 October 2026 presented the Isamu Noguchi Award to Danh Vo and Yohji Yamamoto (museum website).

Red flags: 2024 keffiyeh ban and the firing of three gallery attendants
- The museum issued a new staff dress code on 14 August 2024 barring political messages, which banned keffiyehs (ArtReview; The Art Newspaper).
- A petition against it was signed by about two thirds of the 72 staff (The Art Newspaper; ArtReview counts 54); 14 staff walked out on 21 August (The Art Newspaper; ArtReview counts eight).
- Three gallery attendants, Trasonia Abbott, Natalie Cappellini and Q. Chen, were fired in September 2024 for refusing to comply (The Art Newspaper, 11 September and 19 December 2024).
- The museum said it had decided "to remove political statements from our workplace" (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: 2024 protests against the dress code
- On 8 September 2024 supporters rallied outside the museum and called on director Amy Hau to step down (The Art Newspaper).
- Jhumpa Lahiri withdrew her acceptance of the 2024 Isamu Noguchi Award over the dress code (Al Jazeera; ArtReview).
- In December 2024 anonymous activists placed mock labels around the museum, renaming a bench the "Bench of Banishment" (The Art Newspaper).
- The World Socialist Web Site, a partisan outlet, reported that on 6 June 2025 the Noguchi Community, of current and former employees and community members, called for a boycott of the museum and its leaders' resignation.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art media covered the dress code, the firings, the protests and the union drive, not the museum's funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anni Irish, "Noguchi Museum fires three employees for failing to comply with ban on overtly political dress" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/09/11/noguchi-museum-employees-fired-dress-code-keffiyeh-ban
- Quotes Cappellini calling it ironic for a cultural institution to ban a cultural garment, given Noguchi's own time in a wartime camp.
2. The Art Newspaper: Torey Akers, "Workers at the Noguchi Museum push to form a union" (2024)

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum carries no flag. Its weak point is the 2024 dress code and the firings that followed; watch whether the code stays and what the union contract brings.
