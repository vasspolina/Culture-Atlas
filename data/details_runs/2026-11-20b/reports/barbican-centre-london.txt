BARBICAN CENTRE: London, UK
ID: barbican-centre-london
Website: https://www.barbican.org.uk (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Arts centre on Silk Street in the City of London, inside the Barbican Estate (Wikipedia).
- Built as the City of London's gift to the nation at a cost of £161 million and opened in 1982 (Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Chamberlin, Powell and Bon and listed Grade II (Wikipedia; Barbican website).
- Venues: Barbican Hall (1,943 seats), Barbican Theatre (1,156), The Pit (200), three cinemas, an art gallery and The Curve (Wikipedia).
- Leadership: Abigail Pogson, formerly chief executive of The Glasshouse International Centre for Music, became CEO on 5 January 2026, appointed by the City of London Corporation (Barbican press release, 2 September 2025).
- Governed by the Barbican Centre Board, a committee of the City of London Corporation; the Barbican says its artistic programme is decided independently (Barbican website).

FUNDING
Barbican Centre: mainly public: the City of London Corporation, with ticket, commercial and donated income
- The Barbican calls the City of London Corporation its "founder and principal funder"; other revenue comes from tickets, venue hire, donations, memberships, sponsorships, touring shows, bars and shops (Barbican website).
- Core funding: the 2024/25 business plan budgeted £20.511 million from the Corporation, up from £17.573 million in 2023/24, about 40% of income against 60% earned (Barbican 2024/25 Business Plan).
- Renewal: in December 2024 the Corporation's Court of Common Council approved £191 million for repairs and upgrades, about 80% of the first phase of a five-year renewal programme; a fundraising campaign from 2025 seeks the rest for the foyers, lakeside terrace and conservatory (International Arts Manager).
- Barbican Centre Trust (charity 294282): raised £2.29 million in grants and donations in 2024/25 and granted £1.55 million to the Centre (Charity Commission register; Trust accounts).
- Named Trust donors in 2024/25: the Bagri Foundation and Henry Moore Foundation (Citra Sasmita's Curve commission), the John S Cohen Foundation and CHK Foundation (schools work), Cockayne Grants for the Arts, the Stanley Thomas Johnson Foundation, and individual donors Henrietta Moore, Marcus Margulies, Trevor Fenwick and Jane Hindley (Trust accounts).
- Corporate membership and sponsorship go straight to the Centre; with the Trust's income they reached £2.2 million in 2024/25 (Trust accounts).

Red flags: 2024 protest and loan withdrawals over a cancelled Gaza lecture
- On 6 February 2024 the Guardian reported that the Barbican had decided not to host Pankaj Mishra's London Review of Books lecture "The Shoah after Gaza"; it took place at St James's Church, Clerkenwell, on 28 February (campaign page).
- The Barbican said the event had been advertised "prematurely" and that "There was no intention to censor the speaker involved" (Barbican letter, quoted on the campaign page).
- Collectors Lorenzo Legarda Leviste and Fahad Mayet withdrew two Loretta Pettway quilts from "Unravel: The Power and Politics of Textiles in Art"; Yto Barrada (7 March), Cian Dayrit and Diedrick Brackens (8 March), Mounira al Solh (9 March) and Art Jameel (11 March) followed, nine works in all (campaign page, run by Leviste and Mayet).

Red flags: 2026 open letter over the departure of its arts director
- Over 170 cultural figures, among them John Akomfrah, Isaac Julien, Salman Rushdie and Pankaj Mishra, asked the Barbican Board and the City of London Corporation to explain the departure of Devyani Saltzman, director for arts and participation (ArtReview, February 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage centres on the 2024 lecture cancellation, the 2021 staff accounts of racism and the 2026 open letter.
1. Hyperallergic: Maya Pontone, "Lenders Withdraw Works From Barbican Show, Alleging 'Censorship'" (5 March 2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/875873/lenders-withdraw-works-from-barbican-show-alleging-censorship/
- Reports the lenders' withdrawals after the Barbican decided not to host a talk on the Holocaust and Israel's attacks on Gaza.
- Behind a registration wall; only the headline and standfirst were read.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "London's Barbican shakes up staff following racism allegations published in tell-all book" (10 August 2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/08/10/londons-barbican-shakes-up-staff-following-racism-allegations-published-in-tell-all-book
- "Barbican Stories" gathers more than 100 accounts since 2014 from current and former staff, who called the work culture "subtle and insidiously" racist.
- The City of London Corporation commissioned Lewis Silkin LLP to review staff experiences of racism; managing director Nicholas Kenyon welcomed it.
3. ArtReview: unsigned, "Cultural figures sign open letter over departure of Barbican arts director" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/cultural-figures-sign-open-letter-over-departure-of-barbican-arts-director/
- Quotes the letter: the exit "after a comparatively short time in post" raises questions about "global majority leadership"; it asks for diversity data and an independent review of senior appointments.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from the City of London Corporation, with trust and foundation gifts that carry no flagged industry. The weak points are governance: the 2024 lecture cancellation and the 2026 leadership dispute.
