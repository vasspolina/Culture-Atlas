GRUNT GALLERY: Vancouver, Canada
ID: grunt-gallery-vancouver
Website: https://grunt.ca

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run centre in the Mount Pleasant neighbourhood of Vancouver, British Columbia, at 116-350 East 2nd Avenue (grunt website, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1984 by eight artists: Hillary Wood, Dawn Richards, Kempton Dexter, Glenn Alteen, spike mckinley, Danielle Peacock, Billy Gene Wallace and Garry Ross; Ross designed the logo (grunt website).
- Wikipedia says the first meetings for the Vancouver Fringe Festival took place at grunt in 1985, and that a weekly jazz improvisation series, Jazz in the Gallery, ran from 1987 to 1994.
- It bought a facility in the Main Space building on East 2nd Avenue in 1995 (grunt press release, 11 June 2014).
- Since the early 1990s it has worked with Indigenous art communities, which the gallery says has been central to its identity; its activities have grown to residencies, public art, international exchanges and an archive (grunt website). It created the Live Biennial of Performance Art in 2001 (Wikipedia).
- The gallery is on the unceded territories of the Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-Waututh Nations (grunt website).
- Visiting: open Tuesday, Friday and Saturday noon to 5 pm and Wednesday and Thursday noon to 7 pm. Entrance ramp, automated front doors, no stairs in public areas, one gender-neutral washroom, and low-sensory voice-off hours on Thursdays from noon to 7 pm (grunt website).

FUNDING
grunt gallery: mainly public: federal, provincial and city arts funding, with foundations, donors and a capital grant for its building
- The 2012 to 2013 annual report lists as public funders the Canada Council for the Arts, the British Columbia Arts Council, Heritage Canada, Cultural Spaces Canada, the City of Vancouver, the Canada Interactive Fund, the Canada Cultural Investment Fund (Endowment Incentives Component), Gaming Direct Access, the Government of Nunavut and the Saskatchewan Arts Board.
- The same report lists the Hamber Foundation, the McLean Foundation, the Michael O'Brian Family Foundation, the Audain Foundation and the Vancouver Foundation as foundation funders, and thanks individual donors to its endowment fund.
- The endowment fund was set up in 2006 through the Vancouver Foundation (Wikipedia); the gallery says the province matched funding that year and that $545,000 had been raised in the endowment by June 2014 (grunt press release, 11 June 2014).
- On 11 June 2014 grunt announced a $400,000 cultural capital grant from the Rize Alliance Cultural Amenity Contribution fund, which supports arts organisations in and adjacent to the Mount Pleasant area. The grant will pay off its mortgage and supply $240,000 towards purchasing a new facility; program director Glenn Alteen thanked RIZE and the City of Vancouver (grunt press release).

BOTTOM LINE
grunt gallery is an artist-run centre paid for mainly by Canadian federal, provincial and city arts funders, according to its 2012 to 2013 annual report, with foundations, individual donors and a $400,000 capital grant in 2014 from the Rize Alliance Cultural Amenity Contribution fund behind its building and endowment. The documented funders are public bodies and charitable foundations.
