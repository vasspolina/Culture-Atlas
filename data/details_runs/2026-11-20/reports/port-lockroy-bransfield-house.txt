PORT LOCKROY (BASE A, BRANSFIELD HOUSE): Goudier Island, Antarctica
ID: port-lockroy-bransfield-house
Website: https://ukaht.org/places/port-lockroy/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Former British base on Goudier Island, in a sheltered harbour off Wiencke Island on the west side of the Antarctic Peninsula (UKAHT).
- Set up on 11 February 1944 under Operation Tabarin, a secret wartime mission of the British Government to establish a permanent presence and assert territorial claims; it ran as an atmospheric research base and wireless hub until January 1962 (UKAHT).
- Designated Historic Site and Monument no. 61 in 1995; a British Antarctic Survey team restored the buildings in 1996, and the base has been a museum since November 1996 (UKAHT).
- Three buildings: Bransfield House (museum, post office and shop), built round prefabricated timber from naval stores; a boatshed of 1957; and a modern reproduction of a 1944 Nissen hut, now staff housing (UKAHT).
- The Penguin Post Office is the UK's most southerly public post office, run by the Trust for the territory's government (Government of the British Antarctic Territory). In 2025-26 the team posted 366.5 kg of mail, about 50,000 postcards (UKAHT).
- Visiting: open to ship passengers in the austral summer, November to March (Government of the British Antarctic Territory). In 2025-26, 189 ship and yacht visits brought 22,743 visitors, 11,450 of whom landed; 14 staff worked there over 128 days, and 99.6% of the island's power came from solar (UKAHT).
- The team monitors the island's wildlife for the British Antarctic Survey (UKAHT); half the island is open to visitors and half is kept for the gentoo colony (Wikipedia). The 2025-26 count was 445 gentoo couples and 876 chicks (UKAHT).
- A three-year conservation programme began in 2025-26 after floor timbers under the ionospheric room proved unable to carry their loads, including the half-tonne "Beastie" ionosonde. The 2026-27 team is fitting up to six roof beams and 30 floor props, and the ionospheric room is closed to visitors this season (UKAHT; PA in The Irish News, 12 October 2026).

FUNDING
Port Lockroy: self-funded through its shop and post office, inside a charity backed by UK government bodies, trusts and cruise operators
- Operator: the UK Antarctic Heritage Trust (charity no. 1160847, Cambridge), which took full responsibility for the site in 2006. Shop and post office proceeds pay for running Port Lockroy and help pay for other British historic sites (UKAHT).
- Trust income in the year to 30 April 2025 was £1,645,353: £927,850 turnover of its trading subsidiary Antarctic Heritage Limited (the Port Lockroy and online shops) and £715,974 in donations, membership, grants and events. Spending was £1,689,840, 26% of it on Port Lockroy (2024-25 accounts).
- The £715,974 breaks down as membership £163,990, grants £281,673, donations £134,653, British Antarctic Territory post office £34,385, legacies £29,006, event income £46,626, gift aid £6,851, sponsorship £5,000 and gifts in kind £13,790 (2024-25 accounts).
- Major partners named in the 2024-25 report: the British Antarctic Survey, the Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, the Natural Environment Research Council and the Government of the British Antarctic Territory.
- Conservation and project supporters named in the same report include HX Hurtigruten Expeditions, Hapag Lloyd, Polar Latitudes, Polar Routes and Quark Expeditions, and the Garfield Weston Foundation, the Headley Trust and the Charles Hayward Foundation. HX says it supports series 3 of the Trust's podcast "A Voyage to Antarctica" (HX).
- The Government of the British Antarctic Territory paid for that 1996 restoration (Government of the British Antarctic Territory).
- The current Bransfield House repairs are paid by a donor appeal; visitors made 1,541 donations on site in 2025-26 (UKAHT).
- Trust governance: patron the Princess Royal, chair Kim Crosbie, chief executive Camilla Nichol (2024-25 accounts).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Port Lockroy is clean by the atlas's measures. The weak point is its reliance on cruise tourism, both for sales and for sponsors. Worth watching: whether the appeal covers the structural repair.
