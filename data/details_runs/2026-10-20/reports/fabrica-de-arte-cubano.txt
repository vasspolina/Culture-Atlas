FÁBRICA DE ARTE CUBANO: Havana, Cuba
ID: fabrica-de-arte-cubano
Website: https://fabricadeartecubano.com/ (checked 2026-10-20; serves gambling and spam links in its footer; the older address fac.cu did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Interdisciplinary cultural centre and night venue at Calle 26 and 11 in Vedado, Havana, mixing music, visual art, film, dance, fashion, theatre and food (stated by the space).
- Founded by the musician X Alfonso; after a run at the Pabexpo fairgrounds from mid-2010 it opened at its current site on 13 February 2014 with a concert by Silvio Rodríguez (CubaPLUS, 2026).
- Housed in the former El Cocinero cooking-oil factory, built in 1910 and recognisable by its brick chimney (CubaPLUS; Wikipedia; OnCuba).
- Galleries, concert and theatre spaces, film screenings and a bar (OnCuba, 2014). FAC says it programmes more than 288 concerts a year, gives over 300 direct jobs and runs free workshops for thousands of children and young people (communiqué via Periódico Cubano, 2026).
- Visiting: open Fridays and Saturdays, 8 pm to 2 am (stated by the space). In 2014 entry cost 50 Cuban pesos plus a consumption card (OnCuba). Closed temporarily in June 2026 except for announced special events (Prensa Latina). No reporting found on accessibility.

FUNDING
Fábrica de Arte Cubano: self-funded: door income, members and private actors, in a state-owned building (stated by the space)
- In 2014 the Cuban Music Institute and the Ministry of Culture covered some costs until the project became self-financing; door income went to X Alfonso's business, with a share kept for the building's upkeep (OnCuba, 2014).
- In its 1 January 2026 communiqué FAC said it receives no government money, that it depends on its members and private actors, and that its director and private businesses in the space paid for infrastructure and maintenance (stated by the space, via Periódico Cubano).
- FAC says it has no premises of its own: a state cultural institution oversees the state-owned building and collects the box office (stated by the space). Spanish Wikipedia says a group of artists and musicians bought the factory in 2008; the accounts differ.
- No reporting found of named private partners, accounts or amounts.

Red flags: state censorship of a Celia Cruz tribute
- Teatro El Público's Celia Cruz centenary tribute at FAC, set for 19 October 2025, was cancelled with no official reason given. The Centro Nacional de Música Popular, attached to the Ministry of Culture, posted the notice; Voz, openly critical of the Cuban government, does not say who decided.
- Actor Daniel Triana called the show "censurado"; historian Rosa Marquetti said participants reported "órdenes inapelables y amenazas" (Voz).
- FAC said in its January 2026 communiqué that the event "fue censurado a última hora" (Periódico Cubano, which reports only FAC's account).

Red flags: state pressure after an admission dispute (FAC's account)
- On 26 December 2025 FAC staff refused entry to Alejandro Bridón and two friends under the "right of admission"; he called it racial discrimination (Periódico Cubano).
- On 31 December 2025 the Instituto Cubano de la Música called the refusal "absurda" and announced disciplinary sanctions against the worker (Periódico Cubano).
- X Alfonso said security had mistaken the man for someone linked to thefts, apologised to him, and called the ministry "una espina en el zapato" since July 2018 (Periódico Cubano).
- Unverified: an official campaign to discredit FAC, from FAC and X Alfonso, parties to the dispute (Periódico Cubano).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism. Two pieces criticise FAC as an exclusive, tourist-led business; neither questions where its money comes from.
1. Havana Times: Daisy Valera, "Havana's 'Cuban Art Factory': Poetic Consumption for the New Middle Class" (2014)
URL: https://havanatimes.org/?p=104847
- Calls FAC "the first cultural center designed for the youth of Cuba's emerging middle class" and its consumption "exclusive"; the organiser says prices are aimed at average-income people.
2. OnCuba: Carlos Ávila Villamar, "Fábrica tomada" (2017)
URL: https://oncubanews.com/cultura/artes-visuales/fabrica-tomada
- Argues FAC became "un mero y colosal negocio" filled more by tourists than Cubans, because the Havana public it was built for proved smaller than expected.
- Says Havana's public shares the blame.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Prensa Latina (partisan) blamed the US embargo, "impuesto a Cuba por más de seis décadas", for the June 2026 closure; FAC itself said only that its difficulties "son parte de un contexto más amplio".

BOTTOM LINE
FAC says its money is its own takings and private partners, with early state help in 2014, but it works in a state building whose box office a state institution collects. The weak point is state control: a Ministry of Culture music centre posted the cancellation of a show there in October 2025, and the Music Institute announced sanctions against a worker in December 2025. Worth watching: whether it reopens after the June 2026 closure.
