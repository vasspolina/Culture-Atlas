KOREAN CENTRAL HISTORY MUSEUM: Pyongyang, North Korea
ID: korean-central-history-museum-pyongyang
Website: none (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- National history museum at the north end of Kim Il Sung Square, Pyongyang, in one of the neoclassical buildings round the square (Wikipedia, Koryo Tours).
- Established on 1 December 1945 by Kim Il Sung, originally on Moran Hill (Wikipedia, citing the museum's curator O Hae Ryong on Voice of Korea); the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture, published by the Academy of Korean Studies in South Korea, says it opened in the Moranbong district as the National Central History Museum and became the Korean History Museum in August 1964.
- Wikipedia says the first building was destroyed in the Korean War, which it attributes to US troops, and the museum was rebuilt on the square in 1960, with 1977 given as an alternative date. Koryo Tours and the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture say it moved there in 1977, when it took its present name.
- The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture describes a three-storey building that faces the Korean Art Gallery across the square, with a central hall holding a statue of Kim Il Sung, and says the interior and the relic store were repaired and modernised in 2006.
- Wikipedia counts 19 rooms, 10,500 square metres and about 100,000 relics. The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture gives 103,000 items in 19 rooms. Koryo Tours says "over 10,000 m2" in 19 halls, as an official figure, and over 4,000 objects.
- Displays run from the Old Stone Age to the March First Uprising of 1919, including a mock-up of the Tomb of Tangun and the "Lolang Basket" (Koryo Tours). Koryo Tours says politicised lessons on the struggle against invaders, oppression and revolution are woven in, and calls the evidence for Tangun "supposed".
- The first room shows a painting and photographs of Kim Il Sung and Kim Jong Il visiting historic sites; later rooms cover Kojoson, Koguryo, Paekche, Silla, Palhae, Koryo and the Joseon period, and the last two cover resistance to foreign incursions from the burning of the US ship General Sherman in 1866 to the Japanese occupation (North Korea Information Portal of South Korea's Ministry of Unification).
- The North Korea Information Portal says the museum holds exhibitions and runs exchanges and cooperation with international museum bodies and museums in other countries.
- Wikipedia says that in 2014 Kim Jong Un wanted it to serve as a base standard for history museums, and that the museum claimed 10 million visitors since 1945 by 2015. Both claims come from state-linked media.
- Visiting: open to foreign tourists. Photography inside is prohibited. A small shop sells ceramics and celadon (Koryo Tours, 21 October 2020).

FUNDING
Korean Central History Museum: state-run: North Korea's central history museum
- The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture says the museum was set up in 1945 to preserve relics and to teach residents North Korea's history, and that it represents and oversees the country's history museums, one in each province-level unit.
- The state announces its budget only as yearly percentage changes. KCNA claimed on 24 March 2026 that 2026 revenue would reach 100.5 percent of 2025 and spending 105.8 percent, with funds for the cultural sector at 103.1 percent of last year's.

Red flags: US sanctions on the North Korean government, 2016
- US Executive Order 13722 of 15 March 2016 blocks property in US jurisdiction of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea; section 9 defines the Government as the DPRK government and its agencies, instrumentalities and controlled entities (Federal Register, 18 March 2016).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state institution at the head of North Korea's history museums, and its money is the state's. The weak points are the US blocking order on that state and the political teaching built into the displays. Worth watching: any independent count of its collection and visitors.
