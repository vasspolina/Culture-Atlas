MIGROS MUSEUM: Zürich, Switzerland
ID: migros-museum
Website: https://migrosmuseum.ch (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at Limmatstrasse 270 in the Löwenbräukunst complex, a former brewery (museum website, Facts and Figures).
- Founded in 1996 on the initiative of Arina Kowner, head of culture and social affairs at the Migros cooperative federation, and collection head Rein Wolfs, who became founding director; it opened as "Museum für Gegenwartskunst Zürich" and took the name Migros Museum für Gegenwartskunst in 1998 (Facts and Figures).
- Named for Migros, the retail cooperative whose founder Gottlieb Duttweiler began buying art in the mid-1950s; the museum grew out of that corporate collection and the InK exhibition hall that Urs Raussmüller ran from 1978 to 1981 (Facts and Figures, Migros corporate timeline).
- The brewery was built in 1898 and stopped production in 1986. Gigon/Guyer and Atelier WW renovated it from 2010 to 2012, giving the museum two exhibition floors of about 1,300 square metres; the museum worked from a temporary site in Zürich-Albisrieden from July 2010 to June 2012 (Facts and Figures).
- The collection holds about 1,400 works by 700 artists, with a core of about 600 works by about 300 artists bought since the late 1970s (Facts and Figures). Since 1 December 2023 a five-person collective runs the museum instead of a single director (e-flux).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00, Thursday 11:00 to 20:00; admission is free (museum website). Free guided tours are offered to visitors with disabilities, and the museum holds the "Kultur inklusiv" bronze label since 2026.

FUNDING
Migros Museum: corporate-funded: Migros-Kulturprozent, paid by the Migros cooperative federation
- The museum is an institution of the Migros-Kulturprozent, carried by the Migros-Genossenschafts-Bund (Facts and Figures, stated by the museum).
- Since 1957 the federation pays an annual sum into the Kulturprozent, calculated on turnover and paid even when retail trade declines; it is written into Migros's statutes (Migros-Kulturprozent website). The Kulturprozent invests CHF 121 million a year in culture, society, education, leisure and the economy (Migros-Kulturprozent website).
- The Migros-Genossenschafts-Bund, the City of Zürich and the Stiftung Kunsthalle Zürich each hold one third of Löwenbräu-Kunst AG, which bought the art complex in 2011 and rents the west wing to the museum (Facts and Figures).
- No reporting found of the museum's own budget, a donor list or a public grant.
- The museum's older fact sheet lists adult tickets at CHF 12; its current website says admission is free.

Red flags: fossil fuels, through the parent group's fuel business
- Migros owns the fuel supplier Migrol. Migrol's sales fell 6.5% to CHF 1.3 billion in 2025, against group sales of CHF 31.9 billion, about 4% (Moneycab, 16 January 2026).
- Migrol is reported within the Food Retail segment, which reached CHF 24.3 billion (Moneycab).
- No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at the museum over Migrol.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid by the Migros cooperative's turnover-based culture sum, with no public grant or private donor named. The weak points are that the sole funder is a retailer that also sells fuel through Migrol (about 4% of group sales in 2025) and that the museum publishes no budget or donor list of its own. Watch whether the museum publishes accounts under the new five-person leadership.
