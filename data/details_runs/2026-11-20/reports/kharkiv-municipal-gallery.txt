KHARKIV MUNICIPAL GALLERY: Kharkiv, Ukraine
ID: kharkiv-municipal-gallery
Website: https://mgallery.kharkiv.ua (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery at 15 Chernyshevska Street, Kharkiv, created in 1996; it calls itself the first gallery in Ukraine with municipal status (gallery website). It joined the Association of Art Galleries of Ukraine in 1997 (Wikipedia).
- Co-founded by the curator Tatyana Tumasyan, its director since the start (gallery website); she is also art director of the private VOVATANYA Gallery (Ukrainian Wikipedia).
- Two spaces: a main hall of 100 square metres and the 140 square metre ARTbasement, opened in 2008 for young and experimental artists (gallery website).
- Programmes: the NonStopMedia festival of young artists every two years since 2003, a curatorial intensive first held in 2015, Night of Museums in Kharkiv since 2009, Night of Performance since 2016, the "Our Heritage" series on 20th-century Kharkiv artists and residency exchanges since 2011 (Ukrainian Wikipedia).
- Artists shown include Borys Mykhailov, Sergiy Bratkov, Pavlo Makov, Vagrich Bakhchanyan and Gamlet Zinkivskyi (gallery website).
- Wartime: the upper halls have been closed since February 2022; the ARTbasement served as a shelter for families in the first six months of the war and reopened as an exhibition space on 3 September 2023 (gallery website). Tumasyan said the gallery was closed to visitors by order of the Ministry of Culture (Lyuk, 10 March 2023).
- In March 2023 it had six staff, two of them in Kharkiv (Objectiv.tv, 11 March 2023).
- 2026 programme: shows by Anna Moskalets, Vladyslav Krasnoshchok and a memorial show for Vitalii Kulikov in the ARTbasement, and a Sergey Bratkov performance during the preview week of the 61st Venice Biennale with the CityX Foundation (gallery website).
- Visiting: ARTbasement open Wednesday to Sunday, 1pm to 6pm, in the courtyard behind the grey gates at 15 Chernyshevska Street (gallery website).

FUNDING
Kharkiv Municipal Gallery: municipal: an institution of Kharkiv city
- Premises: the city council gave the gallery the building in operational management in 1996; Tumasyan said it is a city building and the gallery is not its owner (Lyuk, 10 March 2023).
- Repairs: the main rooms were badly damaged in March 2022 by missile strikes on nearby buildings; Tumasyan said the gallery repaired them in late autumn 2022 with money allocated by the city (Lyuk, 10 March 2023). The gallery credits "the joint efforts of the city and friends of the gallery" (gallery website).
- Control: the city council's culture department and Mayor Ihor Terekhov decided in 2023 how the premises are used (Objectiv.tv, 11 March 2023).
- CityX: an NFT project launched in 2022 with a collection of art by Kharkiv artists, co-organised by the Municipal Gallery and VOVATANYA. Gwara Media reported that American partners started it with an NFT agency from Tbilisi, to restore war-damaged Kharkiv architecture and support artists, their families and galleries (Gwara Media, 7 Sep 2022; gallery website).

Red flags: 2023 handover of the gallery's hall to the city news studio
- From February 2023 the city council planned to put the municipal news programme "Kharkiv: novyny" in the closed gallery and move staff to the Repin children's art school at 8A Yesenina Street (Objectiv.tv; Lyuk).
- On 10 March 2023 workers removed the gallery's furniture; the gallery's accountant Yaroslava Zamyslova said the culture department's deputy Tetiana Marakhovska told staff to take out the library and the collection too, though staff had been told these would stay (Lyuk). The curator Anastasiia Khlestova wrote that the city wanted rooms the gallery had just repaired, including a specially built art store (Lyuk).
- After a public outcry, Terekhov phoned Tumasyan on 11 March 2023; the gallery said he promised the studio would stay only during martial law, that the city guarantees the premises' return, and that the collection, archive and library would stay sealed on site. The gallery said it was waiting for documented guarantees (Objectiv.tv).
- The gallery's website states that its "White Cube" hall still serves as a city information media outlet.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean public money: the gallery depends on the city for its premises and repairs. The weak point is that the same dependence lets the mayor's office take its space at will. Worth watching: whether the hall returns to the gallery and who pays for CityX.
