ALMATY MUSEUM OF ARTS: Almaty, Kazakhstan
ID: almaty-museum-of-arts-almaty
Website: https://www.almaty.art (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Private modern and contemporary art museum in Almaty, at the intersection of Nazarbayev Avenue and Al-Farabi Avenue (Wikipedia); the first private museum of its kind in Kazakhstan (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the businessman and collector Nurlan Smagulov, president and founder of the Astana Group; opened to the public on 12 September 2025 after a press preview on 9 September (Forbes Kazakhstan; Wikipedia). The critic Valeria Ibraeva says in Fergana News that the cornerstone was laid in 2021.
- Building by the British practice Chapman Taylor: two interlocking volumes, one clad in limestone and one in aluminium, around an atrium called Art Street, at the foot of the Tian Shan mountains (Chapman Taylor).
- Collection of more than 700 works from Smagulov's own holdings, about 70% by Kazakh artists of the Soviet period; international rooms for Anselm Kiefer, Yayoi Kusama, Bill Viola and Jaume Plensa (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Forbes Kazakhstan reported a standard ticket of 2,000 tenge and a price of 500 tenge for some groups at opening, with free entry for people with disabilities and veterans.

FUNDING
Almaty Museum of Arts: corporate-sponsored by its founder's company, with a bank as 2026 lead partner
- Founder's money: Forbes Kazakhstan (Tatyana Panchenko, 10 September 2025) quotes Smagulov: "Now our company Astana Motors finances everything" (translated from Russian). He said ticket sales would not cover even cleaning, and that he plans to bring in other founders, sponsors and donors, a friends' club and an endowment fund from Astana Motors. After his departure he foresaw hybrid management with state participation and a professional board of trustees.
- Cost: Wikipedia gives about $30M for construction and $70M for the collection and equipment; Bloomberg, as reported by bes.media on 11 May 2026, gives about $70M for acquisitions and $50M for construction.
- Smagulov's fortune comes from car sales and distribution, then commercial property; Bloomberg estimated his net worth at about $1.5 billion in May 2026 (bes.media).
- Halyk Bank: on 5 March 2026 the museum and the bank signed a memorandum making Halyk lead partner for the museum's major exhibitions, education and publishing projects in 2026, including the Bill Viola exhibition opening in June and the second part of Almagul Menlibayeva's "I Understand Everything". (the museum's announcement).

WIDER CONTEXT
- ICIJ (Sydney P. Freedberg, Agustin Armendariz, Tanya Kozyreva, Matei Rosca and Marcos Garcia Rey, "Oil giants ignored red flags, enriched elite for Kazakhstan pipe dream", 22 November 2024, updated 9 December 2024) captions Halyk Bank as majority-owned by Timur Kulibayev, son-in-law of Nursultan Nazarbayev, and says he held a large stake in the bank. ICIJ's concerns centre on the TenizService contracts.
- ICIJ reports that TenizService, partly owned by Kulibayev until 2010, won a no-bid contract from Tengizchevroil in 2012 and turned to Halyk for a $100 million credit line. His lawyers acknowledge an indirect minority stake until 2010, say he had no interest when the contract was awarded, and say they know of no credit line.
- The former banker Mukhtar Ablyazov accuses Kulibayev of taking bribes; Kulibayev's lawyers deny bribery and corruption. In a 2017 Italian case the businessman Agostino Bianchi pleaded guilty to bribing three Kazakh officials, including Kulibayev; the article says Kulibayev was not charged, and his lawyers deny he received payments from Bianchi.
- Connection to the museum: Halyk Bank has been its lead partner since 5 March 2026.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is one businessman's car and property fortune, spent on the building and collection, with running costs paid by his company for now. The 2026 lead partner is Halyk Bank, which ICIJ's reporting on its majority owner Kulibayev links to the TenizService credit line. Worth watching: whether the planned endowment, board of trustees and state participation arrive.
