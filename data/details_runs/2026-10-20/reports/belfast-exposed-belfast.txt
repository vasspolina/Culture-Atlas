BELFAST EXPOSED: Belfast, United Kingdom
ID: belfast-exposed-belfast
Website: https://www.belfastexposed.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Independent photography gallery, archive and training centre at 23 Donegall Street in Belfast's Cathedral Quarter (Belfast Exposed; Wikipedia).
- Started in 1983 when teacher and community activist Danny Burke gathered local photographers for an exhibition of Belfast life; it opened at Conway Mill and the organisation moved to the Cathedral Quarter in 2003 (Belfast Exposed).
- Wikipedia states it was founded "to challenge and subvert media representations of the Troubles-torn city" and was Northern Ireland's first dedicated photographic gallery.
- Premises: four public galleries (Belfast Exposed); Wikipedia lists a 20 by 7 m exhibition gallery, a black-and-white darkroom and a digital editing suite.
- Programmes: a photography training programme, a therapeutic photography programme for mental health and wellbeing, youth programmes, and Futures, a mentoring and exhibiting programme for early-career artists and curators (Belfast Exposed).
- Archive: Belfast Exposed calls it "an archive of international significance"; Wikipedia gives about half a million images.
- Chief executive: Deirdre Robb (Creative Bloq).

FUNDING
Belfast Exposed: mainly public: Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Belfast City Council
- Its support page lists Arts Council of Northern Ireland and Belfast City Council as funders (stated by the space; shown as logos).
- Arts Council NI's 2025-26 Annual Funding Programme was £13.97 million in total: £8.15 million from the Department for Communities and £5.82 million from National Lottery sources (Arts Council NI). A June 2025 reply from the Minister for Communities to the Northern Ireland Assembly lists Belfast Exposed Photography among the organisations that received the award.
- Donations are taken through a Stripe payment page on the support page, and the space cites an independent evaluation by Rose Regeneration of £5.10 social value for each £1 invested (stated by the space).

Red flags: Protest or boycott over AI-generated images in "Beneath the Surface"
- Beneath the Surface, an exhibition by nine Ulster University MFA graduates, included Johnny Sheridan's series "Galatea" of AI-generated images (Creative Bloq, Tom May, 3 August 2026).
- Creative Bloq reports that the online reaction included accusations that Belfast Exposed was promoting "AI slop", and that unnamed campaigners demanded the work's removal, a formal apology and a boycott of the gallery. The gallery is the target of the demands.
- The photographers and artists Chloe Austin and Brendan Harkin objected that AI-generated work has no place on a photography gallery's walls (Creative Bloq).
- Gallery response: the chief executive Deirdre Robb described the work as satirical and controversial in a BBC interview (Creative Bloq). On 22 July the gallery issued a statement that "the views, themes and artistic approaches expressed are those of the individual artists", and on 24 July it held a public panel, "AI, Art and Society", with Robb, the MLAs Kate Nicholl and Sian Mulholland, and Professor Donovan Wylie, director of Ulster University's MFA Photography programme (Creative Bloq).
- Digital Camera World (Alan Palazon, 6 August 2026) reports that Robb and Sheridan both defended the work in a BBC article.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two pieces on one dispute in 2026, about generative AI in a graduate show; both report the objections to the work.
1. Creative Bloq: Tom May, "Everyone in this AI art row has a point, and that's what worries me" (3 August 2026)
URL: https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/everyone-in-this-ai-art-row-has-a-point-and-thats-what-worries-me
- Reports the objections and the demands for removal, an apology and a boycott, and calls the gallery's choice "defensible, provocative".
- Sets out the artists' grievance: generative AI is trained on scraped data without permission or payment. Concludes that both sides argue from principle and that the artists have "a legitimate grievance".
- Names Belfast Exposed and Robb directly.
2. Digital Camera World: Alan Palazon, "It took far more than pressing a button," says creator of controversial AI images shown at Belfast exhibition (6 August 2026)
URL: https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/it-took-far-more-than-pressing-a-button-says-creator-of-controversial-ai-images-shown-at-belfast-exhibition-and-honestly-i-agree
- Reports that the chief executive and Sheridan both defended the work in a BBC article, and that Sheridan says the images took far more than pressing a button.
- The author dislikes generative AI but agrees AI imagery can involve real creative work, and says Sheridan's images are not the best example of this. Does not criticise the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Belfast Exposed is public: Arts Council of Northern Ireland, funded by the Department for Communities and the National Lottery, and Belfast City Council. The one dispute is the 2026 AI exhibition row, in which campaigners called for a boycott and an apology; the gallery issued a statement and held a public panel.
