MANCHESTER MUSEUM: Manchester, UK
ID: manchester-museum
Website: https://www.museum.manchester.ac.uk (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- University museum of natural history, world cultures and archaeology on the University of Manchester campus, Oxford Road.
- Traces its origins to the Manchester Natural History Society and Manchester Geological Society collections, which passed to Owens College, forerunner of the University of Manchester, in 1868 (museum website). It opened on the present site in 1888.
- Not named for a person.
- Building: designed by Alfred Waterhouse (museum website). A pavilion of 1912 was largely funded by the textile merchant Jesse Haworth, and a 1927 extension housed the ethnographic collections (Wikipedia).
- A £15 million redevelopment ("hello future") reopened the museum on 18 February 2023, adding a two-storey extension with a temporary exhibition space and the South Asia Gallery (Apollo). Wikipedia says the South Asia Gallery is run in partnership with the British Museum and gives 18 February 2023 as the reopening date. Other galleries include the Lee Kai Hung Chinese Culture Gallery and a dinosaur hall (The Mill).
- Visiting: free entry. Hours stated by the museum: closed Monday; Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday 10am to 5pm; Wednesday 10am to 9pm; Saturday 8am to 5pm. Changing Places toilet, quiet room and prayer room.

FUNDING
Manchester Museum: mainly public: University of Manchester, Research England (UKRI), Arts Council England and UK Government, plus trusts for the redevelopment
- The museum's supporters page lists four principal funders: Research England (UKRI), the University of Manchester, Arts Council England and UK Government. It gives no amounts for any funder.
- Redevelopment funders listed: Savannah Wisdom Foundation, Lee Kai Hung Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation, Foyle Foundation, John Ellerman Foundation, Art Fund, V&A with Arts Council England (National Lottery funded), Friends of the Nations' Libraries, Oglesby Charitable Trust, Little Greene Paint & Paper, Transport for Greater Manchester, British Council, Headley Trust, B&M Retail, DCMS/Wolfson Museums & Galleries Improvement Fund, Rangoonwala Foundation, The Barker Foundation, Ashok & Anna Kallumpram and Brendan Pittaway. Hyatt Regency and Hyatt House Manchester are official hotel partners.
- Government grant: on 11 December 2020 the museum received a £1,304,489 Capital Kickstart grant from the Culture Recovery Fund; the university then gave the project cost as £13.5 million (University of Manchester). The museum now gives £15 million.
- A £12.5 million Heritage Lottery Fund award in 1997, with other funding, supported the refurbishment that reopened the museum in 2003 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the museum's annual operating budget or of the university's yearly grant to it.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage of the 2023 reopening is mostly admiring, with one caveat about the Chinese gallery. No art-press piece found criticises the museum's funding.
1. Apollo: Tom Emery, "The Manchester Museum is put on community service" (2023)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/manchester-museum-renovation-south-asia-gallery/
- Argues the new displays put the stories behind objects ahead of the objects, with 30 community co-curators and lead curator Nusrat Ahmed on the South Asia Gallery.
- Calls the museum a "real benchmark" for community co-curation, while noting that community projects inside rigid institutions can frustrate the communities involved. This applies to the sector in general, not to Manchester.
- Notes pressure on cash-strapped museums to raise income.
2. The Mill: Joshi Herrmann, "Manchester Museum, fresh from a £15m revamp, has made itself unmissable" (2023)
URL: https://manchestermill.co.uk/manchester-museum-is-now-unmissable/
- Praises the South Asia Gallery but finds the Chinese gallery more cautious and less contemporary, and asks why it omits an attack by Chinese consulate staff on a peaceful protester and Xi Jinping's 2015 visit to the city.
- Notes the gallery was developed with the University of Manchester's China Institute and that the university relies heavily on fees from Chinese students. Deputy head of collections Bryan Sitch denied any censorship by the consulate or the Institute and said the museum is funded separately and sought their advice. Director Esme Ward said the museum does not avoid events in China.
- A local news outlet, not an art-press title; the author visited on opening night.
No reporting found in Artforum, Frieze, Hyperallergic or The Art Newspaper of criticism of the museum's funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public and university money plus named UK trusts, with no flagged funder found. The weak points are that no amounts are published for individual funders and that The Mill questioned whether the Chinese gallery, backed by the Lee Kai Hung Foundation and made with the university's China Institute, avoids politically sensitive subjects; the museum's deputy head of collections denies it. Worth watching: how the university's ties to China shape that gallery.
