RIJKSAKADEMIE VAN BEELDENDE KUNSTEN: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: rijksakademie-amsterdam
Website: https://www.rijksakademie.nl (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Postgraduate art academy and residency at Sarphatistraat 470, Amsterdam, in a former cavalry barracks it has occupied since 1992 (Wikipedia, Rijksakademie site).
- Founded in 1870 by King William III, succeeding the Koninklijke Academie, the Stads Teekenacademie and a 17th-century Konstkamer; it became an independent art institution in November 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Offers a residency of one or two years, most staying two, with a private studio and a stipend; about 23 artists are selected each year, roughly 11 from the Netherlands and 12 from other countries (Rijksakademie site).
- On site: workshops, a library, a public programme and annual Open Studios (Rijksakademie site).
- Open Studios 2025 ran from 22 to 25 May; tickets were online only, EUR 12.50 (EUR 6.25 discounted), free for children up to 12. The building is wheelchair accessible (Rijksakademie site).

FUNDING
Rijksakademie: mainly public: Dutch ministry money, with Ammodo as main partner and a long list of Dutch funds
- The Rijksakademie says it is mainly financed by the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science (Rijksakademie site). No reporting found of the amounts, or of the share of income raised from other sources.
- Ammodo is main partner: it has contributed since 2012 and in 2022 committed to being main partner until 2026, funding lectures, the alumni network, guest advisors, residents' work budgets and Open Studios (Rijksakademie site). The page gives no amount.
- Fellowship support, named by the Rijksakademie: De Nederlandsche Bank, Het Cultuurfonds, the Don Quixote Foundation, the Jacqueline de Jong Foundation. International partners include the Han Nefkens Foundation, Pro Helvetia and Arts Council Korea.
- Technical labs: Rabobank Nederland. Open Studios 2026: Brouwerij 't IJ and Fonds 21.
- Residents pay a participation fee of EUR 1,875 a year and receive a stipend of EUR 19,800 (2026) (Rijksakademie site).

Red flags: main partner Ammodo's source of money
- Ammodo says its funding comes mainly from the Inphykem Foundation, formerly the sole shareholder of the pension insurer Optas N.V., which was sold to Aegon in 2007. Ammodo says the insured parties then claimed part of the proceeds, that Inphykem contributed EUR 500 million once to increase pension entitlements, and that the dispute closed in 2010 (Ammodo statement, stated by the funder).
- Opinion: Timo Demollin, in a "Retort" essay for Platform BK, a membership organisation for visual artists, calls the sale of Optas "rather dubious" because it took place without the knowledge of the employers and employees involved (Platform BK, 24 March 2021).
- The same essay says that in 2014 six scientists and members of the KNAW opposed the presentation of the Ammodo KNAW Award (Platform BK).
- Ammodo issued a statement on the Zwarte Zwanen programme broadcast on NPO 2 on 23 April 2025, saying it does not recognise itself in the account, which it calls "highly one-sided." No reporting found of the broadcast's content.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. One opinion essay from a membership organisation for visual artists names the academy and its funder.
1. Platform BK: Timo Demollin, "Retort #24: The Philanthropy Trap" (2021)
URL: https://www.platformbk.nl/en/the-philanthropy-trap/
- Argues Dutch art institutions depend ever more on private philanthropy after public cuts, giving donors influence.
- Finds it ironic that the Rijksakademie is launching a "social practice" workshop while Ammodo is at the top of its patrons list.
- Says that young artists at De Ateliers, which announced a four-year collaboration with Ammodo, "cannot afford to criticise their patron." This point is about De Ateliers, not the Rijksakademie.
- Names the Rijksakademie and Ammodo.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Unverified: an open letter and call to action posted in December 2024 on rijksakademie.art, an advocacy site, says 12 staff members left in 18 months, three supervisory board members resigned in the previous year, only one board member is an alumnus, and the institute would have no director from summer 2025.
- The letter asks for a community audit of finances and conflicts of interest involving board members and a public meeting on 17 January 2025. No reporting found of the board's reply or of independent coverage.
- The letter concerns the academy's governance. It does not name Ammodo, and no page read shows a funder acting in the dispute.

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the money is Dutch ministry funding, with a list of named Dutch funds and companies; no amounts were found. The weak point is the main partner Ammodo, whose money comes from the 2007 Optas sale, which the funder defends and one opinion essay calls dubious. Worth watching: what follows Ammodo's main-partner term, which runs to 2026, and the amounts in the annual reports.
