MUSÉE NATIONAL DES BEAUX-ARTS DU QUÉBEC: Quebec City, Canada
ID: musée-national-des-beaux-arts-du-québec-quebec-city
Website: https://www.mnbaq.org/

OVERVIEW
- Quebec's national fine arts museum, in Battlefields Park, Quebec City: a complex of four buildings connected by tunnels (Wikipedia).
- A fine-arts collection was created in 1920; the Musée de la Province was inaugurated in 1933, became the Musée du Québec in 1963, a state corporation under the Loi sur les musées nationaux in 1984, and took its present name in 2002 (museum website).
- Buildings (Wikipedia): the Gérard-Morisset Pavilion by Wilfrid Lacroix (built 1928 to 1931), the Charles Baillairgé Pavilion (a prison of 1867 renovated in 1989 to 1991), the Grand Hall visitor centre, and the Pierre Lassonde Pavilion by the Office for Metropolitan Architecture, opened in June 2016 at a cost of about C$103.4 million. Construction of the Espace Riopelle began in spring 2024 (museum website).
- Collection of more than 42,500 works (museum website). In 2023-2024 the museum had 248,643 visitors, 187 employees and 14 exhibitions (2023-2024 management report).
- Governance: a board of 15 administrators (museum website). The Fondation du Musée raises private gifts; a permanent honour roll in the Pierre Lassonde pavilion's grand hall records donors of C$5,000 or above (management report).

FUNDING
Musée national des beaux-arts du Québec: state corporation of the Government of Quebec, with corporate sponsors and donors
- Revenue in 2023-2024 was C$34,142,457 (management report). Within it: Quebec government grants C$24,454,620, Government of Canada grants C$1,261,235, other grants and contributions C$513,106, contributions from the Fondation du Musée C$1,424,616, donated collection items C$2,618,320, ticketing and memberships C$1,504,420, sponsorships C$229,332 and investment income C$437,834.
- Sponsors named for 2023-2024: La Maison Simons, presenting sponsor of "Alexander McQueen. L'art rencontre la mode", Air Canada as partner sponsor, and Hydro-Québec, presenting sponsor of family and public activities for many years; RBC Fondation funded the Prix MNBAQ en art actuel; Caisse Desjardins du Plateau Montcalm, Fondation J. Armand Bombardier and Fondation Madeleine et Jean-Paul Tardif funded specific programmes (management report).
- The Fondation du Musée raised C$280,932 in its 2023-2024 annual campaign (management report). Foundation donors of C$100,000 to C$249,999 that year included the Caisse de dépôt et placement du Québec, Desjardins and RBC Fondation; donors of C$250,000 to C$499,999 were Fondation Rossy, Fondation Sandra et Alain Bouchard and Pierre Lassonde.
- The museum's donors page shows donors by gift tier. The tiers carry no year; a note on the page thanks donors to the 2025-2026 annual campaign. Fondateur (C$10 million and above): Michael Audain and Yoshiko Karasawa, Pierre Lassonde and the Ville de Québec. Exemplaire (C$1 million to C$4,999,999): Hydro-Québec, Banque Nationale du Canada, BMO Groupe financier, Desjardins, Power Corporation du Canada, Québecor and iA Groupe financier. Majeur (C$500,000 to C$999,999): Imperial Oil Limited and Loto-Québec. Remarquables (C$250,000 to C$499,999): Énergir. Importants (C$100,000 to C$249,999): SNC-Lavalin and La Maison Simons. Platine (C$25,000 to C$49,999): Rio Tinto and Osisko.
- Pierre Lassonde is named benefactor of the Pierre Lassonde Pavilion (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a Quebec state corporation: the provincial government grant of C$24.5 million was about 72% of its C$34.1 million revenue in 2023-2024, and sponsorships were C$229,332. Private money comes through the Fondation du Musée and a tiered donor list on the museum's website; Pierre Lassonde's name is on the Pierre Lassonde Pavilion.
