ARTSCIENCE MUSEUM: Singapore, Singapore
ID: artscience-museum-singapore
Website: https://www.marinabaysands.com/museum.html (checked 2026-10-20; timed out on every attempt, so hours below come from the e-flux listing)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of art, science and technology at 6 Bayfront Avenue, inside the Marina Bay Sands integrated resort in Singapore's Downtown Core; Wikipedia calls it the largest private museum in Singapore.
- Built by Las Vegas Sands as part of its Marina Bay Sands casino complex and opened on 17 February 2011 by Prime Minister Lee Hsien Loong (Wikipedia); before the opening a spokesperson said the date was set "as advised by our feng shui master" (Mail & Guardian).
- Designed by Moshe Safdie in a lotus shape: a round base with ten "fingers" of fibre-reinforced plastic on a steel lattice, with skylights at the fingertips and a dish roof that channels rainwater to a reflecting pond.
- Has 21 galleries on about 6,000 square metres (Winkle Picker); the permanent show Future World, made with the Japanese collective teamLab, fills most of the Basement 2 galleries.
- Runs two or three shows at a time, from touring blockbusters (Titanic, 2011 to 2012; The World of Studio Ghibli, 2024 to 2025) to curated shows such as Another World Is Possible (2025) and Lawrence Lek's NOX: Confessions of a Machine (23 January to 19 April 2026).
- Open Sunday to Thursday 10am to 7pm, Friday and Saturday 10am to 9pm (e-flux). No reporting found that could be checked on admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
ArtScience Museum: corporate-owned: part of the Marina Bay Sands casino resort, owned by Las Vegas Sands
- The museum is a department of Marina Bay Sands, not a separate body; Angelita Teo leads it as Marina Bay Sands' Vice President of Attractions (Wikipedia).
- Marina Bay Sands was built by Las Vegas Sands as a $5.5 billion gambling complex (Mail & Guardian, 2011).
- Marina Bay Sands made $5,590 million in net revenue and $2,922 million in adjusted property EBITDA in 2025; casino revenue was $1,204 million in the fourth quarter alone (Las Vegas Sands results release to the SEC, January 2026).
- Las Vegas Sands is majority-held by the Adelson family: 427,894,129 shares, about 56.4 percent, before Miriam Adelson sold $2 billion of stock in 2023 toward a stake in the Dallas Mavericks (iGaming Business).
- No reporting found of public grants, outside sponsors or donors paying for the museum.

Red flags: casino money
- The museum sits inside, and is run by, a casino resort; no reporting found of a separate foundation or budget for the gallery.
- Las Vegas Sands chairman Sheldon Adelson dubbed the building "The Welcoming Hand of Singapore" (Mail & Guardian, January 2011).
- Las Vegas Sands had $4.84 billion available under a delayed draw term loan facility for the Marina Bay Sands expansion project at 28 January 2026 (SEC filing).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism. Art press coverage found is listings and opening news; the only critical pieces are two 2011 reviews of the programme, and none raises the casino ownership.
1. The Daily Star (Star Weekend Magazine): James P Ong, "Lotus, Giant Hand or Flower?" (2011)
URL: https://archive.thedailystar.net/magazine/2011/07/04/architecture.htm
- Praises the building but not the institution: local arts figures gave it an "ambivalent" welcome and questioned what "ArtScience" means.
- Calls the Van Gogh Alive show "essentially an expensive slideshow" and sets it against the S$30 ticket price.
- Does not mention the casino.
2. Winkle Picker: unsigned, "ArtScience Museum, Singapore" (2011)
URL: https://www.winkle-picker.com/?p=1859
- The permanent galleries give only "a cursory impression" of how art and science link, and "feel curiously empty calories."
- The "self-proclaimed iconic architecture" constrains the gallery spaces; the best galleries are underground.
- A journal post on the Winkle Picker site, not an art outlet; the post is unsigned.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is casino money with nothing in between: it is a department of Marina Bay Sands, owned by the Adelson-controlled Las Vegas Sands. The weak point is that ownership itself, which the art press has not examined. Worth watching: the Marina Bay Sands expansion and whether the museum ever publishes its own budget.
