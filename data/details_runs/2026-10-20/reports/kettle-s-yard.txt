KETTLE'S YARD: Cambridge, United Kingdom
ID: kettle-s-yard
Website: https://www.kettlesyard.cam.ac.uk (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- The University of Cambridge's modern and contemporary art gallery: a house with a collection of 20th-century art and a gallery for exhibitions, on Castle Street, about a 30-minute walk from Cambridge main station (stated by Kettle's Yard).
- Founded by Jim Ede and his wife Helen, who moved to Cambridge in 1956 and, with help from Winton Aldridge, converted four small cottages into one house for Ede's collection. Ede gave the house and collection to the University in 1966 (Wikipedia; Heritage Fund).
- Building: a gallery by Leslie Martin was added in 1970. The site closed in June 2015 for a redevelopment that added a four-floor education wing, improved galleries, a new entrance and a cafe; Jamie Fobert Architects added a new courtyard, welcome area and shop. It reopened in February 2018. The interior of the house was left untouched (Wikipedia).
- On site: the house, exhibition galleries, the Clore Learning Studio and a cafe.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 5pm, last house entry 4:15pm. House tickets cost £14.50 (£13.00 without donation); exhibitions are free but booked separately. Free entry for University of Cambridge students, visitors aged 25 and under, Friends and Patrons, and visitors with limited mobility and their assistants. The ground-floor galleries are wheelchair accessible; the house and cafe are not fully accessible.

FUNDING
Kettle's Yard: mainly public and university: the University of Cambridge and Arts Council England, plus earned income, trusts, individuals and corporate supporters
- Annual grants from the University of Cambridge and Arts Council England cover 44% of running costs; the rest comes from earned income, trusts and foundations, and individual and corporate supporters (stated on Kettle's Yard's Jim and Helen Ede Fund page). The University's philanthropy page gives "around 45%".
- Admission charge: from 25 April 2023 the house charges entry, which Kettle's Yard attributes to rising costs, standstill funding and Covid-19.
- Corporate supporters named on its corporate support page: City Asset, Resolute AV, Murray Edwards College and Emmanuel College. Several logos carry no readable name. No amounts are given.
- Endowment: the Jim and Helen Ede Fund campaign aims to raise £5 million by the end of 2027, the 70th anniversary year, through major gifts, patron memberships, corporate partnerships and a June 2026 Sotheby's sale. No donor is named on the page.
- 2018 redevelopment: the Heritage Fund's news page gives £3.65 million from Arts Council England for the galleries and £2.5 million from the Heritage Fund for the education wing; Wikipedia gives £3.7 million and £2.32 million, for a cost of £11 million.
- The website footer carries logos for Arts Council England, Research England and Cambridge City Council, with no text on what each gives. A David Zwirner logo also appears on the corporate support page without a label. The annual reports and supporter list were not opened, so trusts and donors beyond those above are not named here.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little direct criticism. The critical items found are a book review quoting a 1986 critic and a partisan blog post about the admission charge.
1. Literary Review: Tanya Harrod, "Gaudier-Brzeska for the Masses" (2023)
URL: https://literaryreview.co.uk/gaudier-brzeska-for-the-masses
- Review of Laura Freeman's book on Jim Ede and the Kettle's Yard artists. Harrod describes Ede's project as an informal education in living with modern art.
- Cites the critic Peter Dormer, who wrote in 1986 that the house's "spirituality" rings false and disliked its over-conscious domestic aesthetic. Harrod presents him as one of the few critics of Kettle's Yard.
- Criticises the taste of the house, not the funders. Paywalled; only excerpts were read.
2. Inexpensive Progress (blog): Robjn Cantus, "A Way of Life?" (2023)
URL: https://inexpensiveprogress.com/?p=6429
- Argues that charging £10.50 (£12 with a donation) for the house shuts out people in hardship and departs from Ede's wish for it to be open and free.
- Argues the exhibitions have drifted from Ede's values and that curators placed non-Ede works in the house. Concludes the institution has become "another money making venture."
- A partisan personal blog, not an art press outlet: it is openly opinionated against the charge and the current curators, and names only its author.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Kettle's Yard is clean on the evidence found: the University of Cambridge, Arts Council England, earned income, and named corporate and college supporters, with no flagged industry or government patron. The weak points are the 2023 admission charge, the lack of a posted donor list and figures in the pages read, and cost figures for the 2018 building that differ between sources. Worth watching: who gives to the £5 million endowment campaign.
