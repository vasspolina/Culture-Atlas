MORI ART MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: mori-art-museum
Website: https://www.mori.art.museum

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on the 53rd and 54th floors of the Mori Tower in Roppongi Hills, Tokyo (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 2003 by the real estate developer Minoru Mori (1934 to 2012); Yoshiko Mori (1940 to 2025) was its first chairperson (museum website).
- Directors: David Elliott from 2003 to 2006 and Fumio Nanjo from 2006 to 2019; Mami Kataoka was announced as his successor at the end of 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Programme of large international contemporary art exhibitions plus a permanent collection of about 460 works as of January 2023, mostly from Japan and East Asia (Wikipedia).
- A 2015 renovation added the MAM Collection, MAM Screen and MAM Research programmes; in 2018, visitors after 5 pm accounted for 30 percent of its visitors (Wikipedia).
- Mori said "culture shapes a city's identity" (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Mori Art Museum: private: backed by the Mori family, with a corporate membership programme
- Artscape Japan describes the museum as a private institution backed by the Mori family (Lucy Birmingham, "Art Censorship: Whose Problem Is This?").
- Mori Building Co., Ltd. holds the copyright on the museum's website footer (museum website).
- The corporate membership programme has three tiers, Diamond, Platinum and Gold, with the stated goal to "encourage support for new artistic and cultural activities in Japan" (museum website). The list shows 68 members as of October 2026, in order of admission year.
- Diamond members (13): Japan Airlines, Morgan Stanley, Kajima Corporation, Obayashi Corporation, Panasonic Electric Works, Takenaka Corporation, Bloomberg, Shimizu Corporation, Kinden Corporation, Toshiba Elevator and Building Systems, Prada Japan, "MELCO Group Inc." and Semba.
- Platinum members (9): Nihon Sekkei, Taisei Corporation, Sanken Setsubi Kogyo, Fujitec, Kraftia Corporation, Takasago Thermal Engineering, AIG, Saikyu Kogyo and Lotte Holdings.
- Gold members (46) include Hermès Japon, BMW Group Japan, Mizuho Bank, The Pokémon Company, Christian Dior G.K., Mitsubishi Electric Corporation, Accenture Japan and Forbes Japan.
- The Southeast Asia Collectors Circle, launched in 2023, supports the museum's curatorial research in Southeast Asia and artwork acquisitions; six listings were shown in July 2026 (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism of the museum in the art press; the two pieces concern protests over one artist's show and the museum's corporate model.
1. Artscape Japan: Lucy Birmingham, "Art Censorship: Whose Problem Is This?" (2015)
URL: https://artscape.jp/artscape/eng/focus/1509_01.html
- Artscape says the museum has faced protests, and names Makoto Aida's 2013 exhibition there as one target; the 2013 protest is cited only through this article.
- Says that because it is a private institution backed by the Mori family, government censorship of it is "highly unlikely", and credits chairperson Yoshiko Mori with giving curators wide latitude.
- Mostly concerns censorship at the Museum of Contemporary Art Tokyo; does not name Mori Building.
2. Tokyo Art Beat: William Andrews, "Getting Down to Business" (19 November 2011)
URL: https://tokyoartbeat.com/en/articles/-/getting-down-to-business
- Cites Mori as an early example of a business building an art space for international contemporary exhibitions, and suggests that efforts like Mori's in Roppongi also promote the companies' brands and raise land values; questions whether Roppongi needs public art funding when Mori and similar businesses do not need the help.
- Notes that Japan has little tradition of government arts funding, so corporations often lead as sponsors; says Mori's shows "feel planned and fresh almost every time".

BOTTOM LINE
The Mori Art Museum was founded by Minoru Mori, and Artscape Japan describes it as a private institution backed by the Mori family. Its website lists 68 corporate members in three tiers, including Japan Airlines, Morgan Stanley, Bloomberg and Prada Japan.
