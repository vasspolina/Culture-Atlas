FUNDAÇÃO SINDIKA DOKOLO: Luanda, Angola
ID: fundação-sindika-dokolo-luanda
Website: http://www.sindikadokolofondation.org (checked 2026-11-20; serves spam: it redirects through other domains to an unrelated site, 1-win.net.br)

OVERVIEW
- Luanda foundation that looked after Sindika Dokolo's contemporary African art collection and ran the Luanda Triennial (Wikipedia).
- Dokolo started the collection in 2004 with the Angolan artist and curator Fernando Alvim, later the foundation's vice-president, and formed the foundation in 2006 (The Art Newspaper, 2017; Wikipedia); Ver Angola gave 2003 as the founding year.
- Named for its founder, a Congolese collector married to Isabel dos Santos, daughter of former president José Eduardo dos Santos; he died free-diving off Dubai in late October 2020, aged 48, and Dubai Police found no criminal suspicion (Al Jazeera).
- The collection began with the purchase of Hans Bogatzke's collection (Wikipedia) and includes works by William Kentridge, Zanele Muholi, Barthélémy Toguo, Kudzanai Chiurai and Edson Chagas (The Art Newspaper, 2020).
- Programmes included Check List Luanda Pop at the 2007 Venice Biennale (Wikipedia), the 2015 Porto show "You Love Me, You Love Me Not" (Ver Angola) and a Luanda project with Documenta 14 (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- Restitution: in December 2015 it bought back two Chokwe masks and a statue missing from the Dundo Museum (The Art Newspaper), and in June 2018 it helped return six more objects there (Observador).

FUNDING
Fundação Sindika Dokolo: founder-funded: the private fortune of Sindika Dokolo and Isabel dos Santos
- The foundation paid for its restitution purchases itself at undisclosed prices; Dokolo said he offered collectors what they had paid and no more (The Art Newspaper, 2015; Observador, 2018).
- It gave financial support towards the participation of 16 artists in Documenta 14 (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- European base: in January 2016 the company Supreme Treasure bought the Casa Manoel de Oliveira in Porto at public auction for 1.58 million euros as the foundation's European headquarters (Ver Angola).
- Ownership: The Art Newspaper described the collection of about 3,000 works as owned by Dokolo and dos Santos and administered by the foundation (2020).

Red flags: 2020 Luanda Leaks investigation and asset freezes
- An investigation led by the ICIJ with 37 media partners, based on more than 715,000 documents, reported that dos Santos built her $2.2bn fortune by exploiting Angola; she denies that her fortune came from nepotism and corruption (The Art Newspaper).
- Her father appointed her head of the state oil company Sonangol in 2016; President João Lourenço fired her the following year (The Art Newspaper).
- In December 2019 Angola froze the couple's assets and bank accounts to recover $1bn in state loans; on 13 March 2020 a Portuguese judge ordered the seizure of all her assets in Portugal (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: 2020 Luanda court ruling on the de Grisogono diamond deal
- In July 2020 the Provincial Court of Luanda called the deal through which the state diamond company Sodiam invested in the Swiss jeweller de Grisogono "fraudulent", and said it was the "opportunity" for Dokolo and dos Santos to control the company; Sodiam says it lost its entire $120 million investment (ICIJ).
- Dokolo's lawyers said he invested $115 million and later much more; dos Santos's lawyers said she had no interest in the jeweller (ICIJ).

Red flags: 2021 US corruption designation of Isabel dos Santos
- On International Anti-Corruption Day in December 2021 the US State Department designated dos Santos for "significant corruption by misappropriating public funds for her personal benefit", barring her and her immediate family from the United States (ICIJ).

Red flags: 2023 London freezing order against Isabel dos Santos
- On 20 December 2023 London's High Court granted the Angolan telecoms company Unitel a worldwide freezing order on up to £580 million ($733 million) of her assets; Unitel accuses her of procuring over £365 million in loans in 2012 and 2013 for her company UIH and her "own personal benefit", which she denies (ICIJ).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in the art press; the one sustained attack is a polemical 2009 opinion essay.
1. esferapública: Carlos Salazar, "Alfredo Jaar, Muxima y los Dokolo de Angola, Resucitados" (2009)
URL: https://esferapublica.org/alfredo-jaar-muxima-y-los-dokolo-de-angola-resucitados/
- Argues that the family's money contradicts the collection's claim to dignify Africans: it says the bank of Dokolo's father defrauded the Angolan state and small investors of more than $80 million before collapsing in 1986, and ties dos Santos's diamond business to abuses in the Lundas.
- Faults Alfredo Jaar for never speaking out against Dokolo after showing "Muxima" in the 2007 African pavilion, and quotes Barthélémy Toguo, who turned down the invitation: "Under no circumstances whatever should my name be associated with that of Sindika Dokolo".
- An opinion piece; its allegations rest on press and NGO sources it links.
2. The Art Newspaper: Cristina Ruiz, "Africa's top collector returns ancestral works to Angola" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/12/22/africas-top-collector-returns-ancestral-works-to-angola
- Not criticism: reports the restitution purchases and notes his marriage to the president's daughter.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation ran on a private fortune that courts and governments in Angola, Portugal, the United States and Britain have acted against over Angolan state oil, diamond and telecoms deals. Its money is not clean.
