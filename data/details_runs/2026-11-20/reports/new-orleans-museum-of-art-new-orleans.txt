NEW ORLEANS MUSEUM OF ART: New Orleans, United States
ID: new-orleans-museum-of-art-new-orleans
Website: https://noma.org/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- New Orleans' oldest fine arts institution, at One Collins C. Diboll Circle in City Park (museum website).
- Founded with $150,000 that the sugar broker Isaac Delgado gave in 1910; the Isaac Delgado Museum of Art opened on 16 December 1911 and took its present name in 1971 (museum timeline; Wikipedia).
- More than 40,000 objects, strongest in French and American art, photography, glass, and African and Japanese works. The Besthoff Sculpture Garden opened in 2003, doubled in size in 2019 and holds 100 sculptures (museum website).
- Susan M. Taylor is the director; the Victoria and Albert Museum's "Marie Antoinette Style" opens there on 14 November 2027 (museum website).
- Visiting: closed Monday. Adults $18 for Louisiana residents, $23 for others; under-20s free. The sculpture garden is free and open daily (museum website).

FUNDING
New Orleans Museum of Art: privately funded: trustees, foundations and donors
- Finances: in 2024 the museum took in $9,981,201 and spent $13,087,681; contributions were $6,189,419, or 62 per cent of revenue. Net assets were $101,181,856. Taylor was paid $509,414 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- Building: a $23 million expansion opened in 1993; a trustee campaign from 2000 paid to build and endow the sculpture garden (museum timeline).
- The Helis Foundation, "established and funded by the William Helis Family in 1955", pays for free admission for Louisiana residents on Wednesdays and for visitors aged 13 to 19 through its Art for All programme, launched in 2007 (foundation website; museum admission page, checked 20 Nov 2026).
- The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation gave $1 million in January 2020 for a conservation centre and two conservator posts (museum news release).
- Gifts: the Besthoff family gave Isamu Noguchi's Mississippi Fountain, and a new donor group, the Collectors' Circle, backs contemporary art (director's letter, Fall 2026).

Red flags: Helis Foundation oil money behind free admission, 2007 to now
- A 2021 foundation post calls William George Helis, who died in 1950, an oil magnate; a 2025 post describes its leader David Kerstein as "a soft-spoken lawyer, oil-company executive and philanthropist".
- Helis Oil & Gas Company planned in 2014 to drill an oil well in St. Tammany Parish and to frack it if it found commercially viable oil (WWNO, 13 May 2014).
- The open letter of 12 October 2020 supporting #DismantleNOMA says the museum is sustained by "oil money and other harmful private funders" and names the Helis Foundation (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara). Unverified: the letter's description of the foundation as the philanthropic arm of the Helis Oil & Gas Energy Project; the letter gives no document for it.

Red flags: 2020 open letters alleging racism at the museum
- Unverified: claims in a June 2020 open letter by five former workers, including Jennifer Williams, that 30 employees resigned in two years because of a "toxic work environment and institutional racism", with slurs, wage discrimination against Black workers, surveillance and non-disclosure agreements, and that concerns of Black staff about a permanent plantation parlor installation were disregarded. NOMA declined to comment on specifics, citing "privacy restrictions" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara, 25 June 2020).
- A second letter on 12 October 2020, signed by more than 150 people including the Guerrilla Girls and Michael Rakowitz, demanded Taylor's removal and an independent investigation and urged artists to withhold labour; it said Delgado's wealth "derives from a sugar plantation". NOMA said it was "committed to being an anti-racist institution" (Hyperallergic, Hakim Bishara).

Red flags: 2023 backlash over the hiring of a curator of African art
- On 29 June 2023 NOMA announced Amanda Maples, a white woman, as curator of African art, and more than 850 comments on its Instagram post criticised the hire. NOMA promised a town hall and defended the choice to Gambit, citing Maples's academic background (The Art Newspaper, Torey Akers, 3 July 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
A donor-dependent private museum running a deficit. Its funding weak point is oil family money behind its free days; its workplace record has drawn repeated protest. Worth watching: a published donor list.
