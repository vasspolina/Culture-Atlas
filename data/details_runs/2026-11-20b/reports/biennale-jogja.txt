BIENNALE JOGJA: Yogyakarta, Indonesia
ID: biennale-jogja
Website: https://biennalejogja.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennial in and around Yogyakarta, run since 2010 by the Yayasan Biennale Yogyakarta (Yogyakarta Biennale Foundation), launched on 23 August 2010 (Biennial Foundation).
- It began in 1983 as the "Yogyakarta Painting Exhibition" at the Taman Budaya cultural centre, became the Biennale Painting Yogyakarta in 1988, the Yogyakarta Art Biennale in 1997 and Biennale Jogja in 2003; the 1992 Binal Experimental Arts was a protest against its restrictive rules (Contemporary And).
- The artists Mella Jaarsma and Nindityo Adipurnomo, founders of the Cemeti Art House, set up the foundation in 2010 with other artists (Contemporary And).
- From 2011 the "Equator" series paired each edition with countries near the equator, starting with India (Biennial Foundation); over 16 years it involved more than 2,000 artists and brought more than 50 schools and universities into each edition (Biennale Jogja Foundation statement).
- The 18th and last edition, "Kawruh: Tanah Lelaku", ran from 19 to 24 September 2025 in Padukuhan Boro, Karangsewu, and from 5 October to 20 November 2025 across the Yogyakarta region, with about 60 artists and the curators Bob Edrian, Eva Lin and ketjilbergerak (International Biennial Association).
- On 9 October 2026 the foundation announced it would close after 16 years, saying it needed to "REST" (Biennial Foundation; ArtReview).

FUNDING
Biennale Jogja: public: national and Yogyakarta government money through a foundation
- The foundation was described as "state-funded" when it was set up to manage the biennale (Contemporary And, 2015).
- The 2019 edition was "run by Yogyakarta Regional Government and organized by Yayasan Biennale Yogyakarta", at the Taman Budaya and the Jogja National Museum (Biennial Foundation).
- The 2025 edition was supported by the Ministry of Culture, the Indonesian Cultural Endowment Fund (2024), the Cultural Department of the Yogyakarta Special Region Government and the Yogyakarta Art Center; its residency ran with the ministry's Manajemen Talenta Nasional programme (International Biennial Association).
- Dana Indonesiana: Alia Swastika of the biennale said in 2023 that after ten years of struggling for money, the government cultural endowment would let it develop the organisation and its festival (Medcom, 2023).
- History: no public funding in 2001, so the biennale was not held; a commercial gallery sponsored the 2003 edition; funding was again in jeopardy in 2009 (Contemporary And).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Reviews of the last two editions criticise the curatorial concept and the organisation.
1. ArtReview Asia: Adeline Chia, "Biennale Jogja 18 Review: Occasional Moments of Brilliance" (2026)
URL: https://artreview.com/biennale-jogja-18-review-occasional-moments-of-brilliance/
- Finds the concept of kawruh "not exactly radical" and the edition "strangely ahistorical and apolitical"; calls "translocality and transhistoricity" a vague framework.
- In opening week some videos were off and several venues closed; visitors were mostly foreign art professionals, risking an "exoticised 'hardship' tour".
2. New Mandala: Asep Topan, "TITEN: a new phase of Biennale Jogja" (2024)
URL: https://www.newmandala.org/titen-a-new-phase-of-biennale-jogja/
- The Equator series rests on a centre and periphery framing that accepts the dominance it wants to disrupt.
- The 17th edition felt like scattered small exhibitions, with accessibility problems and unclear information.
3. Contemporary And: Christina Schott, "A Biennale with Naughty Coded into its DNA" (2015)
URL: https://contemporaryand.com/en/c-and-magazine/texts/a-biennale-with-naughty-coded-into-its-dna
- Not criticism of the institution: a history from the Suharto-era selection rules to the 2010 foundation, which it ties to the biennale's "urgent need for ongoing, professional management".

WIDER CONTEXT
- The foundation's closure statement cites "a political regime that has failed to recognize people's cultures", "increasing censorship, and violence against activists and protesters" (Biennial Foundation; ArtReview). It names no incident against the biennale itself.

BOTTOM LINE
The biennale ran on Indonesian public money, national and regional, through an artist-founded foundation, with no flagged sponsor on record. Its weak point was a dependence on public money that more than once fell short.
