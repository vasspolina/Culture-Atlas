NATIONAL MUSEUM OF OMAN: Muscat, Oman
ID: national-museum-of-oman
Website: https://www.nm.gov.om/en (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Oman's national museum in central Muscat, in the Hillat an-Ni'am area on As-Sa'idyyah School Street, facing Qasr al-Alam Palace across the ceremonial boulevard.
- Established by Royal Decree 62/2013 of 20 November 2013 and opened to the public on 30 July 2016, after a partial opening the previous December (gallery site, The Art Newspaper). It replaced the Museum of Omani Heritage, founded in 1974 (Wikipedia).
- A purpose-built building of 13,700 square metres, 4,000 of them permanent galleries and 400 temporary exhibition space. The architect is not named in the sources read.
- 14 permanent galleries, including Maritime History, Arms and Armour, Aflaj and Land of Frankincense, more than 7,000 objects, a learning centre, cafe and shop (gallery site).
- Open Saturday to Thursday 10:00 to 17:00 and Friday 14:00 to 18:00. Admission is 1 rial for Omani and GCC nationals, 2 for residents and 5 for foreigners; students, people with disabilities, certified guides and low-income families enter free. It is listed as wheelchair accessible with Arabic Braille (Experience Oman).

FUNDING
National Museum of Oman: state-funded: Government of Oman under the Minister of Heritage and Tourism
- The museum is a legal entity with financial and administrative independence, set up by royal decree (gallery site).
- The government was planned to cover 90 percent of the budget at opening; Oman adopted an austerity budget for 2016, and acting director general Jamal al Moosawi said it "poses challenges when it comes to managing and operating the institution" (The Art Newspaper, 2016).
- Its Board of Trustees is chaired by the Minister of Heritage and Tourism, Salim bin Mohammed al-Mahrouqi, as of its meeting on 13 March 2025 (Times of Oman).
- The board page still names Sayyid Ibrahim bin Saeed Al Busaidi as chair and Minister, so its list is out of date.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors, donors, an annual report or accounts.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The museum is a body of the Omani state, chaired by the Minister of Heritage and Tourism and established by the Sultan's decree (gallery site, The Art Newspaper).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Mikhail Piotrovsky, Director General of Russia's State Hermitage, is a member of the museum's Board of Trustees (board page, 24 October 2026; the museum's 13 September 2025 news release calls him a member).
- Canada sanctioned Piotrovsky on 31 August 2023. The EU listed him in its 20th sanctions package, adopted on 23 April 2026, calling him a "close associate" of Putin and citing his support for the war in Ukraine, for laws allowing Ukrainian museum items into Russia's State Museum Fund and for excavations in occupied Crimea (The Art Newspaper, 29 April 2026). The Hermitage had no comment.
- The listings name Piotrovsky personally. No source read shows that he funds or controls the museum, and no listed person or company is among its funders.
- Connection to the museum: on 13 September 2025 Piotrovsky and the museum's Secretary-General, Jamal al-Moosawi, signed an agreement to open a "Hermitage - Sultanate of Oman Centre" in Dhofar, inside the museum's structure (museum news release).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Omani state's, and no private sponsor or accounts are published. The board includes Mikhail Piotrovsky, who is under Canadian (2023) and EU (2026) sanctions; the sanctions name him, not the museum or its funders. Watch whether the Dhofar centre with the Hermitage opens.
