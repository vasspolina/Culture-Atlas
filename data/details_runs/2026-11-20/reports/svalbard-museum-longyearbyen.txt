SVALBARD MUSEUM: Longyearbyen, Svalbard, Norway
ID: svalbard-museum-longyearbyen
Website: https://svalbardmuseum.no (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Culture and natural history museum for Svalbard and the Arctic, in the Svalbard Forskningspark (research park) in Longyearbyen (svalbardmuseum.no).
- A museum committee formed in Longyearbyen in 1964; the first board met on 18 January 1979, the museum first opened in 1981 in an old pigsty and was officially inaugurated on 20 February 1982 (svalbardmuseum.no).
- Work to professionalise it began in 1999; it became a department of Longyearbyen lokalstyre in 2002 and moved into the new research park in 2006 (svalbardmuseum.no).
- The research park space holds exhibitions, storage, laboratories and offices on about 1,500 square metres; the old pigsty was given up as a store in 2025 (svalbardmuseum.no; annual report 2025).
- The collection holds about 55,000 objects (svalbardmuseum.no) and 183,512 photographs, of which 30,999 are catalogued on DigitaltMuseum (annual report 2025).
- Director: Eystein Markusson; 16 full-time equivalent posts in 2025 (annual report 2025).
- Visitors: 54,110 in 2024 and a record 60,152 in 2025, 43 per cent of the 140,000 tourists who came to Longyearbyen that year (annual reports 2024 and 2025).
- In 2025 it showed "Det nye landet: Svalbard 100 år" for the centenary of Norwegian sovereignty, opened by Queen Sonja on 16 June; its exhibition "The End of Coal", supported by Arts Council Norway, covers mining in Longyearbyen and Sveagruva (annual report 2025; svalbardmuseum.no).
- Open daily 10:00 to 17:00. Admission NOK 160 for adults, NOK 110 for students, NOK 60 for residents, free for children (svalbardmuseum.no).
- Nominated for Norway's Museum of the Year in 2006 and 2024; Council of Europe Museum Prize 2008 (svalbardmuseum.no).

FUNDING
Svalbard Museum: mainly public: Norwegian state grants through the Svalbard budget and the Ministry of Culture, plus ticket and shop income
- Owner: Stiftelsen Svalbard Museum, a foundation whose founders are Longyearbyen lokalstyre, Store Norske, the University Centre in Svalbard (UNIS), the Norwegian Polar Institute and the Governor of Svalbard (Sysselmesteren) (svalbardmuseum.no).
- State operating support in 2025 was NOK 17,770,000: NOK 15,470,000 from the Ministry of Justice and Public Security and NOK 2,300,000 from the Ministry of Culture and Equality; in 2024 the two ministries gave NOK 14,900,000 and NOK 2,220,000 (annual report 2025, note 1).
- The Storting approved NOK 16,000,000 for the museum on the Svalbard budget for 2026, chapter 4, post 70 (Storting recommendation 17 S, 2025 to 2026).
- Project grants in 2025: NOK 1,377,625 from the Svalbard Environmental Protection Fund for six projects, NOK 231,934 from the Directorate for Culture and NOK 150,000 from a foundation the accounts name as Bergensenstiftelsen. The Ministry of Agriculture and Food gave NOK 937,826 in 2024 (annual report 2025, note 1).
- Own operations earned NOK 22,711,007 in 2025; total income was NOK 42,240,566, up from NOK 36,774,981 in 2024 and NOK 34,877,557 in 2023 (annual report 2025; Brønnøysund accounts register).
- A research project funded by the Research Council of Norway, worth NOK 11.49 million, runs from 1 January 2025 (annual report 2025).
- Its conservation centre takes paid commissions from the Governor, the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries, Longyearbyen lokalstyre, Kings Bay AS and Svalbard folkehøgskole (annual report 2025).

Red flags: coal company Store Norske as founder and board chair (2025)
- Store Norske Spitsbergen Kulkompani is one of the five founders, who appoint the board; the chair, Håvar Fjerdingøy, sits for Store Norske (svalbardmuseum.no; board page, June 2025).
- The museum has a cooperation agreement with Store Norske on outreach and deposits, and keeps Store Norske's photographs under a deposit agreement (annual report 2025).
- Store Norske decided in autumn 2023 to close Mine 7, Norway's last coal mine; high coal demand and prices after the war in Ukraine had kept it working two more years, and it closed officially on 4 May 2026 (High North News, 2026).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money. The one weak point is governance, not funding: the coal company's place on the board.
