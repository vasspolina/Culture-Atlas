KIJK-KUBUS: Rotterdam, Netherlands
ID: kijk-kubus
Website: https://www.kubuswoning.nl/en/ (checked 2026-11-20; the visit page still carries a COVID closure notice)

OVERVIEW
- Furnished show cube at Overblaak 70 in Piet Blom's Cube Houses, in the east of Rotterdam's city centre near Blaak station, the Market Hall, the library and the Old Harbour (stated by the museum).
- Ed R. de Graaf founded it, and the site says it opens this year for its 42nd season, with no year given (stated by the museum).
- Piet Blom designed the Cube Houses, built from 1982 to 1984 after plans were first shown in 1978: 38 cube houses and 13 business cubes on the Overblaak pedestrian viaduct, a municipal monument known as the Blaakse Bos, the Blaak Forest (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Each cube sits on a hexagonal pole and has three floors: a living level, a level with two bedrooms and a bathroom, and a top pyramid with 18 windows. All roofs were reclad in zinc in 1997 and 1998, and the houses were repainted in 2015 (stated by the museum).
- On site: a cube furnished with custom-built furniture for its slanted walls, with screens, info panels and a model of the architecture (stated by the museum). The Kunstkubus at Overblaak 30 and a chess piece museum at Overblaak 94 are separate cubes (Dutch Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the home page gives daily 10:00 to 18:00, to 19:00 in July and August; the visit page also lists 11:00 to 17:00. Tickets EUR 3.50, EUR 2.50 for over-65s and students, EUR 1.50 to age 12, free to age 4; Rotterdam Pas holders free, Rotterdam City Card holders EUR 1 off. Three floors with steep stairs (stated by the museum).

FUNDING
Kijk-Kubus: self-funded: ticket income for a small private foundation
- The "Het Blaakse Bos" Foundation runs the show cube, and visitors pay at the door in cash or by card (stated by the museum).
- The foundation pays a yearly contribution towards plants on the Overblaak promenade, as the owners' association recently began to do; the municipality made a one-off contribution in 2017 (stated by the museum).
- The surrounding cube houses are privately owned and lived in (Dutch Wikipedia); the museum gives a price of around EUR 450,000 for a cube house.
- Iconic Houses lists the Kijk-Kubus among its member house museums (Iconic Houses).

BOTTOM LINE
A small private foundation runs the show cube on ticket money, with no public or corporate funder on record. The weak point is transparency: the foundation publishes no accounts or board, and the owner of the cube is not named.
