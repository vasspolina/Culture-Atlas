KW INSTITUTE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Berlin, Germany
ID: kw-institute-for-contemporary-art
Website: https://www.kw-berlin.de (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art institution at Auguststraße 69 in Berlin-Mitte, with no permanent collection (the space's about page).
- Founded in 1991 by Klaus Biesenbach, Alexandra Binswanger, Clemens Homburger, Philipp von Doering and Alfonso Rutigliano in a derelict former margarine factory. Emma Enderby has been director since May 2024 (the space's about page).
- "Kunst-Werke" is a play on words, meaning artworks and a public utility that produces art (Wikipedia). The institute is run by the association KUNST-WERKE BERLIN e. V.
- Building: the complex holds exhibition halls, apartments, offices, event spaces and Café Bravo, designed by Dan Graham in 1999 (the space's about page). The buildings were leased in 1991 from the housing association Wohnungsbaugesellschaft Mitte; in 1995 a fund associated with the Berlin Lottery Foundation bought them and leased them to KW (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibition halls, the Berlin Biennale (founded 1996, a sister organisation under the same association, using KW's spaces since 1998) and Café Bravo.
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Monday 11:00 to 19:00, closed Tuesday. The pages read list no admission prices.

FUNDING
KW Institute for Contemporary Art: mainly public: the Berlin Senate, with foundation, friends' and corporate support
- Institutional support comes from the Berlin Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion; KW calls itself publicly funded (the space's pages). No reporting found of the amount.
- The Berlin Lottery Foundation helped buy the buildings in 1995 and paid for renovations from 2016 to 2018 (Wikipedia).
- KW Friends, a private non-profit, funds projects from 2020 to 2026, among them the 2024 redesign of the main entrance and the 13th Berlin Biennale in 2025 (KW Friends page).
- Volkswagen Group supports the KW Digital Program for 2023 to 2024 (the space's exhibition page). Volkswagen AG and KW Freunde supported the 2021 history book "KW, A History" (e-flux announcement, 19 January 2021).
- The Schering Stiftung presented the 2024 Award for Artistic Research. The Julia Stoschek Collection is listed as a collaborator (Wikipedia) and Stoschek supported the 2021 anniversary weekend (e-flux).
- The 2024 show "Poetics of Encryption" was supported by Apalazzo, Blessed Foundation, max goelitz, Harlan Levey Projects and Sprüth Magers (the space's exhibition page).
- No reporting found of BMW Group or Casa Camper support on KW's own pages.

Red flags: Volkswagen support
- On 11 January 2017 Volkswagen agreed to plead guilty to three felony counts over diesel emissions defeat devices in about 590,000 vehicles sold in the US, with a US$2.8 billion criminal penalty and US$1.5 billion in civil resolutions (US Environmental Protection Agency).

Red flags: artists withdrew from a KW show in a boycott of German state-funded institutions
- In 2024 Morehshin Allahyari and American Artist withdrew from "Poetics of Encryption" (17 February to 26 May 2024). KW's exhibition page states they "have chosen to strike against German State-funded institutions".
- KW left traces of their works in the galleries: wall tags, an unused screen and an unopened crate (Spike Art Magazine).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of KW's funding. The documented criticism concerns the 2024 boycott and how the institute handled it.
1. Spike Art Magazine: Sebastjan Brank, review of "Poetics of Encryption" (issue 79, 2024)
URL: https://www.spikeartmagazine.com/articles/review-poetics-of-encryption-kw-berlin-2024
- Calls the absence of the two withdrawn artists "the most glaring omission" of the show.
- Faults the exhibition for not explaining that they withdrew from a state-funded show to protest German government complicity in the war in Gaza.
- Mostly assesses the art and the curator Nadim Samman's thesis.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Berlin Biennale 2022: Iraqi artists Sajjad Abbas, Raed Mutar and Layth Kareem withdrew their works from the 12th Berlin Biennale over a Jean-Jacques Lebel installation with Abu Ghraib photographs. An open letter drew nearly 400 signatures (The Art Newspaper, Gareth Harris, 17 August 2022).
- Connection: the works of Mutar and Abbas were moved to KW, one of the relocation venues, before all three were taken off display.

BOTTOM LINE
KW's base funding is public and stated by KW, with no amounts published on the pages read. The weak points are its Volkswagen programme and book support, the 2024 withdrawals from its own show, and thin public detail on its sponsors. Watch the sponsor list on its annual reports.
