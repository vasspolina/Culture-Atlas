NTU CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ART SINGAPORE: Singapore, Singapore
ID: ntu-centre-for-contemporary-art-singapore-singapore
Website: https://ntu.ccasingapore.org

OVERVIEW
- National research centre of Nanyang Technological University (NTU), presenting itself as a space for critical discourse on "Spaces of the Curatorial" in Southeast Asia and beyond (centre website).
- Established in October 2013 as the Centre for Contemporary Arts at Gillman Barracks, the visual arts precinct that opened on 15 September 2012. Ute Meta Bauer was founding director and is listed as Acting Director and Principal Research Fellow on the centre's website (Wikipedia, centre website).
- It operated across four buildings: an exhibition hall and artist studios on Malan Road, and a research centre and main office on Lock Road (Wikipedia).
- The exhibition and residency spaces at Gillman Barracks closed in March 2021, after a final Trinh T. Minh-ha show ran from 17 October 2020 to 28 February 2021. Bauer and some staff planned to continue research and curatorial programmes from a Singapore office (The Art Newspaper, Lisa Movius, 12 October 2020).
- Research continues through the frameworks Climates.Habitats.Environments. (2017 to 2024) and Communities of Practice (2024 onward) (centre website).
- Visiting: the address is Block 6 Lock Road, Singapore 108934.

FUNDING
NTU Centre for Contemporary Art Singapore: mainly public: NTU and, until 2021, the Singapore Economic Development Board, with EU money for a residency
- Before the pandemic the centre depended on variable project grants plus core funding from NTU and the Singapore Economic Development Board (EDB) (The Art Newspaper, 12 October 2020). ArtAsiaPacific describes it as co-funded by NTU and the EDB (HG Masters, 20 August 2020).
- Wikipedia says the exhibition space and residency studios closed in March 2021 after EDB funding ceased. NTU's dean Joseph Liow attributed the closure to the Covid-19 pandemic; ArtAsiaPacific notes the financial restructuring had been under consideration before the pandemic (HG Masters, 25 August 2020).
- The SEA AiR residency programme is funded by the European Union. Its first cycle in 2022 placed Vuth Lyno at Villa Arson in Nice, Citra Sasmita at WIELS in Brussels and Hoo Fan Chon at HIAP in Helsinki, and NTU CCA and the EU Delegation to Singapore co-presented the resulting exhibition from 11 January to 5 February 2023 (centre website).
- Donations from Singapore taxpayers qualify for a 250 percent tax deduction and the Culture Matching Fund (centre website).
- The proceeds of an online art auction in October 2020 were to support archiving and a book project on the climate crisis (ArtAsiaPacific, 20 August 2020).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two ArtAsiaPacific pieces from August 2020 criticise the withdrawal of government funding and name the centre as the affected party.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: HG Masters, "Singapore Loses Major Exhibition and Residency Platform" (20 August 2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/singapore-loses-major-exhibition-and-residency-platform
- Argues the closure signals the Singaporean government's wavering commitment to contemporary art and culture: agencies invested heavily in art infrastructure over a decade, then shifted focus or reorganised before those ventures took root.
- Notes that only six of the original galleries remain at Gillman Barracks, though 13 galleries operate there, and links the centre's programming to critical discourse in a city-state known for strict limits on speech.
- Criticises the funders' decision, not the centre.
2. ArtAsiaPacific: HG Masters, "Reactions to the Shuttering of NTU CCA Singapore's Exhibition and Residency Spaces" (25 August 2020)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/reactions-to-the-shuttering-of-ntu-cca-singapore-s-exhibition-and-residency-spaces
- Artist Heman Chong says Singapore values art mainly when it generates quick economic returns; artist Ho Rui An criticises policymakers for short-term cost-benefit analysis; Valentine Willie, creative director of Ilham Gallery, says Covid-19 should not have been a major factor for a non-profit centre.
- Eugene Tan, who oversaw Gillman Barracks' development at the EDB, declined to comment.
- Criticises the funding decision, not the centre.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is public: Nanyang Technological University, the Economic Development Board until 2021, and EU funding for the SEA AiR residency programme. The exhibition and residency spaces closed in March 2021 when EDB funding ceased, according to Wikipedia.
