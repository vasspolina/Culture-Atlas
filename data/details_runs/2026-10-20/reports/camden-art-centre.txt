CAMDEN ART CENTRE: London, UK
ID: camden-art-centre
Website: https://camdenartcentre.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and charity on the corner of Arkwright Road and Finchley Road, London NW3, near Finchley Road Underground station.
- Founded in 1965 in West Hampstead as Hampstead Arts Centre; renamed Camden Arts Centre in 1967 and Camden Art Centre in 2020. Martin Clark has been director since 2017, after Jenni Lomax (1990 to 2017).
- Housed in the Grade II listed former Hampstead Central Library, designed by Arnold Taylor and opened in 1897 by benefactor Sir Henry Harben; Tony Fretton Architects completed a refurbishment in early 2004.
- In 2025 it signed a 99-year lease on the building after raising 1.9 million pounds in about nine months; it had held a 26-year peppercorn lease from Camden Council due to expire in 2027 (IanVisits).
- Exhibitions, artist residencies, off-site projects and learning programmes, with a café, a garden and a bookshop.
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, 11am to 6pm. Level access from Arkwright Road, lifts to the galleries, a wheelchair on request, gender-neutral toilets and one disabled parking bay on request.

FUNDING
Camden Art Centre: mixed: Arts Council England, trusts, galleries and private patrons
- Arts Council England is a lead funder; its grant fell from about 920,000 pounds a year to 600,000 pounds for 2023 to 2026 (The Art Newspaper, 2023), which led to staff redundancies and cuts to learning programmes (IanVisits).
- Other lead support: AKO Foundation, Guy and Alexandra Halamish and Donald Porteous. Programme and exhibition support includes Bloomberg Philanthropies, Art Fund, Freelands Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, Paul Mellon Centre, Kulturstiftung des Bundes, and galleries including David Zwirner and Gagosian (thank-you page, undated).
- Corporate support: J.S. Held, The MBS Group, Mishcon de Reya and TFA London.
- The lease fund names as founders Antony Gormley and Vicken Parsons, Alexandra and Guy Halamish and Donald Porteous; building benefactors A4 Arts Foundation, Erin Bell and Michael Cohen, Batia and Idan Ofer, The Foyle Foundation and Texel Foundation; and galleries including Hauser & Wirth, Victoria Miro and Sadie Coles HQ. Artists and galleries gave about half the money (IanVisits).
- Bloomberg Philanthropies has been title sponsor of the annual New Contemporaries exhibition since 2001; Camden hosted it from 19 January to 14 April 2024.
- No reporting found of the amounts given by individual patrons or trusts.

Red flags: building benefactor with oil refining and chemicals money
- Idan Ofer is majority shareholder of Israel Corporation, which held a stake in Oil Refineries (Bazan) until February 2023 and sold ICL chemicals shares in 2022; his Kenon Holdings has energy interests (Wikipedia).
- He has been criticised in Israel for pollution from his chemical factories (Wikipedia).
- Batia and Idan Ofer are named building benefactors of the 2025 lease fund (thank-you page).
- No reporting found of a protest at Camden Art Centre over the Ofer gift.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is news of the Arts Council cut and the lease.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 more than 260 UK artists, through Artists for Palestine UK, asked the National Theatre, the Royal Shakespeare Company, the Young Vic, the Royal Court and the Almeida to end ties with Bloomberg Philanthropies, citing its Bloomberg-Sagol Center at Tel Aviv University, which trains leaders of West Bank settlements (The New Arab).
- Camden Art Centre is not named in the letter; Bloomberg Philanthropies is a programme supporter and title sponsor of New Contemporaries, which Camden hosted in 2024.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre runs on reduced Arts Council money, trusts, commercial galleries and private patrons, and it now holds a 99-year lease bought largely with artists' and galleries' gifts. The weak points are a building gift from Batia and Idan Ofer, whose wealth includes oil refining and chemicals, and Bloomberg Philanthropies support at a time when artists target Bloomberg elsewhere. Worth watching: whether the Bloomberg campaign widens to visual arts venues.
