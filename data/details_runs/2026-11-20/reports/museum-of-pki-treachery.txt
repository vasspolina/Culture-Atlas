MUSEUM OF PKI TREACHERY (MUSEUM PENGKHIANATAN PKI): Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: museum-of-pki-treachery
Website: none

OVERVIEW
- Museum in the Monumen Pancasila Sakti complex at Lubang Buaya, Cipayung, East Jakarta, on Jalan Raya Pondok Gede (detik, 2023).
- President Soeharto inaugurated it on 1 October 1992 (detik, 2023).
- 34 three-dimensional dioramas with sound effects depict PKI violence and the events of 30 September 1965; objects include General Ahmad Yani's uniform and official car and the car used to abduct D.I. Pandjaitan (detik, 2023).
- Around it stand a "torture house" with life-size figures of the officers being tortured, the house used as the killing squad's command post and a bamboo-walled field kitchen, the last two kept close to their 1965 state (detik, 2023).
- In July 2026 Culture Minister Fadli Zon called for a revitalisation of the complex: fuller collections, a route through the museum before the Lubang Buaya site, immersive technology and exhibits based on documents and archives (RRI).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 08.00 to 15.30, closed Monday; adults 5,000 rupiah, students 3,000; a guide costs 100,000 rupiah per guide or bus; entry is free on Armed Forces Day, 5 October (detik, 2024).

FUNDING
Museum of PKI Treachery: state: the Indonesian armed forces, with the culture ministry
- Origin: proposed by Brigadier General Nugroho Notosusanto, head of the armed forces' Centre for History and Tradition, and approved by Defence Minister and armed forces commander General M. Yusuf and by Soeharto (detik, 2023).
- Operator: the complex is managed by the TNI History Centre, Pusat Sejarah TNI (detik, 2024). The Jakarta Post described the museum in 2015 as jointly managed by the TNI and the Education and Culture Ministry.
- Revitalisation: Colonel Dwi Imam Subagyo of the TNI History Centre said the centre would work with the Ministry of Culture on the plan, using its documents and data (RRI, 2026).

Red flags: 2018 permit rule for foreign researchers
- The TNI announced that foreign researchers need a permit from the military's chief intelligence assistant to research in TNI museums, naming this museum and Satriamandala; a spokesman said the aim was to "ensure research is not conducted haphazardly" (The Conversation, 2018).
- The author, Olivia Tasevski, expected permits to be refused to researchers who question the Soeharto-era account of 1965 or criticise TNI abuses in West Papua and East Timor, and called for the rule to be scrapped (The Conversation).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Historians and commentators fault the museum for presenting the New Order account of 1965 and leaving out the mass killings that followed; the coverage is in national press and academic commentary, not art press.
1. The Jakarta Post: "Museum still conveys New Order version of history" (2015)
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2015/09/29/museum-still-conveys-new-order-version-history.html
- The complex continues to promote the New Order regime's version of the murder of six generals and one lieutenant on 30 September 1965.
- Historian Hilmar Farid said autopsies showed the officers were shot, not tortured as depicted, and that PKI blame rests on circumstantial evidence: "The memorial complex is a means for those agreeing with Soeharto's New Order perspective."
- Notes that school history books named the PKI as one of several instigators in 2004 and returned sole blame to it in 2006.
2. The Conversation: Olivia Tasevski, "What's behind the move to limit foreign researchers' access to Indonesia's military museums?" (2018)
URL: https://theconversation.com/whats-behind-the-move-to-limit-foreign-researchers-access-to-indonesias-military-museums-92056
- Dioramas and captions show the PKI behind the generals' kidnapping and draw no attention to the killing of at least 500,000 PKI members and alleged communists.
- Cites historian Katharine McGregor: the museum seeks to present the mass killings as "legal and necessary".
3. The Jakarta Post: Michael G. Vann, "After Gestapu monuments a Gestok memorial must follow" (2026)
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2026/10/01/after-gestapu-monuments-a-gestok-memorial-must-follow
- Lists the museum among the Lubang Buaya sites that repeat the New Order lesson: the PKI betrayed the nation, the military rescued it.
- Argues for a national memorial to the hundreds of thousands killed in the purge that followed, without demolishing existing memorials.
- Paywalled; only excerpts were read.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Indonesian state's own, which is no red flag on the atlas. The weak points are its purpose, a military account of 1965 that historians dispute, and the armed forces' control over who can study it; the culture ministry's revitalisation will show whether that account changes.
