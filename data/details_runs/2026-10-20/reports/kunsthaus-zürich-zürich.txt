KUNSTHAUS ZÜRICH: Zürich, Switzerland
ID: kunsthaus-zürich-zürich
Website: https://www.kunsthaus.ch/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Heimplatz in central Zürich, run by the Zürcher Kunstgesellschaft, a membership association (Wikipedia; annual report 2024).
- The original building by Karl Moser and Robert Curjel opened in 1910 and was extended in 1925, 1958 and 1976. David Chipperfield designed the extension, which opened on 9 October 2021 and cost SFr206 million (The Art Newspaper).
- Directors: Christoph Becker from 2000 to 2022; Ann Demeester since 2022 (Wikipedia).
- On site: Moser building and Chipperfield extension, collection from the Middle Ages to contemporary art, and the Bührle collection of 205 works on permanent loan since 2021 from the E. G. Bührle Collection Foundation.
- Visiting: Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday until 20:00, closed Monday. The homepage lists no ticket prices.

FUNDING
Kunsthaus Zürich: mixed: city and canton subsidy, members, tickets, and corporate and foundation partners
- 2024 income was CHF 31.3 million, of which the City of Zürich paid an operating subsidy of CHF 13.5 million, the Canton CHF 0.38 million, members CHF 2.65 million, tickets CHF 5.5 million, project support CHF 2.4 million and donations CHF 1.46 million. The association lost CHF 1.53 million that year (annual report 2024).
- Canton Zürich gave CHF 150,000 for the new Bührle display and the federal culture office CHF 31,000 for provenance research (annual report 2024).
- Partners named on the museum's site: UBS ("Partner Kunsthaus Zürich"), Swiss Re ("Partner for contemporary art") and PerArte. Credit Suisse sponsored for more than 30 years; corporate sponsors gave under 10 percent of income, a third of it from Credit Suisse (The Art Newspaper, March 2023).
- The 2024 report also lists as corporate partners Bank Julius Bär, Boston Consulting Group, Siemens Schweiz, Zumtobel, UNIQA and JTI, among others; JTI is credited with supporting the 2024 Long Night of museums. The report does not give JTI's full name, and no reporting found confirming it.
- The extension was funded half by the city and canton and half by private donors, including Credit Suisse, UBS, Swiss Re and Zurich Insurance (the museum's extension page).
- The Bührle Foundation lends the collection; no reporting found of it paying the museum.

Red flags: Bührle arms fortune and Nazi-era provenance
- Emil Bührle was an arms dealer who sold weapons to Nazi Germany and the Allies and bought art from persecuted Jewish owners (The Art Newspaper, 2021).
- A petition with more than 2,200 signatures asked for transparency on provenance in 2021; historian Erich Keller accused the foundation of whitewashing provenance (The Art Newspaper).
- In June 2024 the foundation withdrew five paintings (Courbet, Monet, Toulouse-Lautrec, Van Gogh, Gauguin) under the loan contract, which bars display of works sold by Nazi-persecuted owners, and began talks with heirs (annual report; Times of Israel).
- The Raphael Gross report of June 2024, commissioned by the city, canton and the Kunstgesellschaft, called the foundation's provenance research "superficial". Apollo reports 62 of the 205 works appeared to have had Jewish owners; swissinfo reports 133.
- In October 2023 the advisory panel on the display resigned over how to present the collection's origins (Apollo).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Substantial criticism, almost all of the Bührle loan and the museum's handling of it rather than its general funding.
1. The Art Newspaper: Catherine Hickley, "Zurich takes 'quantum leap' with Chipperfield-designed Kunsthaus extension" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/09/20/zurich-takes-quantum-leap-with-chipperfield-designed-kunsthaus-extension
- Reports critics saying the Kunsthaus has not done enough to confront Bührle's history and the petition's demands for open archives and an independent documentation centre.
- The museum answered with a context room and planned research.
2. The Burlington Magazine: Louis Deltour, "The Chipperfield extension and private collections" (2022)
URL: https://www.burlington.org.uk/archive/exhibition-review/the-chipperfield-extension-and-private-collections
- Names the long links between art and political and economic power as a concern about the display; finds the curatorial display conventional.
- Concludes the collection's public access is worth welcoming.
3. Apollo: staff, "Bührle Collection's provenance research found inadequate by highly critical report" (2024)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/art-news-buhrle-collections-report-provenance-research-inadequate/
- Reports the Gross findings and his recommendations: more research, a committee, a public debate on the loan's future.
4. The Art Newspaper: Martin Bailey, "Zurich's controversial Bührle Collection is rehung" (2026)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/04/17/zurichs-controversial-buhrle-collection-rehung-including-five-paintings-van-gogh-one-forgery
- Calls Bührle "a highly controversial figure"; reports 190 of 205 works shown and open provenance questions on two Van Goghs.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Swissinfo (Catherine Hickley, 17 July 2024): Gross called the collection "historically particularly tainted" and recommended an independent commission and reconsidering the Bührle name for the collection. Swissinfo is a news outlet, not art media.
- Connection: the Bührle collection is on loan to this museum and shown in its building.

BOTTOM LINE
The running money is mainly public plus members, with named banks and other corporate partners whose amounts are not published. The weak points are the Bührle loan and its unresolved provenance questions, and a bank partner list that includes UBS after its Credit Suisse takeover. Watch the loan talks and the 2026 to 2031 provenance project.
