MUSEO DE AMÉRICA: Madrid, Spain
ID: museo-de-america
Website: https://www.cultura.gob.es/museodeamerica/portada.html (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- National museum of the archaeology, art and ethnography of the Americas at Avenida Reyes Católicos 6 in Madrid's Ciudad Universitaria (museum site).
- It was created by a decree of 19 April 1941 to bring together the public collections from the former territories with a Spanish presence (museum site).
- Its collection was first shown in eleven rooms of the National Archaeological Museum from 13 July 1944; its own building opened on 17 July 1965 and was closed for remodelling from 1981 to 1994 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Luis Moya and Luis Martínez-Feduchi designed the building in a historicist neo-Herrerian and neo-colonial style around a central garden cloister, in the manner of colonial missions (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Its catalogues hold about 25,000 objects; the oldest come from the Royal Cabinet of Natural History of Carlos III, founded in 1771 (El Salto, 2024).
- Collections cover pre-Hispanic archaeology, art of the colonial viceroyalties and 19th and 20th century ethnography; holdings include the Tudela Codex, Paracas textiles and the Quimbaya Treasure (museum site).
- The museum recorded 89,077 visitors in 2025 (Ministry of Culture, via La Moncloa).
- A new permanent display, planned "from a decolonial, anti-racist, intercultural and contemporary perspective", is to replace the 1990s one, which the museum says ignored gender and the slave trade (museum site; quotation translated by the atlas).
- Culture Minister Ernest Urtasun told Congress on 22 January 2024 that he would review state museum collections to move beyond a "colonial framework", especially those of this museum and the National Museum of Anthropology (El Salto; quotation translated by the atlas).
- Andrés Gutiérrez directs it (Infobae, 2025).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday 9:30 to 15:00, Sundays and holidays 10:00 to 15:00, closed Monday. Tickets cost EUR 3, reduced EUR 1.50; entry is free on Sundays, on Thursdays from 14:00, for under-18s, over-65s, students aged 18 to 25 and disabled visitors. Ramps and a lift serve all floors (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo de América: public: Spanish central government
- A state museum of Spain's Ministry of Culture, whose site it shares (museum site).
- The ministry put the new permanent display out to tender for an estimated EUR 9.2 million from March 2026, to finish in summer 2028, out of EUR 13.9 million for this museum and the National Museum of Anthropology together (Infobae, citing Europa Press, 2025).
- The Partido Popular asked in 2025 for the director and ministry officials to explain to Congress the criteria and the sums assigned to each museum (Infobae).
- The museum seeks sponsorship and donations from individuals, companies and foundations, which earn a 35% corporate tax deduction under Law 49/2002; it hires out its spaces for events (museum site).
- Members of the Friends of the Museo de América association pay reduced entry (museum site).

Red flags: Colombia's 2024 claim to the Quimbaya Treasure
- On 9 May 2024 Colombia's culture minister Juan David Correa wrote to Urtasun and Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares asking for the treasure's return (El Salto, 2024).
- Colombia's letter said the objects were looted by local grave robbers and handed to Spain in 1893; President Carlos Holguín gave them to Queen Regent María Cristina without the Colombian Congress's approval (eldiario.es, 2026).
- In October 2017 Colombia's Constitutional Court confirmed an order for the state to seek repatriation, finding that the transfer broke the 1886 Constitution (El Salto; eldiario.es).
- In June 2026 Colombia's vice-minister Saia Vergara Jaime said the talks were on "stand by" and would focus on a joint virtual exhibition and workshops with the museum (eldiario.es).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Spanish state money. The weak points are Colombia's unresolved claim to its best-known collection and the political dispute over the cost and aims of its new display.
