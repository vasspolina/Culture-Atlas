PINCHUKARTCENTRE: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: pinchukartcentre
Website: https://pinchukartcentre.org/en/about (checked 2026-10-24; the page showed "Closed" in its hours banner)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art centre in the Besarabka area of Kyiv, at Velyka Vasylkivska 1/3-2 (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Victor Pinchuk, who consolidated his philanthropy under the Victor Pinchuk Foundation in 2006; the centre opened on 16 September 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Occupies a historic complex with interiors by Philippe Chiambaretta, on six floors with more than 3,000 sq m of exhibition space (the centre site).
- On site: exhibition halls, a library, an education room and a video lounge. Programmes include the Research Platform (since 2016) and two prizes.
- Closed for 143 days after Russia's full-scale invasion of 24 February 2022 and reopened on 17 July 2022 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free admission; Wednesday to Sunday, 12 pm to 9 pm, last entry 8:30 pm; closed during air alerts. No accessibility details are given.

FUNDING
PinchukArtCentre: self-funded: founder Victor Pinchuk, through his foundation
- The centre site names Pinchuk as founder. No budget, donor list or board is published on the pages opened; syg.ma says the annual budget is undisclosed.
- Pinchuk's fortune: he founded Interpipe, a producer of seamless pipes and railway wheels, and with his wife is the largest shareholder of StarLightMedia (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors or a state funder for the centre itself.

Red flags: protest after dismissal of guides
- October 2019: guides, whom Wikipedia calls mediators, formed a union. 25 December 2019: union members protested near the centre and gave management a letter asking for talks on a collective agreement. Their fixed-term contracts were not renewed and on 9 February 2020 management announced it was cancelling the position (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper (28 February 2020) reported 28 guides dismissed; the Kyiv Post (23 February 2020) said about 20.
- The centre denies the allegations and says an internal review in September 2019 led to the decision (The Art Newspaper).
- Two nominees for the 2020 PinchukArtCentre Prize, Valentina Petrova and Pavlo Grazhdanskij, withdrew in solidarity (The Art Newspaper; Kyiv Post).

Red flags: founder's ties to the Kuchma privatisations and US donations
- In 2004 the state sold the Kryvorizhstal steel plant to Pinchuk and Rinat Akhmetov for $800 million (Kyiv Post). Kuchma was president at the time and Pinchuk is married to his daughter Olena Pinchuk (Wikipedia; its Pinchuk page carries a neutrality banner).
- The state took the plant back in June 2005 after President Yushchenko called the 2004 sale a theft, and Mittal Steel bought it at a televised auction on 24 October 2005 for more than $4.8 billion (AP via NBC News).
- Trump Foundation: tax returns released on 22 November 2016 showed a $150,000 gift from the Victor Pinchuk Foundation, which its chairman Thomas Weihe said supported Trump's video appearance at the Yalta European Strategy meeting in Kyiv on 11 September 2015 (Kyiv Post).
- Unverified: Ihor Kolomoisky said at a parliamentary hearing in March 2015 that Pinchuk received a bribe of $5 million a month for the rights to manage Ukrnafta; the National Anti-Corruption Bureau opened proceedings on 18 March 2015 (Wikipedia). syg.ma states that Pinchuk has never been charged.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in the established art press; the substantive critiques are a 2020 report on the guides dispute and a 2026 opinion essay on a community platform.
1. The Art Newspaper: Dorian Batycka, "PinchukArtCenter employees claim to have been sacked after attempting to form trade union" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/02/28/pinchukartcenter-employees-claim-to-have-been-sacked-after-attempting-to-form-trade-union
- 28 guides were dismissed after forming a union at the end of October 2019; union head Anastasia Bondarenko alleges threats, denied sick leave and precarious contracts.
- The centre denies the allegations; two prize nominees withdrew.
2. Kyiv Post (reposted by ArtLeaks): Toma Istomina, "Pinchuk Art Center mediators lose jobs after forming union" (2020)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2020/02/26/pinchuk-art-center-mediators-lose-jobs-after-forming-union-kyiv-ukraine/
- Petrova called the centre "a PR project of oligarch Pinchuk" and questioned how a jury could assess twenty works in a day; Grazhdanskij said its avoidance of public discussion raised questions about the prize's transparency.
3. syg.ma (community platform): artur.sumarokov, "The Pinchuk Art Centre: Two Decades of Cynical Art-Washing for Ukraine's Most Polished Oligarch" (2026)
URL: https://syg.ma/@artursumarokov/the-pinchuk-art-centre-two-decades-of-cynical-art-washing-for-ukraine-s-most-polished-oligarch
- Argues the centre launders reputation earned from Kuchma-era privatisations, and that Western institutions that took Pinchuk's money enabled it.
- Openly adversarial opinion essay by an individual author, not reporting; several claims are not documented.

BOTTOM LINE
No reporting found of an outside funder: Pinchuk's own money pays for the centre, and no budget or board is published. The weak points are the 2020 guides dismissal and prize withdrawals, and the founder's Kuchma-era privatisation and political donations. Worth watching: whether the centre publishes accounts or a board list.
