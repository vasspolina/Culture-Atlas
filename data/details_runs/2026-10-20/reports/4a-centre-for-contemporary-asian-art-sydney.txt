4A CENTRE FOR CONTEMPORARY ASIAN ART: Sydney, Australia
ID: 4a-centre-for-contemporary-asian-art-sydney
Website: https://4a.com.au/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit contemporary art centre at 181-187 Hay Street, Haymarket, Sydney, about a 5-minute walk from Central Station (stated by the centre).
- Founded in 1996 by a group of artists as the Asian Australian Artists' Association Inc.; Gallery 4A opened in 1997 on Sussex Street in Chinatown, moved to Liverpool Street in 1999 and to Hay Street in October 2000 (stated by the centre). Wikipedia names Victoria Lobregat, Dacchi Dang, John Young and Melissa Chiu, the inaugural director, among the founders.
- Renamed 4A Centre for Contemporary Asian Art in 2009 (stated by the centre).
- Building: the heritage-listed Corporation Building, owned by the City of Sydney (stated by the centre); the centre occupies two spaces in it (City of Sydney).
- Programmes: exhibitions, studio residencies, curatorial workshop intensives, talks, a library, an archive and the 4A Papers publication (stated by the centre). The Strategic Plan 2024 to 2028 is titled #NuWorlds.
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 11am to 5pm; entry to exhibitions is free. The visit page gives no accessibility information beyond transport directions and asks visitors to email to confirm step-free access.

FUNDING
4A Centre for Contemporary Asian Art: mainly public: Commonwealth, New South Wales and City of Sydney, with a private patron scheme
- Commonwealth: Creative Australia and the Australia Council's Visual Arts and Craft Strategy (4A donate page and about page).
- New South Wales Government: Create NSW (same pages). In November 2021 4A received $200,000 and in March 2025 $167,650, both from the NSW ClubGRANTS Category 3 Fund, for refurbishment and redevelopment of its exhibition space; the NSW recipients page lists the Office of Responsible Gambling as the funding agency and the approving ministers as the Minister for Hospitality and Racing (2021) and the Minister for Gaming and Racing (2025). The page does not say how the fund is financed.
- City of Sydney: its Accommodation Grants Program. On 19 August 2024 Council raised the rent subsidy for two spaces in the Corporation Building from 97% to 100%, so 4A pays no rent for the rest of its lease. The market rent for Level 1 and Shop 3 is $309,515 for 2025-26, and for Shop 2 it is $68,534 for 2025-26 (City of Sydney decision).
- The Myer Foundation: logo on the donate page; no amount or year found.
- Share of public money: Wikipedia puts income in 2020-21 at A$790,232, 78% from government grants and 12% self-generated.
- Patrons (donate page, undated): Backers Johanna Featherstone and Geoff Ainsworth, Susan Acret and James Roth, Vivian Vidulich; Angels Adrian Williams, Dick Quan and John McGrath, John Choi, Gabby Ewington and an anonymous donor; Allies Caroline Choy, Elizabeth Pakchung, Julie Ewington, Richard Funston and Kiong Lee, Simon Chan, Seva Frangos Catlin, Dr John Yu and an anonymous donor; 82 Friends listed, one anonymous. The page says 4A "is supported by a number of trusts and foundations" and names none. Donations are tax-deductible.
- Media partner: Vault Magazine.
- The 2025 annual report was not read, so current income figures are not verified.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind 4A is mostly Australian public funding, a city rent subsidy of 100% and named private patrons; nothing found ties it to a flagged industry. The weak points are the dependence on public grants and the overlap between board and patrons: Julie Ewington (chair), Elizabeth Pakchung, Adrian Williams and Caroline Choy appear on both the board and the patron lists. Watch the size of the NSW gaming-fund grants and the next review of the City of Sydney lease subsidy.
