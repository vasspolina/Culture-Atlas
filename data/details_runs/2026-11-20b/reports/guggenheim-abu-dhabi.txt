GUGGENHEIM ABU DHABI: Abu Dhabi, UAE
ID: guggenheim-abu-dhabi
Website: https://www.guggenheimabudhabi.ae/ (checked 2026-10-24; serves a bot check)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art in the cultural district of Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, beside the Louvre Abu Dhabi and the Zayed National Museum (Wikipedia; Artforum).
- Abu Dhabi signed the agreement with the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation on 8 July 2006; work on the site began in 2011, was suspended, and the opening slipped from 2011 to 2013, 2015, 2017 and 2025 (Wikipedia; Gulf News).
- Designed by Frank Gehry: 28 interior galleries around a central atrium, 11,600 square metres inside and 23,000 square metres of outdoor exhibition space across 10 sculptural cones and terraces (Khaleej Times). Artforum counts 30 galleries in over 800,000 square feet. BESIX subsidiary Six Construct and Trojan Contracting won the construction contract in October 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Collection: built since 2009 and owned by the Abu Dhabi government, not the Foundation; art from about 1960 to now, centred on West Asia, North Africa and South Asia, with reported totals above 970 works by more than 400 artists (Gulf News, September 2026).
- Leadership: Gulf News names Valerie Hillings as inaugural director; The National (February 2026) called Stephanie Rosenthal director of the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi project. Mariët Westermann is director and CEO of the Guggenheim Foundation (Gulf News; Hyperallergic).
- Opens on 11 December 2026. General admission 70 dirhams; free for under-18s, UAE students and over-60s (Khaleej Times).

FUNDING
Guggenheim Abu Dhabi: Gulf state body: the Abu Dhabi government, through its culture department and development company
- Ownership: a 2008 Guggenheim statement said Abu Dhabi's Tourism Development and Investment Company (TDIC) "will own the museum", while the Foundation would "establish and manage" its programme (Wikipedia).
- Development: the museum is developed by the Department of Culture and Tourism, Abu Dhabi (DCT) with the Guggenheim Foundation (Khaleej Times); DCT chairman Mohamed Khalifa Al Mubarak has spoken for its collection policy (Gulf News).
- Cost: undisclosed (The Art Newspaper, July 2026); Artforum reports an estimate of over 1 billion US dollars from The New York Times.
- Labour monitoring: in 2012 TDIC hired PricewaterhouseCoopers as an independent monitor reporting quarterly (Wikipedia).
- Income after opening: admission at 70 dirhams (Khaleej Times).

Red flags: Abu Dhabi state ownership
- The museum, its collection and its development sit with Abu Dhabi government bodies: TDIC, the Department of Culture and Tourism and the Abu Dhabi government (Wikipedia; Khaleej Times; Gulf News).

Red flags: migrant labour abuse on Saadiyat Island
- Human Rights Watch interviewed 113 current and former Saadiyat workers and reported recruitment fees, withheld wages, passport confiscation and poor housing; TDIC's monitor PwC found that 88 percent of 1,050 workers it interviewed for its 2014 report had paid recruitment fees (Architectural Record, 2015).
- TDIC chief executive Ali Al Hammadi wrote to HRW that workers' rights are "not solely a TDIC challenge alone, but rather an international matter" (Architectural Record).
- In July 2026 Artforum noted that the museum and other Saadiyat projects had come under fire from human rights groups over the exploitation of migrant construction workers.

Red flags: Gulf Labor Coalition boycott, unresolved in 2026
- After the July 2026 opening announcement, Gulf Labor co-founder Andrew Ross said: "None of the initial reasons for this action have been adequately resolved over the years" (Hyperallergic, 2026).
- The Guggenheim did not answer Hyperallergic's request for comment (Hyperallergic, 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism for about 16 years, almost all about labour conditions and the artist boycott rather than the art programme.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In March 2011 more than 130 artists and writers vowed to refuse cooperation until workers' recruitment fees were repaid and an independent monitor published its findings (Arab News).
- In February 2014 Gulf Labor activists dropped banners including "1% Museum" from the spiral of the Guggenheim in New York (Democracy Now).
- In 2015 its offshoot G.U.L.F. occupied the Guggenheim in New York on May Day and the Peggy Guggenheim Collection in Venice on 8 May (Hyperallergic, 2026).
- These protests targeted the Foundation over this museum.

BOTTOM LINE
Abu Dhabi state property with the Guggenheim Foundation running the programme, and a labour record its critics say was never put right. Worth watching: whether the Foundation or DCT publishes labour monitoring for the finished build.
