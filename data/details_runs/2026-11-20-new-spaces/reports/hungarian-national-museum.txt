HUNGARIAN NATIONAL MUSEUM: Budapest, Hungary
ID: hungarian-national-museum
Website: https://mnm.hu/en (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Hungary's national museum of history and archaeology, at Múzeum krt. 14-16, 1088 Budapest.
- Its stated mission is to collect and present the past of the peoples of the Carpathian Basin and the heritage of the Hungarian nation, with a collection of several million objects.
- On site: history exhibitions, a museum garden and a shop. The site lists the Coronation Mantle display as closed to visitors at present.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. The full ticket is HUF 3,500, discounted adult and youth tickets HUF 1,750, the family ticket HUF 7,200.

FUNDING
Hungarian National Museum: public: the Hungarian state through its culture ministry
- The ministry responsible for culture is the museum's maintaining authority, and the minister holds the employer's powers over the director general (Telex, 6 November 2023).

Red flags: the state dismissed the director over an exhibition, 2023
- On 6 November 2023 the Minister of Culture and Innovation, János Csák, dismissed director general László L. Simon with immediate effect (Telex).
- The case was the World Press Photo exhibition at the museum, which included Hannah Reyes Morales's series "Home for the Golden Gays" on a care home in Manila for older LGBTQ+ residents (The Art Newspaper).
- Dóra Dúró, an MP of the Mi Hazánk party, had filed a complaint, and the minister ordered the museum to admit only visitors over 18, citing the law on the protection of families (Telex).
- The ministry said Simon had failed to meet the institution's legal obligations. Simon said the museum had complied and had restricted entry for under-18s, and that the minister accused him of sabotaging the Child Protection Act.
- World Press Photo's executive director, Joumana El Zein Khoury, said she was shocked by the dismissal and that the images contained nothing explicit or offensive (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage of the museum centres on the 2023 dismissal and is critical of the government, not of the museum's programme.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "World Press Photo 'shocked' at ousting of Hungarian National Museum director in LGBTQ+ exhibition row" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/11/07/world-press-photo-shocked-at-ousting-of-hungarian-national-museum-director-in-lgbtq+-exhibition-row
- Reports that the government fired Simon for not complying with a 2021 law that bans showing homosexuality to under-18s.
- Quotes World Press Photo's defence of the photographs and Simon's refusal to accept that children need protecting from his institution.
- Notes that the museum had already posted a warning notice after Mi Hazánk opened an inquiry.

BOTTOM LINE
The state pays for and controls the museum, and in 2023 it used that control to remove a director over the content of an exhibition. That makes political interference the point to watch here, more than any private funder.
