MUSEUM MACAN: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: museum-macan-jakarta
Website: https://www.museummacan.org (checked 2026-10-24; a notice says the museum is temporarily closed)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Modern & Contemporary Art in Nusantara, in AKR Tower, Jalan Panjang 5, Kebon Jeruk, West Jakarta; the site lists nearby TransJakarta stops and a KRL station.
- Founded by the businessman and collector Haryanto Adikoesoemo; opened to the public in early November 2017, timed with the Jakarta Biennale and Biennale Jogja (Smithsonian Magazine). The Jakarta Post gave 4 November as the planned opening date.
- Its collection is drawn from about 800 works he collected over 25 years, about half of them Indonesian (The Art Newspaper; Jakarta Post: about 50% Indonesian, 25% US and European, 25% from the wider Asian region).
- The museum occupies a 4,000 square metre horseshoe-shaped floor of a tower in west Jakarta (Smithsonian Magazine); the museum's site gives 5,000 square metres including education and conservation spaces.
- Opened with "Art Turns. World Turns", 90 works by 70 artists from the founder's holdings, including Andy Warhol, Mark Rothko, Jeff Koons and Yayoi Kusama as well as Raden Saleh and F.X. Harsono (Smithsonian Magazine). Director at opening: Aaron Seeto.
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:30, closed Mondays. The site lists no ticket prices or accessibility information.

FUNDING
Museum MACAN: self-funded: the Adikoesoemo family, through a private foundation
- The museum is privately funded; director Aaron Seeto said "Even though we are privately funded" (Smithsonian Magazine).
- It is run by a foundation chaired by Fenessa Adikoesoemo, daughter of Haryanto Adikoesoemo. It holds his collection on long-term loan, and occupies a floor of a multi-purpose complex in West Jakarta managed by his property development company. The project cost is undisclosed (Museums Journal, January 2018).
- The family business: Haryanto Adikoesoemo heads the energy and chemicals conglomerate AKR Corporindo (The Art Newspaper, Smithsonian Magazine). His father Soegiarto Adikoesoemo is listed as AKR's founder, which began in 1978 as PT Aneka Kimia Raya trading basic chemicals (Wikipedia, career details marked "citation needed").
- No reporting found of any corporate sponsor, government grant, published accounts or board for the museum.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (founder's family company AKR Corporindo)
- AKR Corporindo is described as one of Indonesia's largest distributors of petroleum products and basic chemicals. In December 2013 the downstream oil and gas regulator BPH Migas awarded it a contract to distribute 650,000 kilolitres of subsidised fuel for 2014, and in February 2014 it ran 116 fuel stations (Petromindo, 10 March 2014).
- The museum's own address is AKR Tower, and Haryanto Adikoesoemo's property company manages the building (Museums Journal).
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter aimed at the museum over AKR.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage found is descriptive or admiring, and the pieces read do not address the family's petroleum business.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a family project: the founder's collection, a family-chaired foundation and a building managed by the founder's property company. The weak point is the source of the family wealth in AKR Corporindo, a petroleum and chemicals distributor, and the lack of any published accounts, board or sponsor list. Worth watching: whether the foundation ever discloses its finances.
