MIMA: Middlesbrough, UK
ID: mima
Website: https://mima.art

OVERVIEW
- Middlesbrough Institute of Modern Art, an art gallery on Centre Square in central Middlesbrough (Wikipedia).
- Formally launched on 27 January 2007, after construction began in 2004. Its first director, Godfrey Worsdale, left in 2008 for the Baltic Centre for Contemporary Art (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by Erick van Egeraat Associated Architects and cost £14.2 million (Attractions Management, 31 January 2007).
- The former Cleveland Crafts Centre on Gilkes Street closed as an exhibition venue in January 2003 and served as the MIMA offices while the new gallery was built (Wikipedia).
- The building holds gallery spaces, an education suite, an auditorium, a cafe-bar, a shop, a roof terrace, collection stores and a conservation studio. The Middlesbrough Collection covers fine and applied art from the 19th century to the present, with works by Frank Auerbach, David Hockney and L.S. Lowry (Wikipedia).
- Part of Teesside University since September 2014, when it passed from the local council to the university (Teesside University, Wikipedia).

FUNDING
MIMA: mainly public: Arts Council England and Middlesbrough Council, through Teesside University
- Teesside University states that MIMA is "Funded by Arts Council England and Middlesbrough Council" (Teesside University).
- 2024-25: MIMA says it worked with funding from Arts Council England (including the ACE Cultural Development Fund and the ACE V&A Purchase Grant Fund), Middlesbrough Council, Freelands Foundation, Contemporary Art Society, Henry Moore Foundation, the AHRC Impact Acceleration Account, Art Fund and Holiday Action Fund. It describes itself as an Arts Council England National Portfolio Organisation and part of Teesside University, and says Arts Council England funding for its Borderlands programme was extended to 2026 in August 2024 (MIMA Impact Report 2024-25).
- The 2007 build was funded by the local council, Arts Council England, Tees Valley Partnership in Action and the European Regional Development Fund (Attractions Management, 31 January 2007).
- MIMA became part of Teesside University in September 2014 (Teesside University); Wikipedia says it passed from the local council to the university. The university describes the move as combining two public institutions to support each other.

BOTTOM LINE
MIMA is a public gallery: Teesside University says Arts Council England and Middlesbrough Council fund it, and it has been part of the university since September 2014. Its 2024-25 impact report also names Freelands Foundation, Contemporary Art Society, Henry Moore Foundation, the AHRC, Art Fund and Holiday Action Fund. The 2007 building cost of £14.2 million was funded by the local council, Arts Council England, Tees Valley Partnership in Action and the European Regional Development Fund.
