HENRY ART GALLERY: Seattle, USA
ID: henry-art-gallery
Website: https://henryart.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art on the west edge of the University of Washington campus, along 15th Avenue N.E. in the University District (Wikipedia).
- Founded by Horace C. Henry, who funded the museum and donated 178 artworks; it opened to the public on 10 February 1927 and was the first public art museum in Washington state (Wikipedia).
- Named for Horace C. Henry; from 1917 he had run a wing of his Capitol Hill home as a free museum (Wikipedia).
- The original building was designed by Bebb and Gould; Gwathmey Siegel & Associates designed the expansion, finished in 1997, which brought the building to 40,000 square feet and added a 154-seat auditorium (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, an auditorium and James Turrell's Skyspace "Light Reign" (2003); the Skyspace and Sculpture Court were marked temporarily closed for maintenance on the museum's website on 24 October 2026.
- Visiting: the museum states it is accessible to all visitors. No reporting found of current opening hours or ticket prices on the pages that could be opened.

FUNDING
Henry Art Gallery: mixed public and private: University of Washington administration, city, county and state arts agencies, federal grants, foundations and sponsors
- The gallery is administered by the Arts Division of the University of Washington's College of Arts and Sciences (Wikipedia). Its fundraising body is the Henry Gallery Association Inc, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit with EIN 23-7052537 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer). The ProPublica page for EIN 91-6001537 returned HTTP 404 on 25 October 2026, so no filings from that number are cited.
- Form 990 for the year to June 2025: revenue $4,275,948, expenses $4,830,955, net assets $29,355,357. Contributions were $3,508,645, 82.1% of revenue, and investment income was $841,466 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer). The previous year also ran a deficit: revenue $3,629,093, expenses $4,379,856.
- Funders page lists the City of Seattle Office of Arts & Culture as Producing Partner. Sustaining Partners: 4Culture, ArtsFund (Ackerley Excellence Fund and Peter F. Donnelly Merit Fund), the Guendolen Carkeek Plestcheeff Fund for the Decorative and Design Arts, Phillips, the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, the Helen Martha Schiff Foundation, the Volunteer Park Trust, and the Washington State Arts Commission and the National Endowment for the Arts. Hospitality Partners: Graduate Seattle Hotel and Staypineapple. The page gives no amounts. It carries no publication date, "last updated" note or fiscal year (henryart.org, opened 25 October 2026).
- Expansion capital in the 1990s: the Washington State Legislature allocated $8.3 million in 1993, and the Allen Charitable Foundation pledged up to $5 million as a matching gift announced on 22 February 1994 (University of Washington Magazine, 1 June 1994).
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, arms, tobacco or gambling funder, or of a funder tied to a documented controversy.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism. One local review faulted its programming in 2016, and one 2021 essay criticised an outdoor project it took part in.
1. The Stranger: Jen Graves, "When Did the Henry Art Gallery Fall Asleep?" (4 October 2016)
URL: https://www.thestranger.com/visual-art/2016/10/04/24596415/when-did-the-henry-art-gallery-fall-asleep
- Argues the Henry gave its largest galleries for ten months to a Paul McCarthy show it did not organise and that came from a commercial gallery.
- Says the museum's stated commitment to bold work does not match programming that repeatedly features older white male artists, and that a timely Senga Nengudi show was scheduled to run under three months.
- Points to ties between the museum and private collectors connected to its board. The piece carries a correction: the author withdrew her claim of a budget decline after director Sylvia Wolf supplied figures.
- Opinion piece; criticises the institution, under director Sylvia Wolf.
2. Hyperallergic: Matthew Offenbacher, "Seattle Museums Are Artwashing Their Way Back to Normal" (12 October 2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/684194/seattle-museums-are-artwashing-their-way-back-to-normal/
- Argues the Henry's August 2021 outdoor show "Bodies of Discovery" frames downtown space as "our again-accessible public space", which excludes Black, Brown and Indigenous people who already live there, and that its title echoes colonial justifications for land seizure.
- Says the show's sole funder is a business association (the article links to downtownseattle.org) and describes that association's programme as an "aggressive gentrification program".
- Names the Henry, On the Boards and Velocity Dance Center.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Henry is mostly public, university and foundation funding, with no flagged industry or funder tied to a documented controversy found. The weak points are the thin published detail (the funders page has no amounts or dates) and two years of deficits in 2024 and 2025. Worth watching: how far the university and arts agencies cover the shortfall, and who pays for outdoor projects such as "Bodies of Discovery".
