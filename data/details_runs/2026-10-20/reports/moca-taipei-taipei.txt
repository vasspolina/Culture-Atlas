MOCA TAIPEI: Taipei, Taiwan
ID: moca-taipei-taipei
Website: https://www.mocataipei.org.tw/

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at No. 39 Chang-An West Road, Datong District, Taipei, within walking distance southwest of Zhongshan Station on the Taipei Metro (MOCA Taipei, Wikipedia).
- Opened in May 2001 and described as the first art institution in Taiwan dedicated exclusively to contemporary art (Ministry of Education art education site, Wikipedia).
- The building was completed in 1921, in the Japanese colonial period, as Kensei Elementary School. After 1945 it housed Taipei City Government offices; it was designated a municipal historic site in 1996, and the rest of the building is occupied by Jian Cheng Junior High School (Ministry of Education site, Wikipedia).
- The museum was run for its first seven years, 2001 to 2007, by the Contemporary Art Foundation under a "public-owned, privately operated" arrangement with the city. On 1 January 2008 the Taipei Culture Foundation took over operations (Ministry of Education site).
- Directors: its activities became more international under Shih Jui-jen, and Loh Li-chen was director from 2019 to 2025 (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Sunday 10AM to 6PM, closed Monday (MOCA Taipei).

FUNDING
MOCA Taipei: public-owned, privately operated: run by the Taipei Culture Foundation since 2008, with corporate sponsors
- The building is the former Taipei City Government office and the museum has operated under a "public-owned, privately operated" arrangement since 2001 (Ministry of Education site).
- Operator since 2008: the Taipei Culture Foundation. The museum's support page says that since opening in 2001 it has combined official resources with private support (title translated).
- Sponsors listed on the museum's support page: Epson Taiwan and the Contemporary Art Foundation (platinum-level); Lord Hotels (gold-level); Sony as designated annual TV and screen supplier; Thermos as exhibition collaborator.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One dispute between the artist Jun Yang and the museum, which began when Loh Li-chen became director in November 2019 and reached the press in August 2021. The pieces describe a dispute over contracts and procedure; none uses the word censorship.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: no author named, "MoCA Taipei Embroiled in Dispute over Nearly Canceled Show" (16 August 2021)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/moca-taipei-embroiled-in-dispute-over-nearly-canceled-show
- Says the dispute began as a contract disagreement over Yang's three-venue Taipei exhibition. Yang alleges that Loh, who became director on 1 November 2019, tried to cancel his show without negotiating; after he threatened legal action, talks in December 2019 and January 2020 produced a deal to keep it with its three-month run halved. The MoCA chapter opened on 6 February and closed on 31 March.
- Yang alleges that a MoCA curator was fired, that he and his family were targeted by anonymous internet trolls, and that a Facebook post by Loh reading "Fake foreigners who think they're superior come to Taiwan and take all the resources" was aimed at him.
- The museum's 31 July statement said it respected the artist's concept and did not interfere, that no contract had been signed and the show was not in the annual plan, and that Loh staged it at the expense of the 20th anniversary programme. Vice director Chang Yu-Han acknowledged administrative mistakes. A spokesperson said the museum insists on "maintaining the neutrality of art and preserving the artist's rights."
2. ArtReview: no author named, "Row between artist Jun Yang and MoCA Taipei stirs controversy in Taiwan's artworld" (17 August 2021)
URL: https://artreview.com/row-between-artist-jun-yang-and-moca-taipei-stirs-controversy-in-taiwan-artworld
- Reports that after Yang threatened legal action the museum and "the city culture foundation" agreed to keep the show with a shorter run, and that Yang says Loh refused to attend the opening and that delays and bureaucratic excuses undermined the project.
- Reports the museum's 31 July statement that a gap in the handover between directors left Loh without a briefing on Yang's agreement with her predecessor Yuki Pan.
- Reports commentators on the Kaobei Art Facebook page calling for transparency in public institutions and for protocols that protect artists and curators.
3. Taipei Times: Michael Turton, "Notes from Central Taiwan: No place for official insularity" (23 August 2021)
URL: https://taipeitimes.com/News/feat/archives/2021/08/23/2003763064
- Frames the episode as bureaucratic insularity: "Officials have since retreated into silence, clearly hoping the incident will just disappear."
- Quotes Yang on "a strange contrast between a public museum director posting such words, and city cultural officialdom hiding in silence." Names no city body.

BOTTOM LINE
MOCA Taipei is a public-owned, privately operated museum in a municipal historic building, run since 2008 by the Taipei Culture Foundation, with named corporate sponsors including Epson Taiwan and Sony. The documented weak point is the museum's handling of Jun Yang's exhibition, which the press reported as a dispute over contracts and procedure.
