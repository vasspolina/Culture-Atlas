NAM JUNE PAIK ART CENTER: Yongin, South Korea
ID: nam-june-paik-art-center-yongin
Website: https://njp.ggcf.kr/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads in Korean, with an English site at njpart.ggcf.kr)

OVERVIEW
- Public art centre in Giheung-gu, Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, at 10 Paiknamjune-ro (Gyeonggi Province English site); Wikipedia names Giheung station as the nearest transit.
- The basic plan was set with the artist Nam June Paik in November 2001; the building opened with a Nam June Paik Festival on 8 October 2008 (Gyeonggi Province English site). Paik died before he saw it (JoongAng Daily, Chung Jae-sook, 7 April 2010).
- Named for Nam June Paik, the Korean American video artist; its permanent collection is of his work, with temporary exhibitions (Wikipedia).
- Designed by Kirsten Schemel and Marina Stankovic of KSMS Schemel Stankovic Architects, Berlin, who won a 2003 international competition of 430 entries (centre site). Three storeys above ground and two below, about 5,600 square metres on a 30,983 square metre site, with a screen facade of layered reflective and partly printed glass.
- Holds galleries, a video archive and multi-purpose spaces (centre site). It awards the Nam June Paik Art Center Prize, first given in 2009 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday 10am to 6pm (until 7pm in July and August), closed Mondays, 1 January and Thanksgiving. Adults 4,000 won; students and soldiers 2,000 won; under 6, over 66 and people with disabilities free (Gyeonggi Province English site, undated). The centre's own site lists sign language interpretation for exhibition guides. The Gyeonggi page shows a 25% discount for Gyeonggi Province residents and a 50% discount for adult groups of 20 or more. No page opened states that admission is free for all visitors; the centre's home pages opened give no fee. No reporting found of further accessibility details.

FUNDING
Nam June Paik Art Center: public: Gyeonggi Province through the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation, with support from Hyundai Motor for its exhibitions
- The centre is run by the Gyeonggi Cultural Foundation (centre site footer). The site states that individual donations and corporate and organisational sponsorship support its programmes.
- Hyundai Motor money reaching the centre today: Hyundai Motor Group (23 June 2026) says it runs the Hyundai Translocal Series, launched in 2025, in which the centre and the Pinacoteca de Sao Paulo co-organise an exhibition opening in November 2026; the Herald (22 June 2026) describes Hyundai Motor as the series' organiser and does not say Hyundai sponsors the centre directly. The Gyeonggi Ilbo (June 2025) reports that the 2025 programme was set at four exhibitions "thanks to Hyundai Motor's sponsorship" (translation from the Korean), against an exhibition budget of 510 million won.
- No reporting found of the amount or term of Hyundai's support. No reporting found of a KRW 600 million sponsorship from Hyundai. Hyundai's announcement gives no funding figure or duration.
- No reporting found of the centre's own accounts; the foundation's PDF documents could not be read.

Red flags: Hyundai and child labour at a subsidiary
- July 2022: Reuters reported that SMART, a Hyundai subsidiary in Luverne, Alabama, hired minors as young as 12; a former employee estimated about 50 (Wikipedia, summarising Reuters). SMART supplies parts to Hyundai's Montgomery plant. Hyundai said it does not tolerate illegal employment practices at any Hyundai entity.
- Authorities: after an inspection on 9 August 2022, the US Department of Labor fined SL Alabama, another supplier, $30,076 on 29 September 2022 and a federal consent decree barred it from shipping goods made with underage labour. The Alabama Department of Labor fined SL Alabama and JK USA $17,800 each. Neither agency accused Hyundai or Kia of wrongdoing, and the article reports no fine against SMART itself (Wikipedia).
- 24 February 2023: Hyundai CEO Jae Hoon Chang told shareholders Hyundai would divest its controlling stake in SMART (Wikipedia).
- Link to the centre: Hyundai Motor's support for the centre is described under FUNDING; no source opened connects the Alabama case to the centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mainly Gyeonggi Province's public budget, with support from Hyundai Motor for its exhibitions. The weak points are the undisclosed size and term of the Hyundai support and Hyundai's 2022 child-labour case at its Alabama subsidiary, whose controlling stake it said in February 2023 it would divest. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes sponsor amounts for the Translocal Series.
