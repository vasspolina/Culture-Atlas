ID: museum-ludwig

WIDER CONTEXT
- From 2016 the heirs of Heinrich Rieger, a Viennese dentist and collector deported to Theresienstadt in 1942, claimed Egon Schiele's watercolour "Kauernder weiblicher Akt", owned by the City of Cologne and held by the Museum Ludwig; the city and the heirs agreed to put the case to the German government's advisory commission on Nazi-looted art (City of Cologne, 8 February 2021).
- Cologne had argued "it was possible Rieger sold the work before the Nazis annexed Austria". On 8 February 2021 the commission unanimously recommended restitution, finding that most of Rieger's sales "took place after March 1938 as a consequence of the pressures of Nazi persecution" (The Art Newspaper, Catherine Hickley, 8 February 2021).
- The museum's director, Yilmaz Dziewior, said: "We are relieved that there are now prospects for a fair and just solution with the return of the work to the family." (The Art Newspaper)
- On 23 March 2021 the Cologne City Council decided to return the watercolour to Rieger's heirs, following the commission's recommendation (City of Cologne, March 2021).
