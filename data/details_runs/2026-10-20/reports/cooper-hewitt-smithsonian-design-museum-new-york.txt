COOPER HEWITT, SMITHSONIAN DESIGN MUSEUM: New York, USA
ID: cooper-hewitt-smithsonian-design-museum-new-york
Website: https://www.cooperhewitt.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- The Smithsonian's national design museum, at 2 East 91st Street on Manhattan's Upper East Side, along Museum Mile; one of two Smithsonian museums outside Washington (stated by the space, Smithsonian budget justification).
- Founded in 1897 by the Hewitt sisters, granddaughters of Peter Cooper, as the Cooper Union Museum for the Arts of Decoration; the Smithsonian took over the collection in 1967 to 1968 (Wikipedia).
- It opened in the Andrew Carnegie Mansion on 7 October 1976; a $91 million renovation, partly paid from the endowment, was completed in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds about 215,000 objects; Maria Nicanor has been director since 2022 (Smithsonian budget justification, Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 10am to 6pm; adults $10 during the current changeover, $5 for seniors, students and visitors with disabilities, free for 18 and under, and pay what you wish 5pm to 6pm daily (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Cooper Hewitt: mixed: federal Smithsonian money plus private donors, foundations and companies
- In fiscal 2023 the museum had $5.7 million in federal appropriations, $2.9 million in general trust funds, $4.3 million in donor and sponsor money and $114,000 in grants and contracts; the fiscal 2025 request was $6.2 million federal, with $3.75 million trust and $5.0 million donor funds projected (Smithsonian budget justification, fiscal 2025).
- Gifts of $100,000 and above in October 2023 to September 2024 came from Adobe, the Henry Luce Foundation, the Terra Foundation, the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs, Ravenel Curry and Jane Moss, and Shelby and Frederick Gans (stated by the space).
- Gifts of $25,000 to $49,999 included Apple, Bloomberg Philanthropies, PwC Charitable Foundation, the New York State Council on the Arts and the Keith Haring Foundation (stated by the space).

Red flags: 2020 ouster of the director
- The Smithsonian dismissed director Caroline Baumann on 7 February 2020 after an inspector general inquiry into her 2018 wedding dress and venue (Hyperallergic).
- Six of 27 advisory trustees resigned in protest: Kurt Andersen, Judy Francis Zankel, Jon Kamen, Francine S. Kittredge, Avi N. Reichental and David Rockwell (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: political pressure over "Patterns of Life"
- In May 2025 the Israeli NGO Shurat HaDin wrote to Vice President JD Vance and other officials urging the White House to cancel "Patterns of Life", citing President Trump's 27 March 2025 executive order; the Jerusalem Post article of 8 May 2025 was written in collaboration with Shurat HaDin, an advocacy group. The show was Mona Chalabi and SITU Research's reconstruction of homes destroyed in Gaza, Mosul and Manbij (Hyperallergic, August 2025).
- Unverified: a 2026 Smithsonian inspector general report found the museum used federal funds from the American Women's History Initiative pool for the show without notifying the fund's administrators (the article gives the sum as both $36,000 and $36,500), and that the exhibition review committee worked without defined procedures, from The Federalist, an openly conservative outlet. The report itself was not found.
- The show stayed on view from late 2024 until August 2025 (The Federalist, Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the programme in art media. Art press covered the 2020 trustee revolt over the director's ouster; the attacks on "Patterns of Life" came from advocacy groups and conservative outlets, while Hyperallergic ran a feature on the show's models and the families behind them.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "After Cooper Hewitt Director Is Ousted, Six Trustees Resign in Protest" (2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/cooper-hewitt-trustees-resign/
- Zankel called Baumann "an exemplary leader who gave everything to her job" and wrote that her treatment "violates every principle of decency".
- Zankel argued the punishment was out of proportion and suggested sexism played a role; she said staying on the board "tacitly condones this behavior".
- Criticises the Smithsonian's handling, not the museum's funders; the Smithsonian and Cooper Hewitt declined to comment.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 28 May 2026 Artists for Palestine UK published an open letter, signed by 261 artists, asking five British theatres to end Bloomberg partnerships over its funding of a Tel Aviv University centre that trains officials who govern West Bank settlements (Morning Star). The letter does not name Cooper Hewitt. It is listed here, not as a red flag: the campaign targets Bloomberg at other institutions.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is federal Smithsonian funding plus a broad base of technology companies, foundations and New York donors; nothing in it points to a flagged industry. The weak point is the museum's exposure to federal political pressure, shown by the 2025 campaign against "Patterns of Life".
