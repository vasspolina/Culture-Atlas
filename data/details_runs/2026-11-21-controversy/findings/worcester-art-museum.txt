ID: worcester-art-museum

Red flags: 2023 seizure of a Roman bronze bust in a Turkish looting investigation
- In September 2023 New York authorities seized "Portrait of a Lady", a bronze bust dated A.D. 160 to 180 that the museum acquired in 1966, as part of an investigation into antiquities stolen from Turkey; the investigation involves a smuggling network that trafficked objects looted from Bubon in southwestern Turkey through Manhattan (WBUR, Associated Press, 5 September 2023).
- The museum said it transferred the bust to the Manhattan District Attorney's Office after "new information" that it was "likely stolen and improperly imported", and that it "had never previously received a claim or learned of any defect in ownership"; GBH News gave the bust's estimated value as about $5 million (GBH News, 4 September 2023).
- Director Matthias Waschek said: "We are very thankful for the new information provided to us" (WBUR; GBH News).

Red flags: 2025 agreement to return two looted Greek vessels to Italy
- On 28 January 2025 the museum signed an agreement with Italy's culture ministry over a black-figure amphora attributed to the Rycroft Painter and a black-figure kylix, both illegally taken out of Italy; the Carabinieri confirmed both were in Italy's database of stolen cultural property (The Art Newspaper, Daniel Grant, 31 January 2025).
- The museum bought the amphora from Elie Borowski in 1956, and Borowski gave the kylix at the same time; the dealer Robert Hecht was the first known owner of both. The vessels stay on view, labelled as looted, on a loan of four to eight years, then go back to Italy, which lends comparable antiquities in return (The Art Newspaper).
- Claire Whitner, director of curatorial affairs, said the museum "did not have the in-house expertise to know which objects might have provenance issues" (The Art Newspaper).
