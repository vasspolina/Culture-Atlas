TENSTA KONSTHALL: Stockholm, Sweden
ID: tensta-konsthall-stockholm
Website: https://tenstakonsthall.se (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre at Taxingegränd 10 in Tensta, northwest Stockholm, in "formerly unused storage space underneath Tensta Shopping Mall", about 700 square metres at Taxingeplan (tenstakonsthall.se, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1998 as a grassroots initiative, partly in connection with Stockholm being Capital of Europe that year (stated by the space). Wikipedia names the artist and social worker Gregor Wroblewski as founder.
- Directors after him: Rodrigo Mallea Lira, Ylva Ogland and Jelena Rundqvist, William Easton, Maria Lind, and Cecilia Widenheim (Wikipedia).
- Programmes: exhibitions including "Tensta Museum" (2013 to 2014), "Red Love" (2018) and "Hurting and Healing" (2022); The Classroom, a learning space; an allotment garden; and partnerships with Tensta library, Konstfack and KTH (stated by the space).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Friday 11:00 to 17:00, Saturday and Sunday 12:00 to 17:00. Admission is free. There is a metro elevator, a walkway from Tensta C and a permanent wheelchair ramp, and the space says all rooms are accessible (tenstakonsthall.se).

FUNDING
Tensta konsthall: mainly public: the City of Stockholm, the Swedish Arts Council and Region Stockholm
- It has run as a foundation, Stiftelsen Tensta konsthall, since 2000. Its main funders are the City of Stockholm, the Swedish Arts Council (Kulturrådet) and Region Stockholm (stated by the space). The board is chaired by Karin Lekberg (tenstakonsthall.se).
- Wikipedia says public sources, mainly the City of Stockholm and the Swedish Arts Council, provided roughly 50 to 60 percent of income, and gives a 2012 budget of 8.8 million Swedish kronor. No current budget was found.
- International partners include the Mohammad and Mahera Abu Ghazaleh Foundation in Amman, and it has taken part in EU-funded projects (4Cs, FEINART, Agents of Change) (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of an annual report, a donor list or amounts for each public funder.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling or arms funder, or of a Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One sustained attack, over the 2012 Bukowskis partnership; other coverage read is admiring or mixed.
1. Kunstkritikk: Frans Josef Petersson, "Critical Collapse at Tensta Konsthall" (2012)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/critical-collapse-at-tensta-konsthall/
- Reports a debate at the konsthall on the Bukowskis collaboration and argues that Lind entered it "without uttering a critical word about the company's role in the art market".
- Says the konsthall's public funding rose after Lind became director, and that the artists were missing from the debate.
- Calls projects like "Abstract Possible" "a deeply defensive position" that works in favour of the system it criticises.
2. e-flux Criticism: Judith Schwarzbart, "Abstract Possible: The Stockholm Synergies" (2012)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/233489/abstract-possible-the-stockholm-synergies
- Largely favourable. Locates the controversy in "a publicly funded art gallery working with a high-end, market-driven agent", and in the debate over "private-public match funding".
- Notes the show involved a company affiliated with Lundin Petroleum; says the exhibition "does not take on a moral stance" but ignites a debate about how art is funded.
- Main reservation: the works' conceptual subtexts "only slowly (if at all) appear in the show".
3. e-flux Criticism: James Voorhies, "Tensta konsthall" (2019)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/250362/tensta-konsthall
- Not criticism: an admiring report on the konsthall's community model and Lind's "generous edge". Does not discuss funding.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2012: "Abstract Possible: The Stockholm Synergies" (12 January to 22 April 2012, curated by Maria Lind) put its economic strand in the auction house Bukowskis, which Kunstkritikk says the Lundin family owns. Works sold there at fixed prices (e-flux, Judith Schwarzbart, 3 April 2012).
- Kunstkritikk (Frans Josef Petersson, 28 February 2012) reports a debate at the konsthall in which Lundin Petroleum and the Lundin family were named. Artist Andreas Gedin said Lundin Petroleum had faced criticism from Amnesty and the UN. The article does not mention Sudan. Lind said "The project is not about taking a position" and, pressed, that "if the charges leveled against Lundin are true they are serious, and they should be condemned".
- Unverified: that Lind's fee and the sales proceeds were withheld from the public, from Petersson in Kunstkritikk, a critical commentary. The pieces read give no amounts.
- Connection: Bukowskis was a show partner and sales venue, not a listed funder of the konsthall. No reporting found of any Lundin or Bukowskis link to the konsthall after 2012.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the konsthall is public: the City of Stockholm, the Swedish Arts Council and Region Stockholm, with no flagged industry funder found today. The weak point is the 2012 Bukowskis partnership, which Kunstkritikk criticised because of the Lundin family's ownership of the auction house; nothing later was found. Worth watching is whether current budgets and partners are published, since the figures found are from 2012.
