LATVIAN NATIONAL MUSEUM OF ART: Riga, Latvia
ID: latvian-national-museum-of-art
Website: https://lnmm.gov.lv/en/ (checked 2026-10-24; the older lnmm.lv address redirects here)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum with six sites in Riga and more than 100,000 works. The main building is at Jaņa Rozentāla laukums 1; the other sites are Art Museum Riga Bourse, the Museum of Decorative Arts and Design, the Romans Suta and Aleksandra Beļcova Museum, Art Museum Arsenāls, and a storage facility with the Forest of Sculptures (Wikipedia; the museum's site).
- The collection traces back to the Riga City Art Gallery, opened in 1869. Arsenāls merged in 2000, and the name Latvian National Museum of Art was adopted in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- Building: the main building was built in 1903 to 1905 by the Baltic German architect Wilhelm Neumann, the museum's first director, and was the first purpose-built museum building in the Baltic states. A reconstruction approved in 2010 restored it and added an underground block; it reopened in May 2016 (Wikipedia).
- On site: permanent exhibitions of Latvian art from the late 18th century, temporary exhibitions and a sculpture storage forest.
- Director Māra Lāce announced in January 2026 that she would leave on 13 April; the Ministry of Culture opened a competition for her post (LSM).
- Visiting: closed Monday; Tuesday to Thursday 10:00 to 18:00, Friday 10:00 to 20:00, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 17:00. Listed as suitable for people with reduced mobility. The pages read give no ticket prices (muzeji.lv, a catalogue of Latvian museums).

FUNDING
Latvian National Museum of Art: mainly public: the Latvian state through the Ministry of Culture, with sponsors for single shows
- It is a state museum accredited by the Ministry of Culture (muzeji.lv); the Ministry runs the competition for its director (LSM).
- In 2021 the director said the museum was state-funded and had lost 630,000 euros of revenue to closure (LSM, via the New European Museum Observatory).
- The museum's page lists the Ministry of Culture, the State Culture Capital Foundation, Rietumu Banka, ALFOR and the French Institute in Latvia as supporters. No amounts or years.
- The 2025 show "Light from Italy. From Fattori to Morandi" was supported by the Italian Embassy in Riga, Riga City Council, Rietumu Banka and the Ministry of Culture (Italian Embassy, 3 June 2026).
- Rietumu Banka is also linked to the Riga Bourse site through the 2023 exhibition "Reflections of the Era" (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of the museum's budget on the pages read, or of the size of any sponsor's payment.

Red flags: Rietumu Banka fines
- On 15 June 2021 Latvia's financial regulator fined Rietumu Banka 5.85 million euros for anti-money-laundering failures found in 2019 and 2020 inspections. The bank called the shortcomings "minor, technical and formal". It had been fined 1.5 million euros in 2017 for similar problems (LSM, 17 June 2021).
- A Paris court fined the bank 80 million euros on 6 July 2017 in a tax-evasion case brought by France Offshore; the Paris Appeals Court cut the potential fine by 75 percent in 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Rietumu Banka is a current supporter of the museum (2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Finnish outlet Ilta-Sanomat reported in 2026 that Rietumu Banka was used to receive customer payments for Teboil, a Finnish fuel company owned by the Russian oil company Lukoil, after sanctions disrupted Teboil's payments in Finland (as cited on Wikipedia).
- Connection: Rietumu Banka supports the museum and its Riga Bourse site.

BOTTOM LINE
The base money is the Latvian state's. The weak point is Rietumu Banka, a named sponsor with a 2021 anti-money-laundering fine and a 2017 French tax-evasion fine. The museum posts no sponsor amounts on the pages read. Watch for who funds exhibitions under the new director.
