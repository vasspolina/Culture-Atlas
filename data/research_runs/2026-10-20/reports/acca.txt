OVERVIEW
- Australian Centre for Contemporary Art, a non-collecting Kunsthalle-model art centre at 111 Sturt Street, Southbank, in the Melbourne Arts Precinct. Founded in 1983, it moved in 2002 into a rust-steel building by Wood Marsh that shares a courtyard with the Malthouse Theatre and Chunky Move (Wikipedia).
- Admission is "always free" (2025 annual report). In 2025 it ran 115 public programmes on site and its education programme reached 13,273 students, teachers and families.
- Artistic Director and CEO Myles Russell-Cook, formerly of the NGV, was announced on 1 October 2024 to succeed Max Delany (ArtsHub). Executive Director is Laura De Neefe; Board Chair Dr Terry Wu.

FUNDING
ACCA: mainly public core funding plus private donors
- Creative Australia (federal) and Creative Victoria (state) pay core operating costs. Creative Australia also funded projects by Tina Stefanou and Alicia Frankovich. The City of Melbourne funds public programmes, access and digital work. The Victorian Department of Education funds free school programmes. The partners page also lists the federal Visual Arts and Crafts Strategy (2025 annual report and partners page, stated by the space).
- Trusts: The Ian Potter Foundation (Digital Wing) and the Robert Salzer Foundation (Feather Star).
- Individual donors of $100K+: Lisa Fox; Vivien & Graham Knowles; The Macfarlane Fund; Bruce Parncutt AO & Lisa Bowman; Drs Theresia & Kevin Spencer; J. Andrew Cook & Prof. Wendy Brown. $50K–99K: Dr Terry Wu & Lucinda Tee-Wu, Patagorang Foundation, Chris & Cheryl Thomas. Several of these donors (Wu, Spencer, Fox, Bowman) sit on the board.
- Corporate partners: Dulux, King & Wood Mallesons, Wood Marsh, Jackson Clements Burrows, Fish Nankivell, Supernormal, Hannah St Hotel and others. Media partners include The Guardian, Broadsheet and 3RRR.
- No financial figures were found in the 2025 report text retrieved.

BOTTOM LINE
A free, publicly core-funded art centre topped up by a group of private donors, several of whom are board members. No flagged funder or protest was found. Worth watching: the overlap between major donors and the board.
