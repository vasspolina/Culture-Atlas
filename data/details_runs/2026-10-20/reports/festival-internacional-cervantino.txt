FESTIVAL INTERNACIONAL CERVANTINO: Guanajuato, Mexico
ID: festival-internacional-cervantino
Website: https://festivalcervantino.gob.mx/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual performing-arts festival held each October in theatres, squares, churches and museums of the city of Guanajuato (Wikipedia).
- Grew out of the Cervantes entremeses that University of Guanajuato professor Enrique Ruelas Espinosa began staging in 1953; the federal government created the festival in 1972 at the interest of President Luis Echeverría Álvarez (Wikipedia).
- Named for Miguel de Cervantes, through Ruelas's entremeses and his Coloquio Cervantino of 1972 (Wikipedia).
- Venues include the Teatro Juárez, Teatro Principal, Teatro Cervantes, Plaza San Roque and the esplanade of the Alhóndiga de Granaditas (Wikipedia).
- The 51st edition in 2023 ran 17 days with 140 shows, 2,155 artists and 174,646 attendees, plus a Circuito Cervantino in 18 states (FIC press release). The 2025 edition, 10 to 26 October, had 3,458 artists from 31 countries under executive director Romain Greco (Excélsior). France is the 2026 guest country (stated by the festival).
- Visiting: tickets through Ticketmaster in 2026; in 2023, 21,859 tickets were sold at box offices and 20,472 through Boletia (stated by the festival). No reporting found on accessibility.

FUNDING
Festival Internacional Cervantino: mainly public: federal Secretaría de Cultura and the state of Guanajuato, with guest governments and FEMSA-led sponsors
- 2023 (51st edition): total 168,833,958 pesos. Federal Secretaría de Cultura 65,271,152; Guanajuato government 36 million; guest country the United States 20 million; guest state Sonora 22 million; other countries and embassies 10,210,000; sponsors 15,352,806 (FIC press release).
- 2025 (53rd edition): budget of 290 million pesos, announced on 4 August 2025 by Culture Secretary Claudia Curiel (Excélsior). No reporting found of its breakdown by source.
- Sponsors for 2025: Yamaha, MEGA, Mazda, HONOR, and FEMSA with Fundación FEMSA, Colección FEMSA, Coca-Cola FEMSA, OXXO, Tiendas Bara and Caffenio (festival sponsors page).
- FEMSA Foundation, Coca-Cola FEMSA and OXXO were official sponsors in 2021 "for the eighth consecutive time", and the FEMSA Collection mounted two exhibitions for that edition (FEMSA press release). No reporting found of sponsor amounts.
- The 2019 budget fell 20%, from 105 million to 84.1 million pesos, and performances fell from 339 to 155 (La Razón).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press criticism found. Mexican national press has criticised the festival's decline in prestige and the effect of budget cuts; none of it addresses its sponsors.
1. El Universal: editorial staff, "El Cervantino, una fiesta que ha venido a menos" (2017)
URL: https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cultura/el-cervantino-una-fiesta-que-ha-venido-menos/
- Argues the festival "ha ido perdiendo, poco a poco, brillantez", with a scattered programme and few internationally known figures.
- Criticises poor press logistics and the little public presence of director Marcela Diez, and asks organisers for self-criticism.
2. La Razón: Raúl Campos, "Público lamenta Cervantino austero" (2020)
URL: https://www.razon.com.mx/cultura/publico-lamenta-cervantino-austero/
- Visitors and Roberto Serrano Chirino of Canaco-Servytur Guanajuato said the 2019 cuts showed in the quality of artists and the number of shows.
- Director Mariana Aymerich said the festival would keep "los altos estándares que lo han definido".

WIDER CONTEXT
- In April 2017 NGOs and academics in Chiapas protested against the Coca-Cola FEMSA bottling plant at San Cristóbal de las Casas, citing water scarcity and the health effects of soft drinks, and sought to cancel its concession (Business and Human Rights Resource Centre).
- Coca-Cola FEMSA is a named sponsor of the festival; the protest does not name the festival.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival is government money first: federal and Guanajuato budgets plus guest countries and states, with sponsors giving about 9% of the 2023 budget. The weak point is the FEMSA group of sponsors, which includes Coca-Cola FEMSA, the target of a 2017 water protest in Chiapas. Worth watching: how the 2025 budget of 290 million pesos was split.
