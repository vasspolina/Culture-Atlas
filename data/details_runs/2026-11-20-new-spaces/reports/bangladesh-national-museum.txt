BANGLADESH NATIONAL MUSEUM: Dhaka, Bangladesh
ID: bangladesh-national-museum
Website: http://bangladeshmuseum.gov.bd/ (checked 2026-11-20; down)

OVERVIEW
- Bangladesh's national museum on 8.63 acres at Shahbag, Dhaka; officially Bangladesh Jatiya Jadughar.
- It began as the Dacca Museum, formally opened on 7 August 1913 by Lord Carmichael, governor of the Bengal presidency (Banglapedia).
- A Board of Trustees was formed for the Dhaka Museum in 1970 and a National Museum Commission in 1974.
- The Shahbag project was approved in December 1975 and the museum opened there on 17 November 1983; Wikipedia credits the building to Syed Mainul Hossain, Banglapedia to Robert C. Boghe.
- The collection held 91,287 objects in 2017, among them over 50,000 ancient and medieval coins (Wikipedia).
- Branch museums: Ahsan Manzil Museum in Dhaka, Osmani Museum in Sylhet, Zia Smriti Museum in Chittagong and the Shilpacharya Zainul Abedin Sangrahashala in Mymensingh.

FUNDING
Bangladesh National Museum: mainly public: the Government of Bangladesh through the Ministry of Cultural Affairs
- The museum is an autonomous institution under the Ministry of Cultural Affairs; the government appoints its Board of Trustees and its Director General (Banglapedia).
- The state finances it; the museum also earns from entry tickets and from renting its auditoriums.
- The culture sector received Tk 824 crore in the 2025 to 2026 budget, Tk 487 crore for operations and Tk 337 crore for development; the ministry's plans include modernising galleries at the National Museum (UNB, 3 June 2025).
- At its founding the museum relied on nearly 10,000 taka subscribed by Dhaka residents and a 2,000 taka government grant (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Bangladeshi state's, with ticket and auditorium income on top, and no funder is objected to. Worth watching: the gallery modernisation promised in the 2025 to 2026 budget.
