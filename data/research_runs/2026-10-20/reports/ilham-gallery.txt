ILHAM GALLERY – Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia
Website: https://www.ilhamgallery.com (management page loaded 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit public art gallery in Ilham Tower, Kuala Lumpur. It opened around August 2015 (Arts Equator, Feb 2020, says it "turns five in August").
- Gallery director Rahel Joseph says it is "privately funded but operate[s] as a public non-profit arts space" (Arts Equator, 2020).
- The management page names only staff: Rahel Joseph, Hannah Aziz, Linda Valanganny, Syaza Ramli and Eeling Wong.
- Opening hours, admission and accessibility were not checked this run.

FUNDING
ILHAM Gallery: privately funded; no funder list published
- No reporting found of named funders, accounts or a board for Yayasan ILHAM.
- Ilham Tower is controlled by Ilham Baru Sdn Bhd through Ilham Tower Sdn Bhd. Ilham Baru is controlled by Na'imah Abdul Khalid and her sons Mohammed Amin and Muhammed Amir Zainuddin Daim, the widow and sons of former finance minister Daim Zainuddin (Wikipedia).
- No source found that states the gallery's funding comes from the Daim family. The link is through ownership of the building only.

Red flags: Building under anti-corruption seizure and forfeiture proceedings
- On 18 December 2023 the MACC seized Ilham Tower under Section 38(1) of the MACC Act in a probe tied to the Pandora Papers (NST; Wikipedia).
- In June 2025 the MACC re-seized the tower under the AMLATFPUAA 2001 and filed to have it forfeited. It also obtained orders against UK assets valued at about RM700M–758M (Malay Mail/Bernama, 25 June 2025).
- Unverified: the MACC alleges the assets are linked to offences under Section 4(1) of the AMLATFPUAA. No court has ruled on the merits. Daim, who died in November 2024, denied wrongdoing and called the probe politically motivated (Malay Mail, 10 Dec 2025).
- On 4 November 2025 the High Court gave Ilham Tower Sdn Bhd leave to challenge the second seizure and granted an interim stay on enforcing forfeiture (The Edge Malaysia).
- In December 2023 a gallery employee told NST that operations continued normally.

BOTTOM LINE
No reporting found of wrongdoing by the gallery itself, but it has no public funder list. Its home and probable patron family are the subject of MACC seizure and forfeiture proceedings that are still under challenge in court. Worth watching: the outcome of the forfeiture case and what it means for the gallery's premises and funding.
