DALLAS CONTEMPORARY: Dallas, USA
ID: dallas-contemporary-dallas
Website: https://www.dallascontemporary.org/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-collecting contemporary art space, which calls itself a kunsthalle, in a 37,000-square-foot building at 161 Glass Street in the Dallas Design District (stated by the space).
- Founded in 1978 as the Artists Coalition of Texas, an artist-run group; renamed D'Art in 1980 and Dallas Contemporary in 2007 (stated by the space).
- It opened in a Swiss Avenue warehouse in 1981, moved along Swiss Avenue in 2001 with Meadows Foundation money, and bought and renovated the Glass Street building under a $4.5 million campaign led by director Joan Davidow (2001 to 2010) (stated by the space).
- Directors since: Peter Doroshenko (2010 to 2022), Carolina Alvarez-Mathies (2022 to 2024), Lucia Simek (2024 to 2025); board presidents Ann McReynolds and Rod Sager now serve as interim directors while a search runs, with John McBride as interim executive director and chief operating officer (stated by the space).
- Visiting: open Thursday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, closed Monday to Wednesday; admission is free through 2026, with free parking (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Dallas Contemporary: private donors and foundations, with City of Dallas support: McReynolds family, Headington Companies, Eugene McDermott Foundation
- Revenue was $1,935,534 and expenses $2,230,521 in 2024, with $1,517,917 in contributions; expenses exceeded revenue in every year from 2021 to 2024, and net assets fell from $3,645,043 in 2020 to $1,711,837 (ProPublica, Form 990).
- The Eugene McDermott Foundation pays for free admission; the City of Dallas Office of Arts and Culture supports it in part (stated by the space).
- Major 2024 supporters: Ann and John McReynolds and Headington Companies; gold and silver levels include Rochelle Gores, the KHR Family Fund, Jill Parker and Rod Sager, the Dallas Art Fair Foundation and the Eugene McDermott Foundation (stated by the space).
- Ann and John McReynolds are presenting sponsors of the 2026 Candela gala, with Highland Park Village as video sponsor (stated by the space).
- Executive director Carolina Alvarez-Mathies was paid $175,030 in 2024 (ProPublica, Form 990).

Red flags: fossil fuels (Headington Companies)
- Headington Companies is a major 2024 supporter, the top donor level listed (stated by the space).
- Commercial Observer called Headington Companies a Dallas-based "private oil and gas behemoth" in 2018.
- Its owner Tim Headington founded Headington Oil in 1978 and sold Bakken oil acreage to XTO Energy for $1.85 billion in 2008; his Headington Resources still lists oil and natural gas among its businesses (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Epstein files: a 2014 loan request
- On 24 February 2014 director Peter Doroshenko wrote to "Jeffrey" asking to borrow Richard Phillips's 1997 painting "Below" from his collection for "Richard Phillips: Negation of the Universe" (11 April to 10 August 2014); the letter is document EFTA02707793 in the US Justice Department's Epstein release (epstein-data.com transcript).
- No reporting found that Epstein lent the work, gave money or held any role at Dallas Contemporary. This is a loan request, not a funding tie.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the institution itself in the pieces that could be read. The best-known controversy concerned an artist's conduct, not the museum; reports on the 2021 staff dismissals (Glasstire, Artforum) could not be opened.
1. Hyperallergic: Benjamin Sutton, "A French Artist Demonstrates How Not to Handle Criticism" (2015)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/a-french-artist-demonstrates-how-not-to-handle-criticism
- On 17 January 2015 performers hired by Loris Gréaud destroyed nearly half the works in his show "The Unplayed Notes Museum" at a members' party.
- Dallas Observer critic Lauren Smart had panned the show for "decorative hollowness"; Gréaud answered with sexist Facebook messages.
- Criticises the artist, not the institution.

BOTTOM LINE
Dallas Contemporary runs on a small group of Dallas donors and foundations, with free entry paid for by the McDermott Foundation. The weak point is Headington Companies, an oil and gas firm, at the top donor level; the Epstein link is a single 2014 loan request, not money. Worth watching: repeated deficits, falling net assets and three changes of director since 2022.
