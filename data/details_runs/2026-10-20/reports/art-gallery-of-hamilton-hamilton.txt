ART GALLERY OF HAMILTON: Hamilton, Canada
ID: art-gallery-of-hamilton-hamilton
Website: https://www.artgalleryofhamilton.com/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery at 123 King Street West in downtown Hamilton, Ontario.
- Incorporated on 31 January 1914 as the Municipal Gallery of Hamilton and opened on 28 June 1914, in a public library branch on Main Street West; it moved to Westdale in 1953 (Wikipedia).
- The collection began with works by painter William Blair Bruce, donated by his spouse on condition that an adequate facility be founded to house them (Wikipedia).
- The present brutalist building by Trevor Garwood-Jones opened in 1977; a C$18 million renovation by KPMB Architects, design partner Bruce Kuwabara, reopened it in May 2005 with gold steel panels and the Irving Zucker Sculpture Garden (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds over 10,000 works; in 2002 Joey and Toby Tanenbaum gave about 200 19th-century European works, valued at more than C$50 million (The Globe and Mail).
- In November 2014 it agreed to return Verspronck's "Portrait of a Lady", bought in 1987 for $58,000, to the heirs of Alma Salomonsohn, whose possessions were stolen in 1940 (The CJN).
- Open Thursday 11:00 to 21:00, Friday 11:00 to 18:00, weekends 11:00 to 17:00, Monday to Wednesday for booked tours only; Free Thursdays and free admission to Level 2 are sponsored; the Summers Lane entrance is reached only through the parking garage during city construction.

FUNDING
Art Gallery of Hamilton: mixed: three levels of government, events, donors and bank sponsors
- 2024 revenue was C$7,842,740 against expenses of C$8,101,727; grants made up 41% of revenue (28% operating, 13% applied), events 21%, fundraising 16% (2024 annual report).
- Operating support comes from the Canada Council for the Arts, the Ontario Arts Council and the City of Hamilton (2024 annual report).
- The incite Foundation for the Arts sponsored the 2024 "Directors Collect: 110 Years" exhibition, Free Thursdays (with RBC), education and library pass programmes; Carl and Kate Turkstra founded it in 2011, and their wealth came after Carl returned to Hamilton to run the family firm Turkstra Lumber (annual report, Hamilton City Magazine).
- Banks: RBC presented the gala and supported the artist residency, BMO the art+music+food festival, TD Bank Group the Artful Moments programme (2024 annual report).
- Other 2024 supporters: Orlick Industries (free Level 2 admission), Garda World (exhibitions and programmes), and Effort Trust, Fox Harb'r Resort and LiUNA (gala platinum sponsors); Peller Estates and CHCH are on the donor list (2024 annual report).
- In 2015 the gallery sold Munkácsy's "Christ Before Pilate", part of the Tanenbaum gift, to the Government of Hungary for US$5.7 million (AGH press release).
- The 2024 board is chaired by Gary Graham and includes two Hamilton city councillors; Shelley Falconer is president and CEO.

Red flags: TD Bank's 2024 money-laundering plea
- Sponsorship: Artful Moments, the gallery's programme for people living with dementia, was "presented by the TD Bank Group, the Government of Canada and the Province of Ontario" in 2024 (2024 annual report).
- Plea: on 10 October 2024 TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering, in a case announced by the US Department of Justice (NBC News, ABA Journal).
- Amount: penalties came to about US$3 billion, including US$1.8 billion to the Justice Department and US$1.3 billion to FinCEN, the US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (NBC News).
- Three money-laundering networks moved more than US$670 million through TD Bank accounts (NBC News, ABA Journal).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public grants, events, a local arts foundation founded by a lumber-company family, and the big Canadian banks. No sponsor from a flagged industry was found. Weak points: TD Bank Group, presenter of the Artful Moments programme, pleaded guilty to money laundering in October 2024. The annual report gives only a percentage summary of revenue, and the 2024 accounts ran a deficit of about C$259,000.
