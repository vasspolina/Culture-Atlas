MUSEO LARCO: Lima, Peru
ID: museo-larco-lima
Website: https://www.museolarco.org

OVERVIEW
- Privately owned museum of pre-Columbian art in a former mansion in Pueblo Libre, Lima, at Av. Simón Bolívar 1515 (Wikipedia, Hispania Nostra directory).
- Rafael Larco Hoyle founded it on 28 July 1926 as the Museo Chiclín near Trujillo, building on the collection of his father, Rafael Larco Herrera. It moved to Lima in the 1950s and became the Museo Arqueológico Rafael Larco Herrera (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Its formal name, Museo Arqueológico Rafael Larco Herrera, honours the founder's father; after Larco Hoyle died in 1966 his daughter Isabel Larco took over the general management (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Housed in an 18th-century mansion known as hacienda Cueva, bought in the early 1950s and altered to restore the style of a grand house (Spanish Wikipedia). The museum's site describes the building as an 18th-century viceroyalty mansion surrounded by gardens.
- On site: about 45,000 pre-Columbian objects, a permanent exhibition, Visible Storage, an Erotic Gallery and gardens (stated by the museum).
- Visiting: open every day, including holidays, 9:00 to 19:00. Admission is S/ 50 general, S/ 35 for people over 60, S/ 25 for students and minors aged 9 to 17, S/ 1 for children aged 3 to 8, free for children up to 2; online tickets bought in advance get a discount (stated by the museum). Andina reported about 100,000 visits a year in November 2017.

FUNDING
Museo Larco: private museum
- Andrés Álvarez Calderón Larco is president and chief executive and Ulla Holmquist Pachas is director (stated by the museum). Andina (7 November 2017) quoted him as director.
- Spanish Wikipedia describes the museum as a private institution that was given the legal form of a foundation when it moved to Lima.
- The collection began with the holdings of the founder's father, Rafael Larco Herrera, which Larco Hoyle expanded by buying large lots from other collectors (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a privately owned institution built on the collection that Rafael Larco Hoyle expanded from his father's holdings. Andrés Álvarez Calderón Larco is president and chief executive, and ticket admission is charged.
