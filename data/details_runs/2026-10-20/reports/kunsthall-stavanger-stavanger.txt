KUNSTHALL STAVANGER: Stavanger, Norway
ID: kunsthall-stavanger-stavanger
Website: https://www.kunsthallstavanger.no/en

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art kunsthall at Madlaveien 33 in the Eiganes district of Stavanger, a 10 to 15 minute walk from the city centre and the main bus and train station.
- Founded in 1865 as Stavanger kunstforening (Stavanger Art Association) by a group of citizens, with 62 original members and the consul and amateur artist Jens Zetlitz Kielland as first chairman. In 2012 the association's board and general assembly decided to become a kunsthall with an international programme, and it reopened as Kunsthall Stavanger in 2013 with a show by Lina Viste Grønli.
- Building: opened by King Haakon VII in 1925 as part of Stavanger's 800th anniversary and the city's first exhibition space for visual art; designed by the Stavanger architect Erik Erga and built with private funds from individuals and companies.
- The association's art collection, converted into the foundation Stavanger Faste Galleri in 1965, moved to the new Rogaland Art Museum (now Stavanger Art Museum).
- Programme: exhibitions, commissions, public events, an education programme for children and young people, an art club, digital projects and a garden with a permanent ramp. Director: Joseph Constable.
- Partners named on its site include MoMA PS1, the New Museum and Rhizome, Performa, Goldsmiths CCA, Tate St Ives, Spike Island, Kunsthall Oslo, Kunstnernes Hus and MUNCH.
- Visiting: Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 16:00; Thursday 11:00 to 19:00 with free entrance. Adults NOK 70, pensioners NOK 45; students, children under 18 and members enter free. Wheelchair access through the main entrance.

FUNDING
Kunsthall Stavanger: public grants and foundation support: Norwegian state, Stavanger municipality and Norwegian foundations
- The 2024 annual report thanks the Ministry of Culture (Kulturdepartementet), Arts Council Norway (Kulturrådet), Fritt Ord, Bergesenstiftelsen and Norske Kunstforeninger for generous support in 2024. Its closing list of 2024 supporters names the Ministry of Culture, Arts Council Norway, Stavanger kommune, Fritt Ord, Norske kunstforeninger and the Norwegian Consulate in New York.
- The About page lists these supporters: Kultur- og likestillingsdepartementet, Stavanger kommune, Kulturdirektoratet, Fritt Ord, Bergesenstiftelsen, Norske Kunstforeninger, Den Kulturelle Skolesekken, Sparebankstiftelsen SR-bank, Sparebanken Vest, Stiftelsen Sophies Minde, Inge Steenslands Stiftelse, Klosters Legat, Kulturrom, Rogaland fylkeskommune and OCA (Office for Contemporary Art Norway).
- Building and garden: the 2024 report says the board approved in 2024 a project, carried out in 2025, to restore the 1925 garden and give universal access to the building, backed by Stavanger kommune, Sparebankstiftelsen SR-bank, Sparebanken Vest, Kulturrådet's ARENA scheme and regional development funds from Rogaland fylkeskommune. Kulturrådet's Rom for Kunst scheme paid for drainage (2018 to 2019) and a ventilation system (finished December 2020).
- The kunsthall is a membership association; the 2024 report names running the building on an association's finances as the largest threat to its continued operation. Stavanger kommune has a representative on the board, and the chair for 2024 to 2025 is Martha Norheim Lyse.
- Members and volunteers: memberships give free admission and discounts, and volunteers meet weekly to help in the building.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Kunsthall Stavanger is Norwegian public funding (the Ministry of Culture, Arts Council Norway, Stavanger kommune, Rogaland county) together with Norwegian foundations, two savings-bank sources (Sparebankstiftelsen SR-bank and Sparebanken Vest) and members. The 2024 annual report and a published board list document it.
