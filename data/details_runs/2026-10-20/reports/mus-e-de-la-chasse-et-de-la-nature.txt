MUSÉE DE LA CHASSE ET DE LA NATURE: Paris, France
ID: mus-e-de-la-chasse-et-de-la-nature
Website: https://www.chassenature.org (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of the relationship between people and animals, in the Marais in the 3rd arrondissement of Paris, near Rambuteau Métro station (Wikipedia).
- Founded by François and Jacqueline Sommer. The Fondation François Sommer, which they created, dates from 1964 (French Wikipedia); André Malraux inaugurated the museum on 21 February 1967 (museum website).
- Named for its subject, hunting and nature; the foundation carries the founder's name.
- Building: the hôtel de Guénégaud, a 17th-century monument historique by François Mansart. The museum extended in 2007 into the neighbouring 18th-century hôtel de Mongelas and closed for two years of works before reopening on 3 July 2021 with five more rooms (Fondation François Sommer; French Wikipedia). The museum website says six new rooms and about 250 m2 of added space.
- What is there: more than 5,000 works assembled by the Sommers, classed by the Ministry of Culture as "trésors nationaux" (Fondation François Sommer); two or three temporary exhibitions a year; a bookshop and a café on the first floor (museum website). It holds the "Musée de France" label (since 2003, per French Wikipedia).
- Visiting: opening days, prices and accessibility were not found on the pages read.

FUNDING
Musée de la Chasse et de la Nature: privately run: the Fondation François Sommer, created by the family that owned a felt-making firm
- Run by the Fondation François Sommer, recognised as serving the public interest (museum website). It supports the museum's acquisitions, loans, and preventive conservation and restoration (museum website).
- François Sommer (1904 to 1973) took over the family felt-making firm in Mouzon in 1953; in 1965 Sommer SA had 1,916 employees and 237.5 million francs of revenue (French Wikipedia). French Wikipedia says he founded the Maison de la Chasse et de la Nature in 1964.
- André Malraux, then minister for cultural affairs, helped entrust the two hôtels to the foundation, which became responsible for their restoration and upkeep (French Wikipedia).
- Claude d'Anthenaise, the museum's director, said the foundation once ran like a private hunting club, that a president who was a former banker restored its finances, and that attendance rose from about 7,000 family visitors a year to over 120,000 (Académie des Beaux-Arts interview, 2019).
- No reporting found of annual accounts, a donor list, a board or a corporate sponsor for the museum or the foundation, and no source read states how the foundation's income is built up. The address fondationsommer.org does not resolve (checked 2026-10-25); the foundation's pages are at fondationfrancoissommer.org.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. What exists is academic writing and the museum's own account of early suspicion about its direction.
1. Routledge: Sarah Wade, chapter on Art Orienté Objet at the museum in "Revisiting the Past in Museums and at Historic Sites" (2021)
URL: https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/revolting-hunting-trophies-art-orient%C3%A9-objet-at-the-mus%C3%A9e-de-la-c/
- Notes that many visitors find hunting trophies distasteful given concern for threatened wildlife, and that the museum renovated in 2007 and commissioned contemporary art in response.
- Argues the 2013 to 2014 Art Orienté Objet show prompted debate about hunting. Academic writing, not art press; only the abstract was read.
2. Académie des Beaux-Arts: interview of Claude d'Anthenaise by Nadine Eghels (2019; the page shows no date and appears in the spring 2019 issue of the Académie's Lettre)
URL: https://www.academiedesbeauxarts.fr/le-musee-de-la-chasse-et-de-la-nature-la-frontiere-entre-lhomme-et-lanimal
- The museum's own director says hunters distrusted the contemporary art and suspected him of being an anti-hunting "mole," and that artists first saw the museum as propaganda for hunting.
- Not criticism by an outsider; the page reports no anti-hunting campaign.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run by the Fondation François Sommer, created by the Sommers, owners of the Mouzon felt-making firm, and no flagged funder was found. The weak points are that no source read gives the foundation's accounts, donors, board or how it is financed today. The museum shows hunting heritage, and the sources record unease from both hunters and artists but no organised campaign.
