BUSAN BIENNALE: Busan, South Korea
ID: busan-biennale-busan
Website: http://www.busanbiennale.org/BBOCkr (checked 2026-11-20; the server returned intermittent Bad Gateway errors); edition site https://busanbiennale2026.com

OVERVIEW
- International contemporary art biennale held in Busan every two years (Busan Metropolitan City).
- Busan Metropolitan City and Herald Kyungje date it to the Busan Youth Biennale of 1981 and say it took its present name after the organizing committee was established in 2000 (Busan Metropolitan City, 2 September 2026; Herald Kyungje, 24 August 2026).
- It is run by the Busan Biennale Organizing Committee, an incorporated association founded on 31 March 2000 under the supervision of Busan City Hall, with 23 directors and 18 employees in 2025 (2025 public-interest filing).
- In odd years the committee stages the Sea Art Festival; the 2025 edition at Dadaepo cost 1,116,869,638 won to run (2025 filing).
- The 2026 edition, "Dissident Chorus", runs from 29 August to 1 November 2026 with 46 artists and collectives from 22 countries, co-directed by Evelyn Simons and Amal Khalaf, who were chosen through an open call that drew over 70 teams (Busan Metropolitan City).
- 2026 venues: Busan Museum of Contemporary Art on Eulsukdo, Space OneZ, a century-old marine warehouse on Yeongdo, and the former Busan Nam High School (e-flux, Busan Metropolitan City).
- F1963, the former KISWIRE factory in Suyeong, has served as an exhibition hall (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: daily except Mondays, 10:00 to 18:00. The Busan Museum of Contemporary Art charges 16,000 won for adults, 8,000 won for teenagers, soldiers and police and 5,000 won for children; the other two venues are free and a free shuttle links all three (Busan Metropolitan City).

FUNDING
Busan Biennale: public body: Busan Metropolitan City and its Organizing Committee, funded mostly by grants
- Busan Metropolitan City and the Organizing Committee co-host the biennale (Busan Metropolitan City; Herald Kyungje, 24 August 2026). The city endowed the committee at its founding with 672,000,000 won in cash (2025 filing).
- 2025 accounts: income of 1,874,750,866 won, of which 1,700,000,000 won came as grants, 20,500,000 won as donations, 140,530,856 won as other programme income and 13,720,010 won as interest. Spending reached 1,993,927,609 won, a deficit of 119,176,743 won (2025 filing).
- 2024, a biennale year: income of 3,486,543,687 won, including 2,845,819,622 won in grants and 151,200,000 won in donations (prior-year column of the 2025 filing).
- 2025 donors: the Embassy of Switzerland in Korea gave 5,500,000 won, Busan Bank 5,000,000 won and the company 정현전기물류 (in translation, Jeonghyeon Electric Logistics) 10,000,000 won (2025 filing). The donations paid for electrical work, outdoor advertising and photography for the 2025 Sea Art Festival.
- Single works: Jota Mombaça's 2026 installation was co-commissioned by the Organizing Committee and Martins&Montero (e-flux Criticism caption).

Red flags: 2026 damage to Sungsil Ryu's commissioned work
- On 1 September 2026 Sungsil Ryu's (류성실) installation 만 가지 꿈 (in translation, "Ten Thousand Dreams") at the Busan Museum of Contemporary Art was damaged while extra supports were being fitted under an industrial lift in the gallery (Organizing Committee apology, 5 September 2026).
- The committee apologised for failing to inform the artist promptly, for closing off the work with barriers without consulting the artist first and for poor notice to visitors, which it said let the closure be read as a cover-up. It called the damage the organiser's fault.
- KNN reported on 6 September 2026 that the biennale apologised late after responding half-heartedly to the damage.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One review of the 2026 edition calls it uneven; it criticises the curation, not the funding or governance.
1. e-flux Criticism: Adeline Chia, "Busan Biennale 2026, 'Dissident Chorus'" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782475/busan-biennale-2026-dissident-chorus
- Calls it "an uneven biennial, more conceptually focused and powerful in its off-sites than in the main venue".
- Writes that the curation at the Busan Museum of Contemporary Art "feels more staid and generic", citing familiar curatorial tropes.
- Says its open celebration of underdogs and dissent makes it "an accidental foil to the political quarrels plaguing the concurrent Gwangju Biennale".
- Criticises the curation, not the institution or its funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The biennale is a city creation living on public grants, and its small private gifts come from local firms and a foreign embassy, none of them flagged. The weak point on record is the organiser's handling of the damage to a commissioned work.
