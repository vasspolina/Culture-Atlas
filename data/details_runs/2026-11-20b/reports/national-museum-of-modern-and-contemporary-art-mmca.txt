NATIONAL MUSEUM OF MODERN AND CONTEMPORARY ART (MMCA, 국립현대미술관): Seoul, South Korea
ID: national-museum-of-modern-and-contemporary-art-mmca
Website: https://www.mmca.go.kr (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- South Korea's national museum of modern and contemporary art, with four sites: Gwacheon, Deoksugung, Seoul (next to Gyeongbokgung) and Cheongju, a storage and conservation centre (Wikipedia).
- Opened in Gyeongbokgung on 20 October 1969, moved to Deoksugung in 1973 and to Seoul Grand Park in Gwacheon in 1986; the Deoksugung branch opened in 1998, the Seoul branch on 13 November 2013 on the former Defense Security Command site, and Cheongju in December 2018 (Wikipedia).
- A state museum under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism. Culture Minister Chae Hwi-young appointed Ki Hey-kyung (기혜경), a Hongik University professor and former director of the Busan Museum of Art, as director on 13 November 2026, for a term to November 2029 (Korea JoongAng Daily).
- Paid exhibitions drew a record 3.46 million visitors in 2025 (The Korea Herald). "Damien Hirst: Nothing Is True But Everything Is Possible" drew 541,889 visitors in 2026, and Hirst offered the museum two works afterwards (The Korea Times).

FUNDING
National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art: state museum funded by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, with corporate series sponsors
- State budget: 84.8 billion won in 2026, up from 69.1 billion won in 2025, including 5 billion won to tour shows to regional museums; the acquisition budget fell from 4.7 billion won to 4 billion won (The Korea Herald, 6 January 2026).
- Hyundai Motor: a ten-year partnership from 2013 (Hyundai ArtLab) paid for the MMCA Hyundai Motor Series, one commissioned solo show a year from 2014 (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2014). The tenth and last show, Jung Yeondoo's "One Hundred Years of Travels", ran to 25 February 2024 (Hyundai Motor Group).
- LG Electronics: title sponsor of the MMCA x LG OLED Series, a multi-year partnership with one new work a year in Seoul Box; the first, by TZUSOO, opened in August 2025 on 88 LG OLED screens (LG, 31 July 2025).
- Lee Kun-hee estate: in 2021 the family of the late Samsung chairman gave the museum works later catalogued as 1,494 pieces (The Korea Times).

Red flags: 2023 ministry audit of acquisitions and workplace bullying
- An audit by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, reported in April 2023, listed 16 illegal or inappropriate practices under director Youn Bum-mo, including questionable acquisitions at auction and failure to deal with harassment and bullying; Youn resigned in April 2023, before his term ended in 2025 (ArtReview).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concerns appointments, audits and political pressure on the state museum, not its sponsors.
1. ArtReview: "Director of South Korea's MMCA museums steps down amid alleged political pressure" (2023)
URL: https://artreview.com/director-of-south-koreas-mmca-museums-steps-down-amid-alleged-political-pressure
- Korean media speculated that Youn left under political pressure after the March 2022 election; the article says the cause is unclear.
- Critics alleged nepotism in his 2022 reappointment because of his ties to the Moon Jae-in administration.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Korean artists protest appointment of former Barcelona museum director due to censorship concerns" (2015)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/korean-artists-protest-appointment-of-former-barcelona-museum-director-due-to-censorship-concerns
- Reports petitions by petition4art against Bartomeu Marí, who cancelled an exhibition at Macba in 2015.
- Marí replied that he opposes "all sorts of censorship".
3. Korea JoongAng Daily: Moon So-young, "MMCA chief dismissed in recruitment controversy" (2014)
URL: https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/mmca-chief-dismissed-in-recruitment-controversy/10799417
- The ministry dismissed director Chung Hyung-min after the Board of Audit and Inspection found evidence she intervened in an October 2013 recruitment, and asked prosecutors to investigate her for abuse of authority.

WIDER CONTEXT
- November and December 2015: petition4art's first petition against Marí's candidacy drew more than 830 signatures, including Park Chan-kyong and Haegue Yang; a second, launched on 3 December after his appointment, drew 450 and asked the ministry not to censor (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: six politically "awkward" works were dropped from the 2013 opening show "Zeitgeist Korea" less than a week before it opened, and the presidential office had a hand in the selection, from The Korea Times as relayed by the blog London Korean Links; the blog also reports protests outside the museum demanding the director's resignation.
- July 2022: Reuters reported children as young as 12 at SMART, then a Hyundai subsidiary in Alabama; Hyundai said in February 2023 it would divest its stake (Wikipedia). On 30 May 2024 the US Department of Labor sued Hyundai's Alabama plant, SMART and a staffing firm over a 13-year-old worker; Hyundai contests it (CBS News).
- Hyundai Motor paid for the museum's series from 2014 to February 2024.

BOTTOM LINE
Mainly public money, with Hyundai Motor and now LG Electronics paying for single series. The weak points are its exposure to ministry politics in appointments and the 2023 audit findings on how it spent acquisition money.
