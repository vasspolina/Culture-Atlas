HET HEM: Zaandam, Netherlands
ID: het-hem-zaandam
Website: https://hethem.nl

OVERVIEW
- Venue in a former bullet factory ("kogelfabriek") on the Hembrugterrein in Zaandam, with a programme focused on art, music and nightlife; it presents itself as a "house for contemporary culture" (Raad voor Cultuur advice, 2024).
- Founded by Amerborgh, which owns the building (hethem.nl blog, 17 June 2024). The institution is Stichting Het HEM, which the Raad voor Cultuur says has been based in the former bullet factory since its founding in 2019 (Raad voor Cultuur advice, 2024).
- Amerborgh is described in 2018 reporting as an investment company; NH Nieuws, relaying NRC, reported that the architecture office of Rem Koolhaas had been hired for the renovation, and that a restaurant, a cinema and a hotel were planned alongside the cultural centre (NH Nieuws, 1 September 2018).
- The programme ran in "Chapters", group exhibitions built with a different guest each time, and included an online platform, The Couch, set up in 2023, and a youth programme, Homebase (Raad voor Cultuur advice, 2024).
- In June 2024 Amerborgh decided to end the activities of Het HEM in Zaandam; activities were due to stop by 1 August 2024, with talks under way with the city of Amsterdam about a new location. The building is closed for renovation (hethem.nl blog, 17 June 2024).

FUNDING
Het HEM: privately funded: its owner and founder Amerborgh International
- Amerborgh founded Het HEM and owns its building (hethem.nl blog, 17 June 2024). The Raad voor Cultuur wrote that Het HEM is part of the company Amerborgh International, which covers shortfalls in income (translation of "garant staat bij tegenvallende inkomsten") (Raad voor Cultuur advice, 2024).
- Het HEM asked for a subsidy of €600,000 in the national Cultural Basic Infrastructure round for 2025 to 2028. The Raad voor Cultuur advised €0, writing that it lacked confidence in the artistic quality of the programme, saw no strong artistic vision or recognisable signature in the visual arts, and judged the international network and impact too small. The Council also praised Het HEM as a lively meeting place and valued its use of its rooms for initiatives under pressure during the pandemic, such as the Sandberg Institute degree show (Raad voor Cultuur advice, 2024).
- The site's support page offers donations, a "Steunpilaar" (pillar supporter) scheme and a partnership link (hethem.nl).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The critique comes from the Raad voor Cultuur (Council for Culture), the advisory council that assessed Het HEM's subsidy application under the Dutch Cultural Basic Infrastructure scheme, and not from an art outlet.
1. Raad voor Cultuur: advice on Stichting Het HEM, Cultural Basic Infrastructure 2025 to 2028 (2024)
URL: https://www.raadvoorcultuur.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2024/07/03/presentatie-instellingen/Het+HEM+advies+BIS+2025-2028.pdf
- The programme lacks a strong artistic vision and a recognisable signature in the visual arts, and the Chapters lack coherence; only Chapter 6IX, with the designer Martine Rose as guest, was worked out in detail.
- The council called the plan to rely on an uncertain move to the De School building in Amsterdam-West "problematisch" (problematic, title translated), and noted that Het HEM announced in June 2024, shortly before the advice appeared, that it would stop activities in Zaandam.
- Concludes that the international network and impact are too small for a place in the national infrastructure; the plans stress a younger urban audience and the council found its diversity policy on audience little worked out.

BOTTOM LINE
Het HEM is paid for by one private owner, Amerborgh International, which also covers income shortfalls. Public money was requested (€600,000 for 2025 to 2028) and advised against by the Raad voor Cultuur in 2024, and Het HEM ended its Zaandam activities that year.
