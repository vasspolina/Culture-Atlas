MUSEO DE ARTE MODERNO DE BUENOS AIRES (MUSEO MODERNO): Buenos Aires, Argentina
ID: museo-de-arte-moderno-de-buenos-aires-buenos-aires
Website: https://www.museomoderno.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- City art museum at Av. San Juan 350 in San Telmo, Buenos Aires (museum site).
- Created in 1956 by decree 3527/56 on the initiative of Rafael Squirru, its first director. For its first four years it had no permanent home; in 1960 it opened on the upper floors of the Teatro Municipal General San Martín (museum history page).
- Moved to its definitive home at Av. San Juan 350, a former tobacco warehouse, in 1989 (museum history page). Spanish Wikipedia dates the original building to 1918, built for the Piccardo tobacco company.
- The museum closed temporarily in 2005 to expand; the first stage opened in December 2010 as an 11,000 m² building. Emilio Ambasz donated the architectural work for the expansion (museum history page).
- Victoria Noorthoorn has directed it since August 2013 (museum history page). Collection of nearly 11,000 works (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Open Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday 11:00 to 19:00, Saturday, Sunday and holidays 11:00 to 20:00, closed Tuesday. Admission is listed as $16.000 for foreigners and $5.000 for national residents; free on Wednesdays and the last Sunday of each month, and free for people with disabilities and a companion. The page links an "Accessible Visit" section (museum site).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires: mainly public: City of Buenos Aires, with corporate and private supporters
- The museum is owned by the City of Buenos Aires (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper reported in 2023 that 85 percent of the museum's public budget comes from city coffers, not the federal culture ministry.
- The Asociación Amigos del Museo de Arte Moderno was founded in 1986 and raised funds that helped the move to Av. San Juan 350 (museum history page).
- Mecenazgo: the city programme lets companies that pay gross-revenue tax in Buenos Aires direct part of it to museum projects and deduct up to 80 percent of the contribution; the open project is collection digitisation. The page names no contributors or amounts.
- TotalEnergies: the museum's "Support the Museum" page, checked on 24 October 2026, shows a row of logo links with no captions. Their addresses lead to totalenergies.com, plavicon.com, fundacionandreani.org.ar, catenazapata.com, premioazcuy.com and the Asociación Amigos del Moderno page. The page carries no date and gives no text, year or amount for any supporter (museum site).
- Banco Supervielle gave 21 works in 2022 after lending 83 in December 2021; the bank had earlier helped enlarge the building and create an education room (La Nación, Celina Chatruc, 2022).
- A public-private acquisition committee with invited donors was presented in September 2019 (Arte al Día).
- No reporting found of what TotalEnergies or any other supporter paid, or of published accounts or a board.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- TotalEnergies has a logo link on the museum's "Support the Museum" page as of 24 October 2026 (museum site). The page gives no year or amount. No reporting found of any protest aimed at this sponsorship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. The criticism found concerns budget, donation-based acquisitions and staff dismissals, and comes from newspaper and left-wing outlets.
1. La Nación: Verónica Gómez, "Cuestiones de fondo" (2012, opinion column)
URL: https://www.lanacion.com.ar/cultura/cuestiones-de-fondo-nid1493363/
- Names four issues the conflict made unavoidable: the museum budget, acquisitions that do not enter only by donation, funding for producing shows, and recognition for artists and museum workers.
- Describes the petition of the group Artistas Organizados (AO), with 732 signatures, and its artists putting a donation on hold two weeks before the opening; the author writes as an artist in the show.
- Names the then director Laura Buccellato and criticises the pressure on her as political opportunism. A newspaper column, not art press.
2. La Izquierda Diario: anonymous interview, "Museo Inhumano: despidos y pandemia" (2020)
URL: https://www.laizquierdadiario.com/Museo-Inhumano-despidos-y-pandemia
- Nine women working as educators, gallery attendants and event and production staff say their contracts, promised through December, were ended in June 2020, and that their salaries had gone unpaid since January or February. They call the later contracts for June to August "contratos fantasma".
- Management blamed a budget cut of about 30 percent, which the workers say went to the Ministry of Health.
- Partisan: a left-wing outlet whose site links to PTS party sections.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city institution with sponsor logos and a tax-deduction programme, so the main money is public. The weak points are the TotalEnergies listing with no amount, the thin public financial reporting, and past disputes over donation-led acquisitions and staff dismissals. Worth watching: which companies use the mecenazgo programme and whether the museum publishes who they are.
