DE PONT: Tilburg, Netherlands
ID: de-pont
Website: https://depont.nl (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art in a former wool-spinning mill in Tilburg, North Brabant (Dutch Wikipedia; museum website).
- Founded by the Stichting De Pont, a foundation set up in 1988 with part of the estate of Jan de Pont; the museum opened on 12 September 1992 (Dutch Wikipedia; museum website).
- Named for the lawyer and businessman J.H. (Jan) de Pont, born in Tilburg on 22 February 1915, who died there on 18 September 1987 (museum website).
- The mill belonged to Thomas de Beer, which De Pont helped restart in 1969; the foundation bought the closed property for a symbolic sum and Benthem Crouwel Architekten renovated it. Additions: a project space and auditorium in 2002 and a 1,100 square metre wing in 2016 (Dutch Wikipedia; museum website).
- On site: galleries, with Anish Kapoor's "Sky Mirror" made for the 25th anniversary, and a collection that started with about ten works by three artists (Dutch Wikipedia; Omroep Brabant).
- Hendrik Driessen directed it until 2019, Martijn van Nieuwenhuyzen followed, and Dutch Wikipedia states Maria Schnyder succeeded him in 2026.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 11am to 5pm, Thursday until 8pm, closed Monday; the homepage lists no ticket prices and links an accessibility statement that was not read.

FUNDING
De Pont: self-funded: the bequest of Jan de Pont's estate, with no government subsidy
- The foundation was created in 1988 from De Pont's estate, and the museum states it is financially independent to this day (museum website).
- Director Martijn van Nieuwenhuyzen told Omroep Tilburg on 23 January 2024: "We zijn een particulier museum, dus we ontvangen geen subsidie van de gemeente" (translation: we are a private museum, so we receive no subsidy from the municipality). Visitors numbered nearly 75,000 in 2023.
- Omroep Brabant (13 September 2017) also says it runs without subsidy and started with a large budget but no collection.
- Source of the wealth: De Pont practised as a lawyer in Amsterdam and advised AGAM, the Dutch importer of Mercedes-Benz, becoming acting director in 1956 and taking full control in 1960; he also advised other companies and helped restructure Tilburg textile mills (museum website). The museum's biography does not state how large the estate was.
- No reporting found of any sponsor, donor, grant or corporate partner, nor of the foundation's accounts or board, on the pages opened.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage is positive: Omroep Brabant (22 February 2019) calls De Pont one of Tilburg's success stories and records that some people had asked why a museum opened in Tilburg at all, naming no critic.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind De Pont is clean as far as published sources show: a private bequest from a lawyer and car-import businessman, with no public subsidy and no flagged funder named. The weak points are that the foundation's accounts, board and the estate's size were not found in the pages opened, and that the endowment's investments are not described. Worth watching is whether the museum publishes its accounts as a new director takes over in 2026.
