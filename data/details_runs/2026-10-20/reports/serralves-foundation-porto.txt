SERRALVES FOUNDATION: Porto, Portugal
ID: serralves-foundation-porto
Website: https://www.serralves.pt/en/ (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum, villa and about 18 hectares of park in Porto, run by the Fundação de Serralves (Wikipedia).
- The State bought the estate in 1986; the park opened to the public on 29 May 1987; the Foundation was created in 1989 as a partnership between the State and about 51 public and private bodies (Wikipedia).
- The villa, Casa de Serralves, was built between 1925 and 1944 for the second Count of Vizela, to designs by José Marques da Silva (Wikipedia).
- The museum was designed by Álvaro Siza Vieira and opened on 6 June 1999: 13,000 m2 with 14 galleries (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: general ticket EUR 24; park only EUR 15; free for children under 12; free entry on the first Sunday of each month is listed among the residents' discounts (Serralves website).

FUNDING
Serralves Foundation: mixed: State co-financing plus founders from Portuguese business and municipalities
- Founders: Wikipedia counts 181 founders. The Foundation's page of "Patron Founders", Founders who contributed to the Annual Fund, lists names for 2021 to 2024 with no amounts, among them Galp Energia (all four years) and Tabaqueira (2021 and 2022).
- State: the page shows the Portuguese Republic under "Co-financing". The Bloco de Esquerda said in October 2024 that state funding was set at EUR 6.4 million that year (Lusa, via Observador).
- No reporting found of a budget breakdown by funder.

Red flags: Fossil fuels
- Galp Energia is a Patron Founder in each year from 2021 to 2024 (Serralves website). Galp's units include oil and gas exploration and production and crude oil refining (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Tobacco
- Tabaqueira, S.A. is a Patron Founder in 2021 and 2022, and absent from the 2023 and 2024 lists (Serralves website). Wikipedia's page on the Português cigarette brand describes Tabaqueira, its maker, as a subsidiary of Philip Morris International.

Red flags: Protest over the 2018 Mapplethorpe show
- Artistic director João Ribas resigned on 21 September 2018 (Observador); on 24 September an open letter to Foundation president Ana Pinho, signed by more than 150 artists and arts professionals, condemned the removal of 20 works and the age restriction (The Art Newspaper). Protesters gathered outside (ArtReview).
- State bodies: on 24 September 2018 the state inspectorate IGAC told Lusa that art exhibitions need no age classification (CM Jornal). On 25 September Porto mayor Rui Moreira asked for an urgent meeting of its Founders' Council (CM Jornal). On 16 October the parliamentary culture committee heard Ribas and the board, who gave opposing accounts (Observador).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism concentrates on the 2018 Mapplethorpe exhibition, when the board and its artistic director disagreed in public. No piece found examines the funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris and José da Silva, "Wolfgang Tillmans and Tania Bruguera pledge support for censored Serralves museum director" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/09/24/wolfgang-tillmans-and-tania-bruguera-pledge-support-for-censored-serralves-museum-director
- Ribas resigned after 20 works were removed from the Mapplethorpe show and two galleries were limited to visitors aged 18 and over.
- More than 150 artists and arts professionals signed a letter to Foundation president Ana Pinho; it called the use of Portuguese law on "pornographic" material "disingenuous and insulting".
- The Foundation said the 20 works were left out by the curator alone and it was not censorship.
2. ArtReview: news desk, "Serralves Museum removes Mapplethorpe works, artistic director resigns" (2018)
URL: https://artreview.com/news-24-sep-2018-serralves-museum-removes-mapplethorpe-works-artistic-director-resigns
- Reports the removals, the age-restricted rooms and the resignation.
- The museum said it receives almost one million visitors a year and that the public should be alerted.
3. Arte al Día: no author named, "Censorship and controversy at the Serralves Contemporary Art Museum of Porto" (2018)
URL: https://www.artealdia.com/News/CENSORSHIP-AND-CONTROVERSY-AT-THE-SERRALVES-CONTEMPORARY-ART-MUSEUM-OF-PORTO
- Argues the Foundation's action replicates the US culture wars of the 1980s and 1990s.
- Reports that collector Luiz Teixeira de Freitas wrote in solidarity with Ribas and asked for the return of 700 drawings he had lent.
- Reports that the Foundation's leaders deny censorship.
4. Observador (news, not art press): Rita Cipriano and Gonçalo Correia, "João Ribas: demiti-me porque não podia tolerar a censura em Serralves" (2018)
URL: https://observador.pt/2018/10/16/joao-ribas-demiti-me-porque-nao-podia-tolerar-a-censura-em-serralves/
- Ribas told the parliamentary culture committee on 16 October 2018 that two works were removed an hour before the press conference. Board member José Pacheco Pereira said it was false that two works were removed.

BOTTOM LINE
The Foundation is funded by the State and by Portuguese business founders, and the Annual Fund list is public by name though not by amount. The weak points are two named patron founders, Galp Energia (2021 to 2024) and Tabaqueira (2021 and 2022), and the September 2018 dispute over the Mapplethorpe show, where the board and Ribas gave opposing accounts. Watch for amounts per founder.
