KUNSTHALLE BIELEFELD: Bielefeld, Germany
ID: kunsthalle-bielefeld-bielefeld
Website: https://www.kunsthalle-bielefeld.de/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at Artur-Ladebeck-Straße 5 in Bielefeld, North Rhine-Westphalia, with a sculpture park beside the building.
- The collection began in 1950 with a donation from the businessman Rudolf August Oetker. The building opened with a "silent" ceremony on 27 September 1968, accompanied by protests; Joachim Wolfgang von Moltke was the first director (Wikipedia).
- It was planned as the Richard-Kaselowsky-Haus, named for Oetker's stepfather. The city dropped the name in 1998, and the museum is now simply Kunsthalle Bielefeld.
- Designed by the US architect Philip Johnson, his only museum building in Europe: red Main sandstone, three floors above ground and two below, about 1,200 square metres of exhibition space; it is a listed building.
- On site: exhibition floors, a café, an art education space, a film and lecture hall, a public library and the sculpture park.
- Visiting: from October 2026 closed Monday and Tuesday; open Wednesday 11 to 21 and Thursday to Sunday 11 to 18. Adults EUR 10, reduced EUR 6, under 18 free. A general renovation and expansion is planned from mid-2027.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Bielefeld: mixed public and private: three shareholders, the city, a foundation and a savings bank, after a building gift from Oetker
- The museum names its supporters as the City of Bielefeld, the Kulturstiftung Kunsthalle Bielefeld and Sparkasse Bielefeld, and states it is a non-profit limited company (stated by the space; no years or amounts).
- Oetker's group paid 10.25 million Deutsche Mark of the 12.5 million DM building cost, about 4.6 million DM after tax deductions (German Wikipedia).
- In 2022 the city council approved a renovation budget of 40.5 million euros (Wikipedia). The client is the city's property and service agency (Caruso St John).
- No reporting found of the amounts each shareholder pays toward running costs, or of Oetker family payments today. In 1998 Oetker ended his support and withdrew his loans after the council dropped the Kaselowsky name (Wikipedia; WSWS).

Red flags: Oetker family money and Nazi-era history
- Rudolf August Oetker volunteered for the Waffen-SS in July 1941 and became an SS-Untersturmführer der Reserve in October 1944 (BRF Nachrichten, dpa, 20 October 2013).
- The 2013 study "Dr. Oetker und der Nationalsozialismus" by Jürgen Finger, Sven Keller and Andreas Wirsching appeared in October 2013; chairman August Oetker said his father "was a National Socialist" (The Local).
- Forced labour: Wirsching said the group profited from forced labour at companies where it held stakes, but not at the main Oetker plant (BRF). German Wikipedia names the Gundlach printing house and the Adler sewing-machine works.

Red flags: protest over the Kaselowsky name
- Richard Kaselowsky (1888 to 1944) was an Oetker partner, NSDAP member and member of Himmler's circle of friends (Jochen Rath, Bielefeld City Archive).
- The 1968 opening ceremonies were cancelled and a demonstration took place on 27 September 1968. Composer Hans Werner Henze withdrew a piano concerto written for the opening (Wikipedia).
- Protest returned in waves: students in 1971, a 1985 Greens motion to rename the house for Käthe Kollwitz, a residents' petition and the 1998 council vote (Irene Below).
- A plaque listing Kaselowsky among "the victims of the Second World War" was replaced; Rath dates this to 2017, Below to 2018.
- Unverified: that Oetker threatened to return his honorary citizenship and move his company to Hamburg after the 1998 vote, from the World Socialist Web Site, an openly Trotskyist outlet.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press piece on the naming or the funders was found. The criticism sits in the museum's own journal, in texts by two historians for its 2023 symposium.
1. Kunsthalle Bielefeld journal: Jochen Rath, "Repeatedly finally 'discussed out'!?" (2023)
URL: https://kunsthalle-bielefeld.de/en/entdecken/kb-journal/repeatedly-finally-discussed-out-biographical-and-memory-cultural-observations-on-richard-kaselowsky/
- Says the city, the local press and the Oetker family defended the name against left-wing students, national media and academics.
- Argues the family's concession on the plaque ended the public debate. Published by the museum itself.
2. Kunsthalle Bielefeld journal: Irene Below, "Art has WUMM" (2023)
URL: https://kunsthalle-bielefeld.de/en/blog/art-has-wumm-the-kunsthalle-and-its-naming-as-a-site-of-civil-society-debates-and-artistic-interventions/
- Argues the entrance plaque recast the company's Nazi-era role as a sacrifice for the city and omitted the Nazi regime's victims. Published by the museum itself.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The museum's symposium page describes Philip Johnson as "an avowed National Socialist in the 1920s and 30s".
- Connection: Johnson designed the museum's building.

BOTTOM LINE
Running costs come from a city, a foundation and a savings bank with no documented controversy in the pages read. The weak points are the Oetker family money behind the building, the family's Nazi-era record including profit from forced labour at stakeholdings, and the Kaselowsky name dispute. Watch the 40.5 million euro renovation.
