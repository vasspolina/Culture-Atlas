MUSEO NACIONAL DE ARTES DECORATIVAS: Havana, Cuba
ID: museo-nacional-de-artes-decorativas
Website: http://www.cnpc.cult.cu/ (checked 2026-11-20; returned a 403 error)

OVERVIEW
- Decorative arts museum in a mansion at Calle 17 No. 502, between D and E, in the Vedado (EcuRed; English Wikipedia).
- Opened on 24 July 1964 (EcuRed; Spanish Wikipedia).
- Building: designed in Paris by the architects P. Virad and M. Destuque and built between 1924 and 1927 (English Wikipedia; CubaPLUS spells the second name Dastugue).
- It was the home of María Luisa Gómez-Mena, widow of Cagiga and Countess of Revilla de Camargo, sister of José Gómez-Mena Vila, owner of the Manzana de Gómez. The Socarrás family bought it later. Spanish Wikipedia gives this account and has carried a notice asking for references since April 2012.
- Style: historicist, recalling French chateaux, with columns, wood, wrought iron and stairs of stone and Italian marble (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Collection: EcuRed and Spanish Wikipedia claim over 33,000 works from the reigns of Louis XV, Louis XVI and Napoleon III, Asian pieces of the 16th to 20th centuries, and porcelain from Sèvres, Paris, Chantilly, Limoges, Derby, Chelsea, Wedgwood, Worcester and Staffordshire.
- EcuRed and Spanish Wikipedia say a secretaire by Henri Riesener, once the personal furniture of Marie Antoinette at Versailles, is the main piece in the Neoclassical Salon.
- Director: Yosvanis Fornaris, who opened the 60th anniversary show "Arte Ming en Cuba" in July 2024 with China's ambassador Hua Xin and Cuba's deputy culture minister Lizette Martínez (Prensa Latina).
- On site: guided tours, a library, talks and concerts (CubaPLUS).

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Artes Decorativas: mainly public: Ministry of Culture through the National Council of Cultural Heritage (CNPC)
- The CNPC is a Ministry of Culture body. Five institutions answer to it directly: the National Registry of Cultural Property, this museum, the Ernest Hemingway museum house, the National Dance Museum and the Servando Cabrera Moreno Museum-Library (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The CNPC has a presidency and three vice-presidencies, for museums, monuments and economy; Spanish Wikipedia names Gladys Collazo Usallán as its president in 2018.
- Building: the state took the house by expropriation in 1964 and opened the museum the same year (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Other state bodies lent pieces to the 2024 Ming show: the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, the Gabinete de Arqueología and the Museo Casa de la Obrapía (El Imparcial, 25 July 2024).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is paid by the Cuban state through the Ministry of Culture's heritage council. The weak points are the 1964 expropriation of the house it occupies and thin sourcing for the building's history. Worth watching: the provenance of the collection.
