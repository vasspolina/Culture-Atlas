CAIRO OPERA HOUSE: Cairo, Egypt
ID: cairo-opera-house
Website: https://www.cairoopera.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Egypt's main performing arts venue, on Gezira Island in the Nile, in Zamalek, and part of the National Cultural Centre (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 10 October 1988 by President Hosni Mubarak and Japan's Prince Tomohito of Mikasa (Wikipedia).
- It replaced the Khedivial Opera House of 1869, destroyed by fire on 28 October 1971 (Wikipedia).
- Design: planning by the Ministry of Culture and the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) produced a building "suggestive of traditional Islamic architecture" (stated by the house).
- Halls: the Main Hall seats 1,200, the Small Hall up to 500 and the Open-Air Theatre 600 (Wikipedia). The Gomhouria Theatre, the Arab Music Institute and the Alexandria and Damanhour opera houses are affiliated to it (stated by the house).
- Home to the Egyptian ballet and opera company (Al Jazeera); the Cairo Symphony Orchestra plays in the Main Hall (the house).
- Leadership: the house's site still named Reda El-Wakil as chairman on 2026-11-20.
- It hosts the Cairo International Film Festival's closing ceremony on 20 November 2026 and screenings of award-winning films to 24 November (the house).

FUNDING
Cairo Opera House: mainly public: Egypt's Ministry of Culture, with Japanese government grants for the building and its equipment
- The house is a state body, the General Authority of the National Cultural Center (stated by the house).
- Building: the money for the complex was a gift from Japan to Egypt after Mubarak's visit to Japan in April 1983 (Wikipedia); the house says a Japanese government grant built it.
- Equipment: on 26 January 2025 Egypt and Japan signed the executive agreement for a Japanese grant of 180 million yen, about 1.17 million dollars, for the house's infrastructure and equipment and the Main Hall auditorium (Masrawy).
- On 12 March 2026 Culture Minister Gihan Zaki ordered a fast timetable for the Small and Open-Air theatres, alongside the Main Hall work under the JICA protocol (Youm7).
- State budget: in 2013 the Shura Council debated the house's budget for foreign companies, already cut to 5 million Egyptian pounds (Daily News Egypt).
- Arab Music Festival: conductor Hany Farahat said the state and the Ministry of Culture support it every year (Asharq News).

Red flags: 2013 dismissal of the opera's head, Ines Abdel-Dayem
- On 28 May 2013 Culture Minister Alaa Abdel-Aziz dismissed her, saying he wanted to "inject new blood" into Egyptian culture (NPR).
- Protesters said the minister also fired seven senior ministry figures (NPR); staff accused President Mohamed Morsi's Muslim Brotherhood of trying to control cultural institutions (Al Jazeera, 3 June 2013).

Red flags: 2013 staff strike over the dismissal
- That night the cast cancelled Verdi's "Aida", the first cancellation of the work in the house's history, and protested on stage (Daily News Egypt; NPR).
- Artists and staff stopped all performances for three days from 29 May, and the composer Omar Khairat cancelled his concerts there. Their statement called the dismissal random and without legal grounds (Daily News Egypt).
- Staff demanded the minister's resignation (Al Jazeera) and held a sit-in at his office (NPR).

Red flags: 2026 artists' withdrawals from the Arab Music Festival
- At a press conference before the 34th festival, chairman Reda El-Wakil tied the absence of Arab stars to their fees against the festival's budget and said musicians earn little at Opera concerts (Asharq News, 1 October 2026).
- Singer Mohamed Tharwat withdrew, saying he would not take part in "insulting Egyptian art". Ali El-Haggar said he would miss the festival for the first time and called the Opera's band budget "weak and humiliating" (Asharq News; translated from Arabic).
- Composers Walid Fayed and Amir Abdel-Meguid and conductor Hany Farahat criticised the remarks; Fayed said the reasons lay in the festival's management, programme and contracts (Okaz, 1 October 2026).
- El-Wakil announced on Facebook that he was stepping down, less than three weeks before the festival opened, saying he refused interference in his artistic choices (Asharq News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism is in news media, not art journals, and targets ministers and managers, not the artists: the 2013 dismissal and the 2026 festival row.
1. NPR: Dina Salah ElDin, "Burning Down The House: Artistic Freedom Under Fire In Egypt" (2013)
URL: https://www.hppr.org/2013-06-16/burning-down-the-house-artistic-freedom-under-fire-in-egypt
- Frames the dismissal as part of a cultural war between artists and the ruling Muslim Brotherhood, with fears that Islamists would suppress artistic expression.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public: the Egyptian state runs and funds the house, and Japan's government paid for the building and its new equipment. The weak points are political control of the top post and a festival budget that performers called humiliating. Worth watching: who chairs the house next and when the Main Hall work finishes.
