W139: Amsterdam, Netherlands
ID: w139-amsterdam
Website: https://w139.nl (checked 2026-10-25; live)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-driven production and presentation space for contemporary art at Warmoesstraat 139, in central Amsterdam (W139 contact page).
- Founded in 1979 when a group of artists squatted the building; Wikipedia says five young artists squatted the former theatre in October 1979 as "a movement against the closed world of commercial art and museums".
- The name W139 is the street address; no person is honoured in the name (W139 contact page).
- Building: a monumental former theatre (W139, Wikipedia); the pages read give no architect or materials.
- Programme: in 2024 it held four exhibitions and a residency space used by Dutch and international makers; a new programme line, Para-Siting, starts in 2025. The 2024 report counts 9,993 visitors on 172 opening days.
- Member of De Zaak Nu, MOKER and Culturele Wallen (W139).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 18:00; one event ticket on its site costs 5 euros, and in 2024 every exhibition had paid entry; Stadspas holders enter exhibitions free (2024 report).

FUNDING
W139: mainly public: Amsterdam and national arts funds, with private funds and own income
- Structural support for 2025 to 2028 from the Amsterdam Fund for the Arts and the Mondriaan Fund (W139). The 2024 report says W139 learned in summer 2024 that it would again be structurally funded from 2025, after a year with "large financial uncertainties".
- Its ANBI filing says income comes largely from public funds (Amsterdam Fund for the Arts, Mondriaan Fund), then private funds, plus sponsorship, partnerships, and its own income from tickets, bar and merchandise.
- 2026 project funders: Prince Claus Fund, Cultuurfonds, Fonds21. 2025: Cultural Participation Fund, Cultuurfonds, Fonds21 (W139).
- 2024 funders listed on the site: Gieskes-Strijbis Fund, Mondriaan Fund, Amsterdam Fund for the Arts, Fonds21, Cultuurfonds, Creative Industries Fund NL, Netherland America Foundation, Iona Foundation, Het Nieuwe Instituut, Hartwig Art Foundation, Oedipus Brewing and contributors to Supporting W139. 2023 adds Gemeente Amsterdam, Pictoright Fund, Pro Elisarion Association and the Goethe Institut (W139). No amounts are listed on the page. The 2024 report says a two-year Mondriaan Fund grant and Gieskes-Strijbis Fund bridging support for 2023 to 2024 were the base, and that 16 project grant applications were awarded.
- Supporting W139, a foundation set up in early 2021 to raise private money, held equity of 39,730 euros at the end of 2023 (2024 report).
- Result for 2024: a surplus of 14,442 euros; the foundation's equity stood at 73,680 euros on 31 December 2024 (2024 report).
- Board in 2024 (2024 report): Bart Rutten (chair), Remco Mol (treasurer), George Korsmit (secretary until the end of his term in January 2025), Iris Kensmil (until 13 October 2024) and Susan Kooi (secretary from 13 October 2024); Farida Sedoc joined in January 2025. The site lists Marcel van den Berg as a board member. Board members are unpaid (W139). Annette Wolfsberger is director (2024 report).
- Annual reports for 2018 to 2025 are posted (W139).
- No reporting found of artist dues or of commercial gallery representation on the pages read.
- No reporting found of a fossil fuel, tobacco, gambling, arms or Gulf state funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

WIDER CONTEXT
- An open letter signed "Collective Refusal", from artists, cultural workers and publics, "demands the removal of Art Rotterdam from Ahoy" and an "ethical realignment" of the Mondriaan Fund's commitments, and objects to the fund hosting Prospects at Ahoy in 2026. The page describes NEDS, an arms fair held at Ahoy for 35 years, and names Elbit Systems and Lockheed Martin as exhibitors in 2023 and 2024 (Dutch Art Institute, undated page, no named author; advocacy source).
- Connection to W139: the Mondriaan Fund gives W139 structural support for 2025 to 2028. The letter names the fund, Art Rotterdam and Ahoy; it does not mention W139, and no action aimed at W139 was found on the pages read.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind W139 is largely public arts funding, with named private foundations and own income (34% in 2024, against a 27% target), and it publishes annual reports and a board list. The weak points are that funders are listed without amounts and that structural funding resumed only from 2025. Worth watching: the 2025 to 2028 funding period.
