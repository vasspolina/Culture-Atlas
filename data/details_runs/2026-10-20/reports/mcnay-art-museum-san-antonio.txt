MCNAY ART MUSEUM: San Antonio, USA
ID: mcnay-art-museum-san-antonio
Website: https://www.mcnayart.org/

OVERVIEW
- Modern art museum at 6000 N. New Braunfels Avenue, San Antonio, on 23 acres of grounds with fountains and a Japanese-inspired garden (Wikipedia).
- Created from the bequest of Marion Koogler McNay, who died in 1950 and left her art collection, her mansion, 23 acres and an endowment to found a modern art museum (Wikipedia). Wikipedia dates the founding to 1954.
- Named for McNay, a painter and art teacher whose parents' Kansas pasture land turned out to hold oil; Wikipedia describes her as the heir to an oil fortune.
- Her 24-room Spanish Colonial Revival house, built in 1927 and designed by Atlee Ayres and his son Robert M. Ayres, is the original museum building. The Jane and Arthur Stieren Center for Exhibitions, designed by Jean-Paul Viguier, opened in 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Open Wednesday to Sunday, Thursday to 9pm; closed Monday and Tuesday. General admission is $23, teens $10, students with ID and seniors $18; members, children 12 and under, active military and veterans enter free. Most galleries are wheelchair accessible (McNay).

FUNDING
McNay Art Museum: founded on a private bequest; contributions were 43% of revenue in the year to June 2025
- Form 990 for the year to June 2025: revenue $10,040,132, expenses $10,805,899, contributions $4,316,310 (43.0% of revenue), total assets $100,757,212, net assets $100,080,025 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Founding endowment: McNay's bequest, built on an oil fortune her family held (Wikipedia).
- Mellon Foundation: $1.5 million in operating support in 2020 through its Art Museum Futures Fund, one of 12 museums funded and the only one in Texas (San Antonio Current, Kelly Nelson, 17 September 2020).
- Leadership in Art Museums initiative: $650,000 over five years, announced May 2023, for a Latinx art curator, collection and exhibitions; the $11 million initiative is funded by the Alice L. Walton Foundation, the Ford Foundation, the Mellon Foundation and Pilot House Philanthropy. A separate $250,000 grant from the Less Charitable Trust is for art by women of color (San Antonio Report, Nicholas Frank, 17 May 2023).

Red flags: Protest or boycott
- 12 February 2022: artist Sarah Fox said in a Facebook post that the museum rejected her commissioned puppet animation "Bad Bunny Gets Lucky" for the 8 Create [SATX] exhibition because of its sexual content. A museum spokesperson said the video "does not adhere to the exhibition's focus" and later cited the video's sexual content (San Antonio Report, 16 February 2022).
- The McNay withdrew Fox's invitation after she declined to alter or replace the work. Five of the remaining artists, Lisette Chavez, Alyssa Danna, Jennifer Ling Datchuk, Xavier Gilmore and Libby Rowe, then pulled out in protest (San Antonio Report, 16 February and 8 March 2022).
- Outcome: the McNay opened a revised exhibition on 17 March 2022 with two of the original eight artists, Drew Morros and Joshua Perez. Fox organised an independent show at the Blue Star Arts Complex, opening on 1 April 2022, in space offered free by owner James Lifshutz (San Antonio Report, 8 March 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on one episode, the 2022 dispute over Sarah Fox's video, reported by local press. A New Art Examiner essay on it is known only through the San Antonio Report.

- Local press, not art press.
2. San Antonio Report: Nicholas Frank, "McNay exhibition finds new home at Blue Star after censorship controversy" (8 March 2022)
URL: https://sanantonioreport.org/sarah-fox-mcnay-art-museum-censorship-blue-star-move/
- Reports a 7 March 2022 essay in the Chicago-based New Art Examiner by critic Darren Jones, who called the McNay "the trail-blazing first museum of modern art in Texas" and its action against Fox's work "a misstep". The essay's argument is given as the San Antonio Report summarises it.
- Blue Star owner James Lifshutz, who gave Fox space free of charge, said his offer was not a criticism of the McNay.

BOTTOM LINE
The McNay was founded on the 1950 bequest of Marion Koogler McNay, whose family fortune came from oil (Wikipedia). In the year to June 2025 contributions were 43% of revenue, and named grants came from the Mellon Foundation (2020, and the 2023 initiative), the Ford Foundation, the Alice L. Walton Foundation, Pilot House Philanthropy and the Less Charitable Trust (2023). The documented weak point is the 2022 Sarah Fox dispute, in which five artists withdrew in protest after the museum withdrew Fox's invitation.
