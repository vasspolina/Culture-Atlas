STORM KING ART CENTER: New Windsor, USA
ID: storm-king-art-center
Website: https://stormking.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Outdoor sculpture museum on 500 acres in the Hudson Valley, about a one-hour drive north of Manhattan (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- Founded and opened in 1960 by Ralph E. Ogden and H. Peter Stern, co-owners of the Star Expansion Company of Mountainville, New York (stated by the space).
- Began as a museum for Hudson River School painting; by 1961 its founders had turned to modern sculpture, and the 1967 purchase of thirteen David Smith works started siting sculpture directly in the landscape (stated by the space).
- The Ralph E. Ogden Foundation gave the Museum Building and its surrounding property; Star Expansion Company later donated 300 contiguous acres, and 2,100 acres of Schunnemunk Mountain, now a New York State park (stated by the space).
- Collection includes Calder, David Smith, Mark di Suvero, Henry Moore, Noguchi, Serra and Maya Lin's "Storm King Wavefield" (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: the Museum Building opens 10am to 5pm, onsite parking is free during operating hours, and the Beacon station is about 30 minutes away by car; adult admission is $25 on weekdays and $28 on weekends, with free entry for members, Orange County students up to 21, SNAP cardholders and military families on Blue Star dates (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Storm King Art Center: privately funded: founder family and trustee money, foundations, galleries and corporate donors, with some state grants
- The founding Ogden and Stern money came from the Star Expansion Company; the Ralph E. Ogden Foundation is still a 2025 donor at the $100,000+ level.
- 2025 donors at $100,000+ ("Ambassador"): Bloomberg Philanthropies, Hazen Polsky Foundation, Ralph E. Ogden Foundation, New York State Department of Education, and individuals including Janet Benton and David Schunter, Roberta and Steven Denning, Cathy and Peter Halstead, Peter and Beverly Lipman, Nicholas A. Polsky and Eve Yohalem (stated by the space).
- $50,000+ ("Benefactor"): Agnes Gund, Henry Luce Foundation, Institute of Museum and Library Services, Pace Gallery, Samuel Freeman Charitable Trust and others.
- $25,000+ ("Fellow"): Bloomberg L.P., TD Charitable Foundation, Empire State Development, New York State Council on the Arts, Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, Mendes Wood DM, Regen Projects and others.
- No reporting found of annual accounts on the website, of amounts for individual gifts beyond tier ranges, or of a fossil fuel, arms or tobacco funder.

Red flags: TD Bank plea
- The TD Charitable Foundation is on Storm King's 2025 donor list at the Fellow level, $25,000+ (stormking.org donor page, covering 2025 programmes and operations). Vermont Biz described the foundation in 2013 as the charitable giving arm of TD Bank N.A.
- 10 October 2024: TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering. NBC News reported penalties of about $3 billion, including $1.8 billion to the Department of Justice, $1.3 billion to FinCEN and more than $124 million to the Federal Reserve.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Bloomberg Philanthropies ($100,000+) and Bloomberg L.P. ($25,000+) are on the 2025 donor list, and Storm King offers its digital guide on Bloomberg Connects (Chronogram, 1 November 2023).
- 29 May 2026: more than 260 British artists, in an open letter via Artists for Palestine UK, urged five UK theatres to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies, calling it "complicit in grave war crimes" through its involvement with illegal Israeli settlements and the Bloomberg-Sagol Center (The New Arab). The article does not mention Storm King; no campaign aimed at Storm King was found.

BOTTOM LINE
Storm King is funded by its founding family's foundation, trustees, foundations, galleries, state agencies and a few corporate donors. The one documented weak point among its donors is the TD Charitable Foundation, the giving arm of TD Bank, which pleaded guilty in 2024; Bloomberg is a top-tier donor under campaigns that target other institutions. Worth watching: the lack of published accounts and gift amounts.
