PÉREZ ART MUSEUM MIAMI: Miami, USA
ID: pérez-art-museum-miami-miami
Website: https://www.pamm.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum of 20th-century and contemporary art in Maurice A. Ferré Park (formerly Museum Park) on Biscayne Bay, at 1103 Biscayne Boulevard, Miami (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1984 as the Center for the Fine Arts, a partnership between county government and a private nonprofit (PAMM); renamed the Miami Art Museum in 1994 (PAMM) or 1996 (Wikipedia); took its present name, Jorge M. Pérez Art Museum of Miami-Dade County, when the new building opened in December 2013.
- Named for the developer and collector Jorge M. Pérez after his leadership gift (PAMM).
- Herzog & de Meuron designed the three-storey building of about 200,000 square feet, with hanging vertical gardens by Patrick Blanc; the site was a derelict waterfront parcel turned into a public park (PAMM).
- Franklin Sirmans has been director since 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Monday and Thursday to Sunday, 11 am to 6 pm (Thursday to 9 pm), closed Tuesday and Wednesday. Adults $18; seniors 62+, students and youth 7 to 18 $14; children 6 and under and members free; free Thursdays after 5 pm and for military, veterans, first responders, Florida educators and visitors identifying as disabled with a caregiver.

FUNDING
Pérez Art Museum Miami: mixed: public capital bonds, donors and earned income
- Miami-Dade County provided the capital funds for the building through its Building Better Communities programme, and the City of Miami provided land and funding for Museum Park (PAMM). Wikipedia gives two building costs without reconciling them: $220 million, with $100 million from Miami-Dade voters in general obligation bonds and $120 million from private donors, and $131 million for the new building.
- Jorge M. Pérez pledged $35 million, paid over ten years in cash and art from his collection (Chronicle of Philanthropy, 2 December 2011). Wikipedia gives $35 million and also refers to "Pérez's $40 million gift".
- Kenneth C. Griffin, founder and CEO of the hedge fund Citadel, gave $10 million, revealed at the museum's gala and reported by The Art Newspaper on 11 November 2024. WLRN says the gift established the Kenneth C. Griffin Gallery, where an exhibition of 10 Basquiat works from the Kenneth C. Griffin Collection runs 25 June 2026 to 6 June 2027.
- Mellon Foundation gave $1 million in 2019 and Miami-Dade County provided $2.5 million of the 2013-14 budget (Wikipedia).
- 2024 accounts: revenue $38.4 million, of which contributions $30.0 million; expenses $22.5 million; net assets $193.8 million (ProPublica, Form 990). The 2024 director's pay was $719,251.
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors by name on the museum pages opened.

Red flags: Campaigned name, the 2011 renaming
- 1 December 2011: the Miami Herald and The New York Times reported that the Miami Art Museum agreed to be renamed for Pérez for his $35 million pledge. On 2 December the Chronicle of Philanthropy reported three trustee resignations in protest, including Mary Frank, who said "The name of the museum should not be sold to any private individual". On 8 December it reported four resignations.
- Former board president Mary E. Frank took out a full-page newspaper advertisement against the renaming. Rubén A. Rodríguez, another of the departing figures, headed Carnival Cruise Lines, which still owed $3.5 million of a $5 million endowment gift; the article raises the question whether it would pay (Chronicle of Philanthropy, 8 December 2011).
- Collectors Rosa and Carlos de la Cruz said the renaming could harm future donations; critics argued that a publicly funded institution should carry the community's name; trustee Craig Robins said opponents should match Pérez's gift (Chronicle of Philanthropy).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Protesters in a Gaza march at the Whitney Museum in New York on 22 November 2023 demanded that Ken Griffin "resign or be expelled from the board"; Hyperallergic says he sat on the Whitney's board until July 2023 (Hakim Bishara, 23 November 2023).
- Connection to the space: Griffin gave PAMM $10 million in 2024 and the 2026 Basquiat exhibition is from his collection. No reporting found of a protest, petition or boycott aimed at Griffin at PAMM.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's money is private gifts plus county bond capital, with no flagged industry behind its named donors in the sources opened. The weak point is the founding name deal: a $35 million pledge in exchange for the donor's name on a building partly funded by public bonds, which cost the museum four trustees in December 2011. Sources differ on the gift ($35 million or $40 million) and on the building cost ($131 million or $220 million). Worth watching: the Griffin Gallery and Basquiat exhibition, and whether any campaign forms around Griffin at PAMM.
