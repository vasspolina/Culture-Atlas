RUSSIA, MY HISTORY (VDNKH): Moscow, Russia
ID: russia-my-history-vdnkh
Website: https://myhistorypark.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Multimedia history park in Pavilion No. 57 of the VDNKh exhibition grounds, Prospekt Mira 119, building 57, near VDNKh metro station (myhistorypark.ru).
- The project grew out of the Orthodox Rus exhibitions the Russian Orthodox Church began in 1995, which Archimandrite Tikhon Shevkunov later took under his wing (RFE/RL, 2019).
- A Romanov dynasty show in the Manezh in 2013 drew more than 300,000 people in 20 days, and the first permanent exhibition opened in Pavilion 57 in December 2015 (RFE/RL).
- The Moscow park is the head of a network that its site gives as 26 cities, and the site states that 25 million people have visited the parks (myhistorypark.ru).
- Permanent displays cover the Rurikids (862 to 1598), the Romanovs (1613 to 1917) and the years 1914 to 1945, with a show on Peter the Great; the formats are touch screens, cinemas and 3D reconstructions (myhistorypark.ru).
- In 2017 the Education Ministry recommended the parks as teaching material for students and teachers (RFE/RL).
- In 2015 the operator sent its texts to historians of the Institute of Russian History; senior researcher Vladislav Aksyonov told RFE/RL that only a tiny fraction of their changes were adopted.
- From 11 December 2025 Pavilion 57 showed "Znanie. Heroes", an exhibition by the Znanie society on Heroes of Russia that includes participants of the war in Ukraine (Znanie society; title translated by the atlas).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 19:00, last entry 18:00. Entry to the permanent displays is free for under-18s, and free for disabled visitors of groups 1 and 2, large families and combat veterans; the site states that a disabled visitor can reach every hall and that films carry subtitles (myhistorypark.ru).

FUNDING
Russia, My History: Gazprom-funded foundation with state backing
- The parks are run by the Humanitarian Projects Foundation, set up in 2013 by Ivan Yesin, a former legal counsel of Gazprom Neft, who heads the network (Bumaga, 2023). RFE/RL describes Yesin as a former Gazprom lawyer and Shevkunov as head of the foundation's expert council.
- The foundation gets most of its funding from the state-controlled Gazprom and from government grants. It lists as partners the presidential administration, the Moscow Patriarchate, the Culture Ministry and the Russian Military-Historical Society (RFE/RL, 2019).
- Gazprom states that since 2016 it has co-financed the creation of the parks with Russia's regions, signing a contract in each region with an authorised non-profit that carries out the project (Gazprom sustainability report 2019).
- In 2017 Bloomberg reported that Gazprom raised its charitable giving to a record 26.3 billion roubles, most of it for the parks. The foundation denied this and said it spent no more than 120 million roubles on each centre. RFE/RL's own analysis of open sources put total spending at 10 billion roubles or more (RFE/RL).
- Since 2025, on the President's instruction, the parks have been part of the federal project "We Together" within the national project "Youth and Children", which runs to 2030 (myhistorypark.ru; names translated by the atlas).

Red flags: Gazprom and Gazprom Neft money, 2016 to 2021
- Shevkunov told The Bell that Gazprom allocated 360 million roubles in 2018 for three new parks (Bumaga, 2023).
- Gazprom Neft's Rodnye Goroda foundation gave more than 1 billion roubles in 2019 and 3 billion in 2021 to the My History foundation, which Yesin has also led since 2021 (Bumaga, citing Vazhnye Istorii).

Red flags: Sanctions on the Gazprom group, 2018 to 2025
- The US Treasury designated Gazprom chief executive Alexei Miller on 6 April 2018 as an official of the Russian government (US Treasury press release sm0338).
- The UK designated Miller on 10 March 2022 and Gazprom Neft on 10 January 2025 (UK Sanctions List).

Red flags: 2017 open letter from the Free Historical Society
- After the Education Ministry's recommendation, the Free Historical Society published an open letter criticising the "panegyric tone" towards "every tsar without exception" and asked the ministry to stop recommending the exhibitions until a professional review was done (RFE/RL).
- Pavel Kuzenkov, a former content author, called the project "a clear example of the use of historical science in the service of political propaganda" (RFE/RL).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Parks of the network opened in occupied Melitopol and Luhansk in September 2023 and in Donetsk in January 2026 (myhistorypark.ru).
- Bumaga reported in 2023 that the St Petersburg park staged exhibitions supporting the invasion of Ukraine, including a show on the history of Ukraine.
- These parks share the Moscow park's operator, name and content.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the park is Gazprom group and Russian state money, channelled through a foundation run by a former Gazprom Neft lawyer. Its weak points are fossil fuel money tied to sanctioned companies and executives, and a historians' protest against its use in schools; the network's spread into occupied Ukraine is worth watching.
