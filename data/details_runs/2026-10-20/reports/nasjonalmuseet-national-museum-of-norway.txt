NASJONALMUSEET (NATIONAL MUSEUM OF NORWAY): Oslo, Norway
ID: nasjonalmuseet-national-museum-of-norway
Website: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no (checked 2026-10-24; loads; the visit and ticket pages showed no hours or prices to the reader used)

OVERVIEW
- Norway's national museum of art, architecture and design, at Brynjulf Bulls plass 3 on the Vestbanen site in central Oslo (museum site; Wikipedia).
- Created on 28 April 2003 by merging the National Gallery, the Museum of Contemporary Art, the Museum of Architecture and two design museums; it is state-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia).
- The new building opened in June 2022. Kleihues + Schuwerk won the international architecture competition in November 2010; the government presented the project on 22 March 2013 at about NOK 5.3 billion (Wikipedia).
- Holds over 400,000 works, including the first version of Edvard Munch's "The Scream" (1893) (Wikipedia).
- Wikipedia says critics widely derided the building as resembling a prison, quoting the phrase "national prison". It also says the first director, Sune Nordgren, resigned in 2006 after a long period of professional criticism and staff conflicts.
- The director is Ingrid Røynesdal, appointed in 2023, after Karin Hindsbo (2017 to 2023) (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening hours, admission prices or accessibility details; the museum's visit pages showed none to the reader used.

FUNDING
Nasjonalmuseet: mainly public: a state-owned foundation, with private partners including the Fredriksen family
- State-owned and managed by the Ministry of Culture (Wikipedia). The 2024 annual report PDF exceeded the reading tool's size limit, so no income figures could be confirmed.
- The museum lists six partners: AKO Foundation, the Fredriksen Family Collection, Lise M Stolt-Nielsen and family, Sparebankstiftelsen DNB, Stiftelsen Scheibler and Viking. The page states no amounts or years.
- Fredriksen Family Art Company: lends the Fredriksen Family Collection on a long-term basis, initial term ten years, confirmed in 2017; funds a doctoral programme with four fellowships planned for 2023 to 2027, and a separate doctoral project on Frida Hansen. No financial figures are given (museum site). Cecilie and Kathrine Fredriksen, daughters of the shipping magnate John Fredriksen, announced the partnership in 2019 and have lent more than 100 works (Apollo, 2025).

Red flags: protest against the Fredriksen partnership
- 12 March 2022: Jeremie McGowan, former director of Nordnorsk Kunstmuseum, wrote in Klassekampen that Seadrill, "Fredriksen's drilling company", entered a major agreement with Rosneft shortly before Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, and called the museum "naive or complicit". Ruben Steinum, chair of the Association of Norwegian Visual Artists, said the public needs to know how the museum assesses partners whose wealth was partly accumulated in areas of conflict and war (Kunstkritikk, Live Drønen, 18 March 2022).
- 18 March 2022: communications director Eirik Kydland told Kunstkritikk the museum had seen no new circumstances that call for review, that no Fredriksen company had been affected by sanctions, and that the money comes from legitimate business operations. Director Karin Hindsbo did not respond.
- 10 June 2022: artists Nikhil Vettukattil, Knut Asdam, Kajsa Dahlberg and Sille Storihle launched an appeal, "A Gift we can Afford to Refuse", calling for the agreement to be terminated; it had well over 100 signatures when Kunstkritikk reported it (Tiril Flom, 10 June 2022).
- The appeal argues the deal gives one powerful family too much influence, calls it an attempt to improve a company's reputation through art sponsorship, and cites John Fredriksen's business dealings from Iran to Russia, including Russia's Order of Friendship awarded in 2015 (Kunstkritikk, Flom).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Two Kunstkritikk pieces from March and June 2022 criticise the Fredriksen deal; no criticism found of the other partners.
1. Kunstkritikk: Live Drønen, "The National Museum of Norway Linked to Russia" (18 March 2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/the-national-museum-of-norway-linked-to-russia/
- The partnership needs fresh scrutiny after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, given the family's links to Russia.
- Names McGowan, Seadrill, Rosneft and Fredriksen; director Karin Hindsbo did not respond, communications director Eirik Kydland did.
2. Kunstkritikk: Tiril Flom, "Artists Fight Sponsorship Agreement" (10 June 2022)
URL: https://kunstkritikk.com/artists-fight-the-fredriksen-collaboration
- Reports the appeal's question whether public trust and professional integrity should outweigh the financial benefit; Åsdam is quoted as saying it "kills the professional integrity of a national public institution".
- Cites as precedents that the Astrup Fearnley Museum cut ties with Lundin Oil and that the Met and the Serpentine ended Sackler sponsorship.

BOTTOM LINE
Nasjonalmuseet is mostly state-funded, with a named private partner, the Fredriksen family, whose loan and doctoral funding come from a shipping and drilling fortune. The weak points are the artists' 2022 appeal, the Russia links raised by McGowan, and income figures that could not be confirmed from the 2024 report. Worth watching: whether the Fredriksen agreement is renewed when its ten-year term ends.
