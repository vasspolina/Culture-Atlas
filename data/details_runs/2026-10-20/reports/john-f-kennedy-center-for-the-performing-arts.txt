JOHN F. KENNEDY CENTER FOR THE PERFORMING ARTS: Washington, USA
ID: john-f-kennedy-center-for-the-performing-arts
Website: https://www.kennedy-center.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; returned HTTP 403, so the page was not read)

OVERVIEW
- Federally established performing arts centre at 2700 F Street NW on the Potomac River, Washington, D.C. (Wikipedia).
- President Eisenhower signed the National Cultural Center Act in 1958; Congress renamed the project for Kennedy in 1964 as a "living memorial"; it opened on 8 September 1971 (Wikipedia).
- Edward Durell Stone designed the building; construction cost about $70 million, of which Congress allocated $43 million (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: on 15 September 2026 the board voted to close the center immediately, citing financial shortages and renovation needs, hours after a judge again blocked a name inscription; a judge had on 29 May 2026 temporarily barred a two-year closure, and the sources opened do not say whether the building is open today (Democracy Now, Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Kennedy Center: public and private: Congress pays for the building; programmes ran on tickets and gifts
- The 1958 act requires programming to be funded privately, while the federal government covers building maintenance and operations (Wikipedia).
- Federal appropriations are about $45 million a year, used mainly for maintenance (Washington Post). Marc Bamuthi Joseph told Democracy Now the center received $260 million from Congress "this past fiscal year", a figure he did not break down.
- Original construction donors included the Ford Foundation ($5 million) and the Kennedy family (about $500,000); David Rubenstein gave $50 million toward the REACH expansion (Wikipedia).
- Ticket income fell 15 percent and gifts nearly 25 percent in the year to September 2025; the center expects ticket income to fall more than two-thirds in 2026. It wrote off $48 million of pledges and had fully borrowed a $21 million credit line by June 2026 (Washington Post, Federica Cocco and Naema Ahmed).
- The Trump Kennedy Center Foundation raised about $17.5 million according to internal documents; its donors are not disclosed (Washington Post). The center declined to answer the Post's questions.
- The record's $100 million shortfall figure was not found in the sources opened.

Red flags: Presidential takeover of the board, firings and renaming, 2025 to 2026
- In February 2025 Trump ousted board chair David Rubenstein and president Deborah Rutter and made himself chair (Democracy Now); he named Richard Grenell interim executive director on 10 February 2025 (Wikipedia).
- In March 2025 new leadership fired at least seven members of the Social Impact team, including vice president Marc Bamuthi Joseph (Democracy Now). By November 2025 dozens had been fired since February, including the whole dance programming team (Hyperallergic, Emma Cieslik).
- On 18 December 2025 the board voted to rename it the Trump-Kennedy Center though the name is set by statute; on 29 May 2026 Judge Christopher Cooper held that "only Congress can change it" and ordered the name removed (Wikipedia, Democracy Now).
- Grenell threatened to sue drummer Chuck Redd after Redd backed out of hosting a jazz night (NBC News, Kyla Guilfoil).

Red flags: Protest or boycott, artists withdrawing, 2025 to 2026
- Issa Rae, the producers of "Hamilton", Rhiannon Giddens, Renée Fleming and Philip Glass withdrew after the takeover or renaming (Democracy Now, NBC News). The Washington National Opera announced on 9 January 2026 it would leave (Wikipedia).
- On 22 November 2025 Movement Movement and Hands Off the Arts held a protest dance outside, demanding Grenell's dismissal and reinstatement of dance programming (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism in Hyperallergic, mostly opinion and protest reporting, aimed at the takeover and its effect on programming.

- Reports that the National Park Service dismantled the sculpture on 2 September 2026 and that artists and activists condemned it; names no critic and does not clearly say who ordered it.

BOTTOM LINE
Congress pays for the building, and no corporate or flagged funder is named in the sources opened. The weak points are the president's takeover as chair, the firings, a court-ordered reversal of the renaming, artists' withdrawals, and a revenue fall on which the center declined to answer the Post's questions. Worth watching: the closure fight and the undisclosed donors to the Trump Kennedy Center Foundation.
