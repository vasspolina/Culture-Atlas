ARS ELECTRONICA CENTER: Linz, Austria
ID: ars-electronica-center-linz
Website: https://ars.electronica.art/center/en/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Media-art and technology museum at Ars-Electronica-Strasse 1, 4040 Linz (Ars Electronica website).
- Ars Electronica began as a festival in Linz in September 1979, founded by Hannes Leopoldseder, Hubert Bognermayr, Herbert W. Franke and Ulrich Rützel; Ars Electronica Linz GmbH was incorporated as a company in 1995 and the Center and the Futurelab research group opened in 1996 (Wikipedia).
- The new Center opened on 2 January 2009 after about two years of construction at a cost of about 30 million euros, designed by Treusch architecture, with a 5,000 square metre glass skin of 1,100 panels fitted with LEDs (Wikipedia).
- Operated by Ars Electronica Linz GmbH; Gerfried Stocker has been artistic director since 2014 (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibitions on AI, neuroscience, robotics, autonomous mobility, genetic engineering and biotechnology; Deep Space 8K; guided tours, workshops and school programmes; the QUBE restaurant on the top floor (Ars Electronica website).
- More than 170,000 visitors and event guests came in 2024, including 36,264 children and young people in class groups (Ars Electronica, 6 Feb 2025).

FUNDING
Ars Electronica Center: mixed: City of Linz and Upper Austria, federal funds, EU project money, corporate partners and self-earned income
- The Center's partners page shows the City of Linz and Linz AG as host organisations and Land Oberösterreich among its partners; the group's partners page also shows the Federal Ministry for Housing, Arts, Culture, Media and Sport. Partners appear as logos.
- Ars Electronica's 2024 results release (6 Feb 2025) says the Anton Bruckner anniversary offerings were financed by Land Oberösterreich and the City of Linz, that the Connected Earth project was produced in close cooperation with the Austrian Climate and Energy Fund, that the missimo STEM experience space is run on behalf of the Kaiserschild Privatstiftung, and that 19 EU-funded projects are running, mainly financed through Creative Europe and Horizon Europe (translated from the German).
- Corporate partners on the Center's partners page include Siemens, KUKA, Dorotheum, ORF Oberösterreich, Zeiss, Thermo Fisher and Linzer Bier. Project partners shown include Audemars Piguet, NVIDIA, MIT Media Lab, Bellingcat, Forensic Architecture and Bloomberg Philanthropies. Bloomberg Philanthropies appears as a project-partner logo.
- Income model: Futurelab profits, private partnerships and ticket sales produce a surplus, and Linz Mayor Klaus Luger praised "a larger surplus every year"; in 2013, 60% of the revenue of Ars Electronica GmbH was self-financed (Rhizome, Elvia Wilk, 2015).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found covers the festival's programme, not the Center's money.
1. Falter (Austrian weekly): Lina Paulitsch, "Ars Electronica: Das Linzer Digital-Festival ignoriert die aktuelle KI-Bedrohung" (9 September 2025, Falter 37/2025)
URL: https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20250909/das-linzer-digital-festival-ignoriert-die-aktuelle-ki-bedrohung
- The headline (translation) says the Linz digital festival ignores the current AI threat; the subtitle (translation) says tech dystopia appears there only sparingly.
- The opening says visitors need a good sense of direction, that the website gives sparse information beforehand, and that the floor plans confuse.
- Paywalled; only excerpts were read.
2. Furtherfield: "KissMyArs", "The tireless enchantment of technological sorcery | Ars Electronica 2016 Review" (14 August 2016)
URL: https://www.furtherfield.org/?p=5888
- Finds the festival dominated by showy robotics, VR and drone displays, "interactive spectacles that neglect to examine the social & political impact of technology", and says it does not examine how smartphones and big data change privacy and security.
- Argues that Silicon Valley startup culture has persuaded many that every problem has an app-based solution; the CyberArts exhibition is the exception, which the author calls a "top selection" of recent media art.
- Cites the #KissMyArs campaign, which says the Prix art prize is awarded to men "9/10 times".
3. Rhizome: Elvia Wilk, "transmediale 2015: Why we need spaces for art and tech beyond corporate influence" (18 February 2015)
URL: https://old.rhizome.org/editorial/2015/feb/18/transmediale-2015-capture-all
- Not criticism of Ars Electronica: it presents the company's self-financing through Futurelab and partnerships as a route to autonomy, in contrast to the public funding of transmediale.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Center is mostly public (City of Linz, Upper Austria, federal funds, EU programmes) plus corporate partners and its own earnings, and none of the named partners is flagged. Bloomberg Philanthropies is listed among the project partners. Worth watching are the corporate and project partners shown beside the public funders.
