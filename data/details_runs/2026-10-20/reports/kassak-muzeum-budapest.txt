KASSÁK MÚZEUM: Budapest, Hungary
ID: kassak-muzeum-budapest
Website: https://kassakmuzeum.hu/

OVERVIEW
- Museum of the Hungarian avant-garde in the Zichy Castle, Fő tér 1, in Óbuda, Budapest. It is a member institution of the Petőfi Literary Museum (PIM) and has operated in the castle since 1976 (stated by the museum).
- Named for Lajos Kassák (1887 to 1967), the Hungarian avant-garde writer, visual artist and editor whose material and intellectual heritage it preserves, researches and presents (stated by the museum).
- Directors: Ferenc Csaplár (1976 to 2006), Gábor Andrási (2006 to 2010), Edit Sasvári (2010 to 2020) and Merse Pál Szeredi (2020 to the present).
- Building: a one-storey baroque castle built for Count Miklós Zichy between 1746 and 1752 by the stonemason Henrik Jäger, with decoration by the sculptor Károly Bebó. It later served as a military base and a silk manufactory, and became a youth cultural centre in 1976. The outbuildings house the Óbuda Museum, the Vasarely Museum, the Térszínház and the Hello Wood workshops.
- Exhibitions: the permanent exhibition "Kassák!" on Kassák's periodicals, renewed in 2011, and temporary shows that place avant-garde and modernist art in context. Its 2012 identity won a Red Dot (Communication Design) and a Good Design award.
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 17:00; closed Monday and Tuesday. Admission HUF 1,200 for adults, HUF 600 for students and pensioners, and family tickets of HUF 1,700 and HUF 2,500.

FUNDING
Kassák Múzeum: public: part of the Petőfi Literary Museum, with National Cultural Fund and German foundation support for projects
- The museum is a member institution of the Petőfi Literary Museum (museum's mission statement), which manages the ground floor of the castle (museum's building page).
- The catalogue of the permanent exhibition "Kassák!" (renewed in 2011) lists "Supported by" the National Cultural Fund, the Ministry of National Resources and the German foundation Stiftung Ahlers pro Arte.
- The castle is owned by Magyar Nemzeti Vagyonkezelő Zrt. and maintained by the Magyar Kultúráért Alapítvány (museum's building page).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Protest at the parent body: on 2 December 2020 artists and researchers held a demonstration in front of the Petőfi Literary Museum, organised by aHang, demanding the resignation of its director Szilárd Demeter after his Origó article comparing Europe to "George Soros's gas chamber", which he later withdrew. More than 28,000 people had signed the open letter calling for his resignation (444.hu, Szurovecz Illés, "Művészek és kutatók tiltakoztak Demeter Szilárd ellen a Petőfi Irodalmi Múzeum előtt", 2 December 2020; title translated: artists and researchers protested against Szilárd Demeter in front of the Petőfi Literary Museum).
- State money for the foundation: hvg.hu reported on 17 February 2022 that a government resolution published in the Magyar Közlöny and signed by Viktor Orbán gave the Magyar Kultúráért Alapítvány, which it ties to Demeter, more than 48 billion forints over four years, from about 12.73 billion in 2022 to about 11.28 billion in 2025 ("Demeter Szilárd alapítványát 48 milliárd forintnyi közpénzből erősítik meg"; title translated: Szilárd Demeter's foundation will be reinforced with 48 billion forints of public money). The article refers to the Zichy Castle.
- Neither piece names the Kassák Museum. The connection: the museum is a member institution of the Petőfi Literary Museum, and the Magyar Kultúráért Alapítvány maintains its building (museum's building page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kassák Museum is public: it is a member of the Petőfi Literary Museum, and its permanent exhibition catalogue names the National Cultural Fund, the Ministry of National Resources and the German foundation Stiftung Ahlers pro Arte as supporters. Its parent museum drew a protest over its director in 2020, and hvg.hu reported in 2022 on a state grant to the foundation that maintains its building.
