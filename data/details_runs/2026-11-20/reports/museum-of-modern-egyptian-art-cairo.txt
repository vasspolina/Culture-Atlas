MUSEUM OF MODERN EGYPTIAN ART: Cairo, Egypt
ID: museum-of-modern-egyptian-art-cairo
Website: https://www.fineart.gov.eg/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State art museum in the Cairo Opera House grounds in Zamalek, on the former fair grounds (Wikipedia; El Watan).
- Origins: in 1927 Mahmoud Khalil persuaded the palace to form by royal decree an advisory committee for the fine arts, which recommended a modern art museum for works the Ministry of Education bought at the yearly Cairo Salon (El Watan).
- The collection moved among several venues, mostly heritage palaces. In 1983 the building in the Opera grounds became its permanent home, and it opened there in 1991 (Asharq Al-Awsat; El Watan).
- Collection: more than 13,000 paintings and sculptures, including works by Mahmoud Said, Ragheb Ayad, Gazbia Sirry and Abdel Hadi Al-Gazzar (Wikipedia). Asharq Al-Awsat puts about 1,000 works on display and 16,000 to 18,000 in storage; The Art Newspaper gave 12,000 in 2022.
- A wing holds about 60 works that Inji Efflatoun bequeathed to the government in 1989, moved from the Prince Taz Palace for conservation reasons (The Art Newspaper, 2022).
- Closures: the museum reopened in 2005, was closed from 2010, partly reopened in 2014 and closed again, reopened in full in 2020, was partly closed after the COVID-19 crisis and reopened in January 2025 with "Khabiat Bikar", a show honouring Hussein Bikar (Asharq Al-Awsat; El Watan).
- Visiting: in summer 2026 it opened in two shifts, 9 am to 3 pm and 5 pm to 8 pm (Youm7, 4 June 2026).

FUNDING
Museum of Modern Egyptian Art: mainly public: the Egyptian Ministry of Culture
- The Ministry of Culture owns the museum (Wikipedia) and runs it through the Fine Arts Sector (Asharq Al-Awsat).
- 2020 reopening: Culture Minister Ines Abdel-Dayem reopened it with free entry until the end of that December, after work on security and surveillance, registration of the collection, electrical systems and a new display (El Watan).
- 2025 reopening: sector head Walid Qanoush listed a permanent fix for ceiling leaks, central air conditioning for the building and its stores, fire suppression for the stores, lighting, paint and updated security (Asharq Al-Awsat).
- Collection: the state bought Al-Gazzar's paintings from his widow a year after his death (Al Jazeera), and the Efflatoun works came by bequest.
- On 16 June 2026 the sector and the Egyptian national committee of ICOM launched the Cairo stage of the "Museum of Recovery and Development" community project at the museum (El Watan).

Red flags: Works lent to government offices and lost, exposed in 2022
- Al-Gazzar's 1964 painting "From the Inspiration of the Red Sea Lighthouses" left the museum on loan to the Ministry of Culture building in 1971 and was missing until June 2022, when it turned up in a cafe in Shubra El-Kheima and went back to the museum (Al Jazeera; El Watan).
- Days later the same man returned Salah Taher's "The Countryside" and Saleh Reda's "Circle and Trunk", also museum works lent to a government body in the 1970s (Al Jazeera).
- Stated by the artist and critic Ezz El-Din Naguib: a portrait by Ahmed Sabry lent to Egypt's embassy in the United States in the 1970s was stolen and never recovered, and Mahmoud Said's "Storm on the Corniche", lent to America, is unaccounted for (El Watan).
- Naguib said he gave Minister Abdel-Dayem a documented file on thefts and abuses three years earlier and that it was ignored (Al Jazeera).

Red flags: 2017 theft of five Mahmoud Said paintings
- Early in 2017 a photographer took five paintings by Mahmoud Said from the museum, valued at about 50 million Egyptian pounds, leaving copies in their place. The theft was noticed days later, and the authorities recovered the works (Al Jazeera).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Egyptian and Arab news media criticise the ministry's care of the collection: lost loans, a theft and poor storage.
1. Al Jazeera: "تراث مصر التشكيلي بين مطرقة السرقة وسندان الإهمال" (2022)

- Stated by Naguib: 70 of 1,000 paintings that Efflatoun's family gave the ministry were missing when the heirs won them back in court.
3. Asharq Al-Awsat: Mohamed El-Kafrawy, "مصر: متحف الفن الحديث يستعيد بريقه بعد «سنوات الإغلاق»" (2025)

- Mostly admiring; the artist Ashraf Reda asks for a marketing plan and the return of the reproductions shop.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Egyptian state: the Ministry of Culture owns it and the Fine Arts Sector runs it. The weak points are care of the collection, with loans to state offices lost for decades and a 2017 theft, and repeated closures since 2010. Worth watching: whether the sector publishes an inventory of works still out on loan.
