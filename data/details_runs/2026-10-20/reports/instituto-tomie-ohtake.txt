INSTITUTO TOMIE OHTAKE: São Paulo, Brazil
ID: instituto-tomie-ohtake
Website: https://www.institutotomieohtake.org.br

OVERVIEW
- Art institute in Pinheiros, São Paulo, at Rua Coropés 88, about 800 m from Faria Lima station on Metro Line 4 (institute website).
- Named for the artist Tomie Ohtake; it opened on 21 November 2001, her 88th birthday (Carta Capital, Ana Paula Sousa, 13 August 2026).
- The building was designed by the architect Ruy Ohtake for the Laboratório Aché and built from 1996 to 2001. It combines a 23-floor tower with a long, low structure (Carta Capital).
- Portuguese Wikipedia lists four atelier rooms, seven exhibition rooms, a seminar room and a documentation room around a Grand Hall with a restaurant, bookstore, café and object shop, on 7,500 m². It says the space is ceded on a 30-year loan.
- Ricardo Ohtake left the presidency in 2023 and joined the advisory council, and Gabriela Moulin became executive director in 2024. A 23-member council was created in 2021 (Carta Capital).
- Visiting: entry is free, and the institute receives about 600,000 visitors a year (Carta Capital).

FUNDING
Instituto Tomie Ohtake: corporate-sponsored through tax incentives: Nubank, Bradesco, Itaú and Grupo CCR under the Rouanet law
- Nubank: committed R$ 10 million a year through 2027, about 40% of the budget (Carta Capital).
- About 98% of funding comes through the Rouanet tax-incentive law. The institute receives no family funding or government transfers, according to Carta Capital.
- Itaú and Bradesco are also sponsors (Carta Capital).
- Grupo CCR signed an agreement on 29 November 2023 to become one of the institute's main sponsors, through Instituto CCR, for arts-education programmes in public and private schools, the Programa Tomie Territórios, the first season of a podcast and an audiobook (ABC do ABC, 29 November 2023).
- Laboratório Aché built the building. Carta Capital reports that the Aché lease was nearing expiry in 2026 and had to be renegotiated.
- Before its 25th anniversary a funding crisis threatened the institute's continuity (Carta Capital).

BOTTOM LINE
The institute is funded by corporate sponsors through the Rouanet tax law: Nubank at R$ 10 million a year through 2027 covers about 40% of the budget, with Bradesco, Itaú and Grupo CCR also sponsoring. Carta Capital reports no family funding or government transfers.
