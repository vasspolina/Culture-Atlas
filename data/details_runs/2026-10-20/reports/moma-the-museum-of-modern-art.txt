MOMA (THE MUSEUM OF MODERN ART): New York, USA
ID: moma-the-museum-of-modern-art

OVERVIEW
- Modern and contemporary art museum on West 53rd Street in Midtown Manhattan (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 7 November 1929 at 730 Fifth Avenue, with A. Conger Goodyear as president and Alfred H. Barr Jr. as first director (Wikipedia).
- Moved to its current building, designed by Philip L. Goodwin and Edward Durell Stone, in 1939; an expansion adding 47,000 square feet of galleries reopened on 21 October 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Glenn Lowry directed it from 1995 and announced in September 2024 that he would step down in 2025 (Hyperallergic, 10 September 2024); Wikipedia lists Christophe Cherix as director.
- Adult admission is USD 30; New York State residents enter free on Friday evenings, 17:30 to 20:30 (Uniqlo Friday Nights, Wikipedia).

FUNDING
MoMA: private: admissions, trustees' gifts and endowment, with corporate partners
- MoMA is a private nonprofit that does not accept government funding; Wikipedia gives annual revenue of about USD 145 million (undated).
- David Rockefeller gave USD 77 million in cash toward the 2002 to 2004 expansion and pledged USD 100 million to the endowment in 2005 (Wikipedia).
- No page read lists corporate sponsors with amounts.

Red flags: Leon Black, Epstein payments
- Role: Black was MoMA board chair from 1 July 2018 to 1 July 2021 and remains a trustee (Wikipedia). He announced on 22 March 2021 that he was leaving the chair, citing health reasons (Hyperallergic, 23 March 2021).
- Payments: The Art Newspaper (19 August 2025) reported that Black paid Epstein at least USD 158 million between 2012 and 2017 for tax and estate planning advice. CBS News (updated 4 June 2026) reported that Senator Ron Wyden wrote the total was USD 170 million for 2012 to 2017. These were payments from Black to Epstein, not to MoMA; neither article says any money went to the museum.
- Wyden wrote that Black has not given a "credible explanation" (CBS News). Black's lawyers say a Dechert review found he paid Epstein for estate planning and tax advice only. MoMA did not respond to The Art Newspaper.
- Unverified: Hyperallergic's newsletter (5 February 2026) reported allegations against Black in released Epstein files; he denies them.

Red flags: Glenn Dubin, Epstein links
- Role: Hyperallergic called Dubin a MoMA trustee in November 2019, September 2020 and January 2024; The Art Newspaper named him among five targeted trustees in March 2021. No page read says whether he still serves.
- Hyperallergic reported on 10 September 2020 that Epstein received USD 15 million for introducing Dubin to a JPMorgan Chase executive around Dubin's 2004 sale of Highbridge Capital, and invested in Dubin's hedge fund; US Virgin Islands subpoena notices were filed on 3 September 2020. A Dubin representative said the Dubins "categorically reject" the allegations.
- Unverified: Virginia Giuffre testified that Ghislaine Maxwell told her to give Dubin a "massage"; Dubin has denied it (Hyperallergic, 4 January 2024).

Red flags: protests aimed at trustees and the museum
- January 2020: 37 artists in the MoMA PS1 show "Theater of Operations" asked MoMA to divest from trustees whose money profits from others' suffering, naming Fink's BlackRock and Black's Apollo (Hyperallergic, 14 January 2020).
- From 9 April 2021: Strike MoMA, a ten-week coalition action, targeted trustees Black, Steven Tananbaum, Dubin, Steven Cohen and Larry Fink (The Art Newspaper, 24 March 2021).
- 10 February 2024: more than 500 protesters occupied the atrium and MoMA closed its galleries; they named Black, Fink, Paula Crown, Marie-Josee Kravis and Ronald Lauder (Hyperallergic, 10 February 2024). Unverified: the protesters say these trustees fund genocide.

BOTTOM LINE
MoMA's operating money is private, but its board is the weak point: Black, a trustee, paid Epstein at least USD 158 million (The Art Newspaper), and Hyperallergic reported business links between Dubin and Epstein. Worth watching: whether MoMA acts on Black's trusteeship.
