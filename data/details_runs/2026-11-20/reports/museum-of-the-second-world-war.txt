MUSEUM OF THE SECOND WORLD WAR: Gdańsk, Poland
ID: museum-of-the-second-world-war
Website: https://muzeum1939.pl (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State history museum on Plac Władysława Bartoszewskiego 1 in Gdańsk, with a branch, the Museum of Westerplatte and the War of 1939 (museum BIP; NIK).
- Donald Tusk spearheaded the project; Paweł Machcewicz and Piotr M. Majewski submitted the outline of the permanent exhibition in 2008, and the museum opened to the public on 23 March 2017 (Cultures of History Forum, 2017).
- Director: Rafał Wnuk; a new museum council named on 2 July 2024 includes Barbara Engelking and Machcewicz (museum BIP).
- By 28 November 2024 the museum had counted 533,455 visitors that year, more than in 2023, its previous record year (museum news, 2024).
- The cornerstone of the Westerplatte branch building, by JAZ + Architekci, was laid on 25 April 2026 (National Geographic Polska).

FUNDING
Museum of the Second World War: mainly public: Polish Ministry of Culture and National Heritage
- Organised by the Minister of Culture and financed mainly by the ministry's operating subsidies and targeted grants (NIK audit).
- Planned income was PLN 42,537,000 in 2019, PLN 37,133,000 in 2020, PLN 40,770,000 in 2021 and PLN 40,709,000 in 2022 (NIK audit).
- For the Westerplatte branch the ministry signed targeted grants of PLN 26,982,000 in 2020 and PLN 21,429,500 in 2021 (NIK audit).
- The Westerplatte branch is to cost PLN 304,564,000 and open in 2030, three years later than first planned (National Geographic Polska, 2026).

Red flags: 2024 removal of PiS-era additions to the exhibition
- In March 2024 the culture minister dismissed the PiS-appointed director Grzegorz Berendt; Wnuk was appointed in April (Notes from Poland).
- In June 2024 the museum removed PiS-era additions, including portraits of Maksymilian Kolbe and Witold Pilecki, saying it was returning to the authors' original vision; defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz called the removal "unacceptable" (Notes from Poland).

Red flags: 2023 audit criticism of the Westerplatte grants
- The Supreme Audit Office (NIK), reporting in August 2023 on 2019 to September 2022, found the museum's finances properly run except for state money for the Westerplatte branch: by mid-2022 only 12.7 per cent (PLN 6,146,600) of the sums planned for 2020 and 2021 had been spent (NIK audit).
- NIK judged the minister's carrying of 2021 funds into 2022 "nierzetelne" (unreliable; translation), formally lawful but a way around budget rules (NIK audit).

Red flags: 2025 freeze of Westerplatte investment funds
- On 4 April 2025 the culture minister froze PLN 82,915,000 granted to the museum for the Westerplatte programme in the 2025 budget, citing delays (Ministry of Culture answer to Sejm interpellation 10553, July 2025).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Art press coverage centres on the 2017 government takeover and the rewriting of the exhibition.
1. The Art Newspaper: Julia Michalska, "Outcry over Polish government's changes to Second World War museum" (2017)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/outcry-over-polish-government-s-changes-to-second-world-war-museum
- Reports the academics' case: the original concept joined Poland's special role with a universal account of suffering, and the changes risk turning the museum into a propaganda institution.
- A museum spokesman answered that Polish people want the museum they financed to tell their story.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 5 April 2017 the Supreme Administrative Court upheld culture minister Piotr Gliński's order merging the museum with the Museum of Westerplatte; Machcewicz, a former adviser to Tusk, was dismissed on 6 April and Karol Nawrocki became acting director. Supporters of Machcewicz said the merger was a pretext to remove him (Museums Journal).
- In November 2017 a film on the long-term effects of the war was replaced with an animated film on 20th-century Polish history; about 500 academics signed an open letter calling the changes "barbaric" interference (The Art Newspaper).
- Four exhibition authors, Machcewicz, Majewski, Janusz Marszalec and Wnuk, sued. In October 2020 the Gdańsk Regional Court dismissed 26 of their 28 demands; on 17 April 2023 the Warsaw Court of Appeal dismissed their appeal (Ministry of Culture statement, 2023).
- Nawrocki, later head of the Institute of National Remembrance, was sworn in as President of Poland on 6 August 2025 (Notes from Poland).
- In July 2025 the Museum of Gdańsk opened "Nasi chłopcy" (Our Boys), on Pomeranians conscripted into Hitler's army, in collaboration with this museum and the Polish Academy of Sciences. President Andrzej Duda called it a historical falsehood and a moral provocation; the culture ministry accused critics of spreading "disinformation" (Notes from Poland).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean state money. The weak point is political control: each change of government has brought a new director and a reworked exhibition.
