BAKU MUSEUM OF MODERN ART: Baku, Azerbaijan
ID: baku-museum-of-modern-art-baku
Website: https://moma.az

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern art at Yusif Safarov Street 5, Baku (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 20 March 2009 at the initiative of First Lady Mehriban Aliyeva (Wikipedia).
- Aida Mahmudova has been its director since 2012; she is also the founder of YARAT, the Baku contemporary art organisation (Wikipedia, Aida Mahmudova page).
- Building: Wikipedia credits the artist Altay Sadigzade as chief designer and architect. The design avoids halls with corners and uses open passages and walls that meet at different angles, joined by metallic structures and the colour white.
- Collection: over 800 works by Azerbaijani painters and sculptors, particularly avant-garde art of the 1960s and 1970s; Wikipedia adds works by Dalí, Picasso and Chagall from private collectors.
- Wikipedia also describes a wider "eco-cultural zone" conceived by Thomas Krens, former Guggenheim Foundation director, and planned to include a Frank Gehry skyscraper and a walkway over the Caspian Sea.

FUNDING
Baku Museum of Modern Art: funded by a ruling-family foundation: the Heydar Aliyev Foundation
- The Heydar Aliyev Foundation, which the First Lady heads, funded the museum (Wikipedia).
- OCCRP describes the foundation as a non-governmental organisation set up and run by the family of President Ilham Aliyev (2015).

WIDER CONTEXT
- OCCRP and its RISE Project partners (Roxana Jipa, Victor Ilie, Daniel Bojin, 4 December 2015): the Heydar Aliyev Foundation's Bucharest headquarters, a house that cost 6.5 million euros, is provided free of charge by the Romanian company Curzon Properties, whose owners are hidden behind offshore companies. The Seychelles company Asbet Ltd paid the full price. Asbet's majority stake passed in March 2009 to Ciaba Ltd, a British Virgin Islands company that documents obtained by OCCRP show was owned by Arzu Aliyeva, the president's younger daughter. The article is about the foundation and does not mention the museum.
- Hyperallergic (Nevdon Jamgochian, "Artwashing a Dictatorship", 28 February 2021, https://hyperallergic.com/artwashing-a-dictatorship/): argues the Aliyev family uses art and cultural institutions to repair its international image. The author writes that YARAT is "almost certainly funded by the state" and that it is "impossible to see how this organization is funded in any way but through the Aliyevs", which is the author's own inference; the article says Mahmudova claimed an unnamed bank backs YARAT. It names Mahmudova as the dictator's niece who runs YARAT and does not name the Baku Museum of Modern Art.
- The Art Newspaper (Anna Sansom, "Artist demands Baku art centre pulls his exhibition for using it as a 'propaganda tool' in Azerbaijan-Armenia conflict", 30 October 2020, https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/10/30/artist-demands-baku-art-centre-pulls-his-exhibition-for-using-it-as-a-propaganda-tool-in-azerbaijan-armenia-conflict): the artist Ahmet Ogut accused YARAT of using his exhibition banner as a propaganda tool; YARAT's website said the show closed on 28 October 2020, about three weeks early. The article is about YARAT and does not name the museum.
- Hyperallergic (Dorian Batycka, 2 November 2020) calls Mahmudova, the founder of YARAT, the niece of Ilham Aliyev; Wikipedia calls her a niece of Mehriban Aliyeva.
- Connection to the museum: Mahmudova, the founder of YARAT, has directed the Baku Museum of Modern Art since 2012 (Wikipedia, Aida Mahmudova page).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's named funder is the Heydar Aliyev Foundation, a foundation set up and run by the family of President Ilham Aliyev and headed by the First Lady (Wikipedia, OCCRP). Its director, Aida Mahmudova, founded YARAT, which Hyperallergic and The Art Newspaper have covered.
