CHRISTCHURCH ART GALLERY: Christchurch, New Zealand
ID: christchurch-art-gallery
Website: https://christchurchartgallery.org.nz (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Public art gallery, Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū, at the corner of Worcester Boulevard and Montreal Street (gallery about page); Wikipedia says the Christchurch City Council funds and owns it.
- Opened on 10 May 2003, replacing the Robert McDougall Art Gallery, which opened in 1932 in the Botanic Gardens with a £25,000 gift from Robert E. McDougall and closed on 16 June 2002 (gallery history page).
- The council committed $5 million in 1995 to acquire a site, and bought the former Sheraton Hotel site in 1996 (gallery history page).
- Building: an architectural competition was launched in 1998 and The Buchan Group's design was selected (gallery history page); the facade is a glass sculpture wall of 2,184 panels (Art New Zealand).
- After the 2010 and February 2011 earthquakes the building served as Civil Defence headquarters and reopened on 19 December 2015 after refurbishment (Wikipedia). Blair Jackson has been director since 2018.
- Visiting: free entry, open seven days 10am to 5pm, until 9pm on Wednesdays. Wheelchair access, accessible toilets, two loan wheelchairs, a lift and underground parking; a cafe (The Thirsty Peacock) and the Design Store are on site.

FUNDING
Christchurch Art Gallery: mainly public: Christchurch City Council, plus a foundation and corporate partners
- The gallery is a council unit and is funded by the Christchurch City Council (Wikipedia). No reporting found of a current annual council figure.
- The 2003 building and land cost $51.78 million. The council paid the main contractor, C. Lund and Son, $35.544 million, which was $261,390 over budget; community fundraising, central government grants and other donations contributed $14.3 million (Christchurch City Council press release via Scoop, 16 December 2003).
- The Christchurch Art Gallery Foundation raises money for acquisitions (gallery support page). Its credit lines show Bill Hammond's Bone Yard Open Home, Cave Painting 4, Convocation of Eagles (2008), bought in 2021 with assistance from the Foundation, Friends of Christchurch Art Gallery, Lyttelton Port Company and 97 other individuals, and Yona Lee's Fountain in Transit (2024), commissioned by the Foundation for the building's twentieth anniversary (Foundation site).
- Partners named by the gallery: JBWere is Foundation Partner and Anthony Harper is Major Partner. Partners are Bathgate Design, Digilab and Hotel Montreal; supporters include Curiosity Gin, Greystone Wines and Three Boys Brewery (gallery support page). No amounts are published.
- No reporting found of a board or annual accounts for the gallery itself; the Foundation names no trustees on its home page.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found. The one art-press piece read, from the opening year, mixes praise with criticism of the building and of one gallery space.
1. Art New Zealand: Gina Irish, "Exhibitions Christchurch" (issue 108; the page gives no date, and the content points to 2003)
URL: https://art-newzealand.com/?p=4914
- Calls the glass sculpture wall "the focus of awe and criticism" and says new debates challenge The Buchan Group's design.
- Says Virginia King's Antarctic Heart is squeezed into the "pokey" Tait Electronics Gallery and loses the fluidity it had at the Dowse; describes the entrance to the Te Puawai o Ngai Tahu space as dim, eerie and "deliberately unnerving"; and says reception of the inaugural exhibition was mixed.
- Also praises the permanent collection galleries as a clear improvement. It criticises the building and spaces, not the funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is council rates, a charitable foundation and local corporate partners; no funder with a documented controversy was found. The weak points are that the council's current funding figure and the gallery's accounts were not found, and that partner amounts are not published. Watch the Foundation and the partner tiers.
