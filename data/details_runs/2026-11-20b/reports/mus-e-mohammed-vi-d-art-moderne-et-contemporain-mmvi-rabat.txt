MUSÉE MOHAMMED VI D'ART MODERNE ET CONTEMPORAIN (MMVI): Rabat, Morocco
ID: mus-e-mohammed-vi-d-art-moderne-et-contemporain-mmvi-rabat
Website: https://fnm.ma/museums/31 (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of modern and contemporary art at 2 Avenue Moulay Hassan, Rabat, which the Fondation Nationale des Musées (FNM) calls the first museum in Morocco devoted entirely to modern and contemporary art (stated by the FNM).
- King Mohammed VI inaugurated it on 7 October 2014, and it opened to the public on 9 October with "100 ans de création", a survey of Moroccan art from 1914 to 2014 (Le360, 2024; Yabiladi, 2014; Bladi, 2014). Construction began in 2005 and the opening was first planned for 2011 (Bladi, 2014).
- Building: 6,813 m2 over three floors at the corner of avenues Moulay El Hassan and Allal Ben Abdellah, designed by Karim Chakor after the monumental administrative buildings of the French protectorate (Bladi, Fouad Boumnadel, 2014).
- Collection: more than 500 paintings and about ten sculptures, added to by FNM purchases, donations and long-term loans (stated by the FNM).
- Direction: art historian Nadia Sabri, president of AICA Morocco, succeeded Abdelaziz El Idrissi as director in October 2025; El Idrissi, director since the 2014 opening, became head of the FNM's museums department (H24info, Infomédiaire, quoting an FNM statement). Mehdi Qotbi presides over the FNM (FNM).
- Programme: past loans came from the Centre Pompidou (2018), the Musée d'Orsay (2019) and the Société Générale collection ("110 ans, 110 oeuvres", 15 October 2023 to 21 January 2024). In 2026 it showed Younes Rahmoun (12 February to 30 March) and the retrospective "Mohamed Melehi: Œuvre en héritage" (from 29 April); "Horizon(s) en mouvement" (from June 2025) and a show for the 50th anniversary of the Green March (from 6 November 2025) are listed as current (FNM).
- Visiting: open 10:00 to 18:00, closed Tuesdays. Admission 30 DH for Moroccan adults, 15 DH for Moroccans under 18, 60 DH for foreign visitors (FNM).

FUNDING
Musée Mohammed VI d'Art Moderne et Contemporain: mainly public: built by the Fonds Hassan II and the Ministry of Culture, run by the state's museums foundation
- Construction cost more than 200 million dirhams, provided by the Fonds Hassan II pour le développement économique et social; the Ministry of Culture, as project owner, contributed 44 million dirhams (Bladi, 2014).
- The FNM runs the museum. Law No. 01-09, promulgated on 18 April 2011, created it as a non-profit body with legal personality and financial autonomy to manage national museums for the state (FNM).
- FNM president Mehdi Qotbi said in 2024 that the foundation, as a public institution, relies on state subsidies and museum ticket revenue, with partnerships with private actors for renovations, exhibitions and heritage work (Le360, Hajar Kharroubi, 30 July 2024).
- Single shows: the 2023 to 2024 exhibition drew on the Société Générale collection (FNM); the 2026 Melehi retrospective was organised with the support of the artist's rights holders (FNM press release).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. Moroccan press covered a censorship complaint in 2014 and attacked the programming in 2018.
1. Le360: Qods Chabaa, "Polémique autour d'une exposition bâclée au musée Mohammed VI" (2018)
URL: https://fr.le360.ma/culture/polemique-autour-dune-exposition-baclee-au-musee-mohammed-vi-176588/
- Hossein Talal, son of the painter Chaïbia Talal, said he was approached about a solo tribute to his mother and learned of a three-artist group show from the press.
- The piece questions the show's scholarly basis and accuses Mehdi Qotbi of moving heaven and earth for foreign artists while doing the minimum for Moroccan ones (paraphrase of the French).
- Director Abdelaziz Idrissi answered that several people were consulted, no contract was signed, and the group show placed Chaïbia's work in the context of the 1960s.
2. Yabiladi: Ghita Ismaili, "Une oeuvre de Mounir Fatmi censurée ? Le Musée Mohammed VI de Rabat réfute" (2014)
URL: https://www.yabiladi.com/articles/details/30339/oeuvre-mounir-fatmi-censuree-musee.html
- A news report that sets out both sides and reaches no finding of its own.

WIDER CONTEXT
- October 2014: artist Mounir Fatmi said on Facebook that his installation "Face au Silence", a homage to Mehdi Ben Barka, was not shown at the 7 October inauguration, and that, according to visitors, the management had removed political works and works about the body or nudity (Yabiladi).
- The museum told Yabiladi the work was never censored and had been on view since the public opening on 9 October; a technical problem stopped the video on inauguration day and was fixed the next day.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is clean Moroccan public money, and no funder belongs to a flagged industry. The record carries no current red flag.
