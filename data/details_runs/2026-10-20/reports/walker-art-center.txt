WALKER ART CENTER: Minneapolis, USA
ID: walker-art-center
Website: https://www.walkerart.org/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on Lowry Hill in Minneapolis, with the Minneapolis Sculpture Garden across the street; the campus covers 17 acres (Wikipedia).
- Grew from the lumber baron Thomas Barlow Walker's private collection, which he began showing to the public for free at his home in 1879; the Walker Art Galleries opened in 1927 and the Walker Art Center opened in January 1940 with Works Progress Administration help (Wikipedia).
- Named for T.B. Walker.
- Original building by Edward Larrabee Barnes, opened in 1971 and expanded in 1984; Barnes also designed the Sculpture Garden (1988). Herzog & de Meuron designed the expansion that opened in April 2005 (Wikipedia).
- On site: 65,000 square feet of exhibition space, the McGuire Theater, the Walker Cinema, a library and archives (stated by the Walker); the Sculpture Garden is free and open daily from 6 am to midnight.
- Open Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10 am to 5 pm and Thursday 10 am to 9 pm; closed Monday and Tuesday. Adults $18, seniors $16, students $12; free for under-19s, EBT cardholders, members and citizens of Tribal Nations, and free on Thursday nights and the first Saturday. Elevators, lifts and wheelchairs are available.

FUNDING
Walker Art Center: corporate-sponsored and privately endowed: Principal Foundation, U.S. Bank, Ameriprise, Best Buy, Cargill Foundation, 3M and Bloomberg Philanthropies, plus a state arts grant
- The Walker's corporate support page (undated, page footer 2026) lists Bloomberg Philanthropies and Principal Foundation at $100,000 and above; Ameriprise Financial and U.S. Bank and U.S. Bank Foundation at $50,000 to $99,000.
- At $25,000 to $49,000: 3M, Augeo, Best Buy, the Cargill Foundation, The Creative Partners Group, Faegre Drinker Foundation, Pohlad Companies, RBC Wealth Management, Thomson Reuters, Thrivent and United Health Foundation.
- Bloomberg Philanthropies also appears under in-kind gifts, alongside Google, Minnesota Public Radio and the Star Tribune. No amount or purpose is given for either listing.
- The 2005 expansion was financed by a capital campaign: $77 million raised from 120 individuals, corporations and foundations toward a $92 million expansion, announced 12 May 2004 (stated by the Walker). The release names no donors.
- The Walker receives a grant from the Minnesota State Arts Board, funded through the state legislature (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against Bloomberg Philanthropies aimed at the Walker.

Red flags: protests aimed at the Walker
- Scaffold, 2017: about 100 protesters gathered outside the Sculpture Garden to demand removal of Sam Durant's "Scaffold", which included a gallows evoking the 1862 hanging of 38 Dakota men. The Walker delayed the garden's reopening a week, then agreed to dismantle the work after mediation led by Dakota elders (Smithsonian Magazine, MPR News).
- Director: Olga Viso announced in November 2017 that she would leave at the end of the year; ArtsJournal reported four sources close to the board tying the exit to staff turnover and the Scaffold protests. The Walker gave the completed capital campaign as its reason.
- Union rally, 2025: about 100 people rallied on 10 April 2025 for the Walker Worker Union, which says union president Michelle Maser and secretary Gabriela Bruner were let go in March because of their union role. The Walker disputes the union-busting claim and says it restructured visitor and gallery teams (MPR News).
- The same union says gallery assistants lost the right to sit unless they provide a doctor's note; the Walker says requirements are disclosed at hiring and accommodations exist (MPR News).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2017 Scaffold episode and on labour conditions; the pieces found are mostly about how the Walker handled the protest. No criticism of the Walker's funders found in art media.
1. Hyperallergic: Jillian Steinhauer, "Sam Durant Doesn't Need Defending" (2017)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/383929/sam-durant-doesnt-need-defending/
- Argues that critics who cast Durant as a victim misread the power balance between the Walker and the Dakota.
- Names the Walker and defends its decision to mediate and dismantle the work; does not criticise the Walker's handling.
2. ArtsJournal: Matthew Westphal, "Walker Art Center's Director Steps Down After Difficult Year And Tensions With Board" (2017)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=135724
- Reports four sources close to the board saying Viso's departure followed a months-long process driven by high staff turnover.
- Presents the Scaffold protests as one factor. Original outlet not named on the page.
3. MPR News: Alex V. Cipolle, union rally report (2025)
URL: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/11/union-rally-targets-walker-art-center
- Reports the union's claims on firings, hours and seating, and the Walker's denial. The Walker declined an interview.
- A news report, not an art-press critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The Walker's money comes from named Minnesota corporations, foundations and a state grant, and the only name to know is Bloomberg Philanthropies at $100,000 and above, with no campaign found against it at the Walker. The weak points are the 2017 Scaffold protest and the 2025 labour dispute. Worth watching: the amounts behind the tier lists, which the Walker does not publish with a year.
