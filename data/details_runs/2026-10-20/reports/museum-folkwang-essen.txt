MUSEUM FOLKWANG: Essen, Germany
ID: museum-folkwang-essen
Website: https://www.museum-folkwang.de (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Essen with collections of 19th- and 20th-century art, photography, prints and drawings, German posters, and ancient and non-European art; its key work is Caspar David Friedrich's "Mountain Landscape with Rainbow" (Wikipedia).
- Karl Ernst Osthaus began collecting in 1897 and opened the Folkwang Museum in Hagen in 1902. In 1922 a consortium of Essen and Ruhr companies and private individuals acquired the collection, the Folkwang-Museumsverein was founded, and the collection merged with Essen's municipal art museum to open as Museum Folkwang Essen on 29 October (museum chronicle).
- The name comes from Fólkvangr, the afterlife meadow of Norse myth (Wikipedia).
- In 1937 the National Socialists confiscated more than 1,400 works as "degenerate art" (museum chronicle).
- David Chipperfield won the architecture competition in 2007; his building added 16,000 square metres and opened in January 2010 (Wikipedia; museum chronicle).
- The Deutsche Plakat Museum holds about 340,000 posters, and the photography collection holds more than 50,000 photographs (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday and Friday 10:00 to 20:00, closed Monday. Admission to the permanent collection is free on all opening days; special exhibitions cost €16 (Courbet, standard) with concessions at €12. The pages read give no accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museum Folkwang: mixed: city of Essen and the Krupp foundation, with corporate and savings-bank sponsors
- The Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach Foundation decided in 2006 to finance the new building as sole funder (the foundation's culture page; museum chronicle). Wikipedia's article on the foundation gives the amount as €55 million.
- The same foundation offered free admission to the permanent collection from 2015; in 2020 the City of Essen and the foundation agreed to keep it free permanently (museum chronicle).
- The Folkwang-Museumsverein, founded in 1922 and with about 400 members, co-owns the collections with the city of Essen (Wikipedia). The museum chronicle mentions a 1922 contract with the city on shared ownership and support, without amounts.
- Eleven sponsors named on the museum's sponsors page, undated except where stated: Allbau GmbH, Bankhaus Bauer, E.ON SE, Evonik Industries AG, GENO BANK, Merck Finck Privatbankiers AG, NATIONAL-BANK, RWE AG, Sparkasse Essen, Sparkassen-Kulturfonds des Deutschen Sparkassen- und Giroverbandes and Stadtwerke Essen (listed as "since 2018").
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts or the museum's annual accounts.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (RWE and E.ON, sponsors)
- RWE, headquartered in Essen, generated about 32% of its 2019 electricity from lignite and 9% from hard coal, and in 2018 was reported as Europe's largest carbon dioxide emitter. Protests have targeted its Hambach surface mine since 2012 (Wikipedia).
- E.ON, also headquartered in Essen, deals in electricity and natural gas, bought the gas company Ruhrgas in 2003 and announced in November 2014 that it would exit fossil energy by spinning generation into Uniper. It still holds 50% of Enerjisa, which owns coal power in Turkey (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a campaign against these sponsors at the museum.

Red flags: Krupp foundation money (forced labour and arms)
- The Krupp foundation was created from the fortune of Alfried Krupp. He was convicted in 1948 at the Krupp Trial of using forced labourers and plundering economic assets in occupied countries, and sentenced to twelve years and forfeiture of all assets; in 1951 US High Commissioner John McCloy commuted the sentence and restored his property (Wikipedia).
- The foundation holds 20.93% of thyssenkrupp AG, its largest shareholder (Wikipedia, foundation page). thyssenkrupp holds 51% of ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems, which makes frigates, corvettes and submarines for the German and other navies (Wikipedia).
- The foundation commissioned the historian Eckart Conze in 2022 to research Krupp's role in the Nazi era; a study was published in July 2023 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: Protest over a cancelled exhibition section (2023)
- The museum cancelled the Afrofuturism section of the exhibition "We Is Future", due to open on 24 November 2023, after curator Anaïs Duplan's pro-Palestine posts. Director Peter Gorschlüter called some posts "unacceptable" for not acknowledging the Hamas attack; a spokesperson cited a 10 November post supporting the BDS movement, which the Bundestag has labelled antisemitic (Hyperallergic).
- The museum said the decision was not about the exhibition's artistic content (Hyperallergic; Jüdische Allgemeine, 16 November 2023).
- CIMAM, the international committee of modern art museums, published a statement on 15 January 2024 opposing the cancellation and treating it as part of wider censorship of artists who support Palestinians.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns one decision, the 2023 cancellation. No criticism found in art media of the sponsors or the Krupp foundation's money.
1. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "German Museum Cancels Afrofuturism Show Over Curator's Pro-Palestine Posts" (2023)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/856774/german-museum-cancels-afrofuturism-show-over-curators-pro-palestine-posts/
- Reports the cancellation came a day after a member of Duplan's studio team travelled to Essen to help install the section, and that the museum had paid for travel and accommodation.
- Duplan said the museum had not started a real conversation before cancelling and called its approach "really repressive". Most of the artists in the section asked for their works to be returned.
- Names Gorschlüter and the museum's spokesperson position that the decision was not artistic.
2. CIMAM: "Cancellation and censorship in times of war" (2024)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/cancellation-and-censorship-in-times-of-war/
- Argues museums should stay spaces for free artistic expression in crises and calls the Bundestag resolution "disputed and legally not binding".
- A museum-professionals' body rather than an art outlet; takes the details of the case from an Instagram account named archive_of_silence.

BOTTOM LINE
The building and the free admission are paid by the Krupp foundation, whose fortune came from a company whose owner was convicted of using forced labour and which still holds a fifth of thyssenkrupp, a maker of naval vessels. The sponsor list includes RWE and E.ON, both Essen utilities. Worth watching: the foundation's role in running costs and how the museum handles later disputes over curators' political statements.
