GALERÍA DE LA RAZA: San Francisco, USA
ID: galeria-de-la-raza-san-francisco
Website: https://www.galeriadelaraza.org

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit Xicane/Latine art space in San Francisco's Mission District, with two sites: Studio 24 at 2779 Folsom Street (education annex and offices) and Studio 16 at 2840 16th Street (main gallery and performance space, listed as under construction).
- Founded in 1970 by Chicano artists and activists; it began in a storefront at 14th Street and Valencia, and in 1972 moved to 24th Street and Bryant (stated by the space). Wikipedia names Rupert García, Peter Rodríguez and Ralph Maradiaga among the founders.
- Peter Rodríguez named the Galería (stated by the space); René Yañez became its first artistic director and Maradiaga its first administrative director (Wikipedia).
- Stayed at 2857 24th Street for 46 years. In June 2018 it received notice of a 100% rent increase, effective August 2018, and said it would give up the main gallery space (Mission Local, Julian Mark, 12 October 2018); Wikipedia says it vacated in November 2018.
- Studio 16 covers 6,882 square feet in the American Indian Cultural District; as of April 2023 the core "warm-shell" structure was built and tenant improvements had begun (stated by the space, Mighty Cause campaign page).
- Programmes: exhibitions and performance, an artist fellowship, the ReGen Artist Fund and a Youth Media Program started in 2003 (stated by the space); the Digital Mural Project now produces computer-generated images for the Bryant Street billboard instead of painted murals (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Studio 24 is open Tuesday to Friday, 12:00 to 6:00 PM, and on Saturdays during public events.

FUNDING
Galería de la Raza: mainly donations and grants: a 501(c)(3) non-profit whose revenue is almost entirely contributions
- Legal name on the tax filings is Galeria Studio 24 (EIN 94-2495604). For fiscal 2024 revenue was $1,814,748, 99.9% of it from contributions, against expenses of $1,242,133 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Revenue grew from $153,200 in 2019 and $500,538 in 2022 to $1,911,409 in 2023 (ProPublica).
- In 2016 revenue was about $127,000 against expenses of $167,403; revenue had been nearly $500,000 in 2013 (Mission Local, Julian Mark, 29 October 2018).
- Acquisition of the two current sites came through "complex community benefit agreements, private sector and local government investments" (stated by the space). It names no funder or amount.
- In 2018 the gallery was negotiating with the Mission Economic Development Agency and Tenderloin Neighborhood Development Corporation to rent, and possibly buy, ground-floor space in their planned 143-unit affordable housing project at 1990 Folsom (Mission Local, Julian Mark, 12 October 2018).
- Mayor London Breed said she would direct the Office of Economic and Workforce Development and the Arts Commission to explore options for a fair lease for the gallery in October 2018 (KQED, Marissa Shieh, 30 October 2018).

BOTTOM LINE
Galería de la Raza is a community non-profit funded almost wholly by contributions, with revenue of $1.81 million in fiscal 2024. The gallery left its 24th Street home in 2018 after a 100% rent increase and a dispute over accessibility work (Mission Local). It says its two current sites came through community benefit agreements and private sector and local government investments.
