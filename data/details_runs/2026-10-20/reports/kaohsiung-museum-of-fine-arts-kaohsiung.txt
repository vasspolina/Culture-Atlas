KAOHSIUNG MUSEUM OF FINE ARTS: Kaohsiung, Taiwan
ID: kaohsiung-museum-of-fine-arts-kaohsiung
Website: https://www.kmfa.gov.tw/

OVERVIEW
- Municipal art museum in Gushan District, Kaohsiung, set in the Neiweipi Cultural Park; Wikipedia gives the museum about 33,000 square metres and 1994 as its year of establishment.
- Founded by the Kaohsiung City Government. Wikipedia reports that the Kaohsiung Bureau of Cultural Affairs has administered it since 2003; the museum's own sponsorship page says it became an administrative corporation in 2017.
- Building: Wikipedia says the galleries span four floors and that sunlight reaches the Sculpture Hall through a large skylight.
- On site: exhibition galleries, a Children's Art Museum and an Art Ecological Park (museum's site).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 9:30 am to 5:30 pm, closed Mondays and Lunar New Year's Eve; ticket sales run 9:30 to 17:00. General admission is NT$90 and NT$45 for college students and above, and for visitors aged 65 and over, active military and veterans on weekends and public holidays. Entry is free for high school students, children aged 16 and under, people with disabilities and one companion, Kaohsiung residents on weekdays, and Friends of KMFA members. Special exhibitions need a separate ticket.
- Accessibility: the museum runs a dedicated accessibility site, ud.kmfa.gov.tw.

FUNDING
Kaohsiung Museum of Fine Arts: city-founded administrative corporation, with named corporate, foundation and individual sponsors
- The museum's sponsorship page states that since becoming an administrative corporation in 2017 it cannot rely only on public funding, and invites businesses and individuals to sponsor exhibitions, art education, cultural equity, park arts facilities and the programme 生態暨景觀循環共生計畫 (title translated: "ecology and landscape circular symbiosis"). Sponsors are named in the annual report.
- It publishes yearly donation ledgers for 2017 to 2025. The 2025 ledger (ROC year 114) names these sponsors:
- 台灣應用材料公司 (Applied Materials Taiwan): NT$600,000 for cultural equity outreach.
- 國際扶輪3510地區 (Rotary International District 3510): NT$630,000 for the exhibition guide booklet and learning sheets of the special exhibition 《馮．沃爾夫的花園堡壘》 (title translated: "Von Wolff's Garden Fortress"), and NT$750,000 for admission tickets for rural students to the same exhibition.
- 財團法人山藝術文教基金會: NT$1,980,000 across four exhibitions (NT$1,500,000 for "快樂島 日常的史詩", NT$330,000 for "相忘於江湖：李德 x 熊秉明", NT$100,000 for "生活藏寶圖", NT$50,000 for "園區 2025張新丕個展").
- 楠弘集團: NT$1,000,000 for the same special exhibition.
- 高雄市大愛扶輪社: NT$1,000,000 for the same exhibition.
- 財團法人勇源教育發展基金會: NT$500,000 for the same exhibition.
- 富隆船舶股份有限公司: NT$300,000 for the exhibition "繁花夢露 張立曄的靈光樂園".
- 財團法人京城社會福利慈善事業基金會: NT$300,000 for an outreach programme for visitors with intellectual and visual disabilities.
- 明安國際企業股份有限公司: NT$200,000, and 財團法人建弘文教基金會: NT$100,000, both for the exhibition of Chen Cheng-hsiung (陳正雄) and museum operations; 聚和國際股份有限公司: NT$100,000 for a special environmental sustainability prize in the Kaohsiung Award.
- 43 core members paid NT$860,000 in annual fees. Individual donors appear with partly masked names, with gifts from NT$10,000 to NT$1,900,000.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One press episode, about a visitor rule rather than funding or programme.
1. United Daily News (聯合報): Song Yuan-chang (宋原彰), "孩童模仿畢卡索真跡遭制止引眾怒 高美館緊急改規定：開放速寫" (17 August 2024; title translated: child copying a Picasso original stopped sparks outrage, museum urgently changes rules to allow sketching)
URL: https://udn.com/news/story/6885/8167768
- During the special exhibition "Instant" (《瞬間》) staff stopped a child who was sketching a Picasso original on a tablet and said the on-site rules applied.
- Commenters named by the paper, the curator Mickeyelk Gesne, the physician Li Chia-yen (李佳燕) and the Facebook page 高雄好過日, contrasted the rule with museums such as the Chimei Museum in Tainan that allow sketching, and the page said the museum treated children coldly. The cultural worker Wei Tsung-chou (魏聰洲) said copying, which needs an application and review, differs from freehand drawing, and called for better staff training.
- The museum acknowledged lapses in staff conduct and venue management and changed its guidelines to allow personal sketching with pencils, drawing boards and sketchbooks smaller than A3. The Department of Culture pledged to strengthen volunteer training.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum was founded by the Kaohsiung City Government (Wikipedia) and, as an administrative corporation since 2017, asks businesses and individuals for sponsorship. Its 2025 ledger names its sponsors with amounts: Applied Materials Taiwan, Rotary District 3510, the 財團法人山藝術文教基金會 art foundation and other education and welfare foundations.
