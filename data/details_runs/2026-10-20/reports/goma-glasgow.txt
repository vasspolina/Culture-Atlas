GOMA (GALLERY OF MODERN ART): Glasgow, UK
ID: goma-glasgow
Website: https://www.glasgowlife.org.uk/museums/venues/gallery-of-modern-art

OVERVIEW
- Glasgow's main gallery of contemporary art, in Royal Exchange Square in the city centre (Wikipedia).
- Opened in 1996; run by Glasgow Life, the charity that manages the city's museums for Glasgow City Council, and owned by the council (Wikipedia).
- The building began as a townhouse built in 1778 for William Cunninghame of Lainshaw, a Glasgow tobacco merchant whose wealth came from the slave trade (Wikipedia). The Royal Bank of Scotland bought it in 1817 and it became the Royal Exchange; David Hamilton rebuilt it between 1827 and 1832 (Wikipedia).
- Glasgow District Libraries bought it for £105,000 in 1949 and it housed Stirling's Library from 1954, before its conversion to a gallery (Wikipedia). It is a Category A listed building (Wikipedia).
- On site: four galleries of contemporary art, a library with weekly classes and events, a shop, a cafe, a prayer room and free wifi (Glasgow Life).
- Niki de Saint Phalle made the mirrored pediment "Tympanum" (1996) and the mirrored vestibule (Wikipedia).
- In summer 2023 the gallery hosted Banksy's "Cut and Run", open 24 hours a day for ten weeks, with 180,000 visitors (Wikipedia). It drew 576,689 visitors in 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free entry, donations welcome. Open Monday to Thursday and Saturday 10:00 to 17:00, Friday and Sunday 11:00 to 17:00. Accessible toilet on the lower ground floor, assistance dogs welcome, a British Sign Language introduction online (Glasgow Life).

FUNDING
GoMA: mainly public: Glasgow City Council through Glasgow Life, with grants and trusts
- Glasgow Life receives a service fee from Glasgow City Council, about 60% of its income according to Wikipedia. Its 2021/22 budget was £123.4 million, including a £76.7 million service fee; in 2021 the council announced a £100 million financial guarantee for the charity (Wikipedia).
- Glasgow Life's 2025/26 "With thanks" list of supporters names Creative Scotland, the Scottish Government, the UK Government, Glasgow City Council, Art Fund, the National Lottery Heritage Fund, Museums Galleries Scotland, Historic Environment Scotland, the Contemporary Arts Society, the Henry Moore Foundation, the Baring Foundation, the John Ellerman Foundation, Tate, Bloomberg Philanthropies and the Kingdom of the Netherlands, among more than 60 names that include trusts, companies and public bodies (Glasgow Life annual review page).
- The same page names a £2.3 million grant from the Scottish Government (November 2025) to decarbonise The Mitchell Library and £2.3 million from the National Lottery (February 2026) for the Sauchiehall Street Culture and Heritage District project.
- Cuts: in 2023 Glasgow Life set out to save £1.5 million from its museums budget by cutting 37 of 128 posts, after the council cut its service fee to help close a £49.3 million shortfall in the council's budget (Apollo, 28 June 2023).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 29 May 2026 The New Arab reported an open letter from more than 260 UK artists, published through Artists for Palestine UK, urging five UK theatres (National Theatre, Royal Shakespeare Company, Young Vic, Royal Court, Almeida) to end partnerships with Bloomberg Philanthropies. The letter says Bloomberg Philanthropies is "complicit in grave war crimes" because of its involvement with Israeli settlements and urges the theatres to find other funding.
- Connection: the article does not name Glasgow Life or GoMA. Bloomberg Philanthropies is named in Glasgow Life's 2025/26 supporters list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one piece found criticises Glasgow Life's and the council's cuts to Glasgow's museums, not GoMA's programme.
1. Apollo: Conrad Landin, "Glasgow's cuts will hamper its museums for years to come" (28 June 2023)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/glasgow-council-culture-cuts-burrell-unison/
- Argues that Glasgow Life's plan to cut 37 of 128 museum posts will overstretch staff and weaken loans and collections work.
- Says the arm's-length charity structure places the cuts beyond the democratic scrutiny that other council services get; Glasgow Life says more than half of the posts affected are vacant.
- Opens with queues at GoMA for "Banksy: Cut and Run"; the criticism is aimed at Glasgow Life and the council, not at GoMA.

BOTTOM LINE
GoMA is a council-owned gallery run by Glasgow Life, whose income comes mainly from a service fee from Glasgow City Council (Wikipedia), with grants from Creative Scotland, the Scottish and UK governments, the National Lottery Heritage Fund, Art Fund and charitable trusts. Bloomberg Philanthropies is named in Glasgow Life's 2025/26 supporters list; the May 2026 artists' letter against Bloomberg Philanthropies was addressed to five UK theatres.
