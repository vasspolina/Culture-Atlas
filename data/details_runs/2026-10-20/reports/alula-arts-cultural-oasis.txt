ALULA ARTS & CULTURAL OASIS: AlUla, Saudi Arabia
ID: alula-arts-cultural-oasis
Website: https://www.experiencealula.com (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- The arts and culture programme of AlUla, an oasis region in north-western Saudi Arabia that spans roughly 22,561 square kilometres (Royal Commission for AlUla). The name "AlUla Arts & Cultural Oasis" does not appear on the visitor site.
- Run by the Royal Commission for AlUla (RCU), founded in July 2017 and chaired since then by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (RCU website). Arab News (28 April 2021) says the Saudi Ministry of Finance set it up.
- Venues and projects: Maraya, a 45-minute guided tour from SAR 95 per person; the Desert X AlUla biennial, launched in 2020; Wadi AlFann, a planned 65 square kilometre land-art site where Ahmed Mater is building installations (Al-Monitor); and the AlUla Arts Festival with the Centre Pompidou (Finestre sull'Arte).
- Visiting: the visitor site gives a price only for the Maraya tour.

FUNDING
AlUla Arts & Cultural Oasis: state-funded: the Saudi government through the Royal Commission for AlUla
- Arab News (28 April 2021): Saudi Arabia put in $2 billion of seed funding for AlUla's development; $3.2 billion for priority infrastructure was expected through public-private partnerships; the $15 billion masterplan runs in three phases to 2035.
- Desert X AlUla was organised in collaboration with the RCU (The Art Newspaper, 17 January 2020; Hyperallergic, 8 October 2019).

Red flags: Gulf state body
- The RCU describes itself as a government body, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman since 2017 (RCU website). Desert X AlUla was organised in collaboration with the RCU (Hyperallergic, 8 October 2019; The Art Newspaper, 17 January 2020).

Red flags: protest against Desert X AlUla
- October 2019: three of Desert X's 14 board members, Ed Ruscha, Yael Lipschutz and Tristan Milanovich, resigned over Desert X's collaboration with the RCU. Hyperallergic (Hakim Bishara, 8 October 2019) says all three cited Saudi Arabia's human rights abuses and the killing of the journalist Jamal Khashoggi; Lipschutz wrote that Desert X "no longer reflected my values, my humanitarian values".
- 17 October 2019: Hyperallergic reports that the MaddocksBrown Foundation, a Los Angeles-based early donor that had given about $13,000 since 2017, pulled out. The headline ties the withdrawal to the Saudi collaboration.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism targets the Saudi state patronage behind AlUla's art, not individual works. Most dates from 2019.

- Reports the resignations and the reasons given. Lipschutz called the collaboration with the Saudi government "completely unethical". The RCU had not responded to the request for comment when the article ran (8 October 2019).

URL: https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2022/06/saudi-artists-marvel-surprise-patron-their-own-rulers
- Critics of the Saudi royal family call the projects "artwashing", citing Khashoggi's killing and the poet Ashraf Fayadh, sentenced to death for apostasy in 2015 and later to eight years.
- Mater welcomes state backing; AlDowayan says she has never been censored.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Critics of the Saudi royal family call state-backed art projects "artwashing" for a country that silences dissidents (Al-Monitor, 2022).
- The RCU, which the Crown Prince chairs, oversees AlUla, so its arts programme sits inside that critique.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind AlUla's art is Saudi government money, channelled through a commission chaired by the Crown Prince. The weak point is the state patronage, which drew three board resignations and a donor withdrawal at Desert X AlUla in 2019. Worth watching: who the private partners in the public-private partnerships Arab News reported in 2021 are.
