MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART AUSTRALIA (MCA): Sydney, Australia
ID: museum-of-contemporary-art-australia-mca
Website: https://www.mca.com.au

OVERVIEW
- Museum of contemporary art on the western side of Circular Quay in The Rocks, Sydney, on the site of two Commissariat Stores built with convict labour (Wikipedia).
- Its roots lie in the 1943 will of the expatriate Australian artist JW Power, who left money and works to the University of Sydney to educate Australians about contemporary art; the works toured as the Power Gallery of Contemporary Art (Wikipedia).
- After the Maritime Services Board moved out in 1989, the NSW Government donated the building to the museum. The University and the Power Bequest funded its restoration and it opened in 1991 as the Museum of Contemporary Art, Sydney (Wikipedia).
- The building is the former Maritime Services Board headquarters, a six-storey heritage-listed Stripped Classical and Art Deco structure designed by William Henry Withers; construction resumed in late 1944 and the offices opened eight years later. The Mordant Wing, designed by Sam Marshall, was added in 2012 (Wikipedia).
- Its collection focuses solely on Australian contemporary artists (MCA Annual Report 2024).
- Lorraine Tarabay chairs the board and Suzanne Cotter has been director since January 2022 (Wikipedia; MCA Annual Report 2024). A general admission charge began on 31 January 2025 (MCA Annual Report 2024); Wikipedia gives it as 20 dollars, with exemptions for Australian students and under-18s.

FUNDING
Museum of Contemporary Art Australia: independent nonprofit: donors, corporate partners and earned income, with state and federal grants
- Structure: Museum of Contemporary Art Limited is an independent not-for-profit organisation (Wikipedia). Its 2024 audited accounts show revenue from operating activities of 27,040,807 Australian dollars (MCA Annual Report 2024).
- Government: NSW Government recurrent funding of 4.3 million dollars was 14.7 percent of 2024 expenditure, and federal support through Creative Australia of 0.7 million dollars was a further 2.4 percent. Government partners named are Create NSW, Destination NSW and Placemaker NSW. The report says the MCA attracts much lower levels of funding than state-owned museums and self-generates about 85 percent of its revenue (MCA Annual Report 2024, Government section).
- Corporate: the chairman's foreword thanks Telstra, Qantas and Oceania Cruises. In 2024 Qantas concluded its partnership of more than 20 years as the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Collection Partner. Telstra, communication partner of 24 years, supported the Sydney International Art Series and Telstra Free Day. Oceania Cruises is listed as a major partner and Bloomberg as a corporate member (MCA Annual Report 2024, Supporters section).
- Philanthropy: the foreword names Ginny and Leslie Green, Adrian and Charlotte Mackenzie and the Tarabay Langley family. Principal Patrons include Laura Arrillaga-Andreessen and Marc Andreessen and the Balnaves Foundation, which backs a new annual commission series on the museum's lawn from 2025 (MCA Annual Report 2024).
- Deficit: the report gives an operating deficit of 2 million dollars for 2024, funded from cash reserves, and names admission charges, a new hospitality partner (The Big Group) and cost cuts as steps toward a balanced budget in 2025 (MCA Annual Report 2024).
- 2012 expansion: federal, state and city governments contributed about 27 million dollars and private benefactors about 7.5 million dollars, including gifts from Simon Mordant and his wife, after which the wing is named (Wikipedia).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mixed: the MCA reports self-generating about 85 percent of its revenue, with NSW and federal grants at about 17 percent of 2024 expenditure. Named funders are Telstra, Oceania Cruises, Bloomberg and private patrons, and Qantas ended a partnership of more than 20 years in 2024.
