NATIONAL MUSEUM OF THE HISTORY OF UKRAINE IN THE SECOND WORLD WAR: Kyiv, Ukraine
ID: national-museum-of-the-history-of-ukraine-in-the-second-world-war
Website: https://warmuseum.kyiv.ua (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Memorial complex on more than 11 hectares in Kyiv, dominated by the 102 metre Motherland Monument, with the main exhibition inside its pedestal (museum).
- Founded in 1974 in the Klovsky Palace (museum). Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev opened it at its current site on 9 May 1981; President Leonid Kuchma gave it national status by decree on 21 June 1996 (Wikipedia).
- Until July 2015 it was the National Museum of the History of the Great Patriotic War of 1941 to 1945; the minister of culture announced the change after the Verkhovna Rada outlawed that term in April 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Holds over 300,000 exhibits (Wikipedia). Since 2014 it has documented Russia's war on Ukraine; on 8 May 2022 it reopened with the exhibition "Ukraine Crucifixion", which later travelled to Riga, Tallinn, New York and Bratislava (museum).
- In 2023 it replaced the Soviet emblem on the monument's shield with the Ukrainian trident (museum). In January 2026 the Ministry of Culture said Soviet-era Russian-language inscriptions had been removed from its sculpture gallery; Director General Yurii Savchuk called them "unacceptable" in wartime (UNN).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 16:30, weekends 10:00 to 17:30, closed during air raid alerts. Free entry for listed groups, including soldiers, children of fallen defenders and museum staff, and for everyone on the last Tuesday of the month. Ascents to the monument's two observation decks cost extra; one is for adults only and not fully accessible (museum).

FUNDING
National Museum of the History of Ukraine in the Second World War: public: the state budget, under the Ministry of Culture, with company donations for the 2023 shield
- Operator: a state museum under the Ministry of Culture of Ukraine (museum).
- Running and repair costs: its 2026 procurement notes name the State Budget of Ukraine, special fund, as the source of money, and for one floor repair also a charitable fund (museum procurement notes, 2026).
- Own fundraising: its first public appeal seeks UAH 20 million for a 710 square metre space holding a Museum of Military Technologies, a Museum of Russian (Soviet) Propaganda and a conference hall (museum).
- Venue hire: its 320-seat cinema and concert hall and other spaces are rented for events (museum).
- The 2023 shield: the museum paid about UAH 600,000 for the December 2022 engineering study. Donor money came from Monobank, Oschadbank, PUMB, the BGV fund, Nova Poshta, PrivatBank, Ukrainian Agrarian Holding, Persha Pryvatna Brovarnia, KAN Development, OTP Bank, the Independent Association of Banks of Ukraine and the two fuel companies named under Red flags. Metinvest pledged UAH 2.5 million and 1,100 kg of steel; a ministry adviser said there was no information that it had paid, and Metinvest said it was meeting its obligations. An NGO, the Culture Resilience Agency, collected and paid out the donations (Ihor Orel, Forbes Ukraine, 9 August 2023; names transliterated and translated by the atlas).
- Cost: announced at UAH 28 million on 13 July 2023 and revised to UAH 22.9 million on 1 August (Forbes Ukraine). The ministry said no state budget money was used (Ukrainska Pravda, 26 July 2023).

Red flags: Oil and gas company Naftogaz among the 2023 shield donors
- Naftogaz of Ukraine gave money for the shield (Forbes Ukraine). Its own site describes its exploration and production unit as developing oil and gas fields and producing hydrocarbons, alongside gas storage and oil transit (Naftogaz).

Red flags: Fuel station chain WOG among the 2023 shield donors
- WOG gave money for the shield (Forbes Ukraine). It is a chain of filling stations, by its own account in a paid feature for its 25th year (WOG, Forbes Ukraine BrandVoice, October 2025).

Red flags: 2023 shield contract placed without a tender
- Forbes Ukraine reported on 9 August 2023 that the Ministry of Culture approached the general contractor, Osnova, directly and that no tender or competition was held.
- Savchuk told Forbes Ukraine the law did not require one because no budget money was used. Osnova's chief engineer said the contract gave the firm about UAH 22 million.
- Neither the technical documentation nor the cost estimate was public at the time; a ministry adviser promised to publish the estimate that week (Forbes Ukraine).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Culture minister Oleksandr Tkachenko submitted his resignation on 20 July 2023, amid the president's displeasure at his remarks on finishing the Holodomor Museum in wartime, anger at state funding of TV series and disputes over renewing the monument's symbols while money was needed at the front (Ekonomichna Pravda).
- Connection: the shield project was his ministry's.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum's running money is public and clean. Its one weak point is the 2023 shield project, for who paid and how the work was contracted.
