GALLERIE D'ITALIA: Milan, Italy
ID: gallerie-d-italia
Website: https://www.gallerieditalia.com (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum network owned by the bank Intesa Sanpaolo, with sites in Milan, Naples, Turin and Vicenza (Italian Wikipedia).
- Vicenza, in Palazzo Leoni Montanari, opened in 1999; Milan opened on 3 November 2011 in Piazza della Scala; Turin opened on 17 May 2022 in Piazza San Carlo; Naples moved from Palazzo Zevallos to the Palazzo del Banco di Napoli on via Toledo on 21 May 2022 (Italian Wikipedia).
- Milan joins three buildings: Palazzo Brentani, Palazzo Anguissola Antona Traversi and the former Banca Commerciale Italiana headquarters (stated by the space); Michele De Lucchi designed the Naples layout (Italian Wikipedia).
- Milan shows 19th-century art, including Fondazione Cariplo's collection, the "Cantiere del '900" and an open store in the old vault; Turin focuses on photography and holds the Publifoto archive; Naples has Caravaggio's "Martirio di sant'Orsola" (stated by the space; Italian Wikipedia).
- Michele Coppola is general director of the Gallerie and runs Intesa Sanpaolo's Progetto Cultura (Intesa Sanpaolo).
- Milan opens Tuesday to Sunday, 9:30am to 7pm; €6 full, €3 over-65s, free for under-26s, school groups and Intesa Sanpaolo customers; it runs programmes for visitors with motor, cognitive and sensory disabilities (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Gallerie d'Italia: corporate-owned: Intesa Sanpaolo
- Owned and run by Intesa Sanpaolo under its Progetto Cultura, started by chairman emeritus Giovanni Bazoli and part of a business plan that commits €1.5 billion to Italian communities by 2027 (Intesa Sanpaolo).
- The four museums drew more than 750,000 visitors in 2024, a figure stated by the bank.
- No reporting found of a museum budget, accounts or outside donors.

Red flags: owner's fossil fuel finance
- ReCommon, an environmental group, reported in April 2025 that Intesa's lending to coal, oil and gas rose 18% in 2024 to about $11 billion and its investments rose 16% to about $10 billion, with ENI the top client (L'Indipendente). These are ReCommon's figures.

Red flags: Extinction Rebellion occupation in Turin
- On 2 March 2025, the last day of the "American Nature" show, about 30 Extinction Rebellion activists entered with tickets and lay down in the room on fossil fuel damage, protesting the "harmful investments" of Intesa Sanpaolo, the museum's owner (LaPresse).

Red flags: privacy fines against the owner
- Italy's data protection authority fined Intesa Sanpaolo €31.8 million after an employee made more than 6,600 unjustified accesses to the data of 3,573 customers between February 2022 and April 2024, some of them public figures (QuiFinanza, March 2026).
- In March 2026 the same authority fined it €17.6 million for unlawfully processing the data of about 2.4 million customers moved to Isybank (Il Post).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The Extinction Rebellion action was covered by the general press, not the art press.

BOTTOM LINE
The museums are a bank's own cultural programme, paid for by Intesa Sanpaolo with no separate budget published. The weak points are the bank's fossil fuel lending, which drew a protest inside the Turin museum in 2025, and two 2026 privacy fines. Worth watching: whether the Turin site's climate shows keep attracting protest aimed at the owner.
