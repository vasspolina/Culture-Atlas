PUSHKINSKAYA-10 ART CENTRE: St Petersburg, Russia
ID: pushkinskaya-10-art-centre-st-petersburg
Website: https://p-10.ru

OVERVIEW
- Art commune and arts centre at Ligovsky Prospekt 53, St Petersburg, which calls itself the only non-state, self-governed, independent creative commune in Russia (The Blueprint, Pavel Gerasimenko, 9 July 2024, quoting the centre's site).
- The building is a large tenement completed in 1887 and 1888 for Count Pavel Shuvalov and his son; it was emptied at the end of 1988 before a planned renovation, and artists, musicians and theatre people moved in illegally (The Blueprint; T-J (t-j.ru), Asya Filatova, 5 August 2024).
- Residents occupied about 170 apartments, often without electricity, water or heat; talks with state bodies began in 1990, and a 1996 agreement provided for repairs and leasing about a third of the premises to artists for 49 years (T-J (t-j.ru)).
- Repairs finished in 1998, when the cultural centre opened and the Museum of Nonconformist Art was founded (T-J (t-j.ru)).
- T-J (t-j.ru) describes Yuly Rybakov and Sergei Kovalsky as founders and Evgeny Orlov as the director. The Manege exhibition page lists Vadim Voinov, Sergei Kovalsky, Evgeny Orlov and Yuly Rybakov as organisers of the art centre.
- On site: more than a dozen galleries, about 40 artists' workshops, the First National Rock Museum, the Samizdat A4 gallery and exhibition spaces called the Big Hall and the Small Hall (Lidenz, Rachel South, 5 September 2019).
- Visiting: the site says museums and galleries are open from 12:00 to 20:00, with tickets sold online through Timepad.

FUNDING
Pushkinskaya-10 Art Centre: self-funded: income from galleries, venues and tickets, in a building the city lends free for 49 years
- Radio Svoboda (Sofia Rogacheva, 4 May 2020) reports that the city provides the building for 49 years of free use on condition that the centre maintains and pays for it, and that the centre receives no help from the state apart from a small sum once a year, in summer, for the house festival.
- Income came from galleries, a café, a bar, three music clubs and ticket sales, which stopped when venues closed in March 2020; the centre ran online concerts and planned a charity art auction to cover utility bills (Radio Svoboda).
- The Manege exhibition "Pushkinskaya-10. Ark of the 21st Century" ran from 9 July to 13 September 2024 at the Manege Central Exhibition Hall with the support of the St Petersburg Committee for Culture, and the partnership Svobodnaya Kultura (Pushkinskaya-10) gave its collection to the Manege in March 2023 (Manege exhibition page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Pushkinskaya-10 is its own income from galleries, clubs, a café and tickets, with a building the city lends free for 49 years (Radio Svoboda, 4 May 2020). The 2024 Manege exhibition was supported by the St Petersburg Committee for Culture.
