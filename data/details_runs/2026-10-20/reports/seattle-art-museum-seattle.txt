OVERVIEW
- Art museum with three sites: the downtown museum, the Seattle Asian Art Museum in Volunteer Park and the free Olympic Sculpture Park on the waterfront (Wikipedia).
- Traces to the Seattle Fine Arts Society, which became the Art Institute of Seattle in 1931; Richard E. Fuller and his mother Margaret MacTavish Fuller gave 250,000 dollars to build the Volunteer Park museum, which opened on 23 June 1933 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Carl F. Gould of Bebb and Gould designed the 1933 building; Robert Venturi designed the downtown building that opened on 5 December 1991 at a cost of 62 million dollars; Brad Cloepfil designed the 2007 expansion (Wikipedia).
- The Olympic Sculpture Park, a 9-acre waterfront park, opened on 20 January 2007 (Wikipedia).
- The Volunteer Park building became the Seattle Asian Art Museum in 1994 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Wikipedia states indoor facilities are free on the first Thursday and first Saturday of each month and otherwise admission is a suggested, pay-what-you-can amount; the museum's own pages could not be opened to confirm hours.

FUNDING
Seattle Art Museum: private nonprofit: contributions, ticket sales and memberships
- Form 990, fiscal year ending June 2025: revenue 38.3 million dollars, expenses 44.4 million, contributions 17.7 million, net assets 378.8 million (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer; filed 15 May 2026).
- Contributions were 42.3 million dollars in fiscal 2022 and 10.8 million in 2024 (ProPublica).
- Government provides about 4% of funding, the rest from ticket sales and memberships (Wikipedia; the page gives no year).
- Founding donors: the Fuller family; more than a thousand works worth over 1 billion dollars were donated before the 2007 expansion (Wikipedia).
- After Washington Mutual's collapse the museum faced about 56 million dollars of accumulated debt and received grants from JPMorgan Chase and the Gates Foundation (Wikipedia).
- A SponsorPitch listing, a snapshot dated 6 November 2014, is headed "Boeing signs on as sponsor for Seattle Art Museum"; it names no exhibition, term or amount.
- Officers in the FY2025 filing: Scott Morris, Chair; Catherine Roche, President; Bert Valdman, Treasurer (ProPublica).
- No current corporate partner list could be verified: the museum's institutional partners page returned HTTP 403 and could not be opened. No reporting found of Bloomberg Philanthropies, Boeing (beyond the 2014 listing above) or Honeywell as partners.

Red flags: boycott called against the museum
- June 2021: the museum installed stone bollards in an entrance vestibule and contracted private security. By 13 January 2022 the staff collective Decolonize SAM was calling for a boycott, demanding removal of the bollards and an end to removing unhoused people from museum property; the article gives no start date for the call (Hyperallergic, 13 January 2022).
- The museum terminated its contract with the security firm Star Protection Agency after a 1 September 2021 incident in which, Decolonize SAM's documentation says, a Star guard paid an unhoused man five dollars to take an unhoused woman's belongings; as of January 2022 it was finalising a contract with a new firm (Hyperallergic).
- The museum said the security aimed to improve safety for everyone, and formed a Special Advisory Task Force on Homelessness (Hyperallergic).
- Security officers unionised and struck from March 2025 over wages and benefits (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum's funding. The art-press piece found concerns its treatment of unhoused people.
1. Hyperallergic: Amber Cortes, "In Seattle, Staff Call for a Boycott in Response to Museum's Reaction to Unhoused Community" (2022)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/705912/sam-staff-call-for-a-boycott-in-response-to-museums-response-to-unhoused-community/
- Staff say the bollards and outside guards were meant to push unhoused people away; staff member Rayna Mathis wrote the guards "demonizes an entire community that is already vulnerable and mistreated".
- Director Amada Cruz wrote "we are not looking to hire cops or soldiers" and defended the steps as protecting staff.
- Does not discuss funders.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a private nonprofit with a published Form 990 and mostly contributed income; no named sponsor could be verified this time. The weak points are the unconfirmed corporate partner list and the boycott call over the museum's treatment of unhoused neighbours, reported in January 2022.
