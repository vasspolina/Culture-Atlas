MOSCOW MUSEUM OF MODERN ART (MMOMA): Moscow, Russia
ID: moscow-museum-of-modern-art-mmoma-moscow
Website: https://mmoma.ru

OVERVIEW
- City museum of modern and contemporary art with several buildings in central Moscow: Petrovka 25, Ermolaevsky 17 and Gogolevsky Boulevard 10, plus the Zurab Tsereteli Museum-Workshop, the Vadim Sidur Museum and the Dmitry Nalbandyan Museum-Workshop (museum website).
- Opened in December 1999 on the initiative of Zurab Tsereteli, who donated the founding collection (Wikipedia, The Calvert Journal).
- The main building is the former Gubin mansion at 25 Petrovka Street, designed by Matvei Kazakov (Wikipedia).
- Zurab Tsereteli's grandson Vasili Tsereteli became executive director in 2001 or 2002 (The Calvert Journal gives 2001, Snob gives 2002); he was elected president of the Russian Academy of Arts on 19 May 2025 (Snob).
- Programmes include a School of Modern Art, a research library and a publishing programme (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: Petrovka 25, Ermolaevsky 17, Gogolevsky 10 and the Tsereteli workshop are open Tuesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 21:00, closed Monday. The Sidur and Nalbandyan museums are open Wednesday to Sunday, 14:00 to 21:00. Tickets are sold through mos.ru. The Gogolevsky 10 building was temporarily closed for technical reasons, according to a museum news item of 20 October 2026.

FUNDING
Moscow Museum of Modern Art: mainly public: the City of Moscow, through the Department of Culture
- The museum is a state budget institution of the Moscow Department of Culture, as its website footer states (ГБУК г. Москвы).
- Tsereteli opened it in 1999 with backing from Moscow's city government (The Art Newspaper, 24 April 2025).
- In 2008 Vasili Tsereteli described MMOMA as a city museum, in contrast to a federal museum under the Russian Academy of Arts (Radio Svoboda, 10 October 2008).
- The founding collection was donated by Zurab Tsereteli (The Calvert Journal, 19 January 2013).
- The museum runs a patron programme: visitors can buy a loyalty card, donate, or become an individual or corporate patron. Its support page has a "Partners and sponsors" section (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum itself. The early donated collection was criticised, and coverage of the founder concerns his public sculpture and politics.
1. The Calvert Journal: Maryam Omidi, "National pride: putting Russian contemporary art on the map" (19 January 2013)
URL: https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/268/vasili-tsereteli-moscow-museum-modern-art-makeover
- The early collection was "derided for its lack of quality"; after about two years the museum turned to Russian modern and contemporary art.
- Zurab Tsereteli's sculptures are divisive, and critics called them Stalinesque in scale and pomp. Vasili Tsereteli dismissed the criticism as a smear campaign aimed at former mayor Yuri Luzhkov.
- Says the museum's standing has since risen and serious critics respect it.
2. The Art Newspaper: Sophia Kishkovsky, "Zurab Tsereteli, Georgian-born artist and Russian patriot, has died aged 91" (24 April 2025)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/04/24/zurab-tsereteli-georgian-born-artist-and-russian-patriot-has-died-aged-91
- Criticises the founder's 98-metre Peter the Great monument, which critics said distorted the cityscape.
- Does not criticise MMOMA itself.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Art Newspaper's obituary (24 April 2025) says social media posts after Zurab Tsereteli's death noted his "continued support of the Putin regime"; it does not name the posters. Aleksandr Khinshtein, acting governor of the Kursk region, said on Telegram that Tsereteli designed a medal for the "liberation of Sudzha".
- The connection: Tsereteli founded MMOMA in 1999, and his grandson Vasili runs it and was elected president of the Russian Academy of Arts on 19 May 2025.

BOTTOM LINE
MMOMA is a Moscow city museum funded through the city's Department of Culture. Its founder donated the collection, and his grandson still runs it.
