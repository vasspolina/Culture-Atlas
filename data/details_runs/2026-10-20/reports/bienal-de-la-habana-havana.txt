BIENAL DE LA HABANA: Havana, Cuba
ID: bienal-de-la-habana-havana
Website: none found (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- International art biennial in Havana and, since 2019, other Cuban cities, focused on Latin American and Caribbean artists (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1984 and organised from the start by the Wifredo Lam Contemporary Art Center (Wikipedia; Hyperallergic).
- The 13th edition, due in 2018, was cancelled after Hurricane Irma and held from 12 April to 12 May 2019 (Wikipedia).
- The 14th edition, "Futuro y Contemporaneidad", was set to open on 12 November 2021 in three installments (Hyperallergic); the 15th, "Horizontes compartidos" (Shared Horizons), ran from 15 November 2024 to 28 February 2025 and marked the 40th anniversary (Hyperallergic; Biennial Foundation).
- The 15th edition had about 400 artists, about 230 of them foreign (EFE via OnCuba News).
- Visiting: no reporting found of opening hours, admission or accessibility.

FUNDING
Bienal de La Habana: state-run: Cuban Ministry of Culture through the Wifredo Lam Center and the National Council of Visual Arts
- The 15th edition was co-sponsored by the Wifredo Lam Center, the Ministry of Culture and the National Council of Fine Arts, as Prensa Latina via KPL names it.
- Hyperallergic calls the Wifredo Lam Center "government-supported" and the biennial "state-sponsored" (2021).
- In 2003 two Dutch government-sponsored NGOs told US diplomats they would stop funding the biennial in protest at the Cuban government's March crackdown (US State Department cable, 22 August 2003, via WikiLeaks).
- No reporting found of budgets, amounts or private sponsors for any edition.

Red flags: 2021 boycott over the arrest of artists
- On 15 October 2021 an open letter in e-flux asked artists not to take part in, attend or support the 14th edition, citing jailed artists, cultural workers under house arrest and over 1,000 arrests after the 11 July protests, with more than 500 still held (e-flux).
- Signers included Tania Bruguera, Coco Fusco, Tomás Sánchez, Theaster Gates, Doris Salcedo, Julie Mehretu and Hans Ulrich Obrist; it had over 400 signatures by 18 October (e-flux; Hyperallergic).
- Artists Nathalie Anguezomo Mba Bikoro, Ursula Biemann and Aimee Joaristi Argüelles, curator María Belén Sáez de Ibarra and critic Nicolas Bourriaud withdrew (Hyperallergic).
- Bruguera said 12 artists dropped out despite pressure from the organisers; the organisers did not answer The Art Newspaper (The Art Newspaper, December 2021).
- The state agency Prensa Latina reported more than 1,000 artists and intellectuals signed a declaration of support for the biennial (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Decree 349 and the 2019 edition
- Decree 349 requires artists to get official approval before staging shows; independent artists staged the alternative "Bienal Sin 349" during the 2019 edition (The Art Newspaper, April 2019).
- Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara, who was behind the alternative #00Bienal in 2018, was arrested on 11 April 2019, the day before the biennial opened (The Art Newspaper, 12 April 2019). Asked by Reuters, Norma Rodriguez, head of the National Council of Visual Arts, said "he is an activist, not an artist" (The New York Review of Books).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism of the biennial as a state-sponsored event, from 2019 to 2021, in The Art Newspaper and Hyperallergic.
1. The Art Newspaper: Christian Viveros-Fauné, "The 2019 Havana Biennial is a smokescreen for government censorship" (2019)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/04/19/the-2019-havana-biennial-is-a-smokescreen-for-government-censorship
- Cuban officials decide who counts as an artist; approved artists get state shows such as the biennial, while opponents of Decree 349 face harassment and detention.
- Names Otero Alcántara, Amaury Pacheco, Michel Matos, Katherine Bisquet, Coco Fusco and Tania Bruguera as targets.
- Says that, to many, the 2019 edition is the least challenging, most milquetoast Havana Biennial in history.
2. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "As Boycott of Havana Biennial Gains Steam, Artists Withdraw" (2021)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/685533/as-boycott-of-havana-biennial-gains-steam-artists-withdraw/
- Reports the withdrawals and the boycott letter.
- Biemann said director Lisset Alonso Compte responded with understanding but argued the boycott "asked people to take sides"; the organisers did not answer Hyperallergic.
3. The Art Newspaper: Benjamin Sutton with Gareth Harris, "Cuban artists shine a light on state repression" (2021)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2021/12/01/cuban-artists-shine-a-light-on-state-repression
- Bruguera said the biennial's opening "had no impact at all".
- Sets the biennial against the July protests and the state response; gallerist Tuesday Yates asks "How do you have an art biennial without freedom of speech?"

BOTTOM LINE
The biennial is run and paid for by the Cuban state through the Ministry of Culture; state money alone is not the issue. The weak point is that the organising state is the one detaining artists, which led to the 2021 boycott and withdrawals. Worth watching: whether the next edition draws a new boycott, and whether any budget is ever published.
