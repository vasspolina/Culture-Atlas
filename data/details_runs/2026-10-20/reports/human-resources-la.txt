HUMAN RESOURCES LA (HRLA): Los Angeles, USA
ID: human-resources-la
Website: https://h-r.la

OVERVIEW
- Volunteer-run non-profit exhibition and performance space at 410 Cottage Home Street, Chinatown, Los Angeles (Human Resources website).
- Founded in 2010 by siblings Eric and Kathleen Kim with Giles Miller, Devin McNulty and Dawn Kasper, who wanted to put performance art ahead of static displays (Wikipedia).
- The name presents the space as a resource for artists rather than an institution (Wikipedia).
- In 2011 it moved from a small shared space on Bernard Street to a larger, renovated former movie theatre of about 4,000 square feet that had housed the Cottage Home gallery (Wikipedia).
- Programme: exhibitions, performances, music and sound events, readings and screenings, organised by a content programming committee rather than a single curator (Wikipedia). The Mike Kelley Foundation grants page lists the group exhibition "There in Green California", curated by Gelare Khoshgozaran, for April 2024.
- The space states that it supports the Palestinian-led Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions (BDS) movement (stated by the space, About page).
- Visiting: opening hours vary with each exhibition and event. All events are free and all ages unless stated. The front entrance has a step of about seven inches with a folding ramp, the side entrance is wheelchair accessible, and the ground-floor restroom is single-occupancy and wheelchair accessible.

FUNDING
Human Resources LA: grant-funded non-profit: arts foundations and a county arts commission, with individual contributions
- The Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts gave $60,000 through Multi-year Program Support in its Spring 2019 cycle (Warhol Foundation grants archive).
- The Mike Kelley Foundation for the Arts gave $30,000 in its 2024 Organizational Support Grants.
- The Human Resources About page also names the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors through the LA County Arts Commission, the Robert Rauschenberg Foundation and the Ruth Foundation for the Arts as funders, without years or amounts.
- The Support page states that it is a 501(c)(3) supported by grants from public and private foundations and by individual contributions, and sells memberships with ticket discounts.
- The board listed on the About page is Eva Aguila, Jennifer Doyle, Luke Fischbeck, Shoghig Halajian, Eric Kim, Giles Miller and Vardui Sharapkhanyan. The executive director is John Burdle.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the space is grant funding from arts foundations named in the Warhol and Mike Kelley records and on its own About page, a county arts commission and individual donors.
