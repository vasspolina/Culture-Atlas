JEU DE PAUME: Paris, France
ID: jeu-de-paume-paris
Website: https://jeudepaume.org

OVERVIEW
- Art centre devoted to images of the 19th, 20th and 21st centuries, in the Tuileries garden at 1 Place de la Concorde, entrance by Concorde metro station (Jeu de Paume website).
- The building was an indoor tennis court under Napoleon III and became an exhibition gallery in the early 20th century. During the Second World War Rose Valland worked there to safeguard looted artworks, and its terraces now bear her name (Jeu de Paume website).
- The Jeu de Paume has been a reference venue for images since 2004. A 90-seat cinema has operated there since 2024 (Jeu de Paume website).
- It is a non-profit association under the 1901 law, chaired by Alain Dominique Perrin and directed by Quentin Bajac (Jeu de Paume website).
- The Cour des comptes' first audit of it, covering 2019 to 2024, called it a "quasi-opérateur de l'État". It counted 274,468 visitors in 2023 (Le Journal des Arts, Jean-Christophe Castelain, 11 December 2025).
- Visiting: open Tuesday 11:00 to 21:00 and Wednesday to Sunday 11:00 to 19:00, closed Monday. Exhibition tickets cost 14 euros, or 9.50 reduced. Under-18s enter free, as does everyone on the last Tuesday of the month; visitors with a disability card and a companion enter free (Jeu de Paume visit pages).

FUNDING
Jeu de Paume: 68% state subsidies in 2024, with private patrons
- Budget: 8.6 million euros in 2024, of which 68% came from state subsidies, according to the Cour des comptes. The operating result was 137,000 euros and net cash 4 million euros at 31 December 2024 (Le Journal des Arts, citing the Cour des comptes).
- Own resources: ticket sales and bookstore revenue (Le Journal des Arts).
- The Cour des comptes recommended that the association keep its associative status and that the state grant free occupancy of the building instead of the 194,000 euros a year the Finance Ministry sought (Le Journal des Arts).
- Patrons listed on the Jeu de Paume website: Manufacture Jaeger-LeCoultre, described as "mécène privilégié", CHANEL, Fondation Neuflize OBC, Fondation Louis Roederer and Fondation Swiss Life, plus the Ministry of Culture subsidy and the support association Les Amis du Jeu de Paume.
- Partners named by the Cour des comptes: Centre Pompidou, the Cnap, MoMA and Tate Modern (Le Journal des Arts).

Red flags: State censorship or punishment, 2013 Ahlam Shibli exhibition
- On 5 June 2013 Roger Cukierman, president of the CRIF, wrote to Culture Minister Aurélie Filippetti calling the series "particulièrement regrettable et inacceptable" and asking her to intervene (franceinfo, Valérie Oddos, 14 June 2013).
- In a communiqué reported on 14 June 2013, the Ministry of Culture, whose subsidy funds the Jeu de Paume, asked it to "compléter l'information donnée aux visiteurs" (translated: complete the information given to visitors), to clarify the artist's intent and distinguish her proposal from what the institution expresses. It called the reactions understandable and said the artist's claimed neutrality could itself shock and be misread, because it does not explain the photos' context of terrorism (franceinfo, Valérie Oddos, 14 June 2013).
- The Jeu de Paume rejected any propaganda or apology for terrorism (franceinfo). The show, "Foyers fantômes" (Phantom Home), included the 68-photo series "Death", showing people Palestinians call "martyrs" (franceinfo).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One 2013 piece defends the exhibition against the CRIF and the ministry.
1. Politis: Christophe Kantcheff, "Ahlam Shibli au Jeu de paume : La critique contre la censure" (27 June 2013)
URL: https://www.politis.fr/articles/2013/06/ahlam-shibli-au-jeu-de-paume-la-critique-contre-la-censure-22799
- Reports that the CRIF pressed for the closure of the show, and that others used bomb threats and death threats. Argues that censorship denies artworks as works, that most opponents had not seen the works, and that the CRIF's charge of "apologie du terrorisme" shows blindness to them.
- Says the ministry's statement supported the artist and the museum but mainly distanced the ministry from them.
- Says Shibli wrote a presentation text for the "Death" room, which has more developed informational captions than the other rooms, and argues such captions tend to weaken the images.

BOTTOM LINE
The Jeu de Paume is a non-profit association: 68% of its 8.6 million euro budget in 2024 came from state subsidies, the rest from ticket and bookstore income and patrons including Jaeger-LeCoultre, CHANEL and three foundations. In 2013 the Ministry of Culture, whose subsidy funds it, asked it to add visitor information to the Ahlam Shibli exhibition after a complaint from the CRIF.
