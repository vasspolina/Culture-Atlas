MUSEU DE ARTE MODERNA DO RIO DE JANEIRO (MAM RIO): Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
ID: museu-de-arte-moderna-do-rio-de-janeiro-rio-de-janeiro
Website: https://mam.rio (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Flamengo Park on Guanabara Bay, at Av. Infante Dom Henrique 85; it describes itself as an experimental centre for art, culture and education.
- Established in 1948; the modernist concrete building was designed by Affonso Eduardo Reidy with Carmen Portinho as chief engineer and completed in 1955. Roberto Burle Marx designed the gardens (Wikipedia).
- A fire on 8 July 1978 destroyed about 90% of the collection, including works by Picasso, Miró and Dalí (Wikipedia).
- On site: exhibition halls, an art school, a theatre and a cinematheque of more than 7,500 films (Brazil Journal, 2018). The collection holds more than 16,000 works (MAM Rio).
- Admission is free. Open Wednesday to Saturday 10:00 to 18:00, Sundays from 11:00 (10:00 to 11:00 is reserved for people with intellectual disabilities, autistic people and visitors sensitive to sound or light); last entry 17:30. Closed on Sundays 4 and 25 October for elections.

FUNDING
Museu de Arte Moderna do Rio de Janeiro: private and corporate: patrons and sponsors, with Petrobras as official sponsor from 2026
- Petrobras: the relationship began in 1999. From 2026 Petrobras is the museum's official sponsor with an exclusive designation, through the Programa Petrobras Cultural. It pays for school programmes, free transport for school groups, a young-artists space, the Super Sábados activities (over 21,000 people in 2025), cinema screenings and conservation. No figure is published (MAM Rio).
- Also shown on the home page: Lei Rouanet (federal tax incentive), Visit Rio, CCPAR, Prefeitura do Rio, a state secretariat and the federal government.
- In 2019 the museum stated that it received no money from federal, state or municipal governments, and that Rio state's economic crisis had cut patron donations (The Art Newspaper, via Fórum Permanente).
- Finances in 2019, as stated by the museum: debt of about $3m, revenue of about $1.2m, and a need of about $2m a year.
- Endowment: the board sold Jackson Pollock's No. 16 (1950), donated by Nelson Rockefeller in 1952, through Phillips for a figure in the range of $13m to build an endowment (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of the sponsors' individual sums.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Petrobras, official sponsor)
- Petrobras, Brazil's state-owned oil and energy company, has sponsored the museum since 1999 and from 2026 is its official sponsor with an exclusive designation, through the Programa Petrobras Cultural (MAM Rio).
- On 27 September 2018 Petrobras settled bribery-related charges under the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act with the US Department of Justice (a non-prosecution agreement, $853.2 million criminal penalty, of which Brazil received $682.56 million) and the Securities and Exchange Commission (a cease-and-desist order, $933 million in disgorgement and interest, reduced by a related class action payment). Petrobras admitted only accounting violations: it failed to keep books and records that accurately reflected its assets because of contractor bribes. The Justice Department said executives facilitated bribes to Brazilian politicians and parties and concealed them in the company's books (Holland & Knight).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism of the museum centres on one decision, the 2018 to 2019 sale of its Pollock, and on its finances. No criticism found of the Petrobras sponsorship.
1. The Art Newspaper: Mery Galanternick, on the sale of Pollock's No. 16 (6 February 2019; read via Fórum Permanente's copy)
URL: https://www.forumpermanente.org/imprensa/dossie-pollock-do-mam-rj/rio2019s-museum-of-modern-art-finally-sells-pollock-for-around-13m
- A manifesto signed by more than 170 gallerists, curators, photographers and artists, including Waltercio Caldas, Luiz Zerbini, Adriana Varejão and Marcia Fortes, said other solutions existed and pointed to MASP, which recovered from a larger debt through a governance plan. Zerbini called the sale "a very simplistic solution".
- Board president Carlos Alberto Chateaubriand said "It's better to sell one work than lose 16,000 works". The museum declined to comment on the manifesto.
- Criticises a management decision, not the sponsors.
2. Brazil Journal: Mariana Barbosa, "O Pollock da discórdia" (2018)
URL: https://braziljournal.com/o-pollock-da-discordia/
- Argues the sale avoids the structural fiscal adjustment and management overhaul the museum needs.
- Names critics: Ibram, a federal heritage body, which asked for the sale to be suspended, although the Ministry of Culture endorsed the decision; Maria Ignez Mantovani of ICOM Brazil; art historian Paulo Sérgio Duarte. Critics noted a conflict of interest because the museum's president, Carlos Alberto Chateaubriand, heads an institution that holds loaned works from his family's collection; several sources spoke off the record.
- Business outlet rather than art press. Says sponsorship is scarce in a crisis-hit Rio.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is privately run and says it takes no government money, but it depends on corporate sponsors and tax incentives, with Petrobras now its exclusive official sponsor. The weak points are Petrobras's 2018 US settlement and the museum's financial fragility, which led to the Pollock sale. Worth watching: the size of the Petrobras funding in 2026.
