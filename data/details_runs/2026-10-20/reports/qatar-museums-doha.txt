QATAR MUSEUMS: Doha, Qatar
ID: qatar-museums-doha
Website: https://qm.org.qa/en/ (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- National museum organisation of Qatar that runs the Museum of Islamic Art and MIA Park, Mathaf: Arab Museum of Modern Art, the National Museum of Qatar, the 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum and two galleries.
- Founded in 2005 as the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia); under the patronage of the Amir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani.
- Chairperson: Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani; chief executive: Mohammed Al Rumaihi (Wikipedia).
- Opened: Museum of Islamic Art 2008, Mathaf 2010, National Museum of Qatar 2019, Olympic and Sports Museum 2022 (Wikipedia). Planned: Dadu, Children's Museum of Qatar (expected 2029), the Art Mill Museum (2030) and the Lusail Museum.
- Visiting: the National Museum, Museum of Islamic Art and Mathaf open 9 am to 7 pm, to 9 pm on Thursday and from 1:30 pm on Friday, each closed one weekday. Ticket prices were not on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Qatar Museums: state-linked: national museum body under the Amir's patronage, with ExxonMobil Qatar and Qatar Shell sponsoring Dadu
- The Qatar Museums site posts no funding statement or accounts, only a donor contributions programme.
- Dadu is described as "Qatar's first publicly funded national entity" (Gulf Times, 18 April 2022).
- ExxonMobil Qatar announced on 1 December 2021 that it was a founding family member of Dadu, exclusive sponsor of its Sustainability and Valuing the Environment Gallery; the amount was not disclosed (Gulf Times, 1 December 2021).
- Qatar Shell announced the same on 18 April 2022, exclusive sponsor of Dadu's STEM Gallery, under a long-term agreement; the amount was not disclosed (Gulf Times, 18 April 2022).
- Marhaba.qa (20 November 2023, updated 14 November 2024) lists the Dadu Founding Family as the Amir, two ministries, ConocoPhillips, ENI, ExxonMobil, Shell, TotalEnergies, SLB, Ooredoo, Uniqlo and four other companies, and says members give QAR 3.65 million or more. No Dadu sponsor page was found.
- No reporting found of annual budget figures in the sources opened.

Red flags: Gulf state body, Qatar government entity
- Qatar Museums is the state's museum body, chaired by Sheikha Al Mayassa and under the Amir's patronage (Qatar Museums site).
- Unverified: social media users, led by a Qatari journalist, accused the Qatar Museums Authority of corruption, mismanagement and nepotism about a month before October 2013 (Sarah El-Richani, openDemocracy, 16 October 2013); no finding is reported.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Dadu sponsors
- ExxonMobil Qatar (December 2021) and Qatar Shell (April 2022) are named sponsors of Dadu (Gulf Times). Marhaba.qa (2023, updated 2024) also lists ConocoPhillips, ENI and TotalEnergies among the founding family.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism of Qatar Museums; the art press covers Qatar's state role in art and Mayassa's purchasing power.
1. e-flux Criticism: Jörg Heiser, "On Art Basel Qatar" (26 February 2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/6782392/on-art-basel-qatar
- Argues Gulf states treat art as state investment to diversify from oil and gas, "beyond mere artwashing".
- Notes queer and nude work was absent from the fair, since LGBTQ+ people remain criminalised in Qatar; says Sheikha Al-Mayassa's purchasing power shapes the fair.
- Links Gulf spectacle to exploited migrant construction crews.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Qatar opens slavery museum" (2015)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2015/11/26/qatar-opens-slavery-museum
- Reports that human rights organisations have criticised poor labour conditions of migrant workers building stadiums and museums before the 2022 World Cup.
- Does not name Qatar Museums and carries no reply.
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (16 October 2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Says social media reports claimed Sheikha Mayassa intervened to keep Adel Abdessemed's film "Printemps" from showing at Mathaf; officials cited technical issues.
- Argues the Qatar Museums Authority should do more to support local and Arab artists; general-interest outlet, not an art-press title.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Human Rights Watch's 2026 World Report on Qatar documents wage theft, a minimum wage of QAR 1,000 (about US$274) unrevised since 2021, a heat threshold it calls too high, and migrant worker deaths linked to the 2022 World Cup that were neither investigated nor compensated. It does not mention Qatar Museums.
- Connection to the space: the Art Newspaper (2015) reports rights groups criticising conditions for workers building Qatar's museums; no reporting found names a Qatar Museums site or contractor.

BOTTOM LINE
Qatar Museums is the Qatari state's museum body, which makes it a Gulf state body, with ExxonMobil Qatar and Qatar Shell among the sponsors of its planned children's museum, Dadu. The weak points are the absence of any published accounts and Qatar's national labour record, which no source opened ties to Qatar Museums sites. Worth watching: sponsor amounts for Dadu and the Art Mill and Lusail projects.
