CIRCLE ART GALLERY: Nairobi, Kenya
ID: circle-art-gallery
Website: https://circleartagency.com (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Commercial gallery and art agency in Lavington, Nairobi, at Victoria Square, Riara Road (stated by the gallery).
- Established in Nairobi in 2012; its About page names Danda Jaroljmek, Arvind Vohora and Fiona Fox as founders and says the idea was born in 2013 in a converted 1970s house in Lavington. The sources give both years. Jaroljmek is the director.
- In 2015 it built what it calls the first white cube gallery in Nairobi; in 2020 it joined Cromwell Place, a gallery membership club in London (stated by the gallery).
- Moved to Victoria Square in 2023, its tenth anniversary. Gallery 1 is 235 m2 and Gallery 2 is 54 m2; both can be hired for private events (stated by the gallery).
- Runs a Modern and Contemporary East African art auction; the tenth edition, in 2022, is described by the gallery as the most successful to date. It shows at fairs in London, Paris, New York, Lagos and Cape Town.
- Visiting: open weekdays 10am to 5pm and weekends 12pm to 5pm, closed on public holidays, with visits by appointment. No admission or accessibility information is published.

FUNDING
Circle Art Gallery: privately owned company: a limited company with 15 named shareholders
- Circle is a limited company whose shareholders are art collectors and enthusiasts: Philip Coulson, Samit Gehlot (Intex Foundation), Tony Wainaina, Robert Devereux, Dayo Forster, Caroline Fox, Jeremy Block, Fulvio Honneger, Gary Nealon, Karuga Koinange, Angeline Ngugi, Ranjith Cherickel, Richard Harney, Barbara Barungi and Louis Lallia (stated by the gallery).
- The About page describes fairs, an annual auction and gallery rental for private events, but says nothing about the income each brings; no stakes or sales figures are published (stated by the gallery).
- No reporting found of state, corporate or foundation grants to the gallery.
- No reporting found of a board; the site lists shareholders only. In a 2015 C& article by Moses Serubiri, Jaroljmek says "We haven't had a decent budget" and calls Circle "still a small business".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is private shareholders and, as a commercial gallery, its sales; no public funder, flagged industry or documented controversy was found. The weak points are that stakes, accounts and any other funding are not published. Watch the shareholder list as it changes.
