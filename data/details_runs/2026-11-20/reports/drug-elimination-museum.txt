DRUG ELIMINATION MUSEUM: Yangon, Myanmar
ID: drug-elimination-museum
Website: https://drugmuseum.gov.mm (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum on 15.11 acres at the corner of Kyun Taw and Hanthawaddy roads, Kamayut Township (museum website), on the former site of Kyandaw Cemetery, once the city's largest (Wikipedia).
- Built under the guidance of Senior General Than Shwe, chairman of the State Peace and Development Council; Lieutenant General Khin Nyunt, the council's Secretary (1), laid the foundation stone on 25 July 1998 and opened it on 26 June 2001 (museum website).
- Its stated purpose is to show the public, especially young people, the dangers of drugs and to tell the international community about Myanmar's anti-drug work, including military operations from 1975 to 1988 and a 15-year plan for 1999 to 2014 (museum website).
- A four-storey building, ground floor included (museum website). In 2013 the first exhibit floor held maps of trafficking routes, military records of seizures, mannequins re-enacting army drug-war operations and a diorama blaming a Westerner for bringing in drugs; the second, portraits of junta generals and a display of the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control; the third, a five-scene story of a boy's decline to death, a rehabilitation mock-up and photographs of victims and preserved organs (Vice).
- Visitors in 2020 included private school groups, trainees from a rehabilitation camp and a 13-member study delegation from Croatia (museum website).
- Visiting: daily 9.00 to 16.00 except Mondays and public holidays; 100 kyat for Myanmar visitors and 3 US dollars for foreigners, cameras 200 kyat or 5 dollars (museum website).

FUNDING
Drug Elimination Museum: state: the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control under the Ministry of Home Affairs
- Operator: the museum's website is tied to the Central Committee for Drug Abuse Control (CCDAC), whose chairman is the Union Minister for Home Affairs; the chairman's messages on the site are signed by ministers Soe Thwe (2022), Htun Htun Naung (2025) and Nyunt Win Swe (2026) (museum website).
- Staff: a deputy director, Daw Khin Swe Lhaing, runs training sessions for rehabilitation camps at the museum (museum website).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The one critical piece describes a neglected relic of junta propaganda.
1. Vice: Alex Palmer, "From Insanity to Death: Inside Burma's Drug Eradication Museum" (2013)
URL: https://www.vice.com/da/article/inside-burmas-drug-eradication-museum/
- Quotes a general's words near the entrance: "The big nations are giving human rights, democracy and drug elimination as excuses".
- Describes a crumbling, overgrown and nearly abandoned compound with broken exhibits, staff napping at the front desk and few visitors, mostly curious foreigners.

BOTTOM LINE
State money from the Home Affairs ministry's drug committee, which alone is no red flag on the atlas. The weak point is its origin and content as a junta propaganda project.
