CASTELLO DI RIVOLI MUSEO D'ARTE CONTEMPORANEA: Rivoli, Italy
ID: castello-di-rivoli-museo-d-arte-contemporanea
Website: https://www.castellodirivoli.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum in a former residence of the House of Savoy at Rivoli, near Turin; it opened as a museum in 1984 and is described by Wikipedia as the first contemporary art museum in Italy.
- Castle history: first mentioned in 1159, used as barracks from 1863, heavily damaged in the Second World War and restored from 1979; Andrea Bruno was the architect of the first post-war renovation (Wikipedia). It is one of 14 Savoy residences on the UNESCO World Heritage list since 1997.
- Directors: Rudi Fuchs and Johannes Gachnang (1984 to 1991), Ida Giannelli (1991 to 2008), Andrea Bellini and Beatrice Merz (2010 to 2014), Carolyn Christov-Bakargiev (2015 to 2024) and Francesco Manacorda from 2024 (Wikipedia).
- On site: the Castello building, the Manica Lunga gallery wing, a library open since 1999 with about 44,000 books and periodicals, and the Villa Cerruti, which holds the Cerruti Collection (acquisition announced in 2017).
- Permanent collection of Arte Povera and later Italian and international contemporary art; Giuseppe Penone donated more than 200 works on paper in 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Friday 10am to 5pm, Saturday and Sunday 11am to 6pm. Full ticket 15 euros, reduced 6.50 euros (over 65s, teachers, 11 to 18s, students); free for minors and for visitors with disabilities and their caregivers. Two ramps give access, and a wheelchair is available. The Cerruti Collection is open Saturday and Sunday 10:30am to 6pm.

FUNDING
Castello di Rivoli: mainly public money plus two private foundations: Regione Piemonte, City of Turin, Town of Rivoli, Fondazione CRT and Compagnia di San Paolo
- 2021 income was 4.92 million euros: about 55% from public bodies, 23% from private bodies and 22% from accessory activities, Amici del Castello and ticket sales (Il Sole 24 Ore, 12 December 2022).
- Regione Piemonte gave 2.529 million euros in 2021; its 2022 budget projected 1.9 million euros plus 510,000 euros in membership fees. The City of Turin gave 75,000 euros in 2021; the Town of Rivoli gave 15,000 euros (Il Sole 24 Ore).
- Fondazione CRT and Compagnia di San Paolo together gave about 804,000 euros in 2021; Compagnia di San Paolo was projected to give 500,000 euros in 2022. The Ministry of Culture gave 150,800 euros in 2021, against 248,900 euros requested (Il Sole 24 Ore). The same article reports public funding falling year on year, especially from the Region.
- The museum's own page lists Regione Piemonte, Fondazione CRT, the City of Turin and the Town of Rivoli as supporters, lists Intesa Sanpaolo and Gallerie d'Italia as partners, and says Compagnia di San Paolo supported the acquisition of the video collection (stated by the museum). Donors are grouped as Amici Benefattori, Amici Sostenitori and Amici.
- Board: president Francesca Lavazza, vice president Paola Gatto, with Antonella Musy, Bruno Bertero and Anna Beatrice Ferrino; Marco Chiriotti is board secretary (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of figures for 2023 to 2025 from a page that could be read; the museum's 2023 and 2025 accounts are posted as PDFs that could not be opened as text.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is regional and municipal government plus two private foundations (Fondazione CRT and Compagnia di San Paolo), with 23% of 2021 income from private bodies; no funder with a documented controversy was found. The weak points are dependence on falling regional funding and a self-financing share of about 22% in 2021. Watch the 2023 to 2025 accounts, which were not readable here.
