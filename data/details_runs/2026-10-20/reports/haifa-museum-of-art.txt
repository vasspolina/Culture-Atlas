HAIFA MUSEUM OF ART: Haifa, Israel
ID: haifa-museum-of-art
Website: https://www.hms.org.il/eng/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Municipal contemporary art museum at 26 Shabbetai Levi Street in the Wadi Nisnas neighbourhood of Haifa, part of Haifa Museums, the city's group of six museums (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Founded in 1951 for a large Marc Chagall exhibition; moved to its present building in 1977 and opened there as a municipal museum in 1978 (Wikipedia).
- The stone building went up in the late 1920s as an Anglican girls' school and later served as an immigrant centre and a workers' school (Wikipedia).
- About 7,000 items, mostly contemporary Israeli art; Yotam Yakir is general director of Haifa Museums (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Haifa Museums also runs the Tikotin Museum of Japanese Art, the National Maritime Museum, the Haifa City Museum, the Mané-Katz Museum and the Hermann Struck Museum (stated by the space).
- Open Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday 10:00 to 16:00, Thursday to 18:00, Friday to 14:00, closed Monday; adult tickets 30 to 40 NIS by museum, 80 NIS for a seven-day ticket to six museums; free for reservists and security forces personnel (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Haifa Museum of Art: mainly public: Haifa Municipality, with Bank Mizrahi-Tefahot as sponsor
- Haifa Museums is the city's municipal museum body; its board includes the director of the Haifa Municipality's Corporations Bureau, and Yona Yahav chairs it (stated by the space).
- The sponsorship page shows logos for Mizrahi Tefahot, Haifa Municipality, "downtown" and "ethos", and offers benefits to Mifal HaPais subscribers (stated by the space).
- The only named foreign-state donation for 2025 is ₪15,620 from the Embassy of Japan, for an exhibition at the Tikotin Museum, not the art museum (stated by the space).
- No reporting found of the museum's budget, the sponsors' amounts, or published accounts.

Red flags: sponsor Bank Mizrahi-Tefahot
- On 12 March 2019 the bank entered a deferred prosecution agreement with the US Justice Department and paid $195 million, including a $118 million fine, after admitting its employees helped US clients hide income and assets from 2002 to 2012 (Mondo Visione, reproducing the Justice Department release).
- On 12 February 2020 the UN listed the bank in its database of 112 companies involved in Israeli settlement activity in the West Bank and Golan Heights; Norway's KLP pension fund said on 5 July 2021 it would divest over that finding (Wikipedia).

Red flags: "McJesus" protests and removal (2019)
- Jani Leinonen's sculpture of a crucified Ronald McDonald was shown from August 2018 in the exhibition "Sacred Goods" (NPR).
- In January 2019 hundreds of Christian protesters, from Israel's Arab Christian minority, tried to storm the museum; Haifa police said stones and firebombs were thrown, and three police officers were injured (NPR, Fox News).
- Culture Minister Miri Regev threatened to defund the museum, and Deputy Attorney General Dina Zilber rebuked her (Fox News).
- On 16 January 2019 Mayor Einat Kalisch-Rotem announced the work would be removed; Leinonen had also asked for its removal and said he had joined the BDS movement (NPR, quoting the Jerusalem Post).

Not related: the Hecht Museum at the University of Haifa, which cancelled a talk by Palestinian artist Saher Miari in January 2020, saying a Palestinian artist did not conform with the Hecht Foundation's politics (Hyperallergic). It belongs to the university, not to Haifa Museums.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The 2019 dispute was covered by general news outlets.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city institution paid for mainly by Haifa Municipality. The weak point is its bank sponsor, Mizrahi-Tefahot, which paid $195 million under a 2019 US tax-evasion agreement and is on the UN settlement database. The 2019 removal of "McJesus" followed violent protest and a threat by the culture minister to defund the museum.
