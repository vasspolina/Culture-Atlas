BIENNALE OF SYDNEY: Sydney, Australia
ID: biennale-of-sydney-sydney
Website: https://www.biennaleofsydney.art/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennial held across Sydney venues, run by Biennale of Sydney Ltd, a registered charity.
- Founded in 1973 by Franco Belgiorno-Nettis, then co-managing director of the construction company Transfield; the first show, of 37 artists, was in the exhibition hall of the new Sydney Opera House (Wikipedia).
- The 24th edition, "Ten Thousand Suns" (9 March to 10 June 2024), by Cosmin Costinaș and Inti Guerrero, drew 777,386 visitors (stated by the space).
- The 25th edition, "Rememory" (14 March to 14 June 2026), was directed by Hoor Al Qasimi, president of the Sharjah Art Foundation, across five venues including the Art Gallery of New South Wales, Campbelltown Arts Centre and White Bay Power Station (Artribune).
- Visiting: admission is free; the 2026 edition ran Saturday 14 March to Sunday 14 June.

FUNDING
Biennale of Sydney: mixed: federal, state and city money with Gulf state, foundation and corporate partners
- 2024: turnover of $19.6M in cash and in kind; the chair named the federal and NSW governments, the City of Sydney, Fondation Cartier, the Neilson Foundation and Mirvac as patrons (stated by the space).
- Create NSW approved $700,000 for the Biennale under its 4 + 4 multi-year funding in October 2024 (NSW Government).
- Government partners in 2026: Creative Australia, Create NSW, Destination NSW and the City of Sydney (stated by the space).
- 2026 partners (release of 23 February 2026): Fondation Cartier (Visionary Partner); Rubaiya Qatar, Qatar Museums (Major Strategic Partner, 22 artist projects); Arada (Major Partner, Family Days); BEEAH Group (Strategic Partner, co-commissioned Ema Shin's project); SBS and PwC Australia (stated by the space, no amounts).
- The Biennale's archive page credits Franco Belgiorno-Nettis as Founding Patron and Transfield as the Biennale's principal corporate partner for over 40 years (stated by the space).

Red flags: Gulf state partners
- Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, was Major Strategic Partner of the 2026 edition and backed 22 artist projects (stated by the space; Wikipedia).
- Arada, a property developer based in Sharjah, United Arab Emirates, was a Major Partner; the 2026 director heads the Sharjah Art Foundation (stated by the space; Wikipedia; Artribune).
- No reporting found of any objection to the Qatar Museums or Arada partnerships.

Red flags: 2014 boycott over Transfield and offshore detention
- Transfield Holdings, owned by the Belgiorno-Nettis family, sponsored the Biennale for 41 years; Transfield Services, in which it held about 12%, ran detention centres for asylum seekers on Manus Island and Nauru (Hyperallergic; ArtReview).
- Nine artists withdrew, including Ahmet Öğüt, Agnieszka Polska and Ólafur Ólafsson; two installers walked off the job (Hyperallergic, March 2014).
- On 7 March 2014 the board "reluctantly" cut ties with Transfield and chairman Luca Belgiorno-Nettis, also Transfield Holdings' chief executive, resigned (ArtReview).
- Arts minister George Brandis threatened the Biennale's federal funding and asked the Australia Council for a policy to penalise groups that refuse sponsors on "unreasonable grounds" (Starkwhite; Apollo).

Red flags: 2026 opening night and PwC's withdrawal
- At the opening concert, DJ Haram (Zubeyda Muzeyyen) spoke of a "Zio-Australian-Epstein empire"; NSW Premier Chris Minns called the rhetoric "horrid" and the NSW Jewish Board of Deputies filed a police complaint (Biennial Foundation, citing The Guardian).
- PwC withdrew its support; the Biennale launched an internal review; Minns ruled out cutting state funding at once (Biennial Foundation).
- Police dropped the case for lack of evidence (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Strong criticism in 2014 over the Transfield sponsorship; 2026 coverage centres on the opening-night remarks and the director, not the money.
1. Hyperallergic: Jillian Steinhauer, "Sydney Biennale Boycott Grows" (2014)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/sydney-biennale-boycott-grows/
- Artists argued that taking part added brand value to a company profiting from detaining asylum seekers.
- Transfield replied that it had no influence over Transfield Services.
2. Apollo: Tim Walsh, "Sponsorship, ethics and the Biennale of Sydney" (2014)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/sponsorship-ethics-biennale-sydney/
- Calls the artists' win symbolic and its wider effect unclear; quotes Helen Hughes that privatisation lets governments avoid culpability.
- Says hardly any of the more than 90 artists were Indigenous.
3. The Art Newspaper: Elizabeth Fortescue, "Low Kee Hong chosen to curate Biennale of Sydney's next edition" (2026)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/07/27/biennale-sydney-curator-2028-low-kee-hong
- Calls the 2026 edition "beset by controversy"; Al Qasimi was criticised for missing the media preview and said she wanted artists to speak for themselves.

BOTTOM LINE
Public money from Canberra, NSW and the City of Sydney is the base, with Fondation Cartier and private patrons. The weak points are the 2014 Transfield sponsorship that ended in a boycott, and the opening-night row and PwC's withdrawal in 2026; the archive page still credits Transfield as its principal corporate partner for over 40 years. Worth watching: whether the Gulf partners stay for 2028 and what the 2026 accounts show.
