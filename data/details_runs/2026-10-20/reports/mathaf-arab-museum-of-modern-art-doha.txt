MATHAF: ARAB MUSEUM OF MODERN ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: mathaf-arab-museum-of-modern-art-doha
Website: https://www.mathaf.org.qa (checked 2026-10-24; live, carries a "© Qatar Museums 2026" notice)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art from the Arab world in Education City, Doha, with over 9,000 objects; it is a member of Qatar Museums (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Grew from the collection Sheikh Hassan bin Mohammed Al Thani began in the early 1990s, first shown in two private villas in Madinat Khalifa. He donated it to Qatar Foundation, and the Qatar Museums Authority later acquired it (Wikipedia).
- Opened 30 December 2010 in a former school building in Education City, converted by the French architect Jean-François Bodin; about 5,500 square metres (Wikipedia).
- Director since November 2021: Zeina Arida (Wikipedia). Qatar Museums is chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani.
- On site: permanent collection, temporary exhibitions, an online collection and an encyclopedia of Arab art (museum website).
- Visiting: Sunday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday 9 am to 7 pm; Thursday 9 am to 9 pm; Friday 1:30 to 7 pm; closed Monday. The site lists no ticket prices for the main collection and links to a general Qatar Museums accessibility page.

FUNDING
Mathaf: state-funded: Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity
- Qatar Museums was founded in 2005 as a Qatari government entity, formerly the Qatar Museums Authority (Wikipedia). Mathaf's site names Qatar Museums and Qatar Foundation in its footer and lists no other supporters.
- Bloomberg reported in 2013 that Sheikha Al-Mayassa's annual acquisition budget for Qatar Museums is estimated at $1 billion; Wikipedia presents this as an estimate, not a verified figure.
- Qatar Museums describes itself as a "private entity for public good" (openDemocracy, 2013).
- For Mathaf's 2020 show at the Palais de Tokyo, Paris, the Palais said the Qatari government never donated to it and that production costs were shared between the two museums (The Art Newspaper).
- No reporting found of Mathaf's own accounts, donors or board.

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Mathaf is part of Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity chaired by Sheikha Al-Mayassa (Wikipedia, museum website).

Red flags: state censorship
- November 2024: İnci Eviner's video Harem (2009) was removed at the last minute from "Seeing Is Believing: The Art and Influence of Gérôme". Eviner says director Zeina Arida told her the decision came from Qatar's ministry of culture. Mathaf did not answer The Art Newspaper's requests for comment and confirmed the removal to ARTnews without detail.
- 2013: Adel Abdessemed's film Printemps was not shown at Mathaf; officials cited technical issues (openDemocracy, Hyperallergic). Unverified: social media reports said Sheikha Al-Mayassa intervened, from openDemocracy, which treats them as unverified.

Red flags: labour conditions in Qatar
- Amnesty International (2019) says Qatar has roughly two million migrant workers and has repeatedly failed to protect them; it urges an end to the kafala sponsorship system. No reporting found of documented abuses on Mathaf's own conversion or operation.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Sustained criticism of Qatar Museums and its control over what is shown; the Mathaf-specific pieces concern censorship and its use abroad.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anni Irish, "Harem work removed from show at Qatar's Arab Museum of Modern Art" (2024)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/12/11/work-removed-from-show-at-qatars-arab-museum-of-modern-art
- Reports the removal of Eviner's Harem on the ministry's order, per the artist, from a show organised with Qatar's Lusail Museum.
- Eviner says it raises questions about how women's rights and free expression change with local values. Names Arida and Qatar Museums.
2. The Art Newspaper: Nikita Dmitriev, "Artists criticise Palais de Tokyo partnership with Qatari museum over LGBTQ rights" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/01/08/artists-criticise-palais-de-tokyo-partnership-with-qatari-museum-over-lgbtq-rights
- Philosopher Yves Michaud called the partnership part of a "shameless and long-term strategy" by the Qatari government to win over French society on Gulf human rights. Activist Babi Badalov said the gay community should fight for cancellation.
- The Palais said it works with a museum, not a state. No direct reply from Mathaf.
3. e-flux Criticism: Ana Teixeira Pinto, "PR Kingdom: Press Debriefings and a Bit of Art in Doha. 'Tea with Nefertiti' at Mathaf" (2012)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/234065/pr-kingdom-press-debriefings-and-a-bit-of-art-in-doha-tea-with-nefertiti-at-mathaf
- Describes a tightly managed press trip organised by PR agencies for the Qatar Museums Authority, finds the Mathaf show's concept flimsy, argues Qatar's sponsorship system amounts to indentured servitude and asks whether collections belong to the state or to private sponsors.
4. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Argues state-backed art exposes tensions between public funding, religious sensibilities and the state's cultural mission; questions public spending and unannounced acquisitions. Not an art outlet.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Amnesty International (2019): Qatar's planned kafala reforms matter only if workers can leave the country or change jobs freely; a September 2019 report found hundreds of workers still waiting for unpaid wages.
- Connection: Mathaf belongs to Qatar Museums, which she chairs.

BOTTOM LINE
Mathaf is paid for by the Qatari state through Qatar Museums, with no private donors named; the question is state control, not hidden funding. The weak points are the 2024 ministry-ordered removal of a work, earlier cases of withheld work, and the migrant-labour system behind Qatar's building programme. Worth watching: whether Mathaf publishes its own accounts and who decides its programme.
