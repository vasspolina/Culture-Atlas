UNIÓN DE ESCRITORES Y ARTISTAS DE CUBA (UNEAC): Havana, Cuba
ID: union-de-escritores-y-artistas-de-cuba-uneac
Website: http://www.uneac.org.cu (checked 2026-11-20; serves only a default nginx server page; the archived site of February 2026 was read instead)

OVERVIEW
- Membership union of Cuban writers and artists, based in Havana, with 14 provincial committees, whose members join by selection on their artistic record (UNEAC site); its associations cover writers, film, radio and television, performing arts, visual arts and music (Prensa Latina, 2024).
- Founded on 22 August 1961 by the poet Nicolás Guillén; Alejo Carpentier, José Lezama Lima and René Portocarrero served in its leadership (UNEAC site).
- Its 2020 statutes recognise the Communist Party of Cuba as the superior leading force of society and the state (article 2), and list among its objectives to reject and combat any activity contrary to the principles of the Cuban Revolution (article 8).
- Stated by the union: it holds special consultative status at the UN Economic and Social Council (UNEAC site).
- It has issued La Gaceta de Cuba since 1962 (English Wikipedia) and given the Caracol prizes for film, radio, television and criticism since 1978 (Spanish and English Wikipedia).
- On 11 January 2024 the musicologist Marta Bonet replaced Luis Morlote as president, on a proposal from the Communist Party's Ideological Department presented by its head, Rogelio Polanco; Morlote moved to the Party Central Committee's ideological staff (OnCuba).
- Bonet spoke as president at the union's 10th congress in Havana on 1 and 2 November 2024, attended by President Miguel Díaz-Canel (Prensa Latina).

FUNDING
UNEAC: membership union: dues, a state contribution and donations
- Article 81 of the 2020 statutes lists its assets: members' dues; the state contribution, donations, bequests and other financial sources; property it owns; artworks held in its buildings; and other income allowed to social organisations, including proceeds of its own art-market activity at home and abroad.
- Article 83 puts its economic management under the country's rules, article 84 has it reviewed at each ordinary meeting of the governing bodies, and article 85 lets its National Council, Presidency and Secretariat order audits of all its economic operations.

Red flags: 2024 expulsion of the historian Alina Bárbara López
- On 12 September 2024 UNEAC expelled López at a meeting of its Matanzas provincial committee, citing article 40 of its regulation; the grounds included criticising the "alta dirigencia de la Revolución" (the Revolution's top leadership), solidarity with the 11 July 2021 protests, and offending Julio César Pérez, a Communist Party official (14ymedio).
- PEN International records the grounds as "carrying out activities against the revolution" and "showing solidarity with the July 11 movement".
- López says she received no written decision and was told it could not be appealed (14ymedio); article 79 of the statutes says all sanctions can be appealed.
- Unverified: López says State Security agents rather than writers and artists dictated the decision (14ymedio, an independent outlet critical of the government).
- On 14 September 2024 the writer Jorge Fernández Era resigned in protest, writing that the union had done nothing about the harassment he and López had faced for two years (14ymedio).
- 14ymedio states that the union has used expulsions as a political tool since 1961, naming Guillermo Cabrera Infante in 1967, Rafael Vilches and Pedro Armando Junco.

Red flags: 2025 resignation of Laideliz Herrera Laza over the union's silence
- In August 2025 the writer Laideliz Herrera Laza, Fernández Era's wife, resigned, saying the union gave only evasive answers when he asked for legal advice after police beat him in July (Cubanet, 18 August 2025).
- She accused UNEAC of circulating collective letters against critics and of serving "una política de Estado que censura" (a state policy that censors); she spoke to Martí Noticias (Cubanet).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: News reporting on the 2024 expulsion, from an outlet critical of the government.
1. 14ymedio: "Expulsan a Alina Bárbara López de la Uneac por criticar a la 'alta dirigencia de la Revolución'" (2024)
URL: https://www.14ymedio.com/cuba/expulsan-alina-barbara-lopez-uneac_1_1106246.html
- Reports the expulsion and López's reply: the decision was verbal, statutes cannot override the Constitution's guarantee of free expression (article 56), and the union decides without legal argument.
- Names UNEAC, Julio César Pérez and Luis Morlote as president, although OnCuba reported in January 2024 that Bonet had replaced him.
2. 14ymedio: "Jorge Fernández Era renuncia a la Uneac en solidaridad con Alina Bárbara López" (2024)
URL: https://www.14ymedio.com/cuba/jorge-fernandez-renuncia-uneac-solidaridad_1_1106327.html
- Reports Fernández Era's letter to the UNEAC presidency, which says the union ignored the legal complaints he and López filed.
- Names UNEAC and its presidency.

WIDER CONTEXT
- PEN International, an advocacy group, records that police detained López for several hours in April 2024 and for 11 hours on 18 June 2024, and charged her under article 182 of the Penal Code with attempted assault on police officers, which carries three to eight years; PEN holds that the charge aims to limit her critical activity.
- The police, not UNEAC, detained her; UNEAC expelled her three months later.

BOTTOM LINE
Dues and a state contribution pay for the union, with no private sponsor of note. The weak point is that the Party steers its leadership and the union itself punishes critics, as the López expulsion and the protest resignations show.
