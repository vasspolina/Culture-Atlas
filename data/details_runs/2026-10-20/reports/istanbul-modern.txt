ISTANBUL MODERN: Istanbul, Turkey
ID: istanbul-modern
Website: https://www.istanbulmodern.org/en (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Private museum of modern and contemporary art, mainly Turkish art from 1950 on, on the Karaköy waterfront inside the Galataport cruise-port development (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 11 December 2004, founded by the Eczacıbaşı family; Oya Eczacıbaşı chairs the board. The sponsor panel names Oya Eczacıbaşı and Bülent Eczacıbaşı as founders.
- First housed in Entrepo No. 4, a former customs warehouse of about 8,000 square metres converted by Tabanlıoğlu Architects and later demolished for Galataport; from 2018 to 2022 it used the former Union Française building in Beyoğlu (Wikipedia).
- The current five-storey building by Renzo Piano, about 10,500 square metres, opened on 4 May 2023 near the original site (Wikipedia).
- The core collection came from the Eczacıbaşı family, with long-term loans from the Istanbul Museum of Painting and Sculpture, Deutsche Bank and Türkiye İş Bankası; the building has a library and a cinema (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Mondays, open to 20:00 on Fridays; tickets 600 TL and 1,000 TL regular, 350 TL and 650 TL discounted for students, teachers and over-65s; free for disabled visitors and under-12s; free for Türkiye residents on Thursdays 10:00 to 14:00 (sponsored by Paribu) and for residents aged 18 to 25 on Tuesdays 10:00 to 14:00 (ticket site).

FUNDING
Istanbul Modern: family-founded, corporate-sponsored: Eczacıbaşı, Doğuş Group and Bilgili Holding
- Founding sponsor: Eczacıbaşı. The Dr. Nejat F. Eczacıbaşı Foundation supports the permanent collection gallery (sponsor panel dated September 2026).
- Main sponsors: Doğuş Group and Bilgili. Doğuş, Eczacıbaşı and Bilgili co-funded the original museum's investment of about $5 million (Wikipedia).
- Programme sponsors: Paribu (free Thursdays), Volvo, İGA Art, Panço, Nescafe, Bosch Ev Aletleri and Visa; Mercedes-Benz sponsors membership; TAV Airports is travel sponsor and Tepta Aydınlatma gives special support (sponsor panel).
- The panel thanks Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality's Kültür AŞ for its contribution, amount not stated.
- No reporting found of annual accounts or the size of any sponsor's payment.

Red flags: artists' protests, 2011 and 2023
- In December 2011 eight artists and the collective AtılKunst asked to withdraw their works from the exhibition "Reality and Dream" after the museum refused a commissioned work by Bubi Hayon; artists entered the museum on 27 December with a banner reading "There is censorship in this museum" (Hyperallergic).
- In December 2023 protesters held a sit-in over a shortened free day and delivered over 5,500 signatures demanding full-day free access and disclosure of institutional information (ArtReview).
- Chief curator Levent Çalıkoğlu left in 2023, weeks after the new building opened; Milliyet reported "differences in principles concerning acquisitions" and the museum has not given a reason (ArtReview).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2011 censorship row and on the museum's transparency and access after the 2023 move; reviews of the building are mostly descriptive.
1. Hyperallergic: Hrag Vartanian, "Artists Pull Works from Istanbul Modern Over Censorship Claims" (2011)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/censorship-istanbul-modern-bubi-hayon/
- Hayon called the refusal of his bedpan seat a censorship process; Mürüvvet Türkyılmaz, Ceren Öyküt, Gözde İlkin, Güneş Terkol, İnci Furni, Ekin Saçlıoğlu, Neriman Polat, Leyla Gediz and AtılKunst asked to pull their works.
- Ties the row to the lack of transparency among corporate backers of major institutions in Turkey.
- Chief curator Levent Çalıkoğlu said the museum would respect the artists' decision; AICA Turkey backed the museum, saying censorship needs a third party such as the state.
2. ArtReview: Kaya Genç, "Rumours and Whispers in the Istanbul Art Scene" (2024)
URL: https://artreview.com/rumours-and-whispers-in-the-istanbul-art-scene/
- Reports the 2023 sit-in and petition and the unexplained exit of the chief curator.
- Quotes artist Halil Altındere calling the museum's unexplained developments "far worse" than the Istanbul Biennial crisis.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Istanbul Chamber of Architects said Galataport broke planning rules and warned of damage that could not be reversed; the developers rejected that (AFP via Hürriyet Daily News, 2018).
- Doğuş Holding won the 2013 Galataport tender with a $702 million bid for 30 years; in December 2024 it was transferring 49 percent of Galataport to creditor banks after debt on a loan of about 1 billion euros rose to about 1.5 billion euros (Bianet).
- Istanbul Modern's building sits inside Galataport, which its main sponsor Doğuş developed; Oya Eczacıbaşı said the museum keeps full control of its programme (Hürriyet Daily News).

BOTTOM LINE
The money is private Turkish family and corporate money: Eczacıbaşı, Doğuş, Bilgili and a long consumer-brand sponsor list, with no flagged industry among the named sponsors. The weak points are thin disclosure (no accounts, sponsors shown only as a logo panel), protests over censorship in 2011 and access in 2023, and the museum's place inside its main sponsor's waterfront property project.
