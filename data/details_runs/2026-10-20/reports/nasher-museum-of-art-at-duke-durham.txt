NASHER MUSEUM OF ART AT DUKE: Durham, USA
ID: nasher-museum-of-art-at-duke-durham
Website: https://nasher.duke.edu/about/ (checked 2026-10-25; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Duke University's art museum, at 2001 Campus Drive on Duke's central campus in Durham, North Carolina.
- Founded in 1969 as the Duke University Museum of Art in a former science building on East Campus, starting with 200 medieval works from the Ernest Brummer Collection (museum website). The new building opened on 2 October 2005 (INDY Week; the museum website gives 2005).
- Named for Raymond D. Nasher, a Duke alumnus, Texas real estate developer and sculpture collector, who gave $7.5 million in 1998 for a new museum and died on 16 March 2007 (museum website).
- Building: designed by Rafael Viñoly; the Wikipedia article gives a cost of $24 million. It stands on the former 8.8-acre meadow at Duke University Road and Anderson Street that Duke botanists used for research plants (INDY Week).
- Collection: more than 13,000 works, including pre-Columbian objects and contemporary art by artists such as Kara Walker, Kehinde Wiley and Ai Weiwei (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Michael Mezzatesta 1987 to 2003, Kim Rorschach 2004 to 2012, Sarah Schroth 2013 to 2020, Trevor Schoonmaker since May 2020 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: admission is free for all. The museum page showed hours of 12:00 to 17:00 on the day checked; the full schedule was not on the page read. The site links a Parking & Accessibility page, whose contents were not read.

FUNDING
Nasher Museum of Art at Duke: mainly university-funded: Duke University allocation, endowment income and gifts from private donors and foundations
- Revenue for 1 July 2022 to 30 June 2023 was $7,740,913 (2023 annual report). University support was $3,414,824 and endowment distributions $1,765,602.
- The same year: grants $614,980, gifts for acquisitions $513,000, other restricted giving $585,676, annual fund giving $658,176, memberships $186,163 and earned income $102,492.
- Donors giving $100,000 to $499,999 that year included the Fidelity Foundation, the William R. Kenan, Jr. Charitable Trust, the Mary Duke Biddle Foundation and the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, with individuals Trent Carmichael, Joan Kahn, Stefanie and Doug Kahn, and Thomas S. Kenan III. Bank of America gave $10,000 to $24,999.
- In 2022 Jennifer McCracken New and Jason New gave $1 million to fund free general admission (2022 annual report).
- Nancy A. Nasher and David J. Haemisegger funded the purchase of a Vik Muniz print (accession 2023.2.1, 2023 annual report; no amount) and an exhibitions fund carries their names; Nancy Nasher chairs the Board of Advisors (museum website).
- The Duke Endowment paid for the purchase of Alfredo Jaar's Lagos 2002 (accession 2021.22.1), co-funded the purchase of a Suchitra Mattai textile work (accession 2023.7.1) and supported exhibitions (2022 and 2023 annual reports; no amounts).
- Raymond Nasher gave $7.5 million and his foundation $2.5 million toward the $24 million building; donors covered the rest (INDY Week).

WIDER CONTEXT
- History of the Duke Endowment: James B. Duke created it on 11 December 1924 with $40 million, invested primarily in Duke Power Company stock. Duke founded the American Tobacco Company in 1890 and is described as a tobacco and electric power industrialist (Wikipedia). No page read shows the Endowment's money coming from tobacco today.
- The Endowment funds acquisitions and exhibitions at the museum, which is the connection.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Duke University support, endowment income and private gifts, with no current tobacco, fossil fuel, gambling or arms funder named in the sources read. The Duke Endowment funds acquisitions and exhibitions; its 1924 trust from a tobacco and power industrialist is history, not a present tobacco source. Worth watching are the donor tiers in each annual report.
