GREAT NORTHERN ARTS FESTIVAL: Inuvik, Northwest Territories, Canada
ID: great-northern-arts-festival-inuvik
Website: https://www.greatnorthernartsfestival.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual summer arts festival in Inuvik, a town of 2,500 or more people in the Beaufort Delta, on Inuvialuit and Gwich'in lands; its office is at Unit 301, 125 Mackenzie Road (festival website).
- Run by the Great Northern Arts Society, founded in 1988 as a not-for-profit society; it calls the festival the longest standing pan-Arctic arts festival (festival website).
- Began with an art show that Charlene Alexander and Sue Rose organised at the Inuvialuit Corporate Centre in 1989, bringing together about two dozen artists from Beaufort Delta communities (festival website).
- Programme: over 50 art and culture workshops and demonstrations during the festival, a fashion show, art sales, youth outreach and a small art collection shown in its year-round office (festival website).
- Scale: 100 artists from across northern Canada came for the 25th anniversary in 2013; 47 came in 2015, when free youth programming was dropped (CBC, 2015).
- Executive director: Adi Scott (Cabin Radio, 2026). Artist applications for the next festival, 7 to 11 July 2027, run from 1 November 2026 to 4 January 2027 (festival website).

FUNDING
Great Northern Arts Festival: mainly public: Canadian Heritage and the Town of Inuvik, with Indigenous bodies and northern airlines
- Canadian Heritage: $160,000 from the Canada Arts Presentation Fund for the festivals of 2025, 2026 and 2027, agreement dated 9 July 2025 to 12 July 2027 and amended 20 May 2026; an earlier grant gave $20,000 for April 2023 to March 2025 (Government of Canada grants register).
- Employment and Social Development Canada: $15,957 from Canada Summer Jobs for May to August 2025 (grants register).
- Town of Inuvik: has supported the festival since 1988 with its venue and support staff (festival website); in 2026 the council approved an in-kind donation of more than $30,000 (Cabin Radio).
- Gwich'in Tribal Council: provides a year-round office for preparations, workshops and the art collection (festival website).
- Inuvialuit Regional Corporation: pays for Inuvialuit beneficiaries to attend (festival website).
- Air North pays for artists' travel and gives volunteers and staff a flight discount; Aklak Air gives discounted fares to artists from remote communities; Northwestel paid for the 2025 fashion show; Connected North runs youth outreach; Hotií ts'eeda was youth and wellness partner in 2024 and 2025 (festival website). The website names no amounts.
- Members: $25 individual, $50 "Friend of the Festival" family and $100 corporate memberships (festival website).
- Canada Council for the Arts: in 2026 it turned down two applications, for artist travel and core costs, worth about $90,000 in all; the festival scored lowest on feasibility, and the Council said it had not applied consistently year to year (Cabin Radio, April 2026).
- Former oil sponsors: ConocoPhillips and Imperial Oil stopped funding the festival in 2015 after halting their exploration plans in the Northwest Territories; the festival's executive director, Maia Lepage, said "our corporate sponsorship is not where it used to be". That year's festival was estimated to cost nearly $500,000 (CBC, 2 July 2015).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean today: public, Indigenous and community money. The oil sponsorship is history rather than a current tie. The weak point is fragility: the festival depends on grants it cannot count on from one year to the next.
