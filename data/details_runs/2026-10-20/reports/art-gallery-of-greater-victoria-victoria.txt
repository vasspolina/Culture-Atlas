ART GALLERY OF GREATER VICTORIA: Victoria, Canada
ID: art-gallery-of-greater-victoria-victoria
Website: https://aggv.ca (checked 2026-10-20; the site answers automated requests with 403 Forbidden, so its pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Public art museum at 1040 Moss Street, about 20 minutes from downtown Victoria (Tourism Victoria).
- Founded in 1946 as the Little Centre by former members of the Federation of Canadian Artists' Vancouver Island branch; it ran from a former downtown car dealership until 1951 (Wikipedia).
- In 1951 Sarah Spencer donated Spencer Mansion and the gallery took its present name; the house, built in 1889 to designs by William Ridgeway Wilson for the Green family, was called Gyppeswyk (Wikipedia).
- Seven modernist galleries were added between 1955 and 1978; renovations in 2001 to 2003 added climate control (Wikipedia).
- A 2015 expansion design by LWPAC and Moore Architecture drew Mayor Lisa Helps' word "startling" (Attractions Management); construction planned for late 2019 was postponed over rising costs (Wikipedia).
- The collection holds about 20,000 works, the largest of any BC art museum, including about 8,000 Asian works; the garden holds the only Meiji-era Shinto shrine in North America (Wikipedia).
- Its summer street festival, the Paint-In, draws 40,000 visitors annually (Tourism Victoria); no reporting found of current hours, admission or accessibility, as the gallery's site could not be read.

FUNDING
Art Gallery of Greater Victoria: mixed: provincial grants, donations and bank sponsors
- Total revenue was C$6.11 million in 2025, with C$2.31 million in donations (Wikidata, from CRA charity data via charitydata.ca); 2024 revenue was C$4.67 million.
- The provincial government gave C$6 million in 2018 toward a C$30 million renovation project (Canadian Art).
- In 2016 the chairman of Polygon Homes, Michael Audain, pledged C$2 million through the Audain Foundation toward a C$24 million redevelopment that was contingent on C$14 million from the federal and provincial governments (Citified).
- BMO gave C$500,000 in September 2019 for the NEXT Gallery project, which named a BMO Gallery for Canadian and British Columbian Art; private and corporate gifts to the project then exceeded C$9 million (Oak Bay News).
- TD Bank Group presented the 35th Art Gallery Paint-In on 20 July 2024 (CFAX, Tourism Victoria); no reporting found of its sponsorship in 2025 or 2026.
- The 2023-24 annual report (archived copy) names Carol Anne Harper as board president and Nancy Noble as director and CEO.
- No reporting found of the government share of revenue or of whether the federal money for the NEXT Gallery arrived.

Red flags: TD Bank's 2024 money-laundering plea
- Sponsorship: TD Bank Group was the presenting sponsor of the gallery's Paint-In street festival on 20 July 2024 (CFAX).
- Plea: on 10 October 2024 TD Bank pleaded guilty in federal court in Newark, New Jersey, to Bank Secrecy Act program failures and conspiracy to commit money laundering, in a case announced by the US Department of Justice (NBC News, ABA Journal).
- Amount: penalties came to about US$3 billion, including US$1.8 billion to the Justice Department and US$1.3 billion to FinCEN, the US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (NBC News).
- Three money-laundering networks moved more than US$670 million through TD Bank accounts (NBC News, ABA Journal).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is public grants, donations and local and bank sponsorship, with no sponsor from a flagged industry found. The weak points are TD Bank Group, the 2024 Paint-In presenting sponsor, which pleaded guilty to money laundering in October 2024, and transparency: the site blocks automated reading and no current funder breakdown was found. Worth watching: the postponed expansion and who pays for it.
