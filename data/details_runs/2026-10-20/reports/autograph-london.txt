AUTOGRAPH: London, United Kingdom
ID: autograph-london
Website: https://autograph.org.uk/about-us/history

OVERVIEW
- Photography gallery and arts charity at Rivington Place, London, working on race, identity and human rights through photography.
- Founded in 1988 as the Association of Black Photographers, in a small office in the Bon Marché Building in Brixton; the organisation's history page lists seven founding signatories: Monika Baker, Merle Van Den Bosch, Armet Francis, Sunil Gupta, Michael Jess, George Shire and Lance Watson. Wikipedia says the board renamed it Autograph ABP in 2002, and that Mark Sealy became director in 1991.
- Rivington Place was designed by David Adjaye; it is a new-build of 1,445 square metres (Dezeen) that cost £8m (Dezeen, Building). Autograph and the Institute of New International Visual Arts (Iniva) commissioned it jointly in 2002 and it opened in October 2007, formally opened by Stuart Hall (Autograph). Building magazine's subheading calls it the first public gallery to be opened in London for 40 years.
- On site: two project spaces, the Stuart Hall Library, an education space, a café, workspace for local creative businesses and offices for Autograph and Iniva (Dezeen).
- Visiting: exhibitions at Rivington Place are free admission, with free tickets bookable online (Autograph).

FUNDING
Autograph: mainly public and charitable: Arts Council England and the National Lottery Heritage Fund
- Current support: the site's "supported by" footer carries the logos of Arts Council England and the Heritage Fund. Wikipedia states that Arts Council England provides regular annual funding.
- Heritage Lottery Fund: £660,500 in 2008 for a project on the history of culturally diverse Britain through photography (Wikipedia).
- Rivington Place capital cost, 2007: Arts Council England lottery capital grant of £5.9m and Barclays £1.1m (Building). Autograph's history page also names the City Fringe Partnership, the European Regional Development Fund, the London Borough of Hackney and the London Development Agency as building funders.
- Individual donors also gave to the building, and Sonia Boyce, Isaac Julien, Glenn Ligon, Hew Locke, Carrie Mae Weems and Chris Ofili created works for 50 limited-edition portfolios supporting Rivington Place (history page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Autograph is public and charitable: Arts Council England, the National Lottery Heritage Fund and, for the 2007 building, public bodies and one bank. No red flag applies.
