TAXISPALAIS KUNSTHALLE TIROL: Innsbruck, Austria
ID: taxispalais-kunsthalle-tirol-innsbruck
Website: https://www.taxispalais.art/en/

OVERVIEW
- The exhibition house of Land Tirol for international contemporary art, in the historic Palais Fugger-Taxis in Innsbruck (Land Tirol press release, 7 September 2026, translated from the German).
- Founded in 1964 as a Landesgalerie by Paul Flora, Wilfried Kirschl, Oswald Oberhuber and Magdalena Hörmann; it held Austria's first Gerhard Richter exhibition in 1973 and Austria's first solo exhibition of Iman Issa in 2020 (Land Tirol).
- The Tyrolean architect Hanno Schlögl remodelled the building in 1999 (Land Tirol). It offers about 800 square metres of exhibition space, a public library on contemporary art, culture and theory, and a small café (tyrol.tl tourism page).
- Nina Tabassomi has been managing and artistic director since 2017; Marijana Schneider takes over the artistic direction on 4 January 2027 (Land Tirol).
- It was part of the Land Tirol administration until 2021, then became a subsidiary of the Tiroler Landesmuseen following a report by the Landesrechnungshof, the Tirol state audit office (Land Tirol, 19 November 2025).
- Visiting: the building is described as barrier-free accessible (tyrol.tl tourism page).

FUNDING
TAXISPALAIS Kunsthalle Tirol: mainly public: Land Tirol, through the Tiroler Landesmuseen
- Ownership: the Tiroler Landesmuseen Betriebs GmbH holds 100 per cent of Taxispalais Kunsthalle Tirol GmbH (Land Tirol holdings report 2025, covering 2024). Land Tirol holds 60 per cent of the Landesmuseen company and the Verein Tiroler Landesmuseum Ferdinandeum holds 40 per cent (same report).
- The holdings report states that the Landesmuseen company's operations are financed, apart from its own revenue, predominantly by subsidies from Land Tirol (translated from the German).
- Land Tirol announced on 19 November 2025 that the general assembly of the Tiroler Landesmuseen had decided, with effect from January 2027, to dissolve the Kunsthalle company and integrate it administratively into the Landesmuseen (translated from the German). Programming, personnel, brand and communication stay with the Kunsthalle, and Land Tirol keeps full patronage (Land Tirol, 19 November 2025).
- Governor Anton Mattle, responsible for culture, called the integration "a logical step" (translated from the German; Land Tirol).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kunsthalle is public: Land Tirol holds 60 per cent of the Tiroler Landesmuseen, which hold the Kunsthalle company, and its subsidies are the main funding of the Landesmuseen. From January 2027 the Kunsthalle is integrated into the Landesmuseen's administration while keeping its own programme.
