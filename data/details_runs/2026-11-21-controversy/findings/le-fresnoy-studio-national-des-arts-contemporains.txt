WIDER CONTEXT
- On 1 October 2020 the interior minister, Gérald Darmanin, demanded on Twitter the "deprogramming" of "Capture", a work by Paolo Cirio showing the faces of 4,000 French police officers, due to be shown at Le Fresnoy in the group exhibition "Panorama 22"; Cirio was a visiting art professor at Le Fresnoy for the academic year 2019 to 2020 (The Art Newspaper, Anna Larkin, 4 December 2020).
- On 2 October 2020 Le Fresnoy's director, Alain Fleischer, issued a press release stating "we regret to have to give up the presentation of Paolo Cirio's work". Le Fresnoy's official statement said the work was pulled because Cirio had "violated commitments" made with the school, which Cirio denies (The Art Newspaper).
- At the exhibition's opening, artists, students and researchers taking part in Panorama 22 protested against the withdrawal, reading a statement and pasting a phrase The Art Newspaper translates as "the shame!" on the boards covering the work, and questioned Le Fresnoy's ability "to defend the autonomy of its artistic programming" (The Art Newspaper).
- Unverified: La Quadrature du Net, a digital rights campaign group, said the police union Synergie published Le Fresnoy's letter cancelling the exhibition before the artist had been told of it, and called the withdrawal censorship (La Quadrature du Net, 9 October 2020).
