MUSEUM OF THE WAR OF CHINESE PEOPLE'S RESISTANCE AGAINST JAPANESE AGGRESSION: Beijing, China
ID: museum-of-the-war-of-chinese-people-s-resistance-against-japanese-aggression
Website: https://www.1937china.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State museum of the 1931 to 1945 war with Japan inside Wanping Fortress by the Marco Polo Bridge (Lugou Bridge) in Fengtai district, Beijing, where the 1937 incident that began full-scale war took place (China News Service, state media).
- It opened on 7 July 1987; Rana Mitter writes that Deng Xiaoping, Jiang Zemin and the ideologue Hu Qiaomu authorised its construction, and that a second phase opened in 1997 for the 60th anniversary of the incident (The China Quarterly, 2000).
- The Propaganda Department of the Party Central Committee listed it in July 1997 in its first hundred national patriotic education demonstration bases (list published on The Paper's government channel).
- After more than eight months of upgrading it reopened on 7 July 2025 with the exhibition "For National Liberation and World Peace": a new sunken hall raised the display area from 6,700 to 12,200 square metres, showing 3,237 objects and 1,525 photographs (China News Service, state media).
- A museum researcher said the new exhibition strengthens the factual support for the Communist Party's role as the "mainstay" of the war (China News Service, state media; own translation).
- The museum reports more than 40 million visits since 1987 and over 30,000 objects in its collection; the new exhibition drew more than 600,000 visitors by 2 September 2025 (China News Service, state media).
- In August 2025 Beijing opened the first patriotic education base for Hong Kong and Macau youth at the museum; Hong Kong Chief Secretary Eric Chan attended and thanked the central government for its support (The Standard).
- Visiting: free with no booking, with identity document and security check; open 9:00 to 16:30, last entry 16:00, closed Mondays except national holidays and key war anniversaries (Visit Beijing).

FUNDING
Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance Against Japanese Aggression: state-funded: Beijing municipal budget
- The museum is a budget unit in the Beijing government's published 2025 final accounts. Its 2025 income was 809.67 million yuan, of which 809.30 million came from general public budget appropriations and 0.38 million from other income (Beijing Municipal Government).
- 2025 spending was 792.71 million yuan: 711.07 million on projects and 81.63 million on running costs, including 51.46 million on staff, 15.38 million on property management and 4.59 million on electricity (Beijing Municipal Government).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The main critical study is academic, not art press: a 2000 journal article on the museum as an instrument of official memory.
1. The China Quarterly: Rana Mitter, "Behind the Scenes at the Museum: Nationalism, History and Memory in the Beijing War of Resistance Museum, 1987-1997" (2000)
URL: https://library.fes.de/libalt/journals/swetsfulltext/7675425.pdf
- Argues the museum helped shape official memory that legitimises Communist Party rule in a post-Marxist China.
- Says its remote location favours tour groups from schools and work units over individual visitors, which limits dissenting readings of the site.
- Notes that schoolchildren, over 60 percent of its one million visitors in 1994, are its most uncritical audience, guided through official tours.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Chinese public money and carries no funder flag. The weak point is purpose: it is run by the state as a site of patriotic education. Worth watching: what the large 2025 project budget paid for.
