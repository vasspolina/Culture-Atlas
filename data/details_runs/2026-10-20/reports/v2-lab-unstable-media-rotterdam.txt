V2_ LAB FOR THE UNSTABLE MEDIA: Rotterdam, Netherlands
ID: v2-lab-unstable-media-rotterdam
Website: https://v2.nl

OVERVIEW
- Lab for art, technology and society at Eendrachtsstraat 10, off Witte de Withstraat in central Rotterdam, a 15 minute walk from Rotterdam Centraal Station (V2_ contact page).
- Founded in 1981 as an artist collective in a squat at Vughterstraat 234 in 's-Hertogenbosch, hence the name V2_; founding members included Alex Adriaansens and Joke Brouwer (V2_ history page).
- It issued the "Manifesto for Unstable Media" in 1987, moved to its current location in Rotterdam in 1994, and opened the V2_Lab media laboratory in May 1998 (V2_ history page).
- Its Manifestations for the Unstable Media became the biannual Dutch Electronic Art Festival, organised for the last time in 2014 (V2_ history page).
- Summer Sessions residencies for emerging artists began in 2009 and the Test_Lab series in 2006; V2_ also publishes books and keeps an online archive (V2_ history page).
- After severe cuts in government funding of the arts after 2012, V2_ reorganised drastically, let go of several employees and reduced the frequency of events; it changed its name from Institute to Lab for the Unstable Media (V2_ history page).
- Current research theme for 2025 to 2028 is Intersectional Technology (V2_ history page).
- Visiting: opening times are given per event on the website; office hours for appointments are usually Monday to Friday 10:00 to 18:00.

FUNDING
V2_ Lab for the Unstable Media: public structural funding from the Creative Industries Fund NL and the City of Rotterdam, plus EU project money and Dutch foundations
- V2_ is a foundation (stichting) recognised as ANBI (a public benefit organisation), trade register number 41082049 (V2_ funding and ANBI pages).
- 2025 to 2028: the funding page states that V2_'s activities are made possible by structural funding, shown as the logos of the Stimuleringsfonds (Creative Industries Fund NL) and the City of Rotterdam.
- V2_ collaborates in projects co-funded by the Creative Europe Programme of the EU and the European Commission's Directorate-General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology. Recurring project funders in past years include Pro Helvetia and the Mondriaan Fund (V2_ funding page).
- 2020: structural support from the City of Rotterdam and the Stimuleringsfonds Creative Industrie, structural BKV money through the Gemeentefonds, and project contributions from Stichting Bevordering van Volkskracht, the Mondriaan Fund, Société Gavigniès and the Konrad Boehmer Foundation.
- 2018: the City of Rotterdam, the Stimuleringsfonds, Stichting Bevordering van Volkskracht, the Pauwhof Fonds, the J.E. Jurriaanse Stichting and Regieorgaan SIA.
- 2015 and 2016: the Stimuleringsfonds, the City of Rotterdam, Stichting DOEN and the Mondriaan Fund.
- The director is F. Saavedra-Lara with J. C. Brouwer as deputy; the supervisory board is U Yong Hu (chair), Jens Besse, Mieke van Heesewijk and Maaike Harbers, and it works unpaid.
- V2_ publishes annual accounts for 2019 to 2023 and a certified 2025 account, and activity plans for 2021 to 2024 and 2025 to 2028 (V2_ ANBI page).

BOTTOM LINE
The structural money behind V2_ comes from the Creative Industries Fund NL and the City of Rotterdam, with EU project grants and Dutch cultural foundations for individual projects. V2_'s own history records severe cuts in government funding of the arts after 2012 and the reorganisation that followed.
