NAFASI ART SPACE: Dar es Salaam, Tanzania
ID: nafasi-art-space-dar-es-salaam
Website: https://nafasiartspace.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads, with some unfinished placeholder content)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the Light Industrial Area of Mikocheni B, Eyasi Road, Dar es Salaam (Nafasi site).
- Founded in 2008 by a small group of Tanzanian artists (Nafasi's about page). Artribune (2021) dates the idea to 2009.
- Registered as an NGO under Tanzania's NGO Act (2002), number 00NGO/00003977 (Nafasi site). A board of volunteers oversees governance; the director is Lilian Mushi (Nafasi about page).
- Built on an abandoned industrial plot; a large warehouse is the main exhibition space and holds six studios, a library and offices. Fifteen shipping containers from the port were later converted into studios, two galleries and a children's space (Artribune).
- On site: "over 20" studios and workshop spaces on the about page, plus several galleries and performance platforms, a children's art space and an art library (Nafasi site). The 10-year anniversary page counts "37 studios, exhibition and performance spaces" together.
- Programmes: Nafasi Academy for Contemporary Art, an artist residency (over 100 artists from more than 30 countries, per Artribune), and the FEEL FREE grant and incubation programme.
- Visiting: no opening hours on the pages read; the site points to a "Visit Us" section.

FUNDING
Nafasi Art Space: donor-funded non-profit: European embassies and development funders, plus own income from commissions, rents and member contributions
- Own income: commissions on works sold in its galleries, contributions from artists who use the space, and rent from its venues; also individual donations, crowdfunding and project grants (Artribune).
- Embassy of Switzerland in Tanzania: Tanzania Cultural Programme Phase 1, 2022 to 2025; also 2020 to 2021 (partners and funders page). The page also lists Pro Helvetia and SDC in 2017.
- Royal Norwegian Embassy: 2017 to 2018, 2018 to 2021 and 2022 to 2024 (partners and funders page).
- Hivos Voice 2020 to 2021; The African Arts Trust 2017; Hivos 2013 to 2016; European Union 2012 to 2016; Danida (Embassy of Denmark) 2011 to 2014; CKU (Danish Centre for Culture and Development) 2014 to 2017; NMB Bank 2017 (partners and funders page).
- With support from the Danish Embassy, a space was located and six studios were built (10-year anniversary page).
- Under "Friends of Nafasi" the partners and funders page shows Coca-Cola and Pyramid Pharma as logos, with no years or amounts. The African Arts Trust is listed for 2015 as well as 2017.
- No reporting found of amounts for any funder, and no accounts found.

Red flags: TotalEnergies, a fossil fuel partner
- Nafasi's undated VIA Creative page announces its "ongoing partnership with TotalEnergies Tanzania". VIA is a road-safety programme of the TotalEnergies Foundation; Nafasi ran it in 51 public primary schools in Dar es Salaam, and a team of five students was to represent Tanzania in the international competition on 13 October 2022. No amount is stated.
- TotalEnergies does not appear on Nafasi's partners and funders page. No reporting found of the partnership after 2022.
- Documented objection to the funder: Human Rights Watch said in July 2023 that the East African Crude Oil Pipeline, in which TotalEnergies holds a 62% stake, left displaced people with delayed or inadequate compensation. TotalEnergies rejected the accusations (Al Jazeera). The pipeline runs through Tanzania to Tanga. No reporting found of a campaign aimed at Nafasi over this.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Nafasi is funded mainly by European embassies and development funders, with its own income from commissions and rents. The weak point is the 2022 TotalEnergies partnership on VIA Creative, a fossil fuel company that holds 62% of the East African Crude Oil Pipeline, which Human Rights Watch criticised in 2023. Worth watching: whether the partnership continues and whether Nafasi publishes amounts and accounts.
