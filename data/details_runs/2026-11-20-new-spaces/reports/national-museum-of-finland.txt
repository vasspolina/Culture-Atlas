NATIONAL MUSEUM OF FINLAND: Helsinki, Finland
ID: national-museum-of-finland
Website: https://suomenkansallismuseo.fi/en (checked 2026-11-20; the old address kansallismuseo.fi redirects here)

OVERVIEW
- Finland's national museum of cultural history, at Mannerheimintie 34 in the Töölö district of Helsinki.
- Its collections began at the Royal Academy of Turku in the eighteenth century and passed to state administration in 1893 (stated by the space).
- Herman Gesellius, Armas Lindgren and Eliel Saarinen won the 1902 competition for the building, construction began in 1905, the exhibitions opened in January 1916 and the institution took its present name that year.
- The museum works as a department of the Finnish Heritage Agency and also runs nine other sites, among them Seurasaari Open-Air Museum, Olavinlinna Castle and Häme Castle.
- An extension by JKMM Arkkitehdit is being added, with a partial renovation of the main building, and its foundation stone was laid on 17 May 2024 (Senate Properties).
- The National Museum is closed for the works and reopens in April 2027 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
National Museum of Finland: public: the Finnish state through the Finnish Heritage Agency
- The Finnish Heritage Agency is an agency under the Ministry of Education and Culture, and the Minister of Science and Culture appoints the museum's Director General (stated by the space).
- Senate Properties, which is responsible for state-owned properties, is the developer of the building project: about EUR 55 million for the extension and about EUR 35 million for the main building (Senate Properties). SRV is lead contractor for the extension and NCC for the renovation.
- On 12 February 2026 the Finnish Cultural Foundation gave EUR 800,000 for the reopening exhibition, the largest grant of its round.
- NoHo Partners was chosen to run the restaurants from April 2027.
- The museum offers companies sponsorship as main partner, partner or project partner.

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 24 October 2024 the Finnish Heritage Agency ended staff negotiations aimed at cutting EUR 3.2 million from its 2025 operating costs: about 150 of roughly 220 permanent employees were to be laid off temporarily, and the agency announced reduced services and museum closures.
- The agency said the National Museum earns no income during construction and that a late opening would cost more than EUR 250,000 a month.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Finnish state's, with one private foundation grant for the reopening show. The weak point is the state's own budget squeeze, which led the parent agency to lay off staff while the museum was shut. Worth watching: the corporate partners the museum signs for the 2027 reopening.
