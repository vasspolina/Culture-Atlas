ART ENCOUNTERS FOUNDATION: Timișoara, Romania
ID: art-encounters-foundation
Website: https://artencounters.ro (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art foundation at Bd. Take Ionescu 46C, Timișoara, which runs the Art Encounters Biennial, exhibitions, a residency and a curatorial school.
- Real estate developer and collector Ovidiu Șandor started the biennial in 2015 and is the foundation's president (Europa Liberă, foundation site); biennials followed in 2017, 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025.
- Its new venue, the former Casa ISHO, was announced for September 2026; Șandor and his family provide the building as the permanent headquarters (Agerpres).
- The renovation followed a concept by Attila KIM Architects, with the ground floor fitted out with Andreea Andresoiu; it cost several hundred thousand euros (Agerpres).
- The venue holds exhibition halls, an art library and bookshop, education rooms, the Focus Art Encounters platform for experimental practices and the Brewno café with a garden (Agerpres); the opening show "Picasso en visite", with the Musée Picasso, Antibes, runs from 17 September 2026 to 17 January 2027 (foundation site).
- A lift was added and the whole space is accessible to wheelchair users (Agerpres); the 2025 biennial opened Wednesday to Sunday, 12:00 to 20:00 (Canvas). The site gives no hours or admission for the new venue.

FUNDING
Art Encounters Foundation: founder-funded with corporate and public money: Ovidiu Șandor, Ploom, Banca Transilvania, Timișoara municipality
- Ovidiu Șandor makes a steady personal contribution; he says he "spends money" on the biennial rather than making it, and private sponsors make up the rest (Europa Liberă).
- Șandor is a real estate developer (Europa Liberă); he took over the ISHO site, a former wool factory, in 2012 and plans nine buildings there: six apartment blocks, a car park, offices and a hotel (der Freitag).
- 2025 biennial: funded by the City of Timișoara through its Project Center, co-financed by the Ministry of Culture, the National Cultural Fund Administration (AFCN), the French Institute and the German Cultural Center in Timișoara (Canvas).
- 2025 corporate partners: Ploom (main partner), Banca Transilvania (strategic partner), Catena (exhibition partner); supporters ISHO, Bosch, Mewi, Sanmarco and Saint-Gobain (Canvas).
- The 2026 venue was paid for from private resources and partner companies, which the release does not name (Agerpres).
- No reporting found of the foundation's budget, accounts or sponsorship amounts.

Red flags: Japan Tobacco's Ploom as main partner, 2025
- Ploom, a heated tobacco brand marketed by Japan Tobacco, which acquired its patents and trademarks in 2015 (Wikipedia), was main partner of the 2025 biennial and co-organised its Saturday garden parties (Canvas).
- Ploom also supported "Art Encounters by Ploom", a Revista Biz series on the biennial that included a July 2025 interview with Șandor, published under an 18+ tobacco warning (Revista Biz).
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter about the Ploom sponsorship.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism. Reviews are mostly favourable; two pieces question whether a developer-funded biennial resists the gentrification it describes.
1. ArtReview: Rebecca O'Dwyer, "Art Encounters Biennial 2019, Timișoara" (2019)
URL: https://artreview.com/ar-december-2019-review-art-encounters-biennial-2019/
- The curators use existing sites to resist the city's gentrification, but the reviewer finds it hard not to see the biennial as "part of a strategy to do exactly this".
- Ties the biennial and the European Capital of Culture title to a faster transformation of the city, which multinationals favour for cheap labour.
- Names the curators Maria Lind and Anca Rujoiu; does not name Șandor or discuss funding.
2. der Freitag: Philipp Hindahl, "Art Encounters Timișoara: Auf Schnitzeljagd" (2023)
URL: https://www.freitag.de/autoren/philipp-hindahl/art-encounters-timisoara-auf-schnitzeljagd
- The biennial would look quite different without the "Immobilienmogul" (real estate mogul) Șandor, and the author finds this irritating.
- The ISHO development could stand in Frankfurt or Amsterdam; the wish to remake whole districts is rarely stated so openly.
- The biennial model "ächzt unter seinen Widersprüchen" (groans under its contradictions).

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation runs on a developer's money and building, with city, ministry and bank funding on top. The clear weak point is Japan Tobacco's Ploom as main partner of the 2025 biennial and backer of a press series about it. Worth watching: whether Ploom stays on for the new venue, and the tie between the foundation and Șandor's ISHO development.
