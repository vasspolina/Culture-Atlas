NATIONAL ART GALLERY OF NAMIBIA: Windhoek, Namibia
ID: national-art-gallery-of-namibia-windhoek
Website: https://nagn.org.na/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- State-owned art gallery at the corner of John Meinert Street and Robert Mugabe Avenue in central Windhoek (Wikipedia; NAGN annual report 2023/24).
- After independence in 1990 the Arts Association of Namibia operated as the national gallery until the National Art Gallery of Namibia Act, Act 14 of 2000, came into force (NAGN annual report 2023/24).
- It shows a permanent exhibition of Namibian, African and European art and exhibitions by local artists (Wikipedia). In 2023/24 it agreed to absorb the John Muafangejo Art Centre project and its artworks (NAGN annual report 2023/24).
- Leadership: a Board of Trustees chaired by M'kariko Amagulu, with Michelle van Wyk as director since April 2023 (NAGN annual report 2023/24). Snobia Kaputu became chief executive in October 2017 (Namibian Sun, 2019); Hercules Viljoen was director for five years (Windhoek Express, 2022).
- Building: the main gallery roof fell in after heavy rains in December 2022, and the gallery closed for roof repairs in December 2023 and January 2024 (NAGN annual report 2023/24).
- Programme: "Reconnecting with Returned Cultural Belongings" (11 April to 18 May 2024) showed 23 objects returned from Berlin's Ethnologisches Museum (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin). The 2025/2026 Bank Windhoek Triennial opened on 18 August 2026 with 277 submissions; Josef Madisia won (Bank Windhoek).
- Visiting: Monday 14:00 to 17:00, Tuesday to Friday 08:00 to 17:00, Saturday 09:00 to 14:00, closed Sunday (stated by the gallery).

FUNDING
National Art Gallery of Namibia: mainly public: an annual government grant, with foundation project money and bank sponsorship
- Government grant: N$8,348,000 in each of the years to March 2023 and March 2024 (audited statements, NAGN annual report 2023/24, note 12; auditors BDO Namibia).
- Other grants in 2023/24: N$1,500,000 for the roof project, N$473,754 from the Heinrich Böll Stiftung and N$466,000 for the John Muafangejo Art Project. Earned revenue was N$349,499, including N$211,790 in sales and N$28,189 in donations (NAGN annual report 2023/24, notes 10 and 12).
- The report names state strategic partners led by the Ministry of Education, Arts and Culture, and private-sector partners Bank Windhoek, First National Bank, Rand Merchant Bank, Standard Bank, NEO Paints, Old Mutual and Nampost (NAGN annual report 2023/24).
- Bank Windhoek Triennial: the competition began in 2008, and Bank Windhoek calls its support a longstanding commitment; the 2025/2026 edition paid prizes of N$50,000 with a solo show, N$35,000 and N$25,000 (Bank Windhoek, NAGN).
- The 2024 Berlin restitution show was co-financed by the Heinrich-Böll-Stiftung within a research initiative financed by the Gerda Henkel Stiftung (Staatliche Museen zu Berlin).

Red flags: 2023 to 2024 dispute with the Namibian Arts Association over ownership of the collection
- The Namibian Arts Association and its heritage trust say an art collection bought before 2000 was paid for by private funds and donations; the gallery says the Ministry of Education funded those purchases (NAGN annual report 2023/24, trustees' report note 8).
- Signed minutes of a meeting on 30 May 2023 put the collection under shared ownership as the National Art Heritage Collection, and in 2024 both sides agreed to divide ownership, costs and care; no agreement was signed by the time the 2023/24 accounts were finalised (same note).
- The auditors, BDO Namibia, drew attention to the disputed artwork, whose value is left out of the accounts pending the joint ownership agreement (auditor's emphasis of matter).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Forensic audit, 2019: after an opinion from the Attorney-General's office, both boards appointed Ernst and Young Namibia, sharing the cost, to determine who owns the disputed collection; its first phase produced summary findings dated 19 August 2019 (NAGN annual report 2023/24, note 8).
- March 2014: photographer Paolo Solari Bozzi withdrew his series "Namibia Sun Pictures" before its 3 March opening after the gallery said the Himba girls in some images were not old enough to consent to being photographed and asked him to take those out (ARTICLE 19, Censorpedia).
- The gallery denied this was censorship and said withdrawing the whole series was Bozzi's decision; ARTICLE 19 reported critics calling the request arbitrary.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the gallery is clean: Namibian public money, with no funder in a flagged industry. The weak point is governance, not funding: a public collection whose ownership is still unsettled.
