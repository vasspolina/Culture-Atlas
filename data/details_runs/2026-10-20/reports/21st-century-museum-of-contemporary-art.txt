21ST CENTURY MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Kanazawa, Japan
ID: 21st-century-museum-of-contemporary-art
Website: https://www.kanazawa21.jp (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- City museum of contemporary art in central Kanazawa, near the Kenroku-en garden and the Ishikawa Prefectural Museum of Art (Wikipedia).
- Opened on 9 October 2004. The city of Kanazawa owns the project and the public interest foundation Kanazawa Arts Creation Foundation runs the museum (Japanese Wikipedia).
- Building: designed by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa of SANAA, A circular, mostly glass structure about 112.5 metres across, built from April 2002 to August 2004.
- What is there: a collection of work made since 1980, long-term installations such as Leandro Erlich's Swimming Pool (2004), temporary exhibitions, a library, a lecture hall, a cafe-restaurant and a museum shop.
- Visiting: entering the building is free; exhibition tickets vary by show. The exhibition zone is open 10:00 to 18:00 (to 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays) and closed on Mondays and over the New Year (museum.or.jp directory).
- Closure: Japanese Wikipedia reports a city plan to close the museum from May 2027 to about March 2028 for repairs.

FUNDING
21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art: mainly public (the city of Kanazawa), with a corporate and individual membership scheme
- The city of Kanazawa owns the project and the foundation it funds manages the museum (Japanese Wikipedia). No reporting found of a current operating budget figure.
- Sustain membership: the museum's members page lists companies and individuals by tier. The list carries no date; the only year on the page is the 2024 copyright line in the footer. Platinum (500,000 yen a year per unit): My Book Service Co. Gold (300,000 yen a year per unit): Kanazawa Energy, Impress Art Business Division, Fukumitsuya and Dimple. Standard (50,000 yen a year per unit): about 100 further companies and bodies, including Hokkoku Bank, Kanazawa Chamber of Commerce, Nikkiso, Iwatani Central Hokuriku and the SANAA office. The page does not say how many units each member holds. Dues count as donations to the foundation.

Red flags: fossil fuels (gas utility among top-tier members)
- Kanazawa Energy is a Gold sustaining member of the museum, at 300,000 yen a year per unit on the published scale. The members page is undated and states no individual payment. The company was founded on 13 May 2021 (as Kanazawa Gas Denki, renamed on 30 November 2021), so any listing of it falls within the last five years (company site).
- Kanazawa Energy supplies city gas, runs five hydropower plants taken over from the city, and sells electricity; gas and hydropower operations began on 1 April 2022 (company site). The company site names its main shareholders as Hokuriku Electric Power, Toho Gas, the city of Kanazawa, Hokkoku Bank, Hokkoku Shimbun, Matsumura Bussan and Komatsu Gas, without percentages.
- Unverified: ownership split of Hokuriku Electric Power 48%, Toho Gas 43%, the city of Kanazawa 3% and Hokkoku Bank and Hokkoku Shimbun 2% each, from pedia.3rd-in.co.jp, an unofficial encyclopedia site; the company site gives no percentages.
- Iwatani Central Hokuriku is a Standard member, listed in the single Standard list. It is an LP gas retailer in Ishikawa, Toyama and Fukui prefectures (Be Connect Hokuriku company directory; enepi.jp, citing the 2022 LP Gas Yearbook); its own site showed a maintenance notice when opened.
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or boycott against the museum over any member or funder.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism. The one critical piece found dates from the museum's opening year and concerns the building and the museum's idea of itself.
1. Icon: Kieran Long, "21st Century Museum" (issue 020, February 2005; web page dated 23 March 2007)
URL: https://www.iconeye.com/?p=2652
- "The biggest criticism" is that SANAA's Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa "ask us to believe in the building's architectural conceits" instead of making visitors feel their effects; with its monochrome colour scheme and long corridors it is "more like being in a highly-appointed hospital".
- "Liberalism of this kind includes people but does not empower them"; Long calls the museum's description of itself, which appeals to an undefined and vague common consciousness, "in the final reckoning banal".
- Long doubts a collection shaped by consensus will be "of enduring quality" and calls the museum "more like a curiosity shop". The same piece calls the architecture "quietly radical".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is mainly the city of Kanazawa, which is not a red flag by itself. The weak point is a gas utility, Kanazawa Energy, among its top-tier sustaining members, with an ownership split that comes from a non-official source; the amount it paid is not published. Worth watching: the museum's planned 2027 closure and whether it publishes accounts and a funder list with dates.
