MUSEUM ARNHEM: Arnhem, Netherlands
ID: museum-arnhem-arnhem
Website: https://www.museumarnhem.nl/en/support-us/funds-and-partners

OVERVIEW
- Municipal-origin museum of art and cultural history on Utrechtsestraat in Arnhem (Wikipedia, nl).
- Founded in 1856; earlier names Gemeentemuseum Arnhem and Museum voor Moderne Kunst Arnhem (MMKA). The collection grew from the estate of Alexander Ver Huell, who left his paintings, drawings, prints and decorative arts to the municipality when he died in 1897 (Wikipedia, nl).
- The historic building was designed by Cornelis Outshoorn in 1873 as a gentlemen's club, mostly used by sugar planters returned from the Dutch East Indies. It passed to the Gemeentemuseum in 1920 because membership fell (Wikipedia, nl).
- The building was badly damaged in the Battle of Arnhem in 1944 and reopened as a museum of modern art in 1954 (Wikipedia, nl).
- Benthem Crouwel Architects added more than 1,100 square metres of exhibition and public space to the 1,350 square metre monument, clad in 82,000 tiles supplied by Koninklijke Tichelaar. The museum now has 1,935 square metres of exhibition space, and the restored dome houses the entrance, shop and cafe (designboom, Kat Barandy, 11 May 2022).
- The museum closed at the end of 2017; only one contractor bid in the first tender, about ten million euros above the fifteen-million-euro estimate (Binnenlands Bestuur, 27 February 2018). It reopened on 13 May 2022 (designboom).

FUNDING
Museum Arnhem: mainly public: Gemeente Arnhem
- Operator: since 1 January 2014 the museum has been run by Stichting Musea Arnhem; before that it was part of the municipality. It receives an annual municipal subsidy, which was 2,626,000 euros in 2011 (Wikipedia, nl).
- Building project: on 25 January 2016 the Arnhem council decided to extend the museum with a budget of 11.2 million euros for 550 square metres of new exhibition space (Wikipedia, nl). The renovation ended up costing 23 million euros: the Arnhem city council provided 22.5 million euros and the province of Gelderland half a million (Nieuws.nl, ANP, 13 May 2022). NOS, with Omroep Gelderland, put the work at 4.5 years (29 March 2026).
- Tile defect: NOS, with Omroep Gelderland, reported on 29 March 2026 that an independent expert concluded all 82,000 tiles on the new wing could come loose, and that the contractor was formally given notice of default in December. Fences were placed around the building and the museum stayed open.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is public: Gemeente Arnhem pays an annual subsidy to the foundation that runs it, and the city council provided 22.5 million of the 23 million euro renovation and extension that reopened in 2022, with half a million from the province of Gelderland (Nieuws.nl, ANP, 13 May 2022).
