KOORIE HERITAGE TRUST: Melbourne, Australia
ID: koorie-heritage-trust-melbourne
Website: https://kht.org.au (checked 2026-10-24; the about and governance pages returned 403 to every fetch, so its own pages could not be read; the 2022-23 annual report PDF was read)

OVERVIEW
- Indigenous not-for-profit arts and cultural organisation in the Yarra Building at Federation Square, Melbourne, with free entry (Wikipedia, Viva Energy, 2018).
- Established in 1985 by Uncle Jim Berg, Ron Castan and Ron Merkel, who sued the University of Melbourne and the Museum of Victoria for the return of Indigenous cultural material (Wikipedia).
- Holds over 100,000 items, including paintings, artefacts, books, videos and photographs, and runs the Koorie Family History Service, an Oral History program and the annual Koorie Art Show (Wikipedia, annual report).
- In 2017 Federation Square management applied to demolish the Yarra Building and move the Trust for a proposed Apple Store; Heritage Victoria refused the application in April 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Expansion planned: the Trust will take all three floors of the Yarra Building, growing from 1,145 to 1,740 square metres, with new galleries, a foyer and shop (2022-23 annual report).
- Governance: an incorporated association whose rules require more than 50% of the board to be Indigenous; Ian Hamm chaired the board in 2022-23 and Tom Mosby was CEO (annual report).
- No reporting found of opening days or accessibility from a page opened.

FUNDING
Koorie Heritage Trust: mixed: Victorian and federal grants, trading income, sponsors and philanthropy
- Year to 30 June 2023: grant and funding income $1,617,021 (2022: $1,788,011); trading income $2,865,439; other income $517,836; shop income $269,232; surplus $1,217,409; net assets $12,944,580 (annual report).
- Government: Creative Victoria's Organisational Investment Program funds the exhibitions program; the First Peoples State Relations Group funds the Koorie Family History Service, the Oral History Program and operations; also the Australia Council, the Indigenous Visual Arts Industry Support Program, the City of Melbourne and the National Indigenous Australians Agency (annual report).
- Expansion funders: the Indigenous Land and Sea Corporation, the Aboriginal Community Infrastructure Program, Melbourne Arts Precinct Corporation and the Gandel Foundation (annual report).
- Partners and sponsors listed for 2022-23: ANZ, Lendlease, RMIT University, Viva Energy Australia, the Ian Potter Foundation, Gordon Darling Foundation, Krystyna Campbell-Pretty and Family, the Orloff Family Charitable Trust, Metro Tunnel Creative Program, Cross Yarra Partnership and Gandel Philanthropy. Bequests came from the estates of Norma Gleeson, Lilian Renard and Barbara Rae Praetz.
- Donations of $5,000 or more in the annual giving appeal: Chris and Cheryl Thomas and Peter Nash (annual report).

Red flags: fossil fuels, Viva Energy
- Viva Energy became Principal Corporate Supporter for collections, exhibitions and public programs in December 2018 on a three-year term; no amount was disclosed (Viva Energy press release, 13 December 2018).
- It funds the Oral History program, school holiday programs, the Koorie Krismas celebrations and the Viva Energy Australia Award at the Koorie Art Show (Viva Energy, 2018). The 2022-23 annual report still thanks it for the Oral History program and lists the Viva Energy Australia 3D Award.
- Viva owns the Geelong oil refinery, which supplies about 50% of Victoria's fuel, and sells Shell-branded fuel under a licence to 2029; Vitol holds just under 30% (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a protest, open letter or boycott aimed at the Trust over Viva's money.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The Trust's board must be more than 50% Indigenous, and its income comes from grants, trading and a long list of named sponsors. The one flagged funder is Viva Energy, an oil refiner and fuel retailer that has backed the Trust since 2018 and was still thanked in the 2022-23 report, with no amount published. Worth watching: whether Viva is named in later annual reports, since none after 2022-23 was found.
