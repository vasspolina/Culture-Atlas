Red flags: 2026 strikes over pay
- Arts Centre Melbourne workers rejected a pay offer of 3 per cent, which the Community and Public Sector Union called "a wage cut in real terms", and announced protected industrial action for August 2026 (CPSU Victoria, 5 August 2026).
- Union members took protected industrial action for the first time in decades; more than 95 percent of members voted for it, and the unions said staff wages had fallen 10 percent behind inflation since 2022, with almost 40 percent of the workforce paid at the minimum award rate (Limelight, Izabella Nantsou, 3 September 2026; paywalled, only excerpts were read).
- Limelight wrote that management's offer was in line with the Victorian government's wage cap; the unions in the talks were the Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance, CPSU Victoria and the Electrical Trades Union (Limelight, 3 September 2026).
- A 24-hour strike on 11 September 2026 postponed Macy Gray's Hamer Hall concert until October and cancelled Shake & Stir Theatre Co's GRIMM and Burn the Floor: Luminous (Australian Associated Press, Liz Hobday, 11 September 2026).
- The CPSU's Victorian secretary, Jiselle Hanna, said the union was left with no choice "until the government funds the Arts Centre properly" (AAP, 11 September 2026).
- A Victorian government spokesperson said the government respected workers' right to take protected industrial action; Arts Centre Melbourne said it "works closely with their staff and unions to progress a new agreement" (AAP, 11 September 2026).
- Union members then voted to accept an enterprise agreement giving a 13 percent pay rise over three years (5, 4 and 4 percent) and a one-off payment of $4,350 pro rata, after a campaign that included two 24-hour stop-work actions (Limelight, Jason Blake, 29 September 2026; paywalled, only excerpts were read).
