UZBEKISTAN ART AND CULTURE DEVELOPMENT FOUNDATION: Tashkent, Uzbekistan
ID: uzbekistan-art-and-culture-development-foundation-tashkent
Website: https://acdf.uz/ (checked 2026-10-24; loads, but the page carries mostly news headlines and no funding or board information)

OVERVIEW
- State cultural body in Tashkent that commissions the Bukhara Biennial and runs a wider programme of heritage restoration, architecture, education and exhibitions.
- Founded by presidential decree in 2017 (The Spectator Australia). Its site calls it the "Foundation for the Development of Culture and Art under the Administration of President of the Republic of Uzbekistan".
- Chaired by Gayane Umerova, who is also commissioner of the Bukhara Biennial. The Korea Herald also names Saida Mirziyoyeva, head of the presidential administration and eldest daughter of the president, as a central figure in shaping the country's cultural landscape.
- Programmes listed by e-flux: the Aral Culture Summit (2025), the Center for Contemporary Arts in Tashkent with Sara Raza as artistic director, and a new National Museum designed by Tadao Ando.
- Bukhara Biennial: first edition from 5 September to 23 November 2025, theme "Recipes for Broken Hearts", with over 70 works in historic buildings; the biennial site reports 1.8 million visitors.
- Visiting: the foundation has no single venue, and no admission details were found. Pin-Up reports that the 1.8 million visitor count includes people who crossed the free urban zone.

FUNDING
Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation: state-funded: a presidential foundation, with Saudi partners on part of the biennial
- Hyperallergic describes it as state-funded; Pin-Up calls it a "deep-pocketed state body". Neither gives a budget, and no budget, accounts or donor list was found on the foundation's site.
- The foundation commissioned the Bukhara Biennial and produced all 2025 commissions (Bukhara Biennial site).
- AlMusalla Prize display: Saudi companies ACWA Power and Vision Invest made possible the display of the prize-winning prayer space at the biennial, which the Diriyah Biennale Foundation organised (e-flux). No amount is stated.
- ACWA Power also supported the "Central Asian Women" panel (Times of Central Asia).
- A New Eastern Europe interviewee, Tashkent activist Timur Karpov, calls the spending "exorbitant by Uzbek standards" (his claim; no figure).
- No reporting found of Bukhara Oil or the Uzbekneftegaz refinery as a biennial partner in the pages I could open. The partners page in the Internet Archive could not be read.

Red flags: Gulf state partner
- Diriyah Biennale Foundation: the Saudi body organised the AlMusalla Prize shown in Bukhara. On 4 November 2025 Saudi culture minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah bin Farhan and Umerova welcomed the collaboration between the Diriyah Biennale Foundation and the Bukhara Biennale (Arab News).
- ACWA Power and Vision Invest: the Saudi companies behind the display, as above (e-flux). ACWA's chairman said Uzbekistan is its largest investment market outside Saudi Arabia (ACWA Power statement).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Several pieces argue the biennial is state image-building; others call that charge weak because the audience was local. Little criticism of the foundation as an organisation.
1. Hyperallergic: Nevdon Jamgochian, "The Bukhara Biennial Will Break Your Heart" (2025)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/1052634/the-bukhara-biennial-will-break-your-heart
- Argues the biennial is "a part of this system" of artwashing and compares it to Azerbaijan's YARAT Center and Desert X AlUla; "Its very existence depends on a restrictive autocratic structure."
- Says the biennial did not answer questions about government involvement.
- Praises the art itself as "important, valid, and validating".
2. New Eastern Europe: Anna Gzubicka, "Bukhara delights with art, while the authorities count political dividends" (2026)
URL: https://neweasterneurope.eu/2026/02/20/bukhara-delights-with-art-while-the-authorities-count-political-dividends/
- Frames the event as artwashing; cites Uzbekistan's 148th place in the 2025 Reporters Without Borders index and Karpov: the biennale is "exclusively about international PR and artwashing".
- Says some installations are loosely tied to their themes.
3. The Spectator Australia: Alex Diggins, "Am I a useful idiot visiting Uzbekistan's first art biennial?" (2026)
URL: https://www.spectator.com.au/2026/01/am-i-a-useful-idiot-visiting-uzbekistans-first-art-biennial/
- Trip sponsored by the foundation. An artist calls the project "icky" and says it "feels like it's for the government, not the people"; Umerova answers "It's not about trying to change international perceptions."
4. PIN-UP: Andrew Pasquier, "STATECRAFT" (undated)
URL: https://www.pinupmagazine.org/articles/bukhara-biennial-uzbekistan-architecture-art-and-culture-foundation
- Calls the biennial "soft power" but argues the artwashing charge is weaker because the audience was local. The author was hosted by the foundation.
5. Artforum: Rachel Wetzler, column on the Bukhara Biennial (2025)
URL: https://www.artforum.com/columns/bukhara-biennial-on-site-rachel-wetzler-1234736624/
- Paywalled; the page could not be opened, so its argument is not reported here.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Hyperallergic cites Amnesty International on censorship, LGBTQ+ rights violations and harassment of journalists in Uzbekistan, and the violent suppression of the 2022 Karakalpakstan protests.
- The foundation operates under the president's administration, whose head is the president's daughter (Korea Herald), so these criticisms attach to its patron.

BOTTOM LINE
This is state money, directed from the presidency, and the foundation publishes no accounts, donor list or board. The one documented outside funding is Saudi: the Diriyah Biennale Foundation, ACWA Power and Vision Invest at the 2025 biennial. Worth watching: the 2027 edition's partner list and any fossil-fuel partner, which this research could not confirm.
