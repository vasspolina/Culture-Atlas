ONASSIS STEGI: Athens, Greece
ID: onassis-stegi
Website: https://www.onassis.org (checked 2026-10-24; pages returned empty to the reader here, so the site's text was not read; facts below come from other pages)

OVERVIEW
- Performing arts and culture venue on Syngrou Avenue, Athens, owned by the Onassis Foundation (Wikipedia, "Onassis Stegi").
- Built by the Alexander S. Onassis Public Benefit Foundation; construction began in 2004, the opening ceremony was on 7 December 2010 with President Karolos Papoulias, and it opened to the public on 11 December 2010. It was formerly called the Onassis Cultural Centre (Wikipedia).
- The Foundation is named for Alexander Onassis, who died in 1973; Aristotle Onassis set it up in his will and it dates from 1975 (Wikipedia, "Alexander S. Onassis Foundation").
- Building: 18,000 square metres on a three-acre plot, designed by the French firm Architecture-Studio, chosen through a competition; it has two main halls of about 900 and 200 seats, an exhibition space and a restaurant (Wikipedia).
- Programme: theatre, dance, concerts, film, lectures and conferences, co-productions with European theatres, and digital art shows; Wikipedia gives about 300,000 visitors a year.
- Visiting: No reporting found of opening days, admission or accessibility in the pages read.

FUNDING
Onassis Stegi: foundation-funded: the Alexander S. Onassis Public Benefit Foundation, paid for by shipping and real estate profits
- The Public Benefit Foundation's activities "are funded exclusively by the profits of the Business Foundation," which operates mainly in shipping and real estate (Wikipedia, "Alexander S. Onassis Foundation").
- The Foundation funded the construction of the Stegi (Wikipedia, "Onassis Stegi").
- Shipping arm: Olympic Shipping and Management is controlled by the Onassis Foundation and operated 18 crude oil tankers in January 2023 (Indian Transport & Logistics Network, 31 January 2023).
- Origin of the founder's money: Aristotle Onassis built what Wikipedia calls the world's largest privately owned shipping fleet, with long-term fixed-price time charters from Mobil, Socony and Texaco (Wikipedia, "Aristotle Onassis").
- Onassis Public Schools, a separate Foundation programme: the Foundation committed 160 million euros over 12 years for 22 state schools, with teacher salaries paid by the Greek state (To Vima, 28 January 2025).
- No reporting found of corporate sponsors of the Stegi, its budget or its board; the Wikipedia entry names no sponsors.

Red flags: crude oil tanker income (fossil fuels)
- The Foundation's activities are funded by the profits of the Business Foundation, which operates mainly in shipping and real estate (Wikipedia), and the Foundation controls Olympic Shipping and Management, which ran 18 crude oil tankers in January 2023 (Indian Transport & Logistics Network).
- No reporting found of the tanker count or the Foundation's shipping income for 2025 or 2026.

Red flags: strike and protest over Onassis Public Schools
- The Federation of Secondary Education Officials (OLME) held a three-hour work stoppage on 17 February 2025 against converting public schools into Onassis Public Schools, with a protest in Syntagma Square. It argued the change threatens neighbourhood schools and could increase privatisation and weaken state accountability (To Vima, 15 February 2025).
- Teachers at the 3rd General Lyceum of Kozani said no consultation was held with teachers or parents (To Vima, 28 January 2025). Deputy Minister Zetta Makri replied that the state will have a majority on the steering committee (To Vima).
- To Vima reports no direct response from the Foundation to the strike.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The one critical analysis found is an academic article, and it criticises private foundations in Athens in general.
1. International Journal of Urban and Regional Research: Penny Koutrolikou, Antonis Papangelopoulos and Christos Georgakopoulos, "Athens's philanthrocapitalist landscape within the crisis-austerity conjuncture" (2025)
URL: https://www.ijurr.org/article/athenss-philanthrocapitalist-landscape-within-the-crisis-austerity-conjuncture/
- Argues that philanthrocapitalist foundations have grown in influence over Athens during about 15 years of crises, changing the city's image, its governance and its political imaginaries.
- The journal's editorial note names the Onassis and Niarchos Foundations among the entities shaping urban governance and landmark cultural projects. The abstract does not name them.
- Academic journal, not art press; only the abstract and the editorial note were read. It does not name the Stegi.

WIDER CONTEXT
- Stills, 2015: the Stegi's Fast Forward Festival 2 projected Kris Verdonck's video work "Stills", which shows nudes, in Klafthmonos Square. After a priest complained, police were involved and the Stegi halted the projection on 26 May 2015. Its statement said it was "forced" to halt the screening and called nudity "an element of artistic expression" (in.gr, 29 May 2015, quotations translated by me from Greek).
- Connection: the state action was against the Stegi's own programme. The Greek state does not fund or run the Stegi, so this is context about artistic freedom in Greece, not a funding issue.

BOTTOM LINE
The Stegi is paid for by the Onassis family foundation, whose income comes from shipping and real estate, and the shipping arm ran crude oil tankers as of 2023. The weak points are that tanker income, the unpublished budget and sponsors, and the 2025 teachers' strike against the Foundation's school programme. Watch whether the Foundation publishes accounts for the Stegi.
