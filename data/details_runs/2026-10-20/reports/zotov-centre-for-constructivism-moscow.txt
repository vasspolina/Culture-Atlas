ZOTOV CENTRE FOR CONSTRUCTIVISM: Moscow, Russia
ID: zotov-centre-for-constructivism-moscow
Website: https://centrezotov.ru (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for the study of constructivism, with exhibitions, lectures, a cinema, children's programmes and a shop, at Khodynskaya Street 2, building 1, in the Presnya district of Moscow.
- Opened on 23 November 2022 with the exhibition "1922. Constructivism. The Beginning", which showed more than 300 works from 17 museums, institutions and private collections (The Blueprint).
- The idea came from VTB Bank and the architecture bureau SPEECH (Russian: SPICH) led by Sergey Choban (The Blueprint, Vedomosti).
- Housed in Bakery Plant No. 5, named for Vasily Zotov, a constructivist bakery completed in 1931 to a design by Georgy Marsakov; it operated until 2006 and has regional heritage status (centre's about page, Art Focus Now, Mos.ru).
- On site: exhibition floors, two cinema halls, a book club, a lecture hall, a children's club (The Blueprint) and "Forum", a free ground-floor exhibition space.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 11am to 10pm, closed Monday. Ticket prices and accessibility details were not on the pages read.

FUNDING
Zotov Centre for Constructivism: corporate-sponsored: VTB Bank, which also owns the building
- VTB Bank is named "General Partner" on the centre's partners page and describes the centre as its cultural project. VTB owns the building and kept the Zotov name (Art Focus Now, 2022). No amounts are published.
- RBC Style (May 2025) wrote that the centre opened "with the support of VTB" and has a grant programme for research projects whose size and sources are not disclosed.
- Patrons (mecenaty) named on the partners page: Olga and Igor Dergunov, and Natasha Belaya. No reporting found on who they are or what they gave.
- Partners named on the same page include the State Tretyakov Gallery, the Shchusev Museum of Architecture, the Pushkin Museum, the Russian Museum and Mosfilm; media partners include The Art Newspaper Russia and Afisha.
- Land history: according to Art Focus Now (January 2023), the land was bought from the Moscow government in 2006 by businesses owned by Alisher Usmanov and Vasily Anisimov, and VTB then reconstructed the site and built the museum.
- No annual report, accounts or board were found on the pages read.

Red flags: VTB Bank, a sanctioned general partner
- VTB is the centre's general partner and building owner.
- The Russian state agencies held over 90% of VTB's capital as of February 2022: the Deposit Insurance Agency 47.22%, the Ministry of Finance 32.88% and the Federal Agency for State Property Management 12.13% (Wikipedia).
- Sanctions on VTB: US sectoral sanctions from 29 July 2014; EU from 31 July 2014; UK from 1 August 2014; new US and UK sanctions on 24 February 2022; removal from SWIFT in March 2022 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a protest or boycott aimed at the centre.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the centre exists. Two Art Focus Now pieces question its claims and its private-museum model; a Novaya Gazeta review criticises one exhibition's curating. No piece found attacks VTB's role directly.
1. Art Focus Now: Dmitri Bavilsky, "The Rise and Fall of Moscow's Private Museums: A Study in Oligarchic Museology" (2023)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/discoveries/the-rise-and-fall-of-moscows-private-museums-a-study-in-oligarchic-museology
- Argues Zotov is "the apotheosis of the trend towards large, private museums in Moscow", which burnish owners' images and raise property values.
- Links such projects to gentrification: the building is surrounded by three 44-storey residential towers.
- Questions whether, given sanctions and Russia's economic outlook, another project of this kind will follow.
2. Art Focus Now: Irina Mak, report on the opening (2022)
URL: https://artfocusnow.com/news/moscow-constructivist-landmark-turns-into-a-new-arts-institution/
- Calls the press release's claim to be "the first institution in Russia dedicated to Constructivism" "somewhat exaggerating", since constructivism has been studied for decades.
- Notes VTB first promised the site to the Pushkin State Museum of Fine Arts for a video art centre, then took it back.
3. Novaya Gazeta: Grigory Mirsky, review of "Russian Unbelievable" (the exhibition "Русское невероятное") at Zotov (2025, date from the page address)
URL: https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2025/02/04/russkoe-neveroiatnoe-i-likho
- Asks whether the curators' central idea is too broad and overwhelms the material, in his words (my translation).
- Finds the show more educational than artistic and the true constructivist works few.
- Praises the restoration of the building. Does not mention VTB; criticises the exhibition, not the institution's funding.

BOTTOM LINE
The centre is a bank's project: VTB, which is majority held by Russian state agencies and under US, EU and UK sanctions since 2014 with new sanctions in 2022, is general partner and owner of the building. The weak points are dependence on a single sanctioned sponsor, undisclosed amounts and patrons, and the absence of published accounts or a board. Watch whether the patrons Dergunov and Belaya are identified.
