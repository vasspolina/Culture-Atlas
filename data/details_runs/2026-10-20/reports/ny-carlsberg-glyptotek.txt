NY CARLSBERG GLYPTOTEK: Copenhagen, Denmark
ID: ny-carlsberg-glyptotek
Website: https://www.glyptoteket.com (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Art and antiquities museum at Dantes Plads 7, Copenhagen (museum website).
- Built from the private collection of the brewer Carl Jacobsen, first shown in 1882 at his villa; he donated it in 1888 to the Danish State and the City of Copenhagen, and the purpose-built museum opened on 1 May 1897 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Vilhelm Dahlerup's 1897 red-brick building, a later expansion by Hack Kampmann inaugurated in 1906, a 1996 addition by Henning Larsen, and a central Winter Garden with a copper-and-wrought-iron dome (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Greek, Roman and Etruscan antiquities, more than 1,900 Egyptian pieces, French 19th-century art including all of Degas's bronzes, and Danish Golden Age painting; more than 10,000 works (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: closed Mondays; Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 17:00, Thursday until 21:00. Adults DKK 150 at the museum (135 online), under 27 and students DKK 120, under 18 free, free entry on the last Wednesday of each month. The visit page gives no accessibility details (museum website).

FUNDING
Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek: foundation-funded: the Carlsberg Foundation and the Ny Carlsberg Foundation, plus a Møller foundation
- Carlsberg Foundation and Ny Carlsberg Foundation: announced on 5 March 2026 DKK 1.5 billion, DKK 750 million each, for restoration and technical upgrades of the listed building complex; the project budget is DKK 2 billion. The Ny Carlsberg Foundation gave a further DKK 80 million between 2016 and 2025 for studies, the building programme and the schematic design (Carlsberg Foundation).
- Origin of the money: the Ny Carlsberg Foundation was founded in 1902 by the brewer Carl Jacobsen, who that year transferred his Ny Carlsberg brewery to the Carlsberg Foundation, which his father J.C. Jacobsen had founded in 1876 (lex.dk).
- A.P. Møller og Hustru Chastine Mc-Kinney Møllers Fond til almene Formaal: DKK 150 million for the restoration of the dome and the indoor Winter Garden (TV 2 Kosmopol, undated page). The same article states that A.P. Møller Fonden owns A.P. Møller Holding, which owns a large part of the shares of the shipping company Mærsk. The museum closes to visitors when the work starts in 2028 (TV 2 Kosmopol).
- Augustinus Fonden: supported the purchase of Gauguin's "La neige à Copenhague", together with the Ny Carlsberg Foundation and Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond; the foundation's page is tagged 2022 and gives no amounts (Ny Carlsberg Foundation). No reporting found of a 2025 Augustinus Fonden grant of DKK 1.53 million.
- No reporting found of the state operating grant or ticket income.

Red flags: tobacco money (Augustinus Fonden)
- Augustinus Fonden owns Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker (Wikipedia), which held more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group on 1 May 2025; the investor page lists no other holder above 5% except C.W. Obel with more than 10% (Scandinavian Tobacco Group).

Red flags: antiquities from dealers, as reported
- Hecht: ArtsJournal (2009) reported that Italy's dispute with the museum concerned Etruscan and Greco-Roman objects the Glyptotek bought from the American dealer Robert Hecht, then on trial in Rome. Italy calls such objects looted.
- Medici: Chasing Aphrodite, a blog on the antiquities trade, reports that the criminal sentence of the dealer Giacomo Medici identified objects in the museum shown in Polaroids seized in his warehouse, and that the museum denied wrongdoing until 2007 (quoting the Politiken journalist Camilla Stockmann).
- Italy: on 5 July 2016 the museum and Italy announced an agreement to return objects; Politiken listed 501 objects and fragments (both as reported by Chasing Aphrodite).
- Türkiye: the museum announced on 26 November 2024 that it would return a bronze head of Septimius Severus, acquired in 1970, which it concluded came from Bubon (The Local Denmark).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No review criticises the museum's programme or funders. The criticism found concerns how it acquired antiquities, in a blog on the antiquities trade and in news outlets; none of it is by an art critic.
1. Chasing Aphrodite: no byline shown, "The Danish Connection: Holding on to Loot at the Ny Carlsberg Glypotech of Copenhagen" (2013, updated 2016)
URL: https://chasingaphrodite.com/2013/03/14/the-danish-connection-holding-on-to-loot-at-the-ny-carlsberg-glypotech-of-copenhagen/
- Says Giacomo Medici's sentence identified museum objects shown in Polaroids seized in his warehouse, and that the museum denied wrongdoing until 2007, then sent documents on objects bought from Medici and Hecht in the 1970s to Italy after political pressure.
- Updated in July 2016 with the Italy agreement and the Politiken list of 501 objects; the post credits Politiken's reporting.
2. ArtsJournal: Laura Collins Hughes, "Danish Museum Resists Italy's Bid For Artifacts' Return" (2009)
URL: https://artsjournal.org/?p=44471
- Italy found the Glyptotek harder to resolve matters with than American museums, over objects bought from Hecht. Archive excerpt only.
3. The Local Denmark: "Denmark to return severed head of ancient statue to Türkiye" (2024)
URL: https://www.thelocal.dk/20241127/denmark-to-return-severed-head-of-ancient-statue-to-turkiye
- Reports the Turkish embassy requested the head's return in May 2023 and that the museum agreed on 26 November 2024. Director of collections Rune Frederiksen told AFP the museum was "not as sure as we perhaps were 25-30 years ago." News report, not criticism by a critic.

BOTTOM LINE
The restoration money comes from the foundations the Jacobsen family created and from a Møller foundation. The weak points are Augustinus Fonden's support for the 2022 Gauguin purchase, whose parent holds more than 25% of Scandinavian Tobacco Group, and reported purchases of antiquities from dealers, which ended in returns to Italy in 2016 and Türkiye in 2024.
