CHAPTER: Cardiff, UK
ID: chapter
Website: https://www.chapter.cymru/ (checked 2026-10-20; the record's https://www.chapter.org now redirects to askchapter.org, the site of a US Medicare advisory company unrelated to the arts centre)

OVERVIEW
- Multi-artform arts centre on Market Road in Canton, Cardiff, with a free contemporary art gallery, cinemas and theatres (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the Welsh artists Christine Kinsey and Bryan Jones with the journalist Mike Flood; it opened in 1971 (Wikipedia).
- The building was Canton High School, built in 1905; Ash Sakula Architects led a £3.8 million redevelopment that reopened in November 2009 and won a RIBA Wales Award in 2010 (Wikipedia).
- After a donation from the local benefactor David Seligman, the main theatre and studio were renamed Theatr Seligman and Stiwdio Seligman in 2015 (Wikipedia).
- On site: a gallery, two cinemas (188 and 57 seats), two theatres, bars, a café, over 60 workspaces, a community garden and the regional Film Hub Wales; it is a venue for the Artes Mundi prize (Wikipedia, stated by the space).
- Visiting: the building opens from 9am every day, closing at 9pm or 10pm; step-free entry, a lift, a Changing Places toilet, free companion seats and CEA Card and Hynt carer tickets (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Chapter: mixed: Arts Council of Wales, BFI and Welsh Government grants, trusts, and earned income from cinema, café and hire
- Income was £3,567,569 and spending £3,879,071 in the year to 31 March 2025; income was £3,588,469 the year before (Charity Commission data via OpenCharities).
- Arts Council of Wales core grant: £681,491 in 2023-24 and £681,337 in 2022-23 (2024 accounts).
- After the Arts Council of Wales Investment Review, Chapter stayed a portfolio organisation but its grant was cut by £291,000 a year for 2024 to 2027 (2024 accounts). Sharon Stone, Chapter's director of finance, told the Senedd culture committee in October 2024: "We've gone from close to £680,000 a year to £390,000 a year", and pointed to a 43% cut in the latest funding round (Caerphilly Observer).
- In 2023-24 core grants were 19% of income and restricted grants 18%; earned income from tickets, rents, donations and hospitality was 39% of turnover (2024 accounts).
- Welsh Government gave £60,000 for Perspectives, a diversity programme with the Arts Council of Wales and Amgueddfa Cymru (2024 accounts).
- Restricted grants in the 2024 accounts name the BFI, Cardiff Council, Ffilm Cymru, the Colwinston Trust (for Experimentica 2024), the Foyle, Garfield Weston, Freelands, Henry Moore, Rayne and Simon Gibson foundations, the Canada Council and the National Grid Community Matters Fund.
- Donations included £30,000 from the Moondance Foundation in each of 2022-23 and 2023-24; a prior-year restricted fund from Arts & Business was "to match fund Admiral sponsorship" (2024 accounts).
- The 2024 trustees' report says Chapter was developing a relationship with the Fidelity Foundation and had made a "substantial ask" to renovate Stiwdio Seligman; no award is reported.
- The charity owed £472,288 on a Charity Bank loan at 31 March 2025 (2025 auditor letter).
- No reporting found of the Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation UK Branch grant for Experimentica 2026 named in the record; the current supporters page shows only National Lottery and BFI logos.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The only pressure on record is financial: the 2024 Arts Council of Wales cut, which Chapter's own directors raised before the Senedd.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Chapter is a mix of Welsh public funding, BFI and lottery money, trusts and its own cinema, bar and hire income. The record's fossil fuel flag rests on a Gulbenkian grant that no page opened here confirms; the only energy-sector name in the 2024 accounts is the National Grid Community Matters Fund. Worth watching: the record's website now leads to an unrelated US company, and free reserves stood at £16,726 at 31 March 2024, before the grant cut began.
