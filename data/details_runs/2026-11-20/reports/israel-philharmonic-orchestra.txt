ISRAEL PHILHARMONIC ORCHESTRA: Tel Aviv, Israel
ID: israel-philharmonic-orchestra
Website: https://www.ipo.co.il (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Symphony orchestra based in Tel Aviv; since 1957 its principal hall has been Heichal HaTarbut, the Charles Bronfman Auditorium (Wikipedia).
- Founded on 26 December 1936 as the Palestine Orchestra by the violinist Bronislaw Huberman, who persuaded 75 Jewish musicians from major European orchestras to immigrate; Arturo Toscanini conducted the first concert at the Levant Fair in Tel Aviv (the orchestra's history page).
- Renamed the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra in 1948, after the State of Israel was created (Wikipedia).
- The hall opened as the Mann Auditorium with 2,800 seats, replacing Ohel Shem Hall, which seated 620; its renovation began in August 2011 (the orchestra's history page).
- Zubin Mehta was music director from 1977 to 2019; Lahav Shani took over in 2020 and in January 2025 his contract was extended to 2032 (Wikipedia).
- In 1958 it received the Israel Prize in music, the first organisation to do so; since November 1938 it has kept a de facto ban on Wagner's music (Wikipedia).
- 2026 is its 90th season, marked by a 90th Anniversary Festival (the orchestra's website).

FUNDING
Israel Philharmonic Orchestra: mixed: subscriptions and ticket sales, donors in Israel and abroad, and a minority share of Israeli government money
- Budget shares, 2007: 15 percent of the annual budget came from the Israeli government, about 50 percent from subscription sales, and the rest from philanthropists in Israel and abroad (J. The Jewish News of Northern California, 5 January 2007).
- American Friends of the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra (AFIPO), formally established in 1980, says its donors "provide millions of dollars of funding to the Israel Philharmonic each year". Its president is James C. Ackerman; Zubin Mehta, Itzhak Perlman, Lahav Shani and Pinchas Zukerman are honorary co-chairmen (AFIPO).
- The Israel Philharmonic Orchestra Foundation, founded in 1981, pays for international tours, music education and events in Israel and keeps an endowment fund (Wikipedia). Friends' associations abroad were founded under its auspices between 1987 and 1996 (the orchestra's history page); the orchestra's site also links to an Israel Philharmonic Foundation UK.
- The hall: Frederic R. Mann was the initiator and principal benefactor of the 1957 auditorium (the orchestra's history page), which now carries Charles Bronfman's name (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2025 protest at the Paris Philharmonie concert
- On 6 November 2025 protesters set off red flares twice during the orchestra's concert at the Paris Philharmonie, after one shouted "Israel assassin"; the musicians left the stage and later returned to finish the concert. Video showed audience members lunging at protesters, and activists also demonstrated outside (The New Arab, 7 November 2025).
- Four people were detained. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez wrote on X that "nothing can justify" the disruptions (The New Arab).

Red flags: 2025 cancellation of a Ghent concert over Lahav Shani's post at the orchestra
- Flanders Festival Ghent cancelled the Munich Philharmonic's concert of 18 September 2025 because its conductor, Lahav Shani, is music director of the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra. The festival said it was "unable to provide sufficient clarity about his attitude" to the Israeli government, which it called "the genocidal regime in Tel Aviv" (OperaWire, 10 September 2025).
- The Munich Philharmonic and the City of Munich said they were "appalled" and rejected "placing Israeli artists under general suspicion" (OperaWire).

Red flags: 2011 disruption of the BBC Proms concert in London
- On 1 September 2011 pro-Palestinian protesters disrupted the orchestra's concert at the Royal Albert Hall and the radio broadcast was interrupted; the concert was rebroadcast a few days later, and no one was prosecuted (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One piece from the classical music press, which criticises the orchestra's reliance on state money.
1. Slippedisc: Norman Lebrecht, "Lebrecht to Israel Phil: Reject government funding" (2017)
URL: https://slippedisc.com/?p=346679
- Urges the Israel Philharmonic and the Israel Opera to end their reliance on public money, writing that "Government support is choking the Philharmonic".
- Sets this against pressure from the culture minister of the time, Miri Regev, on arts bodies to show "national loyalty" in exchange for state money.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The New Arab reported that boycott calls against Israel, including in arts and culture, have grown since the war on Gaza began in October 2023.
- Yossi Singer, head of the Israeli Union of Performing Artists, said culture receives about 0.18 percent of Israel's state budget, and several Israeli companies said boycotts abroad have cut their touring income (Jerusalem Post, Barry Davis, 18 July 2026). The article does not name the orchestra.
- The orchestra tours abroad and draws part of its budget from the Israeli state, which places it inside both pressures.

BOTTOM LINE
The orchestra lives on ticket income and private donors, many of them abroad, with a smaller share from the Israeli government; the budget shares given here date from 2007. The disputes are protests and a boycott aimed at it as an Israeli institution, in 2011 and twice in 2025. Watch for a current published budget and for further cancellations on tour.
