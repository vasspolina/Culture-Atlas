LONG MUSEUM: Shanghai, China
ID: long-museum-shanghai
Website: http://thelongmuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-25; down, the connection was refused)

OVERVIEW
- Private art museum with two Shanghai sites, Long Museum Pudong and Long Museum West Bund, and a Chongqing branch opened in 2016; a Wuhan branch was planned for 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Founded by the collectors Liu Yiqian and his wife Wang Wei, who curates; Pudong opened on 18 December 2012, West Bund on 28 March 2014, then China's largest private museum (Wikipedia).
- The Chinese name 龙美术馆 translates to "Dragon Art Museum" (Wikipedia). It is not named for a person.
- Building: the West Bund building was designed by Liu Yichun of Atelier Deshaus (Wikipedia); a listing describes fair-faced concrete "umbrella-vault" structure that keeps a 1950s coal-hopper unloading bridge.
- What is there: exhibitions of the couple's collection of over 2,000 pieces (ArtReview) plus temporary shows.
- Visiting: a listing gives West Bund hours of Tuesday to Thursday and Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Friday and Saturday 10:00 to 20:00, closed Monday; the museum site is down, so admission and accessibility could not be checked.

FUNDING
Long Museum: self-funded: the founders' wealth and sales from their collection
- Liu Yiqian, a former taxi driver who built his fortune from share purchases in the 1990s, chairs Sunline Group and founded Guohua Life Insurance in 2004 (Wikipedia). Forbes put his net worth at US$1.37 billion in July 2015 (Wikipedia).
- Running costs: Momentum (2013) gave a building cost of 200 million RMB and an annual operating budget of 10 million RMB for the Pudong site. ArtReview (2019), citing a TEFAF-commissioned report on China, quotes Liu putting the annual loss at the West Bund site at $6 million; it does not say how the loss is covered.
- Collection sales: The Art Newspaper (22 August 2023) reported that about 50 paintings from the collection of Liu and Wang Wei, including works by Modigliani, Kusama and Hockney, would be offered at Sotheby's Hong Kong later in 2023; Sotheby's said the proceeds were "destined to further key initiatives at the Long museum" and would fund future acquisitions. A 27 September 2023 article put the autumn sale at almost 40 works. The 22 August article reported Guohua Life Insurance losses of $23.3 million to $28.7 million in the first half of 2023, citing China's Economic Information Newspaper.
- No reporting found of a state, corporate or foundation funder, a donor list, a board or published accounts.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Some early criticism of the museum and its founders, mostly from its opening years; the 2023 coverage concerns the market and finances rather than the programme.
1. Momentum (Chinese art magazine, translated by Gu Ling): Rebecca Catching, "Art in the Age of Mass Production: Is the Long Museum Just Another Museum?" (2013)
URL: https://www.momentumworldwide.org/?p=8108
- Found the opening show a "Who's Who" of painting, with lesser works by auction-market favourites, and the museum disorganised, with no clear curatorial vision or visible education programme.
- Doubted chief curator Wang Wei's qualifications, and noted that the planned 30,000 sq. m second space would split the collections between two sites.
- Called the 10 million RMB annual budget low for a 20-person team, against a 200 million RMB building cost, and described an opening banquet for over 1,000 guests with raffle prizes.
2. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "Shanghai's Long Museum to sell art at Sotheby's" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/08/22/shanghais-long-museum-to-sell-art-sothebys
- News report on the planned consignment; Sotheby's says the proceeds will fund key initiatives and acquisitions at the museum. It does not report the museum closing.
3. The Art Newspaper: Lisa Movius, "What's behind China's spate of museum closures and downsizings" (2023)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2023/09/27/whats-behind-chinas-spate-of-museum-closures-and-downsizings
- Lists the Long Museum's autumn sale of almost 40 works, estimated at $95.9 million to $135.5 million, among museum closures and downsizings.
- Attributes closures in general to the property crash, Covid-era strain and tightening censorship; says private museums that depend on parent companies are financially fragile. Does not name the Long Museum's finances.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is funded by its founders' private wealth, which Wikipedia traces to Liu's share purchases in the 1990s and the insurance companies he founded in 2004; nothing found ties it to a flagged industry or a state funder. The weak points are the dependence on two individuals, the 2023 sale of works to support the museum, and the absence of accounts, a board or a working website. Worth watching: whether further consignments follow and whether the site returns.
