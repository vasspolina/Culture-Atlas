NATIONAL MUSEUM OF CAMBODIA: Phnom Penh, Cambodia
ID: national-museum-of-cambodia
Website: https://www.nationalmuseum.gov.kh/ (checked 2026-11-20; down, returned a 502 error; the museum's older site https://www.cambodiamuseum.info, dated 2013, loads)

OVERVIEW
- Cambodia's national museum of Khmer art and archaeology on Street 13 in central Phnom Penh, next to the Royal Palace; the visitor entrance is at the corner of Streets 13 and 178 (museum site).
- The foundation stone was laid on 15 August 1917; the museum, linked to the Ecole des Arts Cambodgiens, was called the Musée du Cambodge in 1919 and was inaugurated as the Musée Albert Sarraut, after the Governor-General of Indochina, on 13 April 1920 in the presence of King Sisowath (museum site).
- George Groslier designed the building, drawn from Cambodian temple prototypes on ancient bas-reliefs; wings were added in 1924 (museum site).
- France ceded control on 9 August 1951. Chea Thay Seng became the first Cambodian director in 1966. The museum closed from 1975 to 1979 under the Khmer Rouge, when many staff were killed, and reopened on 13 April 1979 (museum site).
- About 90 per cent of the collection is stored in the basement, which flooded in the wet season; improvements with UNESCO began in 1993. The museum also oversees Cambodia's other museums (museum site).
- Visoth Chhay is director (Expertise France).
- Returned objects shown or held here include a life-sized Dhrishtadyumna statue from Koh Ker, reunited in 2024 with the pedestal the museum had kept (ICIJ, 2024), and 296 objects returned by the Hungarian collector István Zelnik on 20 November 2025, among them 183 Khmer silk textiles (AKP).
- A France-funded study set out a 10-year plan to modernise and expand the museum into neighbouring Royal University of Fine Arts buildings, due to be handed over by the end of 2026; no start date or funding for the works has been set (Cambodianess, October 2026).
- Visiting: daily 8:00 to 17:00, last tickets 16:30. Foreigners 10 US dollars (5 dollars aged 10 to 17), Cambodians 500 riels, children and school groups free; guided tours in Khmer, English, French and Japanese; no photography in the galleries (museum site).

FUNDING
National Museum of Cambodia: public: the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts, with French and other foreign aid
- The museum works under the auspices of the Cambodian Department of Museums (museum site); the Minister of Culture and Fine Arts, Phoeurng Sackona, requested French help to rehabilitate it on 29 June 2022 (Expertise France).
- France paid 900,000 euros for a feasibility study from October 2024 to October 2026, run by Expertise France with the Musée Guimet under the French Embassy; the ministry says it must now secure funding for the works (Cambodianess).
- Earlier aid: training from the National Gallery of Australia in the 1990s, and French funding for the stone and wood conservation workshop, including salary supplements for its staff (museum site).
- The museum site thanks the Leon Levy Foundation, the Getty Conservation Institute, Friends of Khmer Culture Inc., UNESCO, the Japanese government and the Bickford Family Trust, which sponsors the site with Friends of Khmer Culture. It also says the museum lacks adequate funding, equipment and trained staff, and asks for donations.
- Earned income: tickets, a museum shop and donation boxes (museum site).
- Zelnik paid for archaeological surveys, lidar work and studies at Angkor and Koh Ker through his Hungarian Southeast Asian Institute, not for the museum (AKP).

Red flags: 2008 royal honour for museum funding by Douglas Latchford, indicted in 2019
- In 2008 the collector Douglas Latchford received the Grand Cross of the Royal Order of Monisaraphon for financial contributions to Cambodia's national museum; in 2009 he returned six gold pieces and received the Sahak Metrei friendship medal (Bangkok Post, 2023).
- Latchford attended a function at the National Museum with Deputy Prime Minister Sok An on 12 June 2009 (ICIJ, photo caption).
- US federal prosecutors in New York indicted Latchford in 2019 for a leading role in the looting of Cambodia; he died in 2020, and his estate agreed to pay 12 million US dollars and return a 7th-century bronze in a forfeiture (ICIJ, 2024).

Red flags: looted objects returned to the museum, 2021 to 2026
- In 2021 Latchford's daughter, Nawapan Kriangsak, agreed to return his whole collection; the culture ministry said it would be shown in a planned museum extension (The Art Newspaper, Vincent Noce, 2021).
- In August 2024 Cambodia celebrated the return of 70 artifacts, including pieces from the Metropolitan Museum of Art, Netscape founder Jim Clark and the Lindemann family; Clark agreed in 2022 to return 35 relics (ICIJ).
- On 27 February 2026 Cambodia received 74 objects from Latchford's estate in Britain, to join the museum's collection (The Star).
- Zelnik's 2025 return included objects the ministry said were looted during the civil war (AKP).

BOTTOM LINE
A state museum that calls itself underfunded, kept going by Cambodian public money, tickets and foreign governments, France first among them. Its weak point is the past patronage of an accused trafficker, whose looted objects are now coming back to it.
