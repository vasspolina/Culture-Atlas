IKON GALLERY: Birmingham, UK
ID: ikon-gallery
Website: https://www.ikon-gallery.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and educational charity (no. 528892) at 1 Oozells Square, Brindleyplace, Birmingham (Ikon website).
- Founded by the collector Angus Skene with four Birmingham School of Art artists, David Prentice, Sylvani Merilion, Jesse Bruton and Robert Groves; the collection began in 1964 when Skene bought a painting by Prentice. The gallery was established in 1965 in an octagonal glass-walled kiosk in the Bull Ring shopping centre and reached its current site in 1997 (Wikipedia).
- Groves coined the name, inspired by Eastern Orthodox icons (Wikipedia).
- Building: the former Oozells Street Board School, a Grade II listed neo-Gothic building designed by John Henry Chamberlain in 1877. Levitt Bernstein's refurbishment reinstated the tower, which had been demolished in the 1960s (Wikipedia).
- Jonathan Watkins directed the gallery from 1999 to 2022, and Ian Hyde is chief executive (Wikipedia). It also runs off-site projects, a youth programme, residencies in prisons and Ikon Creative Health (Ikon funding page).
- Visiting: gallery open Tuesday to Sunday and bank holiday Mondays, 11am to 5pm. Entry is free and donations are welcome. The site states "Ikon is fully accessible" (Ikon website).

FUNDING
Ikon Gallery: mainly public with trust and corporate support: Arts Council England, Birmingham City Council, foundations and corporate patrons
- Ikon states that it relies upon regular funding from Arts Council England, which covers running costs, and the 2024 annual review names the National Lottery through Arts Council England and Birmingham City Council as core funders.
- Exhibitions, education and off-site projects are paid for mainly through fundraising from trusts and foundations, corporate patrons and sponsors, international sources and donations. Ikon also earns income from venue hire and its shop (Ikon funding page).
- Multi-year funders in the 2024 annual review: Freelands Foundation for the Ikon Youth Programme (2024 to 2027) and the Marie-Louise von Motesiczky Charitable Trust for art at HMP Grendon (2024 to 2026). The Rothschild Foundation funds a residency at HMP Spring Hill.
- 2026 exhibition supporters include the Ampersand Foundation, Henry Moore Foundation, Paul Mellon Centre, Jerwood Foundation, Freelands Foundation, Acción Cultural Española and the Bagri Foundation (lead supporter of the Balraj Khanna show).
- Corporate patrons: Deutsche Bank, Elonex, Howells, Kier and Transport for West Midlands. Patronage starts at £2,500 plus VAT a year (Ikon sponsorship page). Deutsche Bank and Kier also appear among the 2025 partners in the annual review.
- Individual giving: lead benefactor ai.gallery, benefactors Jan Arkwright, Pieter-Laurin Hartwig and Jonathan Wearing, and 19 named patrons. The funding page says Ikon "is dependent on individual giving more than ever before."

Red flags: Deutsche Bank, a corporate patron fined by the New York regulator
- Ikon lists Deutsche Bank among its current corporate patrons on its sponsorship page, which gives no year, and among the partners it names for 2025 in the 2024 annual review (Ikon sponsorship page; 2024 annual review).
- On 7 July 2020 the New York State Department of Financial Services announced that Deutsche Bank agreed to pay $150 million in penalties under a Consent Order, the first enforcement action by a regulator against a financial institution over dealings with Jeffrey Epstein. The order also covered the bank's failures to monitor its correspondent relationships with Danske Bank Estonia and FBME Bank (New York State DFS press release).
- On the Epstein accounts, the regulator found the bank failed to properly monitor account activity for the registered sex offender, including payments to people "publicly alleged to be" his co-conspirators, settlement payments of more than $7 million, payments to law firms of more than $6 million and suspicious cash withdrawals of more than $800,000 over about four years (New York State DFS press release).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Ikon is mainly public, through Arts Council England National Lottery funding and Birmingham City Council, supplemented by trusts, foundations, individual benefactors and five corporate patrons. The one documented concern is the $150 million penalty that the New York State Department of Financial Services imposed on corporate patron Deutsche Bank on 7 July 2020, which covered its handling of Jeffrey Epstein's accounts.
