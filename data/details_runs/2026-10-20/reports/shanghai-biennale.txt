SHANGHAI BIENNALE: Shanghai, China
ID: shanghai-biennale
Website: https://www.powerstationofart.com (checked 2026-10-24; the page returned only the title "Power Station of Art", so no content was read; the e-flux announcement of the 15th edition gives this address)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art biennale organised by the Power Station of Art, a museum on the Huangpu River at 678 Miaojiang Road, Huangpu District, Shanghai (e-flux, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1996 by Fang Zengxian, then director of the Shanghai Art Museum, as China's first international contemporary art biennale; the first edition was approved by the Ministry of Culture and the Shanghai municipal administration (Wikipedia).
- The Shanghai Art Museum hosted the first eight editions; since the 9th edition, "Reactivation" (2012), it has been held at the Power Station of Art (Wikipedia).
- Building: the Power Station of Art is a converted power plant, the Nanshi Power Plant, which served as the Pavilion of Future at Expo 2010 and opened as a museum on 1 October 2012 (Wikipedia). Exhibitions there are free to the public (Wikipedia).
- The 15th edition, "Does the flower hear the bee?", ran from November 2025 to 31 March 2026 under chief curator Kitty Scott, with 67 artists and collectives and over 250 works, 16 of them by Chinese artists (e-flux, Il Sole 24 Ore).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Sunday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. (e-flux, 15th edition).

FUNDING
Shanghai Biennale: state-run: Shanghai city government, with brand partners; total cost undisclosed
- The Power Station of Art is run by the Shanghai Municipal Administration of Culture and Tourism. The renovation cost about US$64 million, paid by the Shanghai municipal government (Wikipedia).
- The Biennale is funded mainly from city government funds plus some sponsors, and its total cost has not been disclosed (Il Sole 24 Ore, November 2025).
- 15th edition partners: Aesop (special partner), DENZA (official travel partner), The Langham, Shanghai, Xintiandi and Brilliant by Langham (official hotel partner) and FLOS (Il Sole 24 Ore).
- No reporting found of a list of sponsors by year or of amounts.

Red flags: state censorship at the 9th Biennale (2012)
- Yuri Albert: about two weeks before 3 September 2012, Chinese officials told him to remove the first two questions of his work "Moscow Poll" for the Moscow Pavilion. He refused and withdrew. He said the curators tried and failed to change the officials' decision (ArtLeaks, translated from Albert's Facebook statement).
- Stella Art Foundation, which curated the Moscow Pavilion, said the censorship came from government officials, not curators, and that other artists were also censored; it gave no details (Nikolay Molok, via ArtLeaks).
- Barbad Golshiri: he says a cultural committee twice rejected his proposed public work, a skinned cow shaped like a whirling dervish, and he was not given an official invitation. Unverified: Golshiri says the committee called it anti-religious and that Chinese officials were influenced by Iranian officials; no response from the committee or officials is on record (open letter, ArtLeaks, an art-activist platform).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found comes from artists' own accounts of 2012 and from a curator's description of working under censorship; no art-press review found makes a full case against the Biennale.
1. ArtLeaks: unsigned post, translated by Corina L. Apostol, "Yuri Albert: I am not going to Shanghai because of censorship" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/09/05/yuri-albert-i-am-not-going-to-shanghai-because-of-censorship/
- Albert says Chinese officials demanded the cuts; he has "no complaints" about the curators Qiu Zhijie, Boris Groys, Jens Hoffmann and Johnson Chan.
- Names the officials only as "Chinese officials".
2. ArtLeaks: Barbad Golshiri, "Shanghai Biennial 2012 open letter" (2012)
URL: https://art-leaks.org/2012/11/02/barbad-golshiri-shanghai-biennial-2012-open-letter/
- Says two proposals were rejected or blocked and that he was kept out through the invitation and visa process.
- Says Boris Groys told him by email on 22 October 2012 that city pavilions were financed and curated under city authorities.
- Says he protests Chinese or Iranian censorship, not the Biennale team.
3. Americas Quarterly (not art press): Benjamin Russell, "AQ Top 5 China Success Stories: Cuauhtémoc Medina" (2019)
URL: https://americasquarterly.org/article/aq-top-5-china-success-stories-cuauhtemoc-medina/
- Medina, curator of the 2018 edition, answers a censorship question: "censorship in China is a negotiation, a ping pong of proposals and counterproposals", and a challenge, not an absolute impediment.
- Describes his relationship with the Power Station of Art as "harmonious, complicit".

BOTTOM LINE
The money is state money: the Shanghai city government pays for the museum and for most of the Biennale, and the total is not disclosed. The weak points are the documented 2012 interventions by Chinese officials and the lack of any published budget or sponsor schedule. Watch for the budget and for how the Power Station of Art handles content demands in later editions.
