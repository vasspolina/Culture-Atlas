GUDSKUL (RUANGRUPA / SERRUM / GRAFIS HURU HARA): Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: gudskul-ruangrupa-jakarta
Website: https://gudskul.art (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Collective learning space for art at Jl. Durian Raya No. 30A, Jagakarsa, South Jakarta; the nearest rail stations are Ragunan (Transjakarta) and Lenteng Agung, each about a 30-minute walk or a short motorbike taxi ride (Gudskul contact page).
- The collectives ruangrupa (founded in 2000), Serrum (2006) and Grafis Huru Hara (2012) formed Gudang Sarinah Ekosistem in 2015 and transformed into Gudskul in 2018 (Sharjah Art Foundation; Wikipedia).
- Building: Gudskul owns land and a building, which an essay co-written by Gudskul says continue to be used "suboptimally" (e-flux, April 2026).
- Programmes: short courses (September to October 2026 topics include archival, film, video and new media art, writing about art and comic-making), the Studi Kolektif programme, a cooperative board game about art collectives, and performances at Aula Gudskul (Gudskul).
- It is organised as a lumbung (shared barn): funds, programmes, equipment and books are pooled and shared by need (Gudskul about page).
- No reporting found of opening hours or admission on the pages read.

FUNDING
Gudskul: collective and donor-funded: donor agencies, sponsors, income from its own business units and members' contributions
- Per its about page (translated from Indonesian), most of each collective's operational support comes from donor agencies and sponsors, with self-funding from its business units; members also give voluntary funds (stated by the space).
- The e-flux essay co-written by Gudskul (2026) says it has not yet built a new economic model and works within formal, informal and mixed economies.
- No reporting found of any named donor or sponsor of Gudskul.
- Founder ruangrupa is listed among the organisations on the Arts Collaboratory site, whose only named funder is the DOEN Foundation; Wikipedia says ruangrupa is part of the Ford Foundation and Arts Collaboratory networks and receives financial support from them. The DOEN tie runs through ruangrupa and Arts Collaboratory only: neither the Arts Collaboratory site nor its Wikipedia page lists Gudskul, and no reporting found of DOEN money going to Gudskul itself.
- Sharjah Art Foundation lists Gudskul's participation in Sharjah Biennial 14, which Wikipedia dates to 2019; no reporting found of any payment.
- No reporting found of accounts, annual reports or a board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little outside criticism of Gudskul itself. The sharpest critique is self-critique, and the wider critique concerns the lumbung model and its funding.
1. e-flux Architecture: Abidin Kusno and Gudskul, "Building Ideas from the Middle" (2026)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/architecture/essays/6783044/building-ideas-from-the-middle
- Introduced by farid rakun and Rifandi Nugroho of Gudskul, who held a series of conversations with Kusno that became the essay; the space's own people name its problems.
- Gudskul has not yet formed a new economic model and works within formal, informal and mixed economies; its owned land and building "continue to be used suboptimally"; ownership and agency remain unsettled ("Who has the right to do what?").
- The authors write that "Power will always try to use the collective to serve the interest of certain political groups", and that Gudskul works to speak against power and injustice; they also write that "We don't need to be too ambitious in thinking that Gudskul will last forever."
2. e-flux Criticism: Skye Arundhati Thomas, "'The double bind': on Documenta 15" (2022)
URL: https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/482210/the-double-bind-on-documenta-15
- Argues that funding builds infrastructure but also advances "the regional foreign policy interests of the donor nation states", so that "collectivity ends up being mediated as a token of difference".
- Describes the non-Western collectives' architecture as using recognisable, simplistic materials from their home countries, including "tin sheds, brick, straw, bamboo".
- Does not name Gudskul; it concerns documenta fifteen, curated by ruangrupa.

WIDER CONTEXT
- documenta fifteen (2022), curated by ruangrupa, drew accusations of antisemitism: a Kassel blog alleged BDS ties in January 2022 (Wikipedia notes the blog contained factual inaccuracies); Taring Padi's mural "People's Justice" was covered and removed after the 18 June 2022 opening; general director Sabine Schormann resigned on 17 July 2022; the artists and ruangrupa apologised for the stereotypes in the mural (Wikipedia).
- Ruangrupa and 65 participating artists signed an open letter on 10 September 2022 responding to the scientific advisory panel's preliminary findings (Wikipedia).
- Gudskul is listed as a participant of documenta fifteen, and ruangrupa is one of its three founding collectives.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Gudskul is described by the space as donor agencies, sponsors, business units and members, with no names published, so it cannot be checked. The weak points are the undisclosed funders, the absence of published accounts or a board, and the connection through its founder ruangrupa to the documenta fifteen controversy. Worth watching is any published donor list.
