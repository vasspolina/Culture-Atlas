ARARIO MUSEUM IN SPACE: Seoul, South Korea
ID: arario-museum-in-space-seoul
Website: https://www.arariomuseum.org/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- Private contemporary art museum at 83 Yulgok-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul, reached from Anguk Station (Line 3, exit 3) (Time Out); Attractions Management says it overlooks Changgyeong Palace.
- Founded by the collector and businessman Kim Chang-il, who also makes art as "CI Kim"; the museum opened in 2014 with an inaugural show from his collection of over 200 works by 43 international artists (Attractions Management, 24 Sep 2014).
- Housed in an office building designed by Kim Swoo-geun and built in 1971, once the home of his Space Group firm; the original structure was left almost untouched in the conversion (Time Out; Attractions Management).
- Kim bought the site for US$14.6 million after Space Group put it up for sale (Attractions Management).
- On site: galleries, and a bakery, a cafe and a restaurant enclosed in glass (Time Out).
- Visiting: Time Out lists daily opening 10am to 7pm and admission of 10,000 won for adults, 6,000 won for ages 14 to 19 and 4,000 won for ages 11 to 13.

FUNDING
Arario Museum in Space: self-funded: the founder's Arario Group businesses
- The museum site presents it as a private museum built on the founder's collection, with a greeting signed by Kim Chang-il as chairman of Arario Co. (stated by the museum).
- Kim runs the Cheonan Bus Terminal and the Shinsegae Chungcheong department store in Cheonan, with yearly sales near 350 billion won, and Arario galleries in Cheonan, Seoul and Shanghai (The Korea Times, Kwon Mee-yoo, 16 Sep 2015).
- Kim founded Arario Industrial in 1986; the group runs the Cheonan intercity bus terminal, a Shinsegae department store, a food street, and galleries and museums in Cheonan, Seoul, Jeju and Shanghai (Monthly JoongAng, via Daum, 3 Apr 2024). In that article he says he will keep running the museums even at a loss (my translation).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Coverage is profiles and interviews; the one critical note is a complaint attributed to unnamed rival gallerists in a 2007 ranking entry.
1. ArtReview: unsigned Power 100 entry on Kim Chang-il, ranked 87th (2007)
URL: https://artreview.com/artist/kim-chang-il/?year=2007
- Writes: "While rival gallerists may complain about Kim flexing his financial muscles to lure artists to the Arario fold", and adds "there is no doubting the seriousness of his enterprise".
- Criticises the founder's market power, before the Seoul museum existed.
2. The Art Newspaper: Lucian Harris, "Collector profile: The unstoppable Mr Kim" (1 March 2005)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2005/03/01/collector-profile-the-unstoppable-mr-kim
- Not criticism: a profile of the collector. Arario Gallery director Henna Joo spoke of "commercial possibilities" for Kim once the planned space "Another Arario" was complete, and the subtitle asks whether he is also a dealer.
- Does not name the Seoul museum, which did not yet exist.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Korea Herald (Park Yuna, 1 Feb 2021) reported that the Daejeon District Court sentenced Kim Chang-il to two years in jail for breach of duty over the operation of a cinema concession stand at the Cheonan Bus Terminal, which the group owns. An Arario Group official said the group would file an appeal.
- Chungnam Ilbo (Cha Ji-hyun, 28 Jan 2021) reported the ruling by the Cheonan branch of the Daejeon District Court and names the defendants only as A, 70, the Cheonan Arario representative (two years), and his son B, 41, the vice chairman (18 months). It says the concession stand was operated by the son's company from 2011 to 2019 and that double contracts on store leases from 2009 to 2016 cost the company 790 million won; it adds that the court noted the double-contract losses had been repaid (translated from the Korean).
- Connection to the museum: the museum site names Arario Co. as its operator, and the case concerns a property of the group Kim chairs.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the founder's own business income, and he has said he will keep running the museums even at a loss. The weak point is Kim's first-instance conviction in January 2021 for breach of duty at a group property, recorded under wider context because it concerns a cinema concession, not the museum's funding. Worth watching is the group's finances, which carry the museum.
