WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2006 Americans could offer technical help but were barred from sending money or materials for the house and Hemingway's boat; the Bush administration ruled that such aid would violate the US trade embargo against Cuba (NBC News, Mark Potter, 3 December 2006).
- The US government's position was that the aid would help the Cuban government by boosting tourism and bringing in foreign currency; Cuban workers rejected this, saying tourist money would go back into the restoration (NBC News).
- Jenny Phillips, head of the Hemingway Preservation Foundation, said: "We need to rise above the politics and passions of the moment." The foundation planned to reapply for a licence the following year (NBC News).
