MOCA TUCSON: Tucson, USA
ID: moca-tucson-tucson
Website: https://moca-tucson.org (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum at 265 South Church Avenue in downtown Tucson, Arizona, in a former fire station (MOCA Tucson website, Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1997 by Julia Latane, James Graham and David Wright, growing out of the Toole Shed Studios that Graham, Latane and Dave Lewis started in 1992; incorporated in spring 1997 (Wikipedia). The museum's own site says artists established it in 1997.
- It first occupied a warehouse at 191 East Toole Avenue, called the HazMat building, and moved into the former Tucson Fire Station No. 1 in 2010 (Wikipedia).
- The fire hall is the main exhibition space; the firefighters' quarters became artist-in-residence housing and the second floor holds offices and an education space (Wikipedia).
- Since 2009 it has shown eight to twelve exhibitions a year and runs an artist-in-residence programme, youth programming and a curator-in-residence programme begun in 2019 (Wikipedia). The museum states it sits on the ancestral lands of the Tohono O'odham.
- Open Thursday to Saturday 11:00 to 18:00 and Sunday 11:00 to 16:00; the page listed a summer closure from 28 June to 3 September 2026. Admission USD 10, USD 5 for seniors, students and military, free for youth, and free on Sundays. Accessible parking, an accessible lobby restroom and ADA-compliant exhibition spaces (MOCA Tucson visit page).

FUNDING
MOCA Tucson: mainly private: foundations, individuals and board, with small public arts grants
- Fiscal year 2024 (Form 990 filed 22 October 2025): revenue USD 858,543, of which USD 742,985 contributions (86.5%); expenses USD 872,617; net assets USD 88,070 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Executive director Julio Cesar Morales was paid USD 74,872 and deputy director Laura Copeland USD 73,614 (ProPublica).
- Donor tiers on the support page (undated): The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation USD 250,000 and above; Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, Ford Foundation, Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, Foundation Teiger and Windgate Foundation USD 50,000 and above; Kasser Family and named individuals USD 25,000 and above.
- Public funders named: Arizona Commission on the Arts at USD 10,000 and above, and the Rio Nuevo District at USD 2,500 and above and USD 1,000 and above (MOCA Tucson support page).
- Other named funders: Arts Foundation for Tucson and Southern Arizona, HSL Properties, The Gadsden Company and Robinette Architects.
- The museum runs a gala and a fall fundraiser each year (Wikipedia, which carries a promotional-content banner).
- No reporting found of dates for any gift.

Red flags: tribal casino donor
- Desert Diamond Casinos and Entertainment is listed at USD 5,000 and above on the support page, which carries no date (MOCA Tucson support page, stated by the museum).
- Desert Diamond Casinos are owned and operated by the Tohono O'odham Nation in Arizona (Wikipedia). The casino is the Nation's own enterprise and the museum says it stands on the Nation's ancestral land.
- No reporting found of a protest or objection aimed at the museum over this gift.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs on a small budget of private foundation money, individuals and two small Arizona public grants. The weak point is the undated casino gift, a gambling funder at the USD 5,000 level with no objection on record. Worth watching: whether the museum dates its donor list and how it relies on a single foundation's top-tier gift.
