KING ABDULAZIZ CENTER FOR WORLD CULTURE (ITHRA): Dhahran, Saudi Arabia
ID: king-abdulaziz-center-for-world-culture-ithra-dhahran
Website: https://www.ithra.com (checked 2026-10-24; the visit page and an annual report PDF timed out, so hours and admission could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Cultural centre in Dhahran, on the site where Saudi Arabia's first commercial oilfield was found in March 1938 (Wikipedia). Ithra is its short name.
- Saudi Aramco built it and runs it (Wikipedia). A 2009 US consulate cable describes it as a King Abdullah initiative that the King gave to Aramco to build; groundbreaking was 20 May 2008 (WikiLeaks, US consulate Dhahran).
- Inaugurated by King Salman on 1 December, which The Art Newspaper dates to 2017 and Wikipedia to 2016; the two sources do not agree. The Art Newspaper said it then entered a soft-opening phase, with full operations expected in the first half of 2018.
- The Norwegian firm Snohetta designed the building, with Buro Happold as engineers; it covers about 80,000 square metres (Wikipedia). The Art Newspaper put the cost at about $400 million "according to The Art Newspaper's understanding".
- Inside: a museum with galleries for contemporary art, Saudi heritage, Islamic civilisation, and natural history and human ecology; a library; a cinema; a 900-seat theatre; exhibition halls including the Great Hall; and an Energy Exhibit (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include the Ithra Art Prize, run with Art Dubai; Arab News, citing the Saudi Press Agency, reported about one million visitors by October 2019 and named Fatima Al-Rashed as director.
- No reporting found of opening days, admission prices or accessibility from a page opened.

FUNDING
King Abdulaziz Center for World Culture (Ithra): corporate-owned: funded and run by Saudi Aramco, the state oil company
- Aramco funds and operates the centre (Wikipedia, The Art Newspaper). No reporting found of an annual budget or of any other funder.
- Where Aramco's money comes from: the Government of Saudi Arabia holds 82.19% of Aramco, the Public Investment Fund 12% and Sanabil 4%. In 2024 Aramco had revenue of US$480.446 billion and net income of US$106.246 billion (Wikipedia).
- Aramco's 11-member board includes chairman Yasir Al-Rumayyan and CEO Amin H. Nasser (Wikipedia).
- The 2009 cable says the King chose Aramco after Aramco's cost estimate came in lower than the Ministry of Finance's.

Red flags: Gulf state body, Aramco
- Aramco, which built, funds and runs Ithra, is owned 82.19% by the Saudi government (Wikipedia); the Atlas treats Gulf state bodies as a flag.

Red flags: Fossil fuels, Aramco
- Aramco is a national oil company, and it pays for and runs Ithra.
- Wikipedia, citing the Carbon Majors database, says Aramco's 2024 emissions, including those from the oil it sells, were 1,653 Mt of CO2, 4.28% of global CO2 emissions.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art-press criticism names Ithra; the main piece is one opinion essay from 2018. Other coverage found is descriptive.
1. Hyperallergic: Rijin Sahakian, "A Coalition of The Willing: How Saudi Arabia and US Arts Institutions Partnered on a Cultural Diplomacy Offensive" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/saudi-arabia-and-us-art-institutions
- Argues Saudi Arabia used art partnerships to rebrand the kingdom and promote Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, and that US institutions kept taking part after the Khashoggi murder.
- Names Ithra: US curators and art directors previewed it in December 2017 on an invitation-only trip and became members of the Arab Art Education Initiative; says Aramco backs Ithra, which cost $400 million to build.
- Names the Met, MoMA and the Brooklyn Museum as institutions that planned related shows; the Met and Brooklyn Museum said they would self-finance their events after the murder.
2. The Art Newspaper: Gareth Harris, "Saudi mega museum, King Abdulaziz Center in Dhahran, is finally moving ahead" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/01/05/saudi-mega-museumking-abdulaziz-center-in-dhahranis-finally-moving-ahead
- Not criticism: it notes "years of delays and a dearth of information" and Aramco's funding.
- No direct criticism of Aramco or Ithra.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Ithra is not clean by the map's rules: Aramco, a national oil company owned 82.19% by the Saudi state, built, funds and runs it. The weak points are that state ownership, Aramco's fossil-fuel business and the use of Ithra in Saudi cultural diplomacy that Hyperallergic criticised in 2018. Worth watching: Ithra's annual reports, since no budget figure was found.
