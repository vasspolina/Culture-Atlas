NATIONAL ART MUSEUM OF CHINA: Beijing, China
ID: national-art-museum-of-china
Website: https://www.namoc.cn (checked 2026-10-25; loads; the address www.namoc.org did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- China's national art museum, at 1 Wusi Street, Dongcheng District, Beijing.
- Built from November 1958; the main building was finished on 15 November 1961. It was first called the China Art Exhibition Hall, and in June 1963 Mao Zedong chose the name China National Art Museum (Chinese Wikipedia, in translation). It opened to the public in 1963 (Beijing municipal government).
- Building: designed by the architect Dai Nianci in a traditional Chinese pavilion style with yellow glazed tiles; the main building covers more than 18,000 square metres, with 6,660 square metres of exhibition space in 21 halls (Beijing municipal government; Chinese Wikipedia gives 22,379 square metres of floor area).
- Collection: the Beijing government page says over 130,000 works; China Daily and Chinese Wikipedia say over 110,000. It includes classical Chinese masters and foreign artists such as Picasso and Dalí.
- Peter and Irene Ludwig donated 117 works (in 89 sets) by European and US artists, which the museum shows from time to time (China Daily).
- Director: Wu Weishan since September 2014 (Chinese Wikipedia). About 1.05 million visitors came in 2016 (Wikipedia).
- New building: Jean Nouvel's design with the Beijing Institute of Architectural Design, of 130,000 square metres at the Olympic Park, was announced on 18 September 2014 (Attractions Management). Chinese Wikipedia gives a planned completion of August 2029 and says that on 27 March 2026 Beijing authorities issued a land pre-review and site opinion allowing a floor area of no more than 128,600 square metres.
- Visiting: open 9:00 to 17:00, no entry after 16:00, closed Monday except public holidays. Admission is free and has been since 2 March 2011; visitors book online or collect tickets on site. No accessibility information found.

FUNDING
National Art Museum of China: mainly public: the Chinese state, through the Ministry of Culture and Tourism
- Chinese Wikipedia names the Ministry of Culture and Tourism as owner and lists the museum among the ministry's directly affiliated public institutions; English Wikipedia names the Ministry of Culture as owner. Neither gives amounts.
- No reporting found of the museum's budget, accounts, sponsors or board in the pages read; the museum's site does not mention funding.
- No reporting found of a cost or funder for the planned Nouvel building; Attractions Management states neither.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Chinese state money with no private or corporate funder named. The weak points are that no accounts, board or budget for the museum or the planned new building were found, and that it is a state institution run under a ministry. Worth watching are the new Olympic Park building and whether any budget or funder for it is published.
