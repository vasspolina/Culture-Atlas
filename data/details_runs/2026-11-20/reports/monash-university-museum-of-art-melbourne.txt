OVERVIEW
- University museum of contemporary art, known as MUMA, on the ground floor of Building F at Monash University's Caulfield campus, 900 Dandenong Road, Caulfield East (MUMA).
- Grew from a fund set up in 1961 by Vice Chancellor Louis Matheson to buy work by living Australian artists. Patrick McCaughey opened the Monash University Gallery in 1975, and it took its present name in 2002 under director Jenepher Duncan (MUMA).
- Moved to Caulfield in 2010 into a remodelled modernist building first used by the Victorian Public Works Department. Competition winners Kerstin Thompson Architects received two Victorian architecture awards for it in 2011 (MUMA).
- Shows the Monash University Collection, more than 2,300 works as of 2022 (Wikipedia), and commissions temporary works for the Ian Potter Sculpture Court (MUMA).
- Dr Rebecca Coates has been director since July 2023, under an advisory committee chaired by Professor Mel Dodd, dean of Art, Design and Architecture (MUMA, archived page).
- Visiting: Tuesday to Friday 10am to 5pm, Saturday 12 to 5pm, Monday by appointment; "Entry is always free" (MUMA).

FUNDING
Monash University Museum of Art: university museum: owned and run by Monash University, with private gifts for the building, commissions and programmes
- Monash University owns the museum (ABC, 26 March 2025).
- Building: MUMA credits philanthropic support for its Caulfield premises and names the Ian Potter Sculpture Court, the Merlyn Myer Gallery, the Helen Macpherson Smith Education Space and the Marc and Eva Besen Commission, Callum Morton's "Silverscreen" of 2010, a gift of Marc Besen AC and Eva Besen AO (MUMA support page, archived 7 October 2024).
- MUMA Contemporaries: annual giving at four levels, Friend $1,000, Circle $2,500, Patron $5,000 and Benefactor $10,000. The 2020 list carried 24 entries, The Bruce Parncutt Family Foundation one of them (MUMA, archived 7 August 2020).
- Media partner: the support page carries the logos of The Saturday Paper, The Monthly and 7am.
- Research money: MUMA takes part in the Australian Research Council Linkage Grant project "Precarious Movements: Choreography and the Museum" (MUMA).
- Earned income: it hires out its foyer, conference room, Education Lab and sculpture court for events and sells publications in a shop (MUMA).

Red flags: Postponement of "Stolon Press: Flat earth" by the university, 2025
- On 25 March 2025 Monash postponed the exhibition, which was due to open on 8 May after 18 months of planning and included work by Khaled Sabsabi. The ABC understood it to be the first exhibition pulled in the gallery's 50 years (ABC, Nicola Heath and Daniel Browning, 26 March 2025; Mojo News for the date).
- Monash's reason: consultation had "identified there is a need for the Museum to deepen its collaboration and engagement on this exhibition" (ArtReview, 26 March 2025).
- Director Rebecca Coates later said: "Our recommendation was to put it on, but to manage it." Stolon Press said it was not consulted beforehand (Mojo News, Sue Kaufmann, 22 August 2025).
- Protest: Penelope Benton of the National Association for the Visual Arts (NAVA) called the decision "a devastating blow to artistic and academic freedom and the integrity of our public institutions", and Ben Eltham, president of the NTEU Monash branch, said "a university censoring its own art gallery is a really bad idea" (NAVA, 27 March 2025). On 2 April fine arts students sent the Vice-Chancellor an open email with over 600 signatures (Mojo News).
- Unverified: Josh Milani, Sabsabi's gallerist, said MUMA's curators "started receiving pressure from their superiors" after Sabsabi's Venice commission was pulled; this is Milani's claim, not a Monash statement (ABC; ArtReview).
- On 22 May Monash announced the show would open; it ran from 29 May to 12 July 2025 (Mojo News; Milani Gallery).

WIDER CONTEXT
- On 13 February 2025 Creative Australia's board voted to drop Sabsabi and Dagostino from the 2026 Venice Biennale, after Coalition arts spokesperson Claire Chandler raised in parliament works of his from more than 15 years earlier. Mikala Tai, head of visual arts, program manager Tahmina Maskinyar and board member Lindy Lee resigned (ABC).
- On 2 July 2025 Creative Australia released its review and reinstated the artistic team (Mojo News).
- The MUMA exhibition included Sabsabi's work, and its postponement came about a month after the Venice decision.

BOTTOM LINE
MUMA is owned and run by Monash University, with named private gifts behind its building, commissions and donor circle. The documented issue is the university's postponement of the Sabsabi exhibition in March 2025 against the museum's recommendation, reversed after protest from NAVA, the staff union and students. Watch how far the university's leadership steers the museum's programme.
