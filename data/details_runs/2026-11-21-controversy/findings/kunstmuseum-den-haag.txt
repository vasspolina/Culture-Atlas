ID: kunstmuseum-den-haag

Red flags: 2022 ruling to return two objects to the heirs of Emma Budge
- On 14 November 2022 the Restitutions Committee issued a binding opinion that The Hague City Council should return a parcel gilt goblet holder made in 1642 by Andries Grill and a Sultanabad pottery dish from about 1285 to 1400, both in the Kunstmuseum Den Haag, to the heirs of Emma Budge (Restitutions Committee).
- The then Gemeentemuseum Den Haag bought both objects in 1937 at Paul Graupe auctioneers in Berlin. After Budge's death the objects went under the hammer contrary to her instructions; the committee found the sale "was not a free choice" and the loss of possession involuntary (Restitutions Committee).
- On 3 June 2022 the council waived the right to invoke good faith. A council provenance note on the website "Museum Acquisitions since 1933" had described the objects as confiscated by the German government; research found they were sold at auction in 1937 (Restitutions Committee).
- Deputy mayor Saskia Bruines said the city had no hesitation in following the committee's recommendations. Doede Hardeman, head of collections at the museum, said "It is important for the museum that there is a verdict that does right by history", and the museum was preparing to return the objects (DutchNews, Senay Boztas, 16 December 2022).

WIDER CONTEXT
- In January 2016 Nairy Baghramian withdrew from the Vincent Award, which the museum, then the Gemeentemuseum Den Haag, had organised and hosted since 2014. She cited the legal dispute between the artist Danh Vo and the collector Bert Kreuk over a work by Vo shown in an exhibition Kreuk organised at the museum, writing: "I have to question the involvement of the museum in the law case between the artist Danh Vo and the collector De Kreuk." Jutta Koether also withdrew, calling it "a personal matter", according to Hyperallergic (The Art Newspaper, Anny Shaw, 8 April 2016).
- Baghramian said the museum "didn't assume a neutral stance" and "rather played an active role in this legal dispute". The award committee said the museum remained neutral in court and "answered questions under oath of both the judge and both parties' lawyers" (ArtReview, 15 January 2016).
- Director Benno Tempel, who chaired the jury, had testified in the case. The jury said the museum "merely witnessed" the dispute and "was never party to the dispute", found that "the recent discussion has overshadowed the intention of the award", and after consulting the Broere Foundation cancelled the 2016 award (The Art Newspaper, 8 April 2016).
