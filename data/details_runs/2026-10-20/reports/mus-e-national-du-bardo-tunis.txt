MUSÉE NATIONAL DU BARDO: Tunis, Tunisia
ID: mus-e-national-du-bardo-tunis

OVERVIEW
- Archaeology and history museum in Le Bardo, Tunis, in a 19th-century Ottoman governor's palace near the parliament (AFP via Al-Monitor, 14 September 2023). Wikipedia dates the palace to the 15th-century Hafsid period, later a beylical palace.
- Proposed in the 1860s by Muhammad Khaznadar and opened on 7 May 1888 as the Alaoui Museum, after the reigning bey; it took the Bardo name after independence (Wikipedia).
- Collections: Roman mosaics, marble statues, Libyco-Punic artefacts, Greek works from the Mahdia shipwreck and Islamic pieces such as the Blue Qur'an of Kairouan (Wikipedia).
- Expansion: a refurbishment finished in 2012 added 9,000 square meters, designed by SCPA Codou-Hindley and Amira Nouira; the new museum was inaugurated on 25 July 2012 (Wikipedia, Leaders).
- On 18 March 2015 gunmen attacked the museum and took hostages; the attack killed 21 foreign tourists and a Tunisian police officer (Leaders, Houcine Jaïdi, 16 May 2022; AFP). A funeral stela for the victims is displayed inside (AFP).
- The museum closed on 25 July 2021 and reopened in September 2023; the Ministry of Culture said the closure was for renovation. AFP notes the closure coincided with President Kais Saied suspending the legislature and assuming wide-ranging powers on 25 July 2021. Several spaces were enlarged and new sections added, including one on Islamic manuscripts (AFP via Al-Monitor).

FUNDING
Musée National du Bardo: state museum: a World Bank loan behind the 2009 to 2012 expansion and corporate patrons of the Louvre partnership
- The 2009 to 2012 extension and renovation was largely financed by a 2001 World Bank loan for a cultural heritage management project (Leaders, Houcine Jaïdi, 16 May 2022).
- Louvre partnership: a patronage agreement for the Bardo was signed on 11 July 2017 between the Louvre and Tunisia's Institut national du Patrimoine, with an envelope of 240,000 dinars over three years (Le Petit Journal Tunis, "Renouvellement du partenariat entre le Louvre et le Musée du Bardo", 25 July 2017).
- Patrons named in 2017: TOTAL Tunisie paid for restoration equipment and part of the Louvre team's mission costs in the second phase; Air France supported the scientific project of the Louvre-Bardo teaching site; Dar El Marsa, ATUGE and Groupe Loukil contributed renovation, project management and training skills; UBCI was named as associated with the institute's heritage strategy (Le Petit Journal Tunis).
- In 2015 UBCI and Total Tunisie were patrons of the last phase of the Carthage Room, opened on 4 November 2015 after five years of work with the Louvre (Webmanagercenter, "L'UBCI et Total s'associent au musée du Louvre et du Bardo pour la réalisation de la 'salle Carthage'", 5 November 2015).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One critique of the museum's management, by a Tunisian academic in a general daily.
1. Leaders: Houcine Jaïdi, "Musée du Bardo (2012-2022) : la décennie de tous les malheurs" (16 May 2022)
URL: https://www.leaders.com.tn/article/33348-musee-du-bardo-2012-2022-la-decennie-de-tous-les-malheurs
- Says the expansion works were left unfinished, with closed rooms, empty display cases and no open restaurant at first, at a cost of tens of millions of dinars to taxpayers.
- Cites the Court of Audit's 32nd annual report, covering 2020, which found design and monitoring faults, reserves too small for the collections and a ministry without effective authority over the Institut national du Patrimoine and the Agence de Mise en valeur du patrimoine.
- Argues that officials did little after 2015 to restore the museum's image, and criticises the abrupt closure of 25 July 2021, which had lasted nearly a year without clear justification.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is state money from Tunisia, with a World Bank loan behind the 2012 expansion. Corporate patronage is dated 2015 and 2017 (the Carthage Room and the Louvre partnership), with TOTAL Tunisie among the patrons both times.
