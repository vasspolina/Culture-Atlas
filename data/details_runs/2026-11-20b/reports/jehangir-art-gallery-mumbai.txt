JEHANGIR ART GALLERY: Mumbai, India
ID: jehangir-art-gallery-mumbai
Website: https://jehangirartgallery.com (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition gallery at 161 Kala Ghoda in the Fort area of South Mumbai, beside the Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya, formerly the Prince of Wales Museum (gallery site).
- Founded by Sir Cowasji Jehangir, 2nd Baronet (1879 to 1962), an industrialist and banker, in memory of his late son Jehangir; B. G. Kher, then Chief Minister of Bombay, inaugurated it on 21 January 1952 (gallery site). Wikipedia says he founded it at the urging of K. K. Hebbar and Homi J. Bhabha.
- The gallery site credits the architect Durga Bajpai; Wikipedia's introduction credits G. M. Bhuta, tagged "citation needed", while its architecture section also names Bajpai.
- Seven exhibition spaces: the Auditorium Hall, Galleries 1 to 4, the Hirji Jehangir Gallery and the Terrace Art Gallery for photography and visual art; it hosts over 300 shows a year, including the Monsoon Art Show for emerging artists (gallery site).
- The complex housed the Samovar cafe and the antique dealer Natesans (Wikipedia); there is an art shop in the foyer and a library with restricted entry (gallery site).
- It closed for 11 months in the COVID-19 lockdown and partly reopened on 16 February 2021 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: entry is free (gallery FAQ); a ramp reaches the foyer, but no wheelchair is available inside; photography and sketching need the exhibiting artist's permission.

FUNDING
Jehangir Art Gallery: run by a committee on the Jehangir family's founding gift, with exhibitors' fees
- Founding gift: the gallery was founded with the help of Cowasji Jehangir's donations (gallery site); Wikipedia says he donated the entire cost of the building.
- Governance: a committee maintains and manages the galleries (gallery site). Adi H. Jehangir chairs it and K. G. Menon is secretary; members are Dilip De, Shirin K. Bharucha, Yogesh Kamdar, Vishvanath D. Sabale, Sushma Gaikwad, Narendra D. Vichare, Prof. Anil Naik and Nitin Gadre, I.A.S. (gallery site).
- Exhibitors' fees: visitors pay nothing, but artists "need to pay a nominal fee to exhibit" (gallery FAQ). Forms are issued each September, Rs 250 by demand draft for applicants outside Mumbai; a Booking Committee of senior artists and experts allots normally seven days per show, and an artist gets space only once in three years (gallery applications page).
- Other income: the foyer art shop sells publications (gallery FAQ).

WIDER CONTEXT
- April 2013: after complaints, Mumbai police and the gallery insisted, the artist said, that Eleena Banik remove two goddess paintings from public view, one of Kali without her garland of skulls and one she said protested the Delhi gang rape; Varsha Thakar of the Hindu Janajagruti Samiti was among the complainants, Banik received a death threat and police protection, and director K. G. Menon confirmed the request (AFP via The National, 11 April 2013).
- December 2007: two policemen visited Jatin Das's show and suggested barring under-18s; Joint Police Commissioner K. L. Prasad called it a suggestion, and Das put up a "Not Under 18" notice. The year before, the gallery had asked a student to cover parts of a nude sculpture (Subir Ghosh, blog, citing Hindustan Times).

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery runs on a family founding gift and on fees from the artists who show there, with no sponsor or flagged funder on record. Its weak point is a record of removing or covering work after complaints and police visits.
