FONDATION BEYELER: Riehen, Switzerland
ID: fondation-beyeler
Website: https://www.fondationbeyeler.ch

OVERVIEW
- Art museum in Riehen, near Basel, at Baselstrasse 101 (Wikipedia; Basel-Stadt government document).
- Founded in 1982 by the art dealers Ernst Beyeler and Hildy Kunz; the museum opened to the public on 18 October 1997 (Wikipedia).
- Until 2007 the museum was run by the Beyeler Foundation; since April 2007 it has been operated by Beyeler Museum AG, a stock corporation (Basel-Stadt government document).
- Building by Renzo Piano, defined by four porphyry walls; Peter Zumthor added three buildings in 2026: the Wyss Museum, the Ammann Pavilion and a gatehouse with offices and art logistics (Wikipedia; the foundation's website).
- The collection holds more than 400 works of modern and contemporary art, including Cézanne, Monet, Picasso, Matisse, Rothko and Warhol, shown with large temporary exhibitions; the grounds include a park and the Beyeler Restaurant in a historic villa (the foundation's website).
- In 2013 Claude and Micheline Renard gave 33 works to the foundation, its first gift of a collection (SRF, 8 March 2013).
- Open Monday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Wednesday until 20:00; special hours apply while the new buildings open in stages; online booking is recommended (the foundation's website).

FUNDING
Fondation Beyeler: mainly self-earned, with the Beyeler Foundation covering the deficit; public money is about 9% of the budget
- For the average year 2026 the Basel-Stadt government budgets income as 42% visitor income, 7% sponsoring, 4% members and friends, 11% donations, 9% state grants from Basel-Stadt and Riehen and 27% deficit cover from the Beyeler Foundation (Basel-Stadt government, Ratschlag 23.1343.01, 27 September 2023).
- The Beyeler Foundation's deficit guarantee is a private patron's guarantee (Basel-Stadt government, 2023). From 2025 the foundation also carries the extra building costs, put at about CHF 3 million a year more than before.
- Basel-Stadt proposed to continue its state grant to Beyeler Museum AG at CHF 1,965,000 a year for 2024 to 2027, alongside a separate contract with the municipality of Riehen (Basel-Stadt government, 2023).
- In 2020 and 2021 the museum received Covid compensation, with the federal government and Basel-Stadt each paying half (Basel-Stadt government, 2023).
- Beyeler Museum AG board: Edgar Fluri (chair), James Koch, Georg Krayer, Eric Lohrer, Christoph Megert and Christoph Stutz (Basel-Stadt government, 2023).
- Sponsors: the sponsoring page names projects titled Globus Public Art Project, UBS Artist Talks and the Piet Mondrian Conservation Project. Wikipedia lists Bayer, Novartis and UBS as major partners, plus Basler Kantonalbank, BNP Paribas Foundation, Rolls-Royce Motor Cars, ISS Facility Services, J. Safra Sarasin, Hatje Cantz and Richard Mille.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One news report criticises the museum's handling of a loaned work.
1. SRF (Swiss public broadcaster): Sedrik Eichkorn, "Verdacht auf Flucht- oder Raubkunst überschattet Ausstellung" (2026)
URL: https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/cezanne-in-fondation-beyeler-verdacht-auf-flucht-oder-raubkunst-ueberschattet-ausstellung
- Provenance researcher Willi Korte, commissioned by the family of the collector Gustav Schweitzer, says the Cézanne watercolour "La montagne Sainte-Victoire" in the 2026 Cézanne exhibition is at least flight-sold property and possibly looted art, and criticises the museum for inadequate checks.
- The foundation rejects the criticism: it says it carried out appropriate checks, had no information pointing to looted or flight-sold art, cannot keep the work after the show because the loan agreements forbid it, and will inform the lender. The lender is a private owner in the United States and is not named.
- Published 23 May 2026; a news report, not an art-press review.

WIDER CONTEXT
- UBS case: on 10 May 2004 the US Federal Reserve Board fined UBS AG $100 million for US dollar banknote transactions with Cuba, Libya, Iran and Yugoslavia, jurisdictions under US sanctions. The transactions ran through UBS's Extended Custodial Inventory facility in Zurich, operated under a contract with the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The Reserve Bank found that former UBS officers and employees intentionally concealed them; UBS consented to the order without admitting the allegations (Federal Reserve press release).
- Connection to the museum: the museum's sponsoring page names a project titled UBS Artist Talks, and Wikipedia lists UBS as a major partner. The case is more than twenty years old.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is financed by visitor income, a private deficit guarantee from the Beyeler Foundation (27 percent of the 2026 budget) and public grants of about 9 percent (Basel-Stadt government, 2023). UBS is named as a project partner and listed by Wikipedia as a major partner; the Federal Reserve fine against the bank dates from 2004. SRF reported in May 2026 a dispute over the provenance of a loaned Cézanne watercolour.
