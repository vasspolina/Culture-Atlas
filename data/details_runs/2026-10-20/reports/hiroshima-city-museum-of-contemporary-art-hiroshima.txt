HIROSHIMA CITY MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: Hiroshima, Japan
ID: hiroshima-city-museum-of-contemporary-art-hiroshima
Website: https://www.hiroshima-moca.jp

OVERVIEW
- Municipal museum of contemporary art in Hijiyama Park, Minami-ku, Hiroshima; the nearest stop is Hiroden Hijiyama-shita (Wikipedia, museum website).
- Opened on 3 May 1989; the museum describes itself as the first public museum in Japan to take on contemporary art in earnest (museum website).
- Named for the city that runs it.
- Designed by Kisho Kurokawa (museum website).
- On site: special exhibitions, collection exhibitions, a café and a museum shop. The collection includes works by Henry Moore, Andy Warhol, Frank Stella and Donald Judd (Wikipedia, museum website).
- In 2008 the collective Chim↑Pom hired a plane to skywrite "Pika" over Hiroshima in preparation for a solo exhibition at the museum; member Ryuta Ushiro apologised before representatives of atomic bomb victim organisations for the lack of advance warning, and the planned exhibition was cancelled (Get Hiroshima, Paul Walsh, 7 December 2013, updated 19 October 2025).
- Open 10:00 to 17:00, last entry 30 minutes before closing, closed Mondays and 27 December to 1 January. Collection exhibition admission is 350 yen, 250 yen for university students and 150 yen for high school students and over-65s; collection exhibitions are free on the third Sunday of each month. Wheelchairs are lent free at the first-floor reception, and there is an accessible restroom (museum website).

FUNDING
Hiroshima City Museum of Contemporary Art: municipal museum of the City of Hiroshima, with project grants from foundations and national bodies
- The museum is a municipal museum of Hiroshima City and organises its own exhibitions; the Hiroshima prefecture and the Hiroshima City Board of Education appear as supporters of its shows (FY2024 annual report, museum website).
- FY2024 grants named per show: the exhibition of Yuki Harada (30 November 2024 to 9 February 2025) received grants from the Ogasawara Toshiaki Memorial Foundation and the Asahi Shimbun Cultural Foundation and support from the FY2024 Agency for Cultural Affairs Media Arts Creator Development Support Project; the exhibition of Tintin Wulia received a grant through the University of Gothenburg's THINGSTIGATE project (European Research Council) (FY2024 annual report).
- Asahi Shimbun is co-organiser of the Mel Chin exhibition (25 July to 12 October 2026), with Chugoku Broadcasting, TV New Hiroshima, Hiroshima Telecasting, Hiroshima Home Television, Hiroshima FM Broadcasting and Onomichi FM Broadcasting as supporters (museum website).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a city institution of Hiroshima; the funders named in its FY2024 annual report are public bodies and cultural foundations (Agency for Cultural Affairs, the Ogasawara Toshiaki Memorial Foundation, the Asahi Shimbun Cultural Foundation, a European Research Council project), with Asahi Shimbun as co-organiser of its 2026 Mel Chin show.
