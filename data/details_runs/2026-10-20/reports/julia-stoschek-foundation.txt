OVERVIEW
- Private foundation showing the Julia Stoschek Collection of time-based media art, with a Berlin venue on Leipziger Strasse and a venue in Düsseldorf (USA Art News, 2026; Apollo, 2016).
- Julia Stoschek opened the Düsseldorf venue to the public in 2007 and the Berlin venue in June 2016 (USA Art News; Apollo). No opened source gives the year the foundation was established.
- The collection holds more than 850 works by about 250 artists, mostly European and American, from the 1960s onward, including video, internet-based works and performance (Wikipedia).
- Berlin building: the former Czech Cultural Centre, a GDR-era building of 3,000 square metres (USA Art News; Apollo). Düsseldorf is a converted industrial building from 1907 with a cinema and an archive (Apollo).
- Berlin ran about 22 exhibitions and drew roughly 450,000 visitors. On 24 April 2026 USA Art News reported that the foundation will close the Berlin venue at the end of October 2026 and keep Düsseldorf open, with more international presentations of the collection.
- Visiting: No reporting found of current opening days, admission or accessibility, because the foundation's site could not be read.

FUNDING
Julia Stoschek Foundation: private foundation of collector Julia Stoschek; no source opened says how it is financed
- W Magazine (Diane Solway, 20 November 2017) says Stoschek is "an active shareholder" in her family's auto-parts company Brose, whose chairman is her father Michael Stoschek. It does not say how the collection is financed.
- No reporting found of how the foundation is financed, of a payment or stake from Brose to the foundation, of a public funder, a sponsor, annual accounts or a board.
- Brose reported sales of €7.7 billion for 2024 (Wikipedia).

WIDER CONTEXT
- Brose was founded in Coburg on 14 June 1919 by Max Brose and Ernst Jühling. Max Brose joined the Nazi Party in 1933. In the Second World War the company made gasoline canisters and armaments for the military and used forced labour, including prisoners of war (Wikipedia). Julia Stoschek is Max Brose's great-granddaughter (Tagesspiegel headline; Berliner Zeitung).
- The company and family say Max Brose treated workers well; Wikipedia states that claim "has not been substantiated".
- Tagesspiegel, Nicola Kuhn, "Die Sammlerin Julia Stoschek und ihr Urgroßvater: Schmutzige Gelder, saubere Kunst" (11 November 2020; paywalled, only excerpts were read): the artist Leon Kahane showed an installation near the Julia Stoschek Collection on Leipziger Strasse during the Art Week, referring to Brose's ties to the armaments industry under National Socialism. The teaser (my translation) says forced labour keeps coming up, also in the cultural sector, and that ducking the issue does not help. The excerpt does not say whether Brose money funds the collection.
- Berliner Zeitung, Hanno Hauenstein, "Ende der Documenta 15: Warum diese Debatte uns noch lange begleiten wird" (25 September 2022; opinion-led commentary in the culture section): the author says Stoschek shields her great-grandfather's Nazi past "aktiv gegen Kritik" (actively against criticism). The piece says the company commissioned a publication from a historian that faced criticism for missing footnotes.
- Unverified: the same Berliner Zeitung piece says Michael Stoschek pressed in 2015 to rename the former Von-Schultes-Strasse as Max-Brose-Strasse and compared Brose to Oskar Schindler; that artist Hito Steyerl bought back her work from the collection shortly after David de Jong's text criticising the lack of transparency of wealthy families' Nazi ties; and that the paper received an Abmahnung (a warning letter) over a phrase in its translation of that text, which it attributes to Stoschek's side without naming a sender. How the matter ended is not reported.
- Connection to the foundation: the founder is an active shareholder in Brose, an auto supplier (W Magazine, 2017). No source opened shows current Brose funding of the foundation.

BOTTOM LINE
No source opened shows how the foundation is financed, or that Brose pays for it. The founder is an active shareholder in Brose, an auto supplier that used forced labour in the Nazi era, and the criticism concerns that family history, not the programme. Worth watching: whether the foundation publishes its financing, and what follows the Berlin closure at the end of October 2026.
