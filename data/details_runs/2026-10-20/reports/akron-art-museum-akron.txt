AKRON ART MUSEUM: Akron, United States
ID: akron-art-museum-akron
Website: https://akronartmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Art museum at 1 South High Street in downtown Akron, Ohio, collecting art made since 1850.
- Opened on 1 February 1922 as the Akron Art Institute in two borrowed basement rooms of the public library, and occupied the Carnegie-era library building from 1948 to 1981 (Wikipedia).
- The Knight Building, named for John S. and James L. Knight, was designed by the Vienna firm Coop Himmelb(l)au after an international competition; ground was broken on 22 May 2004 and it opened on 17 July 2007.
- The 1899 Italian Renaissance revival building holds Western art from 1850 to 1950; eight rooms in the Knight Building hold art since 1950, including Chuck Close's "Linda" and Andy Warhol's "Brillo Boxes".
- Open Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday 12:00 to 17:00 and Thursday 12:00 to 21:00, closed Monday and Tuesday.
- Adults pay $15, seniors $10, students $8 and Ohio EBT holders $3; entry is free on Thursdays and for members, under-18s, University of Akron students and staff, and active military families.
- ADA-compliant entrances, lifts to every level, wheelchairs, large-print labels and KultureCity sensory-inclusive certification.

FUNDING
Akron Art Museum: mainly private: donors, local foundations and members, with some state funding
- In the year to June 2025 revenue was $8,079,628, of which contributions were $4,902,607 and programme revenue $402,801; expenses were $6,491,669 and net assets $67,746,844 (Form 990, ProPublica).
- In the year to June 2023 expenses were $5,393,514 (Form 990).
- Named supporters, undated, include the Knight Foundation, GAR Foundation, Art Bridges, the J.M. Smucker Co, Michelson Philanthropies, Peg's Foundation, the Akron Community Foundation funds and the FirstEnergy Foundation (stated by the space).
- Public funders named: the Ohio Arts Council, the Ohio Facilities Construction Commission, Ohio Humanities and the National Endowment for the Humanities through the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 (stated by the space).
- Director and CEO Jon Fiume was paid $237,966 in the year to June 2025 (Form 990).
- No reporting found of what FirstEnergy gives or pays for.

Red flags: FirstEnergy Foundation as a current supporter
- The FirstEnergy Foundation appears in the museum's current sponsor list.
- In July 2021 FirstEnergy was charged with conspiracy to commit honest services wire fraud over Ohio's House Bill 6 and agreed to pay a $230 million penalty under a deferred prosecution agreement (Ideastream).
- In February 2024 FirstEnergy dropped its 2030 emissions target; its two West Virginia coal plants, Fort Martin and Harrison, account for about 99% of its emissions and are forecast to retire in 2035 and 2040 (Power Engineering).
- No reporting found of a protest at the museum over FirstEnergy.

Red flags: 2019 to 2020 staff complaints and the director's resignation
- In 2019, 27 employees sent an anonymous letter to the board about director Mark Masuoka (WKSU, citing ARTnews).
- Investigators from the law firm Kastner Westman and Wilkins substantiated some of the claims, and the museum made changes it would not describe (The Art Newspaper).
- In March 2020 Masuoka announced staff cuts over a pandemic shortfall of more than $900,000; employees said the cuts were used to retaliate against those who had spoken out (WKSU).
- Masuoka resigned in May 2020, and local businessman Jon Fiume was named interim director (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2019 to 2020 workplace complaints; no reporting found of art press criticism of the museum's funders.
1. The Art Newspaper: Nancy Kenney, "Director of Akron Art Museum resigns after negative reports on management" (2020)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/05/20/director-of-akron-art-museum-resigns-after-negative-reports-on-management
- Reports staff claims of racism, sexism and bullying by managers, and that an outside investigation substantiated some of them.
- Board chair Drew Engles: "the board agreed it was time for a change in leadership."
2. WKSU: Jennifer Conn, report on the director's resignation (2020)
URL: https://www.wosu.org/wksu-stories/2020-05-19/akron-art-museum-director-forced-to-step-down-amid-controversy
- Reports the 27-employee letter accusing Masuoka of "unethical actions and decisions" that drove turnover, and the claim that pandemic cuts were retaliation.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly local private philanthropy with some state and federal grants, and the accounts are public. The weak point is the FirstEnergy Foundation, whose parent paid a $230 million penalty over House Bill 6 and still runs coal plants. The 2020 leadership crisis ended with Masuoka's resignation; Jon Fiume, named interim director then, is now director and CEO.
