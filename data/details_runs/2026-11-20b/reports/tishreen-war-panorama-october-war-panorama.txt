TISHREEN WAR PANORAMA (بانوراما حرب تشرين): Damascus, Syria
ID: tishreen-war-panorama-october-war-panorama
Website: http://www.mod.gov.sy/index.php?node=554&cat=733 (checked 2026-11-20; down; a 2018 archive copy was read)

OVERVIEW
- War museum at the northern entrance of Damascus, in a garden of about 62,700 square metres (Syrian Ministry of Defence page in Arabic, archived 2018; translation ours). Arabic name: Bānūrāmā Ḥarb Tishrīn (بانوراما حرب تشرين), the October War Panorama (English Wikipedia).
- It commemorates the 1973 October War from a Syrian viewpoint, and battles of the 1982 Lebanon War (English Wikipedia).
- Opening: Hafez al-Assad opened it in 1998 (Public Delivery); a visitor gives 9 October 1999 (Frontline Club).
- Building: a domed circular hall 43.1 metres across and 37.1 metres high between two wings; the panorama painting is one circular piece 130 metres long and 15 metres high, 1,950 square metres, showing the Golan Heights from Mount Hermon to Tal al-Faras; two weapons yards of 1,760 square metres each show Syrian and Israeli equipment (Ministry of Defence page, archived 2018).
- Halls: oil paintings of Hafez al-Assad assigning tasks to his commanders, the Ebla treaty, Zenobia of Palmyra, the caliph al-Walid I and Saladin after Hattin, a diorama of the 1973 capture of the Mount Hermon observatory, and a 38-square-metre painting of Hafez al-Assad with the people (Ministry of Defence page, archived 2018).
- Visit: a visitor describes a short film shown to schoolchildren, a rotating platform before a 360-degree painting of Quneitra, and an outdoor display of Syrian and Israeli hardware (Frontline Club).

FUNDING
Tishreen War Panorama: public: Syrian Ministry of Defence
- Operator: the Ministry of Defence ran the museum and listed it among museums in the culture section of its website, which described it as built to fix the October War in the memory of later generations (Ministry of Defence page, archived 2018; translation ours).
- Construction: Mansudae Overseas Projects (MOP) of North Korea helped build it, as shown by its socialist-realist reliefs and a mural of Hafez al-Assad with Kim Il Sung (Public Delivery; English Wikipedia). The Frontline Club visitor also credits North Korean artists with the panorama painting.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One visitor's blog post; it is not art criticism and it criticises the museum's account of the war, not its funding.
1. Frontline Club: unsigned post by a student visitor, "Winning a battle, losing the war: an odd tribute" (undated)
URL: https://www.frontlineclub.com/winning_a_battle_losing_the_war_an_odd_tribute/
- Says the museum celebrates a war that was far from victorious and dwells on the storming of Quneitra, although Israel retook the town.
- Describes a ten-minute war film that the schoolchildren cheered "in all the right places", and calls the North Korean painters "clearly very talented".

WIDER CONTEXT
- The EU's 2017 listing of the Mansudae Overseas Project Group of Companies, which followed a UN Security Council designation of 5 August 2017, says it exported North Korean workers for statues and monuments to earn revenue for the North Korean government, and names Syria among the countries where it has been reported to do business (Implementing Regulation 2017/1457).
- Connection: Mansudae helped build the panorama in the 1990s; it was the builder, not a funder.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: Syrian Ministry of Defence money. Worth watching is what the post-Assad government does with the museum.
