EKKM (CONTEMPORARY ART MUSEUM OF ESTONIA): Tallinn, Estonia
ID: ekkm-tallinn
Website: https://www.ekkm.ee

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run contemporary art museum at Rumbi 3, Tallinn, in the former ancillary building of the city of Tallinn. It has three exhibition floors and a public garden opened in 2020 (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 2006 by Anders Härm, Elin Kard, Neeme Külm and Marco Laimre, in a building that had been used as artist studios (Wikipedia). KUNST.EE says it was started as a squat by Neeme Külm and Marco Laimre sometime in 2006. Its team since 2021 is Kadi Kesküla (administrative manager), Johannes Säre (technical manager), Laura Toots (project manager and curator) and Evelyn Raudsepp (creative producer) (Wikipedia).
- Until the end of 2009 the work was voluntary and unpaid. In a 2013 interview Härm said of the "museum" label: "Labelling things that were actually very far from being true was the strategy that we chose" (Arterritory, 24 May 2013). Scan Magazine (Emma Rodin, issue 167) says the initiative took a stronger hold as the Kumu Art Museum opened and "turned out lacking in the contemporary area".
- The building has no heating system and is heated with electricity (ERR, June 2022); the exhibition season runs from April to December (Wikipedia).
- Hosts the Köler Prize since 2011 (Wikipedia). By the end of 2021 it had held 76 exhibitions with 42 curators and 431 artists since 2007 (ERR, 2022).
- Free to visit (Scan Magazine: "it's all free").

FUNDING
EKKM: mainly public: Estonian Ministry of Culture and Cultural Endowment of Estonia
- Until 2023: €120,000 a year from the Cultural Endowment of Estonia plus €30,000 a year from the Ministry of Culture. ERR News, "EKKM's annual operating grant to increase by €30,000 from 2024" (29 September 2023), reported that from 2024 the state would pay an annual operating grant of €180,000 directly from the Ministry of Culture's budget, an increase of €30,000; moving the grant to the ministry frees Cultural Endowment money for other arts projects.
- The Ministry's operating grant stayed at €30,000 a year from 2019 to 2022. Rent and electricity took about two thirds of it, and EKKM said in June 2022 that without extra money it would cancel the exhibition planned for the end of the year and close the building three months early (ERR).
- The Cultural Endowment funds exhibition production but not collection acquisition, rent or running costs, so EKKM depends on it for exhibitions (KUNST.EE, 2017).
- KUNST.EE credits the financing of the "Tallinn: European Capital of Culture 2011" programme with the first two curated exhibitions, "Next to Nothing" (2010) and "Darkness, Dark" (2011). In November 2016 EKKM was one of three institutions granted a partial operating subsidy for an indefinite period (KUNST.EE, 2017).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The direct criticism is a 2017 essay questioning its name, its Köler Prize format and its dependence on the state.
1. KUNST.EE: Marika Agu and Siim Preiman, "A 'phantom platform' as an Art Institution for All" (2017, issue 2)
URL: https://e-kunst.kultuuriveeb.1kdigital.com/en/?p=19243
- Calling the project an art museum was "a clear bluff" given its artist-run origins and lack of a collection.
- Reports that in 2016 the new management changed the Köler Prize from an annual to a biennial event, and says that without it EKKM "is just another institution among others producing contemporary art exhibitions".
- Warns that "the state may turn off the tap again during the next financial downturn".
- The article describes itself as speculation based on third-party opinions and rumours.
2. Arterritory: interview with Anders Härm by Anna Iltnere, Elīna Zuzāne and Sergej Timofejev, "Behaving as a Museum" (24 May 2013)
URL: http://arterritory.com/en/texts/interviews/2319-behaving_as_a_museum
- Härm says "there are also people who think that we are too mainstream" and that "some even say that the change has not been for the best".
- Härm says Kumu was "very unhappy about us being a museum" and that the relationship between them is now good.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind EKKM is public: ERR News reported an operating grant of €180,000 a year from the Estonian Ministry of Culture from 2024, and the Cultural Endowment of Estonia funds its exhibitions. KUNST.EE warned in 2017 that "the state may turn off the tap again during the next financial downturn".
