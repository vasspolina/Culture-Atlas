THE DRAWING CENTER: New York, USA
ID: the-drawing-center-new-york
Website: https://drawingcenter.org

OVERVIEW
- Nonprofit museum of drawing at 35 Wooster Street, SoHo, Manhattan, which calls itself "New York's museum for drawing."
- Founded in SoHo in 1977 (Wikipedia names former MoMA assistant curator Martha Beck as founder). It began in a ground-floor warehouse at 137 Greene Street and moved to 35 Wooster Street in 1987 (the museum's own About page).
- Building: Wikipedia reports a renovation of about $10 million in 2012 that enlarged the exhibition space by about 50 percent.
- Programme: exhibitions of works on paper, publications and education programmes; shows have included drawings by tattoo artists, chefs, novelists and prisoners alongside visual artists (the museum's own About page).
- Executive director Laura Hoptman, a former MoMA curator, since 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: free admission every day, made possible by the Cy Twombly Foundation. Open Wednesday to Sunday, 12 to 6pm. Galleries, bookstore and restrooms are accessible.

FUNDING
The Drawing Center: donor-funded: foundations, individuals, galleries and corporate gifts, with New York City and State arts agencies
- Form 990, year ending June 2025: revenue $4,468,552, of which contributions $4,112,577 (92.0%); expenses $3,589,378; net assets $10,288,591 (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Donor list covering gifts of $1,000 or more from 1 July 2023 to 1 January 2025 names, in its Partners list (the first on the page): Bloomberg Philanthropies, the Andy Warhol Foundation for the Visual Arts, the Mellon Foundation, the Helen Frankenthaler Foundation, the Cy Twombly Foundation, the Evelyn Toll Family Foundation, the Horace W. Goldsmith Foundation, the May and Samuel Rudin Family Foundation, the Charina Endowment Fund, the New York Community Trust, Gladstone Gallery, the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs and the New York State Council on the Arts. The page credits Barbara Toll and the Toll Charitable Foundation for the website.
- Benefactors include UNIQLO, Christie's Art Finance, Neuberger Berman Private Wealth, the Terra Foundation for American Art, the Henry Luce Foundation, David Zwirner and Hauser & Wirth.
- Capital funding: the Lower Manhattan Development Corporation gave $10 million in 2005 toward a new home (amNY, Josh Rogers, 17 November 2005) and $3 million toward the 2012 renovation (Wikipedia). A 2005 Carnegie Corporation grant to the center was made possible by a donation from Mayor Michael Bloomberg (Wikipedia).

Red flags: state pressure over programming at the World Trade Center site, 2005
- The Lower Manhattan Development Corporation (LMDC), backed by Governor George Pataki, selected the center to move to the World Trade Center site (amNY, 17 November 2005). A Daily News editorial that blamed Pataki and his lower Manhattan officials named the center as one of four cultural organisations designated for the 16-acre site ("Get the Picture, Governor?", 24 June 2005; republished by Gotham Gazette, 8 September 2005).
- The Daily News editorial objected to a "Homeland Security" artwork and an Abu Ghraib-themed piece in the center's exhibits, and told Pataki to "Show these people the door."
- After objections to its art, Pataki demanded an "absolute guarantee" that curators would not offend families of people killed on 11 September 2001. The center refused that summer and said it hoped to find a new home elsewhere. The LMDC then gave it $10 million toward a new home (amNY, Josh Rogers, 17 November 2005).
- Critic Holland Cotter called the uproar "a tempest in a teapot" and said the center's mission is formal rather than ideological (Douglas McLennan, "An Odd Target," ArtsJournal, 26 June 2005).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind The Drawing Center is philanthropic: foundations, individuals, galleries and corporate gifts, with New York City and State arts agencies and Bloomberg Philanthropies in the Partners list. The documented episode is 2005: Governor Pataki demanded a guarantee about its programming for the World Trade Center site, and the center declined to give it.
