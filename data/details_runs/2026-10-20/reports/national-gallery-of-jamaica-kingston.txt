NATIONAL GALLERY OF JAMAICA: Kingston, Jamaica
ID: national-gallery-of-jamaica-kingston
Website: https://nationalgalleryofjamaica.wordpress.com/

OVERVIEW
- National art museum at 12 Ocean Boulevard on the Kingston Waterfront, with a branch, National Gallery West, in Montego Bay (the gallery's own site). It describes itself as the largest and oldest public art museum in the English-speaking Caribbean and holds Jamaica's National Art Collection.
- It is a division of the Institute of Jamaica, under the Ministry of Culture, Gender, Entertainment and Sport (the gallery's site). The Institute was established in 1879 by Governor Sir Anthony Musgrave (Wikipedia, Institute of Jamaica).
- Opened on 22 November 1974 at Devon House with 237 paintings and drawings and 25 sculptures transferred from the Institute of Jamaica; it moved to the Roy West Building downtown in 1982 (Wikipedia). The Jamaica 55 government site dates the move to 1983 and says it was meant to be temporary.
- David Boxer was the first director (appointed 1975); Veerle Poupeye was Executive Director from 2009 until she resigned in January 2018 (Wikipedia).
- Programmes include the Jamaica Biennial, the Young Talent series, the Caribbean Invitational exhibitions, Saturday Art-Time for children and touring exhibitions (Wikipedia). The Edna Manley Galleries, dedicated to Manley's life and work, opened on 1 March 2000 (Wikipedia).
- National Gallery West sits in the Montego Bay Cultural Centre on Sam Sharpe Square, which opened in 2014 (Jamaica Information Service, Garwin Davis, 30 March 2017).

FUNDING
National Gallery of Jamaica: owned by the Government of Jamaica through the Institute of Jamaica, with CHASE and Tourism Enhancement Fund money for programmes and Scotiabank Jamaica as an exhibition partner
- Wikipedia lists the Government of Jamaica as owner of the gallery, which is a division of the Institute of Jamaica (the gallery's site).
- The children's Saturday morning art programme is free and "funded by CHASE", the Culture, Health, Arts, Sports and Education Fund (Jamaica 55 government site); the Jamaica Information Service (Rocheda Bartley, 20 September 2022) describes CHASE as a government fund that supports cultural projects.
- The Tourism Enhancement Fund supported the opening of the Montego Bay Cultural Centre, which houses National Gallery West (Jamaica Information Service, Garwin Davis, 30 March 2017).
- Scotiabank Jamaica is the partner for the exhibition Of Wood and Water: The Scotiabank Collection, extended to 1 November 2026 (the gallery's site).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The documented criticism is a 2014 blogger's case against the then executive director's management, and a news summary of it.
1. Active Voice (blog): Annie Paul, "Mismanagement, machinations and more at the National Gallery of Jamaica..." (29 July 2014)
URL: https://anniepaul.net/2014/07/29/mismanagement-machinations-and-more-at-the-national-gallery-of-jamaica/
- Accuses Executive Director Veerle Poupeye of intimidation and micromanagement, of berating staff and requiring approval for staff Facebook posts.
- Says Poupeye contributed to the resignations of Senior Curator Nicole Smythe-Johnson (2013) and Chief Curator Charles Campbell (2014, about six months into his contract).
- Says the Exhibitions Committee became a "rubber-stamp" body, which led its previous chair, Tina Spiro, to resign. Paul is a blogger and cultural critic; these are her allegations.
2. Global Voices: Matthew Hunte, "National Gallery of Jamaica Director Faces Accusations of Intimidating and Bullying Employees" (15 August 2014)
URL: https://globalvoices.org/2014/08/15/national-gallery-of-jamaica-director-faces-accusations-of-intimidating-and-bullying-employees/
- Summarises Paul's accusations and reports that Campbell resigned in mid-2014, "citing systemic management and leadership issues and a hostile working environment".
- Reports that Poupeye had not formally responded and that the gallery made no official statement.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery belongs to the Government of Jamaica through the Institute of Jamaica, with the CHASE Fund supporting its children's programme, the Tourism Enhancement Fund supporting the Montego Bay Cultural Centre that houses National Gallery West, and Scotiabank Jamaica as partner of the current Of Wood and Water exhibition. The criticism on record, from 2014, concerns management under the then executive director, Veerle Poupeye.
