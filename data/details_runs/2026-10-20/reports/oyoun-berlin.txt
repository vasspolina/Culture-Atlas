OYOUN: Berlin, Germany
ID: oyoun-berlin
Website: https://oyoun.de

OVERVIEW
- BIPOC-led cultural centre for queer feminist, migrant and decolonial perspectives, run by the non-profit Kultur NeuDenken gUG, at Lucy-Lameck-Straße 32 in Berlin-Neukölln (Oyoun website).
- Operated the cultural site at that address from 2020 as a platform for diasporic, migrant and international art and culture (Berliner Zeitung, Susanne Lenz, 2 September 2024).
- Employs 32 people, most from marginalised groups (Oyoun website).
- The Senate Department for Culture and Social Cohesion advertised the Lucy-Lameck-Straße site for a new operator from January 2025, with applications due 9 October 2024 (Berliner Zeitung, 2 September 2024).

FUNDING
Oyoun: mainly public until 2023: the Berlin Senate; now grants, a foundation and donations
- Berlin Senate project grant of 1 million euros a year, a 2022 to 2025 project grant that Oyoun describes as a binding commitment (Oyoun website). Oyoun's operator, Kultur NeuDenken, received 1.052 million euros from Berlin in 2023 up to 15 November, and similar amounts were planned for 2024 and 2025 (Jüdische Allgemeine, 24 July 2024).
- Supporting Act Foundation: Impact Grant for 2025 and 2026 (stated by Oyoun).
- Berlin Lotto foundation project money for individual projects (taz interview with Louna Sbou, Susanne Memarnia, 21 March 2024).
- Donations: an open letter backing Oyoun and a GoFundMe campaign for legal and court costs (Oyoun website; Resident Advisor).

Red flags: State censorship or punishment: Berlin Senate ended funding after a disputed event
- 4 November 2023 event: Oyoun hosted an event by the association Jewish Voice for a Just Peace in the Middle East (Jüdische Stimme), held as a vigil with a panel, workshops, music and an exhibition; about 300 people attended (Resident Advisor, Anu Shukla, 27 November 2023). Authorities told Oyoun the event was too "politically charged" and should not go ahead; it went ahead (Resident Advisor). According to Chialo, the Senate culture administration had asked Oyoun to cancel it; the Berliner Zeitung (Xenia Balzereit, 21 November 2023) reports that the Central Council of Jews attributes closeness to the BDS movement to the group, and that Neukölln MP Susanna Kahlefeld (Greens) had called for ending the funding.
- Funding decision: Oyoun learned on Monday 20 November 2023 in the Berlin House of Representatives' culture committee that the Senate would end its funding, which Resident Advisor puts at 1 million euros a year, supporting 32 salaried jobs and projects contracted until 2025 (Resident Advisor). Culture Senator Joe Chialo (CDU) said his department was committed to combating all forms of antisemitism, "every hidden form" included (Resident Advisor).
- Senate position: a Senate spokesperson said the funding had ended "regulär" (as scheduled) and denied any link to the dispute (Berliner Zeitung, Susanne Lenz, 2 September 2024). The taz interview reports Chialo saying the grant was approved only until the end of 2023. Oyoun's managing director Louna Sbou says a binding commitment ran to the end of 2025, and the taz cites an internal administration assessment that supports her.
- Antisemitism claim: the Berliner Zeitung (2 September 2024) says an antisemitism allegation was raised but was unconfirmed and never officially stated. The Berliner (23 July 2024) reports that Chialo ordered a legal review of whether funding could be withdrawn on antisemitism grounds, citing internal documents reported by nd, and that the review found no violation of criminal law or other applicable law; funding was then cancelled on technical grounds.
- Courts: Oyoun's suits against the funding stop were dismissed by the Berlin administrative court and the higher administrative court, the latter in March 2024 (Jüdische Allgemeine, dpa, 24 July 2024). The Berliner quotes Oyoun's statement that on 18 July 2024 the Constitutional Court of Berlin ruled its constitutional complaint successful and that the higher administrative court must rehear the case. The Jüdische Allgemeine reports that the court set aside the March decision, agreed that Oyoun's rights had been violated and expressly left open whether Oyoun is entitled to further funding.
- Kahlefeld: Resident Advisor reports that her statement accused Oyoun of antisemitism; Sbou called it defamatory and says Kahlefeld received an injunction and removed it (Sbou's account).
- Open letter: more than 12,000 people signed a letter backing Oyoun before the funding was withdrawn (Resident Advisor, 27 November 2023).

BOTTOM LINE
Oyoun's main funder was the Berlin Senate, which paid about 1 million euros a year until the end of 2023 and then ended the grant after the 4 November 2023 event; the Senate says the grant had run out as scheduled. Since then Oyoun reports funding from the Supporting Act Foundation, Lotto foundation project money and donations. The Senate's decision and the court case, remanded on 18 July 2024 with the funding question left open, are the documented issue.
