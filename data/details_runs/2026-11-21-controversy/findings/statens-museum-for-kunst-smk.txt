ID: statens-museum-for-kunst-smk

WIDER CONTEXT
- In January 2014 Danish art expert Bjørn Wandall argued that the museum should return three paintings to the German museums that owned them before the Nazis took them under their law on "degenerate" art in the late 1930s and auctioned them in Switzerland: Emil Nolde's The Last Supper (1909) from Halle, Nolde's Figures and Dahlias (1919) from the Kunsthalle Mannheim and Henri Matisse's Reclining Nude I (1907) from the Museum Folkwang in Essen (The Copenhagen Post, 2 January 2014).
- Wandall told The Art Newspaper's website: "We should not use this regime and their illegal acts to accept this" (The Copenhagen Post, 2 January 2014).
- Chief curator Kasper Monrad replied that the German museums had a chance to reclaim the works after the Second World War and did not come forward, and asked: "What about all the private and public owners who have bought pieces in good faith?" (The Copenhagen Post, 2 January 2014).
