DESIGN MUSEUM: London, UK
ID: design-museum-london
Website: https://designmuseum.org (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of design and architecture at 224 to 238 Kensington High Street, London W8, near Kensington High Street Underground station.
- Founded in 1989 by Sir Terence Conran, with Stephen Bayley as first chief executive, in a former 1940s banana warehouse at Shad Thames; it reopened in Kensington on 24 November 2016. Tim Marlow has been director and chief executive since 2019.
- Housed in the former Commonwealth Institute building, refitted by a design team led by John Pawson, with about three times the space of Shad Thames (Wikipedia). Conran gave £17.5 million toward the move in June 2011 (Wikipedia).
- Exhibitions, the free collection display "Designer Maker User", the Future Observatory research programme, learning programmes, an auditorium, shops and cafés.
- Open daily, 10:00 to 17:00 Monday to Thursday and to 18:00 Friday to Sunday. The collection is free; exhibitions are ticketed, with a concession rate for disabled visitors and free entry for carers. Step-free throughout by lift, with bookable wheelchairs and Blue Badge spaces.

FUNDING
Design Museum: mostly self-generated: tickets, trading, donations and corporate partners, with a small government grant
- In 2018 the museum said it received two per cent of its funding from the public purse (Dezeen).
- The Conran Foundation lent the museum £3 million in 2018 and 2019, after the museum lost £2,026,462 in the year to March 2019; the then chair Lord Mandelson called its finances "not sustainable in the medium term" (Dezeen, 2019).
- Corporate partners named on its site include Bloomberg Connects, Canva, W Hotels, NOT A HOTEL, Nyetimber, Mailchimp (2023), Van Cleef & Arpels (2022), Harvey Nichols (2024), Nike, Greggs and Snap Inc (2025 to 2026).
- Trustees include Sebastian Conran and Lady Victoria Conran; the board is chaired by Stuart Roden, former chair of Lansdowne Partners, since July 2023 (trustees page).
- No reporting found of a current Deutsche Bank sponsorship; neither the museum's partner page nor Deutsche Bank's UK arts scheme names the other.
- No reporting found of the amounts given by individual corporate partners.

Red flags: artists withdrew work over an arms company event
- On 17 July 2018 the museum hosted a private event for Leonardo, the Italian aerospace and defence company, timed to the Farnborough airshow, during the exhibition "Hope to Nope" (The Art Newspaper).
- More than 40 artists, organised as Nope to Arms and including Peter Kennard, Shepard Fairey and Milton Glaser, removed over 40 works, about a third of the show, after an open letter on the Campaign Against Arms Trade site (The Art Newspaper, Dezeen).
- The directors Deyan Sudjic and Alice Black said "professional activists exploited the situation" and promised no such private hires while they reviewed policy; in 2019 the museum said it would judge funding proposals "on a case by case basis" (Hyperallergic).

Red flags: former chair dismissed over Epstein ties
- Lord Peter Mandelson chaired the board from his appointment, reported in December 2016 (Apollo), until Stuart Roden took over in July 2023 (trustees page).
- In September 2025 Prime Minister Keir Starmer dismissed him as UK ambassador to the US after supportive emails he sent Jeffrey Epstein after Epstein's 2008 conviction were revealed (Cherwell).
- In 2019 Nope to Arms raised concerns about his business ties to arms and oil companies (Hyperallergic).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on the 2018 Leonardo event and the museum's handling of it; nothing found on its programme since.
1. The Art Newspaper: Anny Shaw, "Peter Kennard among artists to remove their art from London's Design Museum in protest over arms event" (2018)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2018/08/02/peter-kennard-among-artists-to-remove-their-art-from-londons-design-museum-in-protest-over-arms-event
- Reports the removals and Kennard's demand that the directors rule out further arms trade events; he was "appalled" by their response.
2. Dezeen: Augusta Pownall, "Design Museum removes a third of work from Hope to Nope exhibition, on artists' request" (2018)
URL: https://www.dezeen.com/2018/08/08/design-museum-hope-to-nope-artist-remove-work-design-news/
- Danny Chivers of BP or not BP? called the museum's statement "badly-judged and inflammatory".
3. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Artists Demand Answers One Year After Withdrawing Work From London Design Museum" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/artists-demand-answers-one-year-after-withdrawing-work-from-london-design-museum/
- Argues the promised due diligence review produced no public answer; the 2018-19 annual review called event hire procedures "robust".
- Artists asked the museum to rule out arms, oil and tobacco funding and questioned Mandelson's business ties.
4. Apollo: Rakewell, "Meet your new chairman, the Prince of Darkness" (2016)
URL: https://apollo-magazine.com/meet-your-new-chairman-the-prince-of-darkness/
- Satirical column on Mandelson's appointment; it mocks Mandelson more than the museum.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum runs mainly on tickets, trading, donations and brand partners, backed by the Conran family. The weak points are the 2018 arms company hire and the vague policy that followed it, and a former chair later dismissed over his Epstein ties. Worth watching: whether it publishes a funding policy.
