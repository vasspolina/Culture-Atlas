EYEBEAM: New York, USA
ID: eyebeam-new-york
Website: https://eyebeam.org

OVERVIEW
- Art and technology nonprofit in Brooklyn, with residencies, fellowships, public programmes and exhibitions. It is registered as Eyebeam Atelier Inc, a 501(c)(3) with its exemption issued in October 1997 (ProPublica).
- Established in 1998 by John S. Johnson (Eyebeam). Wikipedia names John Seward Johnson III as founder, with co-founders David S. Johnson and Roderic R. Richardson.
- Spent 17 years in Chelsea and three years in Industry City, then moved in 2017 to the ground floor of a three-storey building at 199 Cook Street, Bushwick, developed by the Mann Group, about 6,000 square feet; the public opening was set for 30 November 2017 (Hyperallergic, Claire Voon, 1 November 2017).
- Programmes: residencies, fellowships such as the six-month Democracy Machine ($20,000 per artist), and the Digital Day Camp for high school students; over 550 artists have received support through residencies, fellowships and public programmes (Eyebeam, The Art Newspaper, Hyperallergic).
- Roderick Schrock, executive director, is listed as having resigned on 30 June 2025 (ProPublica).

FUNDING
Eyebeam: foundation and public grants: Atlantic, Ford, Jerome and Arison Arts foundations, the National Endowment for the Arts, New York State Council on the Arts and New York City Department of Cultural Affairs
- Eyebeam's support page names its institutional supporters: Atlantic Foundation, Ford Foundation, Jerome Foundation, Arison Arts Foundation, the National Endowment for the Arts, the New York State Council on the Arts, and the New York City Department of Cultural Affairs in partnership with the City Council.
- Revenue per its Form 990s (ProPublica): $1,631,254 in fiscal 2023, $1,455,741 in fiscal 2024 and $924,817 in fiscal 2025. Contributions made up $1,542,631, $1,409,470 and $851,756 of those totals.
- Its own programme money: the Democracy Machine awards of $20,000 per artist and the Palestine Futures grants of $12,000 each to five US-based Palestinian activists, artists and writers, launched in August 2024 (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: protest by fellows and a dispute over dismissals
- March 2024: a letter signed by nearly 100 people, about half of them fellowship and residency alumni, former staff or affiliates, asked Eyebeam to join the Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel (Pacbi). Five signatories were Eyebeam staff; three have since left, two terminated and one resigned (The Art Newspaper, Angella d'Avignon, 13 February 2025).
- Ten artist fellows of the 2024 Democracy Machine cohort issued a statement urging Eyebeam to call for a permanent ceasefire in Gaza and to endorse Pacbi. All but one cohort member withdrew their individual projects weeks before the showcase (The Art Newspaper).
- On 28 September 2024 the cohort's statement was shown as a single-channel video at the National Academy of Design in Manhattan, during the cohort's final showcase. It said the artists "found ourselves at odds" with the language built to house them (The Art Newspaper).
- Two staff members were terminated by Eyebeam: Kemi (first name only), given notice on 29 April 2024, and development manager Sharsten Plenge, terminated on 6 June 2024. Both believe they were dismissed for backing the statement. Executive director Roderick Schrock said Eyebeam was "greatly supportive" of the statement and called the dispute a "misunderstanding"; he denied retaliation, saying one termination was based on performance and another position was eliminated for cost reasons; a third staff member resigned for a role elsewhere (The Art Newspaper).
- The cohort says that since the showcase, executive leadership has not publicly addressed its intervention or made any offer of repair (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA

BOTTOM LINE
Eyebeam is funded by private foundations and by federal, state and city arts agencies, with revenue of $1,631,254 in fiscal 2023 falling to $924,817 in fiscal 2025. The documented dispute is the 2024 protest by its own fellows, aimed at Eyebeam, and the disagreement over the dismissals made by Eyebeam's leadership.
