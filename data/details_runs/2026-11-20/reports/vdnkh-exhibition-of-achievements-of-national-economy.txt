VDNKH (EXHIBITION OF ACHIEVEMENTS OF NATIONAL ECONOMY): Moscow, Russia
ID: vdnkh-exhibition-of-achievements-of-national-economy
Website: https://vdnh.ru (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Exhibition grounds, park and museum complex of about 237 hectares in the north of Moscow (Calvert Journal, 2015).
- It opened on 1 August 1939 and has been named VSKhV, VDNKh of the USSR and VVC; in 1992 it became the joint-stock company "GAO VVC" (vdnh.ru).
- It was built with a pavilion for each Soviet republic and each major industry, and its 29 surviving pavilions are listed monuments (Calvert Journal).
- The VDNKh name came back after a public vote in which 90% of Muscovites backed it (Calvert Journal).
- Museums on the site include the Cosmonautics and Aviation Centre in the Cosmos pavilion, the Atom museum in Pavilion 19, the restored Central Pavilion 1, the Karelia pavilion and the Museum of Heroism in the Grain pavilion, opened in December 2024 (mos.ru).
- Pavilion 57 houses the history park Russia, My History (myhistorypark.ru).
- The mayor's office states that 42 monuments, about 90% of the heritage objects on the site, have been restored since 2013 (mos.ru).
- The site quotes Mayor Sergei Sobyanin: about 33 million people visit a year. Sergei Shogurov is general director of JSC VDNKh (vdnh.ru).
- Visiting: a free circular bus runs from 9:00 to 21:00 from the main entrance arch, and less mobile visitors can borrow a wheelchair free at the information centres (vdnh.ru).

FUNDING
VDNKh: public: City of Moscow, with private investors
- The operator is JSC "Exhibition of Achievements of National Economy" (OGRN 1027700008874) (vdnh.ru).
- In 2013 the federal government held 70% and the city of Moscow 30% (Vedomosti, 2013). In November 2013 the federal government said it was handing its 70% share to the Moscow city government within six months (The Moscow Times, 2013). The mayor's office states that Putin decided the transfer in 2013 (mos.ru).
- Sobyanin ordered a city subsidy of 3.09 billion roubles for 2014, more than 1.5 billion of it for emergency repairs to 118 pavilions and structures (BFM, 2014).
- In 2019 the first stage of reconstruction was costed at more than 100 billion roubles: almost 60 billion from the Moscow budget and about 47 billion from private investors. Net profit in 2017 was 8.8 million roubles (Snob, citing Vedomosti).
- The mayor's office names the Culture Ministry, the Federal Guard Service, Roscosmos, Rosatom, the petrochemical company SIBUR and Soyuzmultfilm as bodies that took part at its invitation, and the governments of Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan as contributors (mos.ru).

Red flags: UK sanctions on Moscow's mayor, 2022
- The UK designated Mayor Sergei Sobyanin on 15 March 2022 for speaking in favour of the "special military operation" in Ukraine (UK Sanctions List). His city government owns and funds VDNKh.

Red flags: Investors close to Putin, 2019
- Snob reported in 2019 that companies of Gennady Timchenko would rebuild the Golden Kolos restaurant, and that Arkady Rotenberg was held to own the site's oceanarium; a representative of Rotenberg denied that he owns it.
- The UK designated Timchenko on 22 February 2022 and Arkady Rotenberg on 16 September 2022 (UK Sanctions List).

Red flags: SIBUR sanctions, 2026
- The UK designated PJSC SIBUR Holding on 12 November 2026 for carrying on business in a sector of strategic significance to the Russian government (UK Sanctions List).

Red flags: 2015 preservationists' objections to the tourist zone status
- The Culture Ministry ordered VDNKh redesignated as a "tourist zone"; Konstantin Mikhailov of the preservation group Arkhnadzor said the rules legalised buildings put up in the previous three to four years and left room for new construction (The Moscow Times, 2015).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little art press coverage; one design and culture magazine weighed the renovation against the site's propaganda past.
1. The Calvert Journal: Anya Filippova, "Grand designs: can a Stalinist propaganda park become an appealing public space?" (2015)
URL: https://www.new-east-archive.org/articles/show/3568/vdnkh-moscow-stalinist-renovation-urbanism-public-space
- Reports that critics had dubbed the project "hipster Stalinism" and that the revival could be read as a new empire remaking the old Soviet empire's sacred seat in its own image.
- Notes that the ice rink cost about 784.5 million roubles, which drew questions from the anti-corruption campaigner Alexei Navalny.
- Concludes that the new VDNKh will still be a welcome public space for Moscow.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind VDNKh is Moscow city money, joined by private investors and state corporations. Its weak points are an owner whose mayor is under UK sanctions and investors and partners who are now sanctioned; the money behind individual pavilions is worth watching.
