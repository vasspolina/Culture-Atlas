GALERI NASIONAL INDONESIA: Jakarta, Indonesia
ID: galeri-nasional-indonesia
Website: https://galeri-nasional.or.id (checked 2026-11-20; down: the domain did not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- State art gallery at Jalan Medan Merdeka Timur 14, Gambir, Central Jakarta, opposite Gambir Station (Popmama, May 2026; iNews).
- Dedicated as the national gallery on 8 May 1999 (Wikipedia).
- Gedung A, the main building, was built in 1817 as an Indies residence from the remains of Kasteel Batavia; it housed the Carpentier Alting Stichting school from 1900 to 1961 and an army brigade after 1965, until 1981 and 1982 (Wikipedia).
- It holds 1,770 works, including Raden Saleh, Affandi and Basuki Abdullah, and foreign artists such as Wassily Kandinsky, Sonia Delaunay and Zao Wou Ki (Wikipedia).
- Jarot Mahendra was the person in charge of the gallery unit in January 2025 (Antara).
- The whole site closed on 12 October 2026 for revitalisation until further notice (iNews, 11 October 2026).
- Before the closure it opened Tuesday to Sunday, 9:00 to 19:00, free with online registration (Popmama).

FUNDING
Galeri Nasional Indonesia: public: Indonesia's Ministry of Culture
- Owner: the Ministry of Culture, one of President Prabowo Subianto's new ministries, split from the education, culture, research and technology ministry (The Jakarta Post, 3 January 2025).
- Operating model: in April 2024 the Indonesian Heritage Agency was set to become a public service agency (BLU) running 18 museums, the national gallery among them, and 34 heritage sites; as a BLU it keeps ticket income and investor support instead of paying them to the treasury (Fortune Indonesia, 3 April 2024).
- In April 2026 it shared one BLU with the national museum; agency head Indira Esti Nurjadin said units must meet non-tax revenue targets that rise every year, and Culture Minister Fadli Zon said the gallery should ideally run as its own BLU (RRI, 25 April 2026).
- Revitalisation: on 31 August 2026 architect Jasin Tedjasukmana showed Fadli Zon a first design restoring Gedung A and the auditorium, with a rooftop restaurant (RRI).
- PT Nindya Karya broke ground on 23 October 2026 on a design-and-build contract covering the landscape and Gedung A, C and D (Kontan, 23 October 2026).

Red flags: 2024 cancellation of Yos Suprapto's exhibition
- The show "Kebangkitan: Tanah Untuk Kedaulatan Pangan", due to run from 20 December 2024 to 19 January 2025, was cancelled ten minutes before its opening on 19 December 2024 after Yos refused to remove paintings depicting former president Joko Widodo: he had covered two and refused to conceal three more (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Curator Suwarno Wisetrotomo called the works "too vulgar" and resigned; the gallery blamed "unavoidable technical constraints", and Fadli Zon withdrew from opening the show (ArtAsiaPacific).
- Zamrud Sayta Negara, head of the museum and gallery team, said the gallery offered a postponement, not a ban; Fadli Zon said the five works had no link to food sovereignty and added "mungkin juga ada motif politik" (perhaps there is also a political motive, our translation) (Mongabay, 23 December 2024).
- LBH Jakarta called the request repression of free expression; Komnas HAM asked the ministry and the gallery for an explanation (Mongabay).
- On 24 January 2025 Megawati Soekarnoputri, chair of PDI Perjuangan, criticised the cancellation and said artistic expression must be respected (CNN Indonesia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism centres on the 2024 cancellation; a 2025 essay attacks the rehung permanent display.
1. ArtAsiaPacific: Annette Meier, "Indonesian Artist's Exhibition Canceled Over Political Paintings" (2025)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/indonesian-artist-s-exhibition-canceled-over-political-paintings
- Reports the requests to hide the works and quotes Yos calling the cancellation "an alarm for freedom of expression in Indonesia".
2. CIMAM Museum Watch: "Postponing Censorship? Contentious Positions that Undermine the Museum as a Space for Artistic Freedom" (1 April 2025)
URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/postponing-censorship-contentious-positions-that-undermine-the-museum-as-a-space-for-artistic-freedom/
- Argues that calling the show postponed can become a route to cancellation, a form of censorship.
3. The Jakarta Post: editorial board, "Authoritarian alarm" (2025)
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/01/03/authoritarian-alarm
- Argues the closure should alarm the country about free expression and that the gallery "refused to live in the present". A newspaper editorial, not art media.
4. Indonesia at Melbourne: Laurel MacLaren, "Indonesia's National Gallery rewrites history as Prabowo's revisionism takes hold" (21 October 2025)
URL: https://indonesiaatmelbourne.unimelb.edu.au/indonesias-national-gallery-rewrites-history-as-prabowos-revisionism-takes-hold/
- The permanent display Fadli Zon, a Gerindra party co-founder, relaunched in July 2025 dropped the 2023 gallery on the Lekra movement and the persecution of artists after 1965.
- Objects to a wall text calling the Soeharto era one of "individual freedom". A university blog essay, not art media.

BOTTOM LINE
Clean money: state-owned and state-run, with no private sponsor on record. The weak point is political control by the ministry that runs it; worth watching is how the rebuild is paid for and who programmes the reopened gallery.
