MUSEUM OF ISLAMIC ART: Doha, Qatar
ID: museum-of-islamic-art-doha
Website: https://mia.org.qa (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Islamic art on an artificial peninsula about 60 metres off the Doha Corniche, in a park of roughly 290,000 square metres, linked to the mainland by two bridges (Wikipedia).
- One of the first projects initiated by Qatar Museums (stated by the museum). The then-emir, Sheikh Hamad, opened it on 22 November 2008 and the public was admitted from 8 December 2008 (Wikipedia).
- Designed by I. M. Pei, who took the fountain of the Ibn Tulun Mosque in Cairo as his model: limestone facades, five floors, a central tower and dome. Wilmotte & Associates designed the galleries and the Turkish firm Baytur Construction built it (Wikipedia).
- The collection runs from the 7th to the 20th centuries: manuscripts, metalwork, ceramics, jewellery, woodwork, textiles, coins and glass from three continents (stated by the museum).
- Reopened on 5 October 2022 after a rehang that put more than 1,000 objects on show for the first time and added a restored 19th-century Damascus Room (The Art Newspaper, 31 August 2022). Wikipedia counts 18 renovated galleries.
- Director Shaika Nasser Al-Nassr, at the museum since 2007, succeeded Julia Gonnella, director from 2017, who moved to the Lusail Museum (Artforum).
- On site: a library of more than 21,000 books on Islamic art, a gift shop, a cafe, Alain Ducasse's restaurant IDAM and MIA Park (museum site).
- 2026 exhibitions: "Empire of Light: Visions and Voices of Afghanistan", until 30 May (The Art Newspaper), and "Uzbekistan: Heritage in Motion", 146 objects, until 28 November (The Peninsula).
- Visiting: Saturday to Tuesday 9am to 7pm, Thursday 9am to 9pm, Friday 1:30pm to 7pm, closed Wednesday (museum site). Non-resident adults pay QAR 50; Qatar residents with ID and children aged 16 and under enter free (Qatar Living, July 2026).

FUNDING
Museum of Islamic Art: state-owned: Qatar Museums, a Qatari government entity; no private donor or sponsor named
- The museum is owned by Qatar Museums, which Wikipedia describes as "a Qatari government entity" founded in 2005 and formerly called the Qatar Museums Authority.
- Two unnamed people close to the situation said the museum's costs were ringfenced when the emir approved cuts to culture and other budgets in early 2016. Qatar Museums declined to comment on the culture budget (The Art Newspaper, Georgina Adam, 10 March 2016).
- The 2026 Afghanistan exhibition was made with the Aga Khan Trust for Culture, which handled most of the administrative work in Afghanistan and the transfer of objects from Kabul to Doha (The Art Newspaper, 6 February 2026).
- Qatar Museums opened the 2026 Uzbekistan exhibition with the Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation, "in partnership with Visit Qatar" (The Peninsula, 3 September 2026).
- The first show after the reopening, "Baghdad: Eye's Delight", borrowed from 22 institutions, the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York among them (The Art Newspaper, 2022).

Red flags: Gulf state body
- Qatar Museums works under the patronage of the Amir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, and is chaired by Sheikha Al Mayassa bint Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani (Qatar Museums site), the emir's sister (openDemocracy).
- The same body oversees Mathaf, the National Museum of Qatar and the 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum (Qatar Museums site).
- 10 March 2016: citing the Financial Times, The Art Newspaper reported that 240 Qatar Museums staff had been let go, leaving fewer than 800 of 1,200 two years earlier. Qatar Museums declined to confirm the figures.
- The Art Newspaper described the 2022 overhaul as one of the cultural initiatives "designed to boost Qatar's soft power status" before the Fifa World Cup.

Red flags: 2005 investigation of the minister who bought the collection
- Sheikh Saud bin Muhammed Al Thani, culture minister from 1997, was entrusted with creating the collections for Qatar's planned museums, a Museum of Islamic Art among them (Wikipedia). Bidoun writes that the museum's collection was "built up largely by" him.
- In 2005 he was investigated for misuse of public funds and dismissed from his post (Wikipedia). Bidoun describes a fall "amid speculation of financial irregularities" that "left him under house arrest".
- Wikipedia adds that it was sometimes unclear whether collections he assembled belonged to him or to Qatar.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism of the museum as an institution. The critical pieces concern its displays and the state spending around it.
1. Bidoun: Antonia Carver, "Museum of Islamic Art" (2009)
URL: https://bidoun.org/articles/museum-of-islamic-art
- The galleries are "almost devoid of information"; labels are minimal and audio guides "offered some description".
- Objects are "imbued with power and meaning more through the aesthetics of display than for their place in history".
- Notes the museum's "Bilbao-inspired brand-building potential".
2. The Art Newspaper: Georgina Adam, "Football outscores culture as Qatar spending slumps" (2016)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2016/03/10/football-outscores-culture-as-qatar-spending-slumps
- News report, not a critique of the museum. It relies on unnamed sources, who say staff sign strict confidentiality agreements.
- One source says the cost-cutting under Sheikh Tamim is not "very structured or thought out".
3. openDemocracy: Sarah El-Richani, "The birth pangs of Qatar's art scene" (2013)
URL: https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/birth-pangs-of-qatars-art-scene/
- Reports criticism of how public money was used and where unannounced acquisitions ended up.
- Does not criticise the Museum of Islamic Art, which it cites as evidence that the authority cultivated local and regional culture.

WIDER CONTEXT
- In 2013 social media users led by a Qatari journalist accused the Qatar Museums Authority of corruption, mismanagement and nepotism (openDemocracy).
- The same article reports that Qataris on social media objected to Damien Hirst's bronze sculptures, estimated to have cost $20m, and gives two accounts of why Adel Abdessemed's film "Printemps" was not shown at Mathaf: social media reports that Sheikha Mayassa intervened, and officials citing technical issues.
- The museum is owned by the body these accusations name.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is Qatari state money channelled through Qatar Museums, a body under the emir's patronage and chaired by his sister, with no budget or accounts published. The weak points are that opacity and the 2005 investigation of the minister who assembled much of the collection. Worth watching: whether Qatar Museums publishes accounts or a sponsor list.
