KAESONG KORYO MUSEUM: Kaesong, North Korea
ID: kaesong-koryo-museum-kaesong
Website: https://koryogroup.com/blog/north-korea-guide-kaesong-koryo-museum (checked 2026-10-24; a visitor guide by the tour company Koryo Tours, published 11 December 2018, not a site of the museum; no site of the museum itself found)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of Koryo-dynasty history in eastern Kaesong, open every day according to Koryo Tours; Wikipedia puts the site about 2 km northeast of central Kaesong.
- It has occupied the former Songgyungwan since 1987 (Wikipedia). No reporting found of who founded the museum or of any earlier name.
- The site is the former Songgyungwan (Sungyun Academy), the highest Confucian educational institution of the Koryo dynasty; Wikipedia gives its opening as 992 and its designation as the principal teaching institute as 1089, and Koryo Tours says the academy moved to Seoul in 1394 (Koryo Tours).
- The buildings burned in the Imjin War in 1592 and were rebuilt from 1602; Koryo Tours dates the reconstruction to 1602 to 1610. Koryo Tours counts 12 main and six auxiliary buildings on 20,000 square metres, Wikipedia counts 20 buildings besides Taesong Temple and Myongnyun Hall; the two sources do not agree.
- Over 1,000 artefacts in four halls (Koryo Tours): Hall 1 on politics, economy and society; Hall 2 on science and culture, including movable metal type and celadon; Hall 3 a 1.6 m iron Buddha from Chokjo Temple; Hall 4 on metalwork, sculpture and painting. An open-air exhibition by a stream holds stone pagodas and stupas from former Kaesong temples.
- Visiting: Koryo Tours says Hall 3 is usually left out of tours for foreign visitors. No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility.

FUNDING
Kaesong Koryo Museum: no funder found; the Atlas record classes it a North Korean state museum, but no page opened states its owner or budget
- No reporting found of a budget, accounts, donor list or board.
- A Seoul Shinmun report of 12 October 1991 gave no funding or operating details and said only that the North Korean authorities generally pay some attention to preserving the city's historic sites.
- In October 2015 the museum hosted a special exhibition of finds from the joint North and South Korean excavation of the Manwoldae palace site, organised by South Korea's Cultural Heritage Administration, the Ministry of Unification and the South-North Historians Council (Ajunews, 15 October 2015). That joint dig began in 2007.

Red flags: sanctions on the North Korean government
- Executive Order 13722, signed by President Obama on 15 March 2016, blocks property of the Government of North Korea and the Workers' Party of Korea within US jurisdiction. It defines the Government of North Korea as including its "agencies, instrumentalities, and controlled entities" (Executive Order text, American Presidency Project).
- No reporting found that the museum itself is named on a sanctions list.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media.

BOTTOM LINE
No private funder is found behind the museum, and no page opened states who owns or pays for it. The weak point is that the Atlas classes it as a North Korean state museum, which brings the US sanctions order; there are no accounts to check. Worth watching: any published word on ownership, and further joint excavation exhibitions such as the 2015 one.
