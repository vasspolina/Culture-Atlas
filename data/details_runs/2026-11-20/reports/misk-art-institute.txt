MISK ART INSTITUTE: Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
ID: misk-art-institute
Website: https://miskartinstitute.org (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Non-profit arts organisation in Riyadh that runs exhibitions, the Masaha residencies, education programmes, an annual grant and Misk Art Week from the Prince Faisal bin Fahd Arts Hall (Institute site, e-flux directory).
- Founded in December 2017 (Institute site). Artist Ahmed Mater was its founding director (The Art Newspaper, 2017).
- The hall was established in 1985 by royal decree of King Fahd as the Fine Arts Hall and later renamed for Prince Faisal bin Fahd. It covers 5,600 square metres and reopened under the Institute at the end of December 2019 (Arab News).
- In 2018 it became the commissioner of Saudi Arabia's national pavilion at the Venice Biennale (Arab News).
- The summer 2026 exhibition at the hall showed over 40 works by more than 30 Saudi and resident artists until 1 August (Asharq Al-Awsat, citing the Saudi Press Agency).

FUNDING
Misk Art Institute: state-linked: a subsidiary of the Misk Foundation of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman
- The Institute calls itself "A subsidiary of Mohammed bin Salman Foundation 'Misk'" (FAQ page).
- Wikipedia describes the Misk Foundation, set up by the Crown Prince in 2011, as a non-profit, non-governmental organisation whose other subsidiaries include Misk City, Manga Productions and Misk Schools.
- The Institute has acted as a funder abroad. MoMA's director Glenn Lowry thanked it for financially supporting the publication "Modern Art in the Arab World: Primary Documents" at the January 2018 launch of the Institute at MoMA (Hyperallergic, Rijin Sahakian).
- It was a donor to the Arab Art and Education Initiative in New York (Hyperallergic, Jasmine Weber). By late October 2018 Misk gave 15 percent of that programme's funding, with 85 percent from non-Saudi sources (The Art Newspaper).

Red flags: Gulf state body: founded by the Crown Prince's foundation in 2017
- 8 December 2017: The Art Newspaper reported that the Crown Prince "has launched an artistic arm of his philanthrophic Misk Foundation", with Mater as director (Anna Somers Cocks; spelling as printed).
- At the January 2018 launch Mater called the Institute's approach "instructed by the Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud". Sahakian writes that the Institute "was created and chaired by" the Crown Prince and that he appointed Mater himself (Hyperallergic, 1 November 2018).
- Arab News reported at the end of December 2019 that Culture Minister Prince Badr bin Abdullah, "who is also the chairman of the Misk Art Institute", was patron of the reopening of the hall.

Red flags: Institutions declined Misk money after the Khashoggi killing in 2018
- Misk had planned to pay in full for the Brooklyn Museum exhibition "Syria, Then and Now", which opened on 13 October 2018, and was to fund a Metropolitan Museum seminar on collecting the Middle East. Both museums paid themselves; the Met's event cost under $20,000 (Hyperallergic, Weber, 18 October 2018).
- Columbia University announced that Mater's lecture of 22 October 2018 would not take place, and the Middle East Institute withdrew from the Arab Art and Education Initiative (Hyperallergic, Weber).
- The Art Newspaper wrote on 30 October 2018 that Misk had suspended its foreign programme two months before the killing.
- The Gates Foundation committed $5 million to the Misk Foundation in 2017 and suspended its planned engagement in November 2018, calling the killing "extremely troubling". In April 2024 it announced a regional office in partnership with the Misk Foundation (Wikipedia).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism dates from October and November 2018, after the killing of Jamal Khashoggi, and targets the Institute's patron and its Western partners.
1. Hyperallergic: Rijin Sahakian, "A Coalition of The Willing: How Saudi Arabia and US Arts Institutions Partnered on a Cultural Diplomacy Offensive" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/468850/saudi-arabia-and-us-art-institutions/
- Argues that Mater and Stephen Stapleton, the Institute's director of international programmes, present the Saudi art scene and the Institute as "grassroots" while working "with the backing of MBS and national Saudi funds".
- Says The Art Newspaper's 2018 comment quoted Stapleton at length without mentioning his roles.
- Read in the Internet Archive copy; the live page now asks for an account.
2. Hyperallergic: Jasmine Weber, "Several Major New York Art Institutions Step Away from Saudi-Funded Initiatives" (2018)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/466508/several-major-new-york-art-institutions-step-away-from-saudi-funded-initiatives/
- News report, not an essay. It quotes the museums' statements and Stapleton, who said he was "fully understanding and supportive" of his partners' decisions on funding.
3. The Art Newspaper: Anna Somers Cocks, with words by Stephen Stapleton, "Do not penalise Saudi Arabian artists for the murder of Jamal Khashoggi" (2018)
URL: http://www.theartnewspaper.com/comment/do-not-penalise-saudi-arabian-artists-for-the-murder-of-jamal-khashoggi
- Argues that Saudi artists should not suffer for the killing and reports "even stricter self-censorship than before" in the kingdom.
- Names the Institute and Mater without criticising either.

BOTTOM LINE
The Institute's money comes from the Crown Prince's foundation, and it publishes no budget, accounts or donor list of its own. The weak points are that opacity and the 2018 retreat of its Western partners. Worth watching: which foreign museums take its money now.
