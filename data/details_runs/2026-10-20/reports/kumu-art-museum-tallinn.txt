KUMU ART MUSEUM: Tallinn, Estonia
ID: kumu-art-museum-tallinn
Website: https://kumu.ekm.ee/en/

OVERVIEW
- National art museum of Estonia at Weizenbergi 34 / Valge 1 in Tallinn, set into the limestone slope of Lasnamäe hill (Wikipedia), behind Kadriorg Park (Mart Kalm).
- One of five museums run by the Art Museum of Estonia, which has its main offices in the building; the Art Museum of Estonia dates from 17 November 1919.
- Kumu opened on 17 February 2006 (Wikipedia). The name is a shortened form of the Estonian words for "museum of art".
- Designed by the Finnish architect Pekka Vapaavuori, winner of an architecture competition; construction began in 2002 on Weizenbergi Street (Art Museum of Estonia).
- On site: exhibitions, an auditorium, a library, a gift shop and the Reval Café.
- It received the European Museum of the Year Award in 2008.
- Open Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00, Thursday 10:00 to 20:00, closed Monday; ticket sales end 30 minutes before closing. The site lists accessibility programmes.

FUNDING
Kumu Art Museum: state-founded foundation: Estonian Ministry of Culture operating grant, with own income, targeted grants and sponsors
- Sihtasutus Eesti Kunstimuuseum, the foundation that runs Kumu, was set up by the Estonian Ministry of Culture on 13 June 2016 on the base of the former state museum (2023 activity report).
- The report states its funding as the Ministry of Culture operating grant, own income from its activity, targeted grants and sponsor money.
- The building belongs to RKAS, the state real estate company (2023 activity report).
- The foundation's five-member supervisory board is appointed by the founder, the state, and elects the one-person management board (2023 activity report).
- Building: Kumu was among the first nationally important cultural buildings built with gambling tax paid into the Cultural Endowment of Estonia (ERR, Tiina Jaakson, 26 August 2020). The Cultural Endowment is a public distributor.
- Site sale: the Estonian state sold the historic building in central Tallinn designed by Eliel Saarinen in 2002 to pay for the construction of Kumu (A Shade Colder, Kaarin Kivirähk).
- The Art Museum of Estonia's thank-you page lists as supporters Akzo Nobel Baltics, Cobalt Law Firm, Data Plate, EELK, the Cultural Endowment of Estonia, Estonian Public Broadcasting, the Gambling Tax Council, Tallinna Kaubamaja, the Ministry of Culture, Law Firm Sorainen, Lincona, Liviko, Mammograaf, Merko Ehitus, NG Investeeringud, Nordecon Betoon, Nordic Hotel Forum, the National Culture Foundation, Rahvusooper Estonia, the Finnish Institute, Tallink and Viru Keskus, the Friends of Art Society and six named individuals.
- In 2023 the Cultural Endowment supported the acquisition of 109 works and gave its annual award to the Kumu exhibition "Through the Black Gorge of Your Eyes" (2023 activity report; title translated).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism; the criticism that exists concerns the site, the architecture and some displays, not the funding.
1. Urban Studies Society of Finland: Mart Kalm, "Where in the Capital is the Proper Location for the National Art Museum?" (2006)
URL: https://kaupunkitutkimuksenseura.fi/en/?p=455
- Published 1 October 2006. Questions the site behind Kadriorg Park, chosen in the 1994 competition: it suits a park outing, but he doubts it serves the museum's reach.
- Says the site works for families spending a Sunday in the park and asks whether that is too narrow a share of museum visits; suggests Kumu could have anchored the Lasnamäe housing district or been built on empty state-owned central lots.
- Says critics held back because objecting would cost the museum government funding.
- Criticises the site and planning, not the funders.
2. A Shade Colder: Kaarin Kivirähk, "Art museums and spaces in the Baltics are made of dreams"
URL: https://www.ashadecolder.com/art-museums-and-spaces-in-the-baltics-are-made-of-dreams
- States that Kumu immediately faced sharp criticism of its location, architecture and some of its displays.
- Quotes curator Anders Härm calling Kumu "a complex art political compromise" that combined a national gallery and a contemporary art museum under one roof, which he says left contemporary art at a disadvantage.

BOTTOM LINE
Kumu is run by a foundation that the Ministry of Culture set up in 2016 and funds with an operating grant, alongside own income, targeted grants and sponsor money; the state company RKAS owns the building. The building was among the first nationally important cultural buildings built with gambling tax paid into the Cultural Endowment (ERR, 2020), and A Shade Colder states that the state sold the Saarinen building to pay for it.
