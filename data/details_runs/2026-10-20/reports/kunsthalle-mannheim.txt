KUNSTHALLE MANNHEIM: Mannheim, Germany
ID: kunsthalle-mannheim
Website: https://www.kuma.art (checked 2026-10-25; online)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art at Friedrichsplatz 4 in Mannheim, with a collection running from Édouard Manet to Francis Bacon and a large sculpture collection (stated by the museum).
- Opened in 1909 as a municipal art gallery under its first director Fritz Wichert, in a building designed by Hermann Billing for an international art exhibition in 1907 (Wikipedia; the museum). The museum describes it as one of the world's first civic collections of modern art.
- Directors have included Gustav Friedrich Hartlaub, who coined the term "New Objectivity" for a 1925 exhibition and was dismissed by the Nazis in 1933, and Ulrike Lorenz; Johan Holten has led it since 2019 (Wikipedia).
- Building: Billing's Art Nouveau building plus the Hector building, designed by the Hamburg firm Gerkan, Marg and Partners and opened on 1 June 2018. It has seven exhibition halls and a glass-roofed atrium (Wikipedia; The Art Newspaper).
- The new building is called the Hector Building; Hans-Werner and Josephine Hector donated €50 million for it (the museum).
- Provenance research: Private Art Investor reported on 24 November 2015 that the museum had found clues that 18 works, including works by Moholy-Nagy, Slevogt and Degas, could have been taken from Jewish owners under the Nazis, and had listed them with the German Lost Art Foundation. The article reports no restitution.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday and public holidays, 10am to 6pm; on the first Wednesday of the month open until 10pm, with free admission from 6pm to 10pm (MVV Art Night). Closed Mondays, 24 December and 31 December. The building is described as very nearly barrier-free, with a small part of the Art Nouveau building closed to wheelchair users; manual wheelchairs can be borrowed if reserved. No reporting found of admission prices on the pages opened.

FUNDING
Kunsthalle Mannheim: city and private patrons: the City of Mannheim, the Hector family and foundations, a Förderkreis and a Stifterkreis
- Hector building: Hans-Werner and Josephine Hector gave €50 million through the Hector Foundation II for the €68 million building. The City of Mannheim contributed €10 million and the rest came from public and private sponsors, none named (The Art Newspaper, 31 May 2018). The museum states the €50 million was a donation by Hans-Werner and Josephine Hector. The Art Newspaper describes Hector as one of the founders of the software company SAP; its headline calls him a German billionaire.
- H.W. & J. Hector Foundation: supports exhibitions and artist projects and, every three years, awards the Hector Prize for Contemporary Art in Germany with the Kunsthalle; the 2019 prize went to Hiwa K. The Hector Foundation II Kunst gGmbH paid for James Turrell's light installation "Split Decision" (stated by the museum; no amounts or year).
- Stiftung Kunsthalle Mannheim, founded in 2012, has a foundation council chaired by Susanne Fuchs that includes Mannheim's Lord Mayor Christian Specht and Culture Mayor Thorsten Riehle; its executive board is Luisa Heese (chair) and Christoph Geber (finance). The Stifterkreis was founded on 14 September 2017 and has 57 individuals, foundations and companies as members; the page names none of them (the museum).
- Förderkreis für die Kunsthalle Mannheim: has acquired about 260 artworks that it lends to the museum permanently, and with the BildPaten scheme since 2009 has paid over €300,000 to restore about 40 paintings, sculptures and graphic works (stated by the museum).
- No reporting found of the City of Mannheim's annual grant to the museum, of annual accounts, or of named corporate sponsors on the pages opened.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the Kunsthalle Mannheim is a city museum supported by a large private gift: €50 million from Hans-Werner and Josephine Hector, an SAP co-founder, for the 2018 building, plus the City of Mannheim and a Stifterkreis of 57 members, with no flagged industry or government patron found. The weak points are that the Stifterkreis members and the city's yearly grant are not published on the pages read, and that one private family funds the building and a prize. Worth watching: the Hector Prize and who joins the Stifterkreis.
