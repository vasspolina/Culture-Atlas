MUSEO NACIONAL DEL ECUADOR (MuNa): Quito, Ecuador
ID: museo-nacional-del-ecuador-muna-quito
Website: https://muna.culturaypatrimonio.gob.ec

OVERVIEW
- National museum of Ecuador, housed in the Casa de la Cultura Ecuatoriana building in Quito, on Avenida Patria between 6 de Diciembre and 12 de Octubre (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Opened on 1 December 1969 as the Museo Arqueológico y Galerías de Arte Guillermo Pérez Chiriboga, founded by the Banco Central del Ecuador on the fifth and sixth floors of the bank's main building at La Alameda park (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The government describes the planned new museum as housing 1.2 million cultural and heritage items from 12,000 years of history (Newsroom Infobae with EFE, 10 July 2026).
- The Banco Central managed the museum until 2010; since then the state ministry in charge of culture has run it (Spanish Wikipedia).
- A new building is planned on a site of 13,004 square metres in front of La Carolina park, in Quito's financial and cultural centre (Newsroom Infobae with EFE). The design competition produced a winner on 6 July 2026 that the government rejected on 10 July (see Funding).

FUNDING
Museo Nacional del Ecuador: run by the state ministry in charge of culture
- Since 2010 the museum has been run by the state ministry in charge of culture; before that the Banco Central del Ecuador, a public bank, ran it (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The new building has an estimated investment of US$100 million (Newsroom Infobae with EFE). The Empresa Pública de Vivienda y Desarrollo ran the design competition. On 15 June 2026 it contracted the Colegio de Arquitectos de Pichincha for US$533,615.48 as a consultancy, and transferred a 35% advance of US$186,765.42 on 3 July 2026 (Redacción Primicias, Primicias, 13 and 22 July 2026).
- Competition: the Ministry of Education, Sport and Culture announced the winner on 6 July 2026, "Ecos del sol" by Campo Baeza + MAODA, chosen from 17 preliminary designs by a jury of national and international architects (Newsroom Infobae with EFE, 10 July 2026).
- Rejection: on 10 July 2026 Roberto Luque, Minister of Infrastructure and Transport and a member of the jury, announced on X that the design "no es lo que Ecuador necesita, especialmente Quito" (not what Ecuador needs, especially Quito; translation). Vice Minister of Culture Romina Muñoz, who had defended the design, left her post on 9 July (Newsroom Infobae with EFE; Expreso says she resigned on the afternoon of 9 July).
- Criticism of the first design: a Change.org petition reached thousands of signatures by 10 July, calling the building out of keeping with its surroundings and asking for a review of the nearly US$100 million of public money; urbanist Hernán Orbea said the building could talk better to the city (Expreso, Mariella Toranzos, 10 July 2026). Campo Baeza and Kenny Espinoza of MAODA defended the design (Expreso).
- New selection: six of the original studios were invited again (EFE in Vistazo, 13 July 2026). A new jury of Christian Wiese, Mathieu de Genot and Lu Junliang chose "Estratos Vivos" by SANAA-A0-CPA-JHS on 19 July 2026 (Primicias, 22 July 2026). El Diario (21 July 2026, no byline) reports that the new jury's score counted for 85% and a citizen vote for 15%.
- The prize is US$450,000 plus VAT; Luque said it goes to the new winner because "el contrato se tiene que cumplir" (the contract has to be fulfilled; translation), and a national and international tender for construction follows (Primicias, 22 July 2026).
- Alejandro Zaera-Polo, a member of the original jury, said on the podcast Politizados that weeks before the first ruling the jury was asked to place SANAA's proposal second, and that the jury rejected the request unanimously (El Diario, 21 July 2026). El Diario reports the request without identifying who made it. Luque called the statements "sumamente graves" (extremely serious; translation); he said he never spoke to Zaera-Polo about pressure and that he dealt with the President and with then vice minister Romina Muñoz (Primicias, 22 July 2026).
- The Mexican studio PRODUCTORA, which entered with Handel Guayasamín, called the outcome a "desastre político y mediático" and said participants asked for the jury's scores and technical reports and never received them (El Diario).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Ecuadorian state: a ministry runs it and public funds, estimated at US$100 million, are planned for a new building. The documented dispute is about the design competition: the government overturned the jury's first winner four days after the announcement, a new jury chose a second design, and a member of the original jury says it was asked to rank one team second.
