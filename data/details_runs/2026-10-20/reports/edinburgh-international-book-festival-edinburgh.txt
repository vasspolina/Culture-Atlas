EDINBURGH INTERNATIONAL BOOK FESTIVAL: Edinburgh, UK
ID: edinburgh-international-book-festival-edinburgh
Website: https://www.edbookfest.co.uk (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Annual August festival of authors' talks and events, a charity (SC010120) and company limited by guarantee, held since 2024 at the University of Edinburgh's Edinburgh Futures Institute, 1 Lauriston Place, on the former Royal Infirmary site.
- First held in 1983 in a tent; biennial at first, annual from 1997 (Wikipedia). Jenny Niven is director and chief executive; Sir Ian Rankin is patron.
- Held in marquees in Charlotte Square Gardens until 2019, extending onto George Street in 2017, then at Edinburgh College of Art from 2021 to 2023 (Wikipedia).
- Talks, children's and young adults' events, workshops and livestreams; attendance was about 162,000 in 2025 (Wikipedia).
- The 2026 festival ran 15 to 30 August, site open 09:30 to 23:00. The site is free to enter; adult events cost up to £21, with Pay What You Can and concessions for over-60s, students, under-30s, people on Income Support and disabled visitors. Support workers get free assistance tickets.

FUNDING
Edinburgh International Book Festival: mixed: ticket sales, public funders and private sponsors
- Public funders named on its site: Creative Scotland, the City of Edinburgh Council and Culture Edinburgh. The University of Edinburgh is partner and host (about page).
- The investment firm Baillie Gifford was principal sponsor for about 20 years until the festival ended the deal in May 2024 (Wikipedia).
- The Scottish Government then gave a £300,000 grant to help fill the gap (Holyrood, 2025).
- Ian Rankin became a major sponsor in June 2025, through a company he runs with his wife, Miranda (Wikipedia, Holyrood); no reporting found of the amount.
- The festival says ticket sales alone do not cover its costs (support page).
- No reporting found of the festival's annual income or of the amounts Baillie Gifford paid it.

Red flags: authors' campaign over its main sponsor
- On 12 August 2023 the author Mikaela Loach stopped her festival panel to protest Baillie Gifford, saying it held "£5 billion of investments in the fossil fuel industry"; the audience took the protest into the street (STV).
- More than 50 authors and event chairs signed a letter asking the festival to find new sponsors unless Baillie Gifford divested; Ali Smith, Zadie Smith and Gary Younge threatened to boycott the 2024 festival. Greta Thunberg pulled out in 2023, accusing the firm of greenwashing (STV).
- The firm said only 2% of clients' money went to fossil fuels (STV). Fossil Free Books later added Israel-linked investments to its demands (Prospect).
- In May 2024 the festival ended the partnership; it said "the pressure on our team has simply become intolerable" (UnHerd, quoting the festival).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism comes from both sides of the Baillie Gifford dispute: campaigners who objected to the sponsor, and commentators who said the festival gave in. One 2025 report covered criticism of its line-up.
1. UnHerd: Joan Smith, "Edinburgh book festival bows to activists over climate and Israel funding" (2024)
URL: https://unherd.com/newsroom/edinburgh-book-festival-bows-to-activists-over-climate-and-israel-funding/
- Calls Fossil Free Books "an intolerant movement" and argues the case against Baillie Gifford is misplaced.
- Argues that "poorly paid authors" will suffer from lost festival income.
2. Prospect: Miles Ellingham, "Fossil Free Books organiser: 'We have been called both anonymous and attention seeking'" (2024)
URL: https://prospectmagazine.co.uk/views/people/67189/fossil-free-books-organiser-we-have-been-called-both-anonymous-and-attention-seeking
- The organiser Johannesson says festivals came to depend on sponsors because they "have been neglected by one government after another".
- Says Baillie Gifford's exit removed about £1 million a year across festivals; names Edinburgh only in passing.
3. Holyrood: Kirsteen Paterson, interview with Ian Rankin (2025)
URL: https://www.holyrood.com/inside-politics/view,ian-rankin-divestment-in-the-arts-you-can-never-be-pure-enough-for-some-people
- Rankin: "Shutting down a book festival is not a way to further the debate."
- Defends corporate funding: "the real world is full of compromises."
4. Scottish Daily Express: David Walker, "Edinburgh Book Festival slated for 'platforming' Nicola Sturgeon but hosting no gender critical authors" (2025)
URL: https://www.scottishdailyexpress.co.uk/news/politics/edinburgh-book-festival-slated-platforming-35374012
- The writer Jenny Lindsay and Conservative MSPs Tess White and Pam Gosal said the "Repair" programme excluded gender-critical feminist writers.
- The festival did not respond in the article.

BOTTOM LINE
The festival now runs on tickets, Creative Scotland and city money, a Scottish Government grant and private backers such as Ian Rankin. Its weak point was a 20-year dependence on Baillie Gifford, which ended in 2024 after a documented authors' campaign. Worth watching: who replaces that money and whether new sponsors draw the same scrutiny.
