MUSEO DE LA SOLIDARIDAD SALVADOR ALLENDE: Santiago, Chile
ID: museo-de-la-solidaridad-salvador-allende
Website: https://www.mssa.cl (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of modern and contemporary art at Av. República 475, Santiago, near the República and Toesca Metro stations (museum site).
- José María Moreno Galván proposed it in 1971 and President Salvador Allende approved it; it was inaugurated on 17 May 1972 at the Museum of Contemporary Art of the University of Chile. About 650 works arrived in 1972 and 1973, including gifts from Joan Miró, Roberto Matta, Lygia Clark and Frank Stella (Wikipedia).
- It closed after the coup of 11 September 1973; exiled organisers continued it abroad from 1975 (Wikipedia).
- It reopened in September 1991 at the National Museum of Fine Arts, when the collection passed to the Chilean state. The state loaned the collection to the Salvador Allende Foundation in 1992, and since 2005 the Arte y Solidaridad Foundation has managed it (Wikipedia).
- It occupies the Palacio Heiremans, built in 1925 for the Belgian entrepreneur Amadeo Heiremans Vaerman. During the Pinochet dictatorship the building served as a detention centre and a DINA listening station (Wikipedia).
- Open Tuesday to Friday 10:00 to 18:00, Saturday and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00 (museum site). No reporting found of admission prices or accessibility details.

FUNDING
Museo de la Solidaridad Salvador Allende: mainly public: Chilean state transfers through the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural, plus project grants
- On 4 February 2025 the Servicio Nacional del Patrimonio Cultural approved a transfer of $660,197,000 (Chilean pesos) to the Fundación Arte y Solidaridad, which runs the museum. About 75 percent ($495.8 million) goes to staff costs; pay cannot exceed public-sector pay and unspent funds must be returned at year's end. The service says such transfers have been in the budget since 2005 and Congress approved them in 2024 (Ex-Ante, published by T13, 11 April 2025).
- Tania Bruguera's 2023 visit was supported by the Mellon Foundation, through a project administered by the University of Pennsylvania, and by Chile's Ministry of Cultures, Arts and Heritage (El Mostrador, citing the museum's site).
- The museum's 2025 funding report and 2024 annual report are PDFs whose text could not be read, so no amounts from them are given.

Red flags: Protest over the Tania Bruguera exhibition
- Campaign: Allende's grandson Pablo Sepúlveda Allende wrote to the museum asking for the cancellation of Bruguera's "Magnitud 11.9", then posted comments on X and signed an open letter (The Art Newspaper). He led a declaration against it in August 2023 with over 600 signatures, and said museum director Claudia Zaldívar is not "a la altura del cargo" (El Mostrador, my translation: not up to the post).
- Outcome: the show was delayed and opened on 8 October 2023 in modified form; the museum says one of its central subjects was suppressed (The Art Newspaper).- Museum response: Zaldívar said the attempt came from a small group and that the Salvador Allende Foundation approved the show (The Art Newspaper).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is the 2023 campaign against the Bruguera exhibition, which art outlets and CIMAM treated as a free-expression case. No other criticism of the institution found.

URL: https://cimam.org/news-archive/to-the-public-opinion/
- Backs the museum and Zaldívar, argues museums must offer space for dialogue, and records that the campaigners said Bruguera's presence betrayed Allende's socialist ideology.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The Salvador Allende Foundation, a separate body, was involved in a failed purchase of Allende's house on calle Guardia Vieja; foundation director Genaro Cuadros is a defendant in the criminal case (Ex-Ante, T13). In the Chamber, 38 deputies voted against the state funding, mostly opposition, and four abstained.
- Connection: the museum's transfer was approved about a month after that crisis.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is mostly Chilean state funding, with conditions on staff pay and returned surplus, plus named foundation support for individual projects. The weak points are the 2023 pressure from the Allende family to cancel a show, which the museum modified, and the political attention on state transfers to Allende-linked foundations. Worth watching: the full 2025 funder list.
