NEZU MUSEUM: Tokyo, Japan
ID: nezu-museum-tokyo
Website: https://www.nezu-muse.or.jp/jp/foundation/index.html

OVERVIEW
- Museum of pre-modern Japanese and East Asian art at 6-5-1 Minami-Aoyama, Minato-ku, Tokyo, about a 10-minute walk from Omotesando Station (Exit B3, with an elevator) (museum visit page, Wikipedia).
- Built on the private collection of Nezu Kaichiro (1860 to 1940); after his death a foundation was set up and began public exhibitions in 1941 (Wikipedia). Exhibitions resumed in 1946 after the collection was moved away from central Tokyo and escaped the May 1945 bombing (Wikipedia).
- Nezu Kaichiro bought Ogata Korin's "Irises" screen in 1914; it is shown for a limited time each April and May, when the irises in the garden bloom (Wikipedia).
- Closed from 2006 and reopened in autumn 2009 in a new building by Kengo Kuma (Wikipedia).
- On site: galleries, a garden, a museum shop and NEZUCAFE; the foundation also rents out tea rooms and holds tea ceremonies (museum foundation page).
- Open 10:00 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30), closed Mondays. Collection exhibition 1,400 yen online or 1,600 yen at the door; students 600 or 800 yen; high school students and younger free; 200 yen off for holders of a disability certificate and one attendant. Assistant dogs are allowed (museum visit page).

FUNDING
Nezu Museum: private foundation: the Nezu collection, admissions and earned income
- The museum is run by the Nezu Museum Foundation, a public interest incorporated foundation with its main office in Minato-ku; its listed activities include exhibitions, collection care and restoration, research, tea ceremonies and tea-room rentals, museum merchandise, garden upkeep, cafe operations and research grants (museum foundation page).
- The foundation takes supporting members, individuals or groups who endorse its purpose, on terms set by its board (museum foundation page).
- Yearly business plans, reports, budgets and settled accounts from the 2012 fiscal year onward are posted as PDFs (museum foundation page).
- The chairman is Koichi Nezu (根津公一), honorary chairman of Tobu Department Store. Councillor Yoshizumi Nezu (根津嘉澄) is chairman of the board of Tobu Railway Co. (museum foundation page). The museum's founder, Nezu Kaichiro, became president of Tobu Railway in 1905 and was independently wealthy through stock investments (Wikipedia).
- Other directors and councillors named on the page include Akinobu Maeda (honorary adviser, Mizuho Financial Group), Hiroo Mori (director and executive vice president, Mori Building) and Mamoru Yasuda (representative director and president, Yasuda Real Estate) among directors, and Yoshiteru Yoneyama (chairman of the board, Fukoku Mutual Life Insurance) among councillors.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a foundation built on Nezu Kaichiro's collection, with the Nezu family and executives of Tobu Group companies sitting on its board and council. Its own pages list admissions, shop, cafe and tea-room activities and supporting members, and post yearly accounts.
