MUSEU DO AMANHÃ (MUSEUM OF TOMORROW): Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
ID: museu-do-amanhã
Website: https://museudoamanha.org.br (checked 2026-10-24)

OVERVIEW
- Science museum on Pier Mauá, at Praça Mauá 1 in the port area of central Rio de Janeiro, which describes itself as "um museu da Prefeitura do Rio", a museum of the city government.
- Conceived by the Fundação Roberto Marinho and the city of Rio; managed by the Instituto de Desenvolvimento e Gestão (IDG); the museum site lists the Ministry of Culture under "realização". It opened on 17 December 2015 with President Dilma Rousseff attending (Wikipedia).
- The building was designed by the Spanish architect Santiago Calatrava and cost about 230 million reais; it was presented as an icon of the reurbanisation of the port area ahead of the 2016 Olympics (Wikipedia).
- The main exhibition was organised in five areas: Cosmos, Earth, Anthropocene, Tomorrow and Us (Wikipedia). In December 2025 the Anthropocene room of the exhibition "Do Cosmos a Nós" was replaced by "Onde Estamos?", and a new exhibition, "Oceano – o Mundo É um Arquipélago", opened on 17 December (Folha do Leste).
- More than 8 million visitors came in its first decade (Folha do Leste).
- Open Thursday to Tuesday, 10:00 to 18:00 (last entry 17:00), closed Wednesdays. The site gives no ticket prices on the page read; entry was free for everyone on 17 December 2025.

FUNDING
Museu do Amanhã: mixed: run by a city-linked management body with corporate sponsors led by Itaú, Shell, Vale and Motiva, plus tax-incentive money
- Strategic sponsor: Itaú. Maintainers (mantenedores): Shell, Instituto Cultural Vale and Motiva. Sponsors: IBM and Tag. Strategic partner: Grupo Globo. Co-sponsors: Águas do Rio and Saint-Gobain. Supporters: Bloomberg, Engie, B3, White Martins, Granado, Mattos Filho and Porto. Municipal-incentive sponsors: Accenture, Fitch Ratings and the Prefeitura do Rio de Janeiro (ISS tax). Media partners include Rádio Mix FM, Nova Paradiso FM, jbfm, CurtaOn and Revista Piauí (all from the museum's supporters page, undated).
- In December 2025 Folha do Leste named Banco Santander Brasil, Shell, Vale, Motiva, IBM and TAG among sponsors, and said Shell, Vale and Motiva support the Oceano exhibition.
- Laws used: the federal Lei de Incentivo à Cultura and the municipal incentive law.
- Construction: RioOnWatch reports the Fundação Roberto Marinho, linked to the Globo media group, as the builder in a public-private partnership, and says costs rose from an original R$70 million to R$213 million. It also reports that the foundation received R$24 million from the State Environmental Conservation Fund in 2011, a transfer later investigated by the Public Ministry (advocacy source).
- No reporting found of sponsor amounts or audited accounts for the museum.

Red flags: Fossil fuels (Shell, maintainer)
- Shell is one of three named maintainers on the museum's supporters page and supports the 2025 Oceano exhibition (Folha do Leste).
- No reporting found of a protest or open letter aimed at Shell's sponsorship of this museum.

Red flags: Mining disaster (Instituto Cultural Vale, maintainer)
- The Instituto Cultural Vale is a named maintainer. Vale owned the Córrego do Feijão tailings dam at Brumadinho, which collapsed on 25 January 2019 and killed 270 people (Wikipedia). Prosecutors announced in January 2020 that Vale, TÜV Süd and 16 individuals would be charged with intentional homicide and environmental crimes, and in February 2021 Minas Gerais state reached an agreement with Vale on reparations initially estimated at US$7 billion (Wikipedia).
- Vale denied wrongdoing and said the mine met the latest standards (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a protest aimed at Vale's sponsorship of this museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. The one substantial critique is from a favela-focused advocacy site and was written before the opening; it criticises the Porto Maravilha redevelopment more than the museum's funders.
1. RioOnWatch: Marta Ill-Raga, "A Critique of Rio's Museum of Tomorrow, Set to Open This Weekend" (2015)
URL: https://rioonwatch.org/?p=25724
- Ties the museum to the Porto Maravilha port redevelopment, which the author sees as a model built around mega-events and speculative development under Mayor Eduardo Paes, and compares it to the abandoned Guggenheim project that residents opposed because they wanted a school and public services.
- Criticises its futurist focus given the port area's history as Africa's largest slave port; Rolf Malungo de Souza is quoted: "The City only wants to preserve the festive part of black heritage".
- Reports the cost rise from R$70 million to R$213 million, attributing part of it to Calatrava's history of budget overruns.
- Advocacy outlet, not art press. Merced Guimarães of the Instituto dos Pretos Novos is quoted viewing the museum as a positive asset.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is partly a city project and partly sponsor-funded, with two named maintainers, Shell and Vale, that fall under the atlas's fossil fuel and mining disaster flags. The weak points are those maintainer roles, the undisclosed sponsor amounts and the cost overrun reported on the building. Worth watching: the Oceano exhibition, which Shell, Vale and Motiva support.
