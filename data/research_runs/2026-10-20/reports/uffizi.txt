UFFIZI GALLERIES – Florence, Italy
Website: https://www.uffizi.it (loads normally, checked 2026-10-20; the governance page could not be fetched)

OVERVIEW
- Le Gallerie degli Uffizi, the Italian state museum complex in Florence that also covers Palazzo Pitti and the Boboli Gardens. The site footer carries the Ministry of Culture logo.
- Director: Simone Verde, in post since 2024 (The Art Newspaper). Eike Schmidt was director in 2023.
- In August 2026 it announced a €50M renovation across the complex, due to finish around mid-2028 (The Art Newspaper, 10 Aug 2026).

FUNDING
Uffizi: mainly public, with tickets and private patrons.
- Ministry of Culture: state museum. Half of the €50M 2026 revamp comes from ministry funds for major redevelopments; the other half comes from ticket sales (museum spokesperson, quoted by The Art Newspaper).
- Veronica Atkins: about €4.5M (nearly $5M) to restore the Boboli Gardens amphitheatre, announced March 2023. The museum calls it the largest gift a Florentine museum has received from a private individual. Atkins is president of the Dr. Robert C. and Veronica Atkins Foundation. Through the Friends of the Uffizi she had already paid for other restorations (Finestre sull'Arte; Uffizi donation page).
- Edwin L. Wiegand Foundation (Reno, Nevada): $1M for the new layout of the Vasari Corridor, 11 Oct 2023 (Finestre sull'Arte; The Florentine).
- Salvatore Ferragamo: €600,000 in 2014 to refit Quattrocento rooms (Artribune; The Florentine, 2014).
- Fondazione CR Firenze: partner in the "Uffizi Diffusi" programme (Uffizi news page, as cited in the existing record; it could not be fetched this run). No amount found.
- Friends of the Uffizi Gallery: buys works and pays for restorations.
- Komen Italia: in October 2026, people who donate to breast-cancer prevention get free entry. This is a promotion, not a funding source.
- No reporting found of fossil-fuel, arms or tobacco sponsors.

Red flags: Outsourced staff dispute
- On 31 March 2026 the unions USB and Sudd Cobas began protests over long-serving temporary workers losing their jobs after the services contract changed hands. Dire names the contractor as CoopCulture. Workers say the company did not keep their earlier agreements (Dire, 31 Mar 2026).
- On 5 April 2026 workers protested in front of Botticelli's Venus with a banner reading "Basta vite precarie" (Open, 6 Apr 2026).
- Unverified: the unions say CoopCulture and the ministry use on-call contracts (union claim, reported by Dire).

Red flags: Climate protests (not about funders)
- 22 July 2022: Ultima Generazione activists glued their hands to the glass over Botticelli's Primavera under a "No Gas No Coal" banner (Euronews).
- 13 Feb 2024: Ultima Generazione taped photos of the Campi Bisenzio floods to the glass over the Venus. Nothing was damaged (Finestre sull'Arte).
- Neither protest named a Uffizi funder.

BOTTOM LINE
A state museum funded by the Ministry of Culture and ticket sales, with large private gifts from US patrons and Florentine companies. No reporting found of controversial funders. The one issue worth watching is labour: the dispute over outsourced staff working under the CoopCulture contract.
