OVERVIEW
- Art museum of Indiana University Bloomington, at 1133 East Seventh Street on the Bloomington campus (museum website).
- Opened in 1941 as the Indiana University Museum of Art in a gallery in Mitchell Hall under Henry Radford Hope (Wikipedia).
- Renamed in May 2016 for the Indianapolis philanthropists and IU alumni Sidney and Lois Eskenazi (museum news release; IU Today).
- The I.M. Pei and Partners building, dedicated in 1982, is two concrete triangles joined by a glass-roofed atrium. It closed in May 2017 for a renovation by Susan T. Rodriguez of Ennead Architects with Browning Day Mullins Dierdorf and reopened in November 2019 (Wikipedia; museum news release).
- More than 45,000 works, from ancient jewellery to paintings by Picasso and Jackson Pollock (IU Today; Wikipedia).
- Directors: David A. Brenneman from 2015 to 2024 (IU Today); Mindy N. Besaw, from Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, was appointed on 17 June 2025 (museum news release).
- Visiting: free. Open Tuesday to Saturday from 10am (to 7pm on Thursday, 5pm otherwise), Sunday noon to 5pm, closed Monday. Lifts serve all galleries, wheelchairs can be borrowed, and a sensory-friendly hour runs on Saturdays (museum website).

FUNDING
Eskenazi Museum of Art: university-run: Indiana University, with private naming and renovation gifts
- Owner: Indiana University, a public university. Encouraged by Chancellor Herman B Wells, the university's trustees began budgeting a small yearly amount for the museum, with special allocations for the collection (Wikipedia).
- Renovation: Sidney and Lois Eskenazi gave $15 million in 2016, the largest cash gift in the museum's history, as the lead gift for the renovation, plus nearly 100 works, mostly prints, including 34 by Joan Miró; IU added $20 million through its For All Bicentennial Campaign match (museum news release). IU Today put the renovation at $30 million.
- Source of the naming money: Sidney Eskenazi founded the real estate firm Sandor Development Co. in 1963 and built it to more than 70 properties in 23 states (Eskenazi Health). He died in February 2026, weeks after Lois Eskenazi (WRTV, 2 Feb 2026).
- Endowment: the museum received a $500,000 Andrew W. Mellon Foundation endowment challenge grant in 2012 (Wikipedia).

Red flags: 2023 cancellation of the Samia Halaby retrospective
- On 20 December 2023 Indiana University cancelled "Samia Halaby: Centers of Energy", the first American retrospective of the Palestinian artist and IU alumna, due to open in February 2024 after three years of preparation (Indiana Public Media). IU said "academic leaders and campus officials cancelled the exhibit due to concerns about guaranteeing the integrity of the exhibit for its duration" (The Art Newspaper).
- Madison Gordon, who works at Halaby's studio, said the university told Halaby the show was cancelled because of her pro-Palestinian social media posts and security risks (Indiana Daily Student).
- Halaby said: "Clearly the intent is to suppress Palestinian voices at this very time" (The Art Newspaper).
- On 15 November 2023 US Representative Jim Banks wrote to IU asking about antisemitism on campus and warned it could lose federal funding if it condoned antisemitism (Indiana Daily Student).
- In January 2024 Provost Rahul Shrivastav called the show a "potential lightning rod" and said he would "err on the side of caution" when there was a risk of violence (Indiana Daily Student).

Red flags: 2024 protest over the Halaby cancellation
- A petition by Gordon, Halaby's grandniece, passed 13,500 signatures by 18 January 2024. Julie Trébault of PEN America's Artists at Risk Connection called the cancellation "an alarming affront to free expression", and the National Coalition Against Censorship wrote to IU president Pamela Whitten on 11 January 2024 (Indiana Public Media).
- On 7 February 2024, 75 faculty and staff signed a letter criticising IU's lack of transparency; it said three artists had withdrawn from the Eskenazi School's McKinney Visiting Artist Series (The Herald-Times).
- Michigan State University's Broad Art Museum showed its Halaby retrospective until 15 December 2024 (Michigan State University).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is its university and an Indianapolis real estate couple's naming gift. The weak point is the university's 2023 cancellation of the Halaby retrospective, which drew a petition, faculty protest and artist withdrawals. Worth watching: whether the director appointed in 2025 changes how such decisions are made.
