DIRIYAH BIENNALE FOUNDATION: Diriyah, Saudi Arabia
ID: diriyah-biennale-foundation
Website: https://biennale.org.sa/en (checked 2026-10-20; returned 403 Forbidden to page fetches, so its own pages could not be read)

OVERVIEW
- Saudi state foundation that runs two alternating biennales: the Diriyah Contemporary Art Biennale in the JAX District of Diriyah, near Riyadh, and the Islamic Arts Biennale at the Western Hajj Terminal of King Abdulaziz International Airport in Jeddah.
- Established in 2020 by Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Culture; the Minister of Culture chairs its board (Saudipedia). Aya Al-Bakree is chief executive (e-flux, 2025).
- JAX District is a former industrial site in Diriyah converted into an arts complex (ArtReview).
- Editions: Diriyah Contemporary Art Biennale December 2021 to March 2022, curated by Philip Tinari; Islamic Arts Biennale 2023; "After Rain", 20 February to 24 May 2024, directed by Ute Meta Bauer (Biennial Foundation); Islamic Arts Biennale "And All That Is In Between", 25 January to 25 May 2025 (e-flux); "In Interludes and Transitions", 30 January to 2 May 2026, directed by Nora Razian and Sabih Ahmed (List).
- The foundation says the first Islamic Arts Biennale drew over 600,000 visitors (e-flux).
- General admission to the 2026 contemporary biennale was free, with timed-entry tickets recommended (List).

FUNDING
Diriyah Biennale Foundation: state: Saudi Ministry of Culture
- Created by the Ministry of Culture, which is its responsible entity, and chaired by the culture minister (Saudipedia).
- Hyperallergic describes it as part of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's push to grow tourism and cut the country's reliance on oil (2025).
- Programme partners for 2026 included the Red Sea Film Foundation, Naifactorylab and a Nikon youth initiative (List).
- No reporting found of the foundation's budget, of a sponsor list, or of published accounts.

Red flags: a Saudi government body
- The foundation is an arm of the Saudi state: founded by the Ministry of Culture and chaired by the minister (Saudipedia).
- Critics allege its Islamic Arts Biennale is "artwashing" Saudi Arabia's human rights record (Hyperallergic, 2025, not naming the critics).
- In October 2025 Glenn Lowry, outgoing director of MoMA, agreed to advise the Islamic Arts Biennale (Hyperallergic).
- No reporting found of a documented protest, open letter or boycott aimed at the foundation's biennales.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little direct criticism found. Reviews praise the historical loans and the shows; the artwashing charge appears in passing, without a named critic.

BOTTOM LINE
The foundation is wholly a Saudi government project, run under the Ministry of Culture and chaired by the minister. The weak points are the lack of any published budget or sponsor list and the artwashing charge that follows Saudi state culture spending. Worth watching: whether artists or institutions lending to the biennales face organised protest.
