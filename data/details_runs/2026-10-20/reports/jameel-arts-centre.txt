JAMEEL ARTS CENTRE: Dubai, UAE
ID: jameel-arts-centre
Website: https://jameelartscentre.org (checked 2026-10-24; loads)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art institution at Jaddaf Waterfront on Dubai Creek, on a small manmade island at the tip of the waterfront (Dezeen, 15 November 2018, Ali Morris).
- Founded and funded by Art Jameel (Jameel Arts Centre); it opened on 11 November 2018 (Art Jameel).
- Named for its founder Art Jameel, which was established in 2003 as part of Community Jameel (Art Jameel).
- Building: 10,000 square metres designed by Serie Architects with Dubai-based ibda design as consultant architects; aluminium-clad boxes in two clusters, a single-storey concrete colonnade and local desert planting, Serie's first built project in the Middle East (Dezeen; Art Jameel).
- On site: galleries, an open-access research library, a roof-top terrace, a writer's studio, a members' lounge, a restaurant and a shop (Art Jameel).
- Visiting: open 10am to 8pm daily, Friday 12pm to 8pm, closed Tuesday; admission free; the centre states all public areas are fully accessible (Jameel Arts Centre).

FUNDING
Jameel Arts Centre: privately funded by Art Jameel (Jameel family philanthropy); Dubai government bodies named as supporters, with no funding shown
- The About page says: "founded and funded by Art Jameel, the Centre was developed in dialogue with, and with the support of" Dubai Holding and Dubai Properties, "the master-developers of the Jaddaf area", "as well as other government ministries and entities of the United Arab Emirates". The page does not say these bodies paid for or built the centre, and it names no amounts.
- Dezeen (2018) says the centre is the centrepiece of a mixed-use development "owned by global investment company Dubai Holding". Art Jameel's page describes the Jaddaf Waterfront Sculpture Park as "a collaboration with Dubai Holding". Wikipedia describes Dubai Holding as the personal investment portfolio of Dubai's ruler, lists it as state-owned and shows Dubai Properties as one of its business groups. No page opened shows a government body funding the centre, running it or developing the building itself.
- Art Jameel was established in 2003 as part of Community Jameel, set up to continue the Jameel family's philanthropy begun in the 1940s by Abdul Latif Jameel (Art Jameel). The College Art Association review (published 21 March 2019) describes Art Jameel as "sponsored by the Jameel family". No reporting found of the amount the family gives.
- The family business, Abdul Latif Jameel, was founded in Jeddah in 1945 and became a Toyota distributor in Saudi Arabia in 1955; it acquired the solar developer Fotowatio Renewable Ventures in 2015 (Wikipedia). No reporting found of oil, gas, arms or tobacco interests.
- No reporting found of annual reports, accounts, a funder list or a board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism found. The one review found is largely admiring and queries the audience and one installation.
1. College Art Association (CAA News): Sabrina DeTurk, "International Review: Opening of the Jameel Arts Centre, Dubai, United Arab Emirates" (21 March 2019)
URL: https://www.collegeart.org/news/2019/03/21/international-review-jameel-arts-centre-dubai-sabrina-deturk/
- Calls the centre "a welcome addition to the creative landscape of Dubai", but questions whether it can build a lasting local audience: a weekday visit in January "found the author almost alone in the spaces". The review does not give the year of the visit.
- Says the question of the representation and inclusion of Emirati artists "will undoubtedly also be raised".
- Asks whether Chiharu Shiota's installation served more as "an Instagrammable photo opportunity" than serious art; this criticises the art, not the institution.
- Does not mention Dubai Holding or the Dubai government, and does not discuss how the family's sponsorship shapes the programme.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the centre is the Jameel family's philanthropy through Art Jameel, and the centre states it was founded and funded by Art Jameel. The weak points are that the centre names Dubai Holding, Dubai Properties and other UAE government bodies as supporters of its development without saying what they gave, and that no accounts, funder list or board are published. Worth watching: whether the centre states what the state bodies pay for.
