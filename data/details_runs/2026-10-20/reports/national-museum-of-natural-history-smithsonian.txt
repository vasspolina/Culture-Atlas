OVERVIEW
- Smithsonian natural history museum in Washington, holding over 148 million specimens (Wikipedia).
- The United States National Museum was founded in 1846 as part of the Smithsonian; the Natural History Building opened to the public on 17 March 1910 (Wikipedia).
- The Neoclassical building was designed by the Washington firm Hornblower & Marshall and covers about 1.5 million square feet, 325,000 of them exhibition and public space (Wikipedia).
- Its halls include the Hope Diamond exhibit, the Sant Ocean Hall (opened 27 September 2008), the David H. Koch Hall of Human Origins (opened 17 March 2010) and a dinosaur hall.
- Admission is free and the museum is open 364 days a year; it recorded about 3.3 million visitors in 2025 (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
National Museum of Natural History: mainly public: US Congress through the Smithsonian, with named private donors
- The Smithsonian budget is about 1.25 billion dollars a year, about two-thirds from federal appropriations, plus endowment, gifts and earned income (Wikipedia, Smithsonian Institution).
- David H. Koch gave 15 million dollars toward the 20.7 million dollar Hall of Human Origins in 2010 and 35 million dollars in 2012 toward a new dinosaur hall (Wikipedia).
- The Sant family gave 15 million dollars to endow the Ocean Hall; Kenneth E. Behring gave 20 million dollars in 1997 for modernisation (Wikipedia).
- A 5 million dollar American Petroleum Institute donation for the Ocean Hall was withdrawn by the donor before the Board of Regents considered it, after two regents questioned a conflict of interest (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of a current donor list for this museum on the pages read.

Red flags: David H. Koch (fossil fuels), campaigned name
- Koch became a majority owner of Koch Industries with his brother Charles in 1983; the Koch brothers gave over 145 million dollars to climate-denying think tanks between 1997 and 2018 (NPR, cited by Wikipedia). He died on 23 August 2019.
- In March 2015 an open letter signed by 36 scientists went to more than 330 museums asking them to cut ties with fossil fuel funders; fifteen non-profits asked the Smithsonian and the American Museum of Natural History to remove Koch from their boards (Salon).
- In June 2015 protesters delivered a petition of about 430,000 signatures to the Smithsonian asking it to remove him from the museum's advisory board; he stayed on it (Hyperallergic).
- A museum spokesperson denied that Koch or his money shaped the Hall of Human Origins (Salon).

Red flags: White House review, 2025
- On 12 August 2025 the White House asked Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch for a review of eight museums, this one included, covering exhibition text, social media and curatorial process, with "content corrections" to begin within 120 days (NPR, via WUSF).

Red flags: Arctic exhibit, 2006 to 2007
- Unverified: Robert Sullivan, a former associate director of exhibitions, said executives rewrote "Arctic: A Friend Acting Strangely" to minimise human causes and add uncertainty; the Smithsonian denied political influence (CBS News, 2007). Sullivan said he knew of no Bush administration pressure.

WIDER CONTEXT
- The scientists' letter went to more than 330 museums, and the board campaign also targeted the American Museum of Natural History, where Koch left the board in December 2015 (Salon, Hyperallergic).
- The Smithsonian museums share the White House review of eight museums; this museum is one of the eight (NPR).

BOTTOM LINE
Most of the museum's money is public, through the Smithsonian. The clear weak point is David Koch: his 15 million and 35 million dollar gifts funded two halls, he was still on the advisory board in January 2016, and a 2015 campaign targeted him there. He died in 2019. Also watch the 2025 White House review.
