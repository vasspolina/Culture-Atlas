UCCA CENTER FOR CONTEMPORARY ART: Beijing, China
ID: ucca-center-for-contemporary-art
Website: https://ucca.org.cn/en (checked 2026-10-24; the About and Visit pages loaded, the Support and Gala pages loaded on a second try, and the 2025 annual report PDF timed out)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art centre in the 798 art district, Beijing, with two further sites: UCCA Dune in Beidaihe and UCCA Clay in Yixing, Jiangsu.
- Founded in 2007 as the Ullens Center for Contemporary Art by collectors Guy and Myriam Ullens; renamed UCCA after ownership passed in 2017 to Chinese and international patrons and shareholders (UCCA website).
- Visiting, UCCA Beijing: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 19:00. Discounts for students, over-60s, disabled guests and groups; children under 1.2 metres free.

FUNDING
UCCA Center for Contemporary Art: private non-profit foundation: patrons, an annual gala, corporate sponsors, with Chinese investors as owners since 2017
- 2017 sale: investors led by Jason Jiang, founder of Focus Media, and the education company Future Edutainment bought the centre (The Art Newspaper). ArtReview also names Jerry Mao of Lunar Capital; the price was not disclosed.
- Structure: a non-profit, tax-exempt foundation with a board registered in Hong Kong in 2017 (ArtReview), accredited by the Beijing Bureau of Civil Affairs (UCCA website).
- Patrons: the Patrons Council began in 2012. The Foundation Council lists dozens of names, including Jason Jiang, Jerry Mao and the Bao Foundation (UCCA website, undated). No amounts are given.
- Gala: the 2024 gala raised nearly RMB 8.4 million (stated by the space).
- Sponsors: the Support page describes Lead, Presenting and Supporting Partner tiers and names no company. The About page calls Aranya a "strategic partner" supporting UCCA Dune (stated by the space, undated). Louis Vuitton, Dior, Bloomberg and Aranya as 2025 partners are not confirmed: no page opened names them, and the 2025 annual report could not be opened.

Red flags: UCCA removed a work in 2022
- Li Songsong's painting "Six Men" (2008) was removed from the group show "Common Ground" in early August 2022 after visitor complaints, and the show closed a week early on 7 August. A UCCA spokesperson confirmed the removal; "Police were involved in an investigation of the work", and the page does not say who started it (Art Asia Pacific, 22 September 2022).
- UCCA is privately owned, so this was the centre's own act.

WIDER CONTEXT
- 2011: the Chinese government asked the Ullens Center to move an Ai Weiwei opening from March to a later date. Ai cancelled the show rather than accept censorship, and communications director Vivienne Li told AFP the centre "did not want to upset authorities" (Hyperallergic, 14 February 2011).
- November 2019: the Beijing Municipal Bureau of Culture refused authorisation to import works for Hung Liu's solo show, due to open on 6 December, and gave no explanation. Director Phillip Tinari wrote on 12 November that "There has been a tightening of the civil sphere in China in recent months" (Art Asia Pacific, 21 November 2019). Hyperallergic (20 November 2019) says local authorities cancelled the show.
- Connection: in 2011 and 2019 the actors were Chinese authorities, not the centre's owners; the state-run Seven Star Group is only the landlord of the Beijing buildings (The Art Newspaper, 2018).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism centres on censorship and compliance with officials, with an older piece on management. No critique of the sponsors was found.
1. Hyperallergic: Hakim Bishara, "Chinese Authorities Cancel Exhibition, Signifying Increased Censorship of Political Art" (2019)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/hung-liu-beijing/
- Reports that authorities denied the customs permits and that the show "was canceled by local authorities".
- Frames it as growing censorship; names the authorities, not UCCA, as the party that cancelled.
2. Hyperallergic: Kyle Chayka, on the cancelled Ai Weiwei show (2011)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/18750/ai-exhibition-ullens-center/
- Argues UCCA needs assurance that political pressure will not shape its programming.
- Calls Guy Ullens its only major funder and hopes the new owners avoid "pay-for-play".
3. The Art Newspaper: Chris Gill, on upheaval at the Ullens Center (2008)
URL: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/archive/what-is-going-on-at-the-ullens-center-in-beijing
- Reports staff departures and objections to foreign-run management. Does not concern the current owners.
4. Art Asia Pacific: Pamela Wong and Sharpe Xie, "Painting of Kamikaze Removed at UCCA Beijing" (2022)
URL: https://artasiapacific.com/news/painting-of-kamikaze-removed-at-ucca-beijing
- Reports the removal; says why the work was sensitive "remains unclear".

BOTTOM LINE
The funding is private and non-profit, with no fossil-fuel, arms or Gulf funder found, though no sponsor list could be read. The documented weakness is censorship pressure: Chinese authorities blocked or delayed shows in 2011 and 2019, and UCCA itself removed a work in 2022. Worth watching: who the 2025 partners are, and whether UCCA publishes accounts.
