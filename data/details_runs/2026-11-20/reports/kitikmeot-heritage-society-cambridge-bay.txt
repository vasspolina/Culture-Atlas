PITQUHIRNIKKUT ILIHAUTINIQ / KITIKMEOT HERITAGE SOCIETY (MAY HAKONGAK CULTURAL CENTRE): Cambridge Bay, Nunavut, Canada
ID: kitikmeot-heritage-society-cambridge-bay
Website: https://www.kitikmeotheritage.ca (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Inuinnait heritage and language society based in Cambridge Bay (Iqaluktuuttiaq), working to revive Inuinnaqtun, which it says has fewer than 500 speakers left (society website).
- Incorporated on 6 March 1996 by librarian Kim Crockatt, Emily Angulalik, Ekvana Angulalik and Reverend Keith Todd, out of volunteer oral history and library projects begun in the early 1990s (2020-21 annual report). The name Pitquhirnikkut Ilihautiniq means "learning through culture".
- The May Hakongak Community Library and Cultural Centre opened in May 2002 and is named for the local cultural advocate May Hakongak; it holds a public library, a museum space, an art gallery and regional archives (society website).
- The Kuugalaaq Cultural Campus, a workspace of about 1,300 square feet on a 2,550 square metre site built with Cambridge Bay firms Qillaq Innovations and Aurora Energy Solutions, opened in September 2024 for Inuinnaqtun immersion; it is not open for drop-in visits (annual report 2023-24, society website).
- Programmes: land-based camps, sewing and toolmaking workshops, an Inuinnaqtun dictionary app launched in spring 2024 and archaeology with the University of Toronto (annual report 2023-24).
- Executive director: Emily Angulalik; board president: Annie Atighioyak (society website).
- Visiting: the May Hakongak Centre is open Monday to Friday, 9.00 to 16.00, closed 12.00 to 13.00; programmes are free (society website).

FUNDING
Kitikmeot Heritage Society: mainly public: federal and Nunavut governments, with Inuit organisations, foundations and small donors
- Revenue in 2021-22: CAD 2,471,324, of which CAD 1,006,801 came from the Government of Canada, CAD 420,086 from the Government of Nunavut, CAD 396,378 from corporate grants, CAD 149,735 from donations and CAD 15,124 from the Kitikmeot Inuit Association (audited statements, year to 31 March 2022, Lenehan McCain and Associates).
- Grant-makers named in the 2021-22 schedules include Canadian Heritage, Indigenous Services Canada, Library and Archives Canada, the Canada Council for the Arts, Nunavut Tunngavik Inc. (CAD 255,000 recognised that year for a feasibility study), Bell Let's Talk, RBC Future Launch, the Canadian Women's Foundation, the Full Circle Foundation and the Millipede Foundation (audited statements).
- Current federal grants: CAD 162,608 from Canadian Heritage's Museums Assistance Program for Inuinnaqtun work from April 2025; CAD 52,665 from Library and Archives Canada (2025); CAD 40,000 from Polar Knowledge Canada (2026); CAD 25,000 a year from New Horizons for Seniors (Government of Canada grants register).
- Kuugalaaq building: CAD 766,018 from CanNor's Canada Community Revitalization Fund toward a CAD 1.7 million building, after Nunavut Tunngavik Inc., the Government of Nunavut, the federal government and Indigenous Clean Energy paid for feasibility work (CBC, May 2022); CAD 240,000 from the Canada Cultural Spaces Fund (2023) and CAD 99,000 from Natural Resources Canada for an embodied carbon analysis (2023) (grants register).
- Donors: the Kivgaqtuiniq Circle of individual supporters, listed by name on the website, and the Sobirovs Law Firm; it is a registered charity, number 897090536 RR 0001 (society website).
- Own businesses: it holds an investment in Pitquhikhainik Ilihainiq Inc., whose profit it books as revenue (CAD 5,003 in 2021-22), and runs Kaapittiaq, a social enterprise coffee company (audited statements, annual report 2023-24).

BOTTOM LINE
Clean: public, Inuit and foundation money. The weak point is dependence on government grants for most of its income, which leaves programmes exposed to changes in federal and territorial budgets.
