NEW MUSEUM OF CONTEMPORARY ART: New York, United States
ID: new-museum-new-york
Website: https://www.newmuseum.org

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art museum on the Bowery in Manhattan, at 235 Bowery (Wikipedia).
- Founded in 1977 by curator Marcia Tucker, who led it until 1999; Lisa Phillips succeeded her in 1999, and the museum announced in September 2025 that she would retire in April 2026; Massimiliano Gioni was named director in July 2026 (Wikipedia).
- The building by SANAA with Gensler opened on 1 December 2007 and cost about 50 million dollars (Wikipedia). The museum closed in March 2024 for an 82 million dollar, seven-storey addition by OMA's Shohei Shigematsu and Rem Koolhaas with Cooper Robertson, named for the late philanthropist Toby Devan Lewis; it opened on 21 March 2026 (Wikipedia, 6sqft).
- Programmes include the NEW INC incubator (Mellon Foundation); Rhizome is an affiliated nonprofit (Hyperallergic).
- Admission at the 2026 opening was 25 dollars for adults, 19 dollars for students, free for visitors 18 and under, with pay-what-you-wish on Thursday evenings from 7 to 9 p.m. (6sqft).

FUNDING
New Museum of Contemporary Art: private philanthropy: trustees, foundations and individual donors
- Contributions were 22,476,994 dollars, 87.5% of the 25,693,478 dollar revenue in the year to June 2025; net assets were 121,840,246 dollars (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Form 990 data).
- Capital campaign: in May 2016 the museum launched an 80 million dollar campaign, with 43 million dollars already given by trustees (ArtReview, 11 May 2016). The late philanthropist Toby Devan Lewis gave 30 million dollars to the 2026 addition, the largest gift in the museum's history (6sqft).
- Andrew W. Mellon Foundation: 1,500,000 dollars on 8 December 2022 for 36 months to support NEW INC's new director, Salome Asega (Mellon Foundation).
- Trustees: under Phillips the board grew from 18 to 42 members; as of 2015 it included Maja Hoffmann, Dakis Joannou and Eugenio López Alonso (Wikipedia). James-Keith Brown spoke as board president in 2026 (Hyperallergic, 20 March 2026).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Criticism covers the 2009 to 2010 trustee-collection show, 2020 workplace allegations and the 2024 and 2026 reactions to the building.
1. Hyperallergic: Valentina Di Liscia, "Rhizome Board Member Resigns in Protest of Workplace Culture at New Museum" (6 November 2020)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/600246/rhizome-board-member-resigns-in-protest-of-workplace-culture-at-new-museum/
- Seth Stolbun resigned from Rhizome's board on 5 November 2020 after a New York Times report by Robin Pogrebin, based on more than 30 current and former staff, on the museum's workplace culture. One former assistant described Phillips as emotionally abusive; former financial officer E. Annette Nash Govan accused Phillips of concealing cash flow problems and firing her after she told the board.
- The museum called the complaints "falsehoods and hearsay from disgruntled former staffers". Phillips argued that her management style would be forgiven in a male leader. The New Museum Union filed a National Labor Relations Board complaint in August 2020 over pandemic layoffs.
2. Hyperallergic: Aaron Short, "What's So New About the New Museum Building?" (20 March 2026)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/whats-so-new-about-the-new-museum-building/
- Reports divided reactions to the 82 million dollar OMA addition. Christopher Hawthorne (Punch List) called it "sleek, slick, try-hard, angular, studiously aloof" and wrote that it is better suited to a different kind of institution, one with a sit-down restaurant and showy features, than to a contemporary art kunsthalle. Aruna d'Souza, in an Instagram post, called the building "not at all welcoming".
- At the press preview masking tape remained on the stair railings and wall panels were missing; the gift shop moved to the back of the lobby, replacing a small special exhibition space.
3. Hyperallergic: Rhea Nayyar, "New Yorkers Aren't Thrilled About the New Museum's Expansion" (22 March 2024)
URL: https://hyperallergic.com/new-yorkers-arent-thrilled-about-the-new-museum-expansion/
- Quotes Lower East Side locals, via EV Grieve, calling the 2019 designs an "oversized hulk" and "soulless", and an Instagram commenter writing of the "MoMa-fication" of the museum. Criticises the building, quoting social media comments.
4. Wikipedia: article on "Skin Fruit: Selections from the Dakis Joannou Collection" (show announced September 2009; New York Times review dated 4 March 2010)
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_Fruit:_Selections_from_the_Dakis_Joannou_Collection
- A show curated by Jeff Koons drew on the collection of trustee Dakis Joannou, who has collected Koons heavily. Wikipedia says several bloggers raised ethical red flags and a front-page New York Times article followed; Tyler Green wrote an editorial in The Art Newspaper and Jerry Saltz published responses in New York magazine. The museum responded in defence of its decision.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is private philanthropy: trustees' gifts above all, with foundation grants such as Mellon, and contributions made up 87.5% of 2025 revenue. The criticism on record concerns a trustee's collection in the 2009 to 2010 Skin Fruit show, workplace conditions under the director in 2020 and reactions to the building in 2024 and 2026.
