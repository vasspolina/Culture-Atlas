CEMETI, INSTITUTE FOR ART AND SOCIETY: Yogyakarta, Indonesia
ID: cemeti-institute-yogyakarta
Website: https://www.cemeti.art

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art space at Jl. DI Panjaitan No. 41, Mantrijeron, Yogyakarta; it calls itself "Indonesia's oldest platform for contemporary art".
- Founded in 1988 by the artists Mella Jaarsma and Nindityo Adipurnomo in the front room of their house; first called Galeri Cemeti, then Rumah Seni Cemeti (Cemeti Art House), and given the subtitle "Institute for Art and Society" in 2017 by a new team.
- Directed by co-directors and chief curators Dito Yuwono and Mira Asriningtyas; the board lists Mella Jaarsma, Nindityo Adipurnomo, Jompet Kuswidananto, Natasha Sidharta and Jean-Pascal Elbaz.
- Under the Suharto regime the founders registered Cemeti as a small company, which let them avoid the government permission process for exhibitions (un Projects, Briony Galligan, 2013).
- Programme: exhibitions, workshops, talks, assemblies, publications, long-term research, and a three-month artist residency held twice a year (March to May and September to November), running since 2006 (Res Artis).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Saturday, 10:00 to 17:00.

FUNDING
Cemeti: artist-founded and privately run: sales of works from its stockroom, residency partner funds and foundation grants
- Operating costs: Cemeti sells works from its stockroom to cover operating costs and has never worked as a dealer; Jaarsma notes that the scene is privately funded, with "no subsidies" (un Projects, 2013).
- Residency programme and arts-management training: supported by a Prince Claus Fund scholarship (un Projects, 2013). The Prince Claus Fund lists Cemeti as a Fertile Ground Partner and says it joined the Fund's network in 2018 as a NextGen Partner.
- Residency partners named by Res Artis: Mondriaan Fund (Netherlands), Goethe-Institut Indonesia, National Arts Council Singapore and Australia Council for the Arts, each supporting artists from its own country. Partner funds cover travel, visa, materials, research and public programme costs, allowance, accommodation and publication; Cemeti also pays for travel, housing and production.
- Mondriaan Fund: for the residency work period of 1 March to 31 May 2027 it offers EUR 8,772 per resident, and pays the studio and living rent directly to Cemeti. Its page lists Mirjam Linschooten (2019) and, with no year given, Coco Duivenvoorde, Kevin van Braak, Faysal Mroueh, Lidwien van de Ven and Paul Hendrikse as previous residents.
- 2024: Cemeti received the Art Space Grant, a collaboration between KADIST and AFIELD (AFIELD profile). AFIELD says Cemeti is rethinking its funding strategies under a five-year plan.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Cemeti is artist-founded and mixed: stockroom sales, residency funding from the Mondriaan Fund, Goethe-Institut Indonesia, the National Arts Council Singapore and the Australia Council, a Prince Claus Fund scholarship and partnership, and the 2024 KADIST and AFIELD Art Space Grant.
