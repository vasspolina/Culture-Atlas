GOVETT-BREWSTER ART GALLERY / LEN LYE CENTRE: New Plymouth, New Zealand
ID: govett-brewster-len-lye-centre-new-plymouth
Website: https://govettbrewster.com/ (checked 2026-10-20)

OVERVIEW
- Contemporary art gallery and single-artist centre at 42 Queen Street, New Plymouth, owned and operated by New Plymouth District Council (NPDC).
- Opened on 22 February 1970, funded by Monica Brewster (née Govett), who gave £50,000 to the city in 1962 and a further £72,000 in 1970 to start a collection (Wikipedia).
- Named for its founder, Monica Brewster, born Govett (Wikipedia).
- The gallery converted the city's Regent cinema; the Len Lye Centre, designed by Andrew Patterson in curved stainless steel and concrete, opened on 25 July 2015 (Wikipedia, Pattersons).
- Houses the Len Lye Foundation's archive and collection, which Lye placed at the gallery before his death in 1980, plus an arthouse cinema (stated by the space, NPDC).
- Open daily 10am to 5pm; $17 for visitors, $12 for over-65s and students, free for district residents and under-16s (stated by the space).
- The Len Lye Centre side is closed for changeover until "Len Lye: Energies" opens on 5 December 2026 (stated by the space).

FUNDING
Govett-Brewster / Len Lye Centre: mainly public: New Plymouth District Council, with Todd Energy as founding partner of the Len Lye Centre
- NPDC is the gallery's principal funder and owner; the gallery had more than 75,130 visitors last year (NPDC, 2026).
- The 2012 funding plan for the $10 million Len Lye Centre: TSB Community Trust $1 million (2009) plus an underwrite of the remaining balance, Ministry for Culture and Heritage $4 million, Lotteries Environment and Heritage Fund $0.5 million, Todd Energy $3 million (2011) and private donors $0.2 million (NPDC release on Scoop, 25 September 2012).
- In 2013 ratepayers were due to pay about $360,000 a year in operating costs, shared with the gallery (RNZ). The finished centre cost $11.5 million (Landfall Review).
- Todd Energy, which calls itself New Zealand's leading natural gas provider, is "Founding Corporate Partner" and "Perpetual Foundation Corporate Partner", with the Todd Energy Foyer and Learning Centre, 2015 to 2025 (Govett-Brewster Foundation, Todd Energy).
- Other partners shown as logos, without amounts, include Creative New Zealand, the Ministry of Education, Toi Foundation and Craigs Investment Partners (stated by the space).

Red flags: Todd Energy, a current gas producer, as founding partner
- Todd Energy gave $3 million in 2011, the only company named in the build plan (NPDC on Scoop).
- Its partnership continues: the Foundation lists it as a perpetual partner. Separately, Todd Energy reported on 24 December 2025 that it had received the first NP Partners Community Impact Award for two decades of community partnership in Taranaki; its post does not mention the gallery (Govett-Brewster Foundation, Todd Energy).
- The architects say the building's energy system was negotiated with the project's sponsors, "a major international sustainable energy corporation"; the page does not name the company (Pattersons).
- In August 2025 artist Fiona Clark circled the Len Lye Centre in a hessian-sack dress, a performance the gallery itself describes as "a critique of the institution's willingness to accept financial sponsorship from fossil fuel corporations"; the gallery screens it until 15 November 2026 (stated by the space).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Little criticism in art media. Local news recorded objections to cost in 2013; the sharpest critique of the funding comes from an artist's performance that the gallery itself now shows.
1. Govett-Brewster: Fiona Clark, "What would this place look like without fossil fuel sponsorship?" (2025, shown 2026)
URL: https://govettbrewster.com/exhibitions/2026/open-window-what-would-this-place-look-like-without-fossil-fuel-sponsorship
- Staged in the month the gallery celebrated the centre's tenth anniversary; curated by Simon Gennard.
- Places Clark, who has lived in North Taranaki since 1975, in a tradition of institutional critique of fossil fuel sponsorship.
- An artwork, not press; it does not name Todd Energy.
2. Landfall Review: Andrew Paul Wood, "Kinetic Experience" (2016)
URL: https://landfallreview.com/kinetic-experience/
- A review of the book Zizz! The life and art of Len Lye; it describes the centre in passing as the "mirror-finish, fingerprint-prone" $11.5 million building, entered through the gift shop.
- Prefers Lye's own words to what Wood calls the "bubbly 'Len Lye Industry'" and its province-linked marketing campaigns.
- Does not discuss the centre's funding or Todd Energy.
3. RNZ: "Formal start for Len Lye centre work" (2013)
URL: https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/221182/formal-start-for-len-lye-centre-work
- Reports that the centre was criticised as a waste of money and had run over budget; a protester shouted "no more lies" at the plaque unveiling.
- News report; does not name the critics.

BOTTOM LINE
The gallery is council-owned and mainly ratepayer-funded. The weak point is Todd Energy, a current gas producer that paid $3 million towards the Len Lye Centre and remains its named founding partner. The gallery now shows an artist's critique of that sponsorship.
