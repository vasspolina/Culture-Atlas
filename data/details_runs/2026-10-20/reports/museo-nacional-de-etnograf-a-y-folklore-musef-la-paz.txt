MUSEO NACIONAL DE ETNOGRAFÍA Y FOLKLORE (MUSEF): La Paz, Bolivia
ID: museo-nacional-de-etnograf-a-y-folklore-musef-la-paz
Website: https://www.fundacionculturalbcb.gob.bo

OVERVIEW
- National ethnography museum in La Paz, on the corner of Ingavi and Jenaro Sanjinés streets; it also has a two-room branch in Sucre, open since 2004 (Spanish Wikipedia).
- Opened on 23 August 1962 as the Museo de Artesanía y Arte Popular, with roots in the Departamento Científico de Etnografía created in 1925 for the country's centenary; renamed Museo Nacional de Etnografía y Folklore in 1974 (Spanish Wikipedia). The Fundación Cultural del Banco Central de Bolivia (FC-BCB) says the museum was created in 1962 under the government of Víctor Paz Estenssoro.
- The museum has been managed by the FC-BCB since 1995 (FC-BCB press note, August 2022). Wikipedia dates the museum's passage to the Banco Central to 1983.
- It occupies a house built about 1730, declared a national monument in 1930 (Spanish Wikipedia). Bolivian Express (Isabel Cocker, 20 July 2016) describes a colonial house near Plaza Murillo that has grown into a larger modern building.
- On site: a collection of more than 33,000 cultural objects and more than 280,000 documentary items (FC-BCB, 2022), including a textile hall and a hall of masks (Bolivian Express).
- For its 60th anniversary in 2022 it held the 36th Reunión Anual de Etnología and opened the exhibition "Uyway-Uywaña: Crianza Mutua para la Vida" (FC-BCB).
- Elvira Espejo Ayca has been director since 2013 (CIMAM, June 2020); she left in 2020 and returned in 2021 (see Red flags).

FUNDING
Museo Nacional de Etnografía y Folklore: run by the Fundación Cultural del Banco Central de Bolivia, a central bank foundation
- The FC-BCB manages MUSEF and five other centres; Law 1670 of 1995 created it (Spanish Wikipedia).
- The Banco Central de Bolivia set up the foundation about 20 years before 2016 to oversee the country's cultural heritage (Isabel Cocker, Bolivian Express, 20 July 2016).
- The FC-BCB press note of August 2022 describes MUSEF as a dependency of the foundation.
- The FC-BCB named the directors of its museums in February 2021, among them Espejo at MUSEF (Eju.tv, Nancy Castro, 18 February 2021).

Red flags: protest over the 2020 removal of the director
- On 1 June 2020 the FC-BCB board asked Espejo and the director of the national art museum (MNA), Max Jorge Hinderer Cruz, to make their posts available, according to La Razón of 16 June 2020 as cited by CIMAM. FC-BCB president Guillermo Mariaca called the request natural with a change in presidency (CIMAM).
- The Museum Watch Committee of CIMAM, the international committee of modern art museum professionals (Bart De Baere, Calin Dan, Sarah Glennie, Malgorzata Ludwisiak, Victoria Noorthoorn and Eugene Tan), published an open letter on 29 June 2020. It called the requests a forced resignation without due process, said the two were the only two of nine directors asked to resign, and demanded that the board withdraw them. It wrote to Mariaca on 23 June 2020 and shared a petition supporting the two directors, started by Pablo Lafuente and Miguel A. López (CIMAM, "La Paz: International Museum Best Cases in Danger").
- CIMAM states that the FC-BCB board was entirely replaced in March 2020, after a temporary government took office in November 2019.
- The FC-BCB named Espejo director again in February 2021, about eight months after her departure (Eju.tv, 18 February 2021). The FC-BCB said it placed its trust in people with broad knowledge of Bolivian cultures.
- Unverified: MUSEF workers, in an anonymous communiqué titled "Soy Musef, no Elvira Espejo. No en mi nombre", said former colleagues were dismissed or forced to resign because of her mistreatment, called for clarification of corruption and irregular handling of museum property, and said seven years in post was reason enough to renew the leadership (Correo del Sur, 19 June 2020, no byline). The communiqué is unsigned.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is run through the foundation of Bolivia's central bank. The documented controversy is governance, not money: the FC-BCB board's 2020 request that the director step aside, an open letter and petition against it, and her reappointment in 2021.
