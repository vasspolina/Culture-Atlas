TAI KWUN: Hong Kong, China
ID: tai-kwun-hong-kong
Website: https://www.taikwun.hk (checked 2026-10-24; the pages other than the Jockey Club funding page could not be read, so opening hours and admission are not stated here)

OVERVIEW
- Centre for heritage and arts in the former Central Police Station compound in Central, Hong Kong: 16 historic buildings dating from 1841 to 1925, including three declared monuments, the former Central Police Station, the Former Central Magistracy and Victoria Prison (Wikipedia).
- Opened to the public in part on 29 May 2018 (Wikipedia). "Tai Kwun" is the name of the revitalised compound.
- Revitalised in a partnership between the Hong Kong government and the Hong Kong Jockey Club; operated by The Jockey Club CPS Limited, a not-for-profit set up by the Club's Charities Trust (stated by the space).
- Herzog & de Meuron did the masterplan, Rocco Design Architects the executive design, and Purcell the heritage conservation; two new buildings were added (Wikipedia).
- New buildings include JC Contemporary, whose stairs are a popular visitor spot (Wikipedia).
- Construction began in 2011; a wall and roof collapse in 2016 halted work (Wikipedia).

FUNDING
Tai Kwun: funded by the Hong Kong Jockey Club, a betting operator, through its Charities Trust, in partnership with the Hong Kong government
- Chief Executive Carrie Lam said at the opening ceremony on 25 May 2018 that the project was "a gift from the Hong Kong Jockey Club to the people of Hong Kong", with the Club committing HK$3.8 billion against the government's original estimate of HK$1.8 billion announced in October 2007 (news.gov.hk).
- The space says the project was a partnership between the Hong Kong government and the Club, and that the operator, Jockey Club CPS Limited, was set up by the Club's Charities Trust (stated by the space). The page gives no amounts or years.
- The Jockey Club is a non-profit organisation holding a government-granted monopoly on horse race betting, the Mark Six lottery and fixed-odds overseas football betting. About 90 percent of its annual after-tax surplus goes to its Charities Trust, which gave HK$7.3 billion to approved charities in 2022/2023 (Wikipedia).
- No reporting found of any other funder of Tai Kwun's operations; no annual report or accounts were found.

Red flags: Gambling
- The funder is the Hong Kong Jockey Club, a betting and lottery operator. Its Charities Trust set up the operator (Tai Kwun, undated), and the Club committed HK$3.8 billion to the revitalisation, which opened in 2018 (news.gov.hk, 25 May 2018).

Red flags: Protest over the Ma Jian cancellation
- 7 November 2018: Tai Kwun cancelled book launch events with the exiled writer Ma Jian, part of the Hong Kong International Literary Festival. Director Timothy Calnin said, "We do not want Tai Kwun to become a platform to promote the political interests of any individual" (Hong Kong Free Press, 8 November 2018).
- PEN Hong Kong's president said the cancellation was "at the very least an act of self-censorship" (Hong Kong Free Press).
- Tai Kwun reversed the decision two days later after public outcry; the talk was scheduled for 10 November. On Sunday 18 November 2018 twelve artists marched silently with blank placards and handed in a letter with five demands: a public explanation, a definition of "political interests", the decision criteria, the names of the decision-makers, and a complaints system (Hong Kong Free Press, 20 November 2018).
- No reporting found of a response to the five demands.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Tai Kwun is the Hong Kong Jockey Club's, a betting and lottery operator with a government-granted monopoly, so the Gambling flag stands. The documented weak point is the 7 November 2018 Ma Jian cancellation, reversed two days later after protest. Worth watching: the missing accounts and the lack of any answer to the artists' questions on decision-making.
