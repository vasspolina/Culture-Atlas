GEORGIAN NATIONAL MUSEUM: Tbilisi, Georgia
ID: georgian-national-museum-tbilisi
Website: https://museum.ge (checked 2026-10-20; serves spam: the homepage footer hides links to Turkish removals, escort and TV streaming sites in a display:none block; the former BP page, sec_id=165, now redirects to the homepage)

OVERVIEW
- Unified state museum system headquartered in Tbilisi, with member museums including the Simon Janashia Museum of Georgia, the Art Museum, the Open Air Museum of Ethnography, the Museum of the Soviet Occupation, the Tbilisi History Museum and regional museums in Dmanisi, Vani, Akhaltsikhe, Mestia, Sighnaghi and Bolnisi (Wikipedia).
- Formed on 30 December 2004 (Wikipedia); the museum says it was founded under a decree of Georgia's president in 2004 and is the legal successor of the Caucasian Museum, founded in Tbilisi in 1852 (stated by the space, our translation).
- David Lordkipanidze has headed it since its formation (Wikipedia); in 2022 he chaired its Directorate, whose other members were appointees of the culture minister (Civil.ge).
- It has a conservation laboratory at the Simon Janashia Museum and an auditorium, both set up with BP Georgia's support (BP volume, 2010).
- Exhibition halls open Tuesday to Sunday, 10am to 6pm, last entry 5:30pm, closed Mondays and public holidays (stated by the space, our translation).
- Simon Janashia Museum tickets: 20 lari for Georgian adults and 40 for foreigners, 2 lari for Georgian pensioners, students and pupils; free for disabled visitors and a companion, children under six and ICOM members (stated by the space, our translation).

FUNDING
Georgian National Museum: mainly public: Georgia's Culture Ministry, with BP as corporate partner
- A Culture Ministry-run body (Civil.ge); the museum says its institutional development is supported by UNESCO, the European Union and other international bodies (stated by the space, our translation).
- BP Georgia is its standing corporate partner: on 20 October 2026 the homepage footer carries a BP logo linking to bpgeorgia.ge.
- BP and its partners paid for the Rescue Archaeology Programme on the Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan and South Caucasus pipelines in 2000 to 2005, and for the full cycle of survey, excavation, conservation, storage, exhibition and publication (BP volume, 2010).
- No reporting found of budget figures, accounts or a donor list for recent years.

Red flags: BP oil and gas money
- BP operates three major pipelines in Georgia, including the Baku-Supsa oil pipeline and the Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan oil pipeline, built with the South Caucasus gas pipeline in 2003 to 2006 (BP volume, 2010).
- The museum's 2010 book "Rescue Archaeology in Georgia: Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan, South Caucasian Pipelines" thanks "BP and its Partners for their support"; two of its six editors, Nino Erkomaishvili and Zurab Tskvitinidze, are from BP Georgia.
- In its foreword Lordkipanidze calls BP Georgia "exceptional partners" and the tie a "prime example of public-private partnership".

Red flags: 2022 mass dismissals under minister Tsulukiani
- About 40 staff were dismissed in May 2022; the new Science, Education and Culture Workers' Union said the firings punished staff for forming the union and speaking out (Civil.ge; ArtReview).
- On 24 May the Simon Janashia Museum dismissed 21 employees; archaeologist Iulon Gagoshidze, at the museum for about 60 years, was among those sacked (Civil.ge).
- Curator Maia Pataridze said no reason was given and that new management pointed to her social media posts criticising the government (ArtReview).
- In February 2022 the Directorate refused 13 Education Ministry grants; Lordkipanidze was the only member to vote against (Civil.ge).
- Dismissed staff demonstrated outside the museum on 25 May 2022 (ArtReview; Civil.ge).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found concerns the 2022 dismissals and the ministry's control; no art-press criticism of the BP partnership was found.
1. ArtReview: unsigned, "Mass sackings of Georgian museum staff 'politically motivated'" (2022)
URL: https://artreview.com/sackings-georgian-museum-staff/
- Places the museum sackings in a pattern of dismissals at Culture Ministry bodies driven by Thea Tsulukiani, minister and deputy prime minister.
- Quotes union leader Nikoloz Tsikaridze that management rejects "critical views" on museum policy and politics.

BOTTOM LINE
The museum is a state body run under the Culture Ministry, with BP Georgia as its long-standing corporate partner and current footer sponsor. The weak points are BP's pipeline money, tied directly to the museum's archaeology and publications, and the dismissal of about 40 staff in 2022, which the union tied to criticism of the government. Worth watching: the hidden spam on its own website and whether BP's support continues.
