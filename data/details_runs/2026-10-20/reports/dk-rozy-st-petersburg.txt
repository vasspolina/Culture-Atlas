DK ROZY (ROSA'S HOUSE OF CULTURE): Saint Petersburg, Russia
ID: dk-rozy-st-petersburg
Website: https://chtodelat.org (checked 2026-10-24; live, the site of the founding collective; rosas-house.org does not resolve)

OVERVIEW
- Social centre and School of Engaged Art run by the artist collective Chto Delat in Saint Petersburg; the house has not operated since 2022 (Chto Delat, June 2023).
- Chto Delat was founded in early 2003 in Saint Petersburg. The collective's main page dates Rosa's House of Culture to 2013, alongside the School of Engaged Art; its project page says the House "opens its door on 1st of May in 2015" (Chto Delat website).
- The name follows the Soviet House of Culture model; the pages opened do not say whom "Rosa" honours. Chto Delat researched the history of Soviet Houses of Culture from 2016 (Garage Museum project page).
- Building: its first venue was the ArtMuza space. The pages opened give no street address, architect or building description.
- What was there: workshops, reading groups, a Zetkin library, a teachers' union group, feminist theatre groups, the "Shvemy" sewing cooperative, libertarian robotics and Queer Tango (Chto Delat website).
- Closure: on 24 September 2015 it stopped activity at ArtMuza, whose cluster ended the lease after, in Sobaka.ru's words, a "signal from the FSB" reached its management; Vilensky called this self-censorship on Facebook and the group chose not to challenge it in court (Sobaka.ru, 25 September 2015). After the war escalated in 2022 it suspended public programmes, and by late May 2022 the members decided to leave Russia. They opened the Chto Delat Emergency Project Room in Berlin (Chto Delat, June 2023).
- Visiting: not open. No reporting found of current opening days or admission.

FUNDING
DK Rozy: mainly self-funded by the collective: Mutual Aid Fund, participant donations and grants
- The collective's Mutual Aid Fund and participant donations paid for the House (Chto Delat website). At a 2019 discussion Roman Osminkin described "an almost complete absence of financing from outside sources (government organizations)" (FIELD journal, issue 12/13, 2019).
- Start: support from the international Draft project run by the Institute for Contemporary Art Research at Zurich University of the Arts (Chto Delat website).
- From 2017 to 2019 the Berlin association Chto Delat e.V. held an institutional grant from the Foundation for Arts Initiatives, redistributed through the Mutual Aid Fund; no amount is stated (Chto Delat website).
- Chto Delat lists grants from the Foundation for Art Initiatives, Prince Claus Foundation and Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung Moscow (Chto Delat main page); which of them reached the House is not stated.
- Garage Museum, Moscow: the research on Soviet Houses of Culture "was realized with generous support of Garage Museum, Field Research Programm" (Chto Delat House page); Garage's page dates the research to 2016. The Chto Delat main page says the collective's full archive is in Garage's collection, "purchased in 2022". No price, month or amount for either is stated, and no payment from Garage to the House itself is documented.
- Garage names Roman Abramovich and Dasha Zhukova as co-founders and calls itself a non-profit project of The IRIS Foundation (Garage website). The Art Newspaper reported in 2018 that the two would underwrite about two-thirds of the annual budget. The UK sanctioned Abramovich on 10 March 2022 with an asset freeze and travel ban (Daily Maverick, 11 March 2022). No page opened shows who controls Garage now, or that Garage is itself sanctioned.
- No reporting found of accounts or a board.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: Very little criticism by outsiders. The one critical piece found is a recorded discussion among the House's own residents.
1. FIELD: A Journal of Socially-Engaged Art Criticism: Anastasia Vepreva, Dmitry Vilensky, Roman Osminkin and Jozhi Stolet, "A Discussion with Residents of Rosa House of Culture" (2019)
URL: https://field-journal.com/issue-12-13/near-east-russia/a-discussion-with-residents-of-rosa-house-of-culture/
- Vepreva asks whether the space mainly gives free rent to friends who do not share its socialist framework, and whether its sections have common ground.
- Vilensky says of the House's aim of relaxed open doors, where visitors read, drink tea and take classes, that "we have been mistaken about the last objective", since people's schedules are now too dense; Osminkin warns that a counter-public existence risks "total self-marginalization".
- Criticism comes from the participants themselves, not an outside critic.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the House is mostly the collective's own funds and small international grants, with no state or flagged corporate funder found. The weak point is the Garage Museum link: a museum co-founded by Roman Abramovich, sanctioned by the UK in March 2022, supported the House's research and bought the collective's archive in 2022, though no page shows Abramovich controls Garage. Worth watching is whether the collective publishes accounts and who funds the Berlin successor.
