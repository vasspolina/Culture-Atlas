CUBITT: London, United Kingdom
ID: cubitt-london
Website: https://cubittartists.org.uk (checked 2026-10-24; live)

OVERVIEW
- Artist-run non-profit gallery, studio provider and education programme at 8 Angel Mews, Islington, London N1 9HH, in a former stables near Angel (Cubitt website; Islington Tribune).
- Founded by artists in 1991 in King's Cross; it moved from Goods Way to Cubitt Street, which gave it its name, then to Caledonia Street, and later to Angel (Cubitt website; Wikipedia). Wikipedia does not name the founders.
- Runs 32 artist studios, a non-profit gallery, an 18-month Curatorial Fellowship (the Curatorial Bursary from 2001 to 2015), a Civic Fellowship and Cubitt Community Radio (Cubitt website; Wikipedia).
- The landlord is a private family: Priscilla Ann Meath Baker and her sons William John Clovis Meath Baker and Joshua Ralph Meath Baker, who inherited the land from her father John Gurney (Islington Tribune).
- The landlord told Cubitt the lease would not be renewed beyond October 2026, after more than 25 years on site; the April 2026 end date was extended by six months. No reason was published and no new address is announced (Cubitt statement; Islington Tribune).
- Visiting: open Wednesday to Friday 12 to 6pm and Saturday 12 to 5pm (Cubitt website). No reporting found of admission charges or accessibility details on the pages read.

FUNDING
Cubitt: public and charitable: Arts Council England support and donations; no studio rent figures published
- Arts Council England: the website footer states it is "generously supported by Arts Council England", and Wikipedia says the Council funds the Curatorial Fellowship. No grant amounts were found on the pages read.
- Cubitt says it is not "funded by large amounts of private giving" (Cubitt statement, 2026).
- Fundraising: "Cubitt 30" is a year-long fundraising campaign; donors giving £250 or more receive a limited edition piece. A separate £100,000 appeal covers leaving the Angel premises, dilapidation costs and a move to a new home still under negotiation (Cubitt website).
- Registered charity No. 1049653, as printed on the Cubitt homepage and donation page. The Charity Commission register could not be opened (HTTP 403), so no income figures are stated here.
- Six trustees are listed: Jessica Bennett, Georgia Catarame, Collin Dursteler, Derek Horton, Raja Moussaoui and Giulia Shah (Cubitt website).
- No reporting found of Islington Council, Outset or any named corporate or private funder in the pages read.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No criticism found in art media. Coverage of the 2026 lease loss is supportive; The Art Newspaper (Gareth Harris, 12 January 2026) quotes Jennifer Higgie calling Cubitt an "important and brilliant space" and Helen Nisbet calling the loss "awful news".

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind Cubitt is clean as far as published sources show: Arts Council England support and donations, with no flagged funder found. The weak point is the lack of published funding detail beyond Arts Council England, and the loss of its home in October 2026 with no announced replacement. Worth watching is where the £100,000 appeal ends up and who the new landlord is.
