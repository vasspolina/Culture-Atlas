BELARUSIAN STATE MUSEUM OF THE HISTORY OF THE GREAT PATRIOTIC WAR: Minsk, Belarus
ID: belarusian-state-museum-of-the-history-of-the-great-patriotic-war-minsk
Website: https://war.museum.by/ (checked 2026-10-25)

OVERVIEW
- National war museum at 8 Prospekt Pobediteley, Minsk, beside the "Minsk, Hero City" stela in the Pobeda (Victory) museum-park complex (stated by the museum).
- Opened in October 1944 in Minsk soon after its liberation; Wikipedia gives 25 October 1944 and says the collection began in November 1942. It moved in 1966, in 1977 and to its current building in 2014 (Wikipedia).
- The current building opened on 2 July 2014 (Minsk tourism portal), with the Belarusian president Alexander Lukashenko and the Russian president Vladimir Putin attending (Wikipedia; Attractions Management). The architects call its form an "architectural salute", and a dome houses the Victory Hall (Minsk tourism portal).
- The museum states 10 exhibition halls covering 4,200 m² with over 8,000 exhibition items, and about 30 collections of more than 155,000 storage units, including photographs, documents, letters and personal belongings of war participants.
- Wikipedia calls it the most visited museum in Belarus and gives about 234,900 visitors in 2012.
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 19:00, closed Monday; ticket office closes at 18:30. On-site parking and a site version for visually impaired users.

FUNDING
Belarusian State Museum of the History of the Great Patriotic War: a Belarusian state museum
- The museum's site titles it «Учреждение «Белорусский государственный музей истории Великой Отечественной войны»», in English an institution, the Belarusian State Museum of the History of the Great Patriotic War (translation). Wikipedia names it the Belarusian State Great Patriotic War Museum.
- The 2014 building was opened by the heads of state of Belarus and Russia, Lukashenko and Putin, together with war veterans (Attractions Management, 8 July 2014).

BOTTOM LINE
The museum names itself a Belarusian state institution, and the presidents of Belarus and Russia opened its current building in 2014. Those two facts are the documented link between the museum and state leadership.
