MUSEUM DHONDT-DHAENENS: Deurle, Belgium
ID: museum-dhondt-dhaenens-deurle
Website: https://museumdd.be/

OVERVIEW
- Museum and contemporary art centre at Museumlaan 14, in the Deurle part of Sint-Martens-Latem, near Ghent (museum website, Focus on Belgium).
- Founded by the collectors Jules Dhondt and Irma Dhaenens, who settled in Deurle in 1938 and lived in Villa Zodenhof. In 1967 they donated 95 paintings and 11 sculptures, mostly Flemish art from 1880 to 1950, to the foundation, and a second bequest in 1969 added abstract works (museum website, collection page).
- The museum building dates from 1968 and was designed by the architect Erik Van Biervliet (museum press release, 30 August 2018; VRT, Lucas Vanclooster, 30 August 2018).
- The foundation behind it is the Stichting Mw. Jules Dhondt-Dhaenens, a private foundation recognised by the Flemish Government (museum colophon).
- A building application for an extension of 450 square metres by Robbrecht en Daem was rejected in May 2018 after six years and four attempts, and the museum appealed (museum press release, 30 August 2018). The museum reopened after renovation with two exhibitions from 17 November 2024, and has a new pavilion named after the collectors Jan Andries and Lieve Vanlouwe (Focus on Belgium).
- The museum also manages the listed Woning Van Wassenhove (1974) by Juliaan Lampens and the Wunderkammer Residence (2018) by Hans Op de Beeck (museum press release, 30 August 2018).
- Visiting: open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 17:00. The standard ticket is 20 euros during the 10th Biennial of Painting; 15 euros for groups, seniors and teachers; 10 euros for under-26s, students and people with disabilities; free for children up to 12 (museum website).

FUNDING
Museum Dhondt-Dhaenens: mixed public and private: Flemish Government, municipality and National Lottery, with corporate partners and patrons
- Public partners listed on the museum's site: the Flemish Government, the municipality of Sint-Martens-Latem and the National Lottery.
- Flemish Government: the museum said in 2018 that the government imposes basic tasks on it under its heritage policy, and a 1999 multi-year Flemish subsidy expanded its exhibition programme (museum press release; museum website, collection page).
- Extension budget: in its press release of 30 August 2018 the museum described a 6 million euro investment as a joint venture between government and private parties whose financing was nearly complete.
- Structural partners: Christie's, Zwartopwit, Duvel, Colora Gent and Jet Import. Puilaetco is the 2024 residency partner, Stichting Andries-Vanlouwe the education partner, and the Willame foundation is listed under other partners (museum website, partners page).
- Patrons: the Patroons group of art lovers and collectors meets monthly, with a price of 3,500 euros; 23 patrons are named on the site (museum website).
- The National Lottery in Belgium distributes money under a government-set mandate and counts as ordinary public funding.
- Board: nine members chaired by Christine Claus, with Philippe Leeman as vice-president; the director since 2023 is Goedele Bartholomeeusen, after Antony Hudek (2020 to 2023) and Joost Declercq (2004 to 2019) (museum website, team page).

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is a private foundation set up by the founding collectors, backed by recognition and subsidy from the Flemish Government, the municipality and the National Lottery, plus corporate partners and a patrons' group.
