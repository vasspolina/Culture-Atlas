BAUHAUS-ARCHIV / MUSEUM FÜR GESTALTUNG: Berlin, Germany
ID: bauhaus-archiv-museum-fur-gestaltung
Website: https://www.bauhaus.de/en/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Museum and archive of the Bauhaus (1919 to 1933) at Klingelhöferstraße 14 in Berlin-Tiergarten; it describes its holding as the world's largest collection on the Bauhaus, with more than one million objects (museum website; Berlin Senate).
- Founded as a private association in Darmstadt in 1960 by the art historian Hans Maria Wingler, with the support of Walter Gropius and other former Bauhaus members; it moved to Berlin in 1971 (museum website).
- Building: designed by Gropius from 1964 for Darmstadt and built in changed form in Berlin from 1976 to 1979, with Alex Cvijanovic and the Berlin architect Hans Bandel (museum website). It became a listed building in 1997 (German Wikipedia).
- The collection holds works by Feininger, Itten, Klee, Kandinsky, Moholy-Nagy and Schlemmer, workshop pieces, architectural plans and models, photographs, documents and a library; Gropius gave his private archive on the Bauhaus (German Wikipedia).
- Director: Brigitte Franzen from April 2026; Annemarie Jaeggi was director until 31 December 2025 (German Wikipedia; museum website).
- The main building has been closed since the end of April 2018. The museum works from the Temporary Bauhaus-Archiv at Knesebeckstraße 1 to 2 in Charlottenburg, where "One Million Objects", with photographs by Florian de Brün, runs until 30 April 2027 (German Wikipedia; museum website).
- Extension by Staab Architekten: 2,000 square metres of exhibition space below ground, stores, and a five-storey, 20-metre tower of steel, wood and glass as the entrance (Berlin Senate).
- Visitors: the 2019 anniversary show "original bauhaus" at the Berlinische Galerie drew over 130,000 people (German Wikipedia). "New Woman, New Vision. Women Photographers of the Bauhaus" ran at the Museum für Fotografie from 17 April to 4 October 2026 (Entwicklungsstadt).

FUNDING
Bauhaus-Archiv: mainly public: the State of Berlin and the federal government, carried by the private association Bauhaus-Archiv e.V.
- Carrier: Bauhaus-Archiv e.V. Its executive board is chaired by Markus Klimmer and includes the director and the managing director, Alexander Stockinger (museum website).
- First building: Berlin's Senate offered the site and the money after Darmstadt did not build Gropius's design (museum website; German Wikipedia).
- Renovation and extension: the Berlin Senate's building department carries out the works for the State of Berlin with federal money (Berlin Senate).
- From 2028 to 2037 the federal government pays the museum 4 million euros a year under the capital city financing contract of 2026 (German Wikipedia, citing the federal culture commissioner).
- Members: personal membership of the association costs 80 euros a year; sponsoring and corporate memberships start at 1,000 euros (museum website).
- Bauhaus Council e.V., chaired by Elke Büdenbender, promotes endowments to the museum and links it with designers, companies and researchers (museum website).
- An earlier extension failed for lack of private money: SANAA won a competition on 3 November 2005 that also sought investors to buy part of the site, none came forward and the plan was shelved (German Wikipedia).

Red flags: Cost and delay of the renovation and extension (2018 to 2027)
- At the end of 2013 the federal coalition agreed to pay half of an estimated 56.3 million euros, with Berlin paying the other half (German Wikipedia).
- By the topping-out on 9 May 2023 Berlin's budget held 92 million euros for the project, and the federal share had been raised to up to 43.38 million euros (Berlin Senate).
- The first plans foresaw completion in 2021 (Entwicklungsstadt). A completion date of autumn 2025 was later given, and on 9 February 2026 Entwicklungsstadt reported a new target of the end of 2026, with public opening in 2027, more than two years behind the previous timetable.
- Brigitte Franzen told the Tagesspiegel, as relayed by Entwicklungsstadt, that the project is delayed again and that the closure is a chance to reach new audiences.
- German Wikipedia gives the end of 2027 for the reopening.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is public: Berlin and the federal government pay for the building and, from 2028, a fixed federal grant of 4 million euros a year, with a private association as carrier. The weak point is the building project, whose budget grew from an estimated 56.3 million euros to 92 million in Berlin's books and whose reopening slipped from 2021 to 2027. Worth watching: the final cost and the opening date.
