NATIONAL MUSEUM OF GHANA: Accra, Ghana
ID: national-museum-of-ghana-accra
Website: https://gmmb.gov.gh/national-museum-of-ghana/ (checked 2026-11-20)

OVERVIEW
- Ghana's national museum at 2 Barnes Road, Adabraka, Accra.
- Established on 5 March 1957 as part of the celebrations of independence from colonial rule.
- It closed in 2015 for renovation and restoration and reopened in June 2022 after seven years; President Nana Addo Dankwa Akufo-Addo spoke at the commissioning.
- The opening exhibition after the renovation was titled "Unity in Diversity".
- Visiting: 9:00 am to 4:30 pm daily; Ghanaian adults GHS 20, non-Ghanaian adults GHS 80, lower fees for school and tertiary students.

FUNDING
National Museum of Ghana: mainly public: the Ghana Museums and Monuments Board and the Government of Ghana
- The Ghana Museums and Monuments Board owns and operates the museum and sets its fees.
- At the reopening the president said the government had committed US$10 million to rehabilitate and build tourist and heritage attractions, naming the Kwame Nkrumah Memorial Park, Cape Coast and Elmina Castles and three palace museums (Citi Newsroom, 11 June 2022).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One academic review was read; it praises the restoration and faults what the displays leave out.
1. The Conversation: Jon Olav Hove, "Ghana's National Museum: superb restoration but painful stories remain untold" (2022)
URL: https://theconversation.com/ghanas-national-museum-superb-restoration-but-painful-stories-remain-untold-185145
- The new exhibitions do not discuss the transatlantic slave trade or the colonial period.
- "Unity in Diversity" does not explore what diversity means or how it relates to tolerance.
- The museum could present many histories from varied perspectives in place of a single history of Ghana.
- Names the museum; the author teaches history at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology.

BOTTOM LINE
The money is the Ghanaian state's, through the board that owns the museum, and no funder is objected to. The weak point is the missing breakdown of what the seven-year renovation cost and who paid. Worth watching: whether the displays take up the histories the review found missing.
