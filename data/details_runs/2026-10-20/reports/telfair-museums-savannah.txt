TELFAIR MUSEUMS: Savannah, USA
ID: telfair-museums-savannah
Website: https://www.telfair.org/ (checked 2026-10-24; the corporate partners page, /corporate-partners/our-partners/, returned "not found")

OVERVIEW
- Art and history museum with sites in Savannah's Historic District: the Jepson Center and Telfair Children's Art Museum (207 W. York St.), the Telfair Academy (121 Barnard St.) and the Owens-Thomas House & Slave Quarters (124 Abercorn St.).
- Founded through a bequest from Mary Telfair (1791 to 1875); the Georgia Historical Society ran it until 1920, and it opened in 1886 in the Telfair family mansion (Wikipedia).
- Named for Mary Telfair, who left her home to be a museum.
- Telfair Academy is the 1818 Regency-style Telfair mansion. Owens-Thomas House (1816) and its slave quarters, both designed by British architect William Jay, are National Historic Landmarks. The Jepson Center was designed by Moshe Safdie and completed in 2006 (Wikipedia).
- Holds more than 4,500 American and European paintings, sculptures and works on paper (Wikipedia). The Telfair Children's Art Museum sits inside the Jepson Center.
- Closed Mondays, open 10am to 5pm otherwise. One ticket covers all three sites for seven days: adult $30, senior 65+ and active military $27, student 13 to 25 $20, child 6 to 12 $10, under 6 and members free. SNAP EBT card holders pay $3 per adult. The Jepson Center and Telfair Academy are wheelchair accessible; the Owens-Thomas House itself is not, though its slave quarters, restrooms and garden are.

FUNDING
Telfair Museums: mixed: earned income, donations and an endowment built on the Telfair bequest; corporate sponsors not confirmed
- Form 990 for 2024 (ProPublica): revenue $9.84M, expenses $9.22M, total assets $62.4M. Contributions were $3.01M. Revenue was $7.72M in 2023 against $8.62M expenses.
- Annual ball: raised about $1 million in 2019, roughly 10% of $9.9M revenue that year (The Current GA, 2026).
- Admissions fell by more than 25% in 2025, per a person familiar with the situation; endowment investment income offset the loss (The Current GA, 2026).
- Named donors: Jackie Rabinowitz and Cynthia Willett (Children's Art Museum, over $2M before opening); Walter O. and Linda Evans (28 works by African-American artists); Robert S. Jepson Jr. (artworks); PNC Foundation (Pre-K/Head Start pilot) (2023 annual report).
- The partners page that lists corporate sponsors by tier returned "not found" on 2026-10-24, and the donate page and 2023 annual report name no corporate sponsor tiers. No reporting found that I could open of Kinder Morgan, Georgia Power or Gulfstream as current sponsors.
- No reporting found of government grants in the pages I could open.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: No art-press critique of the institution or its funders found. The criticism that exists is about management: local and trade reporting on layoffs, resignations and fundraising collapse in 2025 and 2026.
1. The Current GA: Margaret Coker, "Telfair Museums Seeks New Leader After Year of 'Self-Inflicted' Wounds" (2026)
URL: https://thecurrentga.org/2026/02/26/telfair-museums-seeks-new-leader-after-year-of-self-inficted-wounds/
- The museum was recruiting its fifth executive director in seven years. Fundraising staff quit amid allegations of a toxic workplace, and the CFO resigned in summer 2025.
- About 15% of staff were cut in January 2026 because of steep revenue losses, including docents at the Owens-Thomas House.
- Board chair Susan Willetts, interim director, called the troubles "self-inflicted" and said the museum is not in distress. Former board members and employees say the museum's standing in Savannah has weakened.
- Local news outlet, not art press. Names no corporate funder.
2. USA Art News: Sarah Mitchell, "Telfair Museums appoints Caitlin Doherty as new director" (2026)
URL: https://usaartnews.com/news/telfair-museums-appoints-caitlin-doherty-as-new-director/
- Republishes ARTnews reporting: staff laid off by Zoom call without advance notice, severance offered, museum cited decreased funding; Brenneman resigned on 20 January citing a medical condition.
- Reports the new director, formerly of MOCA Jacksonville, starts in October.
- Not criticism of the art or funders.

BOTTOM LINE
No reporting found of anything wrong with the money behind Telfair itself: the base is the Telfair bequest, an endowment, admissions and private donors. The weak points are the unconfirmed corporate sponsor list (Kinder Morgan is the fossil fuel name to check once the partners page is back) and a year of layoffs, resignations and lost fundraising staff. Watch the new director's first year and whether the sponsor tiers are published.
