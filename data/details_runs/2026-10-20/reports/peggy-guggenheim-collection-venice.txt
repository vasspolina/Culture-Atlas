PEGGY GUGGENHEIM COLLECTION: Venice, Italy
ID: peggy-guggenheim-collection-venice
Website: https://www.guggenheim-venice.it (checked 2026-10-24; loads normally)

OVERVIEW
- Museum of twentieth-century European and American art in Palazzo Venier dei Leoni, Dorsoduro 701, Venice, on the Grand Canal (museum website, Wikipedia).
- Peggy Guggenheim, an American heiress, lived in the palazzo for about thirty years, having bought it in 1949, and began opening her collection to the public seasonally in 1951 (Wikipedia).
- She gave the palazzo and about 300 works to the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation in 1976 and died in 1979; the Foundation took control that year and reopened the collection in April 1980 as the Peggy Guggenheim Collection (Wikipedia). It is named for her.
- Building and site: an 18th-century palazzo with a low Grand Canal facade; later additions include a garden annex (1993), the Nasher Sculpture Garden (1995) and a new entrance (2003) (Wikipedia).
- Directors: Philip Rylands (2000 to 2017) and, since 2017, Karole Vail, Peggy Guggenheim's granddaughter (Wikipedia).
- Visiting: open daily 10:00 to 18:00 except Tuesdays and 25 December; the ticket office closes at 17:00. Free entry on Thursdays 14:00 to 18:00 for residents and people born in Venice and for students at Venetian universities. The page lists no prices and says the museum "guarantees accessibility" (museum website).

FUNDING
Peggy Guggenheim Collection: parent foundation plus corporate members: the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation and companies in the Guggenheim Intrapresæ programme
- Parent: the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation controls the museum (Wikipedia). No reporting found of the Venice museum's own accounts, budget or board in the pages read.
- Corporate programme: Guggenheim Intrapresæ, started in 1992 as the first museum corporate membership programme in Italy. Members listed: Allegrini, Apice, Eurofood, Florim, Hangar Design Group, Mapei, Pettenon Cosmetics, Roberto Coin, Rubelli, Swatch and Villa Sandi (museum website). The page gives no levels, fees or years.
- The museum's corporate page shows logos for an "Institutional Patron" and an "Inclusivity Supporter" without naming them in its text.
- Nasher Sculpture Garden: named for a patron's donation (Artlyst, 2014); no amount found.
- No reporting found of ticket revenue or state grants.

Red flags: Sackler family (opioids), at the parent foundation
- In February 2019 the protest group P.A.I.N. staged a die-in at the Guggenheim in New York against the Sackler family's support of the museum, with banners reading "Take Down Their Name" (Wikipedia, "P.A.I.N.").
- Later in 2019 the Guggenheim announced it would not accept future donations from any Sacklers involved in Purdue Pharma (Wikipedia, "Sackler family"). Artnet carried a report in 2022 headed that the Guggenheim "Has Finally Quietly Removed" the Sackler name; the page was blocked and only the headline was seen.
- No reporting found of a Sackler gift to, or the Sackler name at, the Venice museum.

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: The criticism found is a family lawsuit against the Foundation, reported in 2014; it concerns how the museum is run, not its funders.
1. ArtsJournal: Matthew Westphal, report on the suit by Peggy Guggenheim's descendants (2014)
URL: https://www.artsjournal.org/?p=173767
- The suit says the Foundation disregarded her wish that the collection be displayed in the palazzo "in its entirety and without additions," and objects to the "expansionist, corporate character" of its activities.
- Names the Foundation and the Venice palazzo. The page does not give the Foundation's reply or any court outcome.
2. Artlyst: News Desk, report on the suit by Sandro Rumney and Nicolas Hélion (2014)
URL: https://artlyst.com/?p=168749
- The plaintiffs say receptions in the garden holding her ashes desecrated it, that 181 works outside the permanent collection were shown, and they want the Nasher donation revoked and the garden name removed.
- The Foundation called the claims "baseless allegations" and said the grandchildren "are not her heirs and are not mentioned in her will." The article reports no ruling.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Guggenheim Foundation's and a published list of eleven corporate members, with no amounts. The weak point is the Foundation's Sackler history in New York, which has not been tied to Venice, and the long-running family complaint that the museum has grown beyond Peggy Guggenheim's wishes. Watch the corporate members and the unnamed institutional patron.
