MUSEO DE ARTE COSTARRICENSE (MAC): San José, Costa Rica
ID: museo-de-arte-costarricense-mac-san-jos

OVERVIEW
- National art museum in the La Sabana area of San José, in the former terminal of La Sabana airport (Wikipedia).
- Created by Law No. 6091 of 7 October 1977; Inés Trejos Araya led its founding, and it opened on 5 May 1978 (Juan Carlos Flores Zuñiga, Meer, 10 October 2023).
- The airport was built in 1937 and closed in 1974, the terminal was named architectural heritage in 1986, and it was renovated and reopened in October 2011 on a site of about 10,800 m² (Wikipedia).
- Holds 7,554 works plus 24 belonging to its dependency, the Casa del Artista (Meer, 2023); the collection includes Teodorico Quirós, Francisco Zúñiga, Francisco Amighetti, Federico Herrero and Luis Chacón (La Nación, 1 October 2026). Amighetti's 1948 mural "Agriculture" is on display (Wikipedia).
- Director in 2026: Katrin Aason Bucher (La Nación, 1 October 2026).

FUNDING
Museo de Arte Costarricense: public: Costa Rica's Ministry of Culture and Youth
- The museum's 2021 budget exceeded 1,768 million colones, about 48 per cent of it going to salaries for 60 employees (Meer, 10 October 2023).
- Its administrative board is chaired by Sally Molina Villalobos, vice minister of culture; the other members are Daniel Francisco Brenes Morales, Carola Fumero Araya, Gary Hior Hidalgo Rodríguez, Adriana Jiménez Beeche, María Otárola Luna and Emmanuel Rodríguez Chaves. No board member receives pay or allowances (La Nación, 1 October 2026).
- The Ministry of Culture and Youth confirmed on 30 September 2026 that the board approved a loan of works from the state collection to the Presidency of the Republic on 8 September 2026 (agreement MAC-017-8-2026, in force from 18 September). Works on paper are to be replaced every three months (La Nación, Fernando Chaves Espinach, 1 October 2026).
- Visitors: 37,000 a year in 2018, per a La Nación report of 3 May 2018 cited by Meer; the museum's 2022 to 2024 plan projects 32,500 a year (Meer).

CRITICISM IN ART MEDIA
Overall: One opinion piece criticises the museum's management, transparency and exhibitions.
1. Meer: Juan Carlos Flores Zuñiga, "A 45 años del Museo de Arte Costarricense" (10 October 2023)
URL: https://www.meer.com/es/76514-a-45-anos-del-museo-de-arte-costarricense
- States that the museum's website shows no current audit data (the latest published audit dates from 2021) and that management reports and minutes are out of date, and notes that the museum must comply with executive decree no. 40200 on transparency.
- Argues that after salaries the budget leaves little room for new projects, that the 32,500 visitor target is a 13 per cent fall against 2018, and that a 2019 visitor study found only 10.5 per cent of visitors were lower income.
- Faults the exhibition "Trayectorias" for a brochure instead of a catalogue and for omitting artists he calls indispensable to local art history, calls the Harold Fonseca retrospective fragmentary, and says the 2023 Salón Nacional jury lacked critical judgment and did not publish its ruling online.
- Argues the museum needs "more vision and criteria," not more money (quotation translated). The author is an art critic, founder and president of AICA Costa Rica.

BOTTOM LINE
The money behind the museum is the Costa Rican state through the Ministry of Culture and Youth, whose vice minister chairs its board. Its 2021 budget exceeded 1,768 million colones, about 48 per cent of it salaries.
